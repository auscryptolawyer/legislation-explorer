# Lay-by sales ¶7-430

Taxable supplies by way of lay-by sales made in the course of business are generally subject to GST.

If the retailer remits GST on a cash basis (¶7-300), it accounts for GST on each instalment as it is received.

Correspondingly, a GST-registered purchaser that accounts for GST on a cash basis claims input tax credits (ITCs) on a creditable acquisition by way of lay-by as the instalments are made.

If the retailer remits GST on an accruals basis, the normal attribution rules would mean that it should account for the whole of the GST in the earlier of the tax period in which the first instalment is made, or tax invoice is issued (¶7-205). This leaves the accruals basis GST remitter at a comparative cash flow disadvantage compared to a cash basis GST remitter, as well as the potential for additional compliance, if for example the lay-by customer, who is not under any obligation to complete the sale, does not complete the sale. To address this, the ATO has issued Goods and Services Tax: Particular Attribution Rules for Lay-By Sales Determination 2017 varying the normal rules (¶7-440) to allow accrual GST remitters to account for the GST on lay-by sales in the tax period in which the final instalment is made (GST Ruling GSTR 2000/12).

Correspondingly, a GST-registered purchaser accounting for GST on an accruals basis cannot claim an ITC for any of its payments until the tax period in which the final instalment is made.

Business customers should ensure that they hold a complying tax invoice, that may comprise the lay-by agreement, to support their claim for ITCs.

Cancellation of lay-by If a lay-by is cancelled, and payments are refunded, a supplier, remitting GST on a cash basis, will be entitled to a decreasing GST adjustment to the extent that it has already accounted for GST on those payments (s 19-40). The adjustment is allowed in the tax period in which the supplier finds out about the cancellation (s 29-20). There is no adjustment for accruals basis suppliers as they would not have accounted for any GST in accordance with the attribution rules described above.

If any payments are retained by the supplier, they will be treated as consideration for the supply of the lay- by service (s 102-5). This also applies to any additional payments recovered from the customer as a result of the cancellation (but not uncollected moneys required to be forwarded to the government under unclaimed moneys legislation (Interpretative Decision ID 2005/306)). The GST is attributed to the tax period in which the amount is retained or recovered (s 102-10). This applies whether the supplier remits GST on a cash or accruals basis.

Correspondingly, if a purchaser remitting GST on a cash basis makes a creditable acquisition by way of lay-by and later cancels, the purchaser will have an increasing adjustment to the extent that it has already claimed ITCs. There is no adjustment for accruals basis purchasers as they would not have claimed any ITCs in accordance with the attribution rules described above.

The business purchaser can, however, claim an ITC for the GST component of the amount retained by the supplier. This credit is attributed to the tax period in which the payment was retained or recovered. This applies whether the purchaser remits GST on a cash or accruals basis.
