# Procedure for registration ¶3-040

An entity is required to apply for GST registration within 21 days of becoming required to do so (s 25-1).

Normally, this means the entity has up to 21 days after the time the enterprise (¶3-020) first meets the relevant turnover threshold test (¶3-030). An entity can register through the ATO online services portal or together with the application for an ABN on the ABR (Australian Business Register) website (¶3-050).

An entity not required to register for GST, may voluntarily register at any time.

Processing of application The ATO must grant an entity's application for GST registration if satisfied that it is carrying on an enterprise, or intending to do so from a specified date. If the ATO is satisfied that an entity is required to be registered, it must register the entity even if the entity has not applied for registration. As a practical matter, GST registration is virtually immediate when lodged electronically.

On registration, the ATO will notify the entity of the date of effect, and their GST registration number (generally the entity's ABN) and the tax periods that apply to it (s 25-5). The date on which an entity's registration becomes effective is entered in the Australian Business Register (s 25-10(2)).

Generally, the registration takes effect from the day specified in the application. This cannot be earlier than the date on which the entity commenced to carry on an enterprise. Registration may be backdated beyond the date specified in the application if the ATO is satisfied that the entity was required to be registered earlier. If so, the entity will be treated as being subject to the GST system from that date (ss 25-10, 25-15).

However, the Commissioner cannot backdate the registration beyond 4 years, unless there has been fraud or evasion (ss 23-20, 25-10).

Illustration: Time limit for backdating GST registration Due to an honest misunderstanding of its GST obligations, a taxpayer does not register and therefore does not lodge BASs (Business Activity Statements). Subsequently, on 20 September 2026, the Commissioner determines that the taxpayer should have been registered from 1 July 2021. Due to the 4-year limit, the requirement to register can only be backdated to 21 September 2022.

The taxpayer is liable to lodge BASs for its transactions from that day onwards.

Backdating of the registration means, for example, that the entity has to account for GST on taxable supplies made since that earlier time, even though they may not have allowed for it in their prices. Penalties and interest may also apply (¶18-300 and following) A branch of a registered supplier can be separately registered (¶17-300).

Simplified rules for some non-residents Simplified registration rules apply to certain non-residents (¶9-120).
