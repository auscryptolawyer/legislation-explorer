Re Taylor; Ex Parte Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1983] FCA 316; (1983) 74 FLR 377 (25 November 1983)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re TAYLOR; Ex parte DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION [1983] FCA 316; (1983) 74 FLR 377

Bankruptcy

COURT

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

GENERAL DIVISION

Sheppard J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Bankruptcy - Bankruptcy notice - Extension of time - Power to extend time - Discretion - Appeal from refusal to set aside default judgment - Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth), s. 41. Held: (1) The discretion conferred by s. 41 of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth) to extend time for compliance with a bankruptcy notice is that provided for in subs. (6A). The only effect of subs. (6C) is to compel the court, in the event of the court making one of the findings of fact which are there provided for, not to extend time, but subject to that matter the discretion is at large.

Clyne v. Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1982) 56 ALJR 857, applied.

(2) Assuming an appeal from a decision refusing to set aside a judgment is within s. 41 the mere fact that an appeal has been lodged is not sufficient to warrant the discretion being exercised.

HEARING

1983, November 1, 24, 25. 25:11:1983

APPLICATION.

Application for an extension of time in which to comply with a bankruptcy notice, under s. 41 (6A) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth) together with an application to set aside the bankruptcy notice.

P. Urquhart, for the applicant.

D.H. Bloom, for the respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

Solicitors for the applicant: Bowman & Mackenzie.

Solicitor for the respondent: T. A. Sherman, Acting Commonwealth Crown

Solicitor.

B.A.G.

DECISION

25 November 1983

SHEPPARD J. The applicant debtor in this matter has made application to set aside a bankruptcy notice and also for an extension of time within which to comply with its provisions. There is not before me today the application to set aside the bankruptcy notice. What I am asked to do over the opposition of the Federal Commissioner of Taxation, who is the judgment creditor, is to extend further the time for compliance with it pending the outcome of an appeal which the debtor has instituted against a judgment given in the Supreme Court of New South Wales by Lee J. The short facts of the matter are that the debtor lodged income tax returns for the years ending 30 June 1970 to 1974 inclusive, in 1981. He did not lodge returns for later years of income. Pursuant to s.167 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth), the Commissioner issued notices of assessments in respect of the years ended 30 June 1970 to 1976 inclusive. The returns which were lodged on behalf of the debtor were lodged by a tax agent. An address for service of notices was given as "c/- Keith K. Stiles, Box 467 G.P.O., Sydney, postcode 2001". The notices of assessment despatched by the Commissioner in 1981 reached Mr. Stiles in the ordinary course of post. He in turn sent them to the debtor's residential address which is also shown in the returns. According to evidence given by the debtor before Lee J., they were not in fact received by him because he did not visit his home during the relevant period. That evidence was given in an affidavit upon which the debtor was not cross-examined.

2. The Commissioner recovered a default judgment on 14 December 1982. Over five months later, on 24 May 1983, the debtor took out a notice of motion seeking an order that the judgment be set aside. The motion was heard on 29 August 1983, and judgment dismissing the motion was delivered on 13 September 1983.

3. It had been argued that the debtor had a defence to the proceedings because the provisions of the Act were such as to require that service of notices of assessment on a taxpayer be effected in such a way as to bring to his personal knowledge the fact that the notices were being served. The Commissioner had acted pursuant to certain regulations which are set out in the judgment of Lee J. and which he claimed authorised him to serve the notices at the debtor's address for service as given in the returns.

4. On 30 September 1983 a notice of appeal was filed. On 4 October 1983 a notice of motion was filed on behalf of the debtor seeking a stay of proceedings on the judgment. On 31 October 1983 Lee J. dismissed that motion.

5. The notice of appeal to the Court of Appeal had endorsed upon it a statement that the appeal papers would be settled on 9 November 1983 at 12 noon. The Commissioner's solicitor attended on that day for the purpose of taking part in the settlement process. He was informed by a clerk in the Supreme Court Registry that the appointment had been cancelled because the debtor's solicitor had not filed an index as was required under the rules. The Commissioner's solicitor was not informed that the appointment to settle the appeal papers had been cancelled, nor that the debtor's solicitor had failed to file an index. I have been informed that a fresh appointment has been fixed for 9 December 1983 when the index will be settled.

6. The evidence in support of this application consists of evidence from the debtor's solicitor deposing to the facts of the matter more or less as I have recounted them. Significantly, there is no affidavit from the debtor, nor does the debtor's solicitor in any affidavit which he has sworn say that he has been advised by counsel that the appeal which has been instituted against Lee J.'s decision has reasonable prospects of success or something of that kind. Counsel appearing for the debtor this morning is not counsel engaged in the appeal, and has not turned his mind apparently to the question of whether there is a reasonable chance of success. All he did in argument was to refer me to Lee J.'s judgment, and say that it was a relevant matter for me to consider whether I thought, upon reading the judgment, the debtor had some prospect of success in the appeal.

7. In the circumstances I find myself in an invidious position. In the absence of evidence of the usual kind to which I have referred, and in the absence of any argument from the debtor's counsel, I am asked to express an opinion on the debtor's chance of success in the appeal. That is not something which I should be asked to do. Nevertheless, I have performed the exercise. Having done so, I can only say that it does not occur to me that there is any reasonable basis for a successful challenge to Lee J.'s judgment. Nothing having been put to me upon which I might rely for a different view, the exercise I have performed has to be understood in that way. But the fact that nothing was pointed out or relied upon by counsel for the debtor is, I think, itself significant. What he wishes me to do really is simply to take into account the fact that an appeal has been lodged.

8. It seems to me, that being my view, that is really determinative of this application, but there are some other matters that I should mention. During the argument there was reference made by counsel for the debtor to the provisions of s. 41 (6C) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth). Relevantly that subsection provides that where the court or Registrar, as the case may be, is of the opinion that the proceedings to set aside the judgment or order have not been instituted bona fide or are not being prosecuted with due diligence, the court or Registrar shall not extend the time for compliance with the bankruptcy notice.

9. On the basis of some authorities, which are collected in a judgment of Sweeney J. in Lipov v. Alexander Fraser and Son Ltd [1978] FCA 38; (1978) 36 FLR 126 at 130 counsel submitted that the amendments to the Act, which were effected in 1980 (at no. 12 of 1980, s. 24), had the effect of reversing the onus which in former times lay upon a debtor to establish that an application to set aside a judgment was instituted bona fide and was being prosecuted with due diligence.

10. In my opinion that is not the position at all. The discretion which s. 41 confers is that provided for in subs. (6A). The only effect of subs. (6C) is to compel the court, in the event of it making one of the findings of fact which are there provided for, not to extend time; but, subject to that matter, the discretion is at large. In this respect I would respectfully agree and adopt what has been said about the exercise of discretion under s. 41 by Lockhart J. in Clyne v. Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (1982) 56 ALJR 857. His Honour's judgment went on appeal, but the appeal was dismissed.

11. A number of matters were put to me by counsel for the Commissioner in order to persuade me that I should refuse to exercise my discretion in favour of the debtor. I have had regard to a number of them but not all of them. I have taken into account what Lee J. said in a second judgment in which he dismissed the application for a stay. I have taken into account particularly what his Honour said in relation to the debtor's failure to give evidence before him in that application. I would respectfully agree with what he there said as to the practice of debtors giving evidence in applications of that kind. One can only take the view that the debtor did not give evidence because he did not wish to expose himself to cross-examination.

12. The history of the litigation as earlier recounted shows how unsatisfactory the debtor's conduct has been. He failed to file a defence, that necessitating his application to set aside the judgment signed by default. The motion to set aside the judgment was filed over five months after it was entered. That delay has not been explained, at least before me. There is the failure of the debtor's solicitor to explain how it came about that he did not attend the first appointment to settle the index and did not notify the Commissioner's solicitors of what had occurred. It is hardly the conduct of a solicitor anxious to prosecute a genuine appeal.

13. I have referred already to the absence of evidence from the debtor's solicitor of the obtaining of any opinion of counsel as to the prospects of success in the appeal and of the way in which the submissions in relation to this matter were put to me by the debtor's counsel. I should say at this point that nothing I have said is intended at all as a criticism of or a reflection on the debtor's counsel today. He has in my opinion presented his client's case with the degree of frankness which his duty to the court requires. At the same time, he has said everything that, in my view, could have been said on behalf of the debtor.

14. In my opinion, there is much to be said for the view that the totality of the evidence coupled with the absence of the debtor from the witness box does establish positively that the appeal has not been instituted bona fide and has not been prosecuted with due diligence. I do not need to come to that question because I am satisfied that in all the circumstances the application should be refused in the exercise of the wide discretion which I have under s. 41 (6A).

15. There is a question as to whether this case is within the subsection at all because the actual application to set aside the judgment has been dealt with. What is pending in the Supreme Court is the appeal. That was a matter argued by counsel for the Commissioner. It is not a matter upon which I propose to express an opinion; for the purposes of this application I have assumed that, although the proceedings on foot are an appeal rather than an application to set aside the judgment, they are within the provisions of s. 41.

16. For the reasons I have given, the application for an extension of time within which to comply with the bankruptcy notice is dismissed. I think it must follow that the application to set aside the bankruptcy notice must also go. Accordingly, the application to set aside the bankruptcy notice is also dismissed. The debtor is to pay the Commissioner's costs, including any reserved costs.

ORDER

Orders accordingly.
