ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NOS. QT85/1550

TAXATION AFFEALS DIVISION ) QT83/95

Re: APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Deputy President D P Breen Deputy President P Gerber Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 7 July 1989

Place: Brisbane

MR K L BEDDOE (SENIOR MEMBER)

The question at issue is whether the company has made a sufficient distribution for the purposes of Division 7 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). There was also an issue before the Tribunal as to primary tax payable by the company, however the applicant's counsel stated that matter was not being pursued. The Tribunal will affirm the latter objection decision, the applicant having failed to discharge the onus.

2. In these proceedings the applicant is the nominated person within the terms of sub-section 1B(3) of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982.

  1. Sub-section 104(1) of the Act provides that a resident private company which is not deemed to have made a sufficient distribution in relation to the year of income is liable to pay additional tax upon the undistributed amount. Section 10B of the Act provides that if amounts are paid or assets distributed by a private company to any of its shareholders by way of advances or loans, or payments are made by the company on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of any of its shareholders so much of such an amount as in the opinion of this Tribunal (for the purpose of these proceedings) represents a distribution of income shall be deemed to be a dividend paid by the company on the last day of the year of income in which the payment or distribution was made.
  2. The applicant's case is that sub-section 108(1) operates so as to deem amounts distributed following the sale of the company to be dividends paid on 30 June 1979 although the transactions in question took place on or about 26 February 1979.
  3. The facts are relatively straightforward. Until 26 February 1979 the applicant and his wife were the only shareholders of a company I shall call "Enterprises Pty Ltd". On that day they sold their shares to a company I shall called "Investments Pty Ltd". That sale took place after the applicant obtained certain advice from his accountant.
  4. Before the applicant and his wife sold their shares in Enterprises Pty Ltd steps were taken to convert the net worth of the company into liquid assets. This was done by selling the company business to another company I shall call "T Pty Ltd". The applicant and his wife also transferred their shares in Enterprises Pty Ltd to a newly created share register in Darwin and gave power of attorney in favour of two accountants in the firm Peat Marwick Mitchell and Co, Darwin.
  5. It will be apparent already to those who are familiar with these types of arrangements that having taken the preliminary steps the shareholders were then in a position to dispossess themselves of the company and the merry-go-round typical of these types of arrangements commenced. I can ignore most of the gyrations and permutations as being irrelevant to the present proceedings. What is relevant are those transactions which affected the vendor shareholders and the purchasing shareholder - Investments Pty Ltd.
  6. The applicant gave evidence before the Tribunal. The effect of his evidence may be summarised by the following questions and answers during cross-examination at page 53 of the transcript:

"One day you went to Sydney - or one time you went to Sydney and like many of us who have tax burdens, you moaned about it to a friend who gave you an idea?-----I did not say I moaned about it.

Well, you spoke about it? --- I just said in general discussion - this topic was discussed.

Yes? Because I think it was fairly general that it was advertised in certain magazines that you could sell shares in your company.

Yes, and - but the transaction was not simply selling shares in a company was it? Well, that is where--- I left it to my solicitor and accountant. I asked them was this possible, because I had been told, but the actual workings of it I had no idea of.

The precise workings you may not have, but the end result was, as you understood it, if it could be done, your potential - your tax potential could be avoided by selling the shares and the profit would go into your pocket without having to pay tax; you thought you could do that?----I understood that " another company could buy the shares of this company and utilise the profits against their losses. That is all I knew.

And the effect, though, from your point of view was would have been that you paid no tax, but got the money? ----- Well, it would have minimised the amount---- of tax to be paid.

But minimised - you would pay none and get the money?.---- Right. "

  1. It was established that the applicant participated in certain arrangements pursuant to and in consequence of the agreement to sell the shares to Investments Pty Ltd (Exhibit 5).
  2. Evidence was also given by the accountant who acted for the applicant and Enterprises Pty Ltd. That evidence established that a preordained procedure had been followed to dispose of the company, and in particular the purchaser (Investments Pty Ltd) was registered as shareholder in respect of all shares as of 26 February 1979.
  3. Exhibit 10 is a letter addressed by the applicant's accountant to the Manager of the ANZ Bank, Broadbeach and dated 23 February 1979. It sets out certain transactions to be undertaken at the bank on the following Monday 26 February 1979. Those transactions were to be as follows:

1. Deposit bank cheques to the current

accounts of: the applicant $72,081

his wife $72,081

2. Deposit personal cheques drawn by the

applicant and his wife in favour of $72,081

T Pty Ltd to T .Pty Ltd account $72, OB1

3. Deposit bank cheque to T Pty Ltd account $17,900

4. T Pty Ltd repays loan to Enterprises Pty Ltd $272,418

The result of these transactions was to transfer the bank overdraft of Enterprises Pty Ltd to T Pty Ltd and leave Enterprises Pty Ltd with cash at bank amounting to $162,052. The copy documents contained in Exhibit 18 establish that these transactions were in fact carried out. It should be noted that the amount of $17,900 came from an account with the Metropolitan Building Society maintained by Enterprises Pty Ltd (Exhibit 12). The amounts of $72,081 deposited to the current accounts of the applicant and his wife were made available by a financier who I infer was associated with the purchaser of the shares.

  1. Share transfers were signed by the applicant and his wife (by their attorney) on 26 February 1979 transferring the shares to Investments Pty Ltd (Exhibit 3). The Darwin share register shows the transfers of shares registered as of 26 February 1979 (Exhibit 4) (Form 43).
  2. A document (Form 43) dated 26 February 1979 and apparently signed by Anthony Twohill and lodged with the Corporate Affairs Commission on 30 May 1979 recorded the resignation of the applicant and his wife, as directors of Enterprises Pty Ltd, effective 26 February 1979 (Exhibit 4). That document shows Anthony Twohill and Investments Pty Ltd as the new directors appointed on 26 February 1979. Exhibit 4 also contains copies of minutes of two separate meetings of directors of Enterprises Pty Ltd held at ll am on 26 January 1979, One set of minutes records the resignation of the applicant and his wife as directors of Enterprises .Pty Ltd and the abovementioned new appointments.
    1. The other minutes record the registration in Darwin of the shares transferred to Investments Pty Ltd.
    2. To succeed in this application the applicant must show that the funds residing in Enterprises Pty Ltd at the time Investments Pty Ltd purchased the company's shares from the applicant and his wife were paid or distributed to Investments Pty Ltd. (Investments Pty Ltd subsequently transferred a share to Anthony Twohill hut Twohill held the share on trust for Investments Pty Ltd).
    3. The funds were, in effect, removed from Enterprises Pty Ltd by the applicant, acting on the instructions of a Mr Ahern, when he signed a cheque dated 26 February 1979 made payable to "ANZ Bank - Bank Cheque Payable Investments Pty Ltd" for an amount of $162,062. That cheque was cleared by the company's bank on the same day and effectively stripped Enterprises Pty Ltd of its assets. The company's journal records a loan to another company I shall call "Nominees Pty Ltd". That shows a conflict between the cheque and the journal entry as to the identity of the payee.

17. There can be no doubt, in my view, that the cheque for $162,062 must have been drawn after the meetings recorded as taking place at 11 air, on 26 February 19.79.

18. Patrick Joseph Twohill gave evidence that at the relevant time he was acting in effect as locum tenen in respect of Anthony Twohill's accounting practice because of the latter's illness. Both Investments Pty Ltd and Nominees Pty Ltd were used by Anthony in the purchase of companies suitable for dividend stripping. It appears, although I cannot come to a conclusion on the point, that the entity involved in particular transactions was sometimes uncertain in the minds of those responsible for the arrangements. Whether or not there was confusion in the present case is not apparent.

19. However, the question is not whether the cheque drawn in favour of Investments Pty Ltd was applied for the benefit or at the direction of that company. The issue is whether Enterprises Pty Ltd made a payment within the terms of section 108. Certainly the cheque in favour of the bank was drawn by the applicant on the bank account of Enterprises Pty Ltd, but after he had resigned as a director. He drew the cheque at the instructions of Ahern. Mr Ahem was not called to give evidence. There is no evidence before the Tribunal as to whether Ahern was acting on the authority of another party. Ahern earned a commission for his trouble. The applicant's accountant gave evidence to the effect that he was dealing with Ahern and he was not aware of who else they were dealing with. He was not aware of Investments Pty Ltd as the purchaser of the shares "until the name cropped up" close to the time of the sale.

  1. There are therefore only two possible ways to characterise the payment on the evidence before the Tribunal. Either it was a loan to Nominees Pty Ltd as the Journal states or It was a distribution to Investments Pty Ltd, albeit a payment without apparent authority, made by a former director at the behest of Ahern. There is no evidence before the Tribunal to show that Ahern was authorised by the company to pay the funds to another party or even to withdraw the funds from the bank account. Nor is there any evidence to support the view that the applicant was so authorised when he drew the cheque.
  2. What is before the Tribunal is evidence to support the conclusion that Nominees Pty Ltd paid the funds to Investments Pty Ltd by a cheque dated 5 March 1979 (Exhibit 17). However, there is uncertainty as to what happened to the funds firstly between 26 February 1979 and 28 February 1979 when the funds were deposited with other funds to the credit of a company called "Arawa Pty Ltd" (possibly the financier) and between 28 February 1979 and 5 March 1979 when the funds were the subject of a round robin of cheques which saw Arawa Pty Ltd pay the funds to Nominees Pty Ltd which in turn paid the funds to Investments Pty Ltd. The funds did not stop with Investments Pty Ltd but their subsequent passage has no relevance to these proceedings.
  3. I find as a fact that Investments Pty Ltd was the shareholder in Enterprises Pty Ltd at all relevant dates. I also find as a fact that the transactions which took place on 26 February 1979 were arranged by Ahem. I am unable to determine and will not speculate as to whether Ahern was acting as agent for another person and in particular I do not know whether Ahern had authority to direct the applicant (who was no longer a director of Enterprises Pty Ltd but still signatory at the bank) to sign the cheque in favour of the ANZ Bank for purchase of a bank cheque payable to Investments Pty Ltd (or any other person).
  4. Mr Russell Q.C. submitted that Ahern was acting as agent for the purchaser, Investments Pty Ltd, when he accepted a bank cheque for $162,062 at the bank. Certainly the applicant's accountant gave evidence to the effect that the applicant was recommended to Ahern by a solicitor. Apparently the applicant also considered using another person but settled on using Ahern's services. The accountant did not know who he and the applicant were dealing with beyond Ahern. While it is clear that the Twohills were also involved in the arrangements it is not clear to the Tribunal that Ahern was acting as their agent, nor is it apparent that he was acting as agent for Enterprises Pty Ltd. I am unable to see any basis for finding that Ahern was acting as agent for Enterprises Pty Ltd. To infer that to be the case when Ahern has not been called to give evidence would be unsafe. Furthermore, the fact that Ahern was not called inclines me to the view that his evidence could not assist the applicant's case by controverting the evidence in the Journal of the loan to Nominees Pty Ltd (Jones v. Dunkel [1959] HCA 8; (1959) 101 CLR 298).
  5. It follows that the evidence does not support a finding that amounts were paid or assets distributed by Enterprises Pty Ltd to Investments Pty Ltd. The mere fact of appropriation of company funds by the applicant at the direction of Ahern does not establish the fact of a payment or distribution by the company.
  6. The applicant has not established that there was a payment or distribution of the kind referred to in section 108 and I can see no basis for drawing the necessary inference.
  7. The objection decision under review should be affirmed.

This and the preceding twelve (121 pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Deputy President D P Breen, Deputy President P Gerber and Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matters of QT85/1550 and QT83/95.

Secretary 7/7/89
