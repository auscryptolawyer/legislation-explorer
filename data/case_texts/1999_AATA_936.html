Sokanos and Commissioner for Superannuation [1999] AATA 936 (10 December 1999)

Last Updated: 13 December 1999

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION [1999] AATA 936

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. S1998/387

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re JAMES SOKANOS

Applicant

And COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal Deputy President B.H. Burns Mr D. Trowse, Member Mrs U. Dahl, Member

Date 10 December 1999

Place Adelaide

Decision The decision under review is affirmed.

...............(Signed). ................

Deputy President B.H. Burns

CATCHWORDS

SUPERANNUATION - calculation of invalid pension - determination of employee's "final annual rate of salary" - employee on leave without pay at date of last service - employee entitled to higher duties allowance on last day worked - whether regard can be had to rate of salary applicable as at last day worked - decision affirmed

Superannuation Act 1976 - s3(1), 5, 67

Superannuation (Salary) Regulations - 4, 5, 6

Public Service Act 1922

Public Service Regulations

Commissioner of Superannuation v Carpenter; Australian National Airlines Commission v Carpenter and Commissioner for Superannuation [1983] FCA 148; (1983) 48 ALR 230

REASONS FOR DECISION

10 December 1999 Deputy President B.H. Burns; Mr D. Trowse, Member; Mrs U. Dahl, Member

1. This is an application by Mr J. Sokanos ("the applicant") for review of a reviewable decision of a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the respondent") dated 22 August 1990, as confirmed on 23 September 1998, that his "final annual salary" as at the date of his retirement on 20 June 1990 was that commensurate with the ASO1 level, namely, $18,904.

2. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged pursuant to s37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (the "T" documents) together with statements of facts and contentions provided by both parties. There was no oral evidence called by the parties and the Tribunal was asked to determine the matter on the material placed before the Tribunal together with the parties' respective submissions.

3. The applicant was represented by Mr T. Stanley and the respondent by Mr T. Howe, both of counsel.´

4. The facts were not in dispute between the parties. By way of background, the Tribunal finds as fact the following (as were contained in the Applicant's statement of facts and contentions - Exhibit A1):

1. The applicant was born on 4 February 1949.

2. On 30 April 1975 the applicant was employed in the ATO and thereby became a contributor for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the superseded Act") and subsequently from 1 July 1976, an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the 1976 Act").

3. On 4 April 1977 he transferred to the DSS as a substantive clerical assistant Grade 1(ASO1).

4. On 18 January 1985 the applicant was an officer of the DSS receiving an higher duties allowance (HDA) equating to the total salary of a CA6 (ASO2). On this date an authorised person, within the meaning of the 1976 Act, formally recognised the applicant's HDA as an allowance of a prescribed kind, pursuant to Regulation 5(1) of the Superannuation (Salary) Regulations.

5. On 4 February 1985 the applicant's superannuation contributions were increased to reflect the total salary he was then receiving inclusive of HDA as a CA6 (ASO2).

6. On 25 March 1985 the applicant worked for the last time at the DSS. At the time he ceased work he was acting in a CA5 position (ASO2) and was being paid an HDA which reflected his performance of the duties of this higher classification.

7. From 25 March 1985 to 10 June 1985 the applicant was on paid sick leave. From 11 June 1985 to 10 September 1985 the applicant was on long service leave. These periods of leave were paid at a rate which reflected the HDA allowance as a CA5 (ASO2).

8. Between 11 September 1985 and 13 December 1985 the applicant was on leave without pay due to illness. During the period the applicant applied for and received sickness benefit from the DSS.

9. Between 16 December 1985 and 30 December 1985 the applicant received paid sick leave at the rate applicable to his substantive classification as a CA1 (ASO1).

10. Between 31 December 1985 and 4 August 1986 the applicant was on sick leave without pay.

11. Between 5 August 1986 and 1 September 1986 the applicant was on paid sick leave at a rate which reflected his substantive classification as a CA1 (ASO1).

12. Between 2 September 1986 and 20 April 1988 the applicant was on sick leave without pay.

13. On 20 April 1988 the applicant was deemed to have forfeited his office as a permanent officer of DSS. His termination payments were calculated at the rate applicable to his substantive classification as a CA1 (ASO1).

14. The applicant lodged an appeal with the Merit Protection Review Agency in relation to the decision of the DSS that he had forfeited his office.

15. On 8 January 1990 the secretary of the DSS reappointed the applicant at the ASO1 classification. The intervening period was treated as leave without pay and not to count as service.

16. The applicant was on sick leave without pay from 8 January 1990 to 28 February 1990.

17. The applicant was on paid sick leave between 1 March 1990 and 28 March 1990 calculated at the rate applicable to an ASO1.

18. The applicant was on sick leave without pay between 29 March 1990 and 20 June 1990.

19. On 20 June 1990 the applicant was retired on the ground of invalidity. His termination pay was calculated at the rate applicable to an ASO1.

20. On 22 August 1990 a delegate of the Commissioner of Superannuation determined that the applicant be retired with superannuation benefits based on a salary commensurate with the ASO1 classification.

21. On 12 September 1990 the applicant requested a reconsideration of the delegate's decision.

22. On 23 September 1998 the delegate confirmed the original decision on reconsideration. The delegate decided that the applicant's final annual salary at the date of his retirement was the salary applicable to an ASO1 as this salary was higher than the salary received by the applicant at 18 January 1985 when the payment to him of an HDA, to reflect the salary payable to a CA6, was recognized as an allowance of a prescribed kind pursuant to regulation 5(l) of the Superannuation (Salary) Regulations.

23. In the period between 4 February 1985 and 20 June 1990 the applicant's salary for superannuation purposes had been calculated to include a prescribed allowance which was regularly reduced to reflect corresponding increases in his substantive salary as a CA1 (ASO1) (see T32).

24. On 13 October 1998 the applicant applied to the AAT for review

5. Section 67 of the 1976 Act is the relevant provision for calculation of the applicant's entitlement to invalidity benefit following his retirement on the grounds of invalidity pursuant to s76W of the Public Service Act 1922. At the relevant time, section 67 of the 1976 Act provided:

"Invalidity benefit where pension not reduced on medical grounds and election not made under section 68 or 69

67. (1) This section applies to a person who-

(a) is entitled to invalidity benefit by virtue of sub-section 66 (1); and

(b) does not make an election under section 68 or 69.

(2) Where the period of prospective service of a person to whom this section applies is not less than 30 years, then, subject to sub-section (3) of this section, the annual rate of the pension to which the person is entitled is 70 per centum of that person's final annual rate of salary.

..."

In addition, s3(1) of the 1976 Act contained the following definitions which are relevant to the interpretation and application of s67.

""final annual rate of salary", in relation to a person who has ceased to be an eligible employee, means-

(a) if paragraph (b) or (c) does not apply in relation to him - his annual rate of salary on his last day of service;

(b) if the annual rate of salary by reference to which his final basic contribution was calculated is higher than the rate specified in paragraph (a) - that higher rate; or

(c) if his annual rate of salary was higher than the rate referred to in paragraph (a) or (b) at any time on or after-

(i) the anniversary of his birth last preceding his or her last day of service; or

(ii) where, on or after that anniversary, he made an election under section 47 in respect of a reduction in his annual rate of salary - the date of the election,

that higher rate;

...

"last day of service", in relation to a person who has ceased to be an eligible employee, means-

(a) except where paragraph (b) applies - the day on which he so ceased; or

..."

It was agreed as between the parties that the applicant was an "eligible employee" for the purposes of s3(1) of the 1976 Act and that he ceased to be an eligible employee on 20 June 1990, the date on which his invalidity retirement took effect (T35 p88). Section 5 of the 1976 Act goes on to define what constitutes a person's "annual rate of salary" for the purposes of the Act. It provides, relevantly, as follows:

"Annual rate of salary

5. (1) In this section, "salary" means salary or wages and includes any allowance, or the value of any allowance, or any fee, that is an allowance or fee of a kind that, under the regulations, is to be treated as salary for the purposes of this Act, but does not include any part of any salary or wages that, under the regulations, is not to be treated as salary for the purposes of this Act.

(2) For the purposes of this Act but subject to subsections (3), (3A), (3B) and (3C), the annual rate of salary of an eligible employee on a particular day is an amount equal to the amount per annum of the salary payable to him or her on that day.

(3) The regulations may provide that, in a case specified in the regulations, the annual rate of salary of an eligible employee on a particular day shall, for the purposes of this Act or a provision of this Act specified in the regulations, be an amount equal to such amount per annum as is ascertained under the regulations.

..."

Having regard to s5 of the 1976 Act, reference must then be had to the regulations which, at the relevant time, were the Superannuation (Salary) Regulations (in force under the Superannuation Act 1976). In this regard, Regulations 4, 5 and 6 provided, relevantly:

"Interpretation

4. (1) In this Part, a reference to an allowance of a prescribed kind shall be read as a reference to an allowance of one of the following kinds:

(a) an allowance that is payable to an eligible employee by reason that he temporarily performs the whole or a part of the duties of an office of a higher classification than the classification of the office occupied by him;

...

...

Certain allowances to be treated as salary

5. Each of the following kinds of allowance is a kind of allowance that is to be treated as salary for the purposes of the Act:

...

(l) an allowance of a prescribed kind, other than an allowance referred to in Part 2C, or a Parliamentary employment allowance within the meaning of Part 2D in respect of which a person has made an election in accordance with that Part, that is payable on a regular basis to an eligible employee.

Payment of allowance of a prescribed kind on a regular basis

6. (1) An allowance of a prescribed kind shall not, for the purposes of paragraph 5 (l), be taken to be payable to an eligible employee on a regular basis unless the allowance is payable in respect of duties or work performed by the eligible employee for a continuous period that commences:

(a) immediately after the eligible employee has performed for a continuous period of 12 months duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of the same kind as that allowance has been paid or is payable to the eligible employee; or

..."

It was also agreed as between the parties that as at 25 March 1985, the last day on which the applicant performed duties, he was being paid a higher duties allowance at the CA5 level (which, in current terms, translates to the ASO2 level). It was not disputed by the respondent that the applicant's higher duties allowance satisfied the test in Regulation 6(1)(a) in so far as it had been paid regularly to the applicant for a period of some 7 years prior to 25 March 1985 and that, on this basis, it was an allowance of a prescribed kind under Regulations 4(1)(a) and 5(l).

Submissions of the Applicant

6. The applicant's primary contention was that on his last day of service as defined in s3(1) of the 1976 Act, namely 20 June 1990, he was on leave without pay. Moreover, he had been on leave without pay and various other forms of leave for some five or so years prior. The effect of this, the submission continued, was that the applicant's "final annual rate of salary" was zero and, on this basis, it would be impossible to calculate an entitlement to pension under s67 of the 1976 Act. In order to overcome this anomaly peculiar to the applicant's circumstances, Mr Stanley submitted that the respondent, and the Tribunal in the respondent's shoes, should calculate his entitlement under s67 on the basis of the salary received by the applicant on the date he last worked, namely, 25 March 1985, inclusive of the higher duties allowance (at the CA5 level) he was then in receipt of. Ultimately, the effect of this would be to increase the applicant's final annual rate of salary from the ASO1 level to the ASO2 level. Mr Stanley submitted that this approach is the only approach that would be consistent with the legislative intent which is to arrive at a salary for pension calculation purposes that best reflects what the employee would have earned if he or she had not been invalided out of the public service.

7. In this regard, Mr Stanley also drew the Tribunal's attention to a letter from P. A. Parkinson, Manager, Personnel Services Unit, Department of Social Security to C Scarlett, Assistant Director, Superannuation Reconsiderations which noted the following (T56 at p163):

"At the time Mr Sokanos last worked, there was some question about his suitability to continue to undertake higher duties at the CA6 level but there is no indication on any of the file papers to suggest that, if he had continued to attend work, he would have performed duty at a level below the CA5 level. In fact the AAT transcripts which have been provided to your office previously records that Mr Hayball and Ms Hawkins had indicated that Mr Sokanos would perform duty at the CA5 level.

...

It is therefore correct to say that Mr Sokanos would have continued at the CA5 level had he not taken leave from the Department and that his salary reduced to the CA1 (ASO1) level for payment of leave entitlements as a result of his absence which has been shown to be related to his illness"

This letter, Mr Stanley contended, confirmed that had the applicant not been retired on the grounds of invalidity, he would have continued to perform higher duties and be paid at the CA5 (now ASO2) level.

8. In the course of his submissions, Mr Stanley also took the Tribunal to various authorities which, he submitted, supported a "common sense" approach that is to be adopted in the course of determining an employee's final annual rate of salary. In this regard, he referred the Tribunal to a passage in the case of Commissioner of Superannuation v Carpenter; Australian National Airlines Commission v Carpenter and Commissioner for Superannuation [1983] FCA 148; (1983) 48 ALR 230 at p234, where Bowen CJ and Woodward J, held :

"In order to ascertain the respondent's "final annual rate of salary" within the meaning of the Act, it is necessary to turn to s 5(2) which provides, so far as is relevant for present purposes, that "the annual rate of salary of an eligible employee on a particular day is an amount equal to the amount per annum of the salary payable to him on that day".

We take this to mean simply that the annual rate is to be determined by examining the employee's terms of employment as at a given day and calculating the amount of salary he would have earned in the following year on the assumption that no changes occurred in those terms of employment. ..."

Mr Stanley submitted that this was clear authority for the proposition that the phrase "final annual rate of salary" is to be interpreted consistently with what the employee would have received by way of salary but for the employee's invalidity.

9. In the event that the Tribunal was to favour the approach urged upon it by the respondent and found that the applicant's final annual rate of salary as at 20 June 1990 was that commensurate with the ASO1 level, the applicant put forward an alternative contention, namely, that in addition to the salary at his substantive classification of the ASO1 level, the applicant was also entitled, for superannuation purposes, to have included in his final annual rate of salary at 20 June 1990 any prescribed allowance he was entitled to. Mr Stanley submitted that the operation of Superannuation (Salary) Regulations 4, 5 and 6 brought about this result. He submitted that the applicant was entitled to a prescribed allowance, namely, a higher duties allowance, the quantum of which was to be determined by reference to the certificate issued by an authorised person for the purposes of the 1976 Act and the Superannuation (Salary) Regulations (T p113).

10. Expressed in other words, Mr Stanley's alternative contention was that the respondent had erred in so far as, from 1985 to 1990, the applicant's level of salary for superannuation purposes had been frozen at the ASO1 level (then $18,480) whilst his entitlement to higher duties allowance of $5,495 per annum had been progressively eroded to maintain the overall total of $18,480. Rather, the correct approach, it was submitted, was to freeze the basic level of salary of $18,480, and include the applicant's entitlement to higher duties allowance in calculation of his final annual rate of salary.

Submissions of the Respondent

11. Mr Howe, on behalf of the respondent, submitted that the phrases "final annual rate of salary" and "annual rate of salary" as utilised in the 1976 Act each contemplates a "rate" of salary which applies to a person regardless of whether or not the person is actually in receipt of any particular payment at any particular time. Mr Howe contended that, as at 20 June 1990, although there was no salary in fact paid to the applicant, it did not follow that there was no rate of salary applicable to him for superannuation purposes. Rather, Mr Howe submitted that at all times and, notwithstanding periods when the applicant was on leave without pay, there was a rate of salary applicable to him, namely, that commensurate with the ASO1 level.

12. Mr Howe suggested that the term "leave without pay" was misleading in its brevity in so far as it suggests that an employee who is on leave without pay has no applicable rate of salary, as was the contention of the applicant. In truth, he submitted, having regard to the Public Service Act 1922, the Public Service Regulations (in force under the Public Service Act 1922), and relevant determinations of the then Public Service Board (now, Public Service and Merit Protection Commission), an employee who is on leave without pay has an applicable rate of salary; the phrase "leave without pay", it was suggested, refers only to the fact that the employee is not entitled to payment in respect of leave (under the terms and conditions of the employee's employment which are referrable to the rate of salary) which is quite separate and distinct from salary. Mr Howe also referred the Tribunal to s82D of the Public Service Act 1922 which, he said, is the legislative basis for the differential treatment of salary and allowances including those pertaining to leave of absence.

13. Mr Howe submitted that given the applicant's concession that his final annual rate of salary is to be calculated as at 20 June 1990, the real issue is whether, as at this date, the applicant was entitled to payment of higher duties allowance. Mr Howe submitted that he was not for two reasons. First, having regard to relevant provisions in the Public Service Act 1922 and the Public Service Regulations, an employee is only entitled to a higher duties allowance where there is in force a direction to this effect. Regulation 116A is the relevant provision in the Public Service Regulations and provides:

"Division 5 - Temporary performance of duties - offices other than Senior Executive Service offices

...

Directions to act

116A. (1) In this regulation:

...

(2) The Secretary of a Department may give a direction in writing to an officer to perform temporarily the whole, or a specified part, of the duties of an office in that Department.

(3) A direction, other than a direction required to be notified, takes effect on the day it is given or, where a later day is specified, on that later day.

(4) A direction shall be expressed to continue in force:

(a) until the expiration of a specified day; or

(b) until the occurrence of a specified event;

and, subject to subregulation (9), shall cease to have effect accordingly.

(5) A direction shall not be expressed to operate "until further notice" or to that effect.

...

(9) Where an officer is given a direction to perform duties of an office in a Department, the Secretary of the Department may, by writing:

(a) revoke the direction at any time; or

(b) vary the direction at any time by requiring the officer not to perform, or to cease performing, part of those duties after that time."

14. Mr Howe submitted that in the applicant's case, as at 20 June 1990, there was no direction under Regulation 116A(2) in force, as the previous direction had expired on 10 September 1985 (T186).

15. Secondly, an employee's entitlement to payment of higher duties allowance ceases when a worker takes leave without pay. In this regard, Mr Howe took the Tribunal to the Public Service Board Determination (No 19 of 1984) in force at the relevant time under the Public Service Regulations which provided, for present purposes, that:

"3.1.2 Additional periods where higher duties allowance is payable

3.1.2(1) In this Division, unless the contrary intention appears, a reference, in relation to an officer to whom this Division applies, to a period during which an officer performs the duties of a higher office shall be read as including -

(a) if, immediately before the officer is absent on leave of absence with pay (other than long service leave) the officer was performing the duties of a higher office - that part of the period of such leave during which, but for the leave, the officer would have continued to perform the duties of a higher office -

(i) in the case of an officer - in pursuance of a direction under regulation ... 116A; and

(ii) ...

..."

16. Mr Howe submitted that so long as an officer's performance of higher duties is interrupted by a period of leave with pay, the officer has a continuing entitlement to be paid that higher duties allowance. For example, in the applicant's case, for the period directly after 25 March 1985 when the applicant was on sick leave, he was entitled to, and indeed was paid, on the basis of his basic salary together with the higher duties allowance. However, under point 3.1.2 above, when leave without pay intervenes, an officer is not so entitled. In other words, Mr Howe's submission was that, in essence, a higher duties allowance is a bonus for actual work performed over and above one's ordinary duties and, thus, is not intended to survive a break in the person's service, except where that break or leave attracts pay. Hence, for both reasons, Mr Howe submitted that the applicant could not, as at 20 June 1990, be said to be entitled to a higher duties allowance over an above his substantive salary classification at the ASO1 level.

17. Mr Howe also provided the following example in rebuttal of the applicant's suggested approach to the interpretation of "final annual rate of salary". Where a worker is, for several weeks prior to retirement on the grounds of invalidity, on sick leave with half pay, adopting the applicant's approach would have the effect of calculating that person's entitlement to pension under the 1976 Act on the grounds of the rate of salary being received as at the last day worked, namely, half pay. This, Mr Howe submitted, would clearly disadvantage such a worker whose actual "rate" of salary was twice that being received as at the last day of service.

18. In answer to questions put by the Tribunal about what was the intention of the legislation in respect of a scenario (like that before the Tribunal) involving an employee who has performed higher duties and been in receipt of higher duties allowance continuously for a period of years and who is then forced, through no fault of their own, to take leave without pay by reason of invalidity and, further, where it is clear that but for invalidity, the person would be back at work performing higher duties and being paid higher duties allowance, Mr Howe replied that the intention was, unfortunately for the applicant, to assess entitlement to invalidity benefit on the basis of the basic salary level without higher duties allowance. Mr Howe submitted that there was no discretion in the legislation for a decision-maker to utilise any other rate of salary than that applicable to the employee on his or her last day of service which, in the applicant's case, did not include an entitlement to higher duties allowance.

19. Finally, addressing Mr Stanley's alternative contention, Mr Howe submitted that the freezing of the figure of $18,480 was done in accordance with s46 and s47 of the 1976 Act. The purpose of this was to maintain at the highest possible level, the rate of the applicant's salary for the purpose of assessing the contributor's basic contributions. Thus, it was said, there was no unlawful "eroding" of the applicant's entitlement to higher duties allowance as was suggested by the applicant. Such entitlement, the respondent submitted, had ceased from 10 September 1985.

20. The Tribunal would indicate that it has had regard to the totality of the material placed before it and, particularly so in respect of the submissions advanced by either side and the relevant authorities referred to therein.

21. In respect of the method by which the applicant's final annual rate of salary is to be ascertained, the Tribunal would indicate that it is in agreement with the respondent's submission, namely, that the relevant provisions in the 1976 Act focus on the applicable "rate of salary" as at the employee's last day of service as opposed to that which the employee may or may not be receiving at that time. Hence, simply because the applicant was on leave without pay as at his last day of service, namely 20 June 1990, does not, in the Tribunal's view, permit the respondent, or the Tribunal in the shoes of the respondent, to utilise for pension calculation purposes the rate of salary he was receiving as at the last day he physically performed his duties, namely, 25 March 1985. Rather, the Tribunal finds that there was, as at 20 June 1990, a rate of salary applicable to the applicant, notwithstanding the fact that he was then on leave without pay. In arriving at this finding, the Tribunal would indicate that it agrees with the respondent's submissions regarding the distinction that is to be drawn between an employee's basic rate of salary and the various allowances, terms and conditions referable to that rate of salary, including those pertaining to leave of absence including leave without pay.

22. The Tribunal finds the applicant's final annual rate of salary on the applicant's last day of service i.e. the day on which he ceased to be an eligible employee namely 20 June 1990 was the rate applicable to the ASO1 level. Further, having carefully considered the legislative scheme established by the 1976 Act in conjunction with that established by the Public Service Act 1922 and relevant delegated legislation, the Tribunal can see no justification for including any higher duties allowance in his final annual rate of salary as at 20 June 1990, over and above the ASO1 rate of salary . Rather, the Tribunal finds that any entitlements in this regard had, for the reasons stated by Mr Howe, ceased upon expiry of the relevant direction under Regulation 116A(2), namely, on 10 September 1985.

23. The Tribunal would indicate that in all other respects, it agrees with the respondent's position as was outlined in the submissions advanced by Mr Howe. It follows, therefore, that the Tribunal rejects the position put forward by the applicant for determination of "final annual rate of salary", namely, by having regard to the rate of salary as at the final date on which the applicant performed duties. The Tribunal's considered view is that this position cannot succeed because it would fail to take into account the relevant provisions of the Public Service Act 1922, the Public Service Regulations and the Public Service Board determination in force at the relevant time.

24. For these reasons, the decision under review is affirmed.
