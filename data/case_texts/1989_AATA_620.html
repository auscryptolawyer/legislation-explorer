QT87/3104 and Commissioner of Taxation [1989] AATA 620; (1989) 20 ATR 4102; 89 ATC 884 (20 October 1989)

Last Updated: 17 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Allowable- deductions - scheme co purchase house and land - land used for extension of hotel premises - scheme to relocate house and sell at a profit - claimed that a loss actually incurred - whether deductible - basis for apportionment of cost of purchase where there is a dual purpose - finding that no loss incurred.

The objection decision under review is affirmed.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s 51, 52

Chapman v. FC of T 1(1968) [1968] HCA 4; 117 CLR 167

SENIOR MEMBER

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBVNAL)

) NO. QT87/3104

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPINDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 20 October 1989

Place: Brisbane

The question at issue before the Tribunal is whether the applicant company is entitled to a deduction for its share of a claimed loss incurred by the partnership of which it is a member. The loss is said to arise out of a profit making scheme involving the sale of a removal house.

  1. The relevant statutory provisions are sections 52 and 51 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. In the return of income lodged by the partners an appropriate declaration in terms of s.52 was included in the return.
  2. The relevant facts are straightforward and may be shortly stated as follows. By a contract, dated 31 January 1984 (Exhibit B), the partners purchased a property I shall describe as Blackacre. Blackacre is located in a large provincial city. The property was a normal residential property of approximately 1/4 acre and with a 30 year old traditional style house on it. The property was unusual, however, in that its boundaries, other than the frontage, adjoined an hotel/motel owned and operated by the partnership. In other words Blackacre was surrounded on three sides by the hotel/motel premises.
  3. Notwithstanding Blackacre's location, the property had continued to be used for residential purposes by a married couple not in any way associated with the partners. The evidence before the Tribunal established that the house located at Blackacre was maintained in good order and was the style of house suitable for sale as a removal house.
  4. The controlling mind of the applicant company and also of the partnership of which the company was a member, was at all relevant times one D. D gave evidence before the Tribunal which established that D had long held the desire that the partnership should purchase Blackacre and incorporate the property into the hotel/motel site. The relevant plan of subdivision makes it very clear that the hotel/motel site would be vastly improved by the addition of Blackacre to it. When Blackacre came on the market for sale D conceived a plan that the partners should acquire Blackacre, remove the house to another block of land yet to be acquired by the partners and then sell the land with the house as a package and at a profit to the partnership.
  5. D's scheme was not conceived out of ignorance of the procedures necessary for resiting and selling of such houses. He had previous experience of a number of such removals as a builder and was confident that the house in question when resited could be sold at a profit or at least on a break even basis. It is clear from the evidence that D had no intention of making a loss out of the scheme.
  6. It will be apparent, and I so find, that the decision to purchase Elackacre disclosed a dual purpose on the part of D as the controlling mind of the partnership. The land was to be incorporated into the hotel site and the house was to be removed to another site which then would be resold at a profit. That this is so is reinforced by the actions of the partnership upon completion of the contract for the purchase of Blackacre. Immediate action was taken to remove the house and to incorporate the land into the hotel site by building a liquor barn on the land.
  7. As it transpired, the market conditions prevailing at the time in respect of removal homes were adverse to vendors of such homes. This was because a relatively large number of such houses had been finding their way on to the market at that time due to a number of development projects taking place in the city area. Furthermore, D's confident expectations of finding a suitable block of land on which to site the house were not realised. He said in evidence that he inspected a number of vacant blocks of land but each had adverse features which would not make it a good resale proposition.
  8. As a result of these adverse factors D abandoned the profit making scheme and decided to sell the house to an associated company carrying on a pastoral business on a property several hundred kilometres from the site in question.
  9. The consideration for the purchase of Blackacre was $60,000. The partners have apportioned this consideration as to $25,000 for the land and $35,000 for the house. These figures do not take into account the costs of purchase which have also been apportioned on the same basis but in respect of which there is no evidence before the Tribunal.
  10. Evidence before the Tribunal established, and I so find, that a comparable block of vacant land would be valued at about $25,000 and a comparable house for removal was selling at the relevant times for around $15,000 to $16,000. (Exhibit 1). It will be apparent that on the basis of those market values the land and the house when removed from the land had a total value of about $40,000. The apportionment of cost adopted by the partners has attributed the extra $20,000 to the cost of the house.
  11. In my view the apportionment adopted does not accord with the facts. It is clear on the evidence that Blackacre had a special value to the partners as a vacant block of land and that value was well in excess of the value that has been attributed to the land as a residential site. In my view a correct apportionment between the land and the house would be to attribute $44,000 as the cost of acquiring the unimproved block of land for incorporation into the hotel site and $16,000 as the cost of acquiring a house for removal to another site.
  12. This is consistent with the intention, at all relevant times, of the partnership to remove the house from Blackacre and return Blackacre to the status of unimproved land in preparation for expansion of the hotel site. With respect, the error that appears to have been made on both sides in these proceedings is to treat Blackacre as an unimproved residential site for the purpose of determining its value. There are two problems with this approach. The first is that the land was zoned so as to be capable of being incorporated into the hotel site and the second is that the land had a special value to the partners which it did not have to anyone else except the vendor who, it must be inferred from the evidence, knew it had a special value. It is established by the evidence that the partners had been offering to purchase the land over many years without success and that lack of success was caused by the fact that the vendor was seeking consideration considerably higher than the partnership was prepared to pay. The special value which the land had to the partnership should be measured by the consideration paid in the arms length sale to the partners being reduced by the market value of the house when sold for removal. No other rational basis presents itself on the evidence before the Tribunal.
  13. The valuer called by the respondent sought to value the land by aligning it with other sales of land in the vicinity and without giving weight to the fact that the land had a special value to the partners. Furthermore, the valuer failed to consider all relevant sales in the area and specifically ignored the sale of Blackacre which is the subject of these reasons. The analysis undertaken by the valuer was therefore incomplete. There is little point coming to this Tribunal with valuations that have been prepared on a hypothetical basis and which ignore actual sales evidence in the market place. The best way to find the value of Blackacre or any other real property is to put it up for sale in an open market. That happened in this case and the purchasers and the vendors, acting at arms length from each other, agreed on a value of $60,000 as the consideration under the contract. The supposition that no other purchaser would have offered $60,000 because of the circumstances of its location is irrelevant. Blackacre had a special value to the partners and that must be accepted as the value of the land at that time.
  14. I am satisfied that the partners had two purposes when they acquired Blackacre (Chapman v. F.C. of T. [1968] HCA 4; (1968) 117 CLR 167). The primary purpose was to acquire Blackacre for the purpose of extending the hotel premises. This is clearly an affair of capital and no consequences flow from it in these proceedings.
  15. The secondary purpose was to remove and sell the house on Blackacre in such a way as to make a profit for the partnership. To achieve this D devised the scheme of removing the house to a suitable block of land to be acquired as part of that scheme, to carry out necessary works to put the house into saleable condition and then to sell the newly acquired property for a proposed figure of $55,000 to $60,000(Exhibit 1).
  16. It will be apparent that the profit to be realised upon the scheme depends upon how the purchase price for Blackacre was to be apportioned between the land and the house. The partners apportionment, which I find to be not supported by the evidence, would have resulted in a small profit. The basis which I have already intimated as being the correct basis of apportionment would have resulted in a somewhat larger profit arising from the scheme.
  17. In the event the scheme proposed by D was abandoned because the partners feared a loss (on their figures) of an amount approaching $10,000. They therefore decided to sell the house as a removal house to the associated company for $17,000 which resulted in a loss of $19,198 on their figures. The paradox is apparent!
  18. The Tribunal finds, on the evidence, that the house was sold as a removal house for approximately the same amount as it cost the partnership. The purchase price attributable to the house as a removal house and the sale price are approximately the same and allow me to conclude, on the evidence of D that the partners sold the house to the associated company for the amount they considered it had cost them. No loss was incurred and the respondent's assessment disallowing the claim for a loss is therefore not excessive.
  19. The objection decision under review will be affirmed. The same decision will also be made in respect of other partners whose applications are before the Tribunal, for the reasons given above.

This and the preceding four (4) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT87/3104.

Secretary 20/10/89
