Last Updated: 1 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - Superannuation Fund - whether in excess of 10% of "in-house assets" -investment in unit trust with employer-sponsor as trustee - application of S.15AA Acts Interpretation Act

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936: ss.23F, 23FB, 26AAB, 121C Taxation Administration Act 1953: S14ZK

Acts interpretation Act 1901: S15AA

Charles v. FCT [1954] HCA 16; (1954) 90 CLR 598

Costa & Duppe Properties Pty Ltd v. Duppe [1986] VicRp 9; (1986) VR 90

AAT Decision No 6095

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No: QT89/350

Date: 6 August 1990

Place: Brisbane

Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

The Tribunal decides that the objection decision under review be affirmed.

(sgd) K L Beddoe

(SENIOR MEMBER)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NOS. QT89/350

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 6 August 1990

Place: Brisbane

In this application the question at issue is whether the applicant superannuation fund had in-house assets exceeding 10% of the cost of all assets of the fund during the year of income ended 30 June 1986.

2. The relevant statutory provision is sub-section 121C(5) which provides that the investment income of a superannuation fund is not exempt from income tax by virtue of sections 23F and 23FB of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Act") unless, at all times during the year of income, the cost of in-house assets of the fund did not exceed either

(i) the cost of in-house assets of the fund as at 11 March 1985; or

(ii) 10% of the cost of all assets of the fund;

whichever is the greater.

For the purposes of subsection 121C(5) of the Act subsection 121C(1) provides, unless the contrary intention appears, that "in-house assets" means an asset of the superannuation fund that consists of a loan or other investment in an employer sponsor of the fund or an associate of an employer sponsor. "Superannuation Fund" is defined to mean a fund to which section 23F or section 23FB apply in relation to the year of income.

3. Subsection 121C(1) also provides that "associate" has the same meaning as the defined meaning in subsection 26AAB(14). "Associate" is there defined in terms appropriate to sales of leased motor vehicles but not quite appropriate to the circumstances dealt with in section 121C.

4. The hearing of this matter proceeded by way of a statement of agreed facts and various primary documents were put into evidence by consent. The agreed facts (edited to maintain confidentiality in accordance with section 14ZK of the Taxation Administration Act 1953) are as follows:

As regards the F Pty Ltd Superannuation Fund:

  1. The Trustees of the Fund are H and W.
  2. The employer contributing to the fund is F Pty Ltd as Trustee of the Family Trust.
  3. The superannuation fund is operated under a Deed dated 1st March 1982.
  4. The members of the Fund as at 30th June 1986 were:

H

W

R

P

  1. The contribution for the year ended 30th June 1986 by the employer-sponsor was $16,507, as shown in the income tax returns.

As regards the Property Trust:

  1. The Trust was established by a Deed dated 21st February 1985.
  2. The Trustee of the Trust if F Pty Ltd.
    1. All of the units of the Property Trust are held by the Superannuation Fund.
  3. As at 30th June 1986 the liabilities of the trust included loans from:

H and W $79,793

The Family Trust $99,766

as shown in the income tax returns.

As regards Employer-Sponsor:

  1. The employer-sponsor for the purposes of the F Pty Ltd Superannuation Fund is F Pty Ltd as Trustee of the Family Trust.
  2. The Directors and sole shareholders of F Pty Ltd are H and W.
  3. The primary beneficiaries of the Family Trust are:

H

W

R

P

L

The latter three are the children of H and W.

As regards the Family Trust:

  1. The Family Trust was established by a trust deed dated 2nd April, 1979.
  2. The primary beneficiaries of the Trust were:

H

W

R

P

L

The latter three are the children of H and W.

  1. The Trustee of the Family Trust is F Pty Ltd.
    1. The directors and sole shareholders of F Pty Ltd are H and W.
  2. The trust deeds of the Family Trust, the Property Trust and the Superannuation Fund are in evidence before the Tribunal (Exhibits 1, F and D respectively).
  3. The trust deed in respect of the Property Trust provides that the trustee shall hold the trust property upon trust absolutely for the unit holders (Clause 2). "Unit holder" is defined by the deed to mean the person for the time being registered as the holder of one or more units and "units" are defined as an undivided part or share in the trust property as described in Clause 5. (Clause 1). Clause 5 prescribed in part that each unit shall confer an equal interest in the trust property as a whole. The 'trust property" is defined by Clause 2 to effectively include all the assets of the trust.
  4. It will be apparent from the facts set out above that the Superannuation Fund owns all the units in the Property Trust It will also be apparent that F Pty Ltd as trustee of the Family Trust is the employer-sponsor of the Superannuation Fund and F Pty Ltd is also the trustee of the Property Trust.
  5. Exhibit B is a copy of the Superannuation Fund Income Tax Return for the year of income ended 30 June 1986. That return reveals that during the year of income the Fund redeemed its units in another property trust and invested the funds in the Property Trust
  6. Because the investment in the property trust of $23,000 occurred during the year of income ended 30 June 1986 and the cost of the units represents approximately 30% of the cost of the assets of the Fund the only question to be decided is whether the investment represents an in-house asset
  7. As the facts show F Pty Ltd is the trustee of the Family Trust and also the trustee of the Property Trust.

11. F Pty Ltd is the legal person in both instances. It therefore follows, and I so find, that the trustees of the Superannuation Fund invested with the employer-sponsor when they purchased the units in the Property Trust.

12. The question is, however, whether the trustees invested in the employer-sponsor. The definition of "in-house asset" in sub-section 121C(1) refers to an asset of the fund that consists of an investment in an employer sponsor of the fund. In this case it cannot be said that the trustees of the Superannuation Fund invested in the employer sponsor. What the trustees did was invest in the units of the property trust thereby investing in the property of the Property Trust. This is made clear by the following dicta of the High Court in Charles v. FCT [1954] HCA 16; (1954) 90 CLR 598 where the Full Court said (at pages 608-0):

"At first sight it may seem that a person who invests in units under a trust deed such as that which is here in question does so with a view to obtaining the half-yearly distributions for which the deed provides, just as he might have bought shares in an investment company with a view to deriving half-yearly dividends from them; and that the periodical distributions received should be regarded as income in the one case just as they would be in the other. Some such view, indeed, would appear to be suggested by the brochure which the managers issued, for the amount to be received by a unit holder in a half-yearly distribution is spoken of throughout that document as income of the unit. But the view is untenable, for a unit held under this trust deed is fundamentally different from a share in a company. A share confers upon the holder no legal or equitable interest in the assets of the company; it is a separate piece of property; and if a portion of the company's assets is distributed among the shareholders the question whether it comes to them as income or as capital depends upon whether the corpus of their property (their shares) remains intact despite the distribution: Inland Revenue Commissioners v. Reid's Twstees (1949) AC at p.373. But a unit under the trust deed before us confers a proprietary interest in all the property which for the time being is subject to the trust of the deed: Baker v. Archer-Shee [1927] UKHL 1; (1927) AC 844; so that the question whether moneys distributed to unit holders under the trust form part of their income or of their capital must be answered by considering the character of those moneys in the hands of the trustees before the distribution is made.

  1. In the present case, if the $23,000 had been loaned to the trustee of the Property Trust then it is clear enough that it would be a loan to the employer sponsor, and therefore an in-house asset, because the loan would be made by the Superannuation Fund to F Pty Ltd. It does not follow, in my view, that the purchase of the units in the Property Trust has the same result The Superannuation Fund has made an investment in the trust by acquiring the units thereby acquiring a proprietary interest in the ownership of the property subject to the trust; the fund being the sole unit holder. The property held by F Pty Ltd as trustee of the Property Trust was therefore beneficially owned by the trustees of the Superannuation Fund (Clause 3 of Exhibit F). It follows that the trustees of the Superannuation Fund invested in the property of the Property Trust; not the employer sponsor (see Costa & Duppe Property Ltd v. Duppe [1986] VicRp 9; (1986) VR 90.)
  2. It cannot be said therefore that the units held in the Property Trust represent an "investment in" the employer sponsor as required by the definition of "in-house asset" in subsection 121C(1).
  3. The next question is whether the units in the Property Trust are an investment in an associate of an employer sponsor.
  4. Mr Bickford, who appeared for the respondent, submitted that the family of H and W and their family are associates of F Pty Ltd as trustee of the Property Trust and associates of F Pty Ltd as trustee of the Family Trust. He went further and submitted that the trusts are also associates by virtue of the operation of paragraph 26AAB(14)(C)(ii) which provides:

"associate'', in relation to a person (in this definition referred to as the "taxpayer") means -

(a) where the taxpayer is a natural person, other than a taxpayer in the capacity of a trustee -

(i) ...

(ii) ... (iii) ...

(iv) a trustee of a trust estate where the taxpayer ... is capable ... of benefiting under the trust,..., or (v) ...

(b) ...

(c) where the taxpayer is a trustee of a trust estate -

(i) any person who benefits or is capable (. . .) of benefiting under the trust estate, either directly or through any interposed companies, partnerships or trusts;

(ii) where a person who is an associate of the taxpayer by virtue of sub-paragraph (i) is a natural person - any person who, if that natural person were the taxpayer, would be an associate of that natural person by virtue of paragraph (a) or this paragraph; or

(iii) . . .

  1. It seems to me that it is beyond doubt that H and W are deemed to be associated with F Pty Ltd. Not only does the definition of associate have that effect but they are associated in the ordinary meaning of the word. That means that the trustees of the Superannuation Fund are associates of F Pty Ltd in its capacity as the employer sponsor.
  2. The result, according to Mr Bickford, is that the trustees of the Superannuation Fund have made an investment in the trustee of the property trust which is associated with the employer sponsor of the Superannuation Fund. As I have already explained I am unable to accept that submission because i cannot see that the investment in the units of the Property Trust is an investment in the employer sponsor or anyone who is an associate of the employer sponsor. Furthermore I do not understand the definition of "associate" to deem F Pty Ltd to be an associate of itself. The result must be that the trustees of the Superannuation Fund have not invested in an associate of the employer-sponsor.
  3. In coming to my decision I have taken into account the normal right of F Pty Ltd to be indemnified from the trust assets in respect of business transactions entered into in its capacity as trustee of the Property Trust (Vacuum Oil Co Pty Ltd v. Wiltshire [1945] HCA 37; (1945) 72 CLR 319). This means that each of the trust assets may be subject to a prior lien in favour of the trustee. In view of the very wide powers conferred upon the trustee by Clauses 10 and 14 of the deed the overall effect is that the funds invested in the units have been invested in such a way that they are not only in the complete control of the employer-sponsor but also subject to the employer's right as trustee, to be indemnified out of the trust assets.
  4. "In-house asset" as defined does not meet the circumstances of this case; but to find for the applicant would be to defeat the clear intention of the legislation. Section 15AA of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 requires that I have regard to the purpose or object underlying an Act The purpose or object of section 121C which is readily apparent from the section is to deny exemption to superannuation funds which invest or loan the funds of the superannuation fund in or to the employer or an associate of the employer, except within the limits set out in the section.
  5. In the present case the investment in the unit trust controlled by the employer sponsor is clearly an in-house arrangement although not falling within the definition of "in-house asset". It would be contrary to the clear intention of the section to find that the units in the Property Trust are not in-house assets. I am satisfied therefore that the contrary intention appears and that I should find that the units in the Property Trust are in house assets for the purposes of section 121C.
  6. I am aware that it is not the function of this Tribunal to remedy defects in the drafting of legislation, especially taxation legislation. However this is a case where section 15AA applies and must be given effect by holding that the investment in the trust is an investment In in-house assets notwithstanding the defined meaning of "in-house assets". The objectionable feature of the arrangement is the position of F Pty Ltd as trustee of the Property Trust and also as the employer-sponsor.
  7. The result is that section 121C operates to deny the applicant exemption. The objection decision under review will be affirmed.

This and the preceding eight (8) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT89/350.

(Secretary)
