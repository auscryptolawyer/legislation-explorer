Tribunal : Deputy President R.K. Todd

Date : 8 June 1988

Place : Canberra

Decision : The Tribunal sets aside the objection decisions under review and remits the same to the respondent for reconsideration in accordance with the directions that:

(i) Effect be given to the concessions and agreements contained in the document headed "Summary of Agreed Facts" which is part of Exhibit 8 herein;

(ii) For the purpose of giving such effect and generally for all purposes that
  • (a) Twin Willows Turf Farm Syndicate never commenced to carry on a business;
  • (b) Pechey Lychee Growers Pty Ltd carried on a business as and from 1 May 1983;

(iii) Save as provided for by the abovementioned concessions and agreements and conformably with (ii)(b) above in respect of any remaining management fees claimed to be allowable as deductions, the applicants' claims for deductions be disallowed.

(sgd.) R.K. Todd

REASONS FOR DECISION

8 June 1988 Deputy President R.K. Todd

  1. These two matters, in which the applicants were husband and wife, were heard together. The relevant facts and the legal situation are essentially the same in both cases. The evidence however for the applicants was given by the husband, and I shall refer to him as H. Although the wife, whom I shall call W, appears to have been involved in decisions that were taken, and to have intended to be involved in business affairs in the future, H was the prime actor and it is primarily his activities that will be referred to.
  2. The matters left in dispute concern the years of income ended 30 June 1982 ("fiscal 1982") and 30 June 1983 ("fiscal 1983") as far as H is concerned, and fiscal 1983 as far as W is concerned. H, whose career had been as an officer in the Defence Force, had for some time been contemplating retirement and considering how after retirement he should employ himself and how he should deploy his financial resources. For some time, he said, he had been extensively engaged in studying the operations of the stock market and other avenues of investment management. He had also had some involvement in the stock market during the heady days of the Poseidon boom, whether successfully or not did not emerge. I accept H's evidence as to his investment studies. The history of his actual investments as revealed in the present cases indicate however that he was also attracted to schemes redolent with tax minimisation. I do not doubt that H was genuinely interested in being involved in a positive way with rural ventures. But the structures of the ventures in which he did invest, and which are referred to below, have an all too familiar ring, needing complex flow charts to explain the myriad transactions and the confusing corporate and incorporate entanglements that were evolved for the purpose of carrying on the task of growing, in these cases, grass and fruit trees respectively. Such schemes, unhappily, appear to draw Defence Force retirees and intended retirees to them like moths to a lamp, and having heard more than one such case I confess that I find it sad to see people, who have for no great financial reward served their country with dedication, enticed into schemes devised by promoters and their associates who are motivated by little but "get-rich-quick" greed. In saying this I have in mind particularly the first of the two ventures which I have to consider here, where a woman involved in managing the affair left, apparently for overseas, with $1.6m contributed by the hapless investors. No doubt this is an extreme example, and no doubt one does not hear so much of the ventures that are successful, such as a mango growing scheme in which H was also involved, but the concern remains.

3. H subscribed to several journals and newsletters, and he considered entry into about seven ventures, including futures trading, avocados, pine trees, pecan nuts and so on. Eventually he and his wife invested in four ventures that are relevant in the present cases. I shall call them "M Growers Pty Ltd"; "G Pty Ltd"; "Turf Farm Syndicate"; and "Lychee Growers". The first two ventures have been the subject of a settlement between the parties since the hearing, and I shall discuss them no further. That settlement also dealt with some expenses claimed to be deductible in respect of the other two ventures with which I have to deal. Some are conceded by the applicants to be non-deductible, others are conceded by the Commissioner to be deductible if Turf Farm Syndicate or Lychee Growers respectively are found by the Tribunal to have commenced to carry on a business. The agreement is contained in a document attached to a letter from the Australian Government Solicitor which was attached to the respondent's primary written submission, all of which is now marked Ex.8. Some items in dispute will, as a result of the agreement, have to be determined in the applicants' favour in any event, and therefore at the end of the day the objection decisions under review will be set aside, and to the extent that particular items are dealt with in the agreement the matters will have to be remitted to the Commissioner for reconsideration in accordance with the direction that the agreement contained in Ex.8 be given effect in accordance with the Tribunal's findings in relation to whether the businesses in question ever commenced to be carried on and if so from when.

  1. The cases unfortunately had to be heard over two days more widely separated from each other than is desirable. Because of restrictions placed on availability of transcript, I invited written submissions from the parties. They were prepared with great care and I thank the parties for them. I have not found the matter easy to resolve and without transcript or such submissions there would have been greater difficulty still. In the event I shall attempt to deal with the matters in dispute as briefly as possible and, as much as I can, in terms of principle rather than by referring to precise figures. The agreement made makes this possible.
  2. I now turn to a summary of the position in relation to the Turf Farm Syndicate.

6. The essential facts of this transaction were as follows:

In 1982 land was acquired by TF Pty Ltd

. which leased the land to TF Leasing Pty Ltd for a term of 10 years and 2 days commencing 7 May 1982.

. TF Leasing Pty Ltd sub-leased the land for a term of 10 years and 1 day to each of 13 syndicates.

. H and W were members of one syndicate.

. Each syndicate further sub-leased the land to TF Management Pty Ltd for a term of 10 years.

. A partnership agreement was entered into between TF Leasing Pty Ltd and TF Management Pty Ltd.

. The effect of the documents as tendered in evidence was that there would be

- payment by each syndicate to TF Leasing Pty Ltd of rental fees

- payment by each syndicate to TF Management Pty Ltd of a management fee

- followed by a partnership between TF Leasing and TF Management Pty Ltd growing turf on the land

- which would be harvested for sale

- with a proportion of the net income from sales being remitted to the syndicates.

7. Not a little went wrong with the plan:

. A leading firm of accountants, as trustee of the funds contributed by the syndicates, paid the whole amount received ($1.75m) direct to TF Management, less $89,320 which went as commission to a newsletter group which had initiated' the scheme as commission, and less $60,103 apparently largely unaccounted for. Some tracing of the expenditure of the $60,103 shows at best, small amounts paid to an earthmoving contractor and to a surveyor as being relevant to the business.

. $1.6m was transmitted overseas, by the woman associated with TF Management Pty Ltd, with an ease, unquestioned by the financial institutions concerned, that is breath-taking.

. Subject to some information that H received that the woman had reached Zurich, she has never been heard of again. It goes without saying that the $1.6m has not been heard of again either.

8. The scheme went as wrong on the ground, literally, as it did in the hands of the financial manipulators. The land in question consisted of around 88ha, but it was a very old subdivision and was not in one parcel. An investigator from the Australian Taxation Office who went there in March 1983, just after the woman's disappearance had been established, had difficulty in establishing precisely where each portion of the land was. H, who had visited the land briefly in March 1982; briefly in July 1982 (although he later suggested that the July visit was more extensive); and in December 1982, said that an area of some 12ha had been planted with turf, or at least something between 8 and 20ha. He thought that there had been scraping or. bulldozing and clearing of shrubs which had been placed in windrows for burning. This was in the July visit. He said that in December there was slightly more under cultivation. During these visits he claimed to have seen boom irrigation systems operating. and other indications of cultivation activity. He was told at some stage that turf had not been cut because of excessive weed growth. The project appears to have been a total failure and to have generated no income. I believe that H attempted to give a truthful picture of what went on on the land, but I very much doubt that what he saw was necessarily all going on on the subject land, and the evidence of how much turf was grown was very imprecise. This however hardly matters, since for the purposes of my conclusion I may accept his evidence as set out above, for, as will appear, I conclude that what H claimed to have seen on the land was no more than activities that were preliminary or preparatory to the carrying on of a business and not the carrying on itself of a business.

  1. The contention for the applicants in relation to the amounts claimed to be deductible is that the payments made by the syndicators were payments made to TF Leasing Pty Ltd for the lease of the land as payments of rent solely for the purpose of the' affairs of the turf farm business being conducted. The syndicators did not conduct a turf farming business but attempted to produce assessable income by sub-leasing the land to an entity that did. In effect, the syndicators were distanced from the carrying on of the business of turf farming and unaffected by the fact, should it be so found, that such a business had not commenced, a conclusion not avoided by the fact that the rent payable under the sub-lease was based on the net profits of the sub-lessor. Section 51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITAA") applied, it was argued, to confer a deduction in respect of the expenses of the syndicators so long as there remained in existence prospects of receiving rents from the turf farming.
  2. In my opinion however the situation is rather that, as contended for by Mr. McGovern of counsel for the Commissioner in his written submission, the moneys paid by the applicants as syndicate members remained capital of the syndicate until applied for the stated purposes of payment of rent and/or of management fees, a conclusion reinforced rather than detracted from by virtue of the misappropriation which occurred before the moneys were earmarked for, or used in, the business of turf farming. Reference was made in support of this submission to Guinea Airways Ltd. v FC of T [1950] HCA 60; (1949) 83 CLR 584 at 590.
  3. If that conclusion is not correct, I agree with the alternative submission put by Mr. McGovern that there was a clear division between the contemplated farming activities proposed to be carried on by the partnership existing between TF Leasing Pty Ltd and TF Management Pty Ltd on the one hand, and the activities of the syndicators on the other. The latter's activities amounted to no more than the investment of moneys in contemplation of an anticipated accretion to capital represented by a hoped for distribution of profits derived from the venture carried on by TF Leasing Pty Ltd and-TF Management Pty Ltd.
  4. There is of course yet another compelling reason for concluding, as I do, that the outgoings incurred by the syndicators were of a capital nature. The misappropriation that occurred had the consequence that the moneys in question were intercepted and applied by the wrongdoer for her own purposes and were never applied for the intended purpose of rent and management fees. Such a fraudulent conversion is not to be regarded as an outgoing of a business so as to be deductible on that account: See Commissioner of Taxation (New South Wales) v Ash [1938] HCA 68; (1939) 61 CLR 263 at 273 per Latham CJ and at 281 per Dixon J.
  5. These considerations are enough to dispose of the main claims in relation to the turf farm venture. For completeness however I should express a view as to whether, on the footing that deductibility of the amount paid by a syndicator is dependent upon whether a business of turf farming was being carried on, that business had commenced to be so carried on. The findings of fact already made are fatal to a conclusion that the business had commenced. The venture had, even if H's account were accepted in full, proceeded no further than the conduct of preliminary or preparatory activities. Such activity as there was occurred over a very short period from about mid 1982, and had ceased by late 1982 or very early 1983. The distinction between preparing to carry on a business and actually commencing to carry on that business is clearly established in such cases as Southern Estates Pty Ltd v FC of T [1967] HCA 16; (1966) 117 CLR 481 and Softwood Pulp and Paper Ltd v FC of T 76 ATC 4439, before the Board of Review reported as Case E29 73 ATC 241. Applying the principles there set out, I conclude that a business had not here been commenced to be carried on, and that any expenditure incurred was not so incurred in carrying on a business but was a loss or outgoing of a capital nature.

The Lychee Project

14. The second venture remaining in question related to a project for growing lychees. Unlike the turf farm this venture is still on its feet. The structure of the operation involved

. the creation of the "Lychee Trust", the trustee being "EF Pty Ltd"

. the purchase of land by the trustee

. the creation of a unit trust, "EF Unit Trust", with "NEF Pty Ltd" as trustee

. a limited partnership formed in acceptance with the laws of the state of Queensland comprised of a "general partner" namely "Lychee Growers Pty Ltd" and "special partners" being the investors

. the leasing by the partnership of land from EF Pty Ltd as trustee of the Lychee Trust for a period of 12 years

. the employment by the partnership of NEF Pty Ltd as manager to prepare the land for the growing of lychees and for that purpose to grow trees and harvest the fruit accordingly

. the payment of a lease payment to EF Pty Ltd prepaid 12 years in advance, that payment together with a fee for management expenses having been paid and claimed as deductions in fiscal 1982, and further management expenses having been paid and claimed in fiscal 1983

. the execution of a Deed of Variation whereby the terms of the Trust were varied so as to ensure that 1600 discretionary units would at the end of the 12 years be issued to the special partners in proportion to their contributions of capital. While the arrangements mentioned above, and the associated documentation, took place, or bore dates, between February and June 1982, all documentation appears to have been in H's hands when he entered into the venture so that it was at least theoretically open to him to understand that at the end of the 12 year lease term in June 1984 he would, by virtue of the units held or to be held by him, ultimately have some kind of interest in the land.

15. There is more detail in the arrangements that were made, but it is enough to say that H may reasonably be considered to have had an expectation of

. incurring substantial tax losses in the earlier years and thus having the advantage of a substantial "up-front" tax deduction

. seeing income from the sale of lychees in later years

. retaining an interest in the property after the expiration of the lease.

  1. In June 1982 some $205,000 was paid by the special partners as an overall prepayment of rent. Substantial management fees were required to be paid in 1982 and thereafter. The manager was controlled and directed by the partnership.
  2. Initial expenditure was incurred and "markots" (trees produced elsewhere by a process like aerial layering) were purchased and planted. Unfortunately, while an apparently correct decision had been made to select a climate that was cool but without frosts, there were freak low temperatures, with frosts not known for 50 years, in two successive years. The first markots planted in 1983 were substantially destroyed and in 1983-84 there was severe damage or growth retardation. Trees that have been lost appear to have been subsequently replaced by other kinds of fruit trees. None of the markots appear to have reached the fruit bearing stage on a commercial basis, although H said that by the end of 1987 they were growing "quite nicely".
  3. The case put for H was essentially that

. the management agreement entered into between the partnership and NEF Pty Ltd provided for a comprehensive range of activities associated with the lychee growing operation

. the partnership was definitely committed, at the stage when the management agreement was entered into, to the conduct of the planned operation well beyond any preliminary stage

. the business of the partnership commenced when the management agreement was signed

- alternatively the activities carried out by NEF Pty Ltd on behalf of the partnership demonstrated a sufficient commercial flavour to themselves constitute the carrying on of a business: See Ferguson v FC of T 79 ATC 4261

. a business had commenced at the time when the rent was prepaid.

19. Unlike the case of the turf farm, the lychee project has not totally foundered and may at some time produce a profit. Up to some time in fiscal 1983 however it had once again to be regarded as an example of an activity that did not yet represent the carrying on of a business but rather its preparatory or "working-up" stages. It is enough to say however that at the very earliest no business had commenced prior to the planting of the first markots in May 1983. Doing the best that I can with the facts, I consider that a business did commence to be carried on from that month, but the exercise of the right of prepayment of rent occurred in June 1982, just before the end of fiscal 1982, and H claimed to deduct the amount paid by him in his return for that year. It follows in my opinion that the prepayment of rent is not deductible having been made at a point prior to the commencement of any income-earning activities or the commencement of any business.

20. That is enough to decide the case in relation to the prepayment of rent, but it was also contended by the Commissioner

(i) that the prepayment expenditure was incurred not for income earning purposes but rather for the purposes of obtaining some hoped-for tax advantages as part and parcel of an overall tax minimisation scheme

(ii) that section 82KJ of the ITAA operated to deny deductibility.

  1. As to argument (i), reliance was placed on a number of authorities decided up to and including John v FC of T 87 ATC 4713 and Just Jeans Pty Ltd v FC of T 87 ATC 4373. With respect, if John's Case was correctly decided, I would uphold the Commissioner's submission on this point. Special leave to appeal having been granted to appeal to the High Court in that case, I content myself with finding that the outgoing representing the prepayment of rent in this case was, on the evidence placed before me, entirely divorced from the principles applicable to the ordinary conduct of a business. The prepayment made far exceeded the amount that would have been prepaid on a sensible commercial basis. The transaction was artificial, and whatever H had in mind it represented, in objective terms, a payment explicable only as one of the planks of a tax minimisation scheme. Curiously, H was at pains to deny that he had realised that the holding of discretionary units at the end of 12 years would confer upon him an enduring advantage. He no doubt had other things in mind, but that denial leaves his exercise of the option to prepay 12 years rent as barely supportable save for reasons connected with tax minimisation.
  2. This conclusion has implications also for the Commissioner's submissions in relation to argument (ii), based upon S.82KH. It is however in my opinion unnecessary to consider that argument in view of my other findings and conclusions and as this aspect of the matter was not fully argued on both sides I prefer not to embark upon it.
  3. As to the management fees paid in fiscal 1983, I was left in doubt as to precisely when these were paid. I have concluded that a business was being conducted as from May 1983 and accordingly management fees paid after the end of that month are in my opinion deductible. A difficulty is that I was informed at the outset of the case that the Commissioner had been prepared to accept that a business was being conducted from March 1983. I was not informed of the basis of such conclusion. I shall remit the matter for reconsideration in accordance with the direction that a business was being conducted from 1 May 1983.

24. Effectively therefore the claims of each applicant substantially fail, but the formal decision of the Tribunal will provide, after formal setting aside of the objection decisions, for remission for reconsideration in order to give effect to the partial settlement referred to in paragraph 3 above.
