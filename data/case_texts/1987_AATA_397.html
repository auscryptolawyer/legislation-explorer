Last Updated: 29 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

RECOUPMENT TAX - vendor and former shareholder - unauthorised execution of Agreement for Sale and Share Transfer - no "sale".

RECOUPMENT TAX - vendor and former shareholder - share allotted to infant daughter at instance of her father non est. factum - undue influence - infancy - whether " effectively registered as shareholder - whether "bare trustee" for father.

RECOUPMENT TAX - vendor-shareholder - daughter as shareholder and father as Governing Director - Governing Director with power to transfer shares - Agreement for Sale and Transfer of Shares executed by appointee of Governing Director - no "sale" by daughter.

RECOUPMENT TAX - daughter as vendor-shareholder - father as Governing Director with power to transfer shares - Agreement for Sale and Transfer of Shares executed by appointee of Governing Director - discretion to relieve daughter from liability.

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 - SS3, 5, 6;

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s264(1)(a);

Taxation (Unpaid Tax-Vendors) Act 1982 - ss 3, 4, 5.

Petelin v. Cullen [1975] HCA 24; (1974-5) 132 CLR 355;

REASONS FOR DECISION

5 November 1987 Mr Peter M Roach (Senior Member)

THE APPLICATIONS

1. In these proceedings a married woman (D) has been assessed to pay Vendor Recoupment Tax by reason of circumstances arising out of the affairs of a company which I shall refer to as "XYZ-CO". The Commissioner's contention is that she is liable to pay $127,880-69 in relation to unpaid company tax for the year of income ended 30 June 1980 and amounts of $226-06 and $22,517-99 in relation to undistributed profits tax for the years of income ended 30 June 1979 and 1980. She disputes the assessments. She claimed in her evidence that her only awareness of any possible connection with such a company as XYZ-CO comes from a realisation that her father (F) had been an active investor; that his initials were "XYZ"; and that in about 1969, when she was a student aged about 20 years - and in the eyes of the law, an infant - and residing in the home of her parents, her father had asked her to sign a document relating to a company he was establishing. She recalled signing a document without knowing what it was and she did so because she then (as she does now) trusted her father. She did not then know, and does not now know, what the document was. When she first received notice from the Commissioner of his claim or pending claim, she immediately contacted her father by telephone. He told her that the matter was no concern of hers and that he would accept all responsibility. Having regard to the assessments in issue (which are in evidence) and the conditions precedent to their issue specified in the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("the T(UCT)A Act"), I think it reasonable to assume that what first brought the matter to her notice was the notice given to her of assessments against XYZ-CO and that that notice was accompanied by a letter from the Commissioner advising her that, as she was a former shareholder in XYZ-CO, it was the Commissioner's contention that, in due course, he would be entitled to issue Vendor Recoupment Tax assessments against her.

  1. A second application was heard jointly. That was the application of M, the mother of D. She too gave evidence. The assessments against her arose out of similar circumstances and the amounts claimed were $2,557-78, $4-51 and $450-39 respectively.
  2. A third application, also arising out of the same circumstances, was also listed for joint hearing. That was the application of S, the son of F and M, and sister to D. As he has resided out of Australia for some years, by consent, the hearing of that matter was adjourned sine die.

THE BACKGROUND

4. At the hearing, both parents (F and M) and their daughter (D) gave evidence. I accept the evidence of both mother and daughter and, to the extent to which his evidence is consistent with that of mother and daughter, I also accept the evidence of F. F is a specialist medical practitioner who married M in 1946. There were two children of the marriage; D born in 1949 and S born in 1952. In 1969, when D was aged 20 years, she was and a tertiary student. At that time the family resided together in the family home in suburban Sydney. During that year, F decided to cause a company to be incorporated. It was to provide a means whereby he could invest in the stock market. His proposal was that he would have a company over which he could have complete control during his lifetime. To enable that objective to be achieved, his solicitors prepared a Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association for a company which, upon registration, was to become XYZ-CO. The Articles of Association provided for shares of "A", "B", "C" and "D" classes. The Articles further provided

(a) that "the holders of each group of shares shall be entitled only to such rights as to dividends as the directors may in their absolute discretion from time to time determine" (Clause 6);

(b) that if upon his death, F should be holder of any "A" class shares, then such shares would not be entitled thereafter to participate in the profits and assets of the company other than the right to receive a fixed cumulative preferential dividend at the rate of 5% per annum on the capital for the time paid-up thereon to be calculated from the date of allotment; and the right to take in a winding-up 'both as regards capital paid-up thereon and dividends' (up to the commencement of the winding-up whether declared or not) in priority to the other shares (Clause 7);

(c) that similar restrictions would apply to any "B" class shares held by M in the event of her death (Clause 7);

(d) for certain powers to effect transfers of shares and for limitations on the right to transfer shares (Clause 15 - cf. post); and

(e) for F to be Governing Director so long as he held at least one "A" class share [Clause 39(a)] with all the powers vested in the Board of Directors [Clause 39(c)].

5. The powers and restrictions referred to in pa. (d) above are set forth in Clause 15 which, so far as is material, read as follows:-

“(i) No transfer of shares may be registered unless......

(ii) Notwithstanding anything herein contained no share (with the exception of the aforesaid 'A' and 'B' class shares) shall be transferred or transmitted to any person company or corporation without the written consent of the original allottee of such 'A' class share if he be then living and the then holder thereof and if such original allottee be not the then holder of such 'A' class share or if he be not then living then no share shall be so transferred without the consent of the original allottee of such 'B' class share if he be then living and the then holder thereof.

(iii) A member being the holder of a share or any class of shares other than the aforesaid 'A' and 'B' class shares shall as one of the terms precedent to issue allotment transfer or transmission of such share appoint (and upon such issue allotment transfer or transmission such member shall be deemed to and doth hereby appoint) the Governing Director for the time being his Attorney for the purpose of transfer of such share and the Governing Director for the time being shall in his absolute discretion be and is hereby empowered to transfer such share to any person company or corporation whom he may in his absolute discretion nominate and appoint. Such member shall be bound by such transfer as though he had duly executed such transfer personally PROVIDED ALWAYS that the Governing Director shall not transfer any such share for a lesser sum than is certified as a fair value of such share by the Auditor of the Company or a member of the Institute of Chartered Accountants so nominated to determine the fair value of such share which shall be final conclusive and binding on all members and in giving such certificate the Auditor or member aforesaid shall act as an expert and not as an arbitrator and accordingly the provisions of the Arbitration Act for the time being in force in the State of New South Wales shall not apply and provided further that the Governing Director shall give notice in writing to the holder of such shares transferred not later than seven days after the execution of such transfer. Notwithstanding anything herein contained the Directors shall not refuse to register a transfer of shares made by the Governing Directors (sic) in pursuance of this Article.

(iv) The receipt of the Governing Director or his appointee for the purchase money shall be sufficient discharge for any purchaser paying same.

(v) No purchaser transferee or other person shall be entitled to enquire whether or not the powers of the Governing Director have been properly exercised.

(vi) Upon presentation of the transfer of shares duly accepted by the transferee it shall be registered by the Directors without further ado.

(vii) Upon registration of the transfer of shares the purchase money (less all expenses incurred by the Company or the Governing Director) shall he paid to the transferror member."

  1. The proposal of F was that shares would initially be allotted to his wife and himself and to their two children upon the applications of each of them. To that end, he requested his wife to complete an application for an allotment of shares and also to subscribe to the Memorandum and Articles of Association. I accept that she did so. Further, I am satisfied that, in doing so, she placed complete trust in F and relied completely on his judgment that the establishment of the company was in the best interests of the family. I am also satisfied that, vague though her understanding was of the nature of a shareholder's interest in a company, and incomplete though her understanding was as to the nature of the rights and responsibilities attaching to such membership, it was her intention to become a member of the company and to accept all rights and responsibilities attending such membership. I also find that, in so far as thereafter she ever had occasion to consider whether or not she was a member of the company, she believed that she was.
  2. I also find that, on or about the date upon which M signed the aforementioned documents, D was asked by her father to sign some papers. Her evidence was that, although she never discussed with her father his or family financial affairs, she did recall that in about 1969 her father had said that "he was forming a family company of which I was to be a member"; and that he had asked her to sign some papers which had "something to do with a family company". I find that what she in fact signed was an application for allotment of shares in XYZ-CO and that she understood that she was to become a member of such a company although she had no clear understanding of what it meant to be the holder of shares in a company. I find that she knew that her mother was to be a member of the company although she did not know at that time whether her mother had signed any documents. I also find that she thought her brother was to become a member of the company. She knew that her father was buying and selling shares although he never discussed his activities in that regard with her. I find that, at the time she was called on to sign, she placed complete trust in her father and that she has since maintained a close and trusting relationship with him.

8. In due course, application was made to the Registrar of Companies for the incorporation by registration of XYZ-CO and that application was granted on 15 April 1969. I find that neither woman advanced any monies to pay for the shares to be allotted. I find that F paid those allotment monies on behalf of his wife and daughter. The company having been incorporated, shares were allotted and scrip written but no scrip was ever delivered to either lady. The scrip remained in the possession of XYZ-CO and under the control of F. I also find that, although the company allotted (or purported to allot), 10 "A" class shares to F, 1 "B" class share to M, 50 "C" class shares to S and 50 "D" class shares to D, that no notice was given to either lady of those allotments.

9. In the years following, neither lady was in any way involved in the operations of the company. Further, neither lady was kept informed in any way whatsoever as to the activities of the company. I find that they received no notices of meeting; no Minutes of any company meeting; and that they received no report from F as to the activities of the company. He exercised autocratic control and acknowledged no liability or accountability to either M or D as shareholders.

THE TRADING OF XYZ-CO

  1. Although the ladies were not aware of it, in fact XYZ-CO was dealing in shares and trading in futures. However, at least to 30 June 1979, there had been no overall success. Despite a profit for that fiscal year of $18,579, there were accumulated losses which were reduced by that profit to $40,894. However, in the same period there had been substantial changes in the domestic circumstances of the family. In 1971 D had left the parental home on the occasion of her marriage and later S, having completed his professional studies, left home. For the most part he has since lived overseas. Then in 1979 F separated from his wife of 33 years. Since that time, they have lived apart. M continued to live in the family home until about 1984. Although separated, F and M have not been divorced and F has continued to ensure adequate financial provision for M: such that she has made no complaint as to the adequacy of that provision.
  2. In the early part of 1980, a dramatic change occurred in the circumstances of XYZ-CO. It made very large profits. F had a discussion with an acquaintance in which he stated: "I said that it appeared that I (sic) would be liable for a lot of tax" and the acquaintance "suggested that this could legally be avoided". The acquaintance introduced F to a firm of accountants (Beta) who - according to his evidence - "assured me that this could legally be done and I asked them to institute this so that this company of mine could legally be sold and not be liable to tax".

THE DISPOSAL OF XYZ-CQ

12. Having received those assurances and F having decided that XYZ-CO should be sold, a flurry of activity followed. What occurred was in large measure represented by the paper-work generated in the course of that activity. It was explained in part by the evidence of F. I find that it did not at any stage involve either M or D. I further find that neither M nor D knew anything of what was being proposed at any stage or of what had been carried out. The trail of documents is incomplete but, in so far as it was evidenced, it establishes the following:-

  1. A new company "Omega" was incorporated and came under the control of F. The evidence did not establish who held the capital in Omega or how it was structured;
  2. Minutes were prepared of a Meeting of Directors of XYZ-CO held at 9.00 p.m. on 31 May 1980 at the residence of F recording that -

"IT WAS RESOLVED to transfer the business of (XYZ-CO) to (Omega). IT WAS FURTHER RESOLVED to sell the following assets and transfer the following liabilities to (Omega) at the value detailed hereunder ........"

The directors present were recorded as being F and M: the only persons recorded as directors of the company. I am satisfied that no such meeting ever took place and that M knew nothing of it. The Minute was signed by F on 5 June 1980;

  1. Minutes of a Directors' Meeting of XYZ-CO attended by F and M were produced (signed by F) purporting to record that, at a meeting on 4 June 1980, it was resolved that an Extraordinary General Meeting be held that day to consider a resolution to accept the resignation of the then auditors and to determine that no further auditors be appointed. The meeting purported to be held at the office of the original and continuing accountants to F (Alpha).

I am satisfied that there was no such meeting and that M knew nothing of the supposed business of the meeting;

  1. By memorandum of 4 June 1980, purporting to be signed by or on behalf of each of F, M, S and D, it was recorded that all shareholders in XYZ-CO consented to the calling of an Extraordinary General Meeting of members of the company to be held on 4 June 1980. The consent did not identify the business of the proposed meeting. I am satisfied that the consent was signed by F for himself in his own right. I am also satisfied that he purported to exercise a power he held as Attorney for S and D and signed in his own name in purported exercise of that power. I am satisfied that the signature purporting to be that of M was not the signature of M; and that it was represented on the form by the hand of her husband seeking to "emulate" the signature of M but without any authority to do so and without her knowledge;
  2. Minutes of a further meeting of XYZ-CO, purporting to have been held at the offices of Alpha on 4 June 1980, were also evidenced. They represented that the meeting was attended by both F and M. I find that they were signed by F as a true record but that M did not attend and had no knowledge of any such meeting and did not consent to the passage of the business recorded by the minutes. I find that no such meeting as the Minutes purport to record was held. The Minutes record the opening of a Branch Register of members in Darwin and that it was resolved that share transmittal requests by all members of the company be approved and that the shares be entered on the Darwin Register. No such transmittal requests were produced in evidence. I find that even if such documents were in existence

purporting to be applications by M and D, neither lady had any knowledge of such documents or of the proposal referred to in them and that neither lady made or consented to any such request;

  1. Photocopies of documents purporting to be original instruments of Powers of Attorney dated 5 June 1980 were produced whereby M, S and D respectively purported to appoint persons in the Northern Territory as their Attorneys, with power:-

"(1) to execute the Agreement for Sale annexed hereto and marked with the letter "A"; and

(2) generally to make, do, sign, sell, deliver and execute all acts, matters, deeds and things in relation to the shares mentioned in the Agreement for Sale as fully and effectually to all intents and purposes whatsoever as I myself could do for the purposes abovementioned;

(3) I, for myself, my successors and assigns, agree to ratify and confirm whatever my said Attorney shall do or cause to be done by virtue of these presents including whatever shall be done between the time of verification of this Power of Attorney and the time such verification becomes known to my said Attorney."

Although in no instance was the Agreement for Sale referred to as Annexure "A" produced in evidence as an annexure, I find that the reference to an annexure refers to an Agreement for Sale in terms to be referred to hereafter.

As to execution of the Power by M: I find that M did not either sign or sign, seal and deliver the Deed; did not know of the document; and did not consent to the appointment purporting to be effected by the Deed. So far as the execution of the Deed by D was concerned, I find that it was, and purported to be, executed by her father claiming to act as her Attorney; and that she did not sign or sign, seal and deliver the Deed; and that she did not know of the Deed or consent to the appointment it proposed to effect;

G. File copies of forms of Transfer of Shares were produced for the shares registered in the names of M, S and D. The copies do not purport to reproduce the detail of the execution of those instruments. In relation to the purported transfer of the shares of M, her initials appear by hand. I am satisfied that she did not place them there or know of them being placed there or consent to them being placed there.

Further, I am satisfied, upon her evidence, that she did not sign or authorise or consent to any person signing any original Transfer of Shares on her behalf. In so far as the copy Transfer purports to be a copy of the Transfer of the shares of D, the copy bears the initials of F purporting to sign as Attorney for D. I am satisfied that the handwriting which appears on the copy is that of F. I also find that the transfer of shares delivered up to the purchasers was more probably than not a document executed in the Northern Territory by someone other than F purporting to act as attorney for D.

I am satisfied that no original Transfer of Shares was executed by D or by any person acting with her authority or consent, unless such power of appointment was conferred on F by the Articles of Association.

H. The next document produced was a copy of an Agreement - the Agreement for Sale previously referred to. The document produced is a photocopy of another document. That other was not, and by its form did not purport to be, the original. Each page of the document copied bears a reproduction of the initials of F and M. The execution Clauses of the document as drafted were in the form:-

"Signed, sealed and delivered by

as Attorney for (named vendor) in the presence of". That it should have been so was to be expected, having regard to the terms of the Powers of Attorney previously referred to.

I find that the initials purporting to be those of M were not placed there by her or by any other person with her knowledge or with her authority and consent. Further, I find that the representations of the initials of both F and M were placed on the document by F. Having regard to the evidence of all surrounding circumstances, I am persuaded that the document produced is one which was in the possession of F in Sydney on 6 June 1980 and that it is not a copy of any agreement made between the vendors and the purchasers which was to be the contractual foundation for the transfer of shares.

More particularly, I am satisfied that no agreement for sale taking effect, or purporting to take effect, between the vendors of shares in XYZ-CO and the purchasers of shares in that company was executed by M or by D, or by any person with the knowledge, authority and consent of either M or D, except in so far as the execution of such an agreement for sale on behalf of D might have been lawfully effected pursuant to a power conferred pursuant to the Articles of Association of XYZ-CO.

13. I shall consider the terms of the Agreement for Sale more fully at a later point in these Reasons. For the moment it suffices to say that it purported to be an agreement entered into on 6 June 1980 providing for completion on that day on the basis of accounts prepared to that day. It provided for the sale of all 111 shares in the company for a sum of $493,250 apportioned between the shareholders in proportion to the number of shares held by each of them. The net assets of the company were warranted to have a value of $530,373-65. At completion, the vendors were to deliver to the purchasers a bank cheque for an amount equal to the net assets of XYZ-CO, naming XYZ-CO as payee of that bank cheque.

THE SETTLEMENT

14. I find that on 6 June 1980 the purchasers received a bank cheque in favour of XYZ-CO for the required sum and that, on that day, there were delivered into the hands of F bank cheques provided by the purchasers in favour of the payees named below:-

For F $44,436

For M $4,444

For S $222,185

For D $222,185

$493,250

  1. I further find that on 6 June 1980 those cheques were deposited to the credit of Omega and that the cheques were honoured on presentation. I find that F made such endorsements as might have been thought to be necessary to enable the cheques to be so credited to Omega. I further find that neither M nor D had conferred authority on F to deal with those cheques in any such way, or in any way. Further, I find that M and D had no knowledge of the existence of the cheques or of any proposals as to the manner in which they would be dealt with and that they did not consent to the cheques being dealt with.
  2. I find that documents recording the resignations of F and M as directors were executed on 6 June 1980. I find that the document effecting the resignation of F was signed by F. I find that the purported resignation of M was signed not by M but by F, and that it was so signed without her knowledge or consent.
  3. I further find that on or about 6 June 1980 F signed typed documents purporting to give notice pursuant to Article 15(iii) of the Articles of Association of XYZ-CO. One such notice was directed to D, care of the then residence of F. By its terms, it stated

"I (F) do hereby give notice that the 50 'D' class shares that you held in (XYZ-CO) were transferred on 6 May (sic) 1980 and that I acted as Attorney for you in this transfer".

Despite the fact that the copy produced in evidence was signed by F and bears an endorsement indicating that it was sent on 6 June 1980, I find that it was not received by D. I further find that the fact of the sale or purported sale was not communicated to D.

18. I also find that the original scrip-certificates for the shares in XYZ-CO continued to be held, as they always had been held, by Alpha as the accountants to F.

THE T(UCT)A ASSESSMENTS

19. The next development in the history of this matter did not occur until July 1983: over three years from the date of the alleged sale, but little more than six months from the passage of the T (UCT) A Act. By notice dated 28 July 1983 directed to F, the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation required F, pursuant to s.264(1)(a) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Tax Act"), to provide information "in relation to (XYZ-CO) and the sale of your shares in that company during the year ended 30 June 1980". F conferred with the accountants (Beta) who had arranged the "sale" and, on 16 August 1983, they replied on his behalf. I find that neither M nor D had any knowledge of the Commissioner's enquiry or of the response of F. I further find that F had no authority from either lady to speak on her behalf. Nonetheless, for completeness, I summarize what flowed from the enquiries of the Commissioner.

20. F advised that the date upon which his shares had been sold was 6 June 1980; that he, together with M, S and D, had been holders of all the shares in the company immediately prior to the sale of his shares; that questions as to whether any of the holders was "a hare trustee (i.e. merely a nominee)" or otherwise a trustee immediately prior to the sale were "not applicable"; as to the identity of the purchasers; and that net profit for the period from 1 July 1979 to date of sale had been $571,157. As requested, he enclosed copies of the accounts of XYZ-CO; of the agreement for sale; and a copy of the Minutes of the Directors' Meeting of 31 May 1980 relating to the sale to Omega. By the reply, F answered the questions as framed by the Commissioner, but did not volunteer any additional information.

21. By letters of 25 August 1983, the Commissioner took the next step. He addressed letters to the alleged vendors, stating (inter alia):-

"According to records held in this office you were a beneficial owner of shares in (XYZ-CO) prior to the sale of those shares in June 1980. On that basis and in accordance with the provisions of the (TUCTA) Act, enclosed are:-

(a) the abovenamed Company's income tax notice of assessment for the year ended 30 June 1980; and

(b) notices of assessment in respect of undistributed profits tax for the years ended 30 June 1979 and 1980.

2. These notices are being served on you as a preliminary step towards the raising of a recoupment tax assessment."

The letters were in a standard form readily recognizable by those who have occasion to consider such letters. Nothing turns on the terms of the letters. However, it is to be noted that F acknowledges having received his copy of the letter which was addressed to him care of his accountants. M has no recollection of having received the letter addressed to her at the former matrimonial home where she continued to reside but, in that regard, I find her memory to be deficient. I find that she received the letter but, by reason of some arrangement with her husband, left responsibility for dealing with the matters raised in the letter with him.

Although the letter said (inter alia):-

"5. The raising of a recoupment tax assessment will be obviated by payment of the Company's outstanding ordinary and undistributed profits tax liability which amounts to $309,460-22",

it did not purport to give M any idea of the amount for which she might later be held liable. I am satisfied that the matters set forth in the Commissioner's letter in large measure were without eaning for her.

As to D, the letter to her was addressed care of a firm of accountants - not being either Alpha or Beta. I am satisfied that on receiving notice she contacted F as described in pa 2 of these reasons and that she thereafter left the matter to him.

22. Once those letters had issued F consulted his solicitors and caused them to write to the Commissioner on 14 October 1983 stating (inter alia) that they had been "instructed to clarify our client's position with you in relation to the transaction the subject of your present enquiries". By that letter they asserted that the Articles of Association of XYZ-CO were in the usual form of a Governing Director type of company and that, as' Governing Director, F exercised control over the company, its assets and other shareholders. The letter proceeded:-

"We are instructed by F that at no time did any of these other shareholders obtain monetary benefit from their respective holdings in the company and, in particular, did not receive any of the purchase monies that flowed from the sale of their respective shareholdings in the company on 6 June 1980. These monies, including the consideration for the sale of F's shares, were advanced by way of loan to another company associated with F, namely Omega and the books of that company disclose a loan account of $493,250 from F." The letter concluded:-

"F stresses that this is supported by all documentation that all of the purchase monies flowing from the sale of the issued capital in XYZ-CO were appropriated to his personal benefit and that none of the other shareholders received any part of the consideration expressed in the individual Transfer of Shares."

23. On 21 February 1984 the Commissioner issued assessments pursuant to the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 against M, directed to her at the former matrimonial home at which she still resided. The amount claimed as the primary taxable amount for the year ended 30 June 1979 was $4-51, and for the year ended 30 June 1980, two amounts, namely $2,557-78 and $450-39. M entrusted responsibility for dealing with the assessments to her husband and he enlisted the services of his accountants. Objections were duly lodged and, the objections' having been disallowed on 30 November 1984, M requested reference to a Taxation Board of Review for review. The matters were referred to a Taxation Board of Review for review on 20 February 1986 and, following the passage of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986, this Tribunal became responsible for the review.

  1. On 21 February 1984 the Commissioner also issued assessments against D. The primary taxable amount claimed for the year ended 30 June 1979 was $226-06 and for the following year $127,880-69 and $22,517-99: in all, $150,624-74. Those notices were addressed to her care of the same firm of accountants as the Commissioner's previous communication. Her evidence was that she received those notices of assessment and that she immediately telephoned her father who said that he would accept full responsibility for the assessments and that his solicitors would be handling it. Later she too was called on to sign notices of objection and, in due course, requests for reference to a Board of Review. Determination of those requests for reference also came to be the responsibility of this Tribunal.
  2. D gave evidence that it was only when she received those notices of assessment that she recalled the events of 1969. She said she recognized the company name because she recognized the initials in the name of the company as those of her father. As I am not persuaded that she did not receive the letter of October 1983, I do not accept that her memory is accurate in that regard. However, I do accept her evidence that she had no knowledge at' all of the affairs of XYZ-CO and had not sought information as to the affairs of XYZ-CO between 1969 when she, as a 20-year old student living at home, signed a document at the request of her father whom she trusted; and receipt of notice of the demands or foreshadowed demands of the Commissioner in the period 1983-1984.

THE OBJECTIONS

26. The objections of mother and daughter were in similar terms, distinguished only by reference to the number and class of shares they held and to family status. The grounds relied upon in the mother's objection were as follows:-

"1. The shares allotted to me in the company (XYZ-CO) were allotted to me at the request of my husband (XYZ). I did not at any stage knowingly request that shares be allotted to me.

  1. The shares allotted to me, namely 1 'B' class share, in the company carried no benefits but were entitled to receive only such rights and as to Dividends as the Directors, that is the Governing Director decides.
  2. During the whole of the time that the said 1 'B' class share was registered in my name I did not knowingly receive any benefits by way of dividends, or otherwise from the company.
  3. Any documents which I may have signed in connection with the allotment, holding or disposal of the said shares, I did so at the direction and insistence of my husband (XYZ) and I was not at any time made aware of the reasons why or effects of, signing any' document relating to the Company.
    1. I did not at any time receive any benefits monetary or otherwise from the disposal of the shares registered in my name in the company.
    2. I honestly believe that the shares registered in my name were held by me nominally on behalf of my husband, who had full and complete control of the company at all times.
    3. I ask that the Commissioner use his discretion in view of the above grounds, and waive the amounts owing on the above numbered assessments under the provisions of Sect 5(4) and 6(18) of the Act."

THE LEGISLATION

26. The Applicants have been assessed by the Commissioner as persons liable to pay tax pursuant to the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax-Vendors) Act 1982. The material provisions of that Act provide as follows:-

"3. The Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 is incorporated and shall be read as one with this Act.

4. Where at any time a vendor's taxable amount exists under the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982, tax is imposed and shall be levied and paid, on that vendor's taxable amount.

5. The amount of the tax imposed by this Act in respect of a vendor's taxable amount is an amount equal to the vendor's taxable amount."

27. In order to determine whether a vendor's taxable amount exists, regard must be had to the provisions of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("the TUCTA Act"). S3 (1) provides that:-

"In this Act, unless the contrary intention appears -

'vendors taxable amount', in relation to a person, means an amount that is a primary taxable amount or a secondary taxable amount in relation to the person."

In the circumstances of this case it is not suggested that there was any secondary taxable amount as defined by s6 of the T(UCT)A Act and, in consequence, it only becomes necessary to consider whether a primary taxable amount exists as defined by s.5 of the T(UCT)A Act. A primary taxable amount may arise by force of sub-section (1) or sub-section (2) of s5 of the T (UCT) A Act:-

"Where sub - section (2) applies in relation to the sale of shares in a company, sub-section (1) does not apply in relation to that sale" [s5(3) ]. As it is not suggested that s5(2) has any application, it is only necessary to consider s5(1).

28. S5(1) provides for a primary taxable amount to exist in two distinct circumstances: first, if all the relevant shares were sold by one person; and, secondly, any other case. These applications arise out of assessments in the latter category and so, in accordance with the provisions of s5(1)(m), assessments have issued against the four originally registered members of the company on the basis that

"a primary taxable amount shall be taken to exist at the relevant time in relation to each person who, as the holder.... of any of the shares referred to in paragraph (a), sold the shares...under the scheme".

It follows that the Commissioner's contention is that the entire share capital was sold under the scheme. In consequence, the assessments have issued on the basis that the "overdue company tax" has been apportioned between those shareholders in proportions in which they have shared "in the amount or value of the consideration paid or given in respect of the sale of the shares or interest by the person".

29. It follows that, if the assessments fail against one or more taxpayers on the basis that there was no sale under a scheme, there will be at least partial inability to recover on the other assessments for overdue company tax. Further, it follows that, if it is held that the shares sold under a scheme were not capable "of controlling more than 90% of the voting power of the company" the assessments against all vendors will fail.

THE LIABILITY OF M

30. I now turn to consider the question as to whether the assessment against M is excessive. M will only be liable, if liable at all, because s5 (1) (m) provides:-

" a primary taxable amount shall be taken to exist at the relevant time in relation to each person who, as the holder of any of the shares referred to in paragraph (a), sold the shares under the scheme..." The reference to paragraph (a) is a reference to

s5(1)(a), which provides:-

"1. Where—

(a) under a scheme entered into, whether in Australia or outside Australia, on or after 1 January 1972 and before 4 December 1980, some or all of the shares in a company were sold;

31. I find that M was not at any time or in any way involved in any "sale" of any shares in XYZ-CO, let alone any sale of such shares "under a scheme". Accordingly, the assessment against M should be set aside. As the shares held by M controlled less than 10% of the voting power in the company, it does not automatically follow that assessments against F, S and D are excessive.

AMENDING THE OBJECTIONS

32. The Commissioner issued the vendor recoupment assessments which have been mentioned against M and D on 21 February 1984. As no objection has been made that the period for objecting against the assessments of the company had not expired or that any such objection had not been finalised, I presume that the requirements of s5(1)(f) were complied with. Objections to the vendor recoupment assessments were lodged in April 1984. They were in terms which have been quoted elsewhere in these reasons. Unlike some other objections which were very thoroughly prepared at about the same period of time, they were brief. It is significant that they were prepared at a time when the T(UCT)A Act had not been considered by any Court under any circumstances. It is also significant that the objections were prepared at a time when objectors were strictly limited by grounds of objection specified within the defined and limited period of sixty days allowed for that purpose.

33. At the hearing, after evidence was presented on behalf of the Applicants, Counsel for the Applicants addressed proposing that relief should be granted in accordance with one or other of two submissions. It was accepted that both were sufficiently raised by the terms of the objection. The first submission was that it should be held that there was no liability because each Applicant was a "bare trustee" within the meaning of s5(5) which provided:-

"Sub-sections (1) and (2) do not apply in relation to the sale of shares or of an interest in shares by a person who was a bare trustee in respect of those shares or that interest but, where, but for this sub-section and sub-section (4), a primary taxable amount (in this sub-section referred to as the "trustee's taxable amount") would exist in relation to the person at a particular time in relation to such a sale--

(a) if there was only one beneficiary in the trust estate—a primary taxable amount equal to the trustee's taxable amount shall be taken to exist at that time in relation to the beneficiary; and

(b) if there were 2 or more beneficiaries in the trust estate—a primary taxable amount shall be taken to exist at that time in relation to each of those beneficiaries and, in relation to each beneficiary, that primary taxable amount is an amount ascertained by multiplying the interest in the trust estate of the beneficiary concerned, expressed as a fraction of the aggregate of the interests in the trust estate of the beneficiaries, by the trustee's taxable amount."

The second argument was that the Tribunal should exercise a discretion conferred by s5 (4) of the T (UCT) A Act, which provides as follows:-

"Where a primary taxable amount exists or existed in relation to a person in relation to an amount of company tax payable by a company and—

(a) the Commissioner, having regard to--

(i) circumstances relating to the sale of the shares or the interest in shares to which the primary taxable amount relates:

(ii) circumstances, whether occurring before or after that sale, that caused or contributed to the failure of the company to pay that company tax; and

(iii) such other circumstances as the Commissioner considers relevant,

considers it unreasonable that the primary taxable amount should be taken to exist or to have existed in relation to the person; or

(b) if the primary taxable amount is less than $100—the Commissioner considers that the primary taxable amount should not be taken to exist or to have existed in relation to the person,

the primary taxable amount shall not be taken to exist or to have existed in relation to the person."

No other issue was sought to be raised. Indeed, in a written outline of submissions, it was submitted:-

"In this case there is no issue between the parties that the provisions of s5(1) of the Act have been satisfied and no issue that either (D) or (M) were at all material times the holders of shares or that there was a sale of those shares held by them on 6 June 1980."

34. In preparing these reasons for decision I became concerned as to a number of matters. One was whether, upon the evidence before me, the provisions of s5(1) were in fact satisfied. Another was whether the discretionary power conferred by the provisions of s5(4) of the T(UCT)A Act could be applied to enable the Tribunal to exercise a discretion to relieve the Applicants of liability for vendor recoupment tax if the Tribunal was satisfied upon the evidence that no such liability existed. I reconvened the hearing and invited submissions from Counsel. In particular, I invited an application by the Applicant to amend the objections to incorporate an additional ground reading

"There is no vendors' taxable amount by reason of there being no primary taxable amount under s5 or any part thereof or any sub-section thereof".

  1. Against the opposition of Counsel for the Commissioner I allowed the amendment and, having allowed it, granted an adjournment to the Commissioner with the right in his Counsel to further cross-examine witnesses who had already been called and to reopen his case generally. I was requested to provide reasons in writing for doing so and indicated that I would provide them. I now do so.
  2. I did so because (inter alia) -

(a) I consider the objective of all procedural rules in litigation to be to enable justice to be done in so far as the parties reasonably conduct themselves and within the limits of the procedural rules enacted by the authority of the Parliament. Procedural matters should promote justice and not, as so frequently occurred in the past, inhibit it;

(b) of the novelty of the proceedings. As I understand it, this is one of the first of the cases to proceed to a hearing on the merits as to liability to vendor recoupment tax; and in any event, the first such case concerning factual circumstances remotely similar to these;

(c) of the circumstance that the objection had had to be drafted in circumstances of extraordinary difficulty having regard to the lack of any judicial consideration of the meaning of the T(UCT)A Act;

(d) of recognition of the circumstance that in these proceedings the Applicants were M and D: not F; and

(e) because I acknowledged that, for similar reasons, I had granted an adjournment to the Commissioner to enable him to more effectively present his case with a view to reaching a sound and just conclusion in the first case to be heard involving promoter's recoupment assessments.
  1. Upon the resumption of the hearing the Commissioner advised that he elected not to cross-examine further. He tendered some further exhibits and both Counsel then argued questions as to the application of s5(1) of the T(UCT)A Act.
  2. Having allowed the amendment, it follows that M's objection may now succeed.
  3. It was argued for D that the requirements of the T(UCT)A Act were not satisfied because she had never been a shareholder and, accordingly, there could have been no sale of shares by her let alone a sale of shares under a scheme. It was put that she had never come to be bound by the Memorandum and Articles of Association because she was, at the time of signing any application for allotment of shares, a minor. It was also put that at all material times, as the infant daughter of her father, there should be found to have been undue influence which would entitle her to equitable relief. It was also put that, in the circumstances, her action in executing any application for allotment of shares was a nullity by reason of the application of the principle non est factum. I reject all three arguments. As to infancy, D was capable of holding an interest in property and of being bound by the Memorandum and Articles of Association. She was capable of benefiting under the contracts arising from the application for allotment of shares and capable of enforcing her rights as a shareholder. In my view, questions of affirmation or repudiation do not arise because, in the particular circumstances of this case, before she had any awareness of the circumstances which might call for a decision as to whether to affirm or repudiate, the shares had been rendered valueless. In my view, upon the evidence before me, at all material times at least up to the date of the alleged sale, D was a shareholder in XYZ-CO.
  4. As to any question of equitable relief in respect of unconscionable conduct on the part of F, it suffices to say that no ground for relief arises out of the allotment of shares despite the dominant influence at that time of her father. Whatever condemnatory terms may be appropriate to characterize the action of F in effecting, or purporting to effect, a "sale" of the shares of D, they are not appropriate to describe his initiatives in making available to his daughter shares with a potential for substantial values. As to the matter of the plea of non est factum I find that, upon the evidence, the tests laid down by the High Court of Australia in Petelin v Cullen [1975] HCA 24; (1974-5) 132 CLR 355 are not satisfied.
  5. As to the contention that D was a "bare trustee" for her father, I reject it. I am satisfied that when the shares were allotted it was the intention of F that D should be beneficial owner of the shares; and that her intention was to accept the provision being made for her by her father. Subsequent events did not alter that, although F may have wished otherwise.
  6. In my view the crucial question for D, as it was for M, is whether, within the meaning of 5(1)(m) of the T(UCT)A Act, she "sold the shares....under the scheme". In addressing that question, I will assume that it is appropriate to characterize as such a "sale" by her, a transfer of shares for a price which was effected by any person invested with sufficient authority to give effect to a transfer in favour of a purchaser. I have already found that no sale was effected personally by D. I have also found that no transfer of the shares in XYZ-CO registered in the name of D was effected as a result of any purported transfer by F. If any transfer of those shares was effective in favour of a purchaser it was a transfer executed by an attorney allegedly appointed by F with a view to empowering that attorney to join with F, M and D in entering into a contract for the transfer of the entire share capital in XYZ-CO and to thereby participate in what the T(UCT)A Act calls a "scheme" (whatever that may mean) and, pursuant to that scheme, to effect a sale of shares and to do so by executing a form of transfer of shares as her attorney.
  7. It is clear that, in the circumstances, F did not have any right or power to so act unless it arose by reason of the power conferred on him by the Articles of Association (cf. pa. 5). The power so conferred was narrowly defined. In pa. 1 it purported to confer power "to execute the Agreement for Sale annexed hereto and marked with the letter A". In pa. 2 it was generally to do such things "as I myself could do for the purposes abovementioned". It did not, by its terms, extend to authority for the persons named as attorneys to do other than that.
  8. I find that, in authorising the attorneys to so execute transfers, F was acting fraudulently and in his own interests with intent to obtain the entire purchase price and to appropriate to himself all monies available from the purchasers and to use those monies to his own ends (cf Kwei Tek Chao v British Traders and Shippers Ltd. 1954 2 QB 459). He may, in his own mind, have been justified in doing so but, if that was his view, it was incorrect. His only authority to act in relation to the shares was that conferred on him by the request. His status as a father conferred no more power on him vis-a-vis his by then adult daughter than his status as a spouse conferred on him vis-a-vis authority to "emulate" the signature of his wife. His action in executing the power of attorney may have conferred on him sufficient authority to entitle others to rely on it and so become registered as transferees of the shares. The authority to effect a transfer of shares pursuant to Article 15 may have been enough to authorise a merely administrative act by his agent and so enable those others relying on that authority to hold D bound to them by the actions of F and his delegates. But such actions did not constitute what F did or what his delegates did as actions authorised by D. I conclude that there was no sale by D within the meaning of s5(1) of the T(UCT)A Act. Nor was there any sale pursuant to "a scheme" for the purposes of that section. Accordingly, as with M, the objecction (as amended) should be allowed.

DISCRETION

45. As I may be incorrect as to that I now proceed to address the question as to whether there should be any exercise of discretion pursuant to s5(4) of the T(UCT)A Act so as to relieve D of liability for vendor recoupment tax which would be hers but for any exercise of discretion in her favour.



  1. In another decision handed down earlier this day I have reviewed at length the history and circumstances which I consider relevant to the exercise of that discretion- I do not repeat what was there said.
  2. However, upon applying the same principles, I take into account the following considerations:-
    • (a) D had no personal knowledge and gave no approval to any action which led to any sale of the shares in XYZ-CO, whether pursuant to a scheme or otherwise;
    • (b) the only basis for holding D to be liable in the circumstances is her action in executing an application for allotment of shares when she was a young, unmarried woman who had not yet attained her majority and who was still living in the home of her trusted parents;
    • (c) that the sale was effected by or pursuant to a fraudulent exercise of power wholly intended to benefit the person purporting to exercise that power; and
    • (d) that she is no more responsible for her father's actions qua father than he was authorised qua father to treat her property as his own.

48. In my view, if there was a sale under a scheme within the meaning of s5(1) of the T(UCT)A Act, nonetheless the "circumstances relating to the sale of the shares" and the other relevant circumstances which have been mentioned are such that, in my view, it is "unreasonable that the primary taxable amount should be taken to exist or to have existed in relation to" D.

49. The order of the Tribunal will be that the determinations of the Commissioner upon the objections of M and D be varied and their objections allowed.
