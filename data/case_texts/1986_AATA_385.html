QT85/1092 and Commissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 385; (1986) 18 ATR 3130; 87 ATC 135 (2 December 1986)

Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - allowable deductions - superannuation fund - claim for management fees - conflicting evidence as to components of sum claimed - whether necessary nexus with the derivation of assessable income established.

Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 – ss 51, 82AAC

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Western Suburbs Cinemas Ltd [1952] HCA 28; (1952) 86 CLR 102.

DECISION AND REASONS

Re:

Applicant And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

No: QT85/1092

AAT Decision No 3096

Date: December 2, 1986

Dr P Gerber (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re

Applicant

And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

The applicant in this case is the trustee of a superannuation fund (SH) which in the 1980 tax year received an amount of $2,417 by way of investment income, being interest on its capital which was, in the main, lent back to the employer. It claimed a like amount by way of deduction as "management fees", paid to the employer "for outgoings met during the financial year on behalf of the superannuation fund. These outgoings included insurances paid along with accounting and management expenses for the maintaining of books and records of the fund, lodgment of income tax returns and calculations of contributions". In the result, after some enquiries from the Tax Office, an amount of $420 was allowed as accounting fees; the balance of the claimed deductions were disallowed. Since the evidence in this case was, to say the least, highly unsatisfactory, I propose to set out the relevant documents in full. Thus, in a letter from SH's accountant to the Tax Office dated August 12, 1981, it is stated:

"With reference to our telephone conversation of the other day and further to my Notice of Objection in relation to the above, I wish to advise as follows.

A management fee of $2417.00 was paid to the [employer]. This management fee has been shown as a deduction against income in the superannuation fund. It is pointed out that this management fee has also been taken up by way of income in the [employer's] income tax return. It is further pointed out that the disallowance of the management fee paid by the superannuation fund, which I contend should not be disallowed, would then result in an equal reduction to the income of the trust.

The management fee is made up as follows:

Accounting Fees $420.00

Insurance $726.00

General Management Fees paid to the Trust 1271.00

$ 2471.00

The insurances paid was for term insurance taken out by the superannuation fund to provide a death and disablement cover on its employees. This insurance has no end benefit and is renewable on an annual basis. It is designed to provide a cover for its employees in the early stages of the fund where there is little cash available or small amounts standing to the credit of the employees.

The General Management fee was charged by the trust to cover the cost of time by management for administration of the fund.

Thanking you.

Yours faithfully,"

After the applicant objected against the disallowance of the balance of its claim, a further requisition issued from the Tax Office. In response, another member of the same firm of accountants replied as follows:

"The management fee of $2,417.00 was made up as follows:-

A proportion of the life insurance premiums incurred by the [employer] 1917.00

Ten hours of accounting charges 0 $50.00/hr 500.00

Total 2417.00

Accounting fees were incurred by the [employer] on behalf of the superannuation fund. In addition the [employer] incurred the cost of life insurance premiums taken out on the lives of the members of the fund. It is contested that this cost was necessarily incurred to protect the members rights. As a consequence, the [employer] raised a management fee on the - superannuation fund to cover some of the costs of these outgoings."

2. To complete the factual narrative, two inconsistent (unsigned) minutes of Board Meetings of the employer - both dated 5 June 1980 - were made exhibits. Each consisted of the same four directors, each resolved to appoint the same Chairman. One minute recorded a resolution to the effect that the employer make contributions in the amount of $31,109 to the SH fund with respect to 13 employees. It went on to record:

"In addition, it was resolved to make a payment to the SH fund of $2,416.56, being for interest accrued on the loan from the superannuation fund.

Further it was resolved that a management fee would be raised to charge the superannuation for numerous services incurred by the employer on behalf of the fund. It was agreed that the management fee of $2,416.56 would be raised.

There being no further business the meeting was adjourned to a date to be faxed."

The other minute recorded that the Chairman "tabled an invoice raised by the [employer] for provision of management services to the fund. It was resolved that instead of paying the [employer] management fee of $2417.00, that the fee would be offset against the outstanding loan balance between the [employer] and the SH fund in the superannuation's account.

There being no further business the meeting was adjourned to a date to be fixed".

The invoice referred to above was not made an exhibit.

3. The oral evidence given by the senior accountant of the of the firm which acted for both the employer and the SH fund and who also wrote the first letter to the Tax Office dated August 12, 1981, did nothing to resolve the irreconcilable differences between the two versions given in response to the enquiry as to how the claimed deduction was made up. At end of the day, I therefore find myself unable to determine on the probabilities how much was expended on "insurance". This deficit in the evidence was sought to be overcome by submitting that even if I ignored the "insurance" component, the amount claimed was a reasonable amount to pay to employer for management of the fund's affairs. The following was elicited in cross-examination:

"Moving down now, to the next component of the amount of the management fee: an amount there of $1271 titled "general management fees paid to the employer". Can you just elaborate on how that figure was calculated? -- The management fees for a superannuation fund are normally calculated out at about 10 per cent of the contributions to the superannuation fund. The employers of this particular superannuation fund had meetings with several insurance companies in relation to the setting up and the operation of a superannuation fund. The companies that they had spoken to had quoted figures in the order of the 10 per cent for management fees. The fee of $1271 would basically just be the balancing charge, or the balancing item, between the other two fees which are distinctly identifiable and -considered to be within the norm for the amount of effort and time that has taken place by the employers who are the directors of the company who - conduct- the business of the superannuation fund in company time, using company assets, etcetera. And I think the basic broad premise was based on what other superannuation funds - other trustees of superannuation funds charge.

This item you quoted of 10 per cent. Is that just an industry norm that you are referring to? Can I relate that 10 per cent to any other figure; or can I suggest to you that the amount of $1271 is, as what you mentioned a moment ago, a "balancing charge", so that the expense claimed for management fee of $2471 is exactly equal to the income derived by the superannuation fund?... I am saying to you that when one looks at the components that go to make up the management fee of $2471, I suggest to you that that amount of $1271 is a balancing entry to ensure that the expenses claimed by the superannuation fund are exactly equal to the income of the superannuation fund? --- I will answer the question on the basis, yes. And then I will further extend the question. If the company had of charged the full ten per cent, it would have created a loss in the superannuation fund; so the company chose to charge no more than what the income of the fund was and not to put the fund into a loss situation.

Can you explain to the tribunal how a charge for a general management fee would have been calculated? That 10 per cent that you mention: it is 10 per cent of what amount? --- Of the contributions to the superannuation fund."

4. On the evidence, I am satisfied that the claim can only succeed to the extent that an identifiable - and allowable expenditure has been demonstrated. An aggregate lump sum, made up of parts, some of which are not allowable deductions, cannot be salvaged on the basis that, claimed as a lump sum under a different head, it would have been allowable. Let me illustrate. If a taxpayer claims $x made up of a + b + c, the claim for $x will fail if it can be shown that any one of the parts that go to make up the total was not an outgoing incurred in the derivation of the assessable income, notwithstanding that the total amount of $x could have been "salvaged" if claimed under a different head of expenditure: FC of T v Western Suburbs Cinemas Limited (1952) 86 CLR102.

5. To the extent that the outcome of this claim depends on whether or not the claimed deduction contains an "insurance" component, it can be quickly disposed of. It is a condition precedent that before an outgoing can constitute an allowable deduction under section 51, it must have a nexus with the derivation of the assessable income. I am unable to perceive how providing life insurance for members of a superannuation fund can be said to be an outgoing necessarily incurred in deriving the income of the fund. It was submitted that coffering life and disability insurance constituted an "inducement" for members to join the fund thus swell its income. It is an ingenious argument, but it cannot succeed - to use a term culled from the Jaw of tort, the connection is too remote to constitute a sufficient nexus.

6. Since none of the members of the fund are "eligible employees", section 82AAC can have no application.

7. For the above reasons, the decision under review is affirmed.
