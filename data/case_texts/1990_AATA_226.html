Re Taxation Appeals [1990] AATA 226; 21 ATR 3713 (25 September 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. QT84/44

AAT No. 6255

Income Tax

[1990] AATA 226; 21 ATR 3713

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

K.L. Beddoe(1) (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income tax - whether amended assessment authorised - amendment arising from subsequent consideration of partnership return - whether full and true disclosure - whether error in calculation or mistake of fact

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 : s.170(2), 170(3)

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 : s.34(2)

Austin Distributors Pty Ltd v. F.C.T. (1964) 13 ATD 429

AL Hamblin Equipment Pty Ltd v. F.C.T.

Batagol v. F.C.T. [1963] HCA 51; (1963) 13 ATD 202

F.C.T. v. Levy [1961] HCA 92; (1961) 106 CLR 448, 12 ATD 360

Foster v. F.C.T. (1951) 82 CLR 606

Cyclone Scaffolding Pty Ltd v. F.C.T. 87 ATC 4021

F.C.T. v. Maurice's Estate 77 ATC 4462

F.C.T. v. Lee [1962] HCA 35; (1962) 107 CLR 329

HEARING

BRISBANE

25:9:1990

ORDER

The Tribunal decides that the objection decision under review be set aside and the objection allowed in full.

DECISION

Two issues arise for decision in this application. The first is whether the applicant is entitled to a deduction in relation to a partnership known as "Australia Flowerland No 3 Inc" and the second is whether the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") authorised the making of an amended assessment to disallow the claimed deduction of $7,527 as a share of the net loss of the partnership. Only the second question has been argued before me in these proceedings. The applicant reserves the right to argue the substantive issue if he does not succeed on the amendment of the assessment issue. The reason for this is that the cost of arguing the substantive issue will be considerable and not justified if the applicant succeeds on the amendment question.

2. The relevant statutory provision is section 170 of the Act as it applied in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1979. The respondent does not allege that paragraph 170(2)(a) applies in this case.

3. The question is whether the applicant failed to make a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment resulting in an avoidance of tax (s.170(2)). If the answer to that question is "yes" then the applicant cannot succeed on the amendment question. If the answer is "no" then subsection 170(3) will only authorise the amended assessment so as to correct a mistake of fact or an error in calculation within three years from the making of the original assessment.

4. In his income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1979 lodged on 15 February 1980 the applicant describes himself as a pharmaceutical chemist and grazier. He derived income from various sources irrelevant to these proceedings. A balance sheet attached to the return and forming part of Exhibit A disclosed an investment described "Investment - Australian Flowerland $2,875". That investment was not in the balance sheet for the prior year. Although quite extensive financial accounts have been enclosed with the return of income there is no other mention in those accounts relevant to the investment in Australian Flowerland. However at item 11 of the return form the applicant disclosed the interest in the partnership by stating the name of the partnership, the Taxation Office at which the partnership lodges its tax return and claimed a primary production loss of $7,527.

5. By notice dated 9 April 1980 the respondent notified an assessment of the applicant's taxable income for the year ended 30 June 1979. Certain adjustments not relevant to these proceedings were made to the taxable income as returned. By notice of amended assessment dated 9 February 1982 the respondent notified that the assessment had been amended "to adjust loss from Australian Flowerland No. 3 Add 7527". Item 11 has been endorsed by someone, I will infer to be the officer of the Taxation Office who made the amended assessment, with the words "Assess as per H/O memo 25/3/80 NIL no 226(2) Pen.". It is that adjustment which is in dispute together with the amended assessment itself; both issues being fairly raised by the applicant's notice of objection dated 24 February 1982.

6. Exhibit B is the partnership return for the year of income ended 30 June 1979 lodged by a partnership described as "Australia Flowerland No. 3 Inc and others" ("the No. 3 Partnership"). The return is dated 27 March 1980 and is stamped as having been received in the Australian Taxation Office on 31 March 1980.

7. The return of the partnership is fulsome in the disclosures made. It reveals the formation of a limited partnership by an agreement in writing dated 7 March 1979 with registration of the partnership deed being effected on 6 April 1979. A schedule attached to the return sets out details of the limited partners including their capital contribution and share of the partnership loss. In the applicant's case his capital contribution is shown as $2500 and share of the net loss is shown as $8149.84. The general partners are shown as Australia Flowerland No. 3 Inc and Australia Flowerland Inc.

8. At this stage I do not need to make findings in relation to the accounts of the partnership except to say that the accounts forming part of the partnership income tax return indicate that a substantial business of growing and selling plants was being undertaken by the partnership or on its behalf. Income exceeding $100,000 was derived during the year of income from sale of plants.

9. At the hearing my attention was directed to two other applications pending before the Tribunal said to relate to the same partnership and to raise issues as to the merits of this application as to the substantive issue. Those applications before the Tribunal were pending in the Sydney Registry. One was subsequently dismissed by consent of the parties. The basis for those consents is not known to me. The other (NT85/5113) was resolved by a decision made pursuant to subsection 34(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 whereby the objection decision under review was set aside and a substantial deduction allowed to the applicant being described in the Tribunal's decision as "the Applicant's contribution of $7500". That direction dated 7 August 1990 had the effect of terminating those proceedings. As to why the respondent so conceded that taxpayer's claim but has apparently refused to concede the present applicant's claim to the extent of the contribution to the partnership I do not know. It may be the applicant refused such a basis of settlement.

10. The issues which I have to decide at this stage are:

(1) Did the applicant make a full and true

disclosure of all the material facts necessary for

his assessment?

(2) If the first question is answered in the

negative; has there been an avoidance of tax?

(3) If the first question is answered in the

positive; was the amended assessment made to

correct an error in calculation or a mistake of

fact?

11. Exhibit C comprises copies of documents emanating from and circulated within the respondent's office. The first two documents in date order, are copies of reports of meetings which took place in the Taxation Office between the respondent's officers and representatives of Australia Flowerland Inc. The first reports a meeting held on 11 April 1979 at which the officers raised a number of issues in relation to deductions to be allowed to investors. The report is in typewritten form but has additional handwriting indicating that the Taxation Office was aware in April 1979 that the No. 3 partnership had been formed.

12. A further meeting took place on 21 June 1979 attended by seven representatives of Australia Flowerland and three officers of the Taxation Office including a Second Commissioner of Taxation. At that meeting a specific question was asked by the Second Commissioner as to whether paragraph 23(q) of the Act applied to the operations. The Taxation Office was apparently made aware again of the formation of additional partnerships with, it seems, different modus operandi.

13. By an internal memorandum dated 8 February 1980 the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation at Brisbane wrote to the respondent advising, inter alia, that action to demand lodgment of income tax returns for 1979 had been taken in relation to the No. 3 partnership. That was followed by a further memorandum dated 14 March 1980 in which the Deputy Commissioner reported arrangements to have the partnership return lodged by 28 March 1980 and the decision not to take formal action to obtain the return.

14. Exhibit 3 is the respondent's answer to the memorandum of 14 March 1980. That memorandum dated 25 March 1980 and stamped as having been received by the Deputy Commissioner for Queensland on 26 March 1980 reads as follows:

"With reference to your memorandum of 14 March 1980

concerning the partnerships of the abovenamed group it

is agreed that, for the present, there is little point

in demanding lodgment of partnership returns.

2. However, it has been decided that assessment of the

limited partners' 1979 returns should now proceed

immediately.

3. In giving effect to this decision, amounts returned

as a share of the profit from Australia Flowerland Inc.

and Others (the first partnership) should be accepted

but claims for a share of the losses from the

partnerships of Australia Flowerland No. 2 Inc. and

Others and Australia Flowerland No. 3 Inc. and Others

should be disallowed. Additional tax under section

226(2) should not be imposed as it is expected that the

expenditure claimed by the partnerships as deductions

will have been actually incurred on the items claimed.

4. Decisions on any objections lodged by partners

should be deferred pending further advice from this

office."

15. The officer who made the original assessment gave evidence. He stated in the course of that evidence that he could not recall making the assessment. That is, of course, perfectly understandable given that the assessment had been made 10 years before he gave his evidence. He was able to say that he assessed the applicant's return on 12 March 1980 i.e. two days before the Deputy Commissioner reported to the respondent arrangements for lodgment of the partnership return by 28 March 1980. I should add that the assessor was in the office of the Deputy Commissioner.

16. In relation to his decision to allow the loss claimed in respect of Australia Flowerland No. 3 Inc he said he would have allowed the deduction because it was claimed (p 48 transcript). He did not question the claim. Although this witness said in evidence that the partnership return was lodged on 31 March 1980, Exhibit 3 bears an annotation which indicates the return was lodged on 28 March 1980. He was then asked whether he would have treated the claimed deduction differently if he had had the partnership return in front of him. He responded to the effect that he would have required further information. If I might say that is a very strange answer. He was prepared to and did allow the claim without reference to the partnership return, but in the hypothetical situation of having the partnership return available to him he says he would have required further information before allowing the claim. That is a position which I find difficult to accept. It is even more difficult to accept in the light of the officer's evidence as to procedures for such claims. He said that he would expect to see a "distribution advice" pinned in the applicant's return. The distribution advice sets out the individual taxpayer's share of the net income or net loss of the partnership as assessed by the partnership assessor. In fact the partnership was not assessed until 11 December 1980. However, the witness stated, with some hesitation, that assessors may have been instructed to accept such claims in respect of partnerships at the relevant time.

17. It also become clear from the evidence of this witness that it is not a function of the assessor to calculate the tax payable by a taxpayer. Therefore, although the assessor ascertains the taxable income of the taxpayer (Exhibit A) the assessor does not ascertain the tax payable by the taxpayer. On the evidence of the witness that function was performed by a combination of data input to a computer and calculation by the computer. It is only after computer processing that the tax income and the tax payable thereon are ascertained. In this case the applicant's return is marked as having been computer processed on 9 April 1980 i.e. the date appearing on the notice of assessment.

18. Another officer who gave evidence explained that it had become too cumbersome for the Taxation Office to hold returns pending receipt of distribution advices from the partnership assessors and the practice had therefore been put in place of accepting, within limits, the amount of the partnership distribution, as returned by the taxpayer.

19. It is beyond doubt, on the evidence that the respondent decided on or before 25 March 1980 that the applicant's claim for the deduction in respect of his share of the net loss claimed by the partnership should be disallowed. That is made clear by Exhibit 3 which is, however, devoid of reason as to why the decision was made. Certainly it was not made in a vacuum because the respondent's officers had been engaged in some sort of investigation of the affairs of Australia Flowerland Inc. That is made clear by the papers in Exhibit C. Whatever the respondent's state of knowledge he had concluded by 25 March 1980 that the claims should not be allowed.

20. That conclusion is supported by the distribution advice eventually issued by the partnership assessor and dated 15 December 1980 (Exhibit D). That advice, which is the basis for the making of the amended assessment notified by a notice dated 9 February 1982, adopts the respondent's memorandum of 25 March 1980 as the basis for disallowing the deduction claimed for the share in the net loss of the partnership. Therefore the respondent had decided to disallow the claimed net loss by 25 March 1980, a matter of two weeks before making the assessment. Given that "assessment" is defined by subsection 6(1) to mean the ascertainment of the amount of taxable income and of the tax payable thereon and there is no contrary intention as to the meaning of the word when used in section 166 of the Act, I am bound to conclude that the original assessment was made on 9 April 1980 when the respondent completed the process by calculating the tax payable on the taxable income previously ascertained by the assessor and issued the notice of assessment. (Batagol v. F.C.T. [1963] HCA 51; (1963) 13 ATD 202).

21. The first question is whether the applicant made a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for making his assessment. So far as the applicant's own return of income is concerned I accept and so find that the applicant made a full and true disclosure of his interest in the partnership. He truthfully answered each of the questions asked of him at item 11 of the return form thereby directing the respondent's attention to the return of the partnership. The fact that no file number was shown is explained by the liklihood that no such number would be in existence until the partnership lodged a return.

22. When the assessor dealt with the applicant's return he did not and could not have had the benefit of the partnership return. It had not been lodged with the respondent at that date. However, the evidence does not suggest that the assessor was concerned to have access to the partnership return, nor indeed does it suggest that he was at all concerned about the claim. Rather than make enquiries or put the return aside for further action in relation to the partnership return he proceeded to calculate the taxable income on the basis that the claim regarding the partnership was allowable.

23. The partnership return when lodged on 28 March 1980, although fulsome in the information supplied, would not by itself represent a full and true disclosure of the affairs of the partnership. Although there is much judicial dicta as to the meaning of full and true disclosure the test enunciated by Menzies J in Austin Distributors Pty Ltd v. F.C.T. (1964) 13 ATD 429 at 432-3 is as follows:

"The requirement of s.170 of the Income Tax and Social

Services Contribution Assessment Act is not met by

anything less than full disclosure of all the material

facts, and a disclosure which leaves the Commissioner

to speculate as to some of the material facts is not

sufficient. I have reached the conclusion that there

was not full disclosure of the facts requisite to

arrive at a determination whether the taxpayer was a

lessee of the land for the purposes of the Income Tax

and Social Services Contribution Assessment Act. The

matter can be tested in this way. If advice were to

have been sought by the taxpayer whether or not the sum

in question was a taxable premium, would the person

from whom that advice was sought have required more

information than this return disclosed to the

Commissioner? I cannot escape the conclusion that he

would and that, in particular, he would have required

to know the purpose for which the licence was granted

and its terms."

That test was approved by Stephen J in AL Hamblin Equipment Pty Ltd v. F.C.T. (1974) 74 ATC 4001 at 4011-2. Applying that test to the partnership return lodged on 28 March 1980, without more, it must be found that the fulsome information supplied raised a very real question as to the nature of the business operations being conducted by the partnership which is not explained by the return. In particular, the expenditure of over $6 million on purchase of seeds and bulbs not described as trading stock raises questions as to the nature and purpose of the purchases. Based upon the information supplied in the partnership return only it must be said that the respondent Commissioner would have been left to speculate as to the nature of the partnership business, particularly the transactions in respect of bulbs, plants and seeds.

24. In the context of disclosure in a partnership return reference must be made to the leading judgment of Owen J. in F.C.T. v. Levy [1961] HCA 92; (1961) 106 CLR 448, 12 ATD 360. There his Honour made it clear that it is not sufficient to find that the taxpayer acted bona fide in lodging the partnership return. If the facts disclosed bona fide were in fact incorrect then a full and true disclosure had not been made. Dixon C.J. qualified his support for the reasons of Owen J. in these words (12 ATD 361):

"But it must be remembered that we are here dealing

with a fact or figure positively mis-stated, not with

an omission to state a fact either because it was not

within the knowledge of the taxpayer or was already

within the knowledge of the Commissioner or was a

matter of public knowledge.

There Dixon J. was acknowledging to his earlier dicta in Foster v. F.C.T. (1951) 82 CLR 606 at 619 when his Honour stated:

"But a taxpayer can hardly be said to fail to disclose

to the Commissioner a fact which is not only within the

Commissioner's knowledge in connection with the

exercise of his functions in the very matter, but which

the taxpayer knows so to be within his knowledge."

In the present case there is no evidence as to whether the taxpayer knew that the Commissioner had already decided to disallow the claims for share of the net loss of the partnership. While Exhibit C makes it clear that the General Partner knew that the respondent was giving consideration to disallowing the claims there is no evidence as to when, or if at all, the General Partner was advised that the claims would be disallowed. What had been made very clear to the General Partner was that the Commissioner was investigating and contesting the claims. That is sufficient to justify a finding that the General Partner knew that the Commissioner was unlikely to allow deductions to the limited partners for their share in the net loss of the partnership.

25. However, I do not need to finally decide whether the partnership return constituted a full and true disclosure by the applicant. I was not addressed on the question as to whether limited partners have the same responsibilities as partners in a partnership under the Partnership Act. I note, however, that the respondent has failed to show that there was any relevant fact of which he was not already aware at the time the assessment was made (Cyclone Scaffolding Pty Ltd v. F.C.T. 87 ATC 4021).

26. What is clear is that the relevant avoidance of tax had absolutely nothing to do with whether or not there had been a full and true disclosure in the partnership return. The respondent made the relevant decision before the partnership return was lodged and also before he made the applicant's assessment on 9 April 1980. The avoidance of tax was caused by the respondent's failure to assess in accordance with his own decision. That in turn was caused by the assessor's failure to refer to the partnership return and also the failure to withhold the making of the assessment pending the implementation of the respondent's decision to refuse the claim. The causa causans of the avoidance of tax is entirely the decision to make the assessment without regard to the Head Office decision and the partnership return. The applicant was not responsible in any way for the avoidance of tax. That is made clear by the decision of the High Court in F.C.T. v. Maurice's Estate 77 ATC 4462.

27. In my view the applicant, as a limited partner, made a full and true disclosure in his own return when he correctly and accurately directed the respondent's attention to the return of the partnership. I am not able to find that the partnership return represented a full and true disclosure of the material necessary to assess the partnership but on the facts of this case that finding is not adverse to the taxpayer. It is clear to me that the respondent had already made his decision in relation to the partnership on information previously disclosed by the General Partner. It was the respondent's failure to implement the decision already made which resulted in the avoidance of tax. I therefore conclude that subsection 170(2) of the Act did not authorise the amended assessment made and notified on 9 February 1982.

28. I turn now to consider whether the amended assessment was authorised by subsection 170(3) of the Act. On the basis that the applicant made a full and true disclosure in his own return and in the partnership return, the question is whether the amended assessment was made to correct an error in calculation or a mistake of fact. Clearly there was no error of calculation and it was not suggested to me that such was the case.

29. Counsel for the respondent said there was a mistake of fact because the assessor wrongly assumed that the partnership was a genuine trading partnership. While it is correct to say that a wrong supposition of fact is a mistake of fact, it is not correct to divorce the assessor from the respondent. Fosters Case (supra) makes this clear. If the respondent was aware of the facts then it is not relevant that one of his assessors is not so aware. I reject the submission that here the respondent did not know all the facts. In one sense that is correct as it would be in respect of all cases. There would always be some fact that the respondent does not know. However, section 170 limits disclosure to material facts. As the respondent had made a decision I must infer that he was in possession of the material facts. I would not attribute to the respondent the dubious attribute of making decisions in a vacuum (even though he does tend to make them in a fish bowl).

30. In my view the decisions of the High Court in F.C.T. v. Levy (supra) and F.C.T. v. Lee [1962] HCA 35; (1962) 107 CLR 329 are authorities which bind me to find in favour of the applicant. In both cases the High Court decided on facts very similar to the present case that amended assessments made to effect adjustments arising from subsequent consideration of partnership returns were not made to correct a mistake of fact in the original assessments. The amended assessments were not therefore authorised by subsection 170(3).

31. I have therefore come to the view that section 170 did not authorise the amended assessment. It will not therefore be necessary for the Tribunal to consider the merits of this application on the substantive issue.

32. The objection decision under review will be set aside and the objection allowed in full.
