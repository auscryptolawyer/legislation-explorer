Re Samuel Charles Gardner-Hudson and Commissioner of Superannuation [1987] AATA 410 (16 November 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: SAMUEL CHARLES GARDNER-HUDSON

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. Q85/149

AAT No. 3913

Superannuation Act 1922

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

D.P. Breen (Deputy President)

J.B.K. Williams CBE (Senior Member)

W.A. De Maria (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation Act, 1922 - Superannuation Act, 1976 - sections 184(1),(2),(7), 66(2) - issue of benefit classification certificate - question whether the incapacity the ground for the applicant's retirement was that named in the benefit classification certificate

Superannuation Act, 1976 - section 184(1),(2),(7)

Superannuation Act, 1922 - section 66(2)

Norman Gunzburg and the Commissioner for Superannuation (W86/300, decision given on 7 May 1987)

HEARING

BRISBANE

16:11:1987

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

The applicant commenced work with the then TAA in 1965. He was retired on health grounds with effect from 15 August 1980.

2. It appears that the applicant underwent a medical examination for the purposes of ascertaining his eligibility for acceptance to the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account established under the provisions of the Superannuation Act, 1922 as amended ("the superseded Act"). Examining medical officer, Dr Parle, referred the applicant to Dr Sampson, a consulting cardiologist. Dr Sampson furnished a report to Dr Parle dated 9 March 1971, in which he reiterated an opinion previously expressed by him that the applicant suffered from chest pains not cardiac in origin and regarded him as fit for acceptance to the Superannuation Fund. In a report dated 29 June 1971, Dr Parle certified that the applicant was unfit to become a contributor for the superannuation benefits and recommended that he be accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account. He gave as his reasons -

"fit Provident Account only. History of chest pain not

cardiac in origin".

3. It appears that the applicant, although he commenced work with TAA in 1965, did not then become entitled to participate in the Superannuation Scheme set up by the superseded Act, section 4 of which required a ministerial direction that particular persons be deemed to be employees for the purposes of the Act. In the applicant's case, that direction was given on 1 September 1971. Following that direction, a delegate of the Superannuation Board, on 11 November 1971, approved the recommendation that the applicant be accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account.

4. As stated earlier, the applicant was retired on health grounds with effect from 15 August 1980. On 16 October 1980 the Commissioner for Superannuation wrote a letter to the applicant in which, inter alia, it was stated -

"As you have completed less than 20 years of contributory

service, the Superannuation Act requires that, if the

Commissioner is of the opinion that the incapacity which was

the ground for your retirement was caused or was

substantially contributed to by the physical or mental

condition(s) that were in existence at the time you became a

contributor for the purposes of the Superannuation Act, the

Commissioner shall issue a Benefit Classification

Certificate in which the physical or mental condition(s) are

specified. The delegate of the Commissioner has now issued

a Benefit Classification Certificate which is enclosed.

In accordance with the provisions of Section 66(2) of the

Superannuation Act 1976, the delegate of the Commissioner is

of the opinion that the incapacity that was the ground for

your retirement was caused or was substantially contributed

to by the physical or mental condition(s) specified in the

Benefit Classification Certificate issued to you, or by a

physical or mental condition(s) connected with such

condition(s)."

5. It will be noted that the letter makes reference to a benefit classification certificate. It appears that a benefit classification certificate was issued on 15 September 1980 pursuant to section 184(2) of the Superannuation Act, 1976. In it the "relevant condition" was stated to be "history of chest pain". Following receipt of the letter of 16 October 1980, the applicant, on 27 November 1980, wrote to the Commissioner a letter which was treated as an application for a reconsideration of the matters conveyed in the Commissioner's letter. The applicant's letter was in the following terms -

"Received your correspondence re my Superannuation pension &

note that you have my pension as 20% Govt. financed & 80%

Employee financed, this being as stated on my Benefit

Classification Certificate, received with your

correspondence because of a 'History of Chest Pain'.

As I have during the last year or two had both high & low

blood pressure & also suffered from depression & nervous

tension which I believe has accellerated my pre-existing

angina condition & has caused the deteriation of my general

health, I would like to apply for an increase in my pension

rate on these grounds & also on the grounds that I was under

the impression that I was first accepted for Provident prior

to 8 years ago."

6. The matter was reconsidered, during the course of which a number of medical reports were obtained. A delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation then on 21 August 1985 decided to vary the decision previously made to issue a benefit classification certificate specifying the physical or mental condition "history of chest pain" by substituting for that condition the condition "history of chest pain not cardiac in origin". The decision that the invalidity benefit of the applicant be determined under section 66(2) of the Act was affirmed. The applicant now seeks a review of those decisions by this Tribunal.

7. The Superannuation Act, 1976 which came into operation on 1 July 1976 effected substantial changes in the Superannuation Scheme. Of importance in the present case are subsections (1), (2) and (7) of section 184 which, at the time of the applicant's retirement, were, so far as is relevant for present purposes, in the following terms -

"184. (1) This section applies to a person who is, or at any

time has been, an existing contributor other than -

(a) ...

(b) ...

(2) Subject to sub-section (4), where -

(a) a person to whom this section applies who, immediately

before the commencing day, was (otherwise than by

reason of an election made under section 81 of the

superseded Act) a contributor to the Provident Account

ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of death or

retirement on the ground of invalidity;

(b) his period of contributory service is less than 20

years and, on the day on which he ceases to be an

eligible employee, he has not attained his maximum

retiring age; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that the death of

the person or the incapacity which was the ground for

the retirement of the person was caused, or was

substantially contributed to -

(i) by the physical or mental condition of the person

that was relevant for the purposes of the

superseded Act or, if there was more than one

such condition, by those conditions or one or

more of those conditions; or

(ii) by a physical or mental condition or conditions

of the person connected with the physical or

mental condition of the person that was relevant

for the purposes of the superseded Act or,

if there was more than one such condition, with

those conditions or one or more of those

conditions,

the Commissioner shall issue in respect of the person a

benefit classification certificate in which there is or are

specified the physical or mental condition or conditions of

the person that was or were relevant for the purposes of the

superseded Act and, for the purposes of this Act, the

certificate shall be deemed to have been issued under

sub-section 16(4) and to have been in force in respect of

the person immediately before his death or retirement.

(7) In this section -

(a) a reference to a contributor to the Fund shall be read

as a reference to a contributor to the existing Fund

under Part III of the superseded Act;

(b) a reference to a contributor to the Provident Account

shall be read as a reference to a contributor to the

Provident Account established under the superseded Act;

and

(c) a reference to the physical or mental condition or

conditions of a person that was or were relevant for

the purposes of the superseded Act shall be read as a

reference to the physical or mental condition or

conditions of the person that, in the opinion of the

Commissioner, was or were the physical or mental

condition or conditions of the person by reason of

which -

(i) the Superannuation Board was not satisfied under

subsection 5(1) of the superseded Act that the

health and physical fitness of the person were

such as to justify his being accepted as a

contributor to the Fund; or

(ii) the person was not, by virtue of a provision of

the superseded Act (other than sub-section 5(1)

or 79(2) of the superseded Act), accepted as a

contributor to the Fund,

other than -

(iii) a physical or mental condition that, in the

opinion of the Commissioner, did not exist -

(A) at the time the person became a contributor

to the Fund or a contributor to the

Provident Account; or

(B) where the person so became a contributor on

more than one occasion - at the time he last

so became a contributor and immediately

before which he was not a contributor to the

Provident Account and was not in receipt of

pension in accordance with section 45 of the

superseded Act; or

(iv) a physical or mental condition that, in the

opinion of the Commissioner, the Superannuation

Board was, as a result of a medical examination

under sub-section 79(2) of the superseded Act,

satisfied no longer existed unless the

Commissioner is satisfied that, but for the

failure of the person to furnish any information

required to be furnished by him or the furnishing

of false information at or in connexion with that

medical examination, the Board would not have

been so satisfied."

8. It is not in issue that the applicant was a person who was immediately before the commencing date, that is 1 July 1976, a contributor to the Provident Account set up under the superseded Act and that he ceased to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity. Further he was a person whose period of contributory service was less than 20 years on the day on which he ceased to be an eligible employee and had not attained his maximum retiring age. What is in issue is the correctness or otherwise of the formation of the opinion by the Commissioner in accordance with section 184(2)(c), and the issue by him of a benefit classification certificate under that sub-section. The Commissioner did form an opinion that the incapacity of the applicant which was the ground for his retirement was caused or was substantially contributed to by his physical or mental condition that was relevant for the purposes of the superseded Act, namely "history of chest pain". Upon formation of that opinion, the Commissioner was directed to and did issue a benefit classification certificate in which there was specified "history of chest pain". Pursuant to the provisions of subsection (7) a reference to the physical or mental condition or conditions that was or were relevant for the purposes of the superseded Act is to be read as a reference to the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person that in the opinion of the Commissioner was or were the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person by which the Superannuation Board was not satisfied under subsection 5(1) of the superseded Act that the health and physical fitness of the person was such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Fund. The Commissioner on the formation of his opinion under section 184(2)(c) identified "history of chest pain" as the condition referred to in sub-section (7)(c). As stated earlier, that condition was varied, on review, to "history of chest pain not cardiac in origin".

9. It is first necessary to identify the physical or mental condition or conditions by reason of which the Superannuation Board was not satisfied under the superseded Act that the health or physical fitness of the applicant was such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund. Reference has already been made to the reports of Dr Parle and Dr Sampson which were apparently before the Board or its delegate at the time when the decision was made not to accept the applicant as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund but to accept him as a contributor to the Provident Account. That decision is contained on the printed form on which Dr Parle's report was made, and appears immediately beside Dr Parle's statement "fit for Provident Account only. History of chest pain not cardiac in origin". The inference is, we think, inescapable that the physical or mental condition by reason of which the Board's delegate was not satisfied that the health and physical fitness of the applicant was such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund was the condition specified by Dr Parle: that the condition specified by Dr Parle existed at the time is supported by the report of Dr Sampson.

10. It is next necessary to identify the physical or mental condition which was the ground for the applicant's retirement in August 1980. That followed a report from Dr Brown, Chief Medical Officer of TAA, dated 10 July 1980 wherein he recommended the applicant's retirement and made the following observations -

"He was placed on Provident in 1971 for 'history of chest

pains not cardiac in origin' present since 1966. ECG at

that time indicated left bundle branch block. The pains in

the chest continued but since 1978 became progressively

worse; hypertension developed which however is well

controlled and a mild anxiety state related to the chest

pains. The chest pains may be cardiac in origin and a

consultant opinion in 1979 stated 'I have always thought

that the retro-sternal pain was indeed angina, and I feel

this would be supported by the finding of a left bundle

branch block on his ECG'.

In my opinion the pains that Mr Hudson is suffering from

presently are the same as those noted in his Provident

medical examination in 1971 and going back to 1966 although

they have become progressively more severe. In my opinion

this man would not be suitable for redeployment as his main

medical symptom i.e. chest pains would continue to dominate

his life as at present".

11. By letter dated 5 May 1981, the Commissioner for Superannuation requested Dr Brown to furnish a further report. Dr Brown was asked to advise what were the conditions (in order of severity) that he considered the applicant was suffering from when he recommended retirement, together with estimated percentages that each condition contributed to his incapacity.

12. Dr Brown replied by letter dated 25 June 1981 in which it was stated:

"I recommended retirement for Mr Gardner-Hudson because he

claimed the chest pains, which had been present for many

years, were becoming progressively more severe to the extent

that they dominated his life. He stated he was unable to

cope with the stresses of his occupation. In addition he

had hypertension which was well controlled. He was not

convinced the pains were angina and did not want open-heart

surgery to be considered.

In my opinion percentage disabilities are:-

Chest pains - 70%

Anxiety/Depression - 20%

Hypertension - 10%"

13. Dr Brown also enclosed a report from Dr Seymour, cardiologist, dated 13 August 1979. Dr Seymour stated that he had first seen the applicant in November 1978 and on several subsequent occasions, the last being in January 1979. He also stated:

"He is a very vague historian and included in his symptoms

are tiredness and exhaustion and also some retro sternal

pain. I have always thought that the retro sternal pain was

indeed angina, and I feel this would be supported by the

finding of a left bundle branch block on his E.C.G.

When I last saw him, he was complaining of the retro sternal

pain being produced by moderate degrees of exertion.

However, his chest pain was over ridden by symptoms of

tiredness, weakness and depression.

There were no significant findings clinically. His B.P. was

140/80.

As I mentioned, the E.C.G. did show a left bundle branch

block.

I would agree with Dr Boyles assessment that he does have

some degree of Coronary Artery Disease and also symptoms

related to anxiety depression".

14. Also enclosed with Dr Brown's report was a report of Dr Boyle, who was apparently the applicant's general practitioner. In this report dated 30 July 1979 it is stated:-

"This man has been progressively getting worse with respect

to Anginal pain since November, 1978. Prior to this, he had

been a controlled hypertensive with only occasional chest

pain. He has been seen by a Cardiologist, who has

recommended Coronary Arteriography, and Mr. Hudson is

considering this at the moment.

He also has a left bundle branch block, and raised Blood

Cholesterol and Triglycerides. Over the past 3 months, he

has developed an anxiety syndrome, partly, I feel, related

to his increasing pain, and partly to the increased work

load, according to his statements.

He is currently taking Betaloc, Enduron and Serepax and is

on a low saturated fat diet. His Blood Pressure has been

averaging 150/95 (sitting & standing) and 145/90 (lying) and

his pulse rate in the high seventies."

15. Dr Boyle furnished another report dated 14 April 1982. In it he stated that the applicant first became a patient of the practice of which Dr Boyle is a member in 1962 and that Dr Boyle first saw him in July 1972 "when he had angina and hypertension". The report also states that the applicant next had chest pain in February 1973 and September 1975. In March 1976 he presented for a review and had had no pain since his previous visit. In December 1976 "he had anterior chest wall pain but no angina". In March 1977 he complained of occasional early morning chest pain associated with tension. In July 1977 he was pain-free but experienced angina on exercise. At that time he was also diagnosed as suffering from anxiety/depression reaction. In January 1980 his anxiety problem became more acute. His blood pressure dropped and he became dizzy. In July 1980 he presented with chest pain on waking on two occasions.

16. Dr Boyle furnished a further report dated 19 June 1982 in which he listed particulars of clinical notes made by members of the practice over the period May 1962 to June 1971. These notes indicate that in May 1962 the applicant was diagnosed as having an anxiety neurosis with tension headaches. In December 1963 he complained of a vague chest pain with no relationship to exertion, and in November 1964 a tightness in the chest with no relationship to exertion. The clinical notes also record "recent coronary insufficiency with pain for 10 minutes" on 3 December 1969. "Pra cordial pain lasting 10 minutes" on 7 December 1970 and angina on 21 June 1971 are also recorded. The notes also show that at various times drugs such as Amytal, Amylobarb and Butisol were prescribed.

17. In March 1983 Dr Brown was requested to obtain a report from a cardiologist. This request was apparently made in the light of the fact that Dr Seymour, to whom previous reference has been made, had indicated that he could add nothing to the contents of his previous reports. Dr Brown sought an opinion from Dr Vohra. Dr Vohra furnished a report dated 1 June 1983 in which he stated that he had studied previous reports furnished in this matter. He noted that the applicant had a left bundle branch block pattern on his ECG in 1966 and was complaining of odd chest pains at that time and gave a history of infrequent pain to the time of his superannuation examination in 1971. Dr Vohra expressed the opinion that the left bundle branch block was likely to be due to conduction tissue fibrosis rather than to ischaemic or coronary artery disease and that the left bundle branch block was probably not related to present chest pain. He expressed the further opinion that the applicant may have had left bundle branch block and unrelated ischaemic heart disease. He thought that it was likely that the applicant did not have ischaemic chest pains but that anxiety played a significant part in his symptoms. He considered the only way to resolve the issue was by further medical tests.

18. Dr Vohra was asked to arrange for further tests and to provide a further report.

19. Dr Vohra arranged for these tests and examined the applicant on 15 September 1983. In a report dated 19 September 1983 Dr Vohra summarised his conclusions as follows:

"I think that this subject has two separate conditions which

are not causally related. He has left bundle branch block

which is probably due to conduction tissue fibrosis and not

related to minimal coronary artery disease. I also think

that the atypical chest pains that he has had for a long

time are non-ischaemic in nature and are probably due to

anxiety. In workers' compensation terms I would think that

his incapacity is due to anxiety/depression 70%, chest pain

20% and hypertension 10%."

20. The applicant in his evidence before the Tribunal claimed that medical conditions other than chest pains brought about his retirement. Asked what he considered to be the medical condition or conditions which led to his retirement, he said:-

"I would say that it was nervous depression, pain in the

joints, whatever comes with nervous depression. I had

trouble breathing, catching the breath and dizziness,

extreme tiredness; I did not know what to do with myself

sometimes, I would get so tired. I did not know how I would

fill the day out. I was in a badly deteriorating state of

health in that manner.

And do you associate these things with the chest pain?---

Possibly they could have been another type of chest pain

developed through that. I believe that may be medically

possible; I do not know.

THE D. PRESIDENT: You say that the chest pain that you went

in with, you had had that for years, anyway, and even if you

still had them, they were not a specific factor in your

retirement?---Yes."

21. He also said in a letter to the Tribunal Registry, received on 25 September 1985:-

"joint pains which developed after my admission to Provident

Fund but during my employment with TAA which would have been

sufficient to force my retirement from TAA not long after my

cessation date".

22. He also gave evidence that he experienced pressures at work with TAA, due it seems to a personality clash with another officer of that organisation. These pressures he claimed were productive of, or at least aggravated an anxiety state.

23. Central to the question of what medical condition or conditions led to the applicant's retirement are the reports of Dr Brown. His report of 25 June 1981 which has been quoted earlier plainly states that he recommended retirement of the applicant -

"because he claimed that chest pains which had been present

for many years were becoming progressively more severe to

the extent that they dominated his life".

24. Unfortunately Dr Brown was not called to give evidence, the reason for which is unknown to us. However, the applicant was given the opportunity to comment on Dr Brown's report. Asked if the report fairly represented what he had said to Dr Brown, he replied:-

"I think he over-emphasizes the heart part. The rest of that

would be pretty near that. Thee were chest pains - probably

a few more than normal, or previously, but they were not the

only symptoms, I think that - the symptoms I constantly

mentioned were the ones that were around, but I am sure that

Dr Brown - I would have mentioned the heart pains to him;

we would have discussed the heart pains. But it is a long

while ago and I could not be 100 per cent sure, but I do not

believe that I emphasized that heavily on that one thing.

That is all I can say on that.

MR WILLIAMS: Well, he reports you as saying that they were

becoming progressively more severe to the extent that they

dominated your life?---I do not believe that is completely

right.

THE D. PRESIDENT: As to what you told him, but is it right

or wrong as to the position - - -?---Well, the chest - - -

- - - with respect to heart pains at the time leading up to

your retirement?---The chest pains were there, but so were

all the other symptoms. I mentioned before I go to the

stage where I felt that to overstress everything all the

time would be to make me sound too neurotic, I suppose is

the thing in your mind. But they were there when they were

reported and they are shown in medical evidence as being

about - being there."

25. We do not regard these answers as amounting to a denial of the statements attributed to the applicant in Dr Brown's report. Rather they appear to be a qualified acceptance of them. Such qualifications as were expressed were in vague and indefinite terms.

26. It is clear upon the evidence that the applicant had experienced chest pains (whatever their origin) over a long period of time dating back, according to the report of Dr Boyle of 19 June 1982, to 1962. It was accepted in the evidence of the applicant quoted earlier that at the time of his examination by Dr Brown he was experiencing chest pains "probably a few more than normal" and that he would have mentioned the heart pains to him and would have discussed them, although he said that other symptoms existed at that time. There is no evidence however that these other symptoms were elaborated upon to Dr Brown, nor that symptoms other than chest pains and stress played any part in the formation of Dr Brown's recommendation. We think it more probable than not that the statements attributed to the applicant by Dr Brown were substantially correct. In these circumstances we are satisfied that the incapacity for work which was the ground for his retirement was caused or contributed to by chest pains, the condition specified in the benefit classification certificate.

27. As has been said, the benefit classification certificate in its original terms referred to "history of chest pain" which on review was varied to "history of chest pain not cardiac in origin". The opinions expressed in the medical reports concerning the origin of the chest pain are to some extent at variance. We are disposed to accept the view expressed by Dr Vohra. He had the advantage of a consideration of all the available medical reports and of assessing the results of tests carried out by him. In summary, Dr Vohra's view was that the applicant suffered two conditions not causally related: one was left bundle branch block probably due to conduction tissue fibrosis and not related to minimal coronary artery disease, and the other was that the atypical chest pain that he had had for a long time was non-ischaemic in nature and probably due to anxiety. This indicates that the chest pains are not cardiac in origin but rather have their origin in an anxiety state. It accordingly appears, as was said by Dr Brown in a report of 14 March 1984, that the chest pain and anxiety state are inter-related. It does not seem possible from the evidence to separate the two. The use of percentages in the medical reports is, we think, misleading and apt to create confusion.

28. It was also decided by the delegate that the entitlement of the applicant to invalidity benefit should be determined pursuant to the provisions of section 66(2) of the Act which is in the following terms:-

"(2) Subject to sub-sections (3), (3A) and (4) of this

section and section 79, where -

(a) a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of

retirement on the ground of invalidity before attaining

his maximum retiring age;

(b) there was in force in respect of the person,

immediately before the person's retirement, a benefit

classification certificate; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that the incapacity

which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or

was substantially contributed to, by a physical or

mental condition or conditions specified in the

certificate or by a physical or mental condition or

conditions connected with such a condition or such

conditions,

the person is entitled - ..."

29. In the light of the view that we have already expressed with respect to section 184(2)(c), it follows that in our view the applicant's entitlement falls for determination pursuant to section 66(2).

30. At the hearing, attention was directed to certain amendments to section 184(2) by section 76 of the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act (No 2), 1986. We do not think that these amendments have any relevance to the present case. The applicant was retired in 1980 and his entitlement is to be ascertained in accordance with the legislation as it then stood. This view receives support from the decision of the Tribunal in Norman Gunzburg and the Commissioner for Superannuation W86/300, decision given on 7 May 1987.

31. For the foregoing reasons, we would affirm the decisions under review.
