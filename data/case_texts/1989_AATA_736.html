VT86/6988, VT86/6991 and Commissioner of Taxation [1989] AATA 736; (1989) 21 ATR 3107; 90 ATC 124 (15 December 1989)

Last Updated: 18 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - assessable income - deposits in building society accounts - whether deposits represent undisclosed assessable income - whether income was derived by applicant.

The Tribunal decides that the objection decisions under review be set aside and the objection allowed.

DECISION AND REASONS

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No: VT86/6988; VT86/6991

AAT Decision No 5542

Date: 15 December 1989 Place: Brisbane

Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

-ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. VT86/6988; VT86/6991

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 15 December 1989

Place: Brisbane

The question at issue in these proceedings is confined to a very narrow question as to whether amounts deposited into two Building Society accounts are undisclosed income of the applicant. The applicant contends that the funds held in the Building Society accounts are simply not his. The respondent contends that the funds are not only the property of the applicant but they represent undisclosed assessable income derived by the applicant taxpayer during the years ended 30 June 1981 and 1982.

  1. The applicant conducted his own case while the respondent Commissioner appeared by counsel.
  2. The applicant is and was at all relevant times a member of a club known as the Hells Angels. No evidence was presented to the Tribunal as to the operations of the club but it appears to be a motor cycle club.
  3. The two Building Society accounts in question were opened by the applicant in false names. The first account opened with the RESI Building Society was in the name of an alias adopted by the applicant. The second account opened at the Eastern Suburbs Permanent Building Society appears to have been in the name of the applicant and his girlfriend with the surname of the account being his girlfriend's name. The RESI account had two deposits made in April and May 1981 for a total of $2,000. The second account was opened on 5 September 1980 with a deposit of $1,000 and was from that time on a recipient of regular deposits of cash in varying amounts. Much smaller amounts were also withdrawn from this account over the next 18 months with a consequence that in March 1982 the account had a balance of $15,421. The account was then closed and the funds transferred to a Permanent Building Society account held by the applicant's solicitors as trustee for the applicant. No attempt was made to use an alias in respect of this account.
  4. On 15 April 1983 the applicant signed income tax returns in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1981 and 1982. The return for the year ended 30 June 1981 discloses income by way of benefits paid by the Department of Social Security and described in the return as unemployment or sickness benefit. The return for the year ended 30 June 1982 disclosed that the applicant had only received unemployment benefits. As a result of investigation, no doubt prompted by matters to which I will allude later, the respondent assessed the applicant for an amount of undisclosed income of $4,276 in the year ended 30 June 1981 and an amount of $19,422 in respect of the year ended 30 June 1982. The applicant objected to these assessments through his solicitor on the basis that the income attributed to him had not been received or earned, the same grounds being taken in respect of both years.
  5. The applicant's case is straightforward and simple. He gave evidence that the amounts deposited in the two bank accounts were amounts paid to him by one H who was another member of the club. The explanation for H paying these amounts to the applicant was that the amounts were to constitute what the applicant described as a fighting fund and would be better described in the light of the applicant's explanations as a fund for payment of legal costs incurred by members of the club as a result of prosecution for criminal offences. Although there is no evidence it is not difficult to imagine why the club decided to establish a fighting fund. The explanation is that the members of the club were engaged in a criminal pursuit described as the "amphetamine factory". It is of course of no concern to this Tribunal as to whether or not it was a fact that the members of the club were engaged in criminal activity. However I note that the applicant was amongst several members of the club charged with criminal offences, committed for trial and, in the applicant's case, subjected to three trials before being convicted.
  6. Although the applicant's evidence was straightforward and clear, to the effect that the amounts deposited to the two bank accounts were payments made to him by H for the operation of the fighting fund, it appears that the explanation about the fighting fund has not been made to the authorities, i.e. the respondent Commissioner or the police who investigated the "amphetamine factory". Counsel for the respondent submitted that the story told by the applicant in his evidence should not be accepted because it was recent invention.
    1. It appears that after a number of members of the club were charged in relation to the "amphetamine factory" that a public fund was established to raise funds for payment of legal costs incurred by those charged. On the applicant's explanation the creation of the public fund made the fund "operated by the applicant" unnecessary and the account which has been conducted through the solicitors was closed. It was not however closed by simply transferring the funds to the public fund. The Tribunal simply does not know as to whether any of the funds withdrawn from that account in August 1982 reached the public fighting fund. Exhibit G is a bundle of cheques drawn on the solicitor's trust account in August 1982. These cheques were apparently financed by drawings from the Permanent Building Society account operated by the solicitors in trust for the applicant. Those cheques disclosed the following payees and amounts:

$

Applicant 4,634

To solicitors re costs 200

Repayment of a loan plus interest 509

Cash 11,000

In respect of the last matter involving $11,000 the applicant's solicitor specifically opened the cheque to make it payable by cash and the applicant has signed the reverse side of the cheque which also evidences payment of the $11,000 in cash. It was not explained to the Tribunal as to why the applicant withdrew $11,000 in cash and also withdrew an amount of $4,634 by cheque in his own name. That action does suggest, however, that the funds were to go in two directions, the cash to one undisclosed direction which as a matter of pure speculation could be the public fighting fund and the remainder contained in a separate cheque of $4,634 to go to the applicant for his own benefit. It should be noted that prior to the account being closed, the police had held the passbook of the account for some period of time during the course of their investigations into the "amphetamine factory" but apparently having satisfied themselves in relation to the contents of the account, took no further action.

  1. The applicant gave evidence that it has been his practice over many years to operate through aliases. He did not give any rational explanation for this behaviour but I accept his evidence that it was his practice to use aliases as a matter of habit.
  2. The applicant's evidence that he received the monies from H as contributions to a fighting fund is uncorroborated. Clearly it would have been in the applicant's interests to call H to corroborate his story if H was able to do so. However the applicant said in his evidence-in-chief that he was unable to call H because H was living under an assumed name and in protective custody. The Inspector of Police who was called for the respondent gave evidence that H had become a police informant and he was in fact living under an assumed name at a secret location which the witness refused to disclose before the Tribunal. This evidence corroborated the evidence of the applicant in relation to the availability of H to give evidence. Although it was in the power of the respondent to call H, he chose not to do so. I infer that H was unlikely to assist the respondent's case.

11. Evidence was also given by a solicitor who has acted for the applicant including the preparation of income tax returns referred to above. However she had no knowledge of the operations of the Building Society accounts conducted by or on behalf of the applicant nor did she have any recollection of instructions given to her by the applicant in relation to his income tax affairs at the time of preparation of the income tax returns.

12. The issue comes down to the credibility of the applicant. The respondent Commissioner has not led any evidence to suggest that the evidence of the applicant was false but has submitted, because of "recent invention" of the applicant's story about the fighting fund, the Tribunal should not accept it. On the evidence before the Tribunal it is correct that the applicant failed to raise this matter of the fighting fund until the hearing before the Tribunal. The applicant did not comply with directions of the Tribunal to file a statement of facts, matters and contentions in dispute in these proceedings which would of course have been a desirable course for the applicant to have adopted. However, the objections taken in August 1983 to the Commissioner's assessments are consistent with the facts asserted by the applicant. Those objections did not raise issues of whether the amounts contested were of a capital nature or in some other way not assessable income. They merely assert that the applicant had not received or earned the income which had been attributed to him.

  1. The amounts deposited in the accounts have been received by the applicant. The question then is the character of those amounts. It has been established that they were gifts made by various people to a fighting fund held by the applicant as a member of the club. It has not been established before this Tribunal that those amounts were assessable income. There is no evidence before the Tribunal to suggest that the applicant's evidence is incorrect and I am merely invited by counsel for the Commissioner to not accept his evidence.
  2. The applicant appeared to the Tribunal to be a man of mature years who perhaps lacks some of the living skills of his peers. He made it clear to the Tribunal that he has a deep and abiding disrespect for authority although his conduct before this Tribunal was without blemish.
  3. I reserved my decision on this matter so that I could reflect carefully on the evidence given by the applicant, given his background. I have reached the conclusion that the explanation given by the applicant is, on the balance of probabilities, the correct explanation. It is logically probative and there is no other explanation apparent to the Tribunal for these deposits in the Building Society accounts. I am satisfied that I should accept the evidence of the applicant. I have taken into account the fact that in the year ended 30 June 1983 the amount of interest derived by the applicant's solicitor as trustee for the applicant was returned as income in his 1983 income tax return. His solicitor, who prepared that return, had no recollection of what transpired at the time she prepared the return other than to say that the return would have been prepared on instructions. As the applicant has no mind for remembering anything to do with officials or laws of the country it is unlikely that any satisfactory explanation can be given as to why the amount of interest was included as assessable income in the applicant's return. I should say, however, that inclusion of the amount of interest as assessable income did not make the applicant subject to tax in the year ended 30 June 1983. Bearing in mind that the solicitors were conducting the Building Society account it is highly likely that the solicitor (regarded the applicant's tax return as an appropriate place to return such income irrespective of whether or not she had instructions as to the ownership of the funds.
    1. In the event the applicant has satisfied me that the assessments are excessive because the applicant has satisfied me that the amounts deposited to the Building Society accounts did not constitute assessable income derived by the applicant. The objection decisions will be set aside and the objections allowed.

The preceding nine (9) eight are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matters of VT86/6988 and VT96/6991.

Secretary

15/12/89
