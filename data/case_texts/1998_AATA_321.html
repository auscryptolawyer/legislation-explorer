Fiorello and Anor and Tax Agent's Board of Queensland [1998] AATA 321 (6 April 1998)

Last Updated: 8 May 1998

Appeals

Tribunal

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No Q97/1059

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re NATALINO FIORELLO

ANTONIO ROMANO

Applicant

And TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF QUEENSLAND

Respondent

Decision No 12867

DECISION

Tribunal Dr P Gerber (Deputy President)

Date 6 April 1998

Place Brisbane

Decision UPON HEARING Mr Logan of counsel for the applicants and Mr O'Brien of counsel for the respondent and for the reasons given orally at the hearing of the matter

AND in accordance with section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975

THE TRIBUNAL DECIDES THAT the decision under review be affirmed.

(Sgd) P Gerber

Deputy President

CATCHWORDS - INCOME TAX - TAX AGENT REGISTRATION - AGENT'S REGISTRATION CANCELLED ON GROUND THAT THE AGENT NOT A FIT AND PROPER PERSON TO BE REGISTERED -CONVICTIONS FOR FAILING TO LODGE RETURNS AND SUBSEQUENTLY IGNORING A COURT ORDER TO LODGE SAME WITHIN 28 DAYS - WHETHER OFFENCES "SERIOUS TAX OFFENCES" - WHETHER BOARD SHOULD HAVE EXERCISED DISCRETION TO CANCEL REGISTRATION - INCOME TAX ASSESSMENT ACT 1936 SEC 251A.

REASONS FOR DECISION

8 May 1998 Dr P Gerber (Deputy President)

1. In this case, the evidence and submissions concluded around 6 p.m. on the day of hearing (6 April 1998). After hearing submissions, I had no doubt whatever that Mr Fiorello was not a fit and proper person to be a tax agent, and that consequently his registration should be cancelled. I indicated to the parties, given the lateness of the hour, that I would be ready to give brief oral reasons there and then provided both sides were prepared to allow me to enlarge on them by written reasons if an appeal was contemplated. Both Mr Logan and Mr O'Brien (counsel for the applicant and respondent respectively) readily agreed to this. I decided on this course of action because I was satisfied that the matter should be speedily resolved, both to protect the public and to enable the partnership to deal promptly with the re-organisation of the accountancy practice (the cancellation of Mr Fiorello's licence to practise as a tax agent, whilst not preventing him practising as an accountant, has certain consequences for his partner, Mr Romano, who, like Mr Fiorello, is an accountant and tax agent).

2. I have now received a request from the solicitors for the partnership for full formal reasons so that they may consider their position in relation to the possibility of an appeal to the Federal Court.

3. I therefore propose to supplement the reasons which persuaded me to uphold the Board's decision to cancel Mr Fiorello's tax agent registration.

4. Mr Natalino Fiorello is one of two partners in the firm of accountants and tax agents known as Fiorello Romano & Associates. The practice is a busy one, doubling each year since its inception in 1992 as a sole practice. In 1995, Mr Romano joined the practice as a partner. Mr Fiorello is the partnership's registered tax agent nominee. Hence his de-registration will have the effect that the practice, as presently structured, will henceforth be unable to practise as tax agents under the firm name.

5. Both partners gave evidence before me. Mr Fiorello claims that most clients of the partnership wished to consult him and that he has had to "re-educate" many clients to see his partner, Mr Romano. The practice deals with a large number of Italian clients as both partners are bi-lingual which Mr Fiorello considered to be of assistance to the ATO and community as such clients prefer to deal with persons familiar with their culture and language. Mr Fiorello claims that he initially worked 100 hours per week in the practice, latterly reduced to 85 hours per week. It is this alleged dedication to his clients' affairs, combined with health, domestic and financial problems, which are claimed to have led to the situation culminating in the Board's decision to cancel his registration.

6. The history leading up to his deregistration can be briefly told.

(i) On 19 December 1996, Mr Fiorello was convicted of an offence against s 8C(1)(a) of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 ("the TAA") for failing to lodge his own income tax returns for the years ended 30 June 1993, 1994 and 1995. I am satisfied that Mr Fiorello was duly served with the summons but chose to not appear nor at that time seek legal representation. On this occasion he was convicted and fined in his absence in the amount of $500 together with costs of $57 and ordered to lodge the required return within 28 days of service of the order upon him.

(ii) Mr Fiorello chose to ignore the orders in that he failed to lodge his outstanding returns within 28 days. This, in turn, resulted in further proceedings against him in that he failed to comply with the court's orders (see ss 8G(1) and 8H of the TAA) and was fined $1500 together with $55 costs. Since Mr Fiorello also failed to lodge his 1996 return, he was also fined $450 and $55 costs. Again, having failed to lodge partnership returns for the (then) partnership of NT Fiorello & PN Keresctes for the years 1993, 1994, 1995 and 1996, he was fined $600 together with costs in the amount of $58.40 and ordered to lodge the outstanding returns within 28 days of the order upon him. I might add that s 8H of the TAA provides for a maximum penalty of $5000 or for a period of imprisonment not exceeding 12 months, or both.

(iii) These convictions persuaded the respondent to issue the partnership a "show cause" notice at its meeting on the 19th of August 1997, the Board taking the view that the above convictions constituted "a serious taxation offence" as defined in s 251A of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 which states that "serious taxation offence" means:

"(c) an offence that is:

(i) a taxation offence within the meaning of Part IIIA of the TAA and

(ii) punishable on conviction by either or both of the following:

(A) a fine exceeding $2000;

(B) imprisonment.

S 251BC(1) of the Tax Act provides, inter alia, that a person is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters if:

....

(e) the person has been convicted of a serious taxation offence during the previous 5 years."

(iv) On 1 September 1997, the Board required the partners to show cause, in writing, within 28 days, why the registration for the partnership should not be cancelled.

(v) On 25 September the partners responded as follows:

"Dear Madam

We refer to your correspondence of 1 September 1997, requiring us to show cause in writing within twenty eight days, why the Tax Agents registration for the partnership should not be cancelled.

In this regard, we wish to make the following submissions in relation to the matters raised in your correspondence:

1. Following receipt of the initial Complaint and Summons, contact was made by Mr Fiorello with Geoff Barrie from the Australian Taxation Office. This was in or about December 1996. Mr Barrie advised that submissions should be made in writing in relation to the same, which was done. A copy of this correspondence is attached.

2. In any event as far as Mr Fiorello was aware, wherever taxable income is below the tax free threshold a notification is the only document which is required to be lodged on behalf of the person or entity. This has been done in each instance.

3. During the course of the past twelve months Mr Fiorello has had his attention drawn away from the practice due to a number of serious problems he has encountered, including serious family and matrimonial problems and serious health problems. These matters have unfortunately compromised his ability to especially deal with his own financial affairs and the affairs relating to companies in which he is involved.

4. Mr Fiorello is normally involved in an extremely busy practice and during the course of the matters referred to above, has had to turn his attention to dealing with matters on behalf of clients in order to maintain his business relationship with those clients. As advised, this has also been at the expense of his own personal financial matters.

5. In any event, Mr Fiorello has unfortunately not been able to comprehend the consequences of the proceedings which were issued against him. it is only now, following receipt of your correspondence, that Mr Fiorello realises the ramifications of failing to lodge the returns. However, as previously mentioned herein, Mr Fiorello is of the opinion that it was not necessary to lodge the returns referred to in the Complaints.

6. More specifically, the N.T. Fiorello and P.N. Keresztes partnership is no longer in existence and the company Nattin Pty Ltd is purely a trustee company which does not trade. In relation to the individual tax returns of Mr Fiorello, as previously stated, due to a number of extraneous factors coupled with the fact that Mr Fiorello is the managing partner of a extremely busy practice has prevented him from adequately dealing with his own financial affairs.

Mr Fiorello is of the opinion that the abovementioned circumstances are most unsatisfactory and is disappointed that he has been unable to adequately deal with his own financial affairs; however, as far as he is concerned it has been imperative and more important that the needs of his clients be put first. Hence the only matters the subject of the proceedings pursuant to the Complaints have been his own personal matters and not matters relating to any of his clients.

In any event, it is intended that all of the Notices of Conviction be complied with, especially in relation to lodgement of the Tax returns referred to therein. For your reference, all fines have also been paid. On the basis of the matters referred to herein, it is requested that the Tax Agents registration for the partnership not be cancelled, as it would have a substantial adverse effect upon the ability of the practice to exist. We should be pleased if you would give favourable consideration to allowing the registration to remain in place.

Should you wish to discuss any aspect of this correspondence further, then please do not hesitate to contact our Mr Fiorello or Mr Romano.

Yours sincerely

(sgd) NATALINO FIORELLO (sgd) ANTONIO ROMANO

Partner Partner"

7. Pausing here, as explanations go, the above leaves much to be desired. In the first place, Mr Fiorello's alleged belief, stated in paragraph 2 of the letter, that "wherever taxable income is below the tax free threshold, a notification is the only document which is required to be lodged" may have been a plea in mitigation before the Magistrate, it does not explain why, after the first court Order, Mr Fiorello failed to comply with it. Mr Fiorello told me at the hearing that he was a member of various partnerships and that the accounting practice employed a service company, perfectly legitimate means of reducing taxable income, but these elaborate arrangements sit uneasily with his subsequent statement that his "attention to dealing with matters on behalf of clients in order to maintain his relationship with those clients ... has also been at the expense of his own personal financial matters." One can therefore be forgiven for thinking that however demanding his practice, he managed to find sufficient time to organise "his own financial matters" to reduce his share of the income from a busy practice to below the tax-free threshold, yet could not spare the time to file his own returns even at the risk of jeopardising his professional standing.

8. On 9 October 1997, the Board responded to the above letter, stating that the explanation did not amount to sufficient reason to excuse the conduct in respect of which Mr Fiorello was convicted, adding:

"The convictions reflect adversely on the manner in which Mr Fiorello approaches his professional task as a tax agent. In the circumstances the Board reached the decision that Mr Fiorello is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters and cancelled the registration of Mr Fiorello as nominee of the tax agent in accordance with s 251K(2)(c) of the Tax Act. In reaching its conclusion, the Board considered not only the convictions, but also took into account the fact that the income tax returns which were the subject of the convictions had still not been lodged."

9. In an affidavit dated 14 October 1997, Mr Fiorello deposed that all the returns the subject of the Magistrates Court convictions "were lodged on computer disk with the Australian Taxation Office prior to 16 September 1997; however, those disks were returned because of computer error. On this basis all of the returns have now been prepared in paper form and will be lodged by Wednesday 15 October 1997."

10. Even if I were to give Mr Fiorello the benefit of the doubt that these returns were lodged "prior to" 16 September 1997 - and here the evidence was less than wholly persuasive - it would seem that such lodgment was later than the due date pursuant to the court Order (28 days after service of the order in relation to the 19 December 1996 conviction).

11. At the hearing, Mr Fiorello gave some evidence as to his state of health and the domestic problems he encountered, the latter mainly as a result of the many hours he spends in the office looking after his client's affairs. Accepting all this, none of it is, in my view, sufficient to explain the contumelious disregard he displayed towards the court Orders made against him which he knew - or should have known - would put his registration as a tax agent at risk. For good measure, the additional evidence adduced by the respondent at the hearing, if anything, lens further weight to my decision that Mr Fiorello is not presently a fit and proper person to practise as a tax agent.

12. I now propose to quote from extracts of the oral reasons I gave at the conclusion of the evidence.

13. After outlining the history leading up to Mr Fiorello's prosecutions and what I referred to as his "sublime indifference" in complying with the court Orders, I went on to add that:

"... if this matter had stood alone - and Mr Logan very plausibly put it on the basis that the gravamen of this offence first and foremost was to be found in a failure by the applicant to deal diligently with his own affairs - ... I might have been tempted to set the Board's decision aside. But, alas, this matter does not, in my view, stand in isolation, but what I have called the `sublime indifference' Mr Fiorello displayed to his own affairs, has, on the evidence, spilled over into his action and attitude to those of his clients."

14. I then proceeded to deal briefly with the evidence of Mr RA Pollock, currently a Regional Tax Agent Strategy Manager for the Queensland Region employed by the Tax Office. His duties involve oversighting the tax agent strategy which has been conducted. This, as I understand it, involves reviewing the return form preparation standards of tax agents who lodge returns for individual non-business taxpayers.

15. I should however here point out that the calling of evidence from witnesses for the respondent was not anticipated by the applicant, nor in any way foreshadowed in preliminary proceedings by way of the respondent filing and exchanging statements of evidence prior to the hearing. The respondent had however forwarded a facsimile to the applicants' solicitors on 3 April 1998 confirming a conversation that those witnesses may be called to give evidence at the hearing (Ex B). With the agreement of Mr Logan, the evidence of both the respondent's witnesses, Mr Pollock and Ms Campion was introduced subject to the reserving of the applicants' position. Through the course of the evidence various objections were raised by Mr Logan and I will deal with these further.

16. Mr Pollock explained that he envisaged over the life of the program that all tax agents who had more than 100 clients who claimed more than $300 for work-related expenses would come onto the program. "Initially, of course, given the scarce resources the ATO has, we try to target those agents that we believe pose more of a risk to the revenue." It seems that Mr Fiorello's practice (as then constituted) came on to the program in the 1995/96 financial year as a result of Mr Pollock randomly selecting some six returns prepared in the previous year by Mr Fiorello's (then) practice for auditing. All six returns required "adjusting". When I enquired what was the nature of the "adjustments", it appeared that the witness approached Mr Fiorello on each of the six returns, seeking some form of substantiation. None was provided. Mr Fiorello had however stated in cross examination that he considered that the client takes responsibility as to the information in the return where there are two certificates of correctness to be completed - one to be certified by the client and one to be certified by the tax agent.

17. More ominously, the current practice has stayed on the program ever since. When asked in cross-examination for the reason for this, the witness replied: "... the reason we kept them on or put them on the `strategy' was because the return forms preparation standard of Mr Fiorello was not satisfactory, and given that he was part of that practice and given that his clients were coming across as well, we believed there was a significant risk there and we needed to continue." In explanation of issues put to him in relation to the apparent high rate of incorrect returns lodged as against the average rate of incorrect returns, Mr Fiorello claimed that there was a lot of inconsistency within the ATO as to this audit assessment process.

18. Mr Pollock went on to explain that he and a tax officer visited the practice and advised Mr Romano and an associate that the ATO was not satisfied with their performance under the program. He stated:

"In fact, we had sent him a letter already saying we were not satisfied with the return form preparation standards of the firm, and it was our opinion that we should continue the strategy for another year unless they had reasons why we shouldn't and we obviously give them an opportunity to present their case as to why they in fact prepare incorrect returns, and why they shouldn't continue on the strategy. I held that interview, and I agreed at the time that rather than simply put them straight back on the strategy because there is some statistical analysis that goes on - and they weren't very happy that the statistical analysis was reflecting an accurate result - so I said to them that I would conduct 15 post issue audits on the clients, some of their clients who had lodged returns in 1995/96 and with the view that those audits would prove one way or another whether they are preparing correct returns or not. They agreed to this; they said that yes, that would be great if they could do that. ... I gave them the undertaking if those returns were satisfactory that we wouldn't continue with the program.

So we then proceeded with those audits. It turned out that one of the clients I think was overseas, so we discontinued that audit; we didn't pursue it. So we actually pursued 14 audits in relation to the strategy or the post issue side of it. Out of those 14 we adjusted 10 returns; three further returns were incorrect. However, due to the nature of the adjustments we decided to take no further action and one return was correct as lodged and on the basis of that we took the decision to continue with them on the strategy for 1997/98.

MR O'BRIEN: You said before that you have responsibility for the tax agency at Chermside, the ATO office, are you aware of anyone being on the program for as long as Mr Romano?---In Chermside? No, no-one has been on the program for that long. In fact in Chermside we have a practice of trying to assess whether an agent is preparing correct returns as quickly as possible for obvious reasons that it is affecting their business, and if they are preparing quick[sic] [correct] returns we want to get them off the `strategy' as soon as we possibly can. So even though they are on a `strategy' for a particular year, if part way through that year we are satisfied that they are preparing correct returns we will advise them that we no longer are requiring them to complete audits or participate on the program for that year. And in our case in Chermside, there are only two agents I believe who remain on the program, Fiorello Romano being one of those, and the other agent has only been on the `strategy' for two years so this is their second year. So and there is no-one in Chermside who has been on as long as Fiorello Romano or Fiorello if you include him as an individual.

MR O'BRIEN: Are you aware - what is the status at the moment, is Fiorello Romano still on the program?---They still are on the program as we speak. We have arranged an interview, I believe for Wednesday, to see them to discuss their progress ... and we will discuss how we think they are going on Wednesday."

19. I accept Mr Pollock's evidence that adjustment was made to 10 of the 14 returns of Mr Fiorello's clients. As a consequence the partnership remained on the Strategy for the 1997/98 year.

20. There is some conflict in the evidence between Mr Romano and Mr Pollock. Thus Mr Romano maintained that he was informed by a Miss J, a tax officer at the Chermside branch of the ATO that the partnership would be "coming off the program". Mr Pollock was adamant that no such decision had been taken. Indeed, he was the only person authorised to make such a decision, adding:

"I have spoken to Ms J and she advised me that she had made an appointment to see Mr Romano on Wednesday and she said that at that stage they would be discussing the progress on the strategy. I believe Mr Romano had asked the question whether that meant they were coming off the strategy and Ms J replied to me that that no decision had been taken."

21. Indeed, it transpired that there had been some discussion between Mr Pollock and Ms J's immediate superior. The result of this discussion was that Mr Pollock indicated that in his opinion "we could not release them from the strategy. Okay, that all we could possibly do was reduce our activity in relation to the amount of resources we are devoting to the audits ...".

22. To the extent that there is a conflict of evidence, I prefer the evidence of Mr Pollock. I accept that Mr Romano did not deliberately attempt to mislead me and that the conflict in the evidence is the result of a misunderstanding.

23. Mr Fiorello and Mr Romano claimed that Mr Fiorello had a good relationship with the Tax Office. Mr Pollock's evidence puts this in issue. It seems that the ATO received a number of complaints from clients of the practice. The following took place in evidence-in-chief:

"MR O'BRIEN: Mr Pollock, out of the taxpayers that you interviewed, were there common complaints and, if so, what were they?---The common complaint - and it went further than just the taxpayers I interviewed - was the lack of support that Mr Fiorello gave to these clients, because I must point out that most of these clients Mr Fiorello personally prepared their tax returns. And the first concern I had personally, and this is I suppose getting into line with the spirit of the Taxpayers Charter which we now have, is the fact that these clients were unrepresented at the interviews that took place between myself and [an]other taxation officer and these clients, and on each occasion we, of course, asked them, you know, why aren't you represented, why isn't your agent here, you know, because I personally believe they probably needed their agent there, and on every occasion they said the agent didn't want to be here, the agent was too busy and that's basically, you know - that struck me personally as showing some disregard for their client given that the - the precarious positions that they were in at the time.

THE D. PRESIDENT: ... Mr O'Brien, that should have been put to Mr Fiorello and it was not. Presumably you had a statement from this witness?

MR O'BRIEN: No, I did not, Deputy President. If I recall correctly I did ask Mr Fiorello whether there were any complaints made to the ATO about his conduct.

THE D. PRESIDENT: Yes. But what comes out now is that in conducting the usual audits, the practice is for the taxpayer to be called before the auditor and in my experience - and I have sat in this jurisdiction for more years that I can count - the tax agent, accountant or solicitor accompanies the taxpayer in an audit. On this occasion what you are telling me now is that on each of the occasions they were unrepresented and the general explanation was that Mr Fiorello refused to come.

MR POLLOCK: We gave them every opportunity to attend with their agent.

THE D. PRESIDENT: Yes. Well, I do not think you need to take it any further than that.

MR O'BRIEN: Yes."

24. I needn't dwell on the evidence of Mr Pollock. Suffice it that he convinced me that he is firmly of the view, after numerous interviews with Mr Fiorello, to quote from the witness that:

"on a number of occasions when he was confronted with a particular issue and said he had acted in a certain way that was not within the law and his response on one occasion was that he thought it was okay. So in relation to that I've got no faith that he can apply the law of tax in relation to preparing his client's returns. In relation to his representations for his clients, and on his client's behalf, he has always struck me as someone who really isn't interested in his clients. He has said on a number of occasions that if his clients are lying to him and, in fact, he's told us that's what he believes is happening, his clients are lying to him, he couldn't care less, and that's up to them, `and as far as I'm concerned, it's no business of mine and I couldn't care less about the taxpayers.' Now, in terms of my dealings with tax agents in the Chermside region, I've never come across an agent who has represented themselves that way. Whether an agent is bad or good in terms of the way they prepare returns, they always, as far as I have seen, have had their clients' interests at heart and I cannot say the same thing for Mr Fiorello. So in terms of my statement that he's bad, that's relative to all the other agents I've ever dealt with, it's based on all the evidence he's ever shown me.

MR O'BRIEN: When you say `it's bad', does that extend to a situation that you can rely on or ...? ---No, it gets down to the bottom. I can't. I could not trust Mr Fiorello in relation to - there have been numerous occasions when we have spoken to Mr Fiorello, sent letters to Mr Fiorello in terms of the practice, and he's let us down at the end of the day in terms of not complying with our requirements. There was a specific instance where one of my auditors interviewed him and asked him why he was not returning calls and why he was not cooperating with us, and he said that he was just too busy ... he was just not going to cooperate with us, which again, in relation to other agents is unusual. This auditor extended him the option of contacting the auditor up till 8 p.m. every single night. Mr Fiorello stated at the time: `That's great. That'd help me out no end' and less than a week after that he sent a fax saying, from now on everything between the tax office and him would be in writing."

25. Lest it be thought that there was "bad blood" between the witness and Mr Fiorello, colouring the former's evidence, the fax referred to above was made an exhibit (Ex 1). It is on the partnership's letterhead and is dated 15 August 1997, addressed to Mr Pollock and reads as follows:

"RE: Communications with Ms R

With reference to prior discussions with your department, we have requested that any queries, request etc. from your department be put to our office in writing.

...

Further, due to pressing commitments, we wish to cancel our scheduled appointment on 22 August 1997 at 2:00 pm and request that you contact this office to arrange an alternative time."

26. The applicant objected to the tender of that document on the basis of the rule in Browne v Dunn (1893) 6 R 67 as being a non-observance of procedural fairness. While the rules of evidence do not apply to the Tribunal, the rules of procedural fairness do, and in this regard the Browne v Dunn rule applies to the Tribunal as explained in Dolan v Australian and Overseas Telecommunications Corporation (1993) 114 ALR. Neither counsel for the applicant nor respondent had apparently seen the document before tender, let alone the applicants. Mr Logan considered that this fact alone did not remove the prejudice of introducing such evidence after cross-examination of his clients had concluded. As previously stated, the nature of the respondent's witnesses' evidence was not previously disclosed prior to the hearing as usually required under the Tribunal's Practice Directions. While in some respects this may come close to offending the rules of procedural fairness, these issues were raised when the respondent proposed to call its witnesses. Mr Logan objected at that point and reserved his client's position to what the respondent ostensibly referred to as the "rebuttal evidence" of its witnesses to the possibly mitigating evidence of the applicants. He made further objections to certain of Mr Pollock's evidence including his investigations as to 14 of the applicant's clients of which he interviewed six.

27. While there had been no opportunity to put this evidence to the applicants' taxpayer clients and the applicants, which Mr Logan contended went to issues of procedural fairness, I considered that Mr Pollock was not precluded from testifying as to his opinions and the outcomes of his investigations on conduct of the audit.

28. In accepting into evidence Exhibit 1 as received from Mr Pollock's files, Mr Logan was given the opportunity to recall his clients if he so wished. Instead, Mr Logan chose to make submissions as to the weight of the respondent's witnesses' evidence as he had indicated prior to the hearing of that evidence. As a result, I admitted the evidence of both Mr Pollock and Ms Campion.

29. On the basis of the evidence of the applicants, Mr Pollock's evidence and the fact that the ATO has since conducted some 52 audits on the partnership's clients of which 40 were "adjusted" and in some a 50% penalty for "recklessness" (90% in one case) imposed, I would have thought that a sufficient case had been made out in support of the Board's decision. The Board taking the view - which I share - that Mr Fiorello's conviction under s 251A constituted a "serious taxation offence" for the purposes of s 251BC(1).

30. However, as noted above, the respondent called additional evidence from Ms Campion, the current Secretary of the respondent Board to provide the Tribunal with some statistics, none of which assisted the applicant's case.

31. After setting out the procedure the Board adopts when receiving complaints by taxpayers about their agents, the witness went on to point out that there were some 62 formal complaints received by the Board in 1995, 42 in 1996, 41 in 1997 and 35 to date - a total of some 184 over a 3 year period. Of these there were "four formal complaints [involving the partnership] plus a number of telephone complaints or inquiries that have not been formalised because the person making the complaint has not followed it by sending it in writing." Since there are 3 thousand tax agents in Queensland, Ms Campion considered the number of complaints against one two-man practice was not insignificant.

32. Mr Fiorello was cross-examined about some of these complaints, naming individual taxpayers said to have been treated in a most unprofessional way. I find this evidence too inconclusive in their particularity to take any more notice of them other than to include them in the total picture, which may reveal a disproportionate number of complaints against this firm.

33. Returning to my oral decision, I noted that Mr Logan had said all that could be said in favour of his clients, adding that I was very mindful:

"that cancellation of a taxation licence is, indeed, a very serious step to take and registration should not be taken away without some very good reason. ... But in this case, I have no doubt at all on the whole of the evidence that in fact, I would say overwhelming evidence has satisfied me that Mr Fiorello is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters.

...

I was impressed by the evidence of Mr Pollock. Without entering into the niceties of the debate as to what the exact functions of a tax agent are and what, if any, responsibility he takes for the returns he prepares, there is in this case a not insignificant history of complaints about clients to both the Tax Office and the Board, to the point where these complaints amount to more than just statistical aberration. It appears to me that even at this stage the firm - and I take that predominantly as a result of Mr Fiorello's attitude - is still on what is called the Tax Audit Program, and not withstanding Mr Romano's statements that he is hopeful that next Wednesday the firm and its past history of infractions in terms of work related expenses, which apparently are claimed with little attempt made to ensure that they are legally allowable, to the point where this firm uniquely in Chermside is the only firm which has remained on the audit program since 1995 - maybe 1996.

So, on the whole of the evidence, I am satisfied that it would be quite wrong to set the decision aside. I am not satisfied that at this point in time Mr Fiorello is a fit and proper person to practise as a tax agent for the reasons I have outlined, and I affirm the decision under review."

34. This conclusion is based on the meaning and application of "fit and proper person" as established by the case law. In Tax Agents Board of Qld v Haddad (1994) 48 FCR 223 at p 226 the full Federal Court in reference to the rationale that must be applied to making this determination (under s 251JC(1)) said:

"When s 251JC(1) requires that the applicant be a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and to transact taxation business, it anticipates that a decision will be made having regard to the individual circumstances affecting the applicant and the applicant's practice and not by reference to a standard which the Board itself has chosen to lay down. The Act must be applied according to the words used. They are words which refer to relevant matters concerning the applicant and his or her practice. It is the individual circumstances which count."

35. The full High Court in Hughes and Vale Pty Ltd & Anor v the State of New South Wales & Ors (No 2) [1955] HCA 28; (1955) 93 CLR 127 at p 156 stated that the words "fit and proper" were words traditionally used in relation to persons holding offices or vocations:

"But their very purpose is to give the widest scope for judgment and indeed for rejection. `Fit' (or `idoneus') with respect to an office is said to involve three things, honesty knowledge and ability: `honesty to execute it truly, without malice affection or partiality; knowledge to know what he ought duly to do; and ability as well in estate as in body, that he may intend and execute his office, when need is, diligently, and not for impotency or poverty neglect it' - Coke."

36. Hill J in Stasos v Tax Agents' Board of NSW 90 ATC 4950 at 4959 cites the above comments, as well as those of Davies J in Re Su and Tax Agents' Board, SA 82 ATC 4284 at 4286 with approval to the extent that they refer to the functions of a tax agent and what constitutes a person as a fit and proper person vis--vis registration as a tax agent with approval, albeit his Honour considered the functions expressed by Davies as too narrow). The applicant sought to distinguish and rely in particular on Re Denton and the Tax Agents' Board of South Australia (1982) 13 ATR 192, Whatson and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland (1984) 13 ATR 940, Re Ziems [1957] HCA 46; [1957] ALR 620; (1957) 97 CLR 279 and Chapman and Tax Agents' Board of South Australia (1984) 15 ATR 691 which, he contended, supported the proposition that a failure with regard to personal affairs as opposed to professional affairs did not warrant striking off as a tax agent.

37. In all instances in this matter, Mr Fiorello had failed to lodge his own returns and those of the partnership (as distinct to those of clients) and was convicted of offences which appear to be the genesis of the Board's decision to exercise its discretion. If this were all that could be said against Mr Fiorello, there would be much in Mr Logan's submission that I should have regard to the authorities which view with sympathy the personal plight of tax agents where, apart from neglecting their own affairs, this has not "spilled over" into their professional commitments. However, while this may be so, I find the evidence of Mr Pollock in relation to the outcome of his audit investigations compelling and accept there was evidence of a considerable lack of proper standards with regards to clients' affairs. And while both Mr Fiorello and Mr Romano explained the former's personal circumstances in the relevant period, which I have accepted, these circumstances are not, in my opinion, sufficiently mitigating when considering the nature and severity of the offences, the continued failure to lodge returns and the apparent ongoing difficulties with meeting tax return standards as regards clients' affairs.

38. I should add a rider to the above. Having closely observed Mr Romano in the witness box, I am satisfied that he is the innocent victim of the partnership perhaps not initially being fully aware of the professional difficulties that had arisen as a result of Mr Fiorello's conduct as a tax agent. I have no hesitation in stating that there is nothing in the evidence which in any way taints Mr Romano's character or capacity to carry on the practice of tax agent. How he copes with Mr Fiorello's "disbarment" is a matter for him and Mr Fiorello, who, as pointed out before, can continue to practise as an accountant.
