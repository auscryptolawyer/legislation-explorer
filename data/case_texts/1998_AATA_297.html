John Darren Kerr and Tax Agent's Board of Queensland [1998] AATA 297 (20 March 1998)

Last Updated: 6 May 1998

Administrative

Appeals

Tribunal

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No Q97/1009

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re JOHN DARREN KERR

Applicant

And TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF QUEENSLAND

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date 30 March 1998

Place Brisbane

Decision The Tribunal decides that the decision under review is affirmed.

Decision No 12844 (Sgd) K L Beddoe

(Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

TAX AGENTS : Registration - substantial involvement in income tax matters - whether satisfies relevant employment test

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s 251BC

Regulations 156(1), 156(2)

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ss 29(7), 27A

Tax Agents' Board of Queensland v Seymour (1990) 21 ATR 200

REASONS FOR DECISION

30 March 1998 Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

1. At a meeting of the respondent Board on 29 July 1997 it decided to refuse the applicant's registration as a tax agent. That decision was notified to the applicant on 6 August 1997. He says he received it on 15 August 1997. On 3 October 1997 the applicant sought review in this Tribunal. Following the submission of further material by the applicant after a section 34 conference in this Tribunal the respondent Board reaffirmed its decision to refuse registration at its meeting on 23 January 1998 (Exhibit A). No issue arose before me as to whether the application for review was within time. As the application was not lodged in accordance with s 29 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act ") and the respondent takes no issue I will treat the application as having been lodged in time by extending time in accordance with s 29(7). In that regard I note that the respondent Board failed to comply with s 27A of the AAT Act in its letter of 16 August 1997 (T17).

2. Section 251BC(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") reads as follows:

"251BC(1) Without limiting the generality of an expression used in this Part, but subject to this section, a person is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters, as at a particular time, if:

(a) the person is not a natural person;

(b) both of the following conditions are satisfied:

(i) the person was not registered as a tax agent, or as a nominee, for the purposes of this Part immediately before the commencement of section 39 of the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No. 2) 1988;

(ii) the person does not hold such qualifications (whether academic, by way of experience or otherwise) as are prescribed:

(c) the person has not attained the age of 18 years;

(d) the person is not of good fame, integrity and character;

(e) the person has been convicted of a serious taxation offence during the previous 5 years; or

(f) the person is under sentence of imprisonment for a serious taxation offence."

3. For the purposes of s 251BC(1)(b)(ii) Regulation 156(1) prescribes the qualifications as follows:

"156(1) For the purposes of subparagraph 251BC(1)(b)(ii) of the Act, the following qualifications are prescribed:

(a) the person:

(i) shall have completed the academic requirements for the award of a degree, diploma or other qualification from an Australian university, college of advanced education or other tertiary institution of an equivalent standard, and have passed examinations in such subjects, under whatever name, which an appropriate authority of the university, college of advanced education or other tertiary institution certifies to the Board to represent a course of study in accountancy of not less than 3 years' duration and in commercial law of not less than 18 months' duration or shall possess such other qualifications as the Board regards as equivalent to those qualifications;

(ii) shall have:

(a) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding 5 years;

(b) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent that the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in sub-paragraph (A); or

(c) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as the Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant employment as referred to in sub-paragraph (A); and

(iii) shall have, by written examination, successfully completed a course of study in Australian income tax law acceptable to the Board; "

4. For the purposes of the Regulation "relevant employment" is defined in Regulation 156(2) as follows:

"156(2) In this regulation `relevant employment' means employment by a person or a partnership or as a member of a partnership in the course of which there has been substantial involvement in income tax matters including:

(a) the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns;

(b) the preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued in respect of such returns; and

(c) the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns, assessments or objections. "

6. For the purposes of the Regulations "income tax" is not specifically defined but is defined in s 6(1) of the Act to mean income tax imposed as such by any Act as assessed under the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 .

7. At the hearing the applicant conducted his own case and Mr O'Brien appeared for the respondent Board. Oral evidence was given by the applicant. The documents filed pursuant to s 37 of the AAT Act were before the Tribunal as the T documents and further documents were tendered and marked as exhibits.

8. The applicant holds a Bachelor of Commerce degree from the University of Melbourne graduating in 1990, completed the professional year for admission to the Institute of Chartered Accountants and was admitted as a member of the Institute in 1994. He achieved a third class honours pass in Taxation Law at the University and completed a course of study in Australian income tax law in 1991 during the professional year studies.

9. He was employed by Daikyo (NQ) Pty Ltd from 18 March 1991 to 20 September 1995. From 23 September 1995 to the date of hearing he has been employed by F. R. Ireland Pty Ltd trading as "Irelands of Cairns". It was not asserted before me that either company was a registered tax agent.

10. Irelands of Cairns is a large motor vehicles dealership with extensive business interests. The applicant is employed as the financial accountant for the company and associated entities including family trusts.

11. The applicant is also a partner in a firm trading as Arrow Taxation Services. The other partner is Darin John Draper who completed a standard form statement of relevant employment submitted to the respondent (T8). That statement said that the applicant was an employee at Arrow Taxation Services on a part time basis from 1 July 1995 to 30 June 1996 and was a partner in the firm from 1 July 1996. The statement also set out details of preparation/examination of income tax returns by the applicant during the financial years ended 30 June 1996 and 30 June 1997. Those details are:

Return type Year Ended Year Ended

30 June 1996 30 June 1997

Individuals 100 300

Partnerships 20 30

Companies 1 1

Trusts 1 1

Superannuation Funds - -

122 332

In his evidence the applicant conceded that the "30" partnership returns for 1997 should read "17".

12. It became apparent during the hearing that the figures submitted by Mr Draper were merely estimates. The figures could not be reconciled with figures supplied to the respondent by the Australian Taxation Office (Exhibit 1). However, the figures supplied by the Australian Taxation Office are based on a year of income analysis rather than by year of preparation and are therefore not comparable with the year of preparation basis required by the respondent Board in its standard form statement. My basis for coming to this conclusion is that the details supplied to the respondent by the Australian Taxation Office in mid 1997 included that there were no lodgments for 1997. However, in Exhibit 1 which seems to represent details as at March 1998 the 1997 lodgments are recorded as 97 individuals and 4 partnerships, ie, returns lodged in the 1998 year.

13. On the material before me it would be reasonable to infer that Mr Draper's estimates are on the high side of reality but I am unable to come to any firm conclusion about that. To accept Mr Draper's figures I would have to infer that the applicant prepared all returns lodged by Arrow Taxation Service. That may well be the case but I have no basis for being satisfied that it is the case.

14. It may well be that the Australian Taxation Office figures in Exhibit 1 do not include all lodgments Australia wide by Arrow Taxation Services. I simply do not know and counsel was unable to take the matter any further.

15. For the purpose of making a decision in this matter I am prepared to infer that the estimated figures supplied by Mr Draper are a reasonable estimate in the circumstances. I might add that it is not unreasonable, in my view, that an accountant should be in a position to supply accurate details of lodgments of income tax returns lodged by the accountant's firm. In that regard I note that the firm was not registered as a tax agent until 19 November 1996.

16. The applicant gave evidence about the taxation aspects of his work in his full time employment as Accountant at Irelands. He said that the group lodged 22 income tax returns for companies and trusts. The principal business has a very high turnover having been conducted previously by a trustee on behalf of a trust. The business is now conducted through a company with aspects of the overall business conducted by associated entitles.

17. The applicant's undisputed evidence is that he does the tax accounting work for the Irelands group as part of his duties as the Accountant. He does not, however, do the final stage of lodgment of the income tax returns. That is done by a firm of chartered accountants who are said to specialise in motor trade matters.

18. The Chartered Accountants require the applicant to complete a computerised package of information necessary to complete the group's taxation returns. I am satisfied that in completing that package of information the applicant is undertaking the tax accounting work which is the essential first step in preparation of income tax returns. I accept as a matter of general knowledge that financial accounting and management accounting are but steps in accounting to comply with the income tax laws. There are many reasons, well known to the accountants and lawyers, as to why the financial accounts of a business prepared on a proper basis may require many and significant adjustments to prepare a correct income tax return - hence the need for specialist tax accounting.

19. The applicant's tax accounting work in his employment was supervised and checked by the firm of chartered accountants who then prepared taxation returns for the various entities. The applicant did not suggest that he participates in the final preparation of the returns, that is the responsibility of the firm of chartered accountants.

20. The applicant submitted that the Tribunal should take both his work with Arrow Taxation Service and his accounting work on preparation of taxation returns at Irelands into account in determining whether he satisfies the definition of "relevant employment".

21. The issue in this application is confined to whether the applicant's employment and his membership of the partnership trading as Arrow Taxation Services brings him within paragraph 156(1)(a) of the Regulations. In particular whether the applicant has:

"(A) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding 5 years;

(B) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent that the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in sub-paragraph (a); or

(C) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as the Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant employment as referred to in sub-paragraph (A)."

That will depend on whether the applicant has had a substantial involvement in income tax matters including the three areas set out in the definition of "relevant employment" in Regulation 156(2).

22. As to whether the applicant has had a substantial involvement in income tax matters I take into account the applicant's experience in relation to Arrow Taxation Services including his time as a partner. That the latter is to be included was made clear by Pincus J in Tax Agents' Board of Queensland v Seymour (1990) 21 ATR 200 at 204 where his Honour stated that it is plain that a partner in a firm holding registration as a tax agent may qualify as to experience albeit that the partner acts under the supervision and control of the registered nominee. Seymour is authority for the proposition that substantial involvement includes experience while self employed as a partner in a firm registered as a tax agent but does not include experience in self employment per se.

23. The test looks to involvement in income tax matters including the three categories set out in Regulation 156(2). Nothing is said in the Regulation as to how to make an objective assessment as to what is substantial involvement in income tax matters. It was put by the respondent that involvement in income tax matters included fringe benefits tax and superannuation matters. In response to the Tribunal's question the respondent says that it does not include matters relating to sales tax but no explanation was given as to why the distinction. The matter was not properly argued before me but I am not satisfied that the Board is correct in its submission that both fringe benefits tax matters and superannuation matters are characteristic of the generic class "income tax matters".

24. In this regard I note that the application for registration (T8) seeks information about five categories of returns involving preparation/examination by the applicant. I include the applicant's details as to these categories. They are:

1996 1997

Individuals 100 300

Partnership 20 30

Companies 1 1

Trusts 1 1

Superannuation Funds - -

In that context I would have come to the view that the reference to superannuation funds must be confined to the preparation of returns, objections etcetera for income tax purposes. In so far as the respondent sought to rely on a broader view in relation to the administration of superannuation funds I reject that view. I also reject the submission that experience with fringe benefits tax comes within the phrase "income tax matters". It is a different tax levied under different legislation.

25. What is substantial involvement in income tax matters?

26. In Re Seymour I considered the effect of the qualifying adjective "substantial" at paragraph 23 of the reasons as follows:

"23. The word `substantial' is notorious for its lack of precision of meaning. It is quantitatively imprecise with the result that no specific level of involvement is defined by so qualifying `involvement'. In Tillmans Butcheries Pty Ltd v A.M.I.E.U. & Ors (1979) 27 A.L.R. 367 Deane J referred to the difficulties inherent in the use of `substantial' in these terms at p. 382:

`the word "substantial" is not only susceptible of ambiguity: it is a word calculated to conceal a lack of precision. In the phrase "substantial loss or damage", it can, in an appropriate context, mean real or of substance as distinct from ephemeral or nominal. It can also mean large, weighty or big. It can be used in a relative sense or can indicate an absolute significance, quantity or size. The difficulties and uncertainties which the use of the word is liable to cause are well illustrated by the guidance given by Viscount Simon in Palser v. Grinling ([1948] 1 All ER 1 at 11; [1948] AC 291 at 317) where, after holding that, in the context there under consideration, the meaning of the word was equivalent to "considerable, solid or big", he said: "Applying the word in this sense, it must be left to the discretion of the judge of fact to decide as best he can according to the circumstances of each case ... `."

27. There was considerable confusion before me as to the number of income tax returns prepared and lodged by Arrow Taxation Services. On any view the number of returns was many more than "a few" or "insignificant number". As already noted I do not have any objective basis for quantifying the number of returns that will constitute substantial involvement. Further it would be unreasonable to apply a test depending on numbers only. Substantial involvement will depend upon factors such as nature of the taxpayers' income producing activities, the size of the income producing operations and other factors, together with actual numbers. There could be substantial involvement in income tax matters but a low number of returns.

28. In the present case I am satisfied that most of the income tax returns prepared by Arrow Taxation Services were in fact prepared by the applicant and were relatively uncomplicated individual returns.

29. The respondent sought to discount the applicant's involvement in the preparation of income tax returns in the course of his employment with Irelands. The Regulation talks of substantial involvement in income tax matters. That, in my view, must include preparation of income tax returns from the beginning and not just the actual completion of the returns. Given the present system of electronic lodgment of income tax returns it is the preparation work that is vital to the insertion of correct data into the program for lodgment of the return..

30. That was in essence what the applicant did in his employment when he worked on preparation of his employer's income tax returns. It was not suggested that this work constituted a major portion of his duties. He prepared information and input that information into a program prepared by the group's tax agents.

31. I am satisfied that the applicant's employment has a limited relevance in deciding whether the applicant has had a substantial involvement in income tax matters.

32. Taking all the material before me into account I am satisfied that the applicant has had a substantial involvement in income tax matters, and I so find.

33. Does that substantial involvement include the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns? The Regulations do not prescribe what is "a broad range of income tax returns". It has generally been accepted that it refers to at least the different categories of income tax returns as discussed at paragraph 24 above. As to what is a typical profile of returns prepared by a tax agent, I do not know. It might be reasonable to presume that a tax agent in North Queensland may have a quite different profile of returns prepared to a tax agent carrying on business in the central business district of Sydney or Melbourne.

34. However, I am not satisfied that the applicant's substantial involvement includes the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns and objections to assessments issued in respect of these returns. The clear predominance is individual and partnership returns through Arrow Taxation Services. To this must be added one company return and one trust return together with the preparation work in his employment with Irelands.

35. While I am satisfied that constitutes substantial involvement it does not meet the inclusory tests in Regulation 156(2) because there is not a broad range of income tax returns in that substantial involvement.

36. For these reasons the decision under review must be affirmed.

37. I should add that I formed the impression during the hearing that the applicant has the qualities necessary to be a good tax agent. However, he lacks the experience necessary to bring him within the terms of Regulation 156(2), which is an onerous test as to experience. I say it is an onerous test because I doubt that many presently registered tax agents could fulfil the requirements of the subregulation.
