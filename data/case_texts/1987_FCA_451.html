Re Giacomo Rizzi v the Commissioner of Taxation [1987] FCA 451 (10 December 1987)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: GIACOMO RIZZI

And: THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. WAG 2090 of 1987

Income Tax

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

WESTERN AUSTRALIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Forster J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - claims for the deduction of rates and taxes levied on a dwelling rejected as the payment was of a private, domestic or capital nature - deduction of motor vehicle expenses partially allowed by the Commissioner - claim for the deduction of the balance rejected as there was no nexus with the earning of assessable income - claim for the deduction of self education expenses rejected as there was no perceived connection between the outgoing and the earning of assessable income.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936

Jurisdiction of Courts (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 1987

HEARING

PERTH

10:12:1987

Counsel for the applicant : The applicant appeared in person

Counsel for the respondents : Mr C.J. Windsor

Solicitor for the respondents : Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The appeal be dismissed with costs.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

This is an objection against an assessment under the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 transmitted in the first instance to the Supreme Court of Western Australia on 17 July 1987 and treated as an appeal pursuant to the written request of the applicant dated 3 September 1983. Pursuant to the Jurisdiction of Courts (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 1987 the appeal was transferred to this Court.

2. At the end of the hearing I dismissed the appeal with costs and said that I would give reasons later which I now do.

3. The applicant's objection related to the assessment of tax upon his income for the financial year 1981-1982. He had three sources of taxable income. First he worked for the State Electricity Commission from 1 July 1981 to 13 November 1981 for which he earned $4,532. Second he was a partner in a firm E. & V. Rizzi and Sons who were builders with respect to which he earned $967 and third he was a partner in another firm E. & V. Rizzi and Family who were flat owners with respect to which he earned $771. Both of these partnerships carried on business at Bunbury.

4. The applicant claimed a deduction of $1,033 for the expense of the use of his motor vehicle which was disallowed to the extent of $633 which is to say he was allowed a deduction of $400. He also claimed to deduct $703 rates and taxes on the house where he lived which was the full amount of such rates and taxes and was entirely disallowed. He also claimed to deduct $455 under the heading "school" which was for self education expenses in connection with a course undertaken at Leederville Technical College. This claim was also rejected in its entirety.

5. Before dealing in detail with the three aspects of the applicant's appeal it is necessary to describe the activities of the applicant during the relevant tax year and his use of the house in which he resided and with respect to which he paid rates.

6. During the relevant year the applicant lived in a dwelling in Karrinyup a suburb of Perth of which the ground floor was set aside by him as a residence and the first floor he said was devoted to study and to work for the building partnership of E. & V. Rizzi and Sons.

7. The first floor consisted of bedroom number one, bedroom number two, a study, a bar room, a living room and a bathroom. The applicant said that he used bedroom number one as a store-room which contained two filing cabinets one of which had all the applicant's "estimating and pricing and everything in it" and the other of which had plans in it. There were also in this room what the applicant described somewhat imprecisely as "a few bits and pieces". In bedroom number two there were a drawing board and bookshelves containing books and another plan filing cabinet. In the study there were a desk and it is said two more filing cabinets. In the bar room there was nothing. In the living room there were a sofa and a television set. In the bathroom there were a shower, a lavatory and a wash-basin. The applicant claimed that he used the whole of the first floor for the purpose of earning income and for the purpose of studying. He contends that if he is allowed a deduction for his expenses of self education so much of the use of this floor as is for the purpose of study should be considered as a basis for a deduction of rates and taxes.

8. So far as the applicant's car is concerned he said that he used it to drive to Bunbury on some twelve or thirteen occasions during the year to look at jobs being done by the building partnership and to inspect the flats owned by the other partnership.

9. So far as the self education is concerned the applicant said that during the relevant year he started on a course of architectural draughting in order as he said to "better himself". He later gave up this course at least temporarily and has not yet completed it.

10. I deal now with the applicant's three claims.

11. There are a number of answers to the applicant's claims to deduct the rates and taxes for the whole building. In the first place if any deduction were to be allowed with respect to the rates and taxes levied on the building such deduction would have to be of a proportion only of the rates and taxes since only a proportion of the building was said to be used for the generation of income. I understood the applicant to concede this as a matter of principle.

12. In the second place during the whole financial year in question the sales of the building partnership amounted to only $10,350. The applicant gave no evidence of tenders which were not accepted nor work otherwise done by him for the partnership which did not result in any work for the partnership. It must be assumed therefore that only a very small amount of work was done by the partnership in the relevant year. Even if the applicant did the necessary estimating and pricing with respect to this very small amount of work and did it in his study on the first floor of the dwelling, the amount of use of this room and any other room on the first floor must have been minute. This use was completely inadequate to alter the essential character of the dwelling and its use was domestic or private and the payment of rates and taxes was of a capital private or domestic nature and therefore not deductible (see for instance Handley v. Federal Commission of Taxation (1981) 34 ALR 274). The applicant's claim for this deduction must be rejected.

13. So far as the car is concerned the applicant kept complete records of expenditure on petrol, oil, repairs, registration, etc. These totalled $1,033. The Commissioner allowed a deduction of $400 apparently with respect to the twelve or thirteen trips to Bunbury undertaken by the applicant. Bunbury is 175 kilometres south of Perth by national highway number one. The applicant admitted that he used these trips to Bunbury in order to see his family who live there as well as looking at the income producing flat building. It seems to me that the allowance by the Commissioner of $400 is a generous one and I cannot find that the applicant is entitled to any more under this head.

14. So far as the applicant's further education was concerned it was undertaken as I have said "to better himself" according to the applicant. This laudable wish to better himself was not directed to any particular opportunity to earn assessable income and was not even directed to any theoretically available source of assessable income. At most it could be said that if the applicant passed the course of study on which he was embarked he might earn more by way of assessable income provided he could get work.

15. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Hatchett [1971] HCA 47; (1971) 125 CLR 494 at 499 Menzies J. said :

"As I have said, I am not able to find any

connexion between the payment of fees and the

assessable income of the taxpayer beyond the

circumstance, which I take to be self-evident,

that a teacher who has pursued university studies

is likely to be a better teacher than if he had

not done so and is therefore more likely to obtain

promotion within the department. In my opinion

this general consideration is not enough to make

the fees deductible; there must be a perceived

connexion between the outgoing and assessable

income."

In the present case there is in my view no perceived connection between the outgoing and assessable income.

16. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. White (1975) 75 ATC 4018 at 4022 Helsham J. said :

"As the result of the decision in the two cases it

seems to me possible to say that expenses incurred

in pursuing studies associated with employment

will qualify as allowable deductions under sec.51

when it can be said that those studies are part

and parcel of the employment, which means that the

expenditure is incurred in the process of carrying

out the employee's duties, or, even if they are

not such, they can be seen to have a direct effect

on income. Where that cannot be seen, I think it

will be difficult to establish the necessary

connection between the study activity and the

employment so as to give study expenses the

character of outgoings incurred in gaining or

producing the income derived from the employment."

17. In the present case the applicant's expenses incurred in undertaking the course at Leederville Technical College were not so incurred in pursuing studies associated with employment (or with the building partnership) nor were they part and parcel of the business of the partnership nor could they be seen to have a direct effect on income either present or future.

18. For these reasons the third claim with respect to education expenses must be rejected and for all the above reasons the applicant's appeal was dismissed with costs.
