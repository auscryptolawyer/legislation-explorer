NT86/9343 and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 67; (1988) 19 ATR 3299; 88 ATC 363 (19 February 1988)

Last Updated: 24 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - assessable income - "profit" derived on maturity of bill of exchange - bill purchased at discount – whether "amount received on redemption of "eligible securities" - whether assessable pursuant to s.25 or 3.26(a)- or S.26AAA or s.26C - whether not exempt by reason of s.23J(2) – whether exempt by reason of s.23J (1).

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - ss. 23J, 25, 26(a), 26AAA and 26C;

Bills of Exchange Act 1909 - ss. 14 and 22.

Eisner v. Macomber [1919] USSC 119; (1919) 252 U.S. 189;

F.C.T. v. D.P. Smith [1981] HCA 10; (1981) 147 C.L.R. 578;

Steinberg v. F.C. of T. [1975] HCA 63; (1975) 134 C.L.R. 640.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT86/9343

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

19 February 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. This reference involves what the Applicant's representative referred to as "a small matter": small that is in the context of the affairs of the Applicant-company. The issue is whether the taxable income of the Applicant for the year of income ended 30 June 1983 should be fixed at $91,157 or increased by $8,846 to $100,003 by reason of "realized profit on redemption of bills (sic) of exchange included as assessable income". In previous years, and again in later years, the Applicant acknowledged that any such gains should be brought to account in determining taxable income. However, the Applicant, by its accountants, contends that that is not so in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1983 by reason of the operation in that year of s.23J of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). I was advised in the course of the hearing that that Section has not previously been considered by any Court or tribunal.
  2. Recognizing the "one-off" nature of the issue and being concerned not to allow the costs of the argument to become unreasonably high, the representative of the Applicant who appeared before me determined to call no witnesses and, instead, to seek to establish his case by relying only on the production of documents and on admissions from the Commissioner.
  3. The Applicant's representative, despite all his professional education and practical experience with a major firm, stood in a similar position to most of his colleagues in the accounting profession: including many with a greater length of service in the profession. He had had no experience of tax litigation either as an observer or as an advocate. The Commissioner, on the other hand, was represented by one of his officers who, in the service of the Commissioner, had gained a reasonable measure of experience in such matters. With the assistance of each representative, both of whom co-operated in an endeavour to ensure the due determination of the reference while each upholding the position of the party represented, the hearing proceeded. However, in that regard matters were somewhat unsatisfactory. The attempt to bypass the need to establish, either by evidence or admission. all the matters of fact which needed to be established led to confusion and difficulty. That was not less because of the novelty of the issue and the law to be applied.
  4. The income tax returns of the Applicant-company for the years of income ended 30 June 1978 to 1986 were presented in evidence and, by consent, accepted as disclosing accurately the financial transactions of the Applicant in that period. I accepted them on a limited basis. It was not conceded on behalf of the Commissioner that assertions contained in those returns as to the absence of any profit-making purpose were true.
  5. Those returns showed that the issued capital of the company amounted to $1,006 and that shareholders' funds rose during the period from $1,070,000 to $1,470,000. Throughout the period the principal assets of the company were comprised in a portfolio of investments which was dominated by shares, but also included investments in property trusts, mortgage loans, other unsecured loans and, on occasion, "bills receivable".
  6. The investment portfolio of the Applicant was in large measure, if not wholly, managed on its behalf by Wales Management Pty. Limited. No evidence was placed before me as to the terms or scope of that management arrangement. I do not consider the terms of such arrangements between a trading bank's customers and the "Management" subsidiary of the trading bank to be so notorious that I could take "judicial notice" of them. That being so, in the absence of evidence, I am not prepared to make any finding as to the terms of that arrangement such as would result in findings of fact favoured by the Applicant.
  7. I do find that in the exercise of its management powers Wales Management bought and sold shares from time to time and did so quite frequently. For instance, at 30 June 1978, the Applicant's portfolio included shares in thirty-three listed companies which had been acquired at a cost of $443,000. In the previous twelve months shares in seven companies had been sold. I do not propose to carry out the task of analysing the information in the other returns to enable me to present a detailed history of share purchases and sales such as might have been conveniently presented in summary form. It suffices for present purposes to say that sales were quite frequently made.

8. I find that from time to time Wales Management, on behalf of the Applicant, invested in bank-endorsed bills of exchange and that those investments were made on behalf of the Applicant in due exercise of the authority of Wales Management to invest. I also find that the powers so exercised were exercised in the interests of the Applicant and with the intended effect of occasioning an improvement in the financial position of the Applicant.

9. The Applicant, but for the matter presently in dispute, accepted in relation to those "bill" transactions that the "profit" (that is, the amount by which the face value of the bill received in full upon its maturity exceeded the cost to the Applicant in acquiring that bill) constituted assessable income. There is no issue as to that to be resolved. On the other hand, it is common ground that the Commissioner in fact accepted that, with the exception of instances to which the provisions of S.26AAA of the Act applied, the "profit" on the sale of shares effected by Wales Management for the Applicant was not assessable.

10. The Applicant contends that the gain on the bill which was realized when it matured and was paid in the 1983 year of income was not assessable. That is said to be because of s.23J. That Section provides as follows:-

"23J. (1) Subject to this section, no part of an amount received by a person upon the sale or redemption of eligible securities purchased or otherwise acquired at a discount on or before 30 June 1982, other than any part of that amount received as accrued interest, shall, for any purpose of this Act, be taken to be income derived by the person.

(2) Sub-section (1) does not apply in relation to an amount received by a person by virtue of a transaction that is part of, or is incidental to, the carrying on by the person of a business that includes buying and selling eligible securities of any kind.

(3) Sub-section (1) does not affect the operation of paragraph 26(a), section 26AAA or section 26C.

(4) In this section, 'eligible securities' means--
  • (a) bonds, debentures, stock or other securities; and
  • (b) any other document evidencing or acknowledging the indebtedness of a person, whether or not the debt is secured."

11. In order to succeed in its application it is necessary for the Applicant to establish:-

(A) that the bill in question fell within the concept of "eligible securities" [sub-section (4)];

(B) that the bill was purchased on or before 30 June 1982 [sub-section (1)];

(C) that the amount in question was part of an amount received upon the "redemption" of the bill [sub-section (1)];

(D) that no part of the amount was received as "accrued interest" [sub-section (1)];

(E) that the "profit" was not assessable as a "profit arising from the sale by the taxpayer of any property acquired by him for the purpose of profit-making by sale..." [sub-section (3) cf. paragraph 26(a) of the Act];

(F) that the "profit" was not assessable as a "profit arising...from the carrying on or carrying out of any profit-making undertaking or scheme" [sub-section (3) cf. paragraph 26(a) of the Act];

(G) that the profit was not assessable pursuant to S.26AAA of the Act [sub-section (3)];

(H) that the profit was not assessable pursuant to S.26C [sub-section (3)];

(I) hat the moneys in question were not received "...by virtue of a transaction that is part of, or is incidental to, the carrying on by the person of a business that includes buying and selling eligible securities of any kind" [sub-section (2)].

I shall consider those propositions seriatim.

12. Before embarking upon a consideration of those particular elements of the claim, it is appropriate to observe that S.23J of the Act does not provide in express terms that a "profit" such as arises upon the maturity of an asset acquired for a sum less than its maturity value constitutes assessable income. Whether such a "profit" is assessable depends upon other provisions of the Act than s.23J and, in particular, the concept of "income" which finds expression in s.25 of the Act. It might well be thought that such a profit is "...not a gain accruing to capital, not a growth or increment of value in the investment; but a gain, a profit, something of exchangeable value proceeding from the property, severed from the capital...": the classic words of Mr. Justice Pitney of the Supreme Court of the United States of America in Eisner v. Macomber [1919] USSC 119; (1919) 252 U.S. 189. If so, it may be that a "profit" such as this did not constitute assessable income by reason of s.25 of the Act. It may be the correct construction of s.25 of the Act only requires that such a "profit" be considered as assessable income if the person deriving the profit is engaged in dealing in such assets on such a scale as to cause the "profit" to be seen as an instance of recurrent profit generated by activities regularly or frequently undertaken with a view to profit. Other sections, such as 26(a), 26AAA and 26C - all of which can extend the basic concept of income and result in what would otherwise be considered capital receipts being treated as assessable income might cause such a profit as this to be treated as assessable income. Whether any particular profit should be so treated depends upon the provisions of those sections.

13. However, in the circumstances of this case as it was presented before me, it is neither necessary nor appropriate to consider those matters. That is because it was expressly conceded for the Applicant that, but for the enactment of s.23J of the Act, the profit in question constituted assessable income. Accordingly, I confine my attention to the questions stated in paragraph 11.

14. Whether the bill was an eligible security? I am satisfied that it was. Paragraph (b) of sub-section 4 extends the concept expressed in paragraph (a) of that sub-section. Paragraph (a) dealt with (inter alia) bonds, debentures and other securities. Paragraph (b) extended that concept to "any other document evidencing or acknowledging the indebtedness of a person". By doing so in those terms it lends support to the view that the securities referred to in paragraph (a) are all "securities" evidencing or acknowledging indebtedness. A bill of exchange, once "accepted", in the sense contemplated by the Bills of Exchange Act 1909 (s.22), by a person upon whom it is "drawn" is very much evidence of acknowledgment of liability to pay "a sum certain" with or without interest (s.14). I am satisfied that for those reasons the bill in question constituted an eligible security.

15. Whether the bill was purchased on or before 30 June 1982?

It was common ground between the parties that the bill was so purchased.

16. Whether the money in question was part of an amount received upon the "redemption" of the bill?

I am satisfied that the term "redemption" is not to be confined, as the Commissioner's representative suggested, to situations in which the acceptor of a bill satisfies the bill prior to the due date. I find that the acceptance by the holder of the bill of its face value upon its maturity and delivery up of the bill constituted its "redemption".

17. Whether any part of the amount so received was "accrued interest"?

By some bills of exchange an acceptor undertakes to pay not only a fixed or determinable principal sum, but also an additional amount by way of interest, usually related to the firstmentioned sum and measured over a period of time. In traditional terms, interest is that recurrent element in a transaction which has the character of income and which is to be distinguished from the principal which is the basis for generating that income. In the image expressed by Pitney, J. in Eisner v. Macomber (ante), the principal is the fruit-bearing tree: and interest is the recurrent crop which it bears [cf. F.C.T. v. P.P. Smith [1981] HCA 10; (1981) 147 C.L.R. 578 at p.583]. In the circumstances of this case I am satisfied that there was no provision for an interest factor in the terms of the bill and that, the bill being duly paid on its maturity, there was no entitlement to or derivation of interest such as might have constituted assessable income by way of interest.

18. Whether there was a "profit arising" from the sale by the taxpayer of any property acquired by him for the purpose of profit-making by sale?

It was conceded that "redemption" did not constitute a "sale" and, accordingly, that there is no scope for the application of the first limb of s. 26(a) of the Act. I agree.

19. Whether the profit in question arose "from the carrying on or carrying out of any profit-making undertaking or scheme"?

I find no basis for the application of the second limb of s.26(a) of the Act. As Stephen, J. said in Steinberg v. F.C. of T. [1975] HCA 63; (1975) 134 C.L.R. 640 at 714-5:-

"An undertaking or scheme must involve a program or plan of action...; it presupposes... activities which are co-ordinated by plan and purpose (although) it may involve no more than a settled purpose on the part of those concerned to turn their purchase to profitable account as best they can when they got possession, depending on how the undertaking then developed. It may involve alternative plans, different routes to the desired profitable end, the selection of one route rather than another being left for determination by future events."

It may of course also involve the implementation without variation of a pre-determined plan. However, what happened in this instance was merely that a bill was acquired by negotiation for a price; it was held to maturity; and upon maturity the holder received the amount due on the bill from its acceptor in discharge of his obligations under the bill. In my view that is not enough to constitute an "undertaking or scheme".

20. Whether the provisions of S.26AAA apply?

It is agreed that there is no scope in the circumstances for the application of that section.

21. Whether the provisions of s.26C apply?

It is agreed that there is no scope in the circumstances for the application of that section.

22. Whether the moneys were received "...by virtue of a transaction that is part of, or is incidental to, the carrying on by the person of a business that includes buying and selling eligible securities of any kind"?

It is upon this point at least that the cause of the Applicant fails. I simply have no evidence before me which would enable me to make findings of fact which would enable me to say that the Applicant had established the existence of circumstances outside the scope of sub-section (2). I have no evidence as to the arrangements which existed between the Applicant and Wales Management. I do not know to what extent, if any, the Applicant exercised supervision over or gave directions as to the buying and selling activity of Wales Management. I do not know whether the portfolio controlled by Wales Management was special to the Applicant. I do not know how Wales Management went about regulating its buying and selling activities or about the review of its portfolio, whether on a daily or other periodic basis, and whether or not regularly. The representatives for the Applicant and the Commissioner sought to overcome some of these difficulties by making admissions but one problem in that regard was that the matters sought to be agreed were not simply matters of fact. One major difficulty was that they endeavoured to express their agreement by reference to the term "business" as used in s.23J. That resulted in formulations such as "the business is one of investment and includes the buying and selling of eligible securities but that buying and selling of eligible securities is not the business". If anything, I would have thought that such a finding by the Tribunal would have exposed the Applicant to tax on the profit if, in the circumstances, s.25 would have constituted the profit as assessable income. However, that is a question I cannot address because of the concession made in that regard (cf pa 13). That resulted in the case being conducted on the basis that neither side addressed argument on that s.25 question.

GENERALLY

23. It was argued for the Applicant that to give too broad an interpretation of sub-section (2) would be to negate the purpose of enacting the section. In response, the Commissioner's representative tendered an extract from the Second Reading Speech of the Honourable the Treasurer, which said:-

"Earlier this year, the Commissioner of Taxation publicly affirmed the view that profits arising from the sale or redemption at par of securities issued or acquired at a discount are assessable income.

It is accepted by the Government, however, that a significant number of small investors had purchased discounted securities in the bona fide belief that the profit on sale or redemption would not be assessable."

The former paragraph confirms the view taken by the Commissioner even though the legislation did not expressly confirm that view as accurate or seek to make it so. The latter paragraph states the problem which prompted the legislative enactment. However, the reference to "small investors" gives to the statement a political complexion which is of little assistance when it comes to interpreting the legislation. As I read the section, it makes no distinction between large and small investors. Further, the statement related to all who had "purchased discounted securities in the bona fide belief that the profit on sale or redemption would not be assessable". As the evidence stands before me, I have no basis for finding whether the Applicant held such a "bona fide belief". However, that would not seem to be a matter of any moment because the operation of s.23J is not by its terms conditioned upon the existence of such a belief.

24. In all the circumstances, the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection will be affirmed.
