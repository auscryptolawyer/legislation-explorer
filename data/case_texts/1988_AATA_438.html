NT87/1633 and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 438; 88 ATC 419 (18 March 1988)

Last Updated: 25 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - assessment on basis of comparison of funds available and funds expended - claim that moneys received from overseas not take into consideration - onus of proof not discharged.

Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936, s.190 (b)

Applicant COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

No. NT87/1633

AAT Decision No 4216

Mr. J.R. Gibson, Senior Member

Sydney

18 March 1988

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT87/1633

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Mr, J.R. Gibson (Senior Member)

Date : 18 March, 1988

Place : Sydney

Decision : The objection decision under review is affirmed.

(Sgd.) J.R. Gibson

Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT87/1633

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

18 March, 1988 Mr. J.R. Gibson (Senior Member)

1. The applicant seeks a review of the decision of the Respondent Commissioner of Taxation which disallowed the applicant's objection to an amended assessment of income tax for the year of income ended 30 June 1979 issued to him by notice of assessment dated 24 September 1981. The amended assessment followed an investigation by officers of the Commissioner of the applicant's taxation affairs for the years ended 30 June 1978, 1979 and 1980 and the adjustment sheet which accompanied the amended assessment showed that the applicant's nett income as returned for the year ended 30 June 1979 had been increased by $21,249. to $28,877. The applicant's notice of objection dated 13 October 1981 was allowed to the extent of reducing the taxable income by $1,115 to $27,762. Otherwise by notice dated 24 June 1982, the objection was disallowed and it is that decision which is the subject of the application to review.

  1. The amended net income upon which the amended assessment was based, was calculated by officers of the Department by ascertaining from the applicant's books and records and from his estimate of living expenses the total funds expended in the year of income, and by deducting from that sum the total funds available during the year of income; the figure for the total funds available was determined by reference to the applicant's books and records and included the funds available at the commencement of the year and all receipts, whether of capital or income, during the year.
  2. As mentioned above, the net income was increased by the Commissioner by $21,249. The applicant claimed in his notice of objection that the assessment should be amended so as to reduce the net income assessed by $14,815, made up of an amount of $1,115 (later allowed) and two amounts of $5,300 and $8,400 received from Israel, said to represent the proceeds of sale of the applicant's home in Israel.
  3. At the hearing the applicant and his wife gave oral evidence and there was tendered a written statement by the applicant's father-in-law in Israel. The "T documents" forwarded by the Respondent to the Tribunal and other documents tendered by the Commissioner were also admitted into evidence.
  4. The applicant is a married man with two children, one born in 1972 and one in 1979. He was born in Romania but went to Israel as a young person and learned a trade there. He gave evidence that he worked hard and with his savings and borrowed money acquired a home in Beer-sheva in 1968. He married in 1970 and his wife also worked and insisted in repaying the moneys borrowed. They came to Australia in 1971 and the applicant's parents followed in 1978. The applicant said that the home in Israel was not sold before he and his wife left in case they should decide not to settle in Australia. He also said that it was not possible for citizens leaving Israel to take more than $10. in cash out of the country. The home in Beer-sheva was left in the care of the applicant's father-on-law.

6. According to a letter to the applicant from a lawyer in Beer-sheva dated 14 August 1981, the apartment in Beer-sheva and most of the furniture in it was sold on 1 June 1976 for 130,000 Israeli pounds or lire. The evidence of the applicant and his wife was that these moneys were retained by the wife's father until 1978. After arriving in Australia the applicant and his wife both worked and acquired successively a house in the north western suburbs in Sydney and a home unit in the eastern suburbs. These properties were later sold and a house was purchased in the eastern suburbs. The applicant worked for some years as a fitter with a large company and during that time acquired a truck which he used to do carrying work on his own account at weekends. He gave up his employment in 1978 and has since been fully engaged as a contract carrier. From the applicant's income tax return it appears that at the commencement of the year ended 30 June 1978 he had a tip truck valued at $1,867 and that during the year he acquired a bob-cat truck and trailer at a cost of $10,325; by the end of the year ended 30 June 1979 he had disposed of the tip truck but had purchased a sedan car for $8,465 and a hoist and body for a leased truck acquired for $2,600.

  1. The applicant and. his wife gave evidence that she had returned from a visit to Israel on 6 June 1978, bringing with her an amount of U.S. dollars which she thought was about $US3,000, which had been given to her by her father and which she exchanged in Sydney for $A5,300 in cash. The applicant said that the moneys represented part of the proceeds of the sale of the home in Israel. In November 1978, according to the applicant, his brother brought to Australia from Israel more of the proceeds of sale, in the form of US currency, which was then converted by the applicant and his wife into $A8,400 Australian currency.
  2. The applicant and his wife said that the proceeds of the moneys brought from Israel were kept in cash in a wardrobe at their home and applied as needed. Out of the amount of $5,300 $3812.40 was expended on the purchase of a motor vehicle and the balance on living expenses, and out of the amount of $8,400 $5,000 was spent on vehicles used in his business and the balance for living expenses.
  3. There was tendered on behalf of the applicant at the hearing a document purporting to be signed by his father-in-law which, omitting formal parts and names, is in the following terms:-

"TO WHOM IT MAY CONCERN

As requested by my daughter, I certify as follows:-

I, (name), of Beer-Sheva, Israel, am the father of Mrs. (name) born (name). On her visit to Israel in April-June 1978, I gave her (sent her) from the money of their house, the sum of 86,500 pounds (Israeli) (Eighty-six thousand, five hundred Israeli pounds) which she took back with her to Australia. The said amount was the remainder of the money she received in selling her house and furniture in 1976.

Dated this 1st day of September, 1981

(sgd.) (name)

(name)"

  1. The applicant's brother did not give evidence. The applicant's representative, Mr. Satill, said that the brother was not available on the day of the hearing as he was required to work on that day.
  2. Included in the documentary evidence tendered on behalf of the Commissioner were tables, based on rates of exchange, showing:-
    • (a) the value of the Australian dollar and of Israeli pounds (or lire) in New York on various dates between 1 June 1976 and 20 November 1978, and
    • (b) the value of the US dollar in Sydney on various dates between 1 June 1976 and 20 November 1978.
  3. From the tables it can be calculated that 130,000 Israeli pounds could have been converted to $US16,900 in New York on 1 June 1976, and that the $US16,900 could have been converted into $A14,531 in Sydney on that date. The value of the Israeli pound dropped considerably over the period in question so that 130,000 Israeli pounds converted in the same way would have produced only $A6,271 on 10 July 1978.
  4. It can also be seen from the tables that more than $US6,000 would have been needed to produce $A5,300 in Sydney on 10 June 1978 and that more than $US9,500 would have been needed to produce $A8,400 there on 20 November 1978.
  5. If one looks in a broad way at the evidence of the applicant and his wife it cannot be said to be improbable that he should have had funds in Israel which were transmitted to Australia in the manner claimed. But under s.190(b) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 the applicant has the onus of proving that the assessment is excessive; for him to succeed the evidence must satisfy the Tribunal on the probabilities that the claim is made out.
  6. The oral evidence of the applicant and his wife as to the receipt of the money from Israel was quite firm, but in some respects inconsistent with documentary evidence. As to the way in which the wife's father managed the proceeds of sale of the Beer-sheva home and when and how they were converted Into US currency, the evidence was vague. Both the applicant and his wife were unable to recall any substantial expenditure from that part of the Israeli funds which was not applied to the purchase of capital assets in the applicant's business as set out in his notice of objection.
  7. The written statement of the applicant's father in law, In which he said he gave his daughter 86,500 Israeli pounds "the remainder of the money she received in selling her house and furniture in 1976", if correct, indicates that the amount said to have been brought to Australia by the applicant's brother cannot have been proceeds of the sale of the home and, if read literally, is inconsistent with the evidence of the wife that the moneys she brought were given to her by her father in US currency.
  8. 17. There is no evidence as to when the 86,500 Israeli pounds was allegedly converted into US currency but, if it was converted in 1978 at favourable official rates, it would have produced only about $US5, 000 which in turn would have produced about $A3,300 in Sydney.

18. As mentioned above, the applicant's brother did not give evidence and the explanation given for his absence, if correct, would not have prevented evidence being obtained from him. It is at least possible that his evidence would not have assisted the applicant.

  1. As will have been noted, the applicant's explanation of the introduction of the funds from Israel does not cover the whole of the understatement of income calculated by the Commissioner. The applicant suggested that the balance could be accounted for by extra living expenses but, while he and his wife stated that extra furnishings were purchased with some of the funds from Israel, neither could specify any substantial outlay. The applicant also put forward as a possibility that the extra funds could have come from his wife's earnings, but had to withdraw that suggestion when shown that, according to his return of income for the year ended 30 June 1979, his wife's separate income was $455.
  2. The reason given by the applicant wife for keeping the funds derived from Israel in the home was that this made her feel more secure but, having regard to the fact that the applicant had a bank account and that he and his wife had moneys on deposit in a building society, this explanation was not convincing.
  3. Another matter which was suggested by the respondent as bearing on the credit of the applicant was that in his income tax return for the year of income ended 30 June 1978 he had indicated that he did not possess property overseas. When cross-examined about this, the applicant said that he had not read the return but he had signed what his agent told him to sign. If true, this is a reflection on both the taxpayer and the agent. To put it neutrally the failure to disclose overseas property does not assist the applicant in discharging the onus of proof in this application.
  4. The evidence as a whole does not leave the Tribunal satisfied on the probabilities that the increase in funds available to the applicant resulted in moneys received from Israel and, accordingly, he has failed to discharge the onus imposed to him by s.190(b) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 of showing that the assessment was excessive.
  5. The Tribunal will therefore uphold the objection decision the subject of the application.
