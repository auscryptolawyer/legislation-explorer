AT86/336 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 800; (1987) 18 ATR 3918; 87 ATC 1072 (12 October 1987)

Last Updated: 29 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - deduction - expenditure on union literature - whether incurred in gaining or producing assessable income

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss 51, 74

Public Service Reform Act 1984

FC of T v Maddalena, 71 ATC 4161

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No AT86/336

AAT Decision No 3822

Deputy President RK Todd

12 October 1987

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No AT86/336 TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Deputy President RK Todd

Date : 12 October 1987

Place : Canberra

Decision : The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

(sgd.) RK Todd

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. AT86/336

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

12 October 1987 Deputy President RK Todd

1. The applicant in this review originally claimed as a deduction in the year of income ended 30 June 1985 the sum of $3,205 as follows:

"(b) National Council Election Expenses

The taxpayer was a successful candidate )

for ACOA National Councillor following )

a total membership Ballot (over 10,000 )

members). Expenses were: Printing )$3,205

Election material $2,500, Stationery $160, )

Labels $80, Typesetting $315, Photographs )

and Bromides $60: Sundries $90 )."

2. After this claim was disallowed, the applicant advised the Commissioner that the claim should be amended. She stated that only $1,120 was spent directly on election expenses as a candidate for ACOA National Councillor: "$2,085 was expended on informing ACOA members on how decisions are made at the National level of the union and what those decisions were, and that I was in fact an effective National Councillor on their behalf. This expenditure of $2,085 was necessary to earn my income as an ACOA National Councillor".

  1. The applicant thus conceded that that portion of the original claim that was referable to election expenses was not deductible. Certainly, they would not have been deductible under s74 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITAA"). Some of the argument, referred to below, that was put in support of the deductibility of the remaining $2,085 under s 51(1) of the ITAA might be said to have been as equally apt to support the deductibility of the $1,120. In view however of the conclusion to which I have come in respect of the maintained claim, it is unnecessary to pursue this point.
  2. The break-up of the amended claim was as follows:

"Printing cost $1,548

Stationery 128

Labels 42

Typesetting 264

Photos and bromides 40

Sundries 63

$2,085"

These sums were said to have been "expended on my share of the costs of providing circulars and magazines to members."

  1. The claims to deduct the amounts in question arise because the applicant, a Commonwealth public servant, became involved in the affairs of Administrative and Clerical Officers' Association ("the ACOA"). The applicant's public service duties were as a computer systems officer, and as such were unconnected with her ACOA activities. As will later appear, however, the applicant saw her future advancement in the public service as being capable of being nourished by a successful performance in the ACOA.
  2. The applicant was elected to the position of National Councillor with the ACOA in November 1984 at an election to fill a casual vacancy. She stood as a "Reform" candidate, against a candidate described as a "Progressive", another as representing "Independents" and two others who were the first to be eliminated in the ballot which ensued. The applicant had hoped as a result of her success in the election to stand for a full time fully paid executive position with ACOA, but failed to gain the necessary support.

7. Examples of the subject of the claimed expenditure were put before the Tribunal by the applicant. They were -

(a) Two issues of "Tocsin", described as "Alternative ACOA Press" in one case and "Alternative ACOA Journal" in another (Exs. B and C). In these the applicant contributed a short note of the results of the 1984 election, claiming that the outcome had not been officially reported to ACOA members. In the other, she contributed a longer article, critical of the "Progressives" and of the administration of her local ACOA branch. She also contributed to other insertions in these publications e.g. an article headed "NC Swallows the Dummy".

(b) Two news sheets headed "Tocsin News Briefing" and "Annual Review: A Year of Disappointment" (Ex.D). The first of these was a report to members of a meeting of the National Council and I read it as a demonstration by the Reform group of its desire and ability to keep ACOA members informed of the activities of the National Council. The second was essentially a criticism of the administration of the ACT Branch by the "Progressives".
  1. In the year of income in question the applicant derived assessable income of $264 as an ACOA National Councillor and as an ACT Branch Executive member.
  2. In her oral evidence the applicant said that the circulars involved in her claim were "about the affairs of ACOA at that point in time". Referring to the report of the National Council meeting the applicant said that it was "my role to brief the members as to the proceedings and the decisions made there". The second circular was a review of what went on in a particular year in ACOA. It was "informing the membership of the decisions made in the executive and the national council of ACOA". The two editions of "Tocsin" were "both examples of the sort of information that the group I was involved in put to members to let them know of relevant questions that were going on at that time". The applicant also explained that "National Consultative Councils are joint councils set up in every department between the unions concerned and the management of those departments. They come under the Public Service Reform Act 1984".
  3. The applicant further explained in evidence how she had since 1978 been a delegate to ACOA within her department, and then a branch executive member before becoming a National Councillor. She had also played a role as "chairer" of the delegates' committee within the ACT, and of the National Delegates' Committee within her department. As such, she said, she negotiated consultative arrangements and was part of a team that negotiated the consultative arrangements whereby union work was acknowledged and the representational role of union delegates was acknowledged by the applicant's department.
  4. Various well known authorities were cited on behalf of the applicant and the respondent Commissioner, but in my opinion the applicant's case must fail simply on an analysis of the facts. The material in respect of which the claimed expenditure was incurred appears to me to be capable of no characterisation other than of a mode of advancing the position of the "Reform" group of which the applicant was a member. The applicant said that "As a result of being elected as a national councillor, the indications were that there was strong support in the union for a different group being given the executive positions ... [T]he purpose of these magazines was also to put before the union our abilities as a group to lead the union in the ACT so that it was to put before the membership that we were a reasonable alternative, and could to the jobs of the full paid executive positions". Asked by her representative why it was necessary to incur this expenditure to maintain her position as an ACOA National Councillor, the applicant answered: "...if we did not do it we would not have been re-elected. It was necessary. We were elected on a platform of providing this information and it was necessary as a national councillor to gain the views of the members and present those views at National Council ... and without being seen to do that ... we would -could not have maintained - well, I could not have maintained my position as a national councillor."
  5. It is no doubt true that in advancing the position of the "Reform" group the applicant advanced her own position, and that this would ultimately, as she saw it, advance her career in the Public Service. But the connection between the incurring of the expenditure and the gaining or producing of the applicant's own assessable income is too remote by far to enable a decision in favour of deductibility to be made. I might add that in any event, as far as the claim was sought to be based on furtherance of the applicant's claim for a fully paid executive position in the ACOA, the expenditure was incurred at a point too soon to be regarded as incurred in the derivation of assessable income: See FC of T v Maddalena, 71 ATC 4161.
  6. In all the circumstances the objection decision under review must be affirmed.
