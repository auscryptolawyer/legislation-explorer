REASONS FOR DECISION



  1. These applications for review which were heard together, involve three applicants. They seek review of the decision by the Respondent Commissioner disallowing objections to amended assessments of income tax for the years of income ended 30 June 1980 and 30 June 1982 for one of the applicants whom we shall refer to as "H", and for the year of income ended 30 June 1982 for each or the other two whom we shall refer to "J" and "M".

2 The assessments were amended by the Respondent by including in the assessable income of the applicants profits realised by them from the sale of land owned in partnership. In addition "His" application concerns the disallowance as a deduction of an amount of $1,000 claimed by him as secretarial fees paid to his wife during the year of income ended 30 June 1982.



3. Amounts added to the incomes originally assessed to the applicants amounted to $4,046 and $7,059 for the years ended 30 June 1980 and 30 June 1982 respectively for "H" and $7,059 for the year of income ended 30 June 1980 for each of the others.



4. The applicants' claimed that the property was not acquired by the partnership for the purpose of profit making by sale within the first limb of sec. 26(a) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") nor to carry on or carry out a profit making undertaking or scheme within the second limb of sec. 26(a) and the profits were not made in the course of a business carried on by the applicants and so are not assessable under sec. 25(1) of the Act. The Applicants also claimed that the sale of the property took place after the expiration of a period 12 months from the time that it was purchased.



5. Two of the applicants, "H" and "J", gave oral evidence at the hearing and documents were produced by the applicants, and the Respondent. Those documents included formal documents forwarded to the Tribunal by the Commissioner and copies of Planning Permits for Erection of a Dwelling, Applications for sub-division of Land, Plan of Consolidation of Land, Certificate of Title, financial statements and bank statements of the partnership together with copies of income tax returns.



6. The facts as I understand them on the basis of documents presented and evidence adduced at the hearing are set out below.



7. A partnership comprising "J", "H", and "M" and a third person who is not a party to these proceedings was formed to purchase 198 acres of land in a rural area, some 6 Kilometres from a town situated approximately 40 Kilometres from a capital city. Each of the partners was at that time and still is a real estate agent. The land was purchased on 9th March 1973 for $40.000.



8. The consideration was payable as to $4,000 on the signing of the contract and the residue by an initial instalment of $16,000 payable on 19 March 1973 and the balance on 1 February 1978 or on such earlier a date as might be agreed between the parties. Interest on the residue of the purchase money was to be payable annually as from the date of possession at the rate of 7 1/2 % per annum. It was also provided that the purchasers had the right to pay the whole of the purchase money on any day appointed for payment of interest or on account thereof sums of $2,000 or multiples thereof.



9. At the time of the purchase the land was on one title and was subdivided into five allotments ranging in area from 13 acres to 82 acres.



10. Neither of the partners who gave evidence at the hearing could remember with any clarity very much about the discussions which took place when the decision to purchase the property was made. "J" said that the main thing he could remember was that one of the members of the partnership, "M", had a property at a country area close to a large provincial city which had been purchased some years before and which had provided a good income from agistment and the growing of crops. As a result they thought that they could get an income from the subject property and also that it would grow in value. He claimed no knowledge of farming.



11. "H"was also vague about farming activities but thought on the basis of "heresay" that it would be possible to make an income from a farming property. When asked how it arose that the land was purchased "H" said: "Well the full story is that I rang a name in the phone book looking for a block of flats to sell as a real estate agent, and it was the wrong person I was talking to, but he came through with this block of land. He said, "as a matter of fact I've got a block of land I would like to sell." We discussed it in the office and decided to have look at it ourselves and we finished up buying it. That was the story on it."



12. At the time of the purchase, the land was leased to a local farmer for the agistment of sheep and for the years of income ended 30 June 1975 to 30 June 1982 inclusive incomes returned by the partnership were as follows.

13. During 1975 the tenant decided that he no longer required the property and. after attempts to find another tenant failed, following advice from the agent that it was not a good time to find a tenant for agistment, the partners decided to sell enough land to repay the outstanding liability on the property of $20,000. The first sale was made on 18 September 1985 and realised $39,950 of which $6,420 was profit and this enabled the partners to repay the $20,000 liability.



14. It was then decided to sell the balance of the land as there appeared to be little opportunity to receive further income from agistment or any other means. Further sales took place on 25 October 1979 and 13 August 1981 and realised profits of $16,184 and $28,239 respectively, one quarter of which was distributed to each of the applicants. It is the assessabi1ity of those profits which is the subject of these references.



15. During the hearing some confusion arose regarding the sub-division of the land at the time of its purchase by the partnership. Both of the Applicants denied that they were aware that the land was sub-divided and that they were even aware of the significance of a Crown Allotment as their experience had been confined to the sale of flats and houses. The Commissioner's representative questioned them closely in respect of this aspect and in her questions referred to the allotments being on separate titles. Neither of the applicants sought to correct this impression which however was not the case. All of the land at the time of the purchase was contained on the one title.



16. Copies of the various permit applications for the Erection of a Dwelling lodged with the local Municipality by prospective purchaser's of the land were tendered. On each of these applications the partners completed a certification indicating their concurrence with the application. The first such application dated 18 March 1975 was lodged On 2 April 1975. A Plan of Subdivision for part of the land was approved by the Titles Office on 17 August 1976.



17. No evidence was presented in support of the claim by H in respect of the deduction of $1,000 claimed as secretarial fees paid to his wife during the year of income ended 30th June 1982.



18. In defence of the amended assessments the Commissioner relied on sec. 25(1) and sec. 26(a) of the Act. Turning firstly to sec. 25(1) it was submit.ted by his representative that the sale of the property was made in the process of carrying on a business and accordingly it was a receipt of a revenue nature. It is uncontested that each of the applicants is an estate agent but there is no evidence whatsoever that they have at any time carried on a business of buying and selling land. The Commissioner's representative did not develop this argument and it is my view that the partnership did not carry on a business of dealing in property and that Section 25(1) does not apply to include the amounts in the assessable incomes of the applicants.



19. Sec 26(a) as it applied during the years under review provided for the inclusion in the assessable income of a taxpayer of:

"(a) profit arising from the sale by the taxpayer of any property acquired by him for the purpose of profit making by sale, or from the carrying on or carrying out of any profit making undertaking or scheme", ----



The Commissioners representative cited a number of cases including Smith v The Federal Commissioner of Taxation 72 ATC 4111, Smith v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 73 ATC 4059, Cole v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4792, Case DIO 72 ATC 46, Case Vl14 88 ATC 727. Those cases follow well established principles in respect of the application of sec 26Ca) as applied to their own particular facts.



20. The Commissioners representative also submitted with respect to the first limb of sec. 26(a) that unless the applicants prove affirmatively that in acquiring the property they were not activated by the sole or dominant purpose of profit making that the case must fail as they have not discharged the onus of proof under Sec 190(b). In that regard reference was made to McCormack v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 79 ATC 4111, and the approval by Gibbs, Stephen and Murphy JJ of the statement of Mason J, in his dissenting judgement in Gauci v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 75 ATC p. 4261.



"The Act does not place any onus on the Commissioner to show that the assessments were correctly made. Nor is there any statutory requirement that the assessment should be maintained or supported by evidence. The implication of such a requirement would be inconsistent with sec. 190(b) for it is a consequence of that provision that unless the applicant shows by evidence that the assessment is incorrect, it will prevail---. The crux of the matter is that when in a sec. 26(a) case an appellant seeks to overcome the onus created by sec. 190(b) by adducing evidence as to his intentions with a view to establishing the purpose of the acquisition was not a sec. 26(a) purpose and that evidence is not accepted, he has not discharged the onus which he bears. At best, from the appellant's viewpoint, the evidence stands in a situation which is equivocal, neither establishing a sec. 26(a) purpose nor denying the existence of such a purpose. At worst the judge may, in the circumstances be able to infer the existence of a sec. 26(a) purpose. In either event the appellant fails to discharge the onus and the appeal fails".



21. The primary claim made by the applicants that the property was ( purchased with the intention of getting income by its use as a farm, possibly by leasing it to farmers for agistment or crop growing, is difficult to accept. The applicants admitted to their almost complete lack of knowledge of farming and provided no evidence in support of their claim that a reasonable income could have been gained from the venture.



22. The applicants also claimed that the property was purchased for purchaser with the approval of the applicants. Other applications followed, including a Plan of sub-division approved by the Titles Office on 17 August 1976.



23. In the circumstance I reject the applicants' evidence that the property was purchased as a source of income from leasing or the qrowing of crops, or that it was their intention to retain it for investment purposes. The applicants have not discharged the onus of proof under 190(b) as required in a sec. 26(a) case, refer, McCormack v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (supra), and I affirm the Commissioner's decision on the objections in the two years before me in respect of "H" and the one year in respect of "J" and "M". .



24. As no evidence was presented by "H" in respect of his claim for a deduction of $1,000 for secretarial fees paid to his wife the Commissioner's decision on the objection for the year ended 30 June 1982 in respect of that claim is affirmed.
