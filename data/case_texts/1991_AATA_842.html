VT91/136-138 and Commissioner of Taxation [1991] AATA 842; (1991) 23 ATR 1239; 92 ATC 264 (13 December 1991)

Last Updated: 27 May 2009



INCOME TAX - Application for adjournment - Hardship to taxpayer if application refused - Inadequacy of notice of objection - Circumstances in which grounds may be amended - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936-1987, secs 185, 187.



Archer Bros Ltd v FC of T [1953] HCA 23; (1952-53) 90 CLR 140

H R Lancey Shipping Co Pty Ltd v FC of T (1951) ALR 507

Lighthouse Philatelics Pty Ltd v FC of T 91 ATC 4942



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. VT91/136-138

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:

APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION



RESPONDENT



DECISION



AAT Decision No 8108



Before: Dr P Gerber (Deputy President)

Date: 13 December 1991

Place: Melbourne



The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.



(Sgd P Gerber)

DEPUTY PRESIDENT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. VT91/136-138

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:



APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT



REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Dr P Gerber (Deputy President)

Date: 13 December 1991

Place: Melbourne



In this matter I affirmed the respondent's decisions on the objections at the hearing on 13 December 1991. My statement in writing of the reasons of the Tribunal for its decision, given orally at the hearing, is set out hereunder pursuant to sec 43(2A) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.



2. The applicant in this case was subjected to an audit for the years 1985 to 1987 inclusive, years in which no returns were lodged prior to such audit. As a result, the Commissioner concluded, by recourse to what is known as an asset betterment, that in each year, the applicant was in receipt of taxable income and issued assessments for all three years. The effect being that in 1985, the taxpayer's taxable income (including additional tax for late return) was $36,312; in 1986 the total came to $29,217 and $28,479 in 1987. Notices of the three assessments were issued on 10 July 1987.



3. On receipt of the assessments, the taxpayer went to a tax agent, Bernie Rose & Associates, who wrote to the respondent on 11 August 1987. I read the contents of the letter in full into the transcript:



Re Mrs S. We are in possession of your assessment number 385012/3 issued on 10 July 1987 and hereby wish to object to your assessment in full.



On 5 August 1987, we lodged income tax returns for the above and in each case it indicated that there was no tax payable at all. The assessments were raised from a betterment statement produced by one of your officers on completely erroneous information. We are at present getting together absolute proof as to the above facts.



In the meantime we request the assessments be set aside and no penalties charged due to the above events.



(sgd) Bernie Rose as Governing Director of the tax agent.



4. The rest is silence. In other words, notwithstanding the assertion by the tax agent more than four years ago that he will be "getting together absolute proof" that the information the Commissioner relied upon was erroneous, there has been no further correspondence from that source or, for that matter, from any other source.



5. The Commissioner now asserts - and has repeatedly asserted - that the letter of 11 August 1987 which I have just read into the transcript is not an "objection" that complies with the Tax Act, a view with which I have considerable sympathy. Be that as it may, the Commissioner treated the letter as an objection and on 24 February 1989, that is some 18 months after Mr Rose's objection he sent a notice to the applicant, stating that "the objection dated 11 August 1987 against the assessment which issued on 10 July in respect of the year ended 30 June 1985, 1986 and 1987 has been considered and disallowed". The taxpayer thereupon elected to have the Commissioner's decision referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal and the matter was referred to this Tribunal for review on 28 August 1991.



6. Today, being the day the case was set down for hearing, Mr Steart, after announcing his appearance, immediately sought an adjournment on the ground that he mistakenly believed that the case was listed for next Monday. He added that as the case involving one K (in which he was retained) was listed for hearing before me next week and was being converted into a return of summons hearing, I would be available to hear this matter in its place. It seems that he did not consult the Australian Government Solicitor whether those days were acceptable to him. I thereupon asked Mr Davies, of learned counsel for the respondent, as to his client's attitude to the application. Mr Davies advised the Tribunal that his client strenuously opposed any application for an adjournment.



7. Dealing with the application to adjourn the matter, the first thing to note is that Mr Steart's conduct of this case leaves much to be desired. As a result of his neglect of his client's interest, substantial costs will be thrown away since this Tribunal is not empowered to award costs. I asked Mr Steart whether a short adjournment to enable him to have a "chat" with Mr Davies might make the latter more amenable to the application. That hint led nowhere.



8. In all the circumstances, Mr Steart has exhausted any sympathy the Tribunal may have had for his plight. In any event, on the facts of this case, the application to adjourn this matter has little merit. Put in chronological order, a Preliminary Conference before me took place in this matter on 19 September 1991, Mr Steart appearing for the applicant, Ms Hillebrand, of the Australian Government solicitor, for the respondent. The Conference Report, written up by my associate, records that the Crown raised the issue of the competence of the objection on the basis that it disclosed no "ground". I thereupon indicated that that was not a matter with which I was prepared to deal at a Preliminary Conference, but suggested to Mr Steart that he might consider supplying the Crown with "further and better particulars" of the grounds on which his client proposes to rely.



9. Pausing here, the applicant was thus on notice that the competence of the objection was in issue. This notwithstanding, Mr Steart made no attempt to enlarge his client's grounds. To add insult to injury, I was informed from the bar table that on 26 November 1991, Ms Hillebrand wrote to Mr Steart as follows:



I refer to our attendance at the Administrative Appeals Tribunal on 19 September 1991 before Dr Gerber.



I confirm that I appeared on behalf of the Respondent. I stated clearly that the letter forwarded by Bernie Rose and Associates Pty to the Commissioner on 11 August 1987 objecting to the assessment issued on 10 July 1987, failed to comply with Section 185 of the Income Tax Assessment Act.



I confirm that at the preliminary conference I raised the question of jurisdiction and it was strongly suggested by Dr Gerber to yourself that you provide my office with the appropriate details prior to the hearing date.



To date I have received no information from your office and I ask that you now forward that information by return of mail.



I confirm that this matter has been listed at the Administrative Appeals Tribunal, Melbourne for 13 December 1991.



10. For good measure, Mr Davies advised me from the bar table that he was instructed that several attempts by Ms Hillebrand to contact Mr Steart by telephone proved abortive and that he ignored all messages to ring back. Mr Steart conceded this.



11. In all the circumstances, there must be a finis litem. In considering the hardship to the taxpayer by a refusal to adjourn this matter to another day, I ask myself: can the application be dealt with without amendment to the objection; if not, what is the likelihood of an application to amend the objection succeeding?



12. Mr Davis relied upon the usual list of cases dealing with the adequacy of objections, and in particular Archer Bros Ltd v FC of T [1953] HCA 23; (1952-53) 90 CLR 140, in which the often quoted dictum of Williams J in the Lancey Shipping Co case (1951) ALR 507 is cited with approval, viz that:



The grounds of objection need not be stated in legal form, they can be expressed in ordinary language, but they should be sufficiently explicit to direct the attention of the Commissioner to the particular respects in which the taxpayer contends that the assessment is erroneous and his reasons for this contention.



13. Applied to this case, I am satisfied that a bald statement that the Commissioner "relied on erroneous information" cannot be said "to have directed the Commissioner's attention to the particular respects in which the taxpayer contends that the assessment is erroneous and his reasons for this contention". In other words, before this taxpayer can get her foot in the door of the Tribunal, her objection clearly requires amendment. True it is that a taxpayer is no longer strictly bound by the grounds as pleaded and can seek to amend the grounds at a hearing; cf Lighthouse Philatelics Pty Ltd v FC of T 91 ATC 4942. However, the success or otherwise of such application is ultimately a matter going to the Tribunal's discretion. I need hardly say that that discretion is unlikely to be applied in favour of an applicant whose advisers have foreshadowed providing "absolute proof" of the Commissioner's "erroneous information" more than four years ago but have failed to provide such proof to this day, have failed to heed the Crown's (and the Tribunal's) warning that such omission could seriously compromise their client's claim and have at all times acted in contumelious disregard of even the most common professional courtesies. The final straw is an application for an adjournment on the basis that the advisers have failed - despite numerous reminders - to note the date of hearing.



14. The adjournment is refused. In the absence of any evidence, the only "ground" pleaded - that the Commissioner acted on erroneous information in arriving at the assessments - has not been made out. In reaching that view, I am mindful that the applicant is not entirely without remedy, albeit whatever rights she may have against her various advisers must be sought in another place. The decision on the objection is upheld.
