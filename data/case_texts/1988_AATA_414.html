Income Tax - allowable deductions - Trade Union Secretary expenses of seeking re-election

REASONS FOR DECISION

24 February 1988 Mrs. R.A. Balmford, Senior Member

  1. This is an application for review of two decisions of the respondent disallowing objections by the applicant to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of his income for the years ended 30 June 1980 and 30 June 1984 of amounts of $1041 and $526 respectively which the applicant claimed as deductions in respect of expenses of election as the Secretary of a trade union ("the Union") in each of those years.
  2. The applicant requested in each case that the objection be referred to a Board of Review, and those requests were complied with by the respondent. By virtue of Section 223(1) of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 and sub-section 189(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") that referral of the decision on the applicant's objection is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision.
  3. Evidence was given at the hearing by the applicant and by the Victorian State President of the Union.
  4. Rule 31 Branch Management of the official rules and constitution of the Union provides that:
    • (b) Each Branch shall have for its management a State Executive consisting of a President, two Vice-Presidents, Secretary-Treasurer, Assistant Secretary, full time Organisers as specified in this sub-rule and not fewer than eight nor more than eighteen Group Representatives and who shall be elected for a period of two years. Provided that any person elected to an office which has been declared a full time office shall be elected for a term of two years and for a term of office of four years on re-election. For the purposes of Branch administration the following Branch offices shall be declared full time positions:

......

Victoria - President, Secretary-Treasurer, Assistant Secretary, five (5) Organisers

......

(c) The President, Vice-Presidents, Secretary-Treasurer, Assistant Secretary and full time Organisers shall be elected by ballot of the financial members of the Branch.

Rule 35 Branch Nominations provides:

Candidates for office on the State Executive except where otherwise provided, must be financial members of the Branch and be nominated in writing by at least two other financial members of the Branch.

That Rule provides for nominations to be called for by a notice published in the Union journal.

Rule 36 Branch Elections provides that the election of officers of the State Executive is to be conducted by secret postal ballot of financial members of the Branch and there are detailed provisions as to the manner of conduct of elections.

Rule 39 Salaries and Allowances reads:

The salaries and allowances of all full-time officials shall be fixed when necessary by Annual Conference and/or the Federal Executive.

5. The applicant was elected in 1978 at a by-election as Victorian Branch Secretary-Treasurer of the Union. The accountant who represented the applicant at the hearing said that his client conceded that any expenses connected with his original election at the by-election in 1978 were expenses of a capital nature and were not allowable deductions. However, he submitted that expenses involved in his client's successful attempts to obtain re-election first in 1979 and secondly in 1983 were allowable deductions in terms of the first limb of sub-section 51(1) of the Act. That provision reads as far as relevant

"all losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income ... shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital or of a capital, private or domestic nature .......”

6. The applicant explained that there are some thousands of Victorian members of the Union and that in seeking re-election it was necessary for him and others seeking election to other positions to prepare and distribute material to these members. The people concerned caused material to be prepared and distributed to the members and divided up the cost among themselves. The only costs of that material which the applicant claimed as a deduction were his share of the costs of printing, postage and the like. He emphasised that in so far as he had any expenses related to the seeking of election or re-election to offices which were not full-time offices of the Union he did not claim those expenses as a deduction. It was only the expenses related to his re-election to his full-time office as Secretary-Treasurer of the Victorian Branch that he made the claim.

  1. At the time of his election in 1978 he had held a position at a level for which the maximum salary in 1980 was $8,964 and in 1984 was $14,397. In contrast his salary as a Union official was in 1980 $21,268 and in 1984 $36,175. He had been given leave without pay by his employer to enable him to carry out his duties as Secretary-Treasurer of the Victorian Branch of the Union. Should he cease to hold that office in the Union he would return to his old classification with his previous employer, having received no promotions while absent from that position on Union duties. Thus the substantially higher level of his income as a Union officer was dependent on his being re-elected. Because of the complexity of the elections, the number of candidates and of positions and the number of voters it was necessary to prepare and distribute material of the kind in respect of which he claimed the deduction.
  2. Asked by the Tribunal what, if he were defeated at an election, would be his position as at the date on which the newly elected Secretary-Treasurer took office, the applicant said that it was a hypothetical question because it had never occurred, but that he would have a continuing statutory responsibility in respect of the finances of the Union for the year which would have concluded at the time of the election. However he agreed that in those circumstances the person who had been successful at the election would be for all present and future purposes the Secretary-Treasurer of the Victorian Branch of the Union.
  3. A copy of the currently operative resolution of the Annual Conference of the Union made pursuant to Rule 39 was made available to the Tribunal after the hearing and a copy forwarded to the officer who represented the respondent at the hearing to enable him to make submissions thereon if he so desired. After consideration of that material, the respondent advised that no further submissions would be made. The applicant said at the hearing that that document together with the rules constituted the only evidence of the terms on which he performed his duties as Secretary-Treasurer of the Victorian Branch of the Union.
  4. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Maddalena (1971) 71 ATC 4161 the High Court found, in the words of Barwick CJ, that "The cost to an employee of obtaining his employment does not form an outgoing incurred in the course of earning the wages payable in the employment. That cost is not deductible under Section 51 of [the Act]".
  5. Essentially the applicant argued that there was a continuity in his employment subject only to the necessity of obtaining re-election and that Maddalena's case thus did not apply. The expenses of re-election could not be regarded as "the cost to an employee of obtaining his employment".
  6. In my view this contention must fail. It is clear to me on the material before me, and in particular the evidence of the two witnesses, that the applicant's office as Secretary-Treasurer of the Victorian Branch of the Union is held for a fixed term governed by the provisions of Rule 31. Initially he held office in terms of the provisions relating to by-elections. After being first re-elected he held office for a term of two years and after being again re-elected he held office for a term of four years and if re-elected in the future he will be re-elected for a term of four years. But each term is a separate discrete period of employment. At the end of the term any person who complies with the requirements of Rule 35 (and with any other relevant requirements of which I may be unaware) may nominate for the office of Secretary-Treasurer. The applicant may also nominate, but he has no continuing position in that office. If another member or members should nominate for the position and he should also nominate, an election will be held. Each person who has nominated will be equally entitled, so far as the rules are concerned, and so far as the applicant's employment with the Union is concerned, to be successful at that election and become Secretary-Treasurer. The incumbent has no special right.

13. Accordingly I find that the facts in this case are indistinguishable from the facts in Maddalena's case and the principle there stated applies to the applicant's election expenses. The objection decisions under review will be affirmed.
