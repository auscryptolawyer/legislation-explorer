Mathieson and Commissioner for Superannuation [1990] AATA 637 (31 August 1990)

Mathieson and Commissioner for Superannuation [1990] AATA 637 (31 August 1990)

Last Updated: 6 June 2008

SUPERANNUATION - deceased a "retirement pensioner" at time of marriage - deceased died within five years of marriage - applicant and deceased not living in de facto relationship prior to marriage - whether applicant a "spouse”.



Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 s.43(2A)

Superannuation Act 1976 ss.3, 55(2)

Acts Interpretation Act 1901 ss.15AA, 15AB



AAT Decision No 6270



D E C I S I O N & R E A S O N S



Re: WINSOME STUART MATHIESON



And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION



No V90/103



Tribunal: Mr J. Handley, Senior Member



Date: 31 August 1990



Place: Melbourne



DECISION



Decision: The decision under review be affirmed.



.........................

Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL)

) No V90/103

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION)



Re: WINSOME STUART MATHIESON



Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION



Respondent



DRAFT REASONS FOR DECISION



31 August 1990 Mr J. Handley, Senior Member



1. At the conclusion of the hearing of this application, on 31 August 1990, oral Reasons for Decision were delivered.



2. The Tribunal then decided that the decision under review should be affirmed.



3. Subsequently, the respondent requested written reasons for the decision pursuant to section 43(2A) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.



4. Annexed to these written reasons is a copy of the reasons delivered orally on 31 August.



5. The applicant applied to review a decision of a delegate of the respondent who decided on 12 December 1989 that the applicant was not a "spouse" as defined by the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") and was therefore not entitled to a pension as a spouse of a deceased person.



6. That decision was subsequently confirmed by another delegate of the respondent on 25 January 1990. The reasons for the confirmation of the decision were found in the T documents at T2.7. The applicant is the widow of the late Dr John Mathieson, who was Director of Health in Perth prior to his retirement in 1976, when he was then 64 years of age. He was a contributor to the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme and was paid a superannuation pension following retirement.



8. Dr Mathieson was widowed in 1987, when his first wife died. He married the applicant on 9 June 1988 but died on 3 November 1989.



9. The effect of this application is to contest the decision of the respondent to refuse payment of superannuation pension to the applicant as the spouse of the deceased contributor.



10. It was not in dispute that, prima facie, a superannuation pension is payable to the spouse of a deceased contributor. What was in dispute was whether the applicant is a "spouse" within the meaning of the Act.



11. A "retirement pensioner" and "spouse" is defined at section 3 of the Act and reads as follows:



"`retirement pensioner' means a person to whom age retirement pension, early retirement pension or invalidity pension is payable;



`spouse', in relation to a person who has died and was, at the time of his death, an eligible employee or a retirement pensioner, means -



(a) a person who was legally married to the deceased person at the time of the person's death and who, at that time, was living with the person on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis;



(b) a person who was legally married to the deceased person at the time of the person's death but who was not living with the person on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis at that time, and

who, in the opinion of the Commissioner, was wholly or substantially dependent upon the deceased person at that time;



(c) a person who was not legally married to the deceased person at the time of the person's death but who, for a continuous period of not less than 3 years immediately preceding the person's death, had ordinarily lived with the person as the person's husband or wife, as the case may be, on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis; and



(d) a person who was not legally married to the deceased person at the time of the person's death but who, for a continuous period of less than 3 years immediately preceding the person's death, had ordinarily lived with the person as the

person's husband or wife, as the case may be, on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis, and who, in the opinion of the Commissioner, was wholly or substantially dependent upon the deceased person at the time of the deceased person's death,



but, where the deceased person was, at the time of his death, a retirement pensioner, does not include -



(e) a person who was legally married to the deceased person at the time of his death if the marriage took place after the deceased person had become a pensioner and after the deceased person had attained the age of 60 years unless -



(i) the marriage took place not less than 5 years before the deceased person's death; or



(ii) the marriage took place less than 5 years before the deceased person's death but -



(A) the person had, immediately preceding the marriage, for a continuous period that commenced not later than 5 years before the deceased person's death but after the deceased person became a

retirement pensioner and after the deceased person attained the age of 60 years, lived with the deceased person as the person's husband or wife, as the case may be, on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis;



(B) the person had, immediately preceding the marriage, for a continuous period that commenced not later than 3 years before the deceased person's death but before the deceased person became a retirement pensioner or attained the age of 60 years, whichever last occurred, lived with the deceased person as the deceased person's husband or wife, as the case may be, on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis; or



(C) the person had, immediately preceding the marriage, for a continuous period that commenced later than 3 years before the deceased person's death but before the deceased person became a retirement pensioner or attained the age of 60 years, whichever last occurred, lived with the deceased person as the deceased person's husband or wife, as the case may be, on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis and was, in the opinion of the Commissioner, wholly or substantially dependent upon the deceased person at the time of the deceased person's death; or



(f) a person who was not legally married to the deceased person at the time of his death if the person commenced living with the deceased person as the person's husband or wife, as the case may be, on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis after the deceased person had become a pensioner and after the deceased person had attained the age of 60 years unless the person had so lived with the deceased person for a continuous period of not less than 5 years immediately preceding the deceased person's death;



..."



12. Section 55(2) of the Act qualified the deceased to receive an age retirement pension and I am satisfied that the deceased was a "retirement pensioner" within the meaning of the above quoted legislation.



13. The summary of the facts recorded earlier reveal that the deceased, after he attained the age of 60 years, became a "retirement pensioner" and (re)married.



14. It follows, therefore, that the applicant can only qualify as a "spouse", and thereby be entitled to receive superannuation pension if the marriage took place not less than five years before the deceased person's death, or in the circumstances described by sub-sections (A), (B) or (C).



15. The evidence discloses that the applicant and the deceased married in June 1988, however the deceased died in November 1989. The marriage therefore did take place within five years of "the deceased person's death".



16. The evidence also disclosed that the applicant and the deceased did not live together as husband and wife on a permanent and bona fide basis prior to the marriage. Sub-sections (A), (B) and (C) are therefore not relevant.



17. The definition of "spouse" insofar as it relates to persons who married "retirement pensioners" after those persons were 60 years of age is, to say the least, peculiar. Mrs Mathieson, for the purposes of the Social Security Act, the Family Law Act, the Income Tax Act, and probably a myriad of other legisation, both State and Federal, was the spouse of the deceased. That the status should change for the purposes of the Superannuation Act after her husband has died, has, not surprisingly, caused anguish and disbelief.



18. The sentiments of Mrs Mathieson and myself, heard and expressed at the hearing of these proceedings, need not be repeated here and it is sufficient to refer to the ex tempore reasons delivered on the conclusion of the proceedings.



19. Section 15AA and section 15AB of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 do not assist Mrs Mathieson.



20. The definition of spouse clearly has a purpose (section 15AA) to disentitle retirement benefit pension where the spouse has married the deceased within five years of death, or where the parties lived in circumstances as described by sub-section (A), (B) or (C).



21. These provisions, whilst seemingly absurd and unreasonable, can only be interpreted in favour of the applicant if the legislation is ambiguous or obscure, or the purpose of the legislation is other than the ordinary meaning conveyed by the text of the legislation.



22. I am unable to find any ambiguity or obscurity nor am I able to find that the purpose of the legislation differs from the ordinary meaning conveyed by the legislation.



23. It follows that I cannot invoke the provisions of section 15AA and section 15AB.



24. The decision under review shall be affirmed.
