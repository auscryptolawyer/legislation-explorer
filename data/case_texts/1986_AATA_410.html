NT85/2606 and ommissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 410; 86 ATC 1196 (11 December 1986)

Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax:: block of 16 units built in 1938 near ocean with ungalvanised arch bars: strata titled in 1977: resolution 1 February 1978 to obtain quotes for the replacement of the arch bars but nothing done: applicant acquired unit in block in 1979 as a residence: informal meeting re state of building in 1980 applicant left unit and let it in 1981: notices of motion in April 1982 to accept quotations for various items including $96,338 and $64,457 for, inter alia, 79 arch bars and 33 aluminium window frames: $96,338 and some smaller items accepted and applicant levied a total of $2,827 under Strata Titles Act 1973: whether allowable deduction section 51(1).

Income Tax Assessment Act (Cth) section 51(1).

W. Thomas and Co v FC of T [1965] HCA 54; (1965) 115 CLR 58

The Law Shipping Company Ltd v C of IR 12 TC 621

Odeon Associated Theatres v Jones (1972) 1 All ER 681

REASONS FOR DECISION

Mr H P Stevens (Senior Member)

The question for decision in this application is whether the applicant is entitled to a deduction in the year of income ended 30 June 1982 in respect of her share of a special maintenance levy imposed by the body corporate of a block of units by virtue of section 51(1).

2. In February 1979 the applicant acquired for her own residence a unit in a block of 16 units built in about 1938. Originally company title the block became strata title in 1977. It was of brick construction with concrete floors, metal windows, concrete roof and ungalvanised arch bars. The block was located near the sea and this caused problems in relation, inter alia, to the arch bars. Whilst in residence an informal meeting was called in 1980 to discuss the problem but the applicant didn't attend it. She agreed the defects were attributable to the age and general deterioration of the building. The applicant moved out of her unit in 1981 and let it. Subsequently in April 1982 she received notice of a general meeting of the body corporate to be held on 4 May 1982. The proposed motions were as follows:-

(c) the sum of $3,000.00 being a provisional sum for remedial brickwork to the outer skin of the subject building, such work to be carried out as required on a cost plus basis

5. In her return of income the sum of $2,828 was claimed as a deduction but on assessment it was disallowed "as capital expenditure". Objection was taken to this disallowance on the grounds that the individual amounts were allowable in terms of section 51(1). Grounds (4) and (5) stated:-

"4. The work carried out on this old building constitutes the partial restoration of the building to its former condition. In each case worn out or dilapidated parts were to be replaced with a view to the restoration of efficiency in function rather than the exact repetition of form and substance.

5. No allowance for the state of disrepair was made in the purchase price of the asset when it was acquired by me and I had no knowledge of the defects until well after the date of settlement."

6. At the hearing of the application the applicant appeared on her own behalf whilst the present manager of the strata units also gave evidence. The minutes reproduced above come from the minute book produced by the manager and he deposed that although at a meeting of 1 February 1978 it was resolved, inter alia, "that quotes for replacement of ARCH BARS be obtained" nothing was in fact done until following the resolutions of May 1982. In her evidence the applicant said that the work was necessary as due to the condition of the building the appropriate local government authority could have forced the body corporate to have it carried out. She also deposed that her unit was not really affected by the state of disrepair as it did not face the sea or bad weather aspect.

7. In support of her application the applicant tendered two letters to the Deputy Commissioner from a firm of chartered accountants. These letters referred to W. Thomas & Co v FC of T [1965] HCA 54; (1965) 115 CLR 58, Case H24 (1957) 8 TBRD 93, Case F97 (1955) 6 TBRD 446, Case 95 7 TBRD 29, Case L13 11 TBRD 80, Case T13 18 TBRD 64, Case N96 81 ATC 513, The Law Shipping Company Ltd v C of IR 12 TC 621 and Odeon Associated Theatres v Jones (1972) 1 All ER 781. The second, inter alia, said "the amount involved would not justify your being represented but you may consider handling the matter yourself" and concluded as follows:-

We understand that in your own case:

1. the state of disrepair of the building did not interfere with its occupation and in the case of your unit, it was occupied by a tenant, before, during and after the repairs;

2. the purchase price which you paid was not reduced because of the state of repair of the building.

For these reasons you could argue that your case is more in line with the Odeon Associated Theatres case than the Law Shipping Case.

If the matter is heard by the Tribunal you could merely raise these issues and leave it for the Tribunal to make up its own mind.

As mentioned above, we would be pleased to give you some assistance in handling the case, should you so desire. If, however, you do not wish to proceed, the Administrative Appeals Tribunal should be advised as soon as possible.

8. The question at issue has been considered in a number of recent cases and in Case R101 84 ATC 673 Mr Roach said at p 675:

2. The facts have been detailed in the reasons of the Chairman and I adopt them as my own.

3. Close consideration of those findings of fact forces me to the conclusion that the painting carried out in the year in question went beyond merely "maintaining" the standard of the premises, and would be more accurately characterised as "restoring" the condition of the painted surfaces in question. Put another way, the expenditure was directed to bringing the premises back to a condition they had been in some years previously, rather than in simply maintaining their condition.

4. That being so the expenditure was an affair of capital (as in the Law Shipping Case (1923) 12 TC 621 and W Thomas & Co Pty Ltd v FC of T [1965] HCA 54; (1965) 115 CLR 58), rather than a matter on revenue account as in Odeon Associated Theatres Ltd v Jones 1972 1 All ER 681. However, I wish to emphasise that it is to be so characterised, not because the work was carried out so soon after the acquisition of the property, but because of the nature and character of what was done and because the doing of it was not occasioned by the income producing activities of the taxpayer.

In the same case the Chairman set out the facts and said at p 624/5:

8. Accordingly, in my opinion the expenditure does not constitute an allowable deduction. It did not arise out of defects caused by the income producing activities of the taxpayer and it improved the condition of the property. It is not a case where "regular maintenance" has been brought forward for some reason or other but one where an old property has been acquired in a condition where immediate work was required to bring it into a state in which it could be maintained by future "regular maintenance". It is also not a case where a property has been, after acquisition, used for income producing purposes for some reasonable period before work has been performed on it in which circumstance the need for the work has a degree of connection with such income producing activities. The expenditure being related solely to the profit yielding subject itself and not to the use thereof by the taxpayer for income producing purposes.

9. Since each case of this nature must be considered on its own particular facts (there is a danger in relying upon other decided cases where the facts are never precisely the same) I see no reason to analyse previous authorities. Particularly is this so when I have not adopted the "temporal" test urged on behalf of the Commissioner but have applied basic well established first principles and reached a decision on the facts. It is also unnecessary to consider Odeon Associated Theatres Ltd v Jones (1972) 1 All ER 681 wherein it was said that a tradesman who acquires a shop painted "two years before his purchase" will not when he repaints it three years later say "only three-fifths of the cost will be chargeable against revenue in my profit and loss account." for this is not such a case - there would in that situation be a cost arising "out of the wear and tear of the asset in the course of earning the profits of the business" but here there is not.

9. Having regard to the facts of the present case - an old building with a resolution for obtaining quotes for the replacement of the arch bars well before the applicant acquired her unit - it is considered the remarks set out above are apposite. The expenditure "was not occasioned by the income producing activities of the" applicant and is therefore not allowable.

10. For the above reasons the Tribunal affirms the Commissioner's decision upon the applicant's objection to her assessment for the year ended 30 June 1982.
