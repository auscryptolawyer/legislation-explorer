QT86/2125 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 107; (1987) 18 ATR 3426; 87 ATC 465 (24 March 1987)

Last Updated: 16 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Superannuation fund allowable deduction section 79 fund - whether investment in works of art disqualifies fund.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s.79.

re Whitely, Whitely v Learoyd (1886) 33 Ch D 347

Mahoney v FCT (1965) 39 ALJR 62H

DECISIONS AND REASONS

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No: QT86/2125

AAT Decision No 3347

Date: 24 March 1987

Place: BRISBANE

Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

The question at issue is whether an investment in works of art by the trustees of an approved superannuation fund thereby disqualifies the Fund from being a Fund to which section 79 of the Income Tax Assessment Act applies. The year of income in dispute is the year ended 30 June 1979.

  1. Taxable Income as assessed by the respondent is $693. The applicant trustees claim it should be reduced to $146 by allowing a deduction under sub-section 79(12) amounting to $547.
  2. The relevant statutory provisions are as follows:-

"79(1) In this section-

'prescribed asset' in relation to a superannuation fund in relation to a year of income, means an asset that was included in the assets of the fund at the end of the year of income;

'superannuation fund' means a provident, benefit, superannuation or retirement fund, ...;

(2) Subject to the succeeding provisions of this section, this section applies, in relation to a year of income, to a superannuation fund if -

(a) the fund is an indefinitely continuing fund established and maintained solely for either or both of the following purposes:

(i) the provision of superannuation benefits for each of the members of the fund in the event of the retirement of the member from any business, trade, profession, vocation, calling, occupation or employment in which he is engaged; and

(ii) the provision of superannuation benefits for dependants of each of the members of the fund in the event of the death of the member,

or for either or both of those purposes and for such incidental and ancillary purposes as the Commissioner approves;

(b) to (f) not relevant to the question to be decided)

and the terms and conditions applicable to the fund during the year of income have been approved by the Commissioner having regard to -

(g) (not applicable)

(h) (not applicable)

(i) such other matters as the Commissioner thinks fit and were complied with by the trustee of the fund during the year of income."

"(12) An amount equal to five per centum of the cost of prescribed assets of a superannuation fund to which this section applies in relation to the year of income is an allowable deduction in the assessment of the trustee of the fund in respect of income derived by the fund during the year of income."

  1. The relevant facts in this application are quite straightforward.
  2. In 1974 by a deed of trust the superannuation fund ("the Fund") was established for the benefit of a barrister at the private bar. Trustees were appointed under the deed and those trustees continue to hold office. On 24 May 1974 solicitors for the member of the Fund sought- approval of the proposed fund from a Deputy Commissioner of Tax t ion (Exhibit; D).
  3. By letter dated 24 June 1074 the Deputy Commissioner advised the solicitors that the terms of the draft deed satisfy the requirements of section 79 of the Income Tax Assessment Act and that formal approval of the Fund would be given on receipt of a copy of the executed deed (Exhibit E).
  4. A letter dated 27 June 1974 addressed to the Deputy Commissioner enclosed the executed deed (Exhibit F) which was formally approved by letter dated 26 June 1975 (Exhibit G).
  5. A copy of the executed deed dated 28 May 1974 is Documents T18-31 in the bundle of documents forwarded to this Tribunal via the Taxation Board of Review by the respondent Commissioner.
  6. The trust deed is unexceptional in its form and I need only draw attention to the following clauses:

"4 The Fund shall be maintained as an indefinitely continuing Fund solely for the purpose of providing individual personal benefits pensions or retiring allowances for the member in the event of his retirement from his work as hereinafter provided and in the event of the death of the member for his dependants."

12 Any monies in the hands of the Trustee upon the trust hereof shall be held by the Trustee upon trust to invest the same in its absolute discretion in its own name or in the name of any company approved by the Trustee as nominee of and custodian trustee for the Trustee in:

(i) ...

(ii)...

( iii)....

(iv) in the purchase of any interest in any other real or personal property approved by the member."

10. During the year of income ended 30 June 1975 the trustees purchased an oil painting and three prints for a total outlay of $488. In following years a number of oil paintings were purchased together with an acrylic painting and a water colour painting. Japanese woodcuts costing $220 were also acquired. Exhibit J sets out the details of the works of art acquired and Exhibit H sets out the amounts invested in works of art on a fiscal year basis. The latter exhibit also sets out the proportion of the total cost of assets (reduced by the amount of any unrepaid borrowings) which was invested in works of art. In fact the trustees have failed to make annual determinations of the rent with the consequence that the rent has remained at $150 per annum in accordance with clause 4 of the agreement.

14. Document T5 is a copy of a notice of assessment issued to the trustees of the Fund on 24 December 1980. Perhaps the date of issue explains why the Commissioner failed to give any explanation as to the basis of assessment; the Fund being assessed for tax on a taxable income of $693. As it transpires the Commissioner failed to allow a deduction calculated in accordance with sub-section 79(12) and also failed to allow a rebate (s160 ACA) claimed in the return. The latter matter has since been corrected by an amended assessment notified by notice dated 3 June 1982 (Document T13).

  1. The trustees duly objected (Documents T6-7), the Commissioner disallowed the objection and on 16 November 1981 the trustees requested that the decision on the objection be referred to a Taxation Board of Review. The Commissioner complied with that request on 21 February 1985. The matter was transferred to this Tribunal on 1 July 1986.
  2. The Tribunal was informed that an objection on the same issue for the year ended 30 June 1981 has been allowed by the Commissioner. The year ended 30 June 1979 is the only year in dispute before the Tribunal.
  3. The Tribunal heard oral evidence from the member, one of the trustees, an expert witness called by the applicant and an expert witness called by the respondent.
  4. The member of the Fund gave evidence as to the acquisition of the artworks and the basis upon which they were acquired. It was clear that the works had been acquired by the trustee with his approval and after he had obtained expert advice as to the long-term value of the works. It was also established that the member has acquired works of art jointly with his wife and these are separate from the works acquired by the Fund.

19. It was made clear to the Tribunal that the member was convinced that the works of art in question were a good long-term investment which would serve the purpose of providing for his retirement.

20. The trustee's evidence was to the effect that although the member had suggested (and approved) the acquisition of the works of art the trustees had exercised their own discretion in making the purchases. In making their decisions they relied upon the same expert as had advised the member. The trustee also gave evidence that although the trustees agreed with the acquisition of the works of art they did not agree to incurring storage charges in respect of those works and it was the responsibility of the member to arrange suitable storage. The trustees were aware that some of the works were located in the member's home while others, those subject to the lease, were hanging in his chambers. There was no doubt in the trustee's mind that the leasing agreement was authorised by the Trust deed. The trustees took the view that quality works of art were a good investment, they having been advised that such works were increasing in value.

  1. Evidence was given by two experts, one called by the applicants and one called by the respondent. Both were persons of considerable relevant experience. There was little divergence in their evidence.
  2. The expert called by the applicants had prepared a. detailed written valuation of the works of art in question. The valuations accorded the various works indicate that the view of the trustees as to these works being a good investment was well founded. Quite substantial increases in value were accorded most of the acquisitions.
  3. Although the expert witness called by the respondent Commissioner had not viewed the works of art she was familiar with the work of the artists in question. She tended to be a bit conservative as to her valuations, which is understandable given that she had not viewed the works, but her evidence also established that substantial increases in value had taken place in respect of most of the acquisitions.
  4. There is no doubt in my mind that the trustees have made purchases which should result in substantial profits for the Fund when the works of art are sold and can quite properly be regarded as sound long term investments albeit they may be unorthodox investments for superannuation funds.
  5. Furthermore there is no doubt in my mind that the trustees were authorised by the trust deed to invest in the works of art. Such acquisitions come within the terms of paragraph 12(v) of the deed (Document T23), had been acquired in accordance with the deed and were therefore authorised by the deed.

26. Document T32 is the statement which the respondent Commissioner was required to supply to the Taxation Board of Review when the matter was referred to the Board. That statement records the Commissioner's reasons for disallowing the claim as follows:-

"The income of the taxpayer has correctly been assessed under Section 121DA of the Act as the fund is not one to which Section 79 of the Act applies."

27. The real reasons for disallowance of the claim are contained in Exhibit C which is an Objection Report prepared by one of the respondent's officers. The principal paragraph of that report reads as follows:

"S121DA has been applied in view of the funds activities. Reference is made to para 65 of C.I.T.C.M. 855 investments in speculative activities are clearly outside those made by conventional super, funds and should not be accepted for the purposes of section 79. Investments in art fall into this particular category and as such it is submitted that the objection be disallowed ... investments in paintings are not suitable superannuation fund investments because they are inherently speculative in nature."

28. It was not explained to the Tribunal by the respondent in the light of the above as to why the Fund, without any significant difference in the facts, had been allowed the section 79 deduction in prior and subsequent years. The evidence is that the subsequent acceptance of the Fund which still retained (and had increased) its holding of works of art was not due to oversight.

29. Counsel for the respondent Commissioner submitted that trustees of a superannuation fund should not invest in speculative investments. I find it bemusing that in making that submission it was directed to the works of art and not to the shares in mining companies also held by the Fund. Each of the mining companies is a reputable large company but anyone with a passing knowledge of the stock exchanges would know that the price of shares in mining companies tends to fluctuate quite markedly in the light of prevailing perceptions in the market place. The evidence does not support the submission that the subject investments in works of art were of a speculative nature, there apparently being negligible down side risk.

30. Counsel directed the Tribunal's attention to the decision in re Whitely, Whiteley v Learoyd (1886) 33 Ch D 347. In that decision all three Judges of the Court of Appeal addressed themselves to the duties and liabilities of trustees. Colton LJ considered the rule relating to the duties and liabilities of trustees to be as follows:-

"They must take such care in conducting the business of the trust as a reasonably cautious man would use, having regard, not only to the interests of those who are entitled to the income, but to the interests of those who will take in future.

Lindley LJ expressed the principle at page 355 as follows:

"The duty of a trustees is not to take such care only as a prudent man would take if he had only himself to consider; the duty rather is to take such care as an ordinary prudent. man would take if he were minded to make an investment for the benefit of other people for whom he felt morally bound to provide.

31. In Mahoney v FCT (1965) 39 ALJR 6 7 at 63-4 Owen J, relying on the dicta of the Court of Appeal, summarised the law in the context of a superannuation fund as follows:

"Like other trustee they must of course conduct the business of the fund in the interests of present-and future employees with the same care which a prudent, honest man of business would exercise if he were investing money for the benefit of those for whom he felt bound to provide or those who have claims upon him ... If they fail to do so, that is a relevant circumstance in considering whether in fact the fund is being applied for the benefit of employees."

  1. Applying the tests laid down by the Courts the only possible basis for saying that the trustees failed in their duty as trustees is to assert that works of art per se are speculative investments and are not therefore suitable investments for a superannuation fund.
  2. The respondent also submitted that works of art were not a normal investment for a superannuation fund. While it might be true that there is some element of speculation in investing in works of art, such investments when made on the advice of experts are likely to be sound investments. That is the evidence in this case where the experts have established that there has been a substantial increment in value to the Fund in respect of the works acquired by the trustees.
  3. As for the submission that works of art are not normal investments of superannaution funds, although there was no evidence on the point I think I can take official notice of the fact that as a general rule superannuation funds invest in securities, loans and real property.
  4. However, I have been unable to discover any basis in either the trust deed or section 79 for holding that works of art are not an acceptable investment. My attention has not been drawn to any decision on the point and my researches have failed to locate such a decision.
  5. Some of the paintings are generating income for the Fund but all the works appear to be generating gains for the Fund on capital account. That is, I would have thought, evidence of the sound investment policy of the trustees.
  6. I would uphold the applicants' claim on those grounds.
  7. In case I be wrong on the question of the investments I should also deal briefly with the alternative argument put by Mr Davies QC for the applicants.
  8. In essence Mr Davies argued that the Commissioner had specifically approved the trust deed and the trustees had carried out the terms of the deed faithfully and without breach during the year of income. Therefore it was not open to the Commissioner to disallow the claim if it is established that the trustees had complied with the terms and conditions applicable to the Fund during the year of income. In fact those terms and conditions had been approved by the Commissioner and were complied with by the trustees, and I so find.
  9. Paragraph 79(2)(i) must apply at the time the Commissioner approves the Fund. It is not therefore open for the Commissioner to introduce "such other matters as the Commissioner thinks fit" in a particular year of income. These matters must be dealt with at the time the Commissioner approves the Fund. A subsequent non-compliance by the trustees with matters so dealt with may justify the Commissioner denying the deduction.
    1. This is not a case of non compliance by the trustees.
      1. The respondent's objection decision dated 18 September 1981 will be set aside and the objection allowed.

This and the preceding thirteen (13) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT86/2125.

Deputy Registrar
