Foley and Tax Agents' Board [1990] AATA 657 (10 September 1990)

Foley and Tax Agents' Board [1990] AATA 657 (10 September 1990)

Last Updated: 6 June 2008

Tax Agent - Refusal of Registration as a tax agent - whether applicant is a fit and proper person - whether applicant holds prescribed academic and experience qualifications.



Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 : s.43

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 : ss.251BC, 251J, 251K(2), 251K(2A), 251L, 251QA, Sub-Reg.N58CA

Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No 2) 1988 : ss.2(4), 39, 46, 55(21), 55(24)



David Earl Pty Ltd v. Tax Agents' Board (Senior Member Beddoe) -11 September 1990

Re Gray (1989) 20 ATR 1017

Tax Agents' Board v. Seymour 90 ATC 4262



DECISION AND REASONS



Re: MICHAEL DAVID FOLEY

APPLICANT



And: TAX AGENTS' BOARD

RESPONDENT



No: N89/98



Date: 11 September 1990



Place: Sydney



Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



DECISION



The Tribunal decides that the decision under review be affirmed.



(Sgd) K L Beddoe

(SENIOR MEMBER)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) N89/98

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )



Re: MICHAEL DAVID FOLEY

APPLICANT



And: TAX AGENTS' BOARD

RESPONDENT



No N89/98

AAT Decision No 6209

REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



Date: 11 September 1990



Place: Sydney



The applicant seeks review of a decision of the respondent Board refusing registration as a tax agent. The date of the respondent Board's decision is a matter of some uncertainty (Documents T4 and T12) but the parties proceeded on the basis that the relevant refusal is that of 6 December 1988 as notified to the applicant by letter dated 6 January 1989 (Document T20). I am satisfied, for the reasons which follow, that is the decision under review.



2. To be registered as a tax agent, an unregistered person may make application to a Tax Agents' Board ("the Board") in the approved form, accompanied by such information as is required by the form and by payment of a fee of $80 (s.251J of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act")).



3. Section 251L of the Act is the motivating statutory provision for an application for registration because it provides that a person shall not demand or receive a fee for or in relation to the preparation of an income tax return etc unless the person is a registered tax agent. Subsection 251L(4) qualifies the general provisions of the section in relation to solicitors, but not to the extent of permitting preparation of income tax returns for a fee by a solicitor.



4. Section 251J of the Assessment Act provides by sub-section(1) that a person desiring to be registered as a tax agent may make application to a Board for registration. Subsection 251J(3) as it applied prior to 1 November 1988 provided that where an applicant satisfies the Board that he is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters then the Board shall register the applicant as a tax agent. Subsection 251J(3) was amended with effect from 1 November 1988 by the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No 2) 1988 ("the Amendment Act") so as to in effect repeal the previous subsection. However, by virtue of subsection 55(21) of the Amendment Act, subsection 251J(3) continues to apply as if the amendments made by section 46 of the Amendment Act had not been made in respect of an application for registration made in respect of an application for registration made before 1 November 1988 which was neither granted nor refused before 1 November 1988.



5. In deciding whether such an applicant is a fit and proper person in terms of the former subsection 251J(3) the Board (and this Tribunal) is required to take into account section 251BC which came into operation on 1 November 1988. That is made clear by subsection 55(21) of the Amendment Act.



6. By virtue of subsection 251BC(1) a person is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters, as at a particular time if:



(a) the person is not a natural person;

(b) both of the following conditions are satisfied;



(i) the person was not registered as a tax agent, or as a nominee for the purposes of this Part immediately before the commencement of section 39 of the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No 2 1988);

(ii) the person does not hold such qualifications (whether academic by way of experience or otherwise) as are prescribed;

(c) ...

(d) the person is not of good fame, integrity and character;

(e) ...; or

(f) ... .



(Section 39 of the Amendment Act came into operation on a day fixed by proclamation - s.s.2(4) of the Amendment Act - there is no evidence of the date proclaimed but the Tribunal's research indicates that the date is 1 November 1988 (Butterworth's Annotations).



7. Sub-regulation 58CA(1) prescribes relevant qualifications for the purposes of subsection 251BC(1) as follows:



(a) ...



(b) the person:



(i) shall have completed the academic requirements for admission as a barrister or solicitor of the High Court or of the Supreme Court of a State or Territory;

(ii) shall have:



(A) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding five years;



(B) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent that the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in sub-subparagraph (A); or



(C) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as the Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant employment as referred to in sub-subparagraph (A);



(iii) shall have, by written examination or examinations set by a college of technical and further education (or an examination or examinations of an equivalent or higher standard), successfully completed a course of study in basic accounting principles; and



(iv) shall have, by written examination, successfully completed a course of study in Australian income tax law acceptable to the Board.



8. "Relevant Employment" is in turn defined by sub-regulation 58CA(2) as follows:



"58CA(2) In this regulation `relevant employment' means employment by a person or a partnership or as a member of a partnership in the course of which there has been substantial involvement in income tax matters including:



(a) the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns;

(b) the preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued in respect of such returns; and

(c) the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns, assessments or objections."



9. Section 251QA provides that application may be made to this Tribunal for review of a decision by a Tax Agents' Board refusing to register a person as a tax agent. For the purpose of conducting such a review the Tribunal may exercise all the powers and discretions conferred by the Act on the Board (s.43(1) Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975).



10. The applicant is a solicitor practising on his own account. He is also the controlling mind, but not a director, of a company (David Earl Pty Ltd) which carries on business as registered tax agents. The applicant was a nominee for the purposes of Part VIIA of the Act in respect of David Earl Pty Ltd but ceased to be a nominee after he failed to obtain renewal of his registration as a tax agent in 1987. That failure to obtain re-registration arose out of his application for renewal being mislaid with the consequence that the Board was required to cancel his registration pursuant to then subsection 251K(2A). It is not relevant to this application to determine where the fault lay in the fact of the alleged mislaid application for re-registration. The result was that after many years as a registered tax agent the registration was cancelled for failure to re-register. No other explanation for the cancellation has been suggested before the Tribunal.



11. By an application dated 16 June 1988 the applicant applied for registration as a tax agent. That application was made under the then statutory provisions. It set out the applicant's experience in tax matters (as a tax agent or nominee of a tax agent). Suffice it to say that the applicant demonstrated a wide general experience in income tax over the previous eleven years (Documents T5-T7).



12. By a standard form letter dated 1 July 1988 the respondent Board wrote to a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation seeking information to assist the Board in determining whether or not the applicant was a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns etc. The first four questions asked related to the applicant's own income tax returns, the fifth question sought details of any conviction for taxation offences and the final question was a "catch all" question seeking any other information which would assist the Board (Document T9). The questions asked were as follows:



(a) Years of income for which income tax returns have not been lodged;



(b) has any income tax liability, which is required to be paid, been paid;



(c) if income tax returns or income tax liability are outstanding, has any action been commenced to either secure the lodgment of those returns or the payment of the liabilities;



(d) has the applicant ever had his income tax affairs subject to an investigation or audit by your office, if so, the result of that investigation or audit;



(e) has the applicant ever been convicted of any offence against the Income Tax Assessment Act;



(f) any other information which you consider would assist the Board in determining the application.



13. By a similar standard form letter dated 11 July 1988 the Australian Taxation Office replied by supplying information. That advice included the following in response to question (a):



"Taxpayer has not lodged returns since 1979. The 1980 return was demanded but no follow up action was taken as the taxpayer was a T/L7."



In relation to question (b) the Taxation Office response was "yes". I do not know how the Taxation Office responded to the next four questions because those responses, if any, have been blanked out on the copy document before the Tribunal (Document T10). As to why the respondent Board considers it necessary to withhold such information from this Tribunal I do not know. I will, however, infer that the Taxation Office answers to those questions were favourable to the applicant. The answers to questions (a) and (b) support this inference.



14. Document T12 is an extract of the Board's minutes of 6 September 1988 which record that the Board decided not to grant registration in these terms:



"The Board gave consideration to Mr Foley's application for registration as a tax agent, individual. The Board was of the opinion, after specific examination of a report on Mr Foley's personal taxation affairs from the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Sydney, that it could not grant registration in this case and therefore directed the Secretary to advise Mr Foley to show cause as to why the Board should consider him fit and proper pursuant to the provisions of subsection 251J(3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act.



Further, the Board felt that the matter also affected Mr Foley's registration as a nominee of David Earl Pty Ltd.



The Board directed the Secretary to advise Mr Foley to provide an explanation of his taxation affairs and to show cause as to why the Board should not consider him no longer a fit and proper person to be a nominee of David Earl Pty Ltd in accordance with the provisions of subsection 251K(2), paragraph (c) of the Act.



The Board also directed the Secretary to advise the Company to show cause as to why the Board should not use its discretion in accordance with subsection 251K(2), paragraph (c) of the Act and cancel the company's registration.



All of these replies are directed to reach the Board in adequate time to receive the Board's proper consideration at its next meeting."



14. If that decision, as recorded by the minutes constituted refusal of the application then that would be the end of the matter, there being no jurisdiction in this Tribunal to review refusals made prior to 1 November 1988.



15. However, that is not the case because the Board did not regard itself as having finally dealt with the application. This is somewhat obliquely made clear by the minutes of the Board's meeting held on 6 December 1988 which are before the Tribunal as Document T19 and read as follows:



"Mr Foley was considered for individual registration as a tax agent at the Board's meeting of 6 September 1988. The Board refused his application on the basis of an adverse report from the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation regarding his personal taxation affairs.



The Board noted that Mr Foley was the sole registered nominee of the company tax agent David Earl Pty Ltd. Accordingly the Board directed that Mr Foley and the company be asked to show cause as to why the Board, having found Mr Foley not to be a fit and proper person to be registered as a tax agent in his own right, should not be of the opinion that it also cancel the registration of the company.



Replies from Mr Foley and the company were received by the Board on 9 November and 14 November respectively.



After consideration of the above the Board noted that Mr Foley's application had not been refused at the meeting of 6 September, rather that it had been of the opinion, after consideration of a report on Mr Foley's personal taxation affairs from the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, that it could not grant registration as it was not satisfied that he was a fit and proper person pursuant to subsection 251J(3) consequently directing that he be asked to show cause as stated above.



The Board was not satisfied that Mr Foley had provided satisfactory explanation nor had he shown sufficient cause so decided that it formally refuse Mr Foley's application for registration as a tax agent."



That the minutes are confused is immediately obvious. However, I take the view that it is the first paragraph which is incorrect. If that were not the case there would be no purpose in the fourth and fifth paragraphs of the minutes. On this basis I accept that the application for registration was refused by the Board at its meeting on 6 December 1988. I therefore find that this Tribunal has jurisdiction to review the refusal of 6 December 1988. That jurisdiction to review the Board's decision depends upon paragraph 251QA(a) of the Act. That section came into operation on 1 November 1988 and applies to the present case by virtue of sub-section 55(24) of the Amendment Act which provided, in effect that paragraph 251QA(a) of the Act applied in relation to decisions of a Board made after 1 November 1988.



16. The advice which was sent to the applicant by the Secretary of the respondent Board dated 6 January 1989 (Document T20) suggests that the decision of 6 December 1988 may have been qualified in some way not recorded in the minutes. It stated, inter alia:



"The delay in advising you of this decision is regretted; however in reaching its decision, the Board also requested that I again contact the Australian Taxation Office to see if there had been any changes to the situation of the non-lodgment of your income tax returns. Advice received from that Office on 21 December 1988 indicates there are still no returns lodged since that of 1979 year."



17. The issue is clearly the non-lodgment of income tax returns over an extended period. That seems to me to be a perfectly legitimate question for the respondent Board to be concerned with and in the ordinary case is a valid consideration in deciding whether or not the applicant is a fit and proper person (s.251J). It is not, however, the function of the Board to enforce the provisions of the Act concerning lodgment of returns. That is a function of the Commissioner of Taxation at first instance and ultimately the courts. In the present case, for reasons unknown to this Tribunal, the Commissioner of Taxation has clearly decided not to take any action to enforce lodgment of the applicant's outstanding income tax returns. I infer that the returns are regarded as being outstanding because the applicant has said they are. But the Commissioner of Taxation has taken no action over a period of nearly ten years, to obtain lodgment of the returns. It must be inferred that the Commissioner is not concerned with the position. Certainly the Commissioner is aware of the position as evidenced by the Australian Taxation Office letter of 11 July 1988 (Document T10).



18. In view of the position adopted by the Australian Taxation Office I do not consider it appropriate to deny an applicant registration as a tax agent merely on the ground that he has failed to lodge income tax returns when it is clear that the Commissioner of Taxation has decided not to enforce lodgment of the returns. To decide otherwise would be to incorrectly assume jurisdiction to in effect enforce the lodgment of income tax returns not so enforced by the Commissioner of Taxation. This is not a case of the type dealt with in Re Gray (1989) 20 ATR 1017 where the tax

agent had regularly failed to pay assessed income tax. Equally the case would be different if the applicant had failed to comply with demands (or orders) for lodgment of returns.



19. The applicant is a solicitor of many years standing practising on his own account as a sole practitioner. For the reasons already stated the Amendment Act requires the Tribunal to consider whether the applicant is not a fit and proper person in terms of subsection 251BC(1). In particular, I am required to consider whether the applicant holds the prescribed qualifications by way of academic qualifications and experience in relevant employment or the equivalent thereof.



20. Subregulation 58CA(2) defines relevant employment to mean employment by a person or a partnership or as a member of a partnership in the course of which there has been substantial involvement in income tax matters. In Tax Agents' Board v. Seymour 90 ATC 4262, a case concerning refusal of registration of a self employed accountant, the Federal Court (Pincus J) made it clear at page 4266 that a self employed person does not come within the definition of relevant employment in sub-regulation 58CA(2) nor does such self employment constitute employment equivalent to being engaged in relevant employment. In that case the applicant was a self employed accountant who prepared income tax returns in the name of registered tax agents and who had not himself been previously registered as a tax agent. It is the applicant's previous experience as a tax agent which particularly distinguishes the facts of this case from the facts in Seymour.



21. In prescribing out the experience qualifications required in Regulation 58CA no mention is made of prior experience as a tax agent. The regulations do not envisage that a self employed person previously registered within the preceding five years as a tax agent will be able to count such experience as relevant employment or employment equivalent to relevant employment. In view of the dicta of the Federal Court in Seymour as to the meaning of "employment" in paragraph 58CA(1)(C) that paragraph cannot be construed to include previous experience as a self-employed registered tax agent.



22. For reasons which appear in David Earl Pty Ltd v. Tax Agents' Board (Decision 11 September 1990) I am unable to find that the applicant was an employee of David Earl Pty Ltd at any relevant time.



23. I am therefore obliged to find that the applicant is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns because he does not hold the necessary qualifications by way of experience as prescribed by Regulation 58CA for the purposes of section 251BC of the Act. I should add that there is no evidence to support a finding that the applicant has complied with paragraph 58CA(1)(b)(iii) of the Regulations.



23. The decision of the Board will be affirmed.



This and the preceding ten (10) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of MICHAEL DAVID FOLEY and TAX AGENTS BOARD - N89/98.
