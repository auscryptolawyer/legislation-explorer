ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - income tax - assessable income - sale of leased plant by lessee - whether excess of sale price over pay-out figure assessable - title to new equipment not acquired by taxpayer

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 19, 25, 26, 26AAA, 190

Taxation Administration Act 1953 s. 14ZG

A. L. Hamblin Equipment Pty. Limited and The Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1974] HCA 44; (1974) 131 CLR 570

REASONS FOR DECISION

22 April 1988 Deputy President R.D. Nicholson

Objection Decision Under Review

The Applicant seeks review of a decision by the Respondent including in the taxpayer's assessable income for the 1980 year of income the amount of $114,573 said to represent "profit on sale of a) Fiat Allis HD41 dozer and b) Wabco truck".

It is not necessary to consider a further objection against the assessment of additional tax in view of agreement by the Commissioner to delete that amount.

The reason given by the Commissioner for disallowing the first objection was that the amount said to be profit was correctly included under section 19, sub-section 25(1), paragraph 26(a) or section 26AAA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").

Evidence

The Tribunal had before it documents pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (as modified by section 14ZG of the Taxation Administration Act 1953) and exhibits received during the hearing. In addition to receiving two sworn affidavits from the Applicant the Tribunal heard sworn evidence from him.

Onus of proof

It is important in this proceeding to bear in mind the requirements of section 190(b) of the Act that the burden of proving an assessment is excessive lies upon the taxpayer.

Facts

On 21 May 1979 the Applicant (as lessee), on behalf of the business name under which he carried on the business of earthmoving, entered into a lease agreement with a finance , company, Ajax Discount Company Limited (as lessor) ("Ajax") to lease for 36 months from 18 May 1979 a second hand bulldozer. The agreement did not contain any provision for termination although clause 23, under the heading "Damages", provided for a sum to be paid by the lessee to the lessor upon termination of the leasing by other than effluxion of time.

On 17 September 1979 the Applicant entered into a further lease agreement with Ajax whereby he undertook to lease on behalf of the same business a dump truck for a period of 36 months commencing on 12 September 1979. Save as to the scheduled particulars, the agreement was in the same form as the agreement previously mentioned and included a clause 23 to the same effect.

In October 1979 the Applicant was engaged as a sub-contractor to carry out earthmoving at Eastlakes. The Applicant's evidence was that some of the equipment which he was using was in a bad state of mechanical repair and new equipment was required. He wanted to upgrade the equipment and to generate some working capital..

To bring this result about the Applicant had dealings with a representative of Ajax, which company is no longer in business. He appointed Lennie's Leasing to obtain financing for the purchase of his equipment.

On 22 November 1979 representatives of a supplier of equipment, Faulks Pty. Ltd. ("Faulks"), visited the Applicant at Eastlakes and the following day proposed to him that Faulks would supply a front end loader and two dump trucks ("the new equipment") for a ' total price of $345,000. That price was to be less a trade-in in respect of the dump truck and bulldozer then leased from Ajax ("the old equipment") at a total price of $190,000.

The Applicant's evidence was that he negotiated with Ajax and that Faulks did not do so on his behalf. He instructed Faulks to deal with Ajax direct from the date of the offer to purchase. His evidence on the course of events was also that they were "really all intertwined".

On 7 December 1979 Ajax advised Faulks it was prepared to purchase the new equipment for onward lease to the Applicant for purposes of his business upon condition that it would receive the sum of approximately $75,000, which equated to the pay-out value of the old equipment at that time.

On 12 December 1979 the Applicant signed an offer to purchase from Faulks the new equipment at the above price and including provision for the above trade-in allowance of $190,000. The Applicant's evidence was that he had entered into a verbal agreement to purchase the new equipment from Faulks subject to finance approximately 1 month before signing the purchase order.

On 13 December 1979 the sales manager of Faulks wrote to the Applicant's business confirming arrangements for the purchase of the new equipment and the provision of the trade-in allowance. This proposed the payment of $175,000 from Ajax to Faulks for two items of the new equipment; payment from Faulks to Ajax of $98,231.84 (being pay out of dozer $48,231.84 and investment of $50,000); payment of a further $170,000 from Ajax to Faulks on delivery of the third item of new equipment; payment from Ajax to Faulks of $77,195.39 (being pay out of truck $27,195.39 and investment of $50,000); and a cheque to the Applicant for $14,572.77. The Commissioner seeks to assess as income to the Applicant the two sums of $50,000 and the cash payment of. $14,572.77 which total $114, 573. This amount is the difference between cost of the old equipment at the pay out figures and the price paid or allowed by Faulks as the supplier of the new equipment for that same equipment as a trade-in.

In addition, an agreement was entered into whereby the Applicant would buy back the dozer for the price of $105,000 within 6 months of delivery of the new equipment and in the meantime he would have uninterrupted use of it at a rate of $2,000 per month for which promissory notes were to be provided.

The depreciation schedule for the Applicant included in his return for the 1980 year of income shows the disposal of the dozer at $150,000 and the truck at $40,000 as each occurring on 16 December 1979.

Cheques necessary to give effect to the transaction as proposed were exchanged in correspondence dated 19, 21 and 27 December 1979.

Section 26(a)

On behalf of the Applicant it was submitted that the facts in this proceeding were akin to those in A.L. Hamblin Equipment Pty. Limited and The Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1974] HCA 44; (1974) 131 CLR 570 so far as that decision dealt with trade-ins. On that point Barwick C.J., McTiernan and Jacobs JJ. with Mason J. dissenting, held that no part of the trade-in price represented assessable income of the taxpayer since the reality of the situation was that the trade-in was not a sale at the price allowed on the trade-in, but was a device having the purpose of obtaining a reduction in the effective price of the new equipment.

In relation to trade-ins Barwick C.J. agreed with the reasons for the conclusion of Jacobs' J. that "the trade-in is not a sale at the price allowed on the trade-in" (at 577). The reasons of Jacobs J. on that point (at 588) make it apparent that he is referring to a trade-in as being a case where the taxpayer himself purchases the new article after trade-in of the old article. That, however, is not the position disclosed by the facts in this proceeding. Here, the effect of the arrangements entered into and payments made was that the new equipment was purchased by Ajax, Although the Applicant signed the "Offer to Purchase" the true character of the arrangements is that described in the letter of 7 December 1979 from Ajax to Faulks. That is confirmed by the payments made between those companies.

Jacobs J. (at 589) distinguished the case of a usual trade-in from that of another of the matters before the Court in Hamblin (supra) by saying that if the equipment purchased by the finance company had been purchased by the taxpayer company, there would be a simple case of trade-in against the purchase of articles of a capital nature. That, however, was not what occurred in relation to an article described as the Le Tourneau scraper and it is the decision of the High Court in Hamblin (supra) in relation to that item which most closely resembles the facts in this proceeding.

Jacobs J. also said that the finance company was charged the full price (as is the case here) so that there was no saving on the capital purchase. Nor was there a resale by the finance company to the taxpayer company so that the receipts on the scraper in that case could be regarded in a real and business sense as a set off against the purchase by the taxpayer company of capital equipment with a consequent purpose of obtaining the benefit and effect of the trade-in. There, as here, the finance company leased the new equipment to the taxpayer so that the position is that the items of old equipment were acquired by the Applicant from Ajax for the purpose of profit-making by selling them at the higher price which was available.

Mason J. (as he then was) reached the same result in relation to the Le Tourneau scraper. At 580 he said:

"... the taxpayer acquired the unit with the intention of selling it for more than the amount of the pay-out figure. The trade-in allowance on each unit was ascertained before the pay-out figure was determined and only after the pay-out figure was settled was a binding order for new equipment placed. Accordingly, the units were acquired by the taxpayers when there was a certainty that they would be disposed of at a profit and the acquisitions were made with that profitable disposition as the sole end in view."

It is apparent that the binding order for new equipment was not placed until after the pay-out figure was settled since the order when signed by the Applicant contained particulars of the pay out. The ascertainment of the trade-in allowance was a necessary pre-requisite to determination of the pay-out figure.

At p. 581-2 Mason J. discussed the question of identity, namely whether the property sold was the property acquired for profit-making by sale. He concluded that in order to succeed the appellants must show that all they acquired for the purpose of profit-making by sale was the reversionary interest which the finance company had, subject to the respective lease agreements. In his opinion the appellants in that case failed to establish that, the onus being on them to do so. His conclusion that "The evidence as to the relevant transactions with the finance companies was equivocal, leaving open the possibility that the finance companies passed absolute property in the units to the appellants on the footing that the lease agreements should be treated as being terminated, the appellants not wishing to complete the contracts according to their terms and thereby continue the bailments" is apposite here.

Barwick C.J. and McTiernan J. were contra Mason and Jacobs JJ. in respect of the Le Tourneau scraper so that the decision of Stephen J. that the amount was assessable income, from which the appeal had taken place, was affirmed. The reasoning of these judgments has been thoroughly explored in Case N59 81 ATC 305 and requires no further elaboration here. See also Case S34 85 ATC. Viewing the facts in the present proceeding in the light of these decisions the Tribunal concludes that the taxpayer is correctly to be assessed to tax as income in his hands on the difference between the cost of leased equipment when sold to him by the finance company at the pay-out figure and the price paid or allowed by the supplier for that same equipment as a trade-in.

In view of this conclusion it is unnecessary for the Tribunal to consider the application of section 19, sub-section 25(1) or section 26AAA.

Conclusion

For the reasons given the Tribunal will affirm the objection decision under review.
