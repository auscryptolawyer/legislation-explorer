Re Kevin Eugene Begaud and Commissioner of Superannuation [1982] AATA 113 (9 June 1982)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re : KEVIN EUGENE BEGAUD

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. N81/50

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mr R.K. Todd (Senior Member)

Mr L.G. Oxby (Member)

Dr E.L. Davis (Member)

CATCHWORDS

superannuation _ endorsement 'hypertension' on benefit classification certificate _ refusal to revoke benefit classification certificate _ whether request for reconsideration of decision to issue certificate or request for reconsideration of decision not to revoke or vary certificate

Superannuation Act 1976, ss.16, 154

Superannuation Act Amendment Act 1978

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s.37

HEARING

CANBERRA

9:6:1982

ORDER

The Tribunal decides to affirm the decision under review.

DECISION

1. The applicant became an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") on 3 July 1978 and accordingly, on 5 July and 31 July 1978, he was medically examined for the purposes of s.16 of the Act. The Commonwealth Medical Officer ("CMO") who examined him reported on 21 August 1978 that the applicant did not meet the general medical standard but that he was 'fit for appointment/ employment'. His report referred to blood pressure readings of the applicant's own doctor and to medication (Chlotride and Valium) that the applicant was said to be taking and he found 'hypertension benign and mild'. His report included the recommendation that:

'...the applicant's Benefit Classification Certificate should be varied to include the conditions (if any) outlined above.'

2. In fact of course no such certificate was then in existence. No doubt the recommendation was taken as recommending that one should issue, and on 6 December 1978 a benefit classification certificate (a 'certificate') specifying 'hypertension' was issued and a copy thereof forwarded to the applicant.

3. On 28 December 1978 the applicant wrote to the respondent a letter in the following terms:

'I refer to your correspondence of December 6 enclosing a benefit classification certificate issued under Section 16(4) of the Superannuation Act and your advice that I would be eligible for a reduced benefit if the condition shown on the certificate is the cause of my retirement.

I wish to exercise my right under Section 154 of the Superannuation Act and request a reconsideration of this decision.

The grounds for such request are:-

(1)The decision was influenced not only by the results of the tests, but by medication prescribed by my own doctor to reduce high blood pressure which had not been previously necessary up to the time of the examination nor has such medication been necessary since. My medical history of blood pressure tests prior to and since the examination without medication have, from my own doctor's and her locum's results, proved to be within quite acceptable limits.

(2)The tests for the purposes of superannuation were conducted within a short period of my resuming a new and senior position with the Australian Meat and Live-Stock Corporation and which followed consequentially on my voluntary retirement as an elected member in Local Government. At the time of this quite complete change in my community involvement and interests which I had pursued for a decade, I was Shire President of Warringah Council.

(3)Adjustment to these changes in employment, position and life style has been ephemeral and I repeat that medication for high blood pressure was discontinued after only a week or two and has not been necessary since.

(4)The decision taken by you did not have the benefit of my prior and present history of blood pressure tests.

(5)My health and physical characteristics are excellent.

(6)The decision to place me on reduced benefits is not in keeping with the general state of my health and I believe has been taken unfairly and discriminatorily vis-a-vis other contributors.

Should you schedule a re-examination, I would appreciate it if you would take steps to obtain details of my blood pressure tests from Dr O. Klika, Starkey Street, Forestville 2087, who is and has been my personal physician for some years."

4. On 6 December 1979 the respondent having arranged for a further medical examination wrote to the relevant CMO a letter in the following terms (so far as material):

'Mr Begaud has requested the Commissioner for Superannuation to reconsider his decision to issue a Benefit Classification Certificate specifying the condition hypertension.

2. Mr Begaud has authorised the release of details of blood pressure readings conducted by his private practitioner Dr O. Klika. The Authority is attached.

3. Earlier medical papers together with a copy of the Benefit Classification Certificate are also enclosed for your information. Documentations provided by Mr Begaud in support of the request is also enclosed.

4. The purpose of the medical examination is to establish whether, in the opinion of the examining medical officer, the condition(s/) should remain in the Benefit Classification Certificate, i.e. whether by reason of the condition(s/) Mr Begaud is not likely to continue to be an eligible employee until attaining maximum retiring age of 65 years.

5. Should the latest medical examination disclose conditions which the examining medical officer considers would have been known to the examinee to have been present at the time of the initial medical examination and were not disclosed by him, specific mention of the condition(s/) and related circumstances should be included in, or attached to the report(s/).

6. ...

7. ...

8. ...'

5. The further examination took place on 8 February 1980. The CMO recorded a satisfactory blood pressure and stated in his comments: 'Blood pressure now normal. No treatment for hypertension for over 12 months. Sick leave record satisfactory.' He considered that the applicant met the general medical standard, and expressed the opinion that the applicant's certificate should be 'revoked'.

6. This certificate certainly reads as if what the CMO was doing was considering whether the certificate should be revoked or varied. Indeed he can hardly be blamed for so doing when regard is had to the terms of paragraph 4 of the letter of 6 December 1979 that is set out in paragraph 5 above. But what was, in legal terms, the application that was being considered that had led to the further medical examination?

7. The answer to the above question goes to the ambit of the decision that had been made and thus in turn to the jurisdiction of the Tribunal to deal with the present application. A discussion of this issue was the substance of a memorandum by the Senior Member of the present Tribunal as a result of a Directions hearing conducted in relation to this application for review. The full hearing of the application has not persuaded us that the conclusions drawn in that memorandum ought to be varied and, so far as the matter of ambit and jurisdiction is concerned, what follows is in essence what was said in that memorandum.

8. Two courses had been open to the applicant. First, he could have sought reconsideration, pursuant to s.154 of the Act, of the decision taken, pursuant to s.16(4), to issue the certificate. Where this course is taken, reconsideration follows and, if the original decision is confirmed or varied, s.154(6) provides that application may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of that confirmed or varied decision. The Tribunal does not have jurisdiction to review a decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation taken under the Act unless it has been reconsidered pursuant to s.154.

9. Secondly, the applicant could have sought revocation or variation of the certificate. Section 16(6), (7) and (8) as amended provide so far as relevant:

'(6) Where a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of an eligible employee, he may, on grounds specified in the request, request the Commissioner to revoke or vary the certificate and, where an eligible employee makes such a request, he shall, within such period as the Commissioner requires, undergo such further medical examination (if any) or examinations by an approved medical practitioner or practitioners as the Commissioner requires.

(7) A report or reports of the result or results of the further medical examination or examinations shall be furnished to the Commissioner.

(8) The Commissioner shall consider the report or reports (if any) and such other matters as he considers relevant, and _

(a) where there is not more than 1 physical or mental condition specified in the benefit classification and the Commissioner is of the opinion that the person is not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with that condition, to cease to continue to be an eligible employee before he attains his maximum retiring age _ the Commissioner shall revoke the certificate;'

If a decision were taken not to revoke or vary the certificate, reconsideration could then, again pursuant to s.154(4), have been sought of the decision not to revoke or vary. In fact of course application for variation of the certificate would presumably not have been the appropriate course in this particular case.

10. For what then did the applicant ask? And what was done in response to his request? In other words for which of the courses set out in paragraphs 8 and 9 above did he opt?

11. The applicant's letter of 28 December 1978, which is set out in paragraph 3 above, seems to us quite clearly to have been a request for reconsideration of the decision that had been taken pursuant to s.16(4) of the Act to issue the certificate. The letter referred specifically to 'a benefit classification certificate issued under s.16(4) of the Superannuation Act' and stated: 'I wish to exercise my right under s.154 of the Superannuation Act and request a reconsideration of this decision.' It is true that the grounds stated went beyond, although they included and perhaps concentrated on, the situation prior to the issue of the certificate. But the request was for reconsideration under s.154 of a decision to issue a certificate under s.16(4).

12. The response to the applicant's request for reconsideration is set out as paragraph 9(a) of the decision on reconsideration which was lodged as part of the statement lodged pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. The following reproduces part of the Decision on reconsideration:

'9(a)Notwithstanding the opinion of the Commonwealth Medical Officer I am not prepared at this time to revoke the decision to issue a benefit classification certificate in respect of Mr Begaud specifying 'hypertension'. It is clear from the medical evidence that Mr Begaud suffered from hypertension in varying degrees from December 1977 into 1978 and was under treatment for it.

(b) ....

(c) I have also noted from the terms of Mr Begaud's request for reconsideration that his change in employment upon becoming an eligible employee under the Superannuation Act 1976 in July 1978 brought about a complete change in his community involvement and interests which he had pursued for a decade, and further, from the report of his private doctor in August 1978, that his blood pressure had responded to disciplined attention to diet and exercise. But the fact remains that Mr Begaud suffered from hypertension for a period and I am not yet satisfied that there will not be a recurrence in the event of circumstances arising akin to those that existed in 1977/78.

(d) For these reasons and having regard to Mr Begaud's present age of 44 years, I am of the opinion that Mr Begaud is not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with the physical condition 'hypertension' to continue to be an eligible employee until he attains his maximum retiring age of 65 years."

It is to be noted that the use of the word 'revoke' in the third line of paragraph 9(a) of the Decision on reconsideration quoted above does not carry any inference that revocation of the certificate was the subject of the reconsideration. The words used are 'to revoke the decision to issue a ... certificate'. And 'revoke' was an appropriate word to use in the context of reconsideration given that it was itself inserted in s.154(4) of the Act by virtue of the Superannuation Act Amendment Act 1978 which came into force on 24 April 1978. It is true that in paragraphs 9(c) and (d) the matter is taken further and comments made which would appear to be relevant to a question of whether the certificate itself should now be revoked, rather than to a question of whether it should have been issued in the first place, but it is clear from paragraph 10 that the decision on reconsideration was not treated as involving consideration of a question of revocation of the certificate but rather of the decision to issue the certificate. It is there stated:

'10. This decision on reconsideration does not prevent Mr Begaud requesting revocation of his benefit classification certificate, pursuant to sub-section 16(6) of the Act, at some future date at which time consideration could be given to the stability of his medical condition during the intervening period....'

13. For his part, the applicant contended that a mere technicality was being raised against him. On this point his submission at the hearing did not appear to add to what he stated in a submission presented by him prior to the full hearing and pursuant to the Directions hearing. The following is extracted from that submission:

'In my request to the Commissioner of December 28, 1978 for a review under Section 154 the substance of the request was the Benefit Classification Certificate and I also referred to a re- examination. Indeed, the whole purpose of my approach was to seek a re-examination so that the Benefit Classification Certificate could be revoked and it is not surprising to me that the medical examination in February 1980 was conducted for that purpose. Indeed, the CMO following that examination specifically recommended that the Benefit Classification Certificate be revoked.

It seems rather pointless for the Commissioner to be relying on legal technicality when clearly a re-examination took place under Section 16(6) which followed my request to the Commissioner for a review under Section 154 for no other purpose than to have the Benefit Classification revoked. That subsequent examination proved that the Classification Certificate could not be sustained.

It was the Commissioner who scheduled the examination and I submit, this was done, knowing that a Benefit Classification existed, and that revocation was being sought under the terms of Section 16(6). Furthermore, that these things were in fact done in reviewing the decision which I requested under Section 154 only serve to support the actual purpose of the exercise.'

14. The fact is, however, that the Act makes a distinction between a request for reconsideration of _

(a) a decision to issue a certificate, and

(b) a decision not to revoke or vary a certificate.

This is a technicality in the legislation but that does not make it a mere technicality to apply the legislation. The applicant himself drew a distinction at the hearing between technicality and illegality. It is not within the power of this Tribunal to ignore legislation simply because in a particular instance it appears to make a merely technical distinction.

15. While it is possible to sympathise with the applicant's view that the re-examination and the report that followed it contained elements that would have been appropriate in the context of the administration pursuant to ss.16(7) and 8(a) of a request for revocation made pursuant to s.16(6), we are satisfied that there has been no decision not to revoke or vary the certificate. We should add that even if we were led to conclude that there had been a decision not to revoke or vary the certificate (as opposed to a decision not to revoke the decision to issue the certificate) the applicant's position would not at all be advanced since there has been no reconsideration of such a decision and consequently there would not be jurisdiction to review such a decision.

16. Before leaving this aspect of the matter we should like to make the following points in regard to the certificate itself. Having inspected a copy of what appeared on the back of the certificate, in what is apparently a standard form, we would agree that the procedure for revocation or variation pursuant to s.16(6), (7) and (8) of the Act is set out, but the reference to s.154 which follows is quite general and there seems to be nothing to indicate to the person affected that it is open to him to complain of the issue of the certificate in the first place. Were it not for the specific references to revocation and variation, a reference to s.154 may conceivably have been enough. But it seems to us that as the form stands it does not clearly explain the existence of the two options. The same comment applies to Notes (b) and (c) on the front of the certificate. The whole topic is, after all, a complex one and since these certificates can confidently be expected to issue to persons far less competent to comprehend them than the present applicant, who despite his obvious skills and ability has had trouble with the matter, some clarification would be desirable.

17. It remains now to consider the decision not to revoke the decision to issue the certificate. The relevant provision is s.16(4) of the Act which states:

'(4) The Commissioner shall consider the report or reports, and such other matters (if any) as the Commissioner considers relevant, and, if he is of the opinion that the person is not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with a physical or mental condition or conditions referred to in the report or reports, to continue to be an eligible employee until the person attains his maximum retiring age, the Commissioner shall issue a benefit classification certificate to that effect, being a certificate in which the relevant condition or conditions is or are specified.'

18. The applicant did not argue that the original decision of 6 December 1978 to issue the certificate was, on the evidence, wrong. On the contrary, he frankly admitted at the hearing in reference to that evidence that: 'What was on that piece of paper, what was written, could not have led to any other decision.'

Later he was asked the following question in relation to the medical examinations of 5 and 31 July 1978:

'...you are conceding that on the evidence revealed by that medical examination _ or those two medical examinations _ perhaps we can leave out the valium for the moment, it may not be significant _ that the Commissioner came to a correct decision. He was justified in deciding to issue a certificate having regard to that. Is that your concession?'

The applicant replied:

'Yes, I think a reasonable person would have to say that.'

19. The applicant submitted, however, that the medical report of 21 August 1978 referred to in paragraph 1 above contained misleading information concerning the medication he was taking. He claimed that he was not in fact taking valium at the time of the medical examinations. Moreover, he stated that he would not have been taking Chlotride had he then known what he alleged to be the respondent's practice of taking the use of Chlotride as a sufficient basis of itself for the issue of a certificate endorsed with the condition 'hypertension'. Hence, he submitted in effect that his use of Chlotride was at the time a luxury in the management of his hypertension and that he could have controlled the problem as he had done since, he said, by proper attention to diet and exercise.

20. Valium is not an anti-hypertensive drug and, therefore, the question as to whether or not the applicant was taking this drug is not strictly relevant to the issue before us. As to the Chlotride, the applicant's own admission at the hearing speaks for itself:

'I went to see my doctor just prior to the examination on 5 July 1978. As can be seen some of the blood pressure readings had been a little high and she put me on Chlotride.'

It is noted also that in his letter of 28 December 1978 (paragraph 3 above) the applicant used the word 'necessary' in relation to the medication prescribed for his blood pressure at the time.

21. The matter therefore reduces to the question whether upon the medical evidence, including the results of the examination of 8 February 1980, the applicant's physical condition at the time of the original decision in December 1978, was such that it should be concluded, in terms of s.16(4) of the Act, that a certificate should have been issued.

22. In addition to the CMO's report dated 8 February 1980 we have for the purpose of our consideration of the applicant's condition at the relevant time taken into account a report by Dr Klika, the applicant's doctor, dated 1 March 1982, which set out 13 blood pressure readings taken between September 1978 and January 1982 inclusive. Even in these readings there are some instances of an abnormally elevated blood pressure. However, it is noted that Dr Klika stated in the report that she considered the applicant's 'blood pressure to be quite stable without any hypertensive treatment'. The applicant also asked us to take into account his excellent record as to sick leave in relation to his employment. We are nevertheless not persuaded that the medical evidence subsequent to that which was the basis of the original decision casts sufficient, if any, doubt on the correctness of that decision or of the evidence upon which it was based.

23. Since we have found no reason to impugn the evidence available in December 1978 as to the applicant's medical condition at that time we are led to ask whether that evidence should lead to a decision to issue the certificate pursuant to s.16(4) of the Act. The applicant himself conceded that the decision to issue the certificate on the basis of that evidence at that time was the correct decision. And it is our opinion that, in view of the evidence of the applicant's condition in December 1978, the applicant was a person in respect of whom the requirements of s.16(4) were met and the decision to issue the certificate was correct on the merits.

24. The applicant made much of a submission that such a decision would cause him grave prejudice. We are quite unable to agree with this. If his condition is, as is to be hoped, as satisfactory as he now believes it to be, it remains open to the applicant to request revocation of the certificate pursuant to s.16(6) of the Act.

25. In the circumstances the decision of the Tribunal is to affirm the decision under review.
