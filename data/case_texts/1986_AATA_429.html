TT85/486 and Commissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 429; (1986) 18 ATR 3146; 87 ATC 167 (19 December 1986)

Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Assessable Income - Taxpayer employed by one employer as seaman - Lump sum payment to taxpayer when ship decommissioned - Whether employment terminated - Whether "bona fide redundancy payment"

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) (Cth) Division 2, Subdivision AA, Section 27A, 27F

Case S17 85 ATC 213

Case J33 77 ATC 324

Case M47 80 ATC 323

No. TT85/486

AAT Decision No 3115

19 December 1986

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No.

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And:

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The Applicant is a seaman. He was born in 1937 and at the relevant time was aged 46 years. He started working in the maritime industry in 1954 and, apart from a period of some 12 years, has pursued the career of a seaman since that time. As a member of a seaman's union he was eligible to procure employment through a system known as the "Seaman's Engagement System" under which he belonged to the A roster. The engagement system was operated on an industry basis and ensured that seamen who attended on a daily basis seeking work were offered work or would be paid "attendance money" if no work was available for them. If work was available but was refused the seaman could be suspended from the roster. When work was available under the roster system a seaman signed articles with the ship for the particular voyage. At the completion of each voyage he was paid all entitlements arising during the course of the voyage by the person employing him and his name was then returned to the roster to await the next employment opportunity.

2. On 4 August 1976 there was a major change in the circumstances of the Applicant. He came to be employed "permanently" by the Union Steam Ship Company of Australia Limited ("Union Steam Ship") as a member of a regular crew for the "Seaway Prince". Thereafter for several years he had only one employer. He was no longer employed from occasion to occasion for the duration of each voyage only. He was no longer returned to the roster at the expiration of each voyage and he was no longer "paid up" all entitlements at the conclusion of each voyage. Instead he was paid fortnightly as is a common practice in relation to persons in long-term employment.

3. On 28 November 1983 there was yet another substantial change in the circumstances of the Applicant. The Seaway Prince was decommissioned by its owners and the employment of the Applicant terminated at the conclusion of his last voyage before the decommissioning. For some few weeks to follow he continued to receive workers compensation benefits to which he had become entitled while in the employment of the company. Upon decommissioning he received payment, subject to on-going workers compensation entitlements, from Union Steam Ship of all monies to which he was entitled under his contract of employment with that company. In addition he received a further amount of $3,992 being payment which resulted from "negotiations" conducted between employer representatives and union representatives in relation to the decommissioning. The amount he received was calculated upon the basis of two weeks' wages for each completed year of service and pro rata for each completed month of service prior to 31 August 1977 and one week's wage for each completed year of service and pro rata for each completed month of service after 31 August 1977. Earlier employment with Union Steam Ship was not taken into account. The only period taken into account was the on-going service of the Applicant on the Seaway Prince which period was identical in time to his period of continuous employment with Union Steam Ship. The Applicant was not entitled to receive that payment pursuant to any statutory or industrial award or any contract of employment. In that sense payment by the employer was gratuitous on its part. The Applicant did not profess to know what arguments had persuaded the employer that the payments ought be made. According to the evidence of the employer, admitted by consent, upon the payment being effected:-

"Tax instalments were deducted, and the payment included in gross earnings in column one of the Group Certificate, in accordance with instructions from the Taxation Office where an employee does not terminate from the maritime industry"

4. When the Applicant completed his period on worker's compensation following decommissioning he returned to the roster system. He obtained some work at sea on a "voyage only" basis and he also received attendance money. His income tax return to 30 June 1984 showed that he had earned $3,132 in a period of approximately 6 consecutive weeks with a particular employer (not Union Steam Ship) gaining remuneration at the rate of approximately $520 per week; and a further $2,049 from Australian Shipping Commission, the employment being described as "casual" but providing an amount which averaged throughout the remaining period of the fiscal year following decommissioning amounted to some $85 per week. How that record is to be reconciled with the Applicant's statement that he received attendance money was not explained, but that causes no difficulty because it was not disputed that the Applicant continued after decommissioning to derive income from within the industry and from time to time to derive income from within the industry as an employee. By the time of the hearing the Applicant had again come to be established in "permanent" employment with one employer within the industry.

5. The issue for determination is whether the whole of the $3,992, or some part of that amount greater than 5%, constituted assessable income. The Applicant concedes 5% of the amount received to be assessable income and so presented his income tax return. As appears from the foregoing account of the circumstances giving rise to the payment, those circumstances are substantially the same as those giving rise to a decision in Case S17 1985 ATC 213 - a decision which related to two earlier Board of Review decisions Case J33 77 ATC 324 and Case M47 80 ATC 323 - decisions in which the Applicant claimed that section 26(d) of the Income Tax Assessment Act operated to constitute only 5% of the amount as assessable, income. In this reference however, the issue falls to be determined only after considering Sub-Division AA ("Superannuation, Termination of Employment and Kindred Payments") of Division 2 ("Income") of the Act: a sub-division which took effect in relation to the year of income ended 30 June, 1984, section 26(d) having been repealed.

6. At the hearing the Commissioner's representative presented a carefully prepared and helpful chart in diagrammatic form indicating the issues to be determined. The questions so proposed were first - was there a termination of employment? If that question was to be answered in the negative the monies received would be wholly assessable, but if answered in the affirmative then the second question would be whether there was "an eligible termination payment", being a payment for "bona fide redundancy". If that question was to be answered in the affirmative (as the Applicant contends it should be) then only 5% of the total amount constituted assessable income. However, if not a third question would be whether the taxpayer at the material date was over the age of 55 years because, although whether he was over 55 years or not, only 5% of the amount received in relation to the period up to 30 June 1983 would constitute assessable income, the maximum rate of tax to be applied to that part of the payment relating to the subsequent period would be 30% if the Applicant was under the age of 55 years and 15% if he was over the age of 55 years.

7. SUB-DIVISION AA commences with a definition section (section 26A) prefaced by the words "In this sub-division, unless the contrary intention appears, ... ". Among the terms defined are: -

“Bona fide redundancy payment", in relation to a taxpayer, means a bona fide redundancy payment in relation to the taxpayer ascertained under section 27F;

"Concessional component", in relation to an eligible termination payment, means so much of the eligble termination payment as consists of or is attributable to a bona fide redundancy payment, an approved early retirement scheme payment or an invalidity payment;

....; and

"Eligible termination payment", in relation to a taxpayer, means:

(a) Any payment made in respect of the taxpayer in consequence of the termination of any employment of the taxpayer, other than a payment -

(i) to which paragraph (b) applies;

(ii) of an annuity, or supplement, to which section 27H applies;

(iii) from a fund in relation to which section 121DA applies or has applied, in relation to the year of income commencing on 1 July 1984 or any subsequent year of income;

(iv) an amount to which section 26AC or 26AD applies; or

(v) of an amount that under any provision of this Act is deemed to be a dividend paid to the taxpayer;

(b) .... (through to) (j) ...."

8. Section 27F only relates to "eligible termination payments". It is appropriate therefore to first consider the application of the definition of that phrase. It is common ground that a payment was made; and that it was made "in respect of" the taxpayer. The crucial question is whether that payment was made "in consequence of the termination of any employment of the taxpayer". I am satisfied that it was for reasons stated in Case S17 (ante). Prior to the decommissioning the relationship between Union Steam Ship and the Applicant was that of master and servant under a single on-going engagement. They had mutual rights and mutual obligations. Apart from the carry over of workers compensation entitlements which had arisen during a period of service, those rights and obligations ceased on decommissioning and with that the relationship of master and servant ceased. I am satisfied that there was a termination of employment and that the payment made by the employer was in consequence of that termination. I further find that none of the provisos set forth in sub-paragraphs (i) to (v) of paragraph (a) have any application.

9. Having so found it is appropriate to now consider section 27F which provides:

"Where:-

(a) an eligible termination payment is made in relation to a taxpayer in consequence of the dismissal of the taxpayer from any employment at any time (in this sub-section referred to as the 'termination time') by reason of the bona fide redundancy of the taxpayerj

(b) the termination time was before -

(i) where there was a date or there were dates on which the termination of the employment of the taxpayer could occur by reason of the taxpayer attaining a particular age or completing a particular period of service - that date or the earliest of those dates, as the case may be; or

(ii) in any other case - the sixty-fifth anniversary of the birth of the taxpayer;

(c) if the Commissioner, having regard to any connection between the employer and the taxpayer and to any other relevant circumstances, is satisfied that the employer and the taxpayer were not dealing with each other at arm's length in relation to the termination of the employment of the taxpayer - the amount of the eligible termination payment does not exceed the amount of an eligible termination payment that could reasonably be expected to have been made in relation to the taxpayer if the employer and the taxpayer had been dealing with each other at arm's length in relation the termination of the employment of the taxpayer; and

(d) there was, at the termination time, no agreement between the taxpayer and the employer, or the employer and another person, to employ the taxpayer,

so much of the eligible termination payment as exceeds the amount of an eligible termination payment that could reasonably be expected to have been made in relation to the taxpayer had he voluntarily retired from that employment at the termination time is a bona fide redundancy payment in relation to the taxpayer."

10. As to paragraph (a) of that section, for the reasons already stated I am satisfied that there was an "eligible termination payment" made in relation to the Applicant and made in consequence of his dismissal from the employment of United Steam Ship. Further, I am satisfied that the reason for the payment was the circumstance that, following decommissioning, the services of crew members of whom the Applicant was one became redundant. Accordingly, I am satisfied that the eligible termination payment was made by reason of the bona fide redundancy of the taxpayer.

11. As to paragraph (b) I am satisfied that the termination time was before the sixty-fifth anniversary of the birth of the taxpayer, that anniversary providing the relevant date. As to paragraph (c) I am satisfied that the Applicant and the United Steam Ship were dealing with each other "at arms length in relation to the termination of the employment of the taxpayer" and that, being so, there is no scope for the exercise of discretion by the Commissioner pursuant to that paragraph. As to paragraph, (d), as the evidence stands I am satisfied that there was no agreement between the taxpayer and Union Steam Ship to employ the taxpayer and I am also satisfied that there was no agreement between Union Steam Ship and any other person to employ the taxpayer. The most that can be said in that regard is that there might have been an agreement between the employers of seamen that they would continue to recruit the services of seamen through the system known as the "Seaman's Engagement System" but that falls short of any agreement that they would employ the taxpayer, assuming that he wished to be employed. For those reasons I am satisfied that the "eligible termination payment" was wholly or in part "a bona fide redundancy payment".

A relevant eligible termination payment only constitutes a bona fide redundancy payment to the extent to which it "exceeds the amount of an eligible termination payment that could reasonably be expected to have been made in relation to the taxpayer had he voluntarily retired from that employment at the termination time...". The evidence of the taxpayer was that independently of the amount in question he received all that he was entitled to from Union Steam Ship under the terms of his engagement at the time of decommissioning. The whole of the payment in question was surplus to those entitlements and expectations. That being so I am satisfied that the entirety of the amount in dispute constitutes a bona fide redundancy repayment.

13. Having reached that conclusion it is clear that the amount in question constitutes a "concessional component" as defined and accordingly it falls to 'be assessed for taxation pursuant to section 27C (2) of the Act which provides:

"Where an eligible termination payment is made in relation to a taxpayer, the assesable income of the taxpayer of the year of income in which the eligible termination payment is made shall include 5% of the amount ascertained in accordance with the formula A-B, where -

A is the amount of the concessional component in relation to the eligible termination payment; and

B is so much of the amount represented by component A as is deemed to have been applied in accordance with section 27D."

14. As there is no suggestion in the evidence that the taxpayer elected to roll-over any portion of the payment as contemplated by section 27D, the assessable income of the taxpayer shall include 5% of the whole of the redundancy payment. That accords with the expectations of the Applicant as expressed in his return of income.

15. I uphold the objection of the taxpayer.
