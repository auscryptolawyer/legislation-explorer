ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

HEARING

CANBERRA

25:8:1986

DECISION

These applications arise under the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax Act) 1982 and relate to the validity and correctness of assessments raised thereunder.

2. When the applications (listed for hearing by notices of 6 December 1985) came on for hearing on 24 February 1986 the then existing Tribunal was asked to hear argument only on some preliminary points. It was decided "we will hear you on the preliminary points, but we do not undertake that that is all we will hear you on. We will take it from there on in once you have finished with your preliminary points". At the conclusion thereof the representative was requested to proceed in relation to the other issues as well and he then elected to lead no evidence. The Tribunal has accordingly heard the applications in full and is deciding them both in relation to the preliminary points and the other aspects i.e. in respect of the validity and correctness of the assessments raised.

3. In this regard I have had the opportunity of perusing the full reasons prepared by my colleague Mr Roach and, since I agree with the conclusions reached by him, it would be otiose to do other than join in the ultimate decision set out in para. 29 of his reasons.

In 1982 the Parliament of the Commonwealth enacted the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax - Vendors) Act 1982; the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax - Promoters) Act 1982; and the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982, all imprecisely but conveniently referred to collectively as "the TUCT legislation". For convenience I shall refer to them respectively as the "TUCT Vendors Act"; the "TUCT Promoters Act"; and the "TUCT Assessment Act". The constitutional validity of the TUCT legislation was unsuccessfully challenged before the High Court of Australia in MacCormick v F.C. of T. 84 ATC 4230. In their joint judgment Gibbs C.J., Wilson, Deane and Dawson JJ. gave an explanation of the TUCT legislation which I respectfully adopt as setting the background for the resolution of the issues before us in these proceedings.

2. Their Honours said:

"In these actions, which come before the Court by way of

demurrer, the plaintiffs attack legislation aimed at the

collection of moneys found to be irrecoverable by way of

company tax from companies which were stripped of the assets,

including profits before tax and any assets representing

provisions for tax, from which payment of the tax might

otherwise have been made or recovered. Such companies may

conveniently be referred to as 'target companies'. The

legislation seeks to impose liability upon the vendors of

shares where the sale of the shares was followed by a

stripping operation and upon those who promoted the scheme of

which the sale formed part. The liability is imposed upon

the vendor shareholders by the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax -

Vendors) Act 1982 (Cth.) and upon the promoters by the

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax - Promoters) Act 1982 (Cth.).

In relation to both those taxing Acts there is the one

assessment Act, namely, the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax)

Assessment Act 1982 (Cth.). In that regard, the trilogy of

Acts follow what Fullagar J. described as "the invariable

practice since the establishment of the Commonwealth",

namely, that the actual imposition and fixing of the rate of

tax is effected by a 'Taxing Act' while an 'Assessment Act'

provides for 'the incidence, assessment, and collection, of

the tax and for a variety of incidental matters' (Re Dymond

[1959] HCA 22; (1959) 12 ATD 1 at P 2; [1959] HCA 22; (1959) 101 CLR 11 at p 18)

The Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act ('the 1982

Assessment Act') provides for the calculation of a primary

taxable amount where shares in a company were sold under a

scheme between specified dates and under specified

conditions. 'Scheme' is defined broadly to mean any

agreement, arrangement, transaction, understanding or scheme.

The conditions which are specified include the requirements

that the rights attaching to the shares sold should have

conferred control of more than 90 per cent of the voting

power in the company, that the total consideration given for

the shares should have exceeded the net value of the assets

of the company after deducting its liabilities, including its

liability for tax, and that an assessment of ordinary company

tax or undistributed profits tax should have been made under

the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.), as amended. There

are further conditions that the period for objecting against

the assessment should have expired and that any objection

against the assessment should have been finalized, leaving

unpaid an amount of ordinary company tax or undistributed

profits tax, referred to as overdue company tax. A further

condition is that there should have been an arrangement or

transaction, whether or not part of the scheme, which secured

or achieved the result that the target company was unable,

having regard to other debts of the company, to pay the

overdue company tax.

It was stressed on behalf of the plaintiffs that the

condition that the consideration given for the shares should

have exceeded the net value of assets after deducting

liabilities, is so framed that it is likely to be satisfied

in the case of some ordinary commercial transactions. The

reason for this is that the amount of the 'liabilities' to be

deducted from the total value of the assets for the purpose

of ascertaining the net value of the assets is to be

calculated on the basis that there was an existing liability

in respect of unassessed primary tax on income which had been

earned and in respect of unassessed undistributed profits tax

which would be payable only if profits which had been earned

were not in fact distributed within a period which had not

expired. As a matter of commercial practice, it would seem

to be unlikely that, for the purpose of calculating the

consideration to be paid for shares in a company, a future

liability in respect of unassessed primary tax and a possible

future liability in respect of undistributed profits tax

which had not been assessed and which might never be payable

would be taken into account, without any discount, on the

same basis as if they were currently due and payable.

Where there is only one vendor shareholder, the primary

taxable amount in relation to that person is the whole of the

overdue company tax. Where there is more than one vendor

shareholder, the primary taxable amount in relation to each

person is that proportion of the overdue company tax which is

commensurate with the proportion of the total consideration

received by that person for the sale of his shares.

There are provisions dealing with the situation where the

shares sold involve more than one company which we do not

find it necessary to set out. Nor do we think it necessary

for the purpose of deciding the issues which arise in these

cases to set out the provisions which apply where the

ownership of the shares involves a trust or trusts.

It is provided that there shall be no primary taxable amount

in relation to a person where the Commissioner, having regard

to certain specified circumstances, considers that the result

would be unreasonable. It is also provided that where the

primary taxable amount in relation to a person is less than

$100 and the Commissioner considers that it should not exist

in relation to that person, then it shall be taken not to

exist or to have existed in relation to that person.

Where a primary taxable amount exists it constitutes,

together with any secondary taxable amount (to which we shall

refer shortly) a vendors taxable amount. A tax equal to the

whole amount is imposed by the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax -

Vendors) Act upon the vendors taxable amount. That tax is

referred to in the 1982 Assessment Act as vendors recoupment

tax.

Where a vendors taxable amount exists in relation to a

company and that company has ceased to exist, or where,

because of the sale of shares in that company or a holding

company, the Commissioner is of the opinion that it would be

unreasonable that the company should be liable to pay

recoupment tax on the vendors taxable amount or where the

Commissioner is of the opinion that recoupment tax payable on

a vendors taxable amount by a company is unlikely to be paid,

a secondary taxable amount exists. It exists, broadly

speaking, in relation to those shareholders who had rights in

a capital distribution by the company. The secondary taxable

amount in relation to each shareholder is that part of the

vendors taxable amount or the unpaid recoupment tax, as the

case may be, which is proportional to the shareholders

entitlement if a distribution of capital equal to the

consideration received for the sale of the shares in the

target company had been made immediately before a specified

time, ordinarily the time of the sale of those shares. This

tracing process is repeated until the liability to pay

recoupment tax falls upon individuals or upon companies which

are able to and can reasonably be expected to pay the tax.

A promoters taxable amount exists where shares in a company

were sold in circumstances which are the equivalent of those

giving rise to a primary taxable amount. The promoters

taxable amount is equal to 20 per cent of the overdue company

tax or, where no vendors recoupment tax has become payable or

will, in the opinion of the commissioner, become payable,

equal to the overdue company tax. The promoters taxable

amount is not, however, to exceed the value of the assets of

the company before it was stripped, less the sum of its

liabilities including company tax and the total consideration

given for the sale of the relevant shares. This limit upon

the promoters taxable amount appears to be designed to ensure

that a promoter is not liable to be taxed upon an amount

which is greater than the amount he received by way of

remuneration for his participation in the scheme.

The persons liable to pay tax in relation to a promoters

taxable amount are those who constitute the eligible

promoters class. That class comprises each person who

purchased the relevant shares, each person who acted as agent

for the purchaser or broker, each person who was an associate

of the purchaser at the time of the purchase and a number of

other specified persons.

Where a promoters taxable amount exists, a tax is imposed

upon that amount equal to the whole amount by the Taxation

(Unpaid Company Tax - Promoters) Act. This tax is called

promoters recoupment tax.

The persons included in an eligible promoters class are

jointly and severally liable to pay the promoters recoupment

tax in relation to which the class exists and, if one such

person pays any of that tax, he may recover by way of

contribution from any of the other persons included in the

class such part of the amount paid as a Court considers just

and equitable. He may also join as co-defendants the other

persons included in the class in any proceedings for recovery

of promoters recoupment tax relates. Where such a

declaration is made, the person concerned is deemed not to

have been included in the relevant eligible promoters class.

Provision is made for the reduction of the liability of a

person liable to pay recoupment tax where the overdue company

tax or recoupment tax is paid by some other person or where

there is a payment of promoters recoupment tax based on the

same overdue company tax. It is also provided that an amount

of promoters recoupment tax will be reduced where a payment

of the corresponding company tax is made. There is, however,

no provision for the reduction of promoters recoupment tax

consequent upon payment of vendors recoupment tax and in this

way the liability falls first upon the promoters within the

limits provided by the legislation. The purpose of these

provisions is evidently to ensure that the amount of the

overdue company tax is only collected once and that the

benefit of any payment of company tax or recoupment tax will

flow through to persons under a related liability. Payment

of recoupment tax does not, however, extinguish the liability

of the target company for the overdue company tax.

Whilst a penalty is imposed for late payment of recoupment

tax, provision is made that recoupment tax shall not be

increased on account of any additional tax payable by the

target company as a penalty for late payment that accrues

after the assessment for recoupment tax is made.

The Act contains provisions which enable those who may become

liable for recoupment tax to contest an assessment of company

tax in relation to the relevant target company. A

representative class is constituted comprising every vendor

shareholder who is a natural person and whose address is

known to the Commissioner, every vendor shareholder which is

a company still carrying on business and the other persons

traceable through a vendor shareholder liable to pay

recoupment tax based on the target company's assessment, but

if those persons exceed five in number, five of those persons

selected by the Commissioner as appropriate representatives

of those persons. Where a notice of assessment of company

tax payable by a target company has not been served on the

company and vendors recoupment tax is payable, or in the

opinion of the Commissioner, it is likely to become payable,

instead of serving the notice of assessment on the company,

the Commissioner is required to serve it on the company by

serving it on any of the persons liable, or likely to become

liable, to pay the vendors recoupment tax. Where there is

more than one such person, the Commissioner is required to

serve a copy of the notice on each person (other than the

person on whom the notice was served) who is included in the

representative class in relation to the vendors recoupment

tax.

Where there is only one person liable, or likely to become

liable, to pay the vendors recoupment tax and notice of the

assessment in relation to the target company is served upon

that person, that person has the same rights as the company

has under Div. 2 of Pt. V of the Income Tax Assessment Act to

object to the assessment. Where there is more than one

person liable or likely to become liable to pay the vendors

recoupment tax, one person only from the representative class

is entitled to the rights which the company would have had to

object to the assessment. That person is required to be

nominated by a notice signed by more than one half of the

persons included in the representative class.

Where a notice of assessment of company tax has not been

served on the target company and promoters recoupment tax is

payable or, in the opinion of the Commissioner likely to

become payable and if payable, is likely to be the only

recoupment tax payable, notice of assessment is to be served

on the person or any of the persons liable, or likely to

become liable, to pay the promoters recoupment tax.

Where a notice of assessment of company tax has been served

on the target company and vendors recoupment tax is, in the

opinion of the Commissioner, likely to become payable, the

Commissioner is required to serve a copy of the notice of

assessment on the person, if there is no more than one,

likely to become liable to the recoupment tax. Where there

is more than one such person, the Commissioner is required to

serve the copy notice upon any of the them and to serve a

copy of the copy notice on each other person included in the

representative class. If the notice of assessment was not

validly served on the target company, service in this manner

is deemed to be service on the company. Where only one

person in these circumstances is likely to become liable to

pay vendors recoupment tax and he is served with a notice of

assessment in this manner, then he is given the same rights

as the target company would have had to object to the

assessment if the notice had been served on the company on

the same day as it was served on that person. Where there is

more than one person likely in those circumstances to become

liable to pay vendors recoupment tax, one person from the

representative class nominated by more than half that class

is given a similar right to object to the assessment.

Where a notice of assessment of company tax has been served

on the target company and promoters recoupment tax is, in the

opinion of the Commissioner, likely to become payable and if

payable, is the only recoupment tax likely to be payable, the

Commissioner is required to serve a copy of the notice upon

the person or any of the persons likely to become liable to

pay the promoters recoupment tax and if service was not

validly effected upon the company, then service in this

manner shall be deemed to be service on the company.

The 1982 Assessment Act is a complex piece of legislation,

but the foregoing brief outline of its substantive provisions

is, we think, sufficient for the purpose of considering the

argument which was advanced. That argument was that the

taxes which the legislation purports to impose are beyond the

power of the Commonwealth."

3. The argument as to invalidity having failed in that case, the issues brought before us by the objectors as persons prospectively liable to recoupment tax, involve questions as to whether the TUCT legislation has been expressed in terms sufficient to make effective the initial steps in proceeding towards the end, perceived by both the objectors (the alleged vendors) and the Commissioner, whereby assessments of tax equal to vendor's taxable amount will come to be assessed against the objectors.

4. The challenge for the objectors was put with great thoroughness both in the preparation of the Objection and in the presentation of argument before us. (The Objection, comprising as it does some 64 pages and over 500 folios (a folio being a unit traditionally used in the law for costing purposes and comprising 72 words or figures) reflects the influence of modern technology and illustrates the need for a substantial revision in procedures for the resolution of tax disputes). Presentation of the argument before us in which over three days were given over to a preliminary point was no less thorough.

5. A basic contention for the objectors was that the "assessments" which had issued in relation to the companies and which had prompted them to object before us were nullities and that in consequence there were no objections which could be considered by this tribunal. If so any determination to be made would itself be a nullity. If this tribunal should not have entertained the arguments presented before us it must be for a higher authority than this tribunal to so declare. I only observe that the request for reference which was transmitted for review by the Commissioner together with its attendant documents appeared to be in order in all formal respects; and that, so far as is known to me, neither party has seen fit to seek any order from any competent authority to restrain the Board of Review from embarking upon and thereafter continuing to hear and determine the Objections so referred being reconstituted for the purpose as the Administrative Appeals Tribunal.

6. Although, as has already been noted in the passage quoted, the Acts constituting the TUCT legislation followed the pattern of a "Taxing Act" (providing for the actual imposition and fixing of the rate of tax) and an "Assessment Act" (providing for the incidence, assessment and collection of the tax and for a variety of incidental matters), it is appropriate at this point to observe that there are two major features in the TUCT legislation which distinguish it from the pattern of the legislation relating to income tax. The first distinguishing feature arises because the Parliament chose to incorporate in the TUCT legislation by way of cross reference, many provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Assessment Act"). The second distinction arises from the circumstance that the liability imposed by the TUCT Vendors Act was to be a function of the income tax liability of companies which, for any one or more of many reasons, may not have been assessed to income tax or might have failed to pay such income tax as had been effectively assessed.

7. The first of those distinguishing features is principally reflected in section 4(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act which provides:

"Unless the contrary intention appears in this Act, sections

6 and 7A, Part II, section 21, Parts IV and V, Division I of

Part VI, and Parts VII and VIII of the Assessment Act, and

regulations made under that Act, apply for the purposes of

the assessment and collection of recoupment tax, and the

collection of late payment tax, in like manner, mutatis

mutandis, as those provisions apply for the purposes of the

assessment and collection and income tax under the Assessment

Act."

8. It is instructive to consider which provisions of the Assessment Act were included by reference and also those which were excluded. The provisions which were included (with a short description of them) were as follows:

Section 6 Interpretation (the "definition" section)

Section 7A Application of Act in relation to certain other

territories.

Part II Administration: the office of Commissioner and

related matters.

Section 21 Consideration not in cash.

Part IV Returns and Assessments

Part V Objections and Appeals.

Div. 1 of

Part VI Collection and recovery of tax: general.

Part VII Penal Provisions and prosecutions.

Part VIII Miscellaneous.

9. The major provisions of the Assessment Act not incorporated by reference into the TUCT Assessment Act were:

Part I Preliminary (other than sections 6 and 7A)

Part III Liability to taxation (other than section 21)

Part IVA Schemes to reduce income tax.

PART VI Only insofar as it dealt with collection by

instalments of tax on companies; collection by

instalments of tax on persons other than companies;

provisional tax; collection of tax in respect of

certain payments for work; collection of

withholding tax; collection of mining withholding

tax; and deductions from certain withdrawals from

Australian film industry trust fund accounts.

Part VIIA Registration of tax agents

PART VIIB Medicare levy.

10. I am satisfied that the intention of the Parliament was that the TUCT Assessment Act, incorporating as it did the provisions of the Assessment Act referred to in section 4(1), should provide a comprehensive and sufficient basis with which to provide for all matters pertaining to the assessment and recovery of tax to be imposed on vendors and promoters pursuant to the TUCT legislation.

11. It is also important to note other features of section 4(1). The section opens with the common qualification "unless the contrary intention appears in this Act . . . .". That qualification recognises that some modifications of application of the Assessment Act provisions may be necessary in order to resolve an express or implied inconsistency between provisions of the TUCT Assessment Act. However, even more significantly it is provided in section 4(1) that the provisions incorporated by reference are to be applied "in like manner, mutatis mutandis". I understand that to mean that those Assessment Act provisions incorporated by reference are to be applied "with the necessary changes" - as the Shorter Oxford English Dictionary has it - or, as it was put by the Full Court of the Supreme Court of South Australia in Gill v State Planning Authority (1979) 20 SASR 580, "With all modifications and adaptations necessary to ensure that . . .".

12. The second difference to be noted results in provision being made by the TUCT legislation that persons who might ultimately be assessed as liable to pay recoupment tax in addition to being entitled to contest any assessment which may come to be made against them personally, may contest "assessments" to income tax made against the target companies as if those taxpayers were the target companies. In order that those taxpayers might have that opportunity to contest the assessment of the target company, provision had to be made for the "assessment" (if appropriate) of the target company; for the vendors potentially liable to the notified of the "assessment"; and for such taxpayers to have status in objecting to the target company "assessments".

13. Now the TUCT Assessment Act recognised that the process of assessing the target companies pursuant to the provisions of the Assessment Act (assuming that it had started at all) might either have been complete with the result that tax had been duly assessed and had become due and payable or have stopped short at any one of many earlier points in the process. That being so the legislation needed to provide for (a) the finalisation (if necessary) of a process fixing the quantum of the tax liability of the target company as it was or might have been (Step A); (b) ensuring that notice was given to the vendors of the establishment in the way of a tax liability in the target companies or of the Commissioner's contention as to the liability the target companies' would have had if they had been assessed pursuant to the Assessment Act (being either (i) a liability previously raised pursuant to the Assessment Act, or (ii) a liability or equivalent arising from action taken pursuant to the TUCT legislation) (Step B); and (c) to establish liability in the vendors as taxpayers by assessing them to tax liability pursuant to the TUCT legislation (Step C).

14. As there is no suggestion before us that the Commissioner has as yet contended that the taxpayers have any personal liability it is unnecessary hereafter to consider the legislative provisions touching Step C.

15. Dealing with Steps A and B the provisions of the TUCT Assessment Act are as follows. Section 18(5) deals with the situation in which a notice of assessment pursuant to the Assessment Act had already been issued. In that case nothing further remained to be done vis-a-vis the establishment of the liability in the target company. As between the Commissioner and the target company, subject to the resolution of any unresolved objections such as might validly have been made, the quantum of liability in the target company to the Commissioner was fixed and final. As a matter of fairness to the vendors it was only necessary to give notice to the vendors of the fact of that assessment having previously been made so that they might invoke in their own right, any challenge to the accuracy of any assessments so made against the company. Hence section 18(5) provided for service on the vendors of "a copy of the notice of assessment" previously issued against the target company. Even that obligation did not bind the Commissioner in all cases because section 18(6) provided:

"Sub-section (5) does not apply in relation to a notice

that has been served or purported to have been served on

a company if the Commissioner has reason to believe that

the service or purported service brought the contents of

the notice to the attention of the person, or any of the

persons, as the case may be referred to in paragraph

(5)(b)."

(Fortunately it is not necessary to embark upon any consideration of the correct interpretation of sub-section (6).) It is also noteworthy that any possible argument that the original notice of assessment was "not validly served on the company" was overcome by 18(5)(e) which provided:

"If the notice of assessment was not validly served on

the company, the service of the copy of the notice in

accordance with paragraph (c) shall be deemed to be

service on the company of the notice of assessment."

As there is nothing before us suggesting that any notice of assessment had already been served on the target companies in the sense contemplated by section 18(5), that provision does not assist the objectors.

16. Section 18(1) dealt with the situation in which an assessment pursuant to the provisions of the Assessment Act was still "required or permitted". In those circumstances the Commissioner could still serve his notice of assessment under the Assessment Act provisions - a situation which could exist where the company remained on the Register, even though lying on the harbour floor. In such a case it is quite feasible that if the notice of assessment had been served on the company in accordance with the provisions of the Assessment Act the fact of such service would not be known to persons such as the objectors. That being so section 18(1) provided that "notwithstanding section 174 of the Assessment Act" such a notice of assessment should "be served on the company by being served on" the vendors, whose rights of objection would then arise (section 18(3)). In that way section 18(1) authorised the action sufficient to provide for Steps A and B.

17. Section 15(4) of the TUCT legislation dealt with a situation in which the company had "ceased to exist" before any assessment of tax liability had been made pursuant to the Assessment Act. A company would have "ceased to exist" if it had either been dissolved following liquidation or "struck-off" the Register. The TUCT legislation did not purport to restore to the register such target companies as had ceased to exist. The legislation did not purport to provide for the assessment of now non-existent entities. Instead it provided as follows. If the company had "ceased to exist before an assessment was made" section 15(4) applied. It authorised the Commissioner to make an assessment of the ordinary company tax and of the undistributed profits tax that "would be payable by the company in relation to the relevant year of income if the company had not ceased to exist". The "assessment" authorised in each case was for convenience referred to as a "notional assessment" of "notional ordinary company tax" or "notional undistributed profits tax" (as the case may be). As by definition the company had ceased to exist it was quite unnecessary to make any reference to service on the company. Nothing more needed to be done in relation to Step A. However, Step B needed to be considered and section 15(7) provided that the Commissioner should serve notice of the "notional assessment" on vendors whereupon their rights of objection would arise (section 15(9).

18. It is appropriate to also consider the provisions of section 15(2) which applied where an assessment had been made of ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax before the target company ceased to exist. The sub-section distinguished two situations, the first where the company "ceased to exist before the tax became due and payable under the assessment" (sub-paragraph (a)); and the other where "the company ceased to exist after the tax became due and payable under the assessment" (sub-paragraph (b)). The problems dealt with by that sub-section did not require that any further consideration be given to Step B because, the assessments having been raised while the company was in existence, section 18 was sufficient to ensure that the vendors had notice of the existence of the assessment against the company and their right to object to it, whether or not the company had itself objected to the assessment raised.

19. On the material before us and insofar as the issue before us relates only to the preliminary points, it appears from the actions of the Commissioner that the Commissioner contends (a) that there were two target-companies (b) the objectors were beneficial owner of those companies up to a date prior to June 1974; (c) the companies ceased to exist prior to any assessment being raised against them either in relation to income tax or undistributed profits tax; (d) the companies would have been liable to income tax for the year ended 30 June 1974 if assessments had been duly raised under the provisions of the Assessment Act prior to the date on which they ceased to exist; (e) on or before 29 July 1983 the Commissioner calculated what would have been the liability of each company in relation to ordinary company tax and undistributed profits tax; (f) the Commissioner gave expression to that view by adopting notices of assessment; (g) the notices of assessment were expressed to be (i) in the case of income tax "a notice of assessment made pursuant to the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (as amended)", and in relation to undistributed profits tax "notice of assessment pursuant to Division 7 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (as amended)". The notices of assessment which so recorded the liabilities of the companies to income tax and to pay undistributed profits tax were addressed to them at their former respective addresses; (h) under cover of a letter of 29 July 1983 addressed to the objectors at their address for service the Commissioner forwarded notice of the aforesaid assessments in original type; (i) on 28 September 1983 the objectors lodged objections in relation to the assessments touching both target companies; (j) by notice dated 5 April 1984 the Commissioner gave notice disallowing the objections; (k) by notice of 29 May 1984 the objectors requested that the objections in all cases be referred to a Board of Review for review; (1) on 13 May 1985 and 26 July 1985 the Commissioner forwarded the four references to a Board of Review for review of his decisions; (m) all four references were listed for hearing before the Board on 24 February 1986 and the days following at which time the objectors appeared by their solicitor and thereupon thoroughly argued the preliminary points mentioned above. (On 28 July 1986, pursuant to the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act, the President appointed my colleague, Mr H.P. Stevens and myself to constitute the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for the purpose of continuing proceedings in these matters.)

20. It may be that if the tribunal had fully heard the objections and the evidence in support of them it might have found that the target companies had had no taxable income. I simply do not know. The only contention before us is that we should either hold that we have no jurisdiction to have entertained or to have embarked upon the hearing of the references or should acknowledge that all of the foregoing actions on the part of the Commissioner specified above are of no effect. I decline to so hold. The basic arguments for the objectors, and my comments on them, can be shortly stated.

The Repeal Argument

21. It was contended for the objectors that there could be no valid or effective assessment with reference to the target companies in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1974 because the Income Tax Act 1984 was repeated on 12 June 1981 (with effect from that date) by the Statute Law Revision Act (No. 61 of 1981). In support of that argument it was contended that section 8 of the Acts Interpretation Act could not apply to avert the consequences of the repeal effected by the Statute Law Revision Act. I think it unnecessary to consider that particular argument as presented. Section 4 of the Income Tax Act 1974 provided that "the Assessment Act is incorporated, and shall be read as one, with this Act". Section 10(A) of the Acts Interpretation Act provided "Where in any Act reference is made to any other Act, and that other Act is subsequently amended, then unless the contrary intention appears the reference shall, from the date of the amendment, be deemed to be to that Act as so amended".

22. That being so the Assessment Act as it has stood from time to time was incorporated with the Income Tax Act 1974.

23. Part XXV of the Statute Law Revision Act provided for the repeal of a number of Acts. It placed the Acts to be repealed into two groups. Those specified in Schedule 4 (dealing with Supply; Appropriation; Validation; Grants; Loans; Bounties; and miscellaneous Acts) were simply repealed (section 118). Section 119 dealt with the repeal of the Acts specified in the Schedule 5. Part I of the Fifth Schedule specified a number of Acts of the period 1972 to 1979 all of which imposed liability to income tax. The Income Tax Act 1974 was one of the Acts specified in that Part. Each of the Acts specified in that Part contained a like provision to section 4 of the Income Tax Act 1974. Section 119 of the Statute Law Revision Act provided:

"(1) Subject to this section, the Acts specified in

Schedule 5 are repealed.

(2) The repeal of an Act by this section does not

operate so as to repeal an Act incorporated with the

repealed Act.

(3) An Act incorporated with an Act repealed by this

section, being an Act specified in Part I of Schedule 5,

applies in relation to the tax or levy that was imposed

by the repealed Act as if the repealed Act were still in

force."

In my view it was the clear intention underlying sub-section (3) and a purpose effected by that sub-section that the Rating Act of 1974 continued in force for the purposes of the Assessment Act. The liability of individuals and companies to assessment in relation to transactions of that period continued. In the circumstances of this case, but for the fact that the target companies had ceased to exist prior to the enactment of the TUCT legislation, they could have been assessed to income tax and undistributed profits tax pursuant to the Assessment Act. But because the companies had ceased to exist such an "assessment" would have been an idle exercise. That being so, as a preliminary to raising an assessment against the vendors pursuant to the TUCT legislation, the TUCT Assessment Act provided that the Commissioner could make "an assessment . . . of the ordinary income tax . . . or undistributed profits tax . . . , as the case may be, that would be payable by the (target company) in relation to the relevant year of income if the (target company) had not ceased to exist". (s15(4)) Those assessments or calculations were referred to in the TUCT legislation as "(a) notional assessment" and section 15(7) provided for service on the vendors of "notice of the notional assessment". I find no substance in the contentions of the taxpayers first because in this context the TUCT legislation does not purport to raise any assessment against the target companies. Secondly, if there had purported to be any assessment in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1974 pursuant to the provisions only of the Assessment Act, such an assessment could have been properly raised fixing liability at the rates propounded by the Income Tax Act 1974 because for that purpose that Act had not been repealed by the Statute Law Revision Act 1981. (Having formed that view it is not necessary to deal with the arguments submitted in writing by, and in response to the Commissioner following the hearing.)

The Section 262A Argument

24. It was contended for the taxpayers that section 262A of the Assessment Act prohibits the raising of any assessment of income tax after the expiration of seven years from the close of the year of income to which any such assessment might relate. The submission has no merit. Section 262A finds its place in "Part VIII - Miscellaneous" of the Act. It provides:

"262A.(1) Subject to sub-section (2), every person

carrying on a business shall keep sufficient records in

the English language of his income and expenditure to

enable his assessable income and allowable deductions to

be readily ascertained and shall retain such records for

a period of at least 7 years after the completion of the

transactions, acts or operations to which they relate.

Penalty: Not less than $4 or more than $200.

(2) This section shall not require the preservation

of any records -

(a) in respect of which the Commissioner has

notified the taxpayer that their

preservation is not required; or

(b) of a company which has gone into

liquidation and which has been finally

dissolved."

25. The section does not in any way purport to regulate any aspect of the raising of assessments to income tax. It merely makes it an offence for persons carrying on business to fail to keep records as required for the minimum period nominated by the section.

26. The Assessment Argument As the matter stands before us it appears that neither target company existed at the date of "assessment". That being so section 15(4) of the TUCT Assessment Act applied. That sub-section required that the Commissioner should make "an assessment" which for convenience would be called a "notional assessment" of the ordinary company tax (or undistributed profits tax, as the case might be) "that would be payable by the (target company) in relation to the relevant year of income if the company had not ceased to exist". No provision of the TUCT legislation required the Commissioner to nominate which head of power he was purporting to exercise. (I do not overlook the circumstance that it seems that at the time the references together with required formal documents were submitted to the Board by a formal covering letter addressed only to the Chairman, the Commissioner referred in one case to a section 15 assessment and in the other to a section 18 assessment. At most that reflects some latter confusion and misunderstanding.) For present purposes I am satisfied that the "assessments" were raised in conformity with the provisions of the TUCT legislation and section 15(4) of the TUCT Assessment Act in particular.

27. The Service Argument In the circumstances appearing before us the TUCT legislation, and section 15(7) of the TUCT Assessment Act in particular, required that the "assessments" raised under section 15(4) should be served in accordance with the provisions of section 15(7). I am satisfied on the materials before us that that was done.

28. Burden of Proof Having heard in full the arguments on the preliminary points, the Board determined to proceed to finality with the reference. Neither the objectors nor the Commissioner placed any further evidence before us. It was submitted that in these proceedings the burden of proof lay against the Commissioner with the result:

(a) that the Commissioner had failed to discharge that

burden in that he had not established that he had ever

formed the opinion required by section 15(4)(b) of the

TUCT Assessment Act "that, if the company had not ceased

to exist and an assessment had been made of the ordinary

tax or undistributed profits tax, as the case may be,

payable by the company in relation to the relevant year

of income - (i) (such tax) . . . would have become due and

payable by the company in relation to the relevant year

of income; and (ii) if (that tax) . . . had remained

unpaid, recoupment tax would have become payable by . . .

(the objectors)"; and

(b) that, assuming that any assessment could be made, it

was the responsibility of the Board to make it pursuant

to section 193.

29. I reject the submission. Section 4(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act makes applicable in these proceedings all of the provisions of Part V of the Assessment Act - including section 190(b) - which, when applied "mutatis mutandis" as required by the TUCT Assessment Act, leaves the burden of proof in these proceedings on the objectors.

30. I would affirm the Commissioner's determination upon the objections.
