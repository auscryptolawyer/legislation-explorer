Last Updated: 30 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX – deductions - investment allowance - eligible property - property used by one company in providing services to another - whether grants of "right to use" property.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) - s 82AA.

Tourapark Pty Limited v FCT [1982] HCA 18; (1982) 149 CLR 176;

W A Hughes Pty Limited v FC of T 81 ATC 4317.

Applicant COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent No NT87/895

AAT Decision No 3926

Mr Peter M Roach (Senior Member)

20 November 1987 Sydney

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No NT87/895

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re :

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

20 November 1987 Mr Peter M Roach (Senior Member)

  1. On 29 May 1979 the Applicant requested the Commissioner to refer for independent review a decision made by the Commissioner upon an objection by the Applicant made 1 December 1978 to an assessment of income tax for the year ended 30 June 1976. By letter of 9 February 1987 (sic) the Commissioner complied with the request for reference. At the hearing, when enquiry was made, no explanation for the delay was forthcoming but the Commissioner's representative did tender an apology to the Applicant.
  2. The reference arises out of the activities in the year 1975 of a car dealership ("the dealership") in a major country town. The dealership through its Sales Division dealt in new and used cars and also in spare parts. In its Service Division it operated and maintained a workshop providing a wide range of services in the field of car-detailing, maintenance and repair but excluding body-works operations and some more specialized areas of service. The dealership was originally conducted by a single company ("Motors") on land owned by Motors. Motors was' wholly owned by its Directors - a married couple - and controlled by the husband.

3. During 1974, on the advice of its accountants, the dealership established a new company ("Services") which became a substantial shareholder in Motors. It was controlled by the same Directors. With the establishment of Services the staff of the old Service Division and the plant and equipment previously used in that Division by Motors were transferred to Services. Thereafter Services operated with its own payroll quite distinct from that maintained for the purposes of the Sales Division of the dealership and its administration. Then with the establishment of a new workshop on the site owned by Motors, in order to provide better facilities for the operation of the Service Division, new plant and equipment was acquired by Services. It was installed in the building so as to be utilised by the staff of Services employed in the Service Division in carrying out works of maintenance and repair on motor vehicles. The staff of Services were the only persons to operate the plant and equipment. The plant and equipment comprised two vehicle hoists; a wheel aligning machine; an electronic engine tuner; and lesser items, including an air conditioner and computer equipment, used in the Service Division. Each item of property was capable of constituting a "unit of eligible property" for the purposes of the investment allowance provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").

4. From the acquisition of all the lastmentioned equipment' the work of the Service Division within the dealership was carried out substantially as before but with two entities. Motors and Services, operating where once only one - Motors - had operated. Customers vehicles were brought to the premises and arrangements were made with the workshop manager for works to be carried out. The works were carried out by employees of Services using the equipment of Services installed in the premises of Motors. The record of work done was maintained and costed on job cards of Services. However, the customer was not aware that he was dealing with anyone other than Motors. There was no suggestion that any contract involving a customer came into existence other than between the customer and Motors. When the works were done the account was rendered by Motors and, when the account was paid, the monies were received to the credit of Motors. By way of return for its contribution in the year of income ended 30 June 1976, Services received payment of $45,000: a sum which I am satisfied was determined in accordance with a recommendation of the accountants as to be the sum of the charge to Motors for the services rendered by Services in that year. The total expenses of Services for the year amounted to $48,927 but, after bringing to account dividends received from Motors of $15,000 and another small item of assessable income, a profit was returned. Over and above the expenses so mentioned. Services claimed a deduction by way of investment allowance in the sum of $5,635 based upon a capital cost of $14,628 for the newly acquired plant and equipment. The claim was disallowed by the Commissioner.

5. As the matter was argued before me, in order to succeed" in its claim for investment allowance. Services must establish that the property in question was: -

(a) "acquired... by the taxpayer... for use by the taxpayer";

(b) that it was "for use... wholly and exclusively... for the purpose of producing assessable income...";and

c） that it was not to be used to produce assessable income by "the granting to other persons of rights to use the eligible property" (s82AA).

  1. In my view the Applicant has satisfied all those tests. The eligible property was purchased by Services and no other. By those purchases it "acquired" the property. Having acquired it. Services enjoyed the rights of possession which attended that ownership. It exercised those rights by the actions of its officers and employees. It did not part with possession of any of the eligible property to any other person or entity. It did not use the eligible property for any other purpose than generating assessable income.
  2. The Commissioner's representative relied upon the decisions of the High Court of Australia in Tourapark Pty Limited v FCT [1982] HCA 18; (1982) 149 CLR 176; and of the Full Bench of the Federal Court of Australia in WA Hughes Pty Limited v FC of T 81 ATC 4317; but, in my view, those decisions are clearly to be distinguished on the facts. In Tourapark the taxpayer conducted the business of a tourist caravan and camping park. It provided accommodation (inter alia) in stationary' caravans. Customers requiring accommodation in a caravan were licensed to occupy and use caravans. In those circumstances, as

Gibbs, CJ said (at p 182):-

"Although the taxpayer retains a right to use the caravans, and does in fact use them, it nevertheless grants to each customer a right to use them."

In WA Hughes Pty Limited the finding of the Court was that the company claiming an investment allowance deduction was the owner of a heavy-haulage truck which was used in the coal-haulage business conducted by a partnership. The finding of the Court was that the claimant/owner had parted with possession of the eligible property so that possession of it came to be held by the partnership under a bailment. As Deane, J stated (at p 4322) :-

" the bailment was not a gratuitous bailment. It was a bailment pursuant to a commercial or business arrangement, on the plain understanding that a hiring

charge would be paid It is incorrect to say that the procedure did not result in the obtaining by the partnership companies of any 'rights' from the taxpayer. The procedure involved the conferring upon the partnership companies of rights to possession and use which were good against all but the taxpayer and against the taxpayer itself until revoked by reasonable notice. Those rights were derived from the co-relative duties of others to abstain from interference with the partnership companies' possession and use of the relevant equipment which included the truck. Just as the use of the equipment explains the payments made, so too the payments explain and qualify the use of the equipment."

In my view, in the circumstances of this case, at no time did Services part with either possession (as in Hughes) or "rights to use" (as in Tourapark).

8. I further find that there was no change in the relevant circumstances touching the use of the eligible property prior to assessment, such as might have caused an entitlement otherwise' arising to be forfeited by reason of the operation of s82AG(1) of the Act.

9. Accordingly, the objection is to be upheld.
