Re Antonia Scrivanich and Australian Taxation Office [1983] AATA 358 (27 October 1983)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: ANTONIA SCRIVANICH

And: AUSTRALIAN TAXATION OFFICE

No. S83/54

Freedom of Information

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mr J.O. Ballard (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information - application to amend doctor's report of consultation - best available record of what was said at interview

Freedom of Information Act 1982, ss.48, 49, 50

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s.35

HEARING

CANBERRA

27:10:1983

ORDER

The Tribunal decides:

(a) To set aside the decision of the authorized officer of the Australian Taxation Office of 11 May 1983; and the same is so set aside;

(b) To substitute therefor a decision that pursuant to s.50 of the Freedom of Information Act 1982, the fifth sentence of the final complete paragraph of page two of the report of Dr Alan Cotton of 16 December 1981 concerning the applicant be noted as follows:

In fact Ms Scrivanich's father, who was born in 1922, had apparently been an accountant overseas and that the family had lost everything in World War I and II in Yugoslavia;

and the same is so substituted.

DECISION

This is an application by Ms Antonia Scrivanich seeking the amendment of a document of the Australian Taxation Office, the respondent agency, under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the Act"). The document in question is a report prepared by Dr A.T. Cotton, at the request of a Commonwealth medical officer, concerning the applicant which forms part of the applicant's personal records held by the respondent. There are three passages of this report which the applicant seeks to have amended.

2. At a directions hearing conducted on 8 June 1983 an order under s.35 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act") was made. At the commencement of the hearing of this matter on 29 August 1983, the applicant requested that the hearings not be in private and that the s.35 order be revoked. The Tribunal directed that if any particular matter arose which caused either party concern, an application for a confidentiality order could be made in respect of particular evidence. Such an application was made by the respondent in relation to the name of an officer of the Australian Taxation Office. An order under s.35 of the AAT Act to delete all reference to the officer's name was granted.

3. The applicant is employed in the Adelaide office of the respondent. She lodged the request for amendment pursuant to ss.48 and 49 of the Act with the Freedom of Information Section of the Adelaide Taxation Office on 15 April 1983. This followed a request by the applicant for access to the report of Dr Cotton on 25 March 1983, and the granting of such access by the respondent on 8 April 1983. On 11 May 1983, the applicant was informed that her request for amendment was refused.

4. The reasons supplied to this Tribunal pursuant to s.37 of the AAT Act state the following grounds for refusing to amend the records:

FINDINGS on material questions of fact (referring to the material on which those findings were based):

That the document contains information, prepared by Dr Alan Cotton,

relating to an interview he had with the applicant.

That the said interview took place on 2 December 1981.

That the Deputy Commissioner's account of the interview was prepared on 16 December 1981 and purports to be a largely verbatim account.

That the applicant's account of the interview is dated 15 April 1983.

That the applicant has produced no evidence supporting her version of the interview.

REASONS for the decision:

The document prepared by Dr Alan Cotton should not be amended pursuant to Section 50 of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 because it does not contain information which is incomplete, incorrect or misleading.

5. At the hearing counsel for the respondent said that the respondent was

prepared to note, annex or append the report but not to amend it.

5. Part V of the Act sets out the provisions for the amendment of personal records. The relevant sections are:

48. Where a person (in this section referred to as the "claimant") who is an Australian citizen, or whose continued presence in Australia is not subject to any limitation as to time imposed by law, claims that a document of an agency or an official document of a Minister to which access has been provided to the claimant under this Act contains

information relating to his personal affairs -

(a) that is incomplete, incorrect, out of date or misleading; and

(b) that has been used, is being used or is available for use by the agency or Minister for an administrative purpose;

he may request the agency or Minister to amend the record of that information kept by the agency or Minister,

49.(1) A request under section 48 -

(a) shall be in writing and be expressed to be made in pursuance of this section;

(b) shall specify an address in Australia to which a notice under this Part may be sent to the claimant; and

(c) shall be sent by post to the agency or Minister, or delivered to an officer of the agency or a member of the staff of the Minister, at an address of the agency or of the Minister, as the case may be, that is an

address set out in regulations made for the purposes of section 19.

(2) A request under section 48 shall give particulars of the matters in respect of which the claimant believes the record of information kept by the agency or Minister is incomplete, incorrect, out of date or misleading and shall specify the amendments that the claimant wishes to be made.

50.(1) Where an agency to which or Minister to whom a request is made under section 48 decides to amend the record of information to which the request relates, the agency or Minister may, in its or his discretion, make the amendment either by altering the record or by adding an appropriate notation to the record.

(2) Where an agency or Minister amends a record by adding a notation to the record, the notation shall -

(a) specify the respects in which the information is incomplete, incorrect, out of date or misleading; and

(b) in a case where the information is claimed to be out of date - set out such information as is required to bring the information up to date.

(3) Where a request is made to an agency or Minister under section 48, the agency or Minister shall take all reasonable steps to enable the claimant to be notified of a decision on the request as soon as practicable but in any case not later than 30 days after the day on which the request is received by or on behalf of the agency or Minister.

(4) Section 23 applies in relation to a decision in respect of a request made under section 48.

(5) Section 26 applies in relation to a decision made under this Part refusing to amend a record in like manner as it applies in relation to a decision made under Part III refusing to grant access to a document in accordance with a request made in accordance with sub-section 15(1).

7. At the hearing the applicant gave evidence and the respondent called Dr

Cotton.

8. The applicant gave evidence that the interview lasted about an hour, that she spoke very fast and that a great deal of material was covered. The applicant did not take any notes of the interview with Dr Cotton, and had not asked for the report to be amended until April 1983, because she did not have access to it until that time. Evidence was tendered by the applicant of various documents in which her story had been set out prior to her interview with Dr Cotton. These were tendered in support of her contention that she had told her version of the happenings recounted in the report in the same terms many times before the interview on 2 December 1981.

9. In evidence Dr Cotton said he prepared his report from longhand notes which he took during the interview with the applicant on 2 December 1981. They were verbatim notes taken as the interview proceeded and they ran to some six or seven pages of handwritten notes. He dictated the report about 15 days after the interview based on the notes.

10. At the hearing these notes were produced and inspected by both parties and the Tribunal

11. The applicant is seeking to have three sections of the report amended. The relevant sections of the report and the notes from which they were taken are as follows:

The first passage of the report is a sentence which, referring to the applicant's father, reads -

"He had apparently been an accountant overseas but lost everything during

World War I and World II in Yugoslavia".

Dr Cotton's handwritten notes read -

"accountant overseas, lost everything, World War I, World War II, Yugoslavia".

The second passage of the report is -

"she said she had had in argument with Mr X. on the 1st of December , 1980. She said this was a 'Lover's argument'. However, when questioned she said they had not been actual lovers and on one occasion had gone to the pictures. She said he had never actually asked her to go to bed with him. She said he was a 'despicable animal'. She said nothing had actually happened between them".

The relevant notes are -

"We had a lover's argument 1 December 1980, not actually lovers, just holding hands and going to the pictures, and once only he propositioned me in 1980, asking me to go to the pictures with him, he suggested I be his girlfriend, I felt he wanted to take me to bed, but never asked me".

The applicant pointed out that the notes showed a notation that she was "propositioned" by Mr X., yet that direct quotation was missing from the report.

The third passage of the report reads -

"She said 'the whole of Adelaide is going to know, I am going to the media, I am going to his wife'".

The doctor's notes are -

"the cause of the trouble was Mr X., public service guidelines, emotional state, looks dreadful, was going to crack up, the whole of Adelaide is

going to know, going to the media, going to his wife."

The inference is: I am going to the media, I am going to his wife".

12. In respect of the first passage of the report the comment about the

applicant's father losing all his money in World War I and World War II is plainly wrong in that the applicant's father was not born until 1922; I think it is clear that the passage should refer to the family losing everything in the two world wars. Dr Cotton thought that he had misdictated the meaning from his abbreviated notes and he had no objection to his report being noted accordingly.

13. In the second section the applicant disputes the sentence "She said he had never actually asked her to go to bed with him".

The applicant questioned Dr Cotton about his meaning of "propositioned" in his notes. His answer was -

"In this particular matter, bearing in mind my notes do not note my questions, what in fact I noted was 'he propositioned me in 1980' and then my next note is, 'asking me to go to the pictures with him'. In fact, what most likely happened is I said, after Ms Scrivanich said, 'he propositioned me in 1980' I would have asked, 'what did you mean?' and her reply to that I have noted as, 'asked me to go to the pictures with him' so I did not make an inference about what proposition meant. I, in fact, asked her what she meant by propositioned, and she replied to me that proposition was to go to the pictures. So that is the particular reference in my notes in that regard".

That seems an entirely convincing explanation of the notes.

14. In evidence the applicant contended that she told Dr Cotton the particular gentleman was propositioning her, in the sense of asking her to go to bed with him. She referred to other documents in which she had made similar allegations such as one in February 1981 in which the applicant wrote "It was written by a man who in 1980 pressured me to go to bed with him". She said that the history she gave Dr Cotton was the same as that set out in the documents tendered which were prepared for different reasons prior to the interview.

15. The document in question purports to be Dr Cotton's report of what the applicant told him during the interview and not a document setting out the facts. The report is based on notes which were taken at the time and is, in my opinion, the best guide to what was said at the interview. Dr Cotton agreed he had made an error in relation to the first passage. Further, he was not certain whether the interview took place on 1 or 2 December. He maintained that with these exceptions the rest of the report was an accurate record of the history which the applicant told him. Although the notes from which the second passage was taken referred to propositioning the applicant they do so with an explanation excluding any suggestion of intimacy.

16. The question therefore in relation to the second passage is not whether the applicant was propositioned but whether she told Dr Cotton that she was. Dr Cotton's expertise is not in issue; in my opinion his expertise must extend to the manner of his conduct of his professional practice. Despite what the applicant had told others in writing at other times and her own recall of what she told Dr Cotton, I accept the notes and evidence of Dr Cotton as the best record of what the applicant said to Dr Cotton at the time.

17. During the hearing no mention was made by either party to the third passage. Here the passage of the report accords also with the notes and I see no reason to vary the report in this respect.

18. Accordingly no amendment or notation is to be made to the second and third passages in question.

The first passage is to be noted that this statement does not accurately reproduce the notes of the interview but that in fact the applicant's father, who was born in 1922, had apparently been an accountant overseas and that the family lost everything in World War I and II in Yugoslavia.
