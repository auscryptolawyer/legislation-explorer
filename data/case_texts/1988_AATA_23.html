Last Updated: 22 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - undeducted purchase price of annuity - Commonwealth Superannuation pension - whether purchased

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, S.26AA

Case U106 87 ATC 642

F.C. of T. v. Knight 83 ATC 4096

Minister for Health v. Charvid Pty Ltd (1986) 10 ALD 124

Case U146 87 ATC 850

Case U114 87 ATC 682

Case S63 85 ATC 454

AAT Decision No 4088

Deputy President R.K. Todd

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Applicant

REASONS FOR DECISION

19 January 1988 Deputy President R.K. Todd

  1. As noted by the applicant, who represented himself at the hearing, the principle involved in this matter is virtually identical to that raised in Case U106 87 ATC 642. By mutual invitation, the applicant in this matter had been present during the hearing of Case U106 and, similarly, the applicant from Case U106 was present at this hearing.
  2. The question to be decided is whether amounts claimed in the years of income ended 30 June 1981 ("fiscal 1981") and 1982 which were sought to be described as the "undeducted purchase price of an annuity" should be excluded from the applicant's assessable income pursuant to S.26AA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITAA").
  3. In his reply to the respondent's submission, the applicant indicated that he believed that fiscal 1984 and fiscal 1985 should also be included in the present review. After having ensured that the Tribunal had no record of references relating to those years, I sought clarification of the situation from the respondent. In a letter dated 10 December 1987 the respondent advised that a request for reference had been lodged in respect of fiscal 1984 on 23 March 1986 but that –

"The Commissioner was of the view that as the question at issue was identical to those previously referred to the Board of Review that this request could remain dependent upon the Board's decision in that matter."

In respect of fiscal 1985 the respondent advised:

"... the taxpayer lodged a valid objection on 23 January 1986 and had previously requested that his case be dependent on a similar case currently before the Board of Review. As there was an identical matter before the Board of Review no formal decision had been made pending that decision."

  1. I am satisfied that no request for reference in respect of fiscal 1984 or 1985 has yet been referred to the Tribunal by the Commissioner pursuant to s.189 of the ITAA and that, therefore, the Tribunal has no power to deal with those years in this decision.
  2. The applicant had, prior to the hearing of Case U106, indicated that he would be satisfied to accept the decision in that matter as being applicable to his case. At the hearing of this matter he explained why he had after all decided to continue his appeal:

"[The applicant's case in Case U106] was based on the judgement of the Supreme Court of the A.C.T. in Knight's case. In that case Justice Kelly was hearing an appeal against a decision of a Taxation Board of Review. In his judgement he found -

First: that the pension was an annuity for the purposes of s26AA;

Second: that the annuity was purchased; and

Third: that Mr Knight failed to demonstrate how much of his pension was due to the undeducted purchase price.

The appeal therefore failed but without affecting the validity of the first two points. These remain as authoritative judgements by the most senior tribunal that has so far considered the matter.

[The applicant] took these points as his starting point, believing it was unnecessary for him to argue them and confining his case to a demonstration of what his undeducted purchase price had purchased. He was supported in his assumption by various dicta, e.g. Board of Review No 3, Case B32/1981 where it was stated '.. this Board is, of course, bound to follow the precedent set by the decision of Kelly J. ..."

  1. The applicant then went on to submit that the Tribunal's decision in Case U106 had been based on its rejection of the reasoning in F.C. of T. v. Knight 83 ATC 4096 and that in taking this course the Tribunal had in its reasons been unresponsive to the case put by the applicant in Case U106, who had no doubt thought himself entitled to assume that Knight's Case was the law on the point in question. It was argued, courteously but certainly firmly, that in so doing the Tribunal had done an injustice in Case U106, and the present applicant sought in this case to rebut the reasons given in Case U106.
  2. It is always a difficult question for the Tribunal to know whether to incur the cost of calling for further submissions, or of going so far as to reactivate a hearing, when its consideration of the matter, after its decision has been reserved, leads it to the view that the arguments at the hearing should have taken a different course. Two factors make the Tribunal's task perhaps harder than it is for a court. One is the comparatively higher incidence of unrepresented applicants. The other is the fact that it has been made very clear by the Federal Court that it is the Tribunal's responsibility to come to the correct, or in a discretionary case the preferable, decision unfettered by the arguments, or lack of them, that have been put to it: See Minister for Health v. Charvid Pty Ltd (1986) 10 ALD 124. It follows that when the Tribunal has developed a very firm view in respect of a matter it may decide not to call for further argument. In Case U106 the Tribunal did develop a firm view, and decided not to return the matter to the parties for further argument. Had the view in question been tentatively held, it would have been so returned. As it turned out, the Tribunal, as constituted in Case U106, did not have long to wait for confirmation of the view which it had formed. Having, identically constituted, applied Case U106 in Case U114 87 ATC 682, the decision in Case U106 was only two months later followed by Mr. B.J. McMahon (Senior Member) in Case U146 87 ATC 850. Had the Tribunal in Case U106 known (which it did not) that the matter was before Mr. McMahon, and that he was to be of the same view, it would have been further emboldened to take the course which it did. There are also to be borne in mind the comments of Mr. Roach (now Senior Member) in the decision of Taxation Board of Review No.1 in Case S63 85 ATC 454.
  3. The applicant in fact proceeded to set out a carefully considered argument in an attempt to show that both the decision in Case U106 and the decision of Mr. McMahon in Case U146 were incorrectly decided. I am not however prepared to find that my decision in Case U106 and Case 114, or the decision of Mr McMahon in Case 146, were wrongly based, and considering the consistency of the Tribunal on this point I do not in any event believe that it would be appropriate for me to disturb this line of authority. I respect the views of the present applicant and of the applicant in Case U106, for they are both possessed of expertise in the area with which these cases are concerned and they both put their cases with care, but I fear that the time has come at which, if the Tribunal's views are to be set aside, that can only occur by appeal to the Federal Court or by amending legislation.
  4. I now simply formally repeat the reasons which I gave in Case U106, specifically that "the applicant did not purchase his pension/annuity".
  5. For the reasons given I affirm the objection decisions under review.
