Denis Gerard Pittorino and Commissioner of Superannuation [1994] AATA 277 (16 September 1994)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DENIS GERARD PITTORINO v. COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. N94/105

AAT No. 9735

Number of pages - 3

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

M.D. ALLEN (Senior Member), J.D. CAMPBELL (Member) AND G.D. STANFORD (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - Application for late election. Requirement Applicant show special circumstances. Held - circumstances not special.

Superannuation Act 1922 - Ss.119J, Ss.119ZEA

Superannuation Act 1976 - S.154

Re Beadle and Director-General of Social Security 6 ALD 1 applied

HEARING

Sydney, 15 August 1994

16:9:1994

Counsel for the Applicant: A.J. McQuillen

Solicitor for the Respondent: Mr Anthony Merrilees

Commonwealth Superannuation

Administration

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

M.D. ALLEN, J.D. CAMPBELL AND G.D. STANFORD The Applicant commenced employment with the Commonwealth Public Service on 5 May 1971. He was then aged 20 years and had studied Engineering at the University of New South Wales for two years.

2. Following a medical examination on 10 May 1971, he was accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account established under the Superannuation Act 1922.

3. By letter dated 22 April 1975, he sought to be accepted into the Superannuation Fund established by the 1922 Act. In evidence he stated that he had taken this step following advice from a superior officer in the Department of Housing, a Mr Bean, who had advised him of the increased benefits available to members of the Superannuation Fund as opposed to members of the Provident Fund.

4. The Applicant was medically examined and, by letter dated 15 June 1976, was informed that his application for entry into the Superannuation Fund was successful.

5. The actual wording of the letter of 15 June 1976 needs to be noted. The operative paragraph of the letter states:

"The Delegate of the Superannuation Board accepted your application on

1 June 1976 and you are, in accordance with that decision now required to

contribute to the Superannuation Fund."

6. On 11 August 1976 the Commissioner for Superannuation wrote to the Applicant advising him of two options now available to him following his entry into the Superannuation Fund. That letter read:

"I refer to my recent letter wherein you were informed that the

Superannuation Board had approved your application to transfer from the

Provident Account to the Pension Scheme. Your contributions for units of

pension are due and payable from the date of the Board's decision or, if

that date is not a Commonwealth Public Service payday, the next
succeeding

payday after that date.

Your entitlement to units of pension is directly related to the maximum
of

the salary range of the position you hold, but actual contributions for

units will be for a lesser number than that determined according to
salary

because of certain eligibilities that you have with regard to your equity

in the Provident Account. You should also note that the fortnightly rate

of your contribution will be at the rate applicable to a retiring age of

65 years. You have two options with regard to your equity in the

Provident Account and they are as follows :-

Option 1 You can elect to transport your Provident Account equity to the

Pension Scheme in which case a Transfer Value will become payable in

respect of you. The amount of Transfer Value in your case will be the

total of your contributions plus interest in the Provident Account (C +

I), plus an employers component equal to twice (C + I). When the

Transfer-Value is paid into the Pension Scheme, it will purchase for you
a

number of fully paid units i.e. units which attract full pension benefits

but in respect of which no fortnightly contributions are required to be

made. Your actual contributions to the Pension Scheme will then be for

such number of units as represents the 'difference' between your normal

unit entitlement according to salary, and the number of units determined

to be fully paid in respect of the Transfer Value applicable to you. In

electing to take advantage of this option, you will have full pension

entitlement in respect of the maximum number of units available under the

Superannuation Act.

Option 2 This option gives you the opportunity to elect to receive a

refund of your contributions plus interest in the Provident Account.

However, in taking this course of action the restrictive provisions of
the

Superannuation Act will have effect and your normal eligibility to

contribute for full unit entitlement according to salary will be

diminished. In effect, the amount of your contributions plus interest in

the Provident Account will be equated as being equivalent to a number of

units in the Pension Scheme, and the number of units so determined will
be

applied in relation to you as a unit restriction i.e. units forfeited for

the whole of your term as a contributor under the Superannuation Act. It

is also mentioned that a contributor with a unit restriction is not

permitted to hold reserve units of pension.

n practice, the number of units determined to be fully paid according to
a

Transfer Value payable in respect of you is equal to the number of units

determined to be a unit restriction if you elected to receive a refund of

your contributions plus interest in the Provident Account, i.e. if for

instance your Transfer Value represented 20 fully paid units, but you

rejected this option in favour of receiving a refund, you would forfeit
20

units of your full unit entitlement according to salary.

Attached hereto is a schedule which gives full details of the
contributory

position applicable in relation to you as at the date of the

Superannuation Board's decision to allow you to contribute to the Pension

Scheme. The schedule should be examined in conjunction with the
foregoing

information and you should ensure that you comply with any instructions

contained therein with regard to the making and forwarding of elections.

I have enclosed a copy of a brochure entitled 'Commonwealth

Superannuation' which I trust will be of further assistance to you."

7. In the interim the Applicant had transferred from the Department of Housing and Construction, where he had been advised by Mr Bean, to the Department of Defence. In retrospect, it is unfortunate that the transfer took place at this time as he may have received more detailed and better advice from Mr Bean. As it was, however, he took the letter of 11 August 1976 and sought advice from the Staff Clerk at the Department of Defence, a Mr Irwin.

8. It is a matter of dispute in these proceedings as to what was said by Mr Irwin to the Applicant at that time and the advice, if any, proffered by Mr Irwin. Having seen and heard Mr Irwin give evidence, the Tribunal accepts his evidence that his attitude was that it was for the Applicant to ask questions and that he, Mr Irwin, would not tell anyone what to do. That is not to say, however, that the Applicant might not have received the impression that Mr Irwin thought it did not matter much either way. We are confident, however, that whatever else he may have done, Mr Irwin would not have committed himself to giving any advice to anyone.

9. The Applicant chose option 2 above and received a refund of contributions from the Provident Fund. It was only in 1990, during the explanatory briefings regarding the change from the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme to the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme, that he became aware that he may have obtained greater benefits by exercising Option 1, ie, the transfer option.

10. In a letter dated 8 March 1993, the Applicant sought to make a late election to preserve his Provident Fund account contributions. That application was made pursuant to Section 119J of the Superannuation Act 1922 which states inter alia:

"(1) If:

(a) a transfer value or transfer values became payable to or in respect
of

the person in accordance with paragraph (a) of subsection (1) of section

119H;

(b) the person elected before the date on which he became an employee, or

elects not later than three months after that date, to pay to the Board
an

amount equal to the amount of that transfer value or, if two or more

transfer values became payable, to the sum of the amounts of those

transfer values; and

(c) that amount has, before the expiration of that period of three
months,

been paid to the Board;

the succeeding provisions of this section have effect.

(2) The Board may, if it is satisfied that there are special
circumstances

that justify it in so doing, extend the period for the making of an

election and the payment of an amount to the Board under subsection (1)."

11. On 17 August 1993 a Delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation decided that, in the terms of Ss.119J(2) of the 1922 Act, he was not satisfied that there were special circumstances that justified extending the period for the making of a late election to pay a transfer value to the Board specified in Ss.119J(1), of not longer than 3 months after the date on which the Applicant was accepted as a contributor to the fund. The Applicant was informed of the Delegate's decision by letter dated 18 August 1993.

12. Section 119ZEA of the 1922 Act states that on or after 1 July 1976 a reference to the "Board" shall be read as including a reference to the Commissioner of Superannuation.

13. As is stated by the clear words of the section, the Commissioner for Superannuation and, on appeal, this Tribunal must be satisfied that special circumstances exist that justify the extension of the period for making an election.

14. Pursuant to S.154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 the Applicant, being dissatisfied with a decision of the Commissioner of Superannuation, sought reconsideration by the Commissioner. That review having affirmed the earlier decision, he sought further review by this Tribunal.

15. The Applicant's case upon review was that whereas he had been advised to transfer from the Provident Fund to the Superannuation Fund, he had not been told that by so doing he would lose six years of service, that is to say his entry into the Fund would only date from his acceptance by the Fund. He was confused by this and when he sought opinions after receiving the letter of 11 August 1976 (T8) setting out the two options, he sought advice which was, in his opinion in retrospect, clearly wrong.

16. In addition, the Applicant pointed to his youth and inexperience at the time and the fact he was given no formal advice as to his options.

17. The term "special circumstances" has been discussed in a number of decisions by this Tribunal. The Full Court of the Federal Court in Beadle v Director-General of Social Security [1984] AATA 176; 7 ALD 670 at 675 stated its broad agreement with what the Tribunal had stated in re Beadle and Director-General of Social Security 6 ALD 1 at 3:

"An expression such as 'special circumstances' is by its very nature

incapable of precise or exhaustive definition. The qualifying adjective

looks to circumstances that are unusual, uncommon or exceptional.
Whether

circumstances answer any of these descriptions must depend upon the

context in which they occur. For it is the context which allows one to

say that the circumstances in one case are markedly different from the

usual run of cases. This is not to say that the circumstances must be

unique but they must have a particular quality of unusualness that
permits

them to be described as special."

18. In this matter, we find that special circumstances have not been shown. The options available to the Applicant are clearly set out in the letter of 11 August 1976. Even without advice, the Applicant should have been able to fully weigh and consider those options. The Applicant then made what, in retrospect, was not the preferable decision. There is nothing unusual, uncommon or exceptional in that.

19. The Applicant referred to his apparent immaturity and inexperience in the Public Service. At the same time, he had the benefit of two year's of tertiary education and, although he may have been inexperienced and indeed naive, the options were clearly spelled out to him. Although the Applicant stated that he regarded the letter as being "gobbledegook", the Tribunal considers the following wording, for example, to be quite clear and unambiguous, namely:

"When the Transfer-Value is paid into the Pension Scheme, it will

purchase for you a number of fully paid units i.e. units which attract

full pension benefits but in respect of which no fortnightly
contributions

are required to be made. ..."

20. As stated above, the Tribunal is satisfied that the Applicant weighed his options and, in retrospect, realises he chose the wrong one. These are not "special circumstances". The decision under review will therefore be affirmed.
