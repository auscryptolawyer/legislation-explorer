Re Taxation Appeals [1993] AATA 5; (1993) 24 ATR 1212 (12 January 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. VT92/83

AAT No. 8470

Number of pages - 7

Taxation

[1993] AATA 5; (1993) 24 ATR 1212

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Mrs R.A. Balmford(1) (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - eligible termination payment ('ETP') made to taxpayer in respect of service between 1959 and 1988 - legislation amended in 1984 to change basis of taxation of lump sum superannuation benefits - criteria for determining what part of ETP to be assessed under old rules - whether discretion conferred by sub-section 27B(2) should be exercised in taxpayer's favour to render payment tax free

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.27A, 27B(1), (2)

Acts Interpretation Act 1901 s.15AB

Klein v. Domus Pty. Limited [1963] HCA 54; (1963) 109 CLR 467

The Queen v. Anderson Ex parte Ipec-Air Pty. Limited [1965] HCA 27; (1965) 113 CLR 177

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 4 November 1992

12:1:1993

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

DECISION

This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent disallowing an objection by the applicant to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of his taxable income for the year ending 30 June 1988 of an amount of $108,462 received by him from a superannuation fund.

2. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and numbered T1 to T20, together with an extract from the 1983-84 Budget Statements relating to taxation of lump sum superannuation. Evidence was given at the hearing by the applicant, who was represented by his accountant. The respondent was represented by an officer of the Australian Taxation Office.

3. The submission of the applicant was that the respondent should have exercised his discretion under sub-section 27B(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ('the Act') in favour of the applicant so as to exclude the amount in question from the applicant's assessable income.

4. Sub-sections 27B(1) and (2) read, at the relevant time:

'27B.(1) Subject to sub-section (2), where in a year of income an

eligible termination payment is made in relation to a taxpayer, the

assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income shall include

the amount (if any) ascertained by deducting from the amount (in this

sub-section referred to as the "relevant amount") ascertained in

accordance with the formula A - AB - D, where -

C

A is the amount or value of the eligible termination payment -

(a) reduced by the amount of the concessional component in relation

to the eligible termination payment; and

(b) if the eligible termination payment is an immediate annuity

eligible termination payment, reduced also by the non-qualifying

component in relation to the eligible termination payment;

B is the number of whole days (if any) in the eligible service

period that occurred before 1 July 1983;

C is the number of whole days in the eligible service period; and

D is the amount of undeducted contributions in relation to the

eligible termination payment,

so much of the relevant amount as is deemed to have been applied in

accordance with section 27D.'

27B.(2) For the purposes of the application of sub-section (1) in

relation to an eligible termination payment made in relation to a

taxpayer, where the Commissioner, having regard to -

(a) if the eligible termination payment relates to employment in

which the taxpayer was engaged on 30 June 1983 - the amount of an

eligible termination payment that could, in the opinion of the

Commissioner, reasonably be expected to have been made in relation to

the taxpayer in consequence of the termination of that employment if

that employment had been terminated on that date;

(b) if the eligible termination payment relates to membership of the

taxpayer of a superannuation fund on 30 June 1983 - the amount of an

eligible termination payment that could, in the opinion of the

Commissioner, reasonably be expected to have been made in relation to

the taxpayer from the fund in consequence of the termination of the

taxpayer's membership of the fund if that membership had terminated on

that date;

(c) any previous application of this sub-section in relation to the

taxpayer; and

(d) such other matters as the Commissioner considers relevant,

considers that it is appropriate that a greater amount be substituted

for the amount represented by the component AB in the formula in

C

that sub-section, that sub-section applies as if the greater amount

were substituted for the amount represented by that component.'

5. The facts in this matter are not in dispute and on the evidence before me I find them to be as set out in this and the following paragraph. The applicant, who was then self-employed as a partner in a professional firm, established a superannuation fund on 17 June 1959 and made contributions to the fund until 1980. He was at all relevant times the sole member of the fund. Increases in the value of the fund after 30 June 1980 were due only to capital appreciation and reinvestment of dividends. At 30 June 1983 the market value of the fund was $182,836.

6. On 30 April 1988, on his retirement from the partnership, the applicant received a payment from the fund of $650,773, being the whole amount of the fund at that date.

7. The payment came within paragraph (a) of the definition of 'eligible termination payment' in sub-section 27A(1) of the Act as it then stood. Accordingly the relevant 'eligible service period', in terms of the definition in that sub-section, was the period of employment to which the payment related. This was calculated to be a period of 10,536 days, of which 1756 days were after 30 June 1983 and 8780 days before that date. That calculation was not challenged by the applicant.

8. As to the application of the formula set out in sub-section 27B(1), it was not suggested that either undeducted contributions or section 27D, or either of the amounts described in paragraphs A(a) and (b) of the sub-section had any relevance to this matter, and they may thus be ignored. In those circumstances, sub-section 27B(1) directs that the assessable income of the taxpayer include an amount calculated according to the formula A - AB as there defined.

C

The effect of this provision is that tax is assessed on a part of the eligible termination payment which bears to the whole amount of the payment the same proportion as the period of 'service' after June 1983 bears to the whole of the 'eligible service period'. The calculation made by the respondent, in the present case, proceeded as follows:

9. Sections 27A and 27B appear in Subdivision AA of Division 2 of Part III of the Act. Subdivision AA was introduced with effect from 25 June 1984 by Act No. 47 of 1984. The Budget Statements for 1983/84 indicated that there would be changes in the basis of taxation of lump sum superannuation payments associated with termination of employment. In future, the part of any such payment relating to service before 30 June 1983 would be taxable on the previous basis. Amounts relating to service after 30 June 1983 (apart from certain employee contributions not here relevant) would be fully taxable. Under the heading 'Transitional Arrangements' in the Budget Statements the following passage appears:

'Lump sums or parts thereof that relate to service before 30 June

1983 will remain taxable under the old rules (principally the 5

per cent basis), and only amounts relating to service after 30

June 1983 will be subject to the new rules.

The Commissioner of Taxation will have a power to vary that rule

in cases where it would otherwise operate inequitably. The

principle to be followed will be to protect (that is, leave

subject to the old tax rules) such part of the amount that a

person receives as is equal to the payment that person would have

received had he or she retired and been paid a lump sum at the

change-over date.'

The Commissioner's power to vary the rule is conferred on him by sub-section 27B(2).

10. For reasons which it is not necessary to go into here, the effect of 'the old rules' in the present case is that the portion of the payment which relates to service prior to 30 June 1983 is free of tax. The applicant's representative submitted that the legislative intention was that, where an eligible termination payment was based on contributions all of which were made prior to 30 June 1983, the whole of the payment should be taxed 'under the old rules'. Thus consistently with that intention, the discretion conferred by sub-section 27B(2) should be exercised to render the payment received by his client wholly tax-free. In support of that submission he relied on the passage from the Budget Statements cited in the preceding paragraph.

11. Dixon C.J. said in Klein v. Domus Pty. Limited [1963] HCA 54; (1963) 109 CLR 467 at 473:

'We have invariably said that wherever the legislature has given a

discretion of that kind you must look at the scope and purpose of the

provision and at what is its real object. If it appears that the

dominating, actuating reason for the decision is outside the scope of

the purpose of the enactment, that vitiates the supposed exercise of

the discretion. But within that very general statement of the purpose

of the enactment, the real object of the legislature in such cases is

to leave scope for the judicial or other officer who is investigating

the facts and considering the general purpose of the enactment to give

effect to his view of the justice of the case.'

The general principle controlling the exercise of broad discretionary powers was expressed by Kitto J. in The Queen v. Anderson Ex parte Ipec-Air Pty. Limited [1965] HCA 27; (1965) 113 CLR 177 at 189, as follows:

'It is a general principle of law, applied many times in this Court

and not questioned by anyone in the present case, that a discretion

allowed by statute to the holder of an office is intended to be

exercised according to the rules of reason and justice, not according

to private opinion, according to law, and not humour, and within those

limits within which an honest man, competent to discharge the duties

of his office, ought to confine himself: Sharp v. Wakefield (1891)

AC 173, at p 179.'

12. In the context of those judicial statements, it is appropriate that, in considering whether to exercise the discretion conferred on me by sub-section 27B(2), I should, in reliance upon paragraph 15AB(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901, give consideration to the passage cited above from the Budget Statements to confirm that the meaning of sub-sections 27B(1) and (2) is the ordinary meaning conveyed by the text of those provisions taking into account their context in the Act and, in particular, the purpose or object underlying the legislation in which they were enacted.

13. Having so considered the cited passage, I am satisfied that that is the case. The criterion for determining what portion of an eligible termination payment is to be assessed 'under the old rules' is the period of service to which the payment relates, not the period within which the contributions were made. The assessment with which I am concerned applies that criterion correctly, as appears from paragraph 8 supra.

14. Accordingly, having regard to the matters set out in sub-section 27B(2)(a) to (d), I do not consider that it is appropriate that a greater amount be substituted for the amount represented by the component AB in the formula in sub-section 27b(1).

C

For the reasons given, the objection decision under review will be affirmed.
