REASONS FOR DECISION

11 August 1989 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. In these proceedings the First Applicant, a limited liability-company, claims income tax deductions in amounts greater than are considered by the Commissioner to be allowable in relation to way of contributions to a superannuation Fund ("the Fund") established for the benefit of employees of the company. The Second Applicant is the trustee of the Fund. She claims that the Fund should be considered exempt from liability to income taxation by reason of Section 23F(15) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").
  2. In about 1950, a married couple, then aged in their mid-30's, purchased a small suburban grocer's shop. They purchased the freehold. For some few months the husband continued in his regular employment assisting his wife when free to do so. Thereafter they both worked full-time in the business which they conducted in partnership. For some years they lived above the shop. When he left school, one of their three sons - born in 1937 - joined them in the business. I am satisfied that they worked hard, working long hours with hardly any holidays. I accept that they spent little on themselves, with all available moneys being committed to the business, whether in increasing stock or in effecting alterations or extensions to the premises.

3. By 1981 they were both aged 66 years, and their son was now in his 40's. The prospect of old age, attended by deterioration in health and a reduction in income-earning capacity was a matter in their minds. So were questions as to how to make provision for their financial security in that old age.

  1. It was said in evidence that they had made no financial provision for their later years. I am well satisfied that that evidence was not intended to mislead, for I am persuaded that the Trustee was completely honest in her evidence. But what was not to her mind was that there were substantial assets of considerable value that had been generated by their endeavours of the years: the increased value of the freehold; the plant and equipment; the stock; the goodwill; and some other financial resources.
  2. It was at about that time that the husband became involved with other storekeepers in negotiations to establish a buying group. Through his association with those fellow shopkeepers he became aware that many of them took comfort in the thought that they had made provision for financial security for their dependants and for themselves in old age by way of superannuation. In consequence the couple consulted their accountant. He advised them that to gain substantial superannuation benefits they would have to be employed by a company which would take over the partnership business. He also advised that in order for them to get "sufficient superannuation benefits" it would be necessary to move quickly to ensure that the company would contribute sufficient sums prior to their retirement to provide substantial benefits. In so advising, he was aware of the Commissioner's guidelines and, in particular, that as a general rule the Commissioner did not approve of superannuation schemes which would provide benefits in excess of seven times "final average salary" at retirement. His understanding was that it was a practice of the Commissioner to allow "catch-up" contributions from employers. Such contributions would enable persons who had worked for prolonged periods, but without the benefits of superannuation in earlier years, to have the benefit of accelerated contributions so as to bring their final superannuation benefits to figures more nearly equal to the benefits available to those for whom benefits had been provided throughout their service. He also took the view that the Commissioner's guidelines - the evidence did not indicate which particular guidelines he had in mind - were all based upon an expectation that employment would terminate no later than 65 years. Since the couple were already passed that age, he took the view that the more detailed prescriptions of the guidelines were not relevant to the resolution of the problems raised by the couple.
  3. His advice was accepted. A company (the First Applicant) was incorporated. Three shares were issued and they were held beneficially by both parents and the son. (Whether the son had become a partner in the partnership was not made clear.) The company became the owner of the plant and equipment and of the stock. It did not purchase the goodwill. Nor did it acquire the freehold of the premises. It was to carry on the business at the will of the couple and it was to come to enjoy the benefit of both personal and local goodwill.
  4. The couple, having previously traded on their own account in partnership, now became employees of the company at a substantial wage. It was considered that, although substantial, it was not excessive, in the sense that the application of Section 109 of the Act was not contended for. That substantial salary was taken into account in assessing what might be allowed as maximum superannuation benefits. The son was also employed by the company but at a much lower salary - a salary much closer to award standards. The mother believed that at all times the partners drew substantially less from the business than their son. It may well have been so but as employees of the company I find that the remuneration paid to their son was substantially less than that paid to them.

8. The parents also became trustees of a superannuation fund ("the Fund") established under a Deed of Trust by the comply on 1 July 1980. By letter of 29 October 1980, the Commissioner informed the trustees of the Fund:

"... you are advised that contributions made by the company in accordance with the deed governing the abovenamed Fund will be allowable deductions under Subdivision AA of Division 3 of the Part III of the Income Tax Assessment Act, subject to the limitations expressed in the Subdivision.

The deed is also considered to be satisfactory for the general purposes of section 23F. Subject to the Fund, in the year of income, meeting the tests prescribed in that section, the income of the Fund will qualify for exemption from income tax. The exemption of any investment income will, of course, depend upon the Fund complying with Division 9B in relation to its investment in public securities."

At the hearing it was acknowledged on behalf of the Commissioner that the Fund had complied with the investment requirements of the Act and of the Commissioner.

  1. The rate of contributions to the Fund made by the company was substantial, particularly so in the first year. The contributions made, and the calculations and projections in support of them, were detailed fully in the first return of income of the Fund and details of the contributions were also given in the first income tax return of the company.
  2. I am satisfied that at 1 July 1980 neither spouse was contemplating retirement. They enjoyed good health and the shop was their life. They had no plan to change their ways although they did contemplate the possibility of a substantial trip.
  3. Then, unexpectedly, tragedy befell them. The husband was killed in a motor vehicle accident on 27 January 1983. His death so distressed the widow that some few months later she finished working as an employee of the company. Even so she maintained her interest in the business and attended at the premises from time to time and has continued to do so.

17. In the foregoing I have set out the findings of primary fact relevant to the issue to be determined upon the application of the company; whether or not it is entitled to a deduction for all of the moneys contributed to the superannuation scheme in the years of income ended 30 June 1981 and 1982 or, alternatively, to a deduction in some greater amount than has been allowed by the Commissioner. I now proceed to set out the further findings of primary fact relevant to the claim of the Fund.

18. In its returns of income for the' year of income ended 30 June 1981 to 1983 (inclusive) the Fund acknowledged deriving income as follows:

The question for determination in relation to the Fund is whether the Fund is liable to assessment to income tax.

19. THE CLAIM TO DEDUCTIONS

The first matter which needs to be determined in considering the claim of the company to deductions to the contributions made to the Fund is to determine whether or not the Fund constituted in the relevant sense "a superannuation fund". The phrase is not defined by the Act (Scott v F.C.T. (Wo.2) (1966) 14 A.T.D. 333 and Mahony v F.C.T. (1967) 41 A.L.J.R. 232). As was indicated by the decision in Scott it is not every fund which describes itself as a superannuation fund which is such a fund in the sense required by the Act. But, guided by the view expressed by Windeyer J. in Scott when he said:

"I have come to the conclusion that there is no essential single attribute of a superannuation fund established for the benefit of employees except that it must be a fund bona fide devoted as its sole purpose for providing for employees who are participants money benefits or benefits having a monetary value) upon their reaching a prescribed age,"

I am satisfied that the Fund was such a fund, notwithstanding the circumstance that it did not prescribe an age for retirement. Having reached that conclusion it follows that Section 82AAR of the Act applies. The result of that is that no deduction for contributions to the Fund is to be allowable except upon satisfying the provisions of Subdivision AA - "Contributions to Superannuation Funds for Benefit of Employees" - of Division 3 of Fart III of the Act.

20. Section 82AAC expressly provides that there is an entitlement to a deduction:

"Where a taxpayer, for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, an eligible employee, sets apart or pays in the year of income an amount or amounts as or to a fund or funds from which the benefits are to be provided,"........

provided that the rights to receive the benefits are fully secured but only -

'subject to the succeeding provisions of this subdivision.'"

The relevant succeeding provisions are Section 82AAD and Section 82AAE.

21. Section 82AAD, so far as this material provides:

"Where an amount is ......... set apart or paid in the year of income by a taxpayer as or to a fund .... for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, an eligible employee, being an employee associated with the taxpayer, the amount is, subject to the succeeding provisions of this Subdivision, allowable as a deduction by virtue of Section 82AAC only to the extent to which, in the opinion of the Commissioner, the amount ... would have been so set a part or paid if the employee had not been associated with the taxpayer."

The circumstances in which an employee will be considered to be "an employee associated with the taxpayer" are defined by Section 82AAB of the Act. It is not disputed that the parents were associated persons in relation to the Applicant.

22. Section 82AAD needs to be understood in its context. The Act recognises that the cost to an employer of engaging the services of others as a general rule shall be deductible. It also recognises that the rewards to an employee for such service may be either immediate, as with the payment of salary or wages (when under ordinary circumstances income is derived by the employee as the relevant outgoing is incurred by the employer) or deferred as with the payment of superannuation benefits directly to an employee upon retirement of indirectly as with the payment of benefits to dependants following the death of the employee. However, provision is also made in the Act to specially reserve to the Commissioner the right, subject to review by this Tribunal, to determine what is "reasonable" by way of payments of immediate benefits (cf. Section 65 and Section 108) or deferred superannuation benefits (Section 82AAD). It is also appropriate to observe at this point that, in relation to deferred benefits by way of superannuation, the Act places a limit on the amounts deductible to employers. The result is that the total of the deductions allowable to the employer for contributions is not to exceed 5% of the total remuneration paid to the employee during the year of income (Section 82AAE(a)(ii)) unless the deduction is no greater than $400 (Section 82AAE(a)(i)) or "special circumstances" exist which justify the allowance of a greater deduction, in which case such further amount as the Commissioner considers reasonable shall be deductible (Section 82AAE(b)). Together those provisions all reflect a situation in which employers are assured of a right to deductions for the cost of services rendered to them by employees so long as, in the case of associated persons, that remuneration is not unreasonable by reason of being excessive, and so long as, in the absence of "special circumstances", the cost of deferred benefits is not greater than $400 or 5% of the total remuneration, whichever is the greater.

23. Against that background it is now necessary to consider whether in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1981 the action of the company in paying $106,500 to the Fund in relation to both husband and wife ($53,250 each) as superannuation contributions was greater than the amounts which would have been so paid if they had not been associated with the taxpayer. No provision was made for superannuation for the employed-son.

24. In judging that question it is appropriate to have regard to the fact that the couple had rendered service to the company for only 12 months; had already passed the age of 65 years; were being remunerated in sums more than treble the remuneration paid to their son who was also working full time in the business; and that their remuneration was already substantially in excess of award wages such as might have been paid to any person who could have been procured to discharge their responsibilities for merely award rates. On the other hand in favour of the company's claim it should be borne in mind that both husband and wife were well experienced and highly motivated and, by their continued service, were ensuring the benefit to the company of the personal goodwill they had generated by their participation in the business over some 30 years. By making available the premises from which the company traded, they also made available to the company the site goodwill, but the latter advantage to the company did not arise out of the employment.

25. Even so, in my view, I must conclude that amounts of $53,250 would not have been paid by way of superannuation contributions - to any persons in the service of the company who were not associated with the taxpayer. That being so, the deductions available to the company are to be limited to such amounts as, in the opinion of the Tribunal, would have been so set apart or paid if the parents had not been associated with the taxpayer. For my part I consider that by allowing c.33% of a generous salary ($6,887 each) for the year of income ended 30 June 1981 the Commissioner has already taken a liberal view of the matter. I would allow no more.

26. A similar question as to the application of Section 82AAD arises in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1982. In that year of income the salary of the parents was marginally increased (from $20,876 to $21,240): as was the salary of the son (from $6,176 to $6,240). The superannuation contributions made in respect of the parents were substantially less than the previous year - $34,000 in lieu of $53,250 (cf. but again no contributions for the son). Once again I am satisfied that Section 82AAD operates to limit the deduction available to the company to such amount as in the opinion of the Tribunal would have been paid if the parents had not been associated with the taxpayer. The question then arising is whether the amount so to be allowed should be greater than $1,062 each or 5% of salary. That question may conveniently be addressed by considering Section 82AAE.

27. The company cannot succeed in this reference unless it establishes that the deduction to be allowed should exceed "5% of the total remuneration paid to the employee .... during the year of income". That is so because Section 82AAE(b) provides that:

"The deduction allowable under this Subdivision in an assessment of a taxpayer .... in respect of income during the year of income in respect of amounts set apart or paid by the taxpayer as or to a fund or funds for the purpose of ma ing provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependents of, any one employee -

(a) shall not exceed whichever is the greater of

( i ) ; and

( ii) ; or

(b) if the Commissioner is of the opinion that there are special circumstances that justify the allowance of a greater deduction.........than the amount ascertained in accordance with paragraph (a) - shall be such amount, being greater than the amounts so ascertained as he considers reasonable."

It follows that in order to succeed the company must establish that "there are special circumstances"; that they are special circumstances that justify the allowance of a greater deduction"; and, in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1981, that those special circumstances are such as to make "reasonable" the deduction of amounts equal to c.33% of salary."

28. It was contended that the Commissioner, and in these proceedings in his place this Tribunal, should consider the matter as one of substance: to regard the business as one of continuing existence and the corporate shell as if it had existed throughout: and the service of the parents as if they had been employed throughout that period. I am not persuaded by that view of the matter. The provision of salary and attendant (superannuation benefits from the resources of others are rewards which flow from employment. But for those who venture capital assets such as freeholds and goodwill in undertakings of trade the rewards of their enterprise, should they come, are quite different in character than rewards for personal service to others as employers. Such persons will take their rewards (if any) by way of profit of their trade and, commonly, by way of capital appreciation of the assets used in that trade. Such benefits were available to the parents in this case. They were benefits, the value of which could have been realised when they ceased to carry on business. In the event they retained the freehold and, by reason of their continued ownership of the freehold, also controlled the local goodwill attending the business. It was only in so far as they personally rendered service as employees that the company secured the benefit of their personal goodwill. That personal goodwill was unquestionably a matter of some advantage to the company. But it was an advantage which the company had already paid for quite liberally by the provision of a high rate of salary and by reason of the payment of superannuation benefits at the rate of 5% of that salary - the maximum rate allowable in the absence of "special circumstances".

  1. Having regard to all the circumstances and particularly the already high, but not excessive, salaries being paid I am not persuaded that there were any "special circumstances that justify a greater allowance than 5%".
  2. EXEMPTION OF THE FUND

Section 23F(15) provides that the income derived by a superannuation fund to which Section 23F of the Act applies in relation to the year of income is exempt from income tax. The Section applies only to superannuation funds being superannuation funds which satisfy the criteria specified in Section 23F(2), Of all the criteria specified in that sub-section, only one is material to the determination of this reference. It is paragraph (h) which, in context, reads as follows:

"(2) Subject to the succeeding provisions of this Section, this Section applies in relation to a year of income, to a superannuation fund, not being a fund of kind referred to in paragraph 23(jaa), if

..........

(h) the benefits that any employee has, or the dependants of any employee have, the right to receive from the fund are not excessive in amount having regard to -

(1) the remuneration paid to the employee for services rendered by him to his employer;

(2) the period of service rendered by the employee to his employer;

(3) the benefits, pensions and allowances that have been, are being or may be provided for the employee or his dependants from any other fund to which this Section applies in relation to the year of income or has applied in relation to a previous year of income;

(4) any other matters which the Commissioner considers relevant; and

..........”

Section 23F(7) provides that a fund may be considered to have satisfied that requirement when it did not if -

"the trustee of the fund satisfies the Commissioner that, by reason of special circumstances that existed in relation to the fund during that year of income, it would be reasonable for this Section to have effect as if that requirement had been complied with, "

31. It was submitted on behalf of the Fund that the reference to "special circumstances" should not be narrowly construed. I accept that. But adopting that view of the matter does not persuade me that there were any "special circumstances that • existed in relation to the fund" in any relevant sense. What made the Fund distinct from many other funds was the circumstance that in the course of two years, it had received for the benefit of its only two members - employees already beyond the commonplace retiring age of 65 years - by way of contributions amounts equal to c.207% of their salaries in the same period, those salaries being already substantial but not so "excessive" as to be partially disallowed as deductions to the employer. But for all of that they were salaries substantially in excess of salaries that might reasonably have been paid to others for the services rendered. The circumstance is also relevant, but not of great significance, that the two members of the Fund were shareholders in and directors of the employer company and trustees of the Trust and, therefore, between them capable of controlling the affairs of the company and the Fund in their own interest.

32. Having so found, the question to be considered is whether the benefits were to be provided were "excessive in amount" as provided for by Section 23F(2)(h). That concept of benefits being "excessive in amount" is not a concept at large. The section provides that the judgment called for is one to be made having regard to remuneration paid; the period of service to the employer; benefits to the employee from other funds; and other matters considered relevant. In the circumstances of this case there were no other funds.

33. In my view, if one were to have regard only to remuneration paid and the period of service rendered or even to be rendered, the benefits were substantially excessive. It follows that, if they are not to be so characterised for the purposes of the sub-paragraph, it will have to be by reason of "other matters" which the Tribunal, in place of the Commissioner, considers relevant. In my view there are no such "other matters" to be taken into account. Sympathy for the couple and concern that they should find themselves in their mid-60's with only the value of their freehold; their goodwill; and the value of their business and without the benefits of substantial superannuation accumulated by the maintenance of a fund over many years supported by employer's contributions is not enough.

  1. In all the circumstances I am not persuaded that the Second Applicant is entitled to the relief sought.
  2. The determinations of the Commissioner upon the objections under review of both Applicants will be confirmed.
