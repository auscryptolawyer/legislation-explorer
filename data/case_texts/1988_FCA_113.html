Re Western Australian Capital Investment Co Ltd and Grant Heaton Johnston v the Commissioner of Taxation [1988] FCA 113 (18 April 1988)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: WESTERN AUSTRALIAN CAPITAL INVESTMENT CO. LTD. and GRANT HEATON JOHNSTON

And: THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Nos. WAG 2049, 2050, 2052, 2054, and 2055 of 1987

Taxation

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

WESTERN AUSTRALIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

French J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 - appeal challenging satisfaction of preconditions for service of company's assessment on vendor shareholder - challenge open in appeal against assessment - motion for summary dismissal of appeal dismissed.

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Cripps & Jones Holdings Pty Ltd (1987) 19 ATR 540.

HEARING

PERTH

18:4:1988

Counsel for the Applicants: Mr. D.H. Bloom Q.C. with Mr. S.R. Paterniti

Solicitors for the Applicants: Parker & Parker

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr. P. Macliver

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The respondent's motion dated 17 March 1988 is dismissed.

The respondent is to pay the applicant's costs of the motion.

Note: Settlement and entry of Orders is dealt with in

Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

There are before the Court for decision five motions filed by the Commissioner of Taxation seeking summary disposal of five out of a group of seven cognate appeals brought by Western Australian Capital Investments Co. Ltd ("WACIC") and Grant Heaton Johnston ("Johnston") respectively. WACIC and Johnston have both received from the Commissioner, notices of assessment served under the provisions of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("TUCTA Act"). WACIC is said to have been a shareholder in a company called Clifton Towers Pty Ltd, assessed to be liable to ordinary company tax and undistributed profits tax for the year ended 30 June 1981 for amounts of $470,924.31 and $165,847 respectively. Notices of assessment have been served on WACIC pursuant to s.18 of the TUCTA Act on the basis that it was a vendor shareholder for the purposes of that Act. Objections to the assessments have been disallowed and the disallowance has given rise to appeals 2049 and 2050 of 1987.

2. By a similar process, Johnston has been served with an assessment and amended assessment for ordinary company tax said to have been payable by Oakengates Pty Ltd for the year ended 30 June 1979. This has given rise to appeals 2051 and 2052. He has also been served with assessments for ordinary company tax and undistributed profits tax said to be due by Benson Investments Pty Ltd for the year ended 30 June 1979. These are challenged in appeals 2053 and 2054. He has further received an assessment in respect of undistributed profits tax owing by Oakengates Pty Ltd for the same year which is the subject of appeal 2055 of 1987.

3. On 29 September 1987 directions were given in each of these matters requiring that the applicant file and deliver a statement of its grounds of appeal on or before 30 October 1987. As initially filed the "Points of Claim", as they were called, relied upon and repeated all facts and matters set out in the original notice of objection to the assessments in question. By a motion dated 3 November 1987, the Commissioner sought to dismiss the appeals summarily on the basis that the points of claim as filed failed to comply with the directions. On 21 December 1987 I dismissed the Commissioner's motions but directed that the applicant in each case file and deliver amended Points of Claim by 25 January 1988. These were filed on 21 January. They each included a paragraph in the following terms:-

"The Applicant reserves its rights to rely upon any one

or more of the grounds set out in its objection."

4. On 9 March, in response to a motion filed by the Commissioner on 12 February 1988, that paragraph was struck out in each of 2049, 2050, 2052, 2054 and 2055. In 2051 and 2053 I acceded to motions also dated 12 February 1988 to strike out the amended points of claim in their entirety and gave leave for further amended points of claim to be filed on or before 15 April 1988.

5. By five further motions each dated 17 March 1988, the Commissioner now seeks summary dismissal of 2049, 2050, 2052, 2054 and 2055.

6. In each case the applicant relies in the points of claim upon the provisions of sub-s.18(1) of the TUCTA Act:-

"Where -

(a) under the Assessment Act, the Commissioner is

required or permitted to serve a notice of

assessment on a company in relation to the

liability of the company to pay ordinary company

tax or undistributed profits tax in relation to a

year of income; and

(b) vendors recoupment tax is payable, or the

Commissioner is of the opinion that vendors

recoupment tax is likely to become payable, by a

person or persons in relation to ordinary company

tax or undistributed profits tax, as the case may

be, that is payable or may become payable by the

company in relation to that year of income,

the notice shall, notwithstanding section 174 of the

Assessment Act, be served on the company by being served

on -

(c) where the vendors recoupment tax is payable, or in

the opinion of the Commissioner, is likely to

become payable, by only one person - that person;

and

(d) in any other case - any of the persons referred to

in paragraph (b),

and, where the notice is served in accordance with

paragraph (d), the Commissioner shall serve a copy of

the notice on each person (other than the person on whom

the notice was served) who is included in the

representative class in relation to the vendors

recoupment tax."

7. Under sub-s.18(3) the person served with a notice of assessment under sub-s.18(1) has the same rights under Division 2 of Part V of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 in relation to the company's liability for tax, as the company has and, for the purpose of the exercise of those rights, the provisions of Division 2 apply in like manner as they would apply in relation to the exercise of the rights by the company. As can be seen by reference to sub-s.18(1), the section operates to extend the way in which a notice of assessment may be effectively served for the purposes of the Income Tax Assessment Act so that good service on the company is effected by service on a person by whom vendors recoupment tax is payable or likely to become payable.

8. Pursuant to the provisions of s.204 of the Income Tax Assessment Act, tax is due and payable by the company on the date specified in the notice, being not less than 30 days after service or, if no date is specified, on the thirtieth day after service. Where the Commissioner relies upon service on a shareholder as service on the company under sub-s.18(1), the primary liability which is that of the company, will depend upon that service being effected in circumstances which attract the operation of the sub-section. The applicant in each case contends that the conditions upon which service on the shareholder would be good service on the company have not been fulfilled. The contention is pleaded as follows:-

"(1) At no time prior to the issue of the TUCTA

assessment was vendors recoupment tax payable by

the applicant.

(2) At no time prior to the issue of the TUCTA

assessment did the respondent address his mind to

the matters, set out in sub-paragraph (b) of

section 18(1) of TUCTA ("the Section 18(1)(b)

matters") and the respondent was therefore not in a

position to and did not in fact form the opinion

that vendors recoupment tax was likely to be

payable by any person or persons."

9. For these reasons it is contended that "the issue and service of the TUCTA assessment is ultra vires and of no force and effect".

10. The Commissioner, in effect, says that these grounds of objection cannot be taken at this stage as they go to the issue of liability of the vendor shareholder for vendors recoupment tax. That liability, it is said, may be contested in proceedings under the TUCTA legislation but not as part of an objection to the assessment of the company's liability. It is on this basis that he seeks summary dismissal of each of the appeals or at least in so far as they rely upon that point.

11. Prima facie the points taken by the applicant go to the question whether there has been effective service on the company and thus to the question whether any tax is due and payable by the company.

12. Inconvenient as it may be, I do not consider that it has been demonstrated by the Commissioner that the points so taken may not properly be raised on an objection to the assessment and on an appeal to this Court against the disallowance of that objection.

13. Further, his proposition seems directly opposed to what was said by the Full Court in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Cripps & Jones Holdings Pty Ltd (1987) 19 ATR 540. The case concerned an appeal to the Federal Court in respect of an assessment issued under the TUCTA Act for a company which had been dissolved. Provision for such notional assessments is made subject to certain conditions under sub-s.15(4) of the Act. There is also provision for service of notices of notional assessments on promoters and vendor shareholders in the defunct company.

14. The vendor shareholder who was the applicant in the case, argued, inter alia, that the Commissioner had not formed the opinions required in sub-s.15(4)(b) of the Act, namely, that ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax would have been payable if the company had not ceased to exist and that if unpaid, recoupment tax would have been payable by the shareholder. The applicant succeeded before Beaumont J. at first instance and on appeal by the Commissioner to the Full Court. In its judgment dismissing the Commissioner's appeal the Full Court said at 548:-

"The next argument put on behalf of the Commissioner took

as its starting point the proposition that the rights of

the respondent to contest the assessments were, by

s.15(9), "the same rights under Div.2 of Pt.V of the

Assessment Act in respect of the notional assessment as

the company would have had if it had continued to exist

and the notional assessment were an assessment of

ordinary company tax...". It was pointed out that the

Income Tax Assessment Act, which is not concerned with

notional assessments under s.15, provides no right to

contest an assessment on the ground that a pre-condition

imposed by s.15 was not fulfilled. There may be put to

one side the question whether the appeal rights being

asserted are not, having regard to the form of the

assessments and the matters already discussed, rights

arising under s.18(3) rather than s.15(9), since s.18(3)

employs a similar form of language. The true answer to

the contention is that legislation of this kind, by

reference to rights created in another Act for other

purposes, cannot be construed in a blindly literal

spirit. In cases arising under Div. 2 of Pt.V of the

Income Tax Assessment Act, a taxpayer has the right "to

challenge the existence of the conditions giving rise to

the exercise of a power which imposes on him a new

substantive liability to tax". F.J. Bloemen Pty Ltd v

F.C.T. [1981] HCA 27; (1981) 147 CLR 360 at 374; [1981] HCA 27; 11 ATR 914 at 922, per

Mason and Wilson JJ. Sections 15 and 18 of the Act

should both be construed as conferring a corresponding

right to challenge the existence of the conditions

giving rise to the exercise of the power, under which an

assessment issued, that is served pursuant to the

provisions of s.15 or s.18 of the Act. The rights

asserted are "the same rights", but the issues which

arise, when they are asserted in respect of assessments

under s.15, are necessarily different from the issues

which arise when they are asserted in respect of

assessments issued under the Income Tax Assessment Act.

In any case, if there is any ambiguity in the provisions

of ss.15(9) and 18(3), a construction which would remove

an ingredient of liability from the possibility of

challenge should be rejected, both upon general

principles of statutory construction, and also in

accordance with the approach to the constitutional

invalidity of an uncontestable tax taken in MacCormick v

F.C.T., supra."

15. This, I think, is sufficient to dispose of the Commissioner's motion in each case, and the motions will be dismissed with costs.
