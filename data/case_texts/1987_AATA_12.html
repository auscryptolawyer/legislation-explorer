1062/1985 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 12; 87 ATC 233 (19 January 1987)

Last Updated: 9 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Allowable deductions _ horse-breeding as primary production - imported stallion - brood Hares.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) S51(1)

DECISION AND REASONS



Re: 1062/1985

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

1062/1935

AAT Decision No 3161

Mr PM Roach (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. 1062/1985

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re: 1062/1985

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

Mr. P.M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. The issues for determination relate to losses incurred in relation to horse-breeding in the years of income ended 30 June 1980, 1981 and 1982. The applicant is a person who by family background and early education was prepared for a life on the land. After leaving school he became a jackaroo. That was in 1969. He worked on a cattle property on which horses were used extensively. While there he became aware of the special qualities of the quarter-horse which had been developed in the United States of America and which was distinguished by its suitability for handling stock. Quarter-horses had first been introduced to Australia in 1954.By 1973 there were some 5,000 in Australia and by 1986 something of the order of 50,000. In the period 1969 to 1976 the applicant was employed on several rural properties, principally run as cattle studs.

2. In 1973 he purchased his first quarter-horse, a mare in foal. In due course he sold the mare and the foal. He then entered into partnership with an investor and together they purchased a quarter-horse stallion at a cost of some $15,000.00. It had been bred from a world-champion cutting horse. Their expectation at that time was to profit by putting the stallion to stud. Within the partnership the applicant was wholly responsible for management of the stallion and, unless it was with a trainer, the applicant trailered the stallion from property to property as he worked. In 1976 the applicant commenced work on his own account as a hoof-trimmer. He travelled extensively but was able to provide for the stallion. He promoted it as a sire by advertising and by performing with it at shows. By 1980 the applicant and his partner recognised that the stallion was no longer commercially viable because stallions bred in another breeding-line had become more fashionable. They contemplated selling the stallion but were unsuccessful and it was then decided that the partnership would be dissolved with the applicant buying out the interest of his partner. He did so as of 30 June 1980. He paid his partner $8,000.00, having for the most part borrowed the necessary moneys. He took over the stallion and four mares with two colts. The accounts of the partnership to 30 June 1980 showed that $34,410.00 had been invested by the partners and that the only significant assets were the stallion ($14,000.00) and stock ($2,560.00). The recorded partnership loss for the year was $4,563.00, half of which was claimed as an income tax deduction by the applicant. That claim was conceded by the Commissioner at the commencement of the hearing.

3. During the same year of income the applicant had commenced his own livestock trading account purchasing three horses for $4,250.00. Natural increase for the year was one which, on being brought to account at $2 in the livestock trading account, gave rise to a claimed net loss of $2,280.00. That claim was disputed, although quantum was conceded.

4. Having taken over the stallion during the 1980/81 year, the applicant campaigned him heavily at shows and advertised through magazines but the endeavour was largely unsuccessful. The only service fee for the year amounted to $850.00. However, the taxpayer continued about his breeding program using the stallion but with a long-term objective that he would acquire another sire with higher commercial value. He did that in February 1985 importing a well-bred quarter-horse stallion from the United States of America. The price at the time of purchase was $US15,000.00 but the applicant's estimate of the landed cost of the horse was $A25,000.00.

5. During the year ended 30 June 1981 the taxpayer purchased a further six horses ($2,380.00). There was natural increase of three. Eight horses were sold for $2,890.00 and there was one death. There was a recorded loss on horse-trading account of $1,700.00 against which was to he off-set service fees of $850.00 and prize money of $3,182.00. Deductions were claimed at $12,966.00 and that claim included a claim to amortize the stallion at $3,000.00 on the basis of 20 per cent of a cost price of $15,000.00. Quantum was conceded on behalf of the Commissioner and in particular in relation to the claim for amortization. The applicant's Income and Expenditure Statement for the year ended 30 June 1982 disclosed income of $1,504.00 (service fees $1,500.00) and claimed expenses of $4,844.00 (including a further $3,000.00 amortization). Quantum was again conceded on behalf of the Commissioner.

6. No precise financial records were available in relation to the later period because in the years following the applicant took the practical view that if the Commissioner would not allow him to claim deductions on the basis that he was not in business, the Commissioner would not seek to assess him upon income arising from those "activities. The chronology is not unimportant. The Income Tax Returns of the applicant up to 30 June 1982 were prepared and objections relating to them lodged by a firm of chartered accountants. When the Commissioner acted to disallow the applicant's claims in relation to the 1980 year (May 1981). They then lodged an objection on behalf of the applicant and when that objection was disallowed in December 1982, they requested that the disallowance be referred to a Board of Review for review. They also lodged objections in February 1983 and July 1983 in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1981 and 1982 respectively. At that point, after conferring with his accounting advisers, the taxpayer recognised that the Commissioner took the view that his activities had no tax significance. He decided that he did not need the services of chartered accountants and thereafter he retained the services of a firm of tax agents. No further claims were made on the applicant's behalf in his income tax returns and no - financial records for the later period were produced at the hearing. When (the objections in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1981-1982 were disallowed in October 1985, (sic) the new firm of tax agents requested reference of the objections to a Board of Review for review.

7. When the matters came on for hearing before this Tribunal, the taxpayer was asked to complete the history of his trading stock. His evidence can be summarised as follows:

In addition it needs to be borne in mind that over and above trading stock he has had the original stallion (initial cost $14,000.00 to $15,000.00) and the young stallion purchased in 1985 at a cost in the range of US$15,000.00 to A$25,000.00.

The reference to stock "on loan" in the livestock figures refers to mares owned by the applicant's wife and mother-in-law respectively and made available to him for breeding purposes.

8. In my view, it is clear from the evidence and upon the figures that the applicant has continued about his horse-breeding program. The numbers of horses; the cost of the stallions; the sale prices (particularly the mare sold for $5,500.00 in 1985 as a coming three-year old) are all indicative of an investment and a scale of activity in the years in question more appropriate to a business-like activity directed to earning money than to a hobby or other form of recreational.activity or amusement. It is also material that he has lived and worked in the country where he has been able to attend to his horses himself, with limited assistance from others.

  1. In support of the Commissioner's view of the matter it must be recognised that the circumstances of the applicant were unusual in that he did not have control of any property on which the horses were run, although from time to time he did incur expenses in relation to leasing land or agisting horses on the land of others. However, his evidence is that prior to taking up his present position as a property manager in 1982, he was able to provide for his horses by using the property of others, for reward if necessary, and by accommodating them with trainers. Since then he has been able to provide for the increased numbers of horses by running them on the property he now manages the, quid pro quo being that he allows appropriate horses to be worked on the property and on occasion used by the property-owner on visits from the U.K. and by his friends for their recreational purposes. So far as the applicant is concerned, his only use of the animals for recreational purposes has been in the course of "showing" the stallions.
  2. It was contended for the Commissioner that at least so far as the period 1980 to 1982 is concerned; that he business previously conducted by the partnership had ceased as a business; that the new business, (if any), which might have been embarked upon with the acquisition of the second stallion in 1985 had not been commenced; and that in the intermediate period there was no business carried on. Having regard to the evidence as to continuing activities and the scale of them in the years ended 1983 and 1986 inclusive, I am satisfied on balance that the appropriate judgment to be made as a matter of fact in relation to the years 1980 through to 1982 is that there has been a continuing and ongoing set of activities, commencing with the partnership business, which can fairly and appropriately be described as having constituted a " business" in the three years of income before me.

11. I would uphold the objections of the applicant in all respects including the claim conceded by the Commissioner to be allowable, namely to deduct the partnership loss of $2,282.00 in the year of income ended 30 June 1980. Accordingly the income of the Applicant is to be reduced by sums of $4,562; $10,634; and $3,340 in the years of income ended 30 June 1980, 1981 and 1982 respectively.
