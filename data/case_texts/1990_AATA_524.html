Last Updated: 4 June 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

INCOME TAX - moving to Western Australia - taxpayer responsible for costs of getting to the place of employment - costs exceeded employers subsidy - whether excess cost allowable deductions - expenses not incurred in course of employment duties - essential character of outgoings is private or domestic.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 S51, S187

REASONS FOR DECISION

22 June 1990 Mr R D Fayle, Member

The facts of this case fall within a small compass and are not in issue. The applicant, a geologist, lived and worked in Melbourne since 1975 prior to moving to Western Australia. The taxpayer was at all material times an employee and not in business. He applied for an advertised position as a geologist in Western Australia. He was interviewed in Melbourne by an officer of the exploration company in question and appointed to a position as geologist to take effect from 26 October 1987. He was responsible for the costs of getting to the place of employment, Perth and he arrived there the day before he was to commence work. His family remained in Melbourne pending the sale of their home. He leased a home unit and resided there until his family joined him subsequent to the sale of the Melbourne home and the purchase of one in a Perth suburb. The employer subsidised the costs of bringing the family furniture to Perth but these costs exceeded the subsidy by $1,445. It is this amount together with an amount of $1,200 for rent of a home unit in Perth whilst living away from the family which were claimed as allowable deduction pursuant to S51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 which were disallowed and which have been referred to this Tribunal for review pursuant to S187(b) of the said Act.

Subsection (1) of S51 provides -

"All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income."

The expenditure was no doubt associated with the earning of assessable income and in that it was an essential prerequisite for the derivation of the salary which was earned from the employment in Perth. Nevertheless its character as expenses of moving household chattels and of renting accommodation was neither relevant nor incidental to the operations by which the applicant derived the assessable income. The expenses were not incurred in the course of his employment duties. To come within the provisions of S51(1) it is necessary that the outgoings be incurred in the course of gaining or producing assessable income. (See Ronpibon Tin NL and Tongkah Compound NL v FCT [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47 at 56-57). The outgoings therefore do not fit within the first limb of the subsection and it is unnecessary therefore to look further. However, if this is not enough it is relevant to add that the essential character of the outgoings is private and or domestic and would not qualify for deduction under the subsection on that basis either.

In deciding that the decision in this reference should be affirmed the Tribunal, whilst sympathetic to the applicant's circumstances, is bound by the principle expressed in Lunney v FCT (1958) 11 ATD 404. In that case Chief Justice Dixon reflecting on an apparent anomaly in the law in relation to the non-deductibility of the cost of fares to and from work said, at 405,-

"I confess for myself, however, that if the matter were to be worked out all over again on bare reason, I should have misgivings about the conclusion. But this is just what I think the Court ought not to do. It is a question of how an undisputed principle applies. Its application was settled by old authority long accepted and always acted upon. If the whole subject is to be ripped up now it is for the legislature and not the Court to do it."

Of course it follows that it is hardly the province of an administrative review tribunal to do it. Similar sentiments were expressed by French J as recently as 1988 in Hyde v FCT 88 ATC 4,748 a case concerning the non-deductibility of child minding expenses incurred to enable a taxpayer to work, where His Honour said at 4,751 -

"One can accept that the taxpayer may well feel some sense of grievance at the fact that the expenditure cannot be claimed as a deduction, but as the courts have said on occasions before today, the answer to that grievance will not be found in the courts but in changing the law and that is a matter for the legislature."

The respondent cited several cases which lend support to the conclusion that expenses incurred in gaining employment or in providing accommodation whilst working in employment at a location away from the family residence are outgoings which do not qualify for deduction under S51(1) ( c. f. FCT v Maddalena 71 ATC 4,161; FCT v Charlton 84 ATC 4,415; 17 CTBRCNS) Case 96; 15 CTBR (NS) Case 80; Case P8 82 ATC 45 and Case X4 90 ATC 116).

In his brief address the applicant relied on a chance remark of Senior Member Beddoe in the decision of this Tribunal in Case X4 90 ATC 116 by suggesting that there is a distinction between the costs incurred in the upkeep of a "home away from home" and those incurred in staying in an hotel whilst away on business. In that case Mr Beddoe said –

"It is undoubtedly the case that if the applicant had adopted a different course and merely put up at a convenient hotel when in the city on business, the hotel account would fall within the positive limbs of S51(1) and be allowable as a deduction, not being outgoings of a private or domestic nature."

However this does not assist the applicant since he, unlike the applicant, in Case X4, was not away from his usual place of residence in the course of conducting a business. Clearly, it follows that if the applicant could establish that the income earning activities commenced before the trip and were integral to it then the outgoing would qualify for deduction; Ballesty v FCT (1977) 7 ATR 711 and Collings v FCT (1976) 6 ATR 476. The decision in Case X4 cannot assist the applicant but on the contrary, provides further support for the respondent's decision. It is not to the point that the expenditure was viewed by the applicant as unavoidable since his quest for work, in the immediate locale of Melbourne had been unsuccessful and this move to Perth was perceived as the only apparent opportunity he had of becoming satisfactorily gainfully employed. The objective purpose of the rent expenditure was to provide alternate accommodation to home which was then too far away and the objective purpose of the removal costs was to move the family's personal effects to Perth - it does not assist that the motive behind these outgoings was to enable the applicant to work to derive assessable income. Where the objective purpose is decisive then motive or subjective purpose have no bearing on the tests for deductibility under S51(1).

For the reasons given, the decision under review is affirmed.
