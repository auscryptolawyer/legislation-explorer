REASONS FOR DECISION

August 1987 Mr Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. These references, heard together by consent, involve two companies - Investments and Holdings - the latter being a major shareholder in the former. Both companies for all practical purposes are wholly under the control of an individual (Manager) who is a Director of both companies. Manager was the only Director to be remunerated for his services and was the only person engaged as an employee of the companies during the years of income ended 30 June 1983 and 1984. Although the events giving rise to the objections before me relate to both companies in both years, there is no objection from Investments before me in relation to the latter year.

  1. In both years both companies made contributions to a superannuation fund ("the Fund") in relation to their employee, Manager. The evidence of Manager was that in each case the amount paid to the Fund was determined by him when he adopted a recommendation made by his accountants. His belief was that they had used a computer facility to determine for each company the maximum amount payable by the companies by way of superannuation contributions which would be allowed as deductions by the Commissioner upon the application of his "Guidelines". (How any computer program could be written to establish the amounts allowable pursuant to the "Guidelines", let alone the Act, was not explained.) The Commissioner says that by the standards of his "Guidelines" no more should be allowed to the companies as deductions for superannuation contributions than has been allowed. The Applicants contend that the Commissioner has erred in applying his own "Guidelines".
  2. Accordingly, as the matter was argued, the first issue to be determined is whether upon a correct application of the "Guidelines" the sum of $1,375 (or a sum greater than the $288 allowed by the Commissioner) is allowable to each company as an income tax deduction. A further question, and really the only question for the Tribunal, is whether $1,375 (or a greater amount than $288) is allowable to each company as a deduction pursuant to Sub-Division AA - "Contributions to Superannuation Funds for Benefit of Employees" - of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act").

4. Manager was born in 1929 and for many years prior to 1978 he was a Director, employee and shareholder in a company which provided superannuation benefits for employees. In 1978, following the sale of his shares in that company, he retired from its service and received c. $14,000 from the superannuation fund conducted by an Australian life assurance office.

  1. In 1961 Manager caused Investments to be incorporated. It purchased a block of nine residential units in suburban Sydney (6x2 bedroom and 3 single bedroom flats). The all-up cost of the purchase would seem to have been $70,280. The evidence was that at the time of purchase the property was returning about 12% gross. Manager's estimate of the current market value of the complex was $500,000 to $550,000. In the same month, Manager also caused Holdings to be incorporated. In 1977/78 Holdings purchased a complex of suburban flats comprising five units in an older structure. The all-up cost of the purchase was approximately $95,000 and the gross rental return was 8-8.5% per annum. Current market value was estimated by Manager to be c. $250,000.

  1. Adjusting the foregoing figures to allow for current net worth at present values of realty (say, $500,000 Investments and $250,000 Holdings), the net worth of the companies would now be of the order of $500,000 and $235,000. Why such companies should only have returned marginal losses in the years in question, even after allowance for the non-income producing shares in Investments held by Holdings, is not easy to understand and was not explained.
  2. However, what does clearly emerge from the foregoing figures, is that, after providing for all other creditors and claims, the amounts which were available for distribution to Manager, and which could be paid without causing a loss on Profit and Loss account were limited to $8,240 (Investments 1983); and to $5,897 and $5,921 (Holdings 1983 and 1984 respectively.) It is in face of that prospect that Manager, as "the controlling mind and will" of the companies, decided that they would pay to, or in respect of, himself the following:

  1. I find that those decisions were expected by Manager to have several tax consequences: the companies would have no taxable income; Manager's assessable income would include the amounts for Director's fees and salary, but not the amounts of $1,375; and the Fund would receive from each company each year $1,375, which would contribute to the further generation of income at a substantial rate of return and be exempt from tax.
  2. I now proceed to a consideration of the position of the Fund. At 30.6.82 the Fund, which existed only for the benefit of the Manager, is said to have stood at $28,163. In the next year it increased as follows:

11. The "loan" to the Fund, which on the evidence did not attract interest, contributed substantially to the profitability of the Fund. Why anyone should favour the Fund with an interest free loan was not explained. In the circumstances I consider the only reasonable explanation to be that it was the sum received by Manager from the earlier superannuation scheme. Further, I observe that, in as much as the accretions to the Fund only came

to hand as the year advanced and, a fortiori, if they only came to hand as the year closed, the returns expressed as a percentage of moneys invested would have been greater.

12. The claims of the companies to deductions in relation to their superannuation contributions are founded in a contention that those contributions constituted an expense incurred by the companies in procuring the services of Manager which were rendered in the course of the derivation of income by the two companies. The superannuation contributions were but one head of expense incurred in relation to the provision of those services. In the course of argument. Manager acknowledged that even the motor vehicle expenses conferred on him a "fringe benefit". However, as the value of that fringe benefit was not considered or conceded in the course of evidence by either party, I will ignore it as a factor advancing the cause of either of them. I will not treat it as favouring the Commissioner by lending support to any contention that the rewards to Manager were excessive. Nor shall I treat it as a factor establishing superannuation contributions to be a lower percentage of the total remuneration package than would otherwise be the case. Accordingly, I approach the issue on the basis that the remuneration package for 1983 was made up as follows:

  1. As the issue was presented before me the Commissioner accepts that the amounts paid by way of Director's fees and salary were not unreasonable. He does not contend that any portion of them should be disallowed as deductions to the companies by force of section 109 of the Act, or treated as dividends in the hands of the Manager by force of the same section. The Commissioner also accepts that superannuation contributions amounting to $288 in each case were reasonable. He seeks only to deny the allowance of any greater deduction to the companies in respect of superannuation contributions. In doing so the Commissioner opposes any contention that the services provided to the companies by Manager merited any greater reward, such as might found a claim to increased provision for superannuation.
  2. Manager gave evidence of his actions as an employee and Director of the companies. I accept that he paid quite close attention to the management of the properties; that he frequently attended the premises; that, as a qualified electrician by trade, he did minor works of maintenance himself; that he received some of the requests of tenants and responded to the calls of the estate agents; that he arranged with others for them to do other maintenance work and carry out repairs; that he played a part in arranging for the cleaning out of flats when tenants vacated; that he maintained the primary financial records of the companies, sufficient to record the rent (less commissions) received from the collecting agents and the other expenses incurred by the companies; that he attended to their investments (and to the investments of the Fund); and that he arranged preparation of all income tax returns and all returns required by the Office of the Commissioner of Corporate Affairs. However, I am not persuaded that those concerns occupied him for up to 4 days on the change of some tenancies; or on average caused him to be engaged for 25 to 30 hours per week. Further, I am not persuaded that the "remuneration package" was determined and structured other than by a concern to distribute the benefits of the package so as to maximise the benefits to Manager and confine as closely as possible the amount of tax to accrue in the public interest to the Commissioner. That is not unlawful. There is no obligation in law to contribute to the community more by way of tax than the law requires, and the requirements of the law are those determined by the enactments of the Parliament. It is therefore necessary to consider whether or not the companies are entitled to the relief they claim by reference to the standards enacted by the Parliament. Before doing so, it will be appropriate to consider the question whether the claims are allowable by the standards of the "Guidelines".
  3. Except to the extent denied by other provisions of the Act, section 51 allows for the deduction of all losses and outgoings "to the extent to which" they were incurred in the derivation of the assessable income of the companies. Expenses incurred in procuring the services of Directors and employees are usually allowable. However, quite apart from any limitations inherent in section 51, such expenses are not always allowable to companies such as these insofar as the expenses relate to the services of a Director and shareholder. Section 109 of the Act provides that amounts paid to shareholders and Directors by a company as remuneration for services rendered by that person are only to constitute an allowable deduction to the extent thought reasonable by the Commissioner. Other provisions of the Act deal with other expenses. Provisions relevant to the present issues are those set forth in sub-division AA of Division III of the Act: - "Contributions to Superannuation Funds for Benefit of Employees".
  4. The provision of reasonable superannuation benefits for employees is encouraged by the provisions of the Act. Of particular relevance to the present applications is section 23F(15) which provides that:

".... the income derived during the year of income by a superannuation fund to which this section applies in relation to the year of income is exempt from income tax".

17. It is common ground that the Fund is just such a fund. Before any fund acquires that status it must satisfy the Commissioner as to a number of requirements, including (inter alia) that the interests of employees in the Fund should be "fully secured." (23F(2)(d); that the benefits are not to be "excessive in amount" (23F(2)(h)); and that "the amount of the fund is not substantially in excess of the amount that is necessary for the purpose of providing benefits ...." (23F(2)(i)) In this way the Act is set to strike a balance between the community and individual interest and between the present and future benefits generated by current service.

  1. In doing so it recognises the desirability of provision being made by those who generate income by "personal exertion" (in the tax sense) for a measure of financial independence in "retirement". The promotion of superannuation schemes also serves other needs by ensuring a measure of "saving" in the community which can support national development; and by reducing the need for, and any expectation of, the elderly being supported by the public purse once income-earning actitivities by way of "personal exertion" have ceased or have been substantially reduced.
  2. One feature of that balance is the concept that the benefits from the fund should not be excessive in amount having regard to:

(i) the remuneration paid to the employee for services rendered by him to his employer;

(ii) the period of the service rendered by the employee to his employer;

(iii) the benefits, pensions and allowances that have been, are being or may be provided for the employee or his dependants from any other fund to which this section applies in relation to the year of income or has applied in relation to a previous year of income; and

(iv) any other matters that the Commissioner considers relevant;" (S23F(2)(h))

20. Another consideration is that ordinarily it is to be expected that provision for superannuation benefits will be made for those fortunate enough to enjoy them throughout all, or much of, their working lives. In consequence, in addressing any question as to what contributions need to be made in any particular year in order to provide for future "maximum benefits"; it is appropriate to have regard to the benefits which can be expected to flow from past superannuation benefits which have either been accumulated and carried forward; or have been taken. Similarly, it is reasonable to assume that, at least unless there are indications to the contrary, contributions will continue to be made in future years.

21. The Applicants say that not only is that balance achieved in the setting of the remuneration package specified in paragraph 7 hereof, but more particularly that, to the extent to which it was provided by way of superannuation contributions, the companies are entitled to the deductions they seek. That entitlement must be established by reference to the provisions of the The Act. As to that the Act is quite specific, even if not precise. Section 82AAR provides:

"A deduction is not allowable under any provision of this Act other than this Sub-division in respect of an amount set apart or paid by a taxpayer as, or to a fund, for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, an employee or employees." (S82AAR)

22. The Sub-division includes Section 82AAE which provides:

"The deduction, or the sum of the deductions, allowable under this Subdivision in an assessment or assessments of a taxpayer or taxpayers in respect of income of the year of income in respect of amounts set apart or paid by the taxpayer or taxpayers as or to a fund or funds for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, any one employee -

(a) shall not exceed whichever is the greater of the following amounts:

(i) $400; and

(ii) 5% of the total remuneration paid to the employee by taxpayers during the year of income of the employee that, in the opinion of the Commissioner, corresponded to the first mentioned year of income in respect of his employment by those taxpayers; or

(b) if the Commissioner is of the opinion that there are special circumstances that justify the allowance of a greater deduction, or of deductions of a greater sum, that the amount ascertained in accordance with paragraph (a) - shall be such amount, being greater than the amount so ascertained, as he considers reasonable."

23. Where, as here, two taxpayers contribute to such a fund for the one employee the amounts allowable to each are to be reduced in accordance with the provisions of section 82AAF. In the circumstances of these cases, each Applicant has been allowed one-half of "5% of the total remuneration" and it is not suggested that any other basis of apportionment should have been used.

  1. It follows then that, if both Applicants are to succeed, they must establish that there are "special circumstances" such as would "justify the allowance of a greater deduction", such as is to be considered "reasonable".
  2. The Applicants rely on calculations claimed to have been made in accordance with the Commissioner's "Guidelines" and in particular Taxation Rulings IT294, IT2067, and IT2201. Those Rulings assume (inter alia) that what is "reasonable" is to be determined by taking into account the prospect of:
    1. Retirement at age 65;
    2. An annual increment in salary until that time at the rate of 8% per annum;
    1. Benefits at age 65 of up to 7 times "final average salary"; and
    1. A Fund earning rate of 12% per annum.

26. The Applicants do not question:

a) Why maximum permitted benefits should be a function of "final average salary" rather than, for example, of average earnings over the whole period of the employee's service;

  1. The assumption that final salary will be greater than current salary;
  1. The assumption that salary will increase at 8% per annum;
  1. Why maximum benefits should be 7 times "final average salary" rather than 6 times or 8 times, or any other multiplier of "final average salary";
  2. Why the multiplier of "final average salary" should be reduced if "final average salary" is above $27,500 per annum.

27. Against that background it is appropriate to look more closely at the Applicants' calculations and compare them with those of the Commissioner, particularly as each claims to implement the same principles. Both take into account current remuneration at $11,500 per annum; years to retirement at 11 years; annual salary increasing at 8% per annum; projected annual income of Fund increasing at 12% per annum; balance in Fund at 30th June, 1983 at $38,494; and that, in accordance with the foregoing the maximum permitted retirement benefit of seven times "final average salary" will be $174,132. The Applicants, by taking into account the Fund at 30th June, 1983 at $38,494 and projecting an earning rate on that Fund of 12% per annum projected that the value of the Fund at date of retirement would be only $133,903, leaving a short-fall of $40,229. Therefore, the Applicants say, sufficient additional money is needed to be injected into the Fund to achieve the permitted result of $174,132.

  1. In those calculations the Applicants have not taken into account the prospect of future contributions to the Fund or of the carry-forward of benefits from the earlier superannuation Fund - represented by the interest free loan. It is possible that I have erred in finding that the interest free loan does represent the proceedings of an earlier fund. Even if I should be incorrect as to that, it is still appropriate that regard should had to the amount which was taken from that earlier fund and to reduce appropriately what might otherwise have been the "maximum benefits". It is also appropriate to have regard to the fact that that capital sum is generating tax free income to augment the earnings of the Fund and with that its capacity to pay future benefits.
  2. The Commissioner accepts that, given the premises, the "short-fall" arises but proposes that, by taking a period of eleven years to retirement and a projected earning rate of the Fund of 12% per annum that the appropriate 'sinking fund factor' which needs to be applied is 0.048415 which, as a matter of calculation, establishes that the "short-fall" could be made up in a period of eleven years by an annual contribution of a further $1,948 to the Fund. However, the Commissioner does not accept that that needs to be done. He points out, correctly in my view, that the calculations of the Applicants did not take into account the amount earlier received from another superannuation fund by Manager and which I have found, as a matter of fact, to be the amount lent interest-free to the Fund. Using the rounded figure of $14,000 and applying a long-term projected earning rate (assuming no loan back - cf ITR 294) of only 9% per annum and allowing fifteen years to retirement from the date the moneys were received, he applies a future value factor of 3.642482 to deduce a future value of $50,995. He then makes the point that the future value of the superannuation of a $14,000 already received exceeds the "short-fall" to be funded by $10,776. So, the Commissioner contends that "excessive benefits" will result without the necessity for any further contributions.

30. Having regard to what I have found, I am satisfied that the contributions of the Applicants in the years of income ended 30th June, 1983 and 1984 as allowed by the Commissioner were more than enough to enable the Fund on then current projections to pay Manager at age 65 the multiple of seven times his projected final average salary and that that could be achieved without any further contributions being received by the Fund so long as the Fund could maintain an earning rate of 12% per annum (cf actual 14.39%) and the "loan" of $14,179 was maintained as part of the Fund. Even if the latter amount was only with the Fund as an interest free loan, I am not persuaded that that fact is likely to render the Fund incapable of providing the reduced maximum benefits which would be appropriate having regard to the earlier superannuation payment.

31. Further, to address the question which most troubled Manager, I am satisfied that it was never represented by the Commissioner by the "Guidelines" or otherwise that the deductions claimed by the Applicants were to be allowed. What seems to be all too often forgotten by tax agents and officers of the Commissioner and by others who look at the tables of figures incorporated in such Tax Rulings is that they are qualified in their terms and are to be read as a whole. For example, in Taxation Ruling IT294 it is said:

"It will be appreciated that the guidelines contained in this statement are in the nature of general rules appropriate to the general run of cases and are in no way intended to supplant the express provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act ....... " (paragraph 2)

32. Furthermore, such a Ruling goes on to say such things as " Cases may arise in which, because of particular circumstances, it would not be appropriate to apply the guidelines. Such cases will have to be determined in the light of their own particular circumstances." (paragraph 2); "The guidelines will apply to superannuation funds that provide retirement benefits for both arm's length employees and/or associated employees subject, of course, to the express provisions of the income tax law relating to associated persons." (paragraph 5); "The projection of current salaries at the specified rate is not, however, intended to apply to employees of all types or ages." (paragraph 13); and so on. Similar qualifications and many other statements relevant to the present issues appear in the other Rulings.

  1. Having satisfied myself that the case for the Applicants as it was presented for them has no merit, I now address the substantial question for determination: do "special circumstances" exist such as would make the allowance of higher deductions than have been allowed by the Commissioner "reasonable". The amounts to be allowed, should that be established, may be as much as is claimed by each Applicant or may be some lower figure but one exceeding that allowed by the Commissioner. As to that, the Applicants bear the responsibility of persuading me that the assessments which have issued are excessive (c.f. Section 190 (b)). I am simply not persuaded that they are. In circumstances such as these determination of what is reasonable in the way of salary; Director's fees; allowances; and superannuation contributions must always be difficult, but I do not find anything in the evidence which persuades me to the view that there are such "special circumstances" as would justify a greater allowance than that already allowed.
  2. The best evidence of "special circumstances" would be the Commissioner's "Guidelines" themselves, but, for the reasons I have stated, I find nothing in them suggesting that the amounts which have been allowed are less than "reasonable" by those standards.
  3. I uphold the determinations of the Commissioner upon the objections.
