ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - Moneys received by association from proceeds of trade fair conducted on behalf of members of the association by a professional conference/fair organizer - whether moneys so incurred were income of the association for the prupose of taxation - the mutuality principle

Income Tax Assessment Act - S.25, 51

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 - S.37

Income Tax and Social Services Contribution Assessment Act - S.117

(Applicant)

REASONS FOR DECISION

11 May 1989 The Hon. Mr Justice Rodney N. PURVIS

(Presidential Member)

APPLICATION FOR REVIEW

This is an application by the AMT Association seeking a review of the objection decision made by the respondent referable to the assessability or otherwise of certain moneys received by :he Association during the 1985 financial year.

In the latter mentioned year, the Association received by way of proceeds from an annual trade fair the sum of $19,000. The spondent sought to bring to tax this amount as part of the income of the Association.

RELEVANT RETURN

In the return of income lodged by the applicant in respect of the 1985 financial year, the taxable income of the association was stated to be $3903. In a schedule to the return it was stated that the Association was a mutual organization and that such of its income as was derived from members was not subject to income tax on the mutuality principle. The schedule also contained a statement that the:

"Proceeds from the annual trade fair of $19,000 represent a return of contributions made by members and are consequently excluded from taxation on the mutuality principle. A copy of the agreement between the [AMT] Association and Exhibits and Trade Fairs Pty Limited is enclosed."

In a note to the accounts forming part of the return, it was stated, "The Association is a mutual organization and income tax has been provided on income derived from non-members only. The principles of tax affect accounting have been applied".

Other than for the inclusion of the $19,000 in the income of the Association sought to be brought to tax, the respondent did not put at issue the inclusion in the taxable. income of the Association of income derived from non-members and interest on investments. The Commissioner did not seek to bring to tax members' subscriptions.

ASSESSMENT AND OBJECTION

The gross proceeds received by the Association consequential on the annual trade fair of $19,000 was thus sought to be made part of the assessable income of the Association. By Notice of Objection made on 4 February 1986, the applicant by its accountants objected to the assessment claiming that the $19,000 should be excluded from assessable income or some part of it. The grounds relied on were:

"1. AMTA is a mutual non-profit organization which sponsors exhibitions and trade fairs to promote the Australian music industry, and its income from members is not subject to income tax under the mutuality principle. Proceeds from the annual trade fair of $19,000 which were included in assessable income by your office represent a return of contributions made by members and are consequently excluded from taxation under the mutuality principle. Clause 6 of the agreement between AMTA and the company, Exhibitions and Trade Fairs Pty Limited (the organizer of the exhibition) clearly states that the income received by AMTA was paid out of the moneys contributed by AMTA members.

  1. The amount of $19,000 is not income and is not assessable pursuant to Section 25 or any other provision of the Income Tax Assessment Act.
  2. Alternatively, and without prejudice to the foregoing, if it is considered that proceeds from the trade fair are assessable in the hands of AMTA then, as assessed in prior years, expenses incurred by AMTA pursuant to Section 51 of the Act relating to the fair should be deducted under Section 51(1), and the balance proportioned between AMTA members and non-members with only that income attributable to non-members being assessable."

Details of the expenditure relating to the fair and a dissection of rental proceeds between AMTA members and non-members were included as an appendix to the objection.

In due course and on 16 May 1986, an amended assessment issued which amended assessment sought then to include in the taxable income of the Association the gross proceeds from the trade fair less expenses of AMTA relating to it. The applicant indicated that it was dissatisfied with the respondent's decision on the objection and requested that the matter be referred to review. Consequently, the matter comes before the Tribunal for consideration.

In the statement required to-be lodged pursuant to Section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the respondent, in stating the reasons for disallowing the objections, stated that the taxable income of the applicant for the year ending 30 June 1985 was not less than the sum referred to in the amended assessment, and that "the amount of $19,000, being proceeds from the annual trade fair, was correctly included in the taxpayer's assessable income for the year ending 30 June 1985 under Sub-section 25(1) of the Act".

RELEVANT FACTS AND CIRCUMSTANCES

The AMT Association is an unincorporated body comprised of companies and individuals who import musical instruments and music for sale in Australia. It was said that the prime object of the Association is to promote the musical industry in Australia. The Association was formed in 1977 and since that time, has each year held an annual trade fair. Up until the 1985 financial year, the Association itself organized this fair. It was said by the person called to give evidence on behalf of the Association, a gentleman who had been a president of it on two occasions, that as most of the members gave of their time on a voluntary basis, the task of organizing the fair was thought to be more appropriately handled by a trade fair organizer. Accordingly, a decision was made that in relation to the 7th fair, that to be held in the 1984/1985 financial year, a professional organizer, Exhibitions and Trade Fairs Pty Limited, should be retained for this purpose. The Association would be the sponsor, the company the organizer.

The Association at all relevant times carried on its activities pursuant to its constitution which had been adopted by the members of it at or about the time of the formation of the Association. The objects of the Association as set forth in the constitution included the following:

to protect and promote the interests of wholesalers, importers, dealers, agents and all other persons engaged in the industry of importing, selling by wholesale and dealing by wholesale in musical instruments and their accessories, sheet music, gramophone records, amplification equipment and the like throughout Australia.

to organize, conduct and manage music trade fairs in Australia and elsewhere and to arrange for the exhibition at such fairs of goods dealt in by members and other goods related to the music industry.

The constitution made reference to the position that would arise in the event of the association being dissolved. Provision was made that:

"If upon the dissolution of the association there remains after satisfaction of all its debts and liabilities any property whatsoever, such property shall not be paid to or distributed among the members of the Association but shall be given or transferred to some other institution or institutions having objects similar to the objects of the Association, such institution or institutions to be determined by the members of the Association at or before the time of dissolution."

As I have mentioned, it was in the 1984 calendar year that a decision was made for the 7th trade fair to be organized by Exhibitions and Trade Fairs Pty Limited. Under date 8 May 1984, an agreement was entered into between the company as organizer and the Association as the sponsor. The agreement recited that:

"A. The sponsor is an association of companies, firms and persons engaged in the industry of importing, selling and dealing by wholesale in musical instruments, sheet music, gramophone records and the like.

  1. The sponsor has from time to time conducted exhibitions and trade fairs (hereinbefore referred to as "Exhibitions") promoting the Australian Music Industry.
  1. The sponsor desires to conduct exhibitions throughout Australia for the promotion of the music industry and has requested the organisers to organise and stage the exhibition under its sponsorship.
  1. The Organisers carry on the business of organising, staging and managing exhibitions and have agreed to organise, stage and manage such exhibitions under the sponsorship of the sponsor"

The agreement specified the obligations to be assumed by the organizers, namely, to organize stage and manage and promote exhibitions in accordance with the terms and conditions of the agreement, and further provided that a bank account would be opened in a name to be determined by the parties into which would be deposited "all money received by the organisers from exhibitions space sales and that unless agreed by the sponsor, the funds will be used firstly to discharge the sponsor's liability for rental, cleaning and electrical costs (if any) relating to the exhibition and to the venue". Joint signatures were required in order that access might be had to the bank account. Provision was made for the conduct of the exhibition and it was then provided by clause 6, that:

"In consideration of this agreement and the appointment in clause 1 hereof, the sponsor shall be entitled to received out of the moneys contributed by its members to the exhibition an amount calculated in accordance with the following formula:

(a) FOR THE EXHIBITION YEAR 1984

$13,0000.00 plus $1,000.00 for every 100 square metres of floor space sold in excess of 1800 square metres up to and including 2400 square metres;"

It was further provided:

"The sponsor shall be entitled to withdraw the said amounts of members contributions in accordance with clause 6 within fourteen (14) days of the end of each exhibition."

The exhibition was held and the amount payable to the sponsor pursuant to clause 6 was calculated to be $19,000, which sum was withdrawn from the bank account and paid to the Association on 6 September 1984.

Prior to the holding of the trade fair, each exhibitor was required to enter into an agreement with the organizer stipulating the space sought to be occupied and the payment to be made proportionate to the "stand size". Whilst the agreement was between the exhibitor and the organizer, the agreement stated that the exhibition was "sponsored by the [AMT] Association".

The Association was, however, otherwise not a party to the agreement.

The officer of the Association called to give evidence said that clause 6 in the agreement between the sponsor and the organizer was inserted in order to "assure that the Association received the funds it was relying on to continue work for members and that this should come from members' bookings for the trade fair". He further said that the clause "was written into the agreement in order that it might be clear that the money to be received by the Association was only to come out of moneys paid to the organizer by members". In all, no less than 48 companies, individuals or firms were exhibitors at the trade fair of which 29 were members of the Association. The floor space taken by the members was sufficient to satisfy the ceiling set by clause 6(a) of the agreement. A bank account was opened under the name "Exhibition and Trade Fairs P/L Music 84" in January 1984. It was closed in June 1985. It was said on behalf of the Association that discussions had been taking place between the organizer and the Association sometime prior to the May agreement being executed between them, and that the bank account was opened, be it before the agreement was signed, pursuant to "a gentleman's agreement". The bank account so opened was, it was said, the bank account envisaged by the agreement. The Tribunal finds this to have been so.

The reason for retaining the organizer was "to ensure that the trade fair was run with as little trouble as possible' to the Association", and the only moneys received by the Association consequent on the trade fair was the sum of $19,000.

Likewise, the moneys paid by members of the Association to the organizer were the only moneys paid by members in connection with the trade fair. Whether they were paid to the organizer in respect of space sold or not, they were clearly the payments of money contemplated by clause 6 of the agreement between the organizer and the sponsor.

MUTUALITY

The applicant, consistent with the objection taken by it to the assessment and amended assessment, submitted that the moneys received by it from the organizer should not be brought to tax and should not be regarded as income subject to tax pursuant to Section 25(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. That is, the applicant submitted that the principle of mutuality based as it is upon the proposition that one cannot derive income from one's own self should be applied to the circumstances of this case and where the Association was comprised of the persons from whom the moneys were received. It was said that the members of the Association had joined together to form the Association and contributed to a common fund created and controlled for the benefit ' of all members of the Association, irrespective of the proportions in which the contributions were made. Accordingly, the sum of the moneys paid by the members, that is, the $19,000, be it indirectly to the Association, was not income subject to tax.

The respondent contended that the situation was otherwise and that the applicant was a resident Association in the business of inter alia promoting the Australian music industry and the interests of its members. It was said that the amount of $19,000 was received by the Association in the ordinary course of its business, was fully assessable and not a capital receipt. It was not contended that the moneys so received were of a capital nature.

The respondent, consistent with the assessment issued, and the contention that mutuality did not apply to exclude the moneys received from the taxable income of the applicant, relied upon the fact that, the members of the applicant taxpayer were not entitled, pursuant to the Constitution of the Association, to participate in any surplus of funds upon a dissolution of the Association. It was submitted on behalf of the Commissioner that where a club in its constitution provides for the non return to members of a surplus arising on dissolution that mutuality does not apply, and members7 subscriptions, for example, are in these circumstances to be included in the club income for the purpose of arriving at its assessable income.

Reliance in this regard was placed upon what was said in the reasons for decision in Municipal Mutual Insurance Limited v Hills (1930) 16 TC 430; Revesby Credit Union Co-operative Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1965] HCA 2; (1965) 13 ATD 449, and Sydney Water Board Employees' Credit Union Limited v F.C. of T, 73 ATC 4129. In the Municipal Mutual Insurance Limited case (supra), the court was concerned with the bringing to tax of a ' surplus arising on employers liability and miscellaneous insurance business done with fire policy holders, the latter having structured the company for the purpose of insuring against fire on the most favourable terms, and not initially for the purpose of the company conducting employers liability and miscellaneous insurance. It was said in bringing to tax the surplus on the employers liability and miscellaneous insurance business, and at 448:

".... as the common fund is composed of sums provided by the contributors out of their own moneys, any surplus arising after satisfying claims obviously remains their own money .... The cardinal requirement is that all the contributors to the common fund must be entitled to participate in the surplus and that all the participators in the surplus must be contributors to the common fund; in other words, there must be complete identity between the contributors and the participators. If this requirement is satisfied, the particular form which the association takes is immaterial."

Thus it was said that in the subject case, and having mind the provision in the Constitution as to the disposition of funds on a dissolution of the Association, that the members were not entitled to participate in any surplus, any surplus was not theirs, and thus mutuality did not apply. In Revesby Credit Union Co-operative Limited (supra), a case concerned with the question as to whether the receipts of the society were non-assessable income, and the applicability of the principle of mutuality, and at 453, McTiernan J, in the course of his reasons for decision, said:

"Before considering the interpretation of Section 117 [Income Tax and Social Services Contribution Assessment Act] I will deal with the appellant's contention that by virtue of the principle of mutuality, the dividend paid to the members is not income at all, but is merely a repayment of part of their contributions made to a common fund for a common purpose. The principle of mutuality seems to me to be settled. Where a number of people contribute to a fund created and controlled by them for a common purpose, any surplus paid to the contributors after the use of the fund for the common purpose is not income but is to be regarded as a mere repayment of the contributor's own money. The Bohemians Club v Acting Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1918] HCA 16; (1918) 24 CLR 334. What is required is that the fund must have been created for the common purpose and owned or controlled wholly by the contributors. If it is owned or controlled by anyone else, the principle cannot apply. The Equitable Life Assurance Society of the United States v Bishop (1900) 1 QB 177."

In Sydney Water Board Employees' Credit Union Limited (supra), a case concerned with the assessability or otherwise of interest received from members and the applicability of the mutuality principle, it was held that the mutuality principle insofar as it was not excluded by a statutory provision was applicable in Australia and was not confined to cases of insurance. (see The Social Credit Savings and Loans Society Limited v F.C. of T. 71 ATC 4232; Fletcher v I.T. Commr (1972) AC 414). At 4134, in the course of his reasons for decision, Mason J (as he then was) said:

"It is evident from the terms in which the principle was expressed (the principle of mutuality) that it extends beyond the field of insurance although it is most frequently applied in cases of mutual insurance. It is equally apparent that just as the receipt of surplus contributions was not in the circumstances "gains or profits" in the ordinary sense of that expression, so also it does not constitute in the hands of the recipients "income" in the natural and ordinary sense of that word.

In this respect, the decision and the judgment of Griffiths CJ in the Bohemians Club v The Acting F.C. of T. [1918] HCA 16; (1918) 24 CLR 334 are illuminating. It was decided that the receipt by a social club of annual subscriptions from its members was not income of the club. In reaching that conclusion, Griffiths CJ (at p.337) pointed out that the subscriptions were advances of capital for a common purpose which were expected to be exhausted and went on to say, "If anything is left unexpended, it is not income or profits but savings which the members may claim to have returned to them". This was because there was identity between those who paid and those who received the contributions in question.

More recently in Social Credit Savings and Loan Society Limited v F.C. of T. (supra) at p.4238, Gibbs J rejected the argument that the principle has no application under the Income Tax Assessment Act. After referring to the Bohemians Club case (supra) and to Revesby Credit Union Co-operative Limited v F.C, of T. [supra] at p.574, his Honour said:

"A return to contributors of a saving for them of the surplus of their contributions to a common fund is no more income than it is a profit. The mutuality principle has been rendered inapplicable to certain cases by the specific provisions of the Act, but where not excluded, it will in appropriate circumstances lead to the conclusion that the surplus arising from a transaction within the principle cannot be treated as income"."

The respondent further submitted that the amount of 9,000 paid to the taxpayer by the organizer was paid pursuant to an agreement to which the members of the Association were not parties. Hence no mutuality. It was said that the doctrine of privity of contract applied and that accordingly, the payment made by the organizer could not be said to be from funds obtained out of' moneys paid by members. The factual evidence was otherwise. It is quite apparent that by reason of the agreement between the Association and the organizer, and the agreement between the member and the organizer, reciting as it does the involvement of the Association, that the payment made by the member calculated on a floor space basis is directly related to the payment made by the organizer to the Association, likewise based on a floor space basis.

It was further contended on behalf of the respondent that no part of the $19,000 paid to the Association pursuant to the agreement "constituted payments of subscriptions by members". Hence no mutuality. However, it is clear on the evidence that the means utilized whereby members made payment to the Association by way of the organizer was one of convenience to the Association and the organizer and not otherwise. It was clearly envisaged that the organizer would be making a payment to the Association proportionate to space taken by members and out of the "contributions" made by such members. Whether by "subscription" or "contribution", the money came from members. It was further said that the subject amount was paid to the Association pursuant to an agreement with a non-member of the Association, the organizer, and accordingly, an amount derived from sources outside the Association. I do not accept this to have been so. The Association, being one entitled to enter into an agreement pursuant to its Constitution, by its authorized officers, agreed on behalf of its members that payment would be made to it by the organizer out of contributions paid to the organizer by members. The necessary nexus is clearly established.

In this matter, the Tribunal finds that the payments made by members of the Association to the organizer out of which moneys the organizer, pursuant to the agreement, made payment to the sponsor, the Association, such moneys then to be retained by the sponsor and used by it in furtherance of its objects, was money advanced by the members for a common purpose. It is implicit from the evidence that if the $19,000 was not to be paid to the sponsor that then the charge made in respect of floor space would have been other than what it was. It would have been correspondingly less.

The clause in the Constitution relied upon by the respondent referable to distribution of any surplus arising on dissolution places the disposal of any such surplus at the decision of the members of the Association. Accordingly, it is the view of the Tribunal that not only are the funds of the Association pursuant to the Constitution owned by the members of the Association, but they are controlled wholly by such members. A decision as to disposition is one for the members and they control such disposition. No other person has a part to play in 3 use to which the funds are to be put. The Tribunal does not consider that anything said in the reasons for decision in the Municipal Mutual Insurance Limited v Hills (supra), Revesby Credit Union Co-operative Limited v F.C. of T. (supra) or Sydney Water Board Employees' Credit Union v F.C. of T. (supra) is other than supportive of mutuality being a principle appropriate to be applied in this matter.

DECISION

The Tribunal is of the opinion that the principle of mutuality does apply in the circumstances of this matter, and for the reasons hereinbefore set forth. The moneys received by the Association were so received as a consequence of moneys having been advanced by members and were paid out of a fund into which such moneys had been placed. The fact that the moneys paid by members to the organizer might have been intermixed with other moneys received by the organizer from non-members does not preclude this conclusion being available. The Association was one controlled by its members. The disposition of its funds was at their discretion and subject to their decision.

Accordingly, the objection decision is set aside and the matter referred back to the respondent for the issue of an assessment in accord with these reasons.
