Last Updated: 29 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - notice of assessment issued in accordance with taxpayer's return - income shown as from partnership - objection on basis that return wrong and no partnership existed - onus of proof not discharged

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 8, 188, 190(b)

Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986

DECISION AND REASONS

Re:

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No ST82/1

AAT Decision No 3837

Deputy President I R Thompson

Date: 16 October 1987

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. ST82/1

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal: Deputy President I R Thompson

Date: 16 October 1987

The objection decision under review is affirmed.

(Sgd I R Thompson)

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No ST82/1

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

REASONS FOR DECISION

  1. The taxpayer is a general medical practitioner. In July 1960 he lodged a return of his income for the income year which ended on 30 June 1979. That return had been prepared by an accountant. It showed income of $43,794 as the applicant's share of partnership income, the partners being Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones. (These names are pseudonyms which I have applied for the purposes of this statement of reasons, as are the names of other medical practitioners referred to in it.) In due course, the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in Adelaide issued a notice of assessment based on that return. He accepted that the applicant's taxable income during that income year had been as shown in the return. However, upon receipt of the assessment, the applicant lodged an objection to it. In his statement of the grounds for his objection, he said that he had not been a partner in any partnership of medical practitioners during that year of income, and that his income had been $12,000 less than had been shown in his income tax return. The Deputy Commissioner disallowed the objection on the ground that the applicant had been a partner in the partnership during the income year and $43,794 was his share of the income of the partnership.
  2. The applicant then requested that the matter be referred to a Taxation Board of Review pursuant to section 188 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). It was referred to a Taxation Board of Review on 9 May 1986. It had not been reviewed by the Board of Review by 30 June 1986, when the Taxation Boards of Review were abolished by the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986. Any references not dealt with by then were deemed to have been referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal pursuant to the provisions of the Act as amended by the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act (supra). That Act provides that such referrals are to be treated as though they were applications to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal and dealt with as such.
  3. At the hearing by the Tribunal, the applicant was represented by Mr D Le Poidevin, a barrister and solicitor, and the respondent by Mr N Oliver, a departmental officer. Oral evidence was given by the applicant and a number of documents were tendered in evidence. They included copies of the applicant's income tax returns for the years of income which ended on 30 June 1978 and 30 June 1979 respectively, income tax returns for the same years in respect of a partnership of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones, and memoranda of transfer of a portion of land in a certain town in South Australia. They also included copies of two documents which were undated and unsigned; they respectively took the form of an agreement between Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones to establish a partnership, and of a deed dissolving a partnership of Dr Smith Jnr, the applicant, Dr Brown and Dr Green.
  4. The income tax return of the partnership of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones for the year of income which ended on 30 June 1978 disclosed partnership income of $49,667, with each of the four partners' share being one quarter of that amount. It was signed by Dr Smith Jnr and had been prepared by an accountant, Mr S, who recorded that double entry records had been maintained as the books of account of the partnership and that the answers given in the return, which included the details of the partners, were true and correct in every particular. The applicant's income tax return for that year of income, which was prepared by another accountant, Mr B, showed that the applicant received income during that year in three different capacities. It showed that he received $11,200 as remuneration paid to him by Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr; a group certificate was annexed to the return in respect of that income. The return also showed that he had received fees of $14,822.90. Finally it showed that he had received $12,417 as his share of the income of a partnership comprising Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones.

5. The partnership return of the partnership of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones for the year of income which ended on 30 June 1979 was also signed by Dr Smith Jnr and again was prepared by Mr S. He again recorded that double entry records had been kept of the books of account of the partnership; he certified that the answers set out in the return, which included details of the partners, were true and correct in every particular. The return showed that the income of the partnership had been $175,175 and that the share of each of the partners was one quarter, namely $43,794. Annexed to the return was a balance sheet showing, inter alia, the interest of each partner in the capital of the partnership on 30 June 1979; it showed that during that year, the applicant had drawn $49,185, so that his share of the capital was a negative amount.

  1. The applicant's personal income tax return for the income year ended 30 June 1979 showed his only income as being the amount of his share of the income of the partnership. The return was again prepared by Mr B and, as required, the applicant signed a declaration that the particulars shown in it were true and correct in every detail and disclosed a full and complete statement of the total income derived by him from all sources during the year of income.
  2. The first of the memoranda of transfer of the portion of land shows that it was purchased by Dr Smith Jnr, Dr Jones and the applicant for $15,500 and transferred to them as tenants-in-common. Each of them signed accepting the transfer. The transfer took place on 21 December 1978.
  3. The second memorandum of transfer shows that Dr Smith Jnr, Dr Jones and the applicant transferred the land on 26 November 1979 to a private company and that it did so pursuant to the terms of a deed dated 1 November 1979. Neither the deed nor any copy of it was tendered in evidence.
  4. It was against that background that the applicant gave his evidence. He said that he had qualified as a medical practitioner overseas, that he had known Dr Smith Jnr at school and had maintained contact with him while studying medicine. He had visited Australia in 1975 to explore the possibility of coming to work in Australia. He had taken the opportunity to obtain registration as a medical practitioner in South Australia. Subsequently, after he had returned to his home overseas, Dr Smith Jnr had invited him to come and work as locum for him for a short period; he had done so and then again returned to his home overseas.
  5. However, in 1977 the applicant had come to Australia to settle permanently and Dr Smith Jnr and his father, Dr Smith Snr, who were carrying on a medical practice together, employed him to work in that practice. He said that he did so until 2 December 1977, when he returned to the country which had formerly been his home to spend Christmas there. During that period of employment he had resided in a small country town about 40 kilometres from the town in which Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr resided, which was the town in which the portion of land was subsequently purchased.
  6. Dr Smith Jnr had previously undertaken an obligation to provide medical services to a hospital in the small country town in which the applicant resided, and also in another small country town a further 70 or 80 kilometres from the town in which Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr resided. The applicant provided those services and also saw patients privately as an employee of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr.
  7. The applicant said that after Christmas 1977 he returned to Australia; Dr Smith Jnr then proposed that the applicant should no longer be an employee but an associate in the practice which Dr Smith Jnr carried on with his father. The applicant said that that meant that he was to retain the income which he earned in the practice and to pay half the expenses of the practice. That is consistent with the information in his income tax return for the year of income ended 30 June 1978 that income was derived from fees during that year and that expenditure was incurred in earning that income, including expenditure on wages, electricity, telephone, rent, cleaning expenses and repairs and maintenance. I accept the applicant's evidence that that was the manner in which he worked in the practice during the period immediately after his return to Australia at the start of the calendar year 1978.
  8. The applicant gave evidence that at some time early in 1978 Dr Jones, who had been a student with him at his medical school, visited him while en route from another State to Adelaide and that, following that visit, Dr Smith Jnr invited Dr Jones to come into the practice as an associate like the applicant. He said that the accounting procedures were then changed because of the increase in the size of the practice. Previously all the moneys which he had earned had been paid into his own personal bank account, and he had paid Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr his share of the expenses of the practice; but, after Dr Jones joined the practice, all money received for work done by any of the doctors working in the practice was paid into a joint bank account in the names of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones. The expenses of the practice were, the applicant said, paid out of that account, one third of them being debited against each of Dr Smith Jnr, Dr Jones and the applicant. The amounts paid to Dr Jones and the applicant respectively were the amounts earned by each of them, less the one third share of the expenses. He said that that arrangement continued until 30 June 1979.
  9. At one point in 1978, the applicant said, Dr Smith Jnr had handed to him and to Dr Jones a copy of a draft agreement for a partnership between them; that, he said, was the document tendered in evidence. Dr Smith Jnr had suggested that they consider the draft and decide whether to establish a partnership. However, the partnership had not been established and the agreement had remained unexecuted.
  10. The applicant gave evidence that by 1 July 1979 Dr Brown, who had also been a fellow student of the applicant at his medical school, had arrived and joined the practice. On that happening, he said, a partnership was established by Dr Smith Jnr, Dr Jones, Dr Brown and the applicant. That there was such a partnership in the year of income which ended on 30 June 1980 is consistent with what was shown in a partnership income tax return for that year. However, the deed tendered in evidence, which the applicant said was executed, shows that Dr Green was also a partner by May 1980. The applicant gave evidence that Dr Green had joined the practice at some time after 1 July 1979. Although the applicant gave evidence that the deed was executed, the copy tendered in evidence contains a number of hand-written additions and presumably is not a photocopy of the deed as eventually drawn up for execution.
  11. The applicant gave evidence that the land that was transferred to Dr Smith Jnr, Dr Jones and himself was land on which it was intended to erect premises for the medical practice. He said also that the private company to which they subsequently transferred their title to the land was established to manage the practice and that, when it was established, he was a shareholder. Although apparently $15,500 was paid for the land, he did not contribute any part of that price, he said, and did not undertake to do so. Asked why in those circumstances he became a tenant-in-common with a one-third share in the land, he was led by Mr Le Poidevin to say that he was expected by Dr Smith Jnr to guarantee a loan to be provided by the bank for the erection of the premises for the practice, as the bank would not lend the money to Dr Smith Jnr on his own. However, he said that all those working in the practice, by then Dr Jones, Dr Brown, Dr Green and Dr White, were also expected, like him, to guarantee the loan. He did not explain why, in those circumstances, he should have taken a one third share in the ownership of the land. The fact that it was owned by three tenants-in-common and not a single proprietor, would not have affected its value to the bank as security for a loan.
  12. In support of his assertion that the first date on which a partnership was formed of which he was a member was 1 July 1979, the applicant tendered in evidence a statement lodged under the Business Names Act 1973 showing a change of the persons in relation to whom a business name was registered. The business name was "Smith Clinic". The statement was lodged in December 1979 and contained a statement that on 30 June 1979 Dr Smith Snr ceased to carry on business under the registered business name and that on 1 July 1979 Dr Smith Jnr, the applicant, Dr Jones and Dr Brown began to carry on the business under that name. That statement was signed by the applicant and the other three doctors named in it and was lodged in January 1980, some months before he lodged his personal income tax return for the year of income ended 30 June 1979. So there is no reason to suspect that it was made ex post facto to support the objection which he subsequently made against his assessment of income tax for that year.

18. However, the statement made by Dr Smith Snr that he had carried on the business up to 30 June 1979 appears not to have been correct. It is inconsistent with the group certificate issued by him and Dr Smith Jnr in respect of the applicant's employment by them jointly in 1977. It is inconsistent also with the partnership income tax returns for the years ended 30 June 1978 and 30 June 1979 respectively, which were certified by Mr S to contain the correct particulars. It seems, therefore, that steps had not been taken earlier at the proper times to regularise the registration of the business name, and all that occurred in January 1980 was that it was regularised with effect from July 1979. There seems to have been little reason for Dr Jones, Dr Brown and the applicant to state that a change had occurred on 1 July 1979 if it was not in fact so. Accordingly I accept that the partnership which included Dr Brown was established on that date; but, because the statement showing that Dr Smith Snr had operated the business on his own before 1 July 1978 cannot be relied on, it does not afford evidence that no partnership of which the applicant was a member existed before that date.

  1. The applicant gave evidence that all amounts received by him in respect of his work in the medical practice in the year of income which ended on 30 June 1979 were paid into his bank account. He tendered in evidence a book containing the customer's copy of paying-in slips relating to payments into his bank account. During the year there were seventeen payments into the account; they totalled $31,775. He tendered a book containing the butts of cheques drawn on the joint bank account in the names of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, himself and Dr Jones but the period over which the cheques were drawn was only from 19 June 1978 to 29 September 1978. He did not tender the butts of cheques drawn during the remaining nine months of the year; nor were the double entry records of the practice, to which Mr S referred in the income tax return of the partnership for the year, tendered in evidence. So it is impossible to know why Mr S prepared that return showing that the applicant had drawn $49,185 during that year. Either Mr S was in error or the applicant's evidence is not correct that all amounts received by him from the practice were paid into his personal bank account.
  2. On the customer's copy of a paying-in slip dated 31 May 1978, someone has written:-

"1/4 share from partnership to 18/5/78

4481.27

2000.00 paid for partnership to Smith and Smith banked $2481.27"

On the customer's copy of the paying-in slip dated 9 August 1978, there is a notation:-

"097344 by cheque July partnership"

  1. Each of those notations clearly was written directly onto the customer's copy of the paying-in slip and is not a carbon copy of anything written on the original of the paying-in slip. The handwriting of both notations is the same. The applicant gave evidence that he did not know whose it was. I accept that it was not his. It is different from the handwriting appearing on the carbon copy as that of the person who wrote the entries on the original of the paying-in slip dated 31 May 1978; but it is similar to the handwriting of the person who apparently made the entries on the original of the paying-in slip dated 9 August 1978. The applicant gave evidence that the payments into his account were made by "girls" in the office of the practice who kept the accounts of the practice. The persons who made the payments in signed the paying-in slips. They were clearly two different persons. However, I am satisfied that the notations were probably made by a member of the office staff of the practice who was responsible for maintaining its accounting records.
  2. Another feature of the two paying-in slips is that the one dated 31 May 1978 shows the drawer of the cheque paid in as Smith, Smith and the applicant, whereas that dated 9 August 1978 shows the drawer as Smith, Smith, Jones and the applicant. The conclusion can, I think, be drawn that, before a joint account was opened in the names of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones, a joint account had been opened in the names of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr and the applicant. However, no further conclusions can properly be drawn from that either favourable or adverse to the applicant's case.
  3. The butts of the cheques drawn on the joint account of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr, the applicant and Dr Jones contain brief statements of the purpose of the payment in respect of which each cheque was issued. The butt in respect of a cheque issued on 8 August 1978 to Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr for $10,450 records that it was "share for July". The butt of a cheque drawn on the same day in favour of the applicant for $3,225 similarly shows "share for July". The butt of a cheque issued on 15 September 1978 in favour of Drs Smith Snr and Smith Jnr is for $5,500 and is shown as being in respect of "share 1.8.78 to 14.9.78 and partnership". The butt of a cheque issued on the same day in favour of the applicant is for $250 and shows "partnership share". Another cheque drawn on the same day in favour of the applicant for $3,000 shows "share - cheque to cover transfer". Again no conclusion either favourable or adverse to the applicant's case can, I believe, be drawn from those facts.
  4. The cheque for $3,000 was not paid into the applicant's personal bank account. He gave no explanation of that. Prima facie it is evidence that his income was more than the $31,775 paid into his account, which he said was his total income for the income year. Further it casts doubt on whether reliance can be placed on the paying-in slips as evidence of all the income derived by the applicant in that year.
  5. Section 190(b) of the Act imposes upon the applicant the burden of proving that the assessment was excessive. The applicant's case, based on an assertion that his own income tax return was totally inaccurate, is so bizarre that he might be thought unlikely ever to have contemplated presenting it if it were not true. However, he has not explained satisfactorily why his evidence is inconsistent with the income tax returns prepared by Mr S and Mr B. He did not know how Mr B obtained the information which he included in his own return. He said that Mr B and Mr S were not associated with one another in any way, but that he might well have told Mr B that the tax return for the practice had been prepared by Mr S. He surmised that Mr B had obtained the information from Mr S. Early in the hearing, I enquired whether the accountants were to be called as witnesses; Mr Le Poidevin informed me that that they were not. I indicated my concern about that. That concern increased as the evidence of the applicant proceeded, because in many instances, when asked why certain things had been done, he said, in effect, that he did not know because he had left his financial affairs for others to manage for him.
  6. As I have already noted, Mr S stated in the returns for the partnership that it had maintained double entry records; the Tribunal was not told of any effort having been made by the applicant or on his behalf to obtain those records. The apparent payment of $3,000 to the applicant on 15 September 1978 has not been explained and is inconsistent with the evidence given by the applicant.

27. If all the evidence which should have been available had been presented to the Tribunal, it might possibly have explained some of the matters which the applicant himself was unable to explain and it might have given support to the substance of his evidence. As it is, the Tribunal is left with an account of events which is at variance with the income tax returns prepared by Mr S and Mr B. In Mr S's case the returns were prepared from records of the practice. The account given by the applicant is not even fully consistent with the small amount of documentary evidence which was presented. It is possible that the applicant has been truthful and also correct in his understanding and recollection of his role in the practice during the income year ended 30 June 1979. However, I am unable to find on a balance of probabilities that that is so. He has, therefore, not discharged the onus imposed upon him by section 190(b) of the Act; the objection decision under review must be affirmed.
