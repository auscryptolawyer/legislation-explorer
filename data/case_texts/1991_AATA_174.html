Re Taxation Appeals [1991] AATA 174; 22 ATR 3337 (24 July 1991)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

Nos. NT90/177 and NT91/6

AAT No. 7211

Income Tax

[1991] AATA 174; 22 ATR 3337

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Dr P. Gerber(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Assessable Income - Whether an ETP from a superannuation fund payable on redundancy was a payment in lieu of superannuation benefits under sec 27F(2) such that it lost the concessional treatment accorded by sec 27C(2) - Whether an ETP from a superannuation fund made on termination of that fund was a payment in lieu of superannuation benefits under sec 27F(2) such that it lost concessional treatment accorded by sec 27C(2) - Method of calculation of termination amount in sec 27F discussed.

Acts Interpretation Act 1901 (Cth) as amended, sec 15AB.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1937-1989 (Cth) secs 27A, 27C(2), 27F(1) and (2).

HEARING

SYDNEY

24:7:1991

ORDER

The Tribunal finds that $10,704.96 of the amount of $51,136.56 received by the applicant in the 1988 tax year and all of the $14,254.03 received by the applicant in the 1989 tax year are bona fide redundancy payments entitled to the 5% concessional treatment provided for by sec 27C(2). The matter is to be remitted to the respondent to be dealt with in accordance with the Tribunal's findings.

DECISION

In outline, on 29 February 1988, the applicant was dismissed from employment with Charge Card Services Limited ("the company") by reason of redundancy due to the voluntary winding up of the company. On that date, he received $26,926.46 by way of a bona fide redundancy payment from the company (with which this reference is not concerned), and $51,136.56 (less $317.80 tax instalment deduction) from Charge Card Services Limited Superannuation Fund ("the fund") of which he was a member. In his 1988 tax return, the applicant returned the amount ("the first payment") as an eligible termination payment ("ETP") assessable as follows:

The respondent's position with respect to those paragraphs was that, while he would not be producing evidence to establish a contrary version of those paragraphs, he did not want to be held responsible for the way those paragraphs were expressed as he wished to test the calculations of the actuary who was to be called by the applicant in relation to the matters dealt with therein.

The first payment

9. Both parties accept that the first payment is an ETP as defined in sec 27A of the Act (see paragraph 40 of the Agreed Facts). The issue is to what amount, if any, is a "bona fide redundancy payment" in accordance with sec 27F coming within the definition of "concessional component" in sec 27A(1), and attracting the concessional treatment accorded by sec 27C(2) (with only 5% of such amount being included as assessable income).

10. Section 27F of the Act provides:

27F(1) Where -

(a) an eligible termination payment is made in relation to a

taxpayer in consequence of the dismissal of the taxpayer from

any employment at any time (in this section referred to as the

"termination time") by reason of the bona fide redundancy of the

taxpayer;

(b) the termination time was before -

(i) if there was a date before the sixty-fifth anniversary of

the birth of the taxpayer on which the termination of the

employment of the taxpayer would necessarily have occurred

by reason of the taxpayer attaining a particular age or

completing a particular period of service - that date; or

(ii) in any other case - the sixty-fifth anniversary of

the birth of the taxpayer;

(c) if the Commissioner, having regard to any connection between

the employer and the taxpayer and to any other relevant

circumstances, is satisfied that the employer and the taxpayer

were not dealing with each other at arm's length in relation to

the termination of the employment of the taxpayer - the amount

of the eligible termination payment does not exceed the amount

of an eligible termination payment that could reasonably be

expected to have been made in relation to the taxpayer if the

employer and the taxpayer had been dealing with each other at

arm's length in relation to the termination of the employment

of the taxpayer; and

(d) there was, at the termination time, no agreement between the

taxpayer and the employer, or the employer and another person,

to employ the taxpayer after the termination time, so much of

the eligible termination payment as exceeds the amount (in this

section referred to as the "termination amount") of an eligible

termination payment that could reasonably be expected to have

been made in relation to the taxpayer had he voluntarily

retired from that employment at the termination time is a bona

fide redundancy payment in relation to the taxpayer.

27F(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), where -

(a) in the opinion of the Commissioner, it is appropriate to regard

a part (in this subsection referred to as the "forgone benefit

part") of an eligible termination payment made in relation to a

taxpayer as having been made in lieu of superannuation benefits to

which the taxpayer may have become entitled at the termination time

or a later time; and (my italics)

(b) the forgone benefit part would not, but for this

subsection, be included in the termination amount,

the termination amount shall include the forgone benefit part.

11. Both parties are prepared to accept that sec 27F(1)(a), (b), (c) and (d) are satisfied in relation to the first payment (see paragraphs 25 - 29 and 40 of the Agreed Facts).

12. Further, the parties agree that "the termination amount" referred to in the concluding words of sec 27F(1) is not less than $13,358.26 (see paragraphs 36 and 41). The only real issue is whether there is a "forgone benefit part" (and if so, what amount) to be included in the abovementioned "termination amount" pursuant to sec 27F(2).

13. Sec 27F(2) was inserted by sec 5 of the Income Tax Assessment Amendment Act 1985 (No. 129 of 1985). Neither counsel has been able to find any authority on the interpretation of these amending provisions.

14. The primary submission of Mr Slater of learned counsel for the applicant was that lump sum benefits were already dealt with by sec 27F as it stood before the insertion of subsection (2), so that sec 27F(2) is to be interpreted as denying the 5% concessional treatment to those benefits which were not payable as a lump sum, but payable as a pension amount. Hence the subsection was inapplicable to the payment in question.

15. On the other hand, Mr Durack, of learned counsel for the respondent, submitted that "it is appropriate to regard" $37,778.30, being the excess over the amount of the ETP that could reasonably expected to have been made in relation to the applicant had he voluntarily retired from the company's employment at the time of his redundancy (the resignation benefit), $13,358.26, "as having been made in lieu of superannuation benefits to which the applicant may have become entitled at the termination time or a later time." This submission was based upon Rule 11A(A) of the rules made in accordance with the power to do so contained in clause 3 of the fund's trust deed. Rule 11A(A) provided:

Notwithstanding any other provision of the Deed or these rules, a

member who leaves employment by reason of bona fide redundancy

shall, at the election of the member to be notified in writing to

the trustees, be entitled to the alternative benefit provided for in

sub-rule (B) hereof which benefit shall be payable in lieu of and to

the exclusion of all other benefits payable pursuant to the Deed

(other than under Clause 16(3)(ii) of the Deed) or these rules.

16. Mr Durack submitted further that that rule clearly supports his submission that the first payment was made in lieu of benefits to which the applicant may have become entitled at the termination time or a later time. I agree. However, it does not automatically follow that it is appropriate to regard all of the $37,778.30 excess over the $13,358.26 resignation benefit as having been made in lieu of superannuation benefits to which the taxpayer may have become entitled. Effectively, the respondent's submission is that the words "may have become entitled" should be construed widely to mean "might possibly have become entitled". Such an interpretation would have the effect that the amount of the ETP bona fide redundancy payment would simply be viewed against the amount of possible future benefits (which in practical terms would invariably be in excess of the amount of the ETP), with the result that the excess of the amount of the ETP over the resignation benefit would always be regarded as the "forgone benefit part" for the purposes of sec 27F(2). This interpretation would have the consequence of leaving sec 27F(1) with limited operation, as no amount paid from a superannuation fund would ever receive concessional treatment as "a bona fide redundancy payment" pursuant to sec 27C(2).

17. In this state of affairs, Mr Slater invited me to have recourse to the Explanatory Memorandum in relation to the Bill which became the abovementioned Act and, which he submitted, supported his interpretation of the section.

18. I am not satisfied that the ordinary meaning conveyed by the text of the section supports the interpretation sought to be given to it by Mr Slater. However, having said that, the literal interpretation contended for by Mr Durack gives rise to a result which is both manifestly absurd and unreasonable.

19. It is this kind of vacuum which in my view jusitifies, for calculation purposes, recourse to the Explanatory Memorandum as sanctioned by sec 15AB(1)(b)(ii) of the Acts Interpretation Act. The Memorandum states:

(This) amendment will enable the current value of retirement

benefits forgone - for example, a superannuation pension on

retirement - to be taken into account in determining, for the

purposes of the early retirement and redundancy payment concessions,

the amount that would have been received on voluntary termination of

employment. (my italics)

20. Both parties, in the alternative, made the same submission based upon the passage of the Explanatory Memorandum quoted above (and in the respondent's case also in reliance on ITR 2620), that is, that the "current value of retirement benefits forgone" referred to in that Memorandum is the present value of the applicant's accrued benefit (as generally understood in the superannuation industry) and which fits the description of the "benefits to which the taxpayer "may have become entitled at the termination time or a later time" in sec 27F(2). Further, it was submitted that "the forgone benefit part" would thus be that present value less the resignation benefit. The reason for this reduction, it was submitted, was that the effect of sec 27F(2)(b) was to prevent the amount of the resignation benefit from being included in the forgone benefit part since it was already included in the termination amount. I agree with this submission, which receives clear support from the quoted passage from the Explanatory Memorandum and is consistent with the approach taken in the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations. Further, I consider the submission as to the effect of sec 27F(2)(b) is sound. In the absence of clear wording to the effect that Parliament intended to include the amount of the resignation benefit twice, a result which would appear to be inherently absurd and unreasonable, one must presume that the section in question is to be interpreted to avoid such a result.

21. The applicant called Mr M.A. Stevenson, the actuary who advised the fund over some 14 years up until the termination of the fund. I found Mr Stevenson to be an extremely impressive witness who gave what appeared to be an accurate and impartial account of actuarial matters affecting the fund. It was his evidence, based upon what I might add were complicated calculations which he painstakingly worked through for the Tribunal, that the present value of the applicant's accrued benefit (for which he considered it was appropriate to use a 1% discount factor and a commutation factor of 10 in relation to the applicant's potential pension) was $40,431.60. I have no hesitation in accepting his evidence, particularly in this instance where the respondent chose not to adduce his own actuarial evidence, hoping like Mr Micawber, that something might turn up in cross-examination (which it didn't).

22. I am satisfied therefore, that that amount, reduced by the amount of the resignation benefit, is the "forgone benefit part" pursuant to sec 27F(2) which is to be included in the "termination amount" in sec 27F(1). The result - when the "forgone benefit part" is added to the amount of the resignation benefit (already part of the "termination amount" pursuant to sec 27F(1)) - is a "termination amount" of $40,431.60. The amount of $10,704.96 is thus the difference between the amount of the ETP and the "termination amount", and is a bona fide redundancy payment in relation to the applicant entitled to 5% concessional treatment pursuant to sec 27C(2).

The second payment

23. Although not expressly stated in the Agreed Facts, I take the effect of paragraphs 51 and 52, and the fact that no submission to the contrary was put to me, that both parties accept that the second payment is an ETP as defined in sec 27A of the Act. Indeed, it is difficult to come to any other conclusion. The issue is to what amount, if any, of the second payment constitutes a "bona fide redundancy payment" in accordance with sec 27F, thereby coming within the definition of "concessional component" in sec 27A(1) attracting concessional treatment (with only 5% of such amount being included as assessable income) accorded by sec 27C(2).

24. Both parties are prepared to accept that sec 27F(1)(a), (b), (c) and (d) are satisfied in relation to the second payment (see paragraphs 25 - 28 and 51 of the Agreed Facts).

25. Further, the parties agree that the amount of the second payment is in excess of the amount that could reasonably be expected to have been made in relation to the applicant had he voluntarily retired from his employment with the company at the termination time (see paragraph 52 of the Agreed Facts). Therefore the only real issue is whether, pursuant to sec 27F(2), the amount of the second payment is a "forgone benefit part" so as to deny concessional treatment to that amount under sec 27C(2).

26. As identified by paragraph 46 and 51 of the Agreed Facts, the second payment was made to the applicant pursuant to clause 16(3)(ii) of the fund trust deed which provided:

If the fund should provide (sic) to be more than sufficient to

provide in full the benefits under Clause 17 the surplus shall be

used firstly, to the extent such surplus is available and to the

extent it is necessary to increase the benefits under Clause 17 in

accordance with increases in costs of living since the termination

date, such increase being made in a manner which in the opinion of

the trustees having regard to the advice of an actuary is fair and

equitable; secondly, to increase all benefits under Clause 17

proportionately or in such other manner as the trustees on the

advice of an actuary may determine to be fair and equitable,

thirdly, to pay to such of the members or former members of the fund

who have left employment on or after 1st July, 1986 at or after

normal retirement date or who have left employment by reason of bona

fide redundancy as defined in sub-rule (E) of Rule 11A benefits in

amount which in the opinion of the trustees having regard to the

advice of an actuary are fair and equitable and, fourthly, to pay

the balance of any surplus remaining after providing for the above

benefits to the company.

27. That sub-clause was one of those amended to give effect to the agreement referred to in paragraphs 17 - 19 of the Agreed Facts. Prior to those amendments that sub-clause read:

if the Fund should prove to be more than sufficient to provide in

full the benefits under Clause 17 the surplus shall first be used,

to the extent such surplus is available and to the extent it is

necessary, to increase the benefits under Clause 17 in accordance

with increases in costs of living since the Termination Date, such

increase being made in a manner which in the opinion of the Trustees

having regard to the advice of an Actuary is fair and equitable.

Any remaining surplus shall be repaid to the Company upon request

and to such extent as may be requested and any surplus thereafter

remaining in the Fund shall be applied by the Trustees to increasing

all the benefits under Clause 17 proportionately or in such other

manner as the Trustees on the advice of an Actuary may determine to

be fair and equitable.

28. Effectively, that amendment, from the point of view of the company's employees would appear to have been a more effective provision to hold the company to its agreement (see paragraph 19 of the Agreed Facts) not to make a request under clause 16(3)(ii) (as it was before the amendment) for a return of any surplus existing in the fund after provision of all benefits. Mr Stevenson's evidence was to the effect that the rationale behind the provision for a further payment based upon actuarial advice to members of the fund who left the company's employment post 30 June 1986 either on or after normal retirement date, or by reason of bona fide redundancy as defined in sub-rule (e) of rule 11A, was that the company needed to retain employees during the winding down process, and therefore wished to recognise those employees who had not resigned en masse, but had stayed with the company up to their normal retirement date or later, or who had not ceased employment prior to their position becoming redundant.

29. Paragraphs 48 and 49 of the Agreed Facts (after confirmation in evidence by Mr Stevenson) can be taken to set out effectively how the amount of the applicant's second payment was calculated.

30. Mr Slater submitted that the second payment was simply the applicant's proportion of the distribution of surplus remaining in the fund after payment of all benefits provided for under the fund's trust deed (including the benefit already paid to the applicant, that is, the first payment), and therefore could not be said to have been made in lieu of superannuation benefits to which he may have become entitled at the termination time or a later time.

31. The respondent's submission was that:

(a) whatever was in the fund was effectively equity which

belonged to the members of the fund at the time;

(b) if everything in the fund was paid out to them, then

in the absence of evidence to the contrary, it could only be

assumed that it was paid out to them pursuant to and in

accordance with the purpose of the fund;

(c) clause 2 of the fund deed expressed the main purpose

of the fund to be the provision of benefits, pensions and

allowances for members and their dependants, a purpose which

was the provision of what was extremely close to the

definition of "superannuation benefits" in sec 6(1) of the

Act.

32. From this it was said to follow that whatever was paid to those members out of the fund constituted superannuation benefits, or at worst, payments in lieu of superannuation benefits.

33. Doing the best I can with this convoluted piece of legislation, I am satisfied that the first payment, to the extent of the present value of the applicant's accrued benefit, was made in lieu of superannuation benefits to which the applicant may have become entitled and that the second payment was simply a proportion of the surplus in the fund after such benefits had been paid. The respondent's argument, although lacking little in ingenuity, amounts to a submission that if a cat has kittens in an oven they must be cakes. Clause 2 expressed the main function of the fund to be the provision of superannuation benefits, not the sole function. I consider that the fact that clause 16(3)(ii) of the fund deed provided that any surplus remaining in the fund (albeit after payment of all benefits, including the types of payments in question and the fund's expenses) was to be paid to the company further illustrates that all payments made out of the fund are not necessarily payments of superannuation benefits or payments in lieu of such benefits.

34. It follows that no part of the second payment is a "forgone benefit part" under sec 27F(2) and therefore, all of that payment is a "bona fide redundancy payment" in accordance with sec 27F(1), thus coming within the definition of "concessional component" in sec 27A(1) so as to attract the concessional treatment (with only 5% of such amount being included as assessable income) accorded by sec 27C(2).

35. It was agreed that the Tribunal need not be troubled with re-calculating the assessments in question, as there are various complex calculations resulting from the my findings on the issues as raised. Therefore, as the parties have requested, I remit the matter to the respondent to deal with those calculations in accordance with my decision.

36. Liberty to Apply.
