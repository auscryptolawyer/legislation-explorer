Last Updated: 29 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation Appeals - dependant spouse rebate - dependant imprisoned for part of year of income - whether Director of Prisons a person contributing to maintenance of dependant amount of rebate reasonable in circumstances - whether full and true disclosure of all material facts made by taxpayer in giving partial name of prison

Words and phrases: "person"

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 6, 159J, 170, Division 17

Acts Interpretation Act 1901 s 22

Prisons Act 1981 (WA) ss 4, 6, 16, 17

The Scottish Australian Mining Company Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1950] HCA 16; (1950) 81 CLR 188

Australasian Jam Co. Pty. Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1953] HCA 52; 10 ATD 217

REASONS FOR DECISION

16 October 1987 Deputy President R.D. Nicholson

Objection Decision Under Review

The Applicant seeks review of an objection decision allowing in part only an objection dated 17 February 1985 to an amended assessment notice which was issued on 12 February 1985 in respect of income derived during the year ended 30 June 1984. The objection was that the Applicant was entitled as a supporting spouse to claim a spouse rebate of $830 as a supporting spouse and that the Commissioner was not entitled to amend the assessment to disallow a claim for that amount. In his objection decision the Commissioner allowed the claim for a spouse rebate in respect of $254 for the period 1 July 1983 to 20 October 1983, that is the period in the year of income prior to the Applicant's husband being committed to prison.

Relevant statutory provisions

During the period in question the immediately relevant provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") read as follows:

"159J. (1) Where, during the year of income, a taxpayer contributes to the maintenance of a person (in this section referred to as a "dependant") specified in column 2 of the table set out in sub-section (2) and that person is a resident, the taxpayer is entitled, in his assessment in respect of income of that year of income, to a rebate of tax ascertained in accordance with this section.

159J. (2) Subject to this section, the amount of the rebate allowable in the assessment of the taxpayer in respect of a dependant under this section is the relevant amount specified in column 3 of the following table:-

Column 1 Column 2 Column 3

Class Dependant Amounts of Rebate

1 Spouse of the taxpayer $830

...

...

159J. (3) Where -

(a) the taxpayer contributes to the maintenance of a dependant during part only of the year of income;

(aa) ...

(b) during the whole or part of the year of income, 2 or more persons contribute to the maintenance of a person who is a dependant in relation to 1 or more of the persons so contributing;

(c) ...
  • (d) ...
  • (e) ...

the rebate allowable to the taxpayer in respect of that dependant shall be such part of the relevant amount specified in column 3 of that table as, in the opinion of the Commissioner, is reasonable in the circumstances."

The Respondent's reasons for disallowing the taxpayer's claims were that it was not reasonable to allow any greater rebate than $254 and that the amended assessment was authorised by sub-section 170(2) or sub-section 170(3) of the Act.

Applicant's evidence

The Applicant and her husband were married on 29 May 1982. During the period in which her husband was imprisoned in the year of income, the Applicant's evidence was that she purchased for him certain clothes (shirts, socks, underwear, trousers, handkerchiefs and footwear, including replacement sizes as he lost weight); a radio; tapes for use in a pre-owned tape recorder; certain toiletries (shampoos, soap, conditioner and deodorant); stationery (2 or 3 pads a month); stamps (costing $4 to $5 per week and used in connection with correspondence relating to legal proceedings). She also paid to his account at the Prison an amount of approximately $110. She kept no receipts in respect of these expenditures.

The house occupied by the Applicant and her husband prior to his imprisonment was owned by her. Her husband paid mortgage and maintenance charges. She assumed responsibility for these from the time of his imprisonment.

In claiming the spouse rebate in respect of her husband in her return for the 1984 year of income, the Applicant gave his place of residence as "Bartons Mill". The column in which the address appeared bore the instruction that if the spouse was not residing with the tax payer, the taxpayer was required to "state full address of dependant".

Application of section 159J.

The taxpayer succeeds in establishing that she contributed to the maintenance of her spouse and that pursuant to sub-section 159J (1) she is entitled to a rebate in accordance with section 159J. The amount of the rebate is specified in sub-section 159J (2) which is to be read "subject to this section". This means that sub-section 159J (2) must be read subject to sub-section 159J (3). It is not the case, as submitted on behalf of the Applicant, that she is entitled to a full rebate merely as a consequence of satisfying the requirements of sub-section 159J(1).

Pursuant to sub-section 159J (2) the Applicant would be entitled to the full rebate of $830. The only question is whether that sub-section is qualified by application of sub-section 159JO) so that a partial rebate is payable in an amount being reasonable in the circumstances.

The Respondent having conceded the entitlement to rebate up to the date of imprisonment it was not argued on his behalf that paragraph (a) of sub-section 159J (3) has any application, evidence having been given that the taxpayer did contribute to the maintenance of her husband during the remainder of the year, that is during his period of imprisonment.

On behalf of the Respondent it was argued that paragraph 159J (3)(b) applied to justify a partial rebate. On behalf of the Respondent it is said the Prisons Department as well as the Applicant contributed to the maintenance of her dependant husband. At the hearing it was argued that this involved consideration of whether the word "persons" in paragraph 159J (3)(b) includes the Prisons Department.

The word "person" is not defined in section 159J nor in Division 17 in which it appears. Sub-section 6(1) of the Act defines the word, subject to appearance of contrary intention, to include a company. The word "company" is defined in the same sub-section to include all bodies or associations corporate or unincorporate but not to include partnerships.

Section 22 of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 provides that in any Act, unless the contrary intention appears, "person" includes a body politic or corporate as well as an individual. At the hearing it was contended that the question for decision was therefore whether the provisions in sub-section 6(1) of the Act amount to a contrary intention so that the Prisons Department comes within the description "body politic".

The Prisons Act 1981 (WA) provides in section 7 that subject to that Act and to the control of the Minister, the Director of the Western Australia Prisons Department appointed pursuant to section 6 of that Act is responsible for the management, control and security of all prisons and the welfare of all prisoners. By operation of section 4 of the same Act the place at which the Applicant's husband was incarcerated is "Barton's Mill Prison". By operation of section 17 of that Act, a prisoner who is in prison is taken to be serving his sentence. Sub-section 16(1) of the Act provides that every prisoner is deemed for so long as he continues to be a prisoner to be in the custody of the Director. Therefore, the legal position is that the Applicant's husband during the relevant year of the period of income under consideration was in the custody of the Director and pursuant to a statutory duty that Director was responsible for his welfare. The Director comes within the primary meaning of the word "person" and therefore, together with the Applicant, satisfies the requirements for "2 or more persons" for the purposes of paragraph 159J(3)(b) of the Act. It is not necessary to consider whether or not the Prisons Department is a "body politic" or whether the word "person" in that paragraph should be given a meaning extended beyond that of a natural person because the Director, being a natural person, satisfies the requirements of the paragraph together with the Applicant's wife. In the opinion of the Tribunal the Director, having responsibility for the welfare of the prisoner, must be taken to have contributed to the maintenance of that person for the purposes of paragraph 159J(3)(b) of the Act.

For the Applicant it was argued that the Director could not qualify as a "person" in paragraph 159J(3)(b) of the Act because he was not a person who could make a claim for a spouse rebate and the paragraph was limited in its application to that situation. In the opinion of the Tribunal that view is not open, given the explicit requirements of the paragraph which require a relationship of dependancy in relation to "1 or more" of the "2 or more persons" contributing to maintenance - such a relationship with one is enough to make the paragraph applicable.

The consequence is that the rebate allowable to the taxpayer in respect of her dependant husband is that part of $830 as, in the opinion of this Tribunal standing in the place of the Commissioner, is reasonable in the circumstances. The Commissioner formed the view that the total amount should be apportioned so that the rebate allowable to the taxpayer was in respect of the period of the year of income which her dependant was not in prison. He did so without evidence being available to him of the amount actually expended by the Applicant on maintenance of her husband during his period of imprisonment. It is apparent she expended money on clothing and other personal items as well as making the cash contribution of $110 or thereabouts. In the view of the Tribunal it is reasonable these be taken into account now that evidence has been given of them. The view of the Tribunal is therefore that a rebate of $500 (including the $254 previously allowed) would be reasonable in the circumstances.

Power to amend assessment

For the taxpayer it is argued that neither sub-section 170(2) or (3) of the Act empowers the Respondent to amend the Applicant's assessment because it is not the case that she failed to make a full and true disclosure of all material facts. On behalf of the Respondent it is said that the reference to "Bartons Mill" in the return falls short of the requirement to make disclosure of that nature in the following respects. Firstly, it is said that it suggests that the Applicant's dependant is resident at "Bartons Mill" for the whole of the year of income. That submission overlooks the requirement placed on the taxpayer by the note on the return requiring her to complete the place of residence of the dependant which by implication must be that at the date of completion of the return. Secondly, on behalf of the Respondent it is said the description "Bartons Mill" does not give the geographical location of the prison or if it is given as a. description of the prison, it is not a statement of the full address, the correct name being "Barton's Mill Prison". The Respondent argued that if the prison had been properly identified the assessor would have been alerted by that material fact to make further enquiry in relation to eligibility for the spouse rebate. Evidence did not establish whether the words "Bartons Mill" are understood in the community to be the same as "Barton's Mill Prison". It was said the Respondent had written to the Applicant's husband at "Bartons Mill" but no proof of that was given to the Tribunal.

Bearing in mind the nature of the requirements of the relevant words in sub-sections 170(2) and (3) of the Act as referred to in The Scottish Australian Mining Company Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1950] HCA 16; (1950) 81 CLR 188 at 198 and Australasian Jam Co. Ptv. Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1953] HCA 52; 10 ATD 217 at 222, the Tribunal is satisfied that full and true disclosure of all material facts necessary to the assessment was not made by the taxpayer and that accordingly the Respondent was not lacking power for that reason to act pursuant to those sub-sections in amending the assessment.

Conclusion

For the reasons given the Tribunal will vary the objection decision under review so that the Applicant is entitled to a spouse rebate of $500 in the 1984 year of income.
