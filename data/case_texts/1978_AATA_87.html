Rodgers and Tax Agents' Board of Western Australia [1978] AATA 87 (20 December 1978)

Last Updated: 14 March 2007





Tax Agent - Permanently ceasing to carry on business as Tax Agent - Purposes of registration of tax agents. Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 X.251J, s. 251K.





Kim Anthony Rodgers



Tax Agents' Board Of Western Australia



AAT Decision No 127

No. 78/18006

Smithers J.

20 December 1978

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No. 78/18006

RE: KIM ANTHONY RODGERS

AND: TAX AGENTS'BOARD OF WESTERN AUSTRALIA

DECISION

The Tribunal decides to review the decision of the Tax Agents' Board of Western Australia dated 19 September 1978, orders that the decision be set aside, that the applicant's registration as a tax agent No. 32612 be restored and not be cancelled on the ground of his having permanently ceased to carry on business as a tax agent within the meaning of S.251K(3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936.

Dated the 20th day of December 1978

REASONS FOR DECISION

This is an application for review by this Tribunal of a decision made by the Tax Agents 'Board of Western Australia (the Board) on 19 September 1978 pursuant to s.251K(3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Act), that the registration of the applicant as a. tax agent, originally granted in February 1974 and renewed annually thereafter be cancelled on the ground that the applicant had permanently ceased to carry on business as a tax agent. The applicant was advised of this decision by letter dated 5 October 1978.

On the material before it the Board drew the not unreasonable inference that as the applicant did not appear to intend lodging any returns in the future he had permanently ceased to carry on business as a registered tax agent. But there is further material before this Tribunal, which the Board did not have the benefit of considering when reaching its decision.

The applicant was registered as a tax agent pursuant to S.251J of the Act. It is not a condition of registration under that section that the applicant for registration should at the time of registration actually be carrying on business as a tax agent. Indeed it could hardly be. The prescribed conditions are in substance, that he be a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of tax payers in income tax matters and that he be a person of good character. Section 251K (2) provides for cancellation of the registration of a tax agent where the Board is satisfied that inter alia there has been neglect or misconduct in certain respects on the part of a registered agent. Section 251K(3) provides that the Board shall cancel the registration of an individual as a tax agent upon his death or bankruptcy, or his permanently ceasing to carry on business as a tax agent.

The provisions in s.251K(3) are not apt literally to refer to the case of a person who has never commenced to carry on business. However, it would be more than strange that such a person could remain as a registered tax agent forever if the situation were that he intended never to carry on such a business It would seem therefore that if the provisions of s.251K(3) are capable according to the proper principles of construction of extending to the case of a registered tax agent who has never carried on business as a tax agent and never intends to, that construction should be adopted.

Having regard to the purposes of registration and the general intention to be perceived in the express provisions of the Act concerning the registration of tax agents I think the sub-section is properly to be construed as applicable not only to registered tax agents who have commenced to carry on business as tax agents and then ceased to do so, but also to those who have never commenced so to carry on business. Where, in either case, the circumstances at any particular time are that business is not being carried on, the question arises whether the registered agent has permanently ceased to carry on business. In the case of the registered agent who has never commenced business the substance of the enquiry is the same as in the case of the agent who has commenced business but who has ceased to carry it on.

The nature of that enquiry may be expressed in the words used by Jenkins J. in In re Coxen, McCallum v. Coxen [1948] Ch 747 at 760-761. The question before Jenkins J. involved the interpretation of a codicil which provided that certain dispositions should take effect after the death of the testator's wife, or "if in the opinion of my trustees, she shall have ceased permanently to reside" at certain premises. His Lordship said:

. "It remains to consider what is meant by the word 'permanently'. This occasions some difficulty as the word is so placed as to be grammatically capable of qualifying either 'ceased' (so as to give the meaning 'permanently ceased to reside') or 'reside"' (so as to give the meaning 'ceased to reside permanently', or, splitting the infinitive, 'ceased to permanently reside'). I think it is reasonably plain that the former (that is 'permanently ceased to reside') is what is meant. Therefore, the condition is, as a matter of definition, to operate in the double event of (a) Lady Coxen ceasing personal residence in the house, and (b) such cesser being permanent. The latter event involves intention. Lady Coxen must have given up all intention of ever again resuming personal residence in the house, or in other words, must have no 'animus revertendi'. To put it objectively, she must have given up personal residence once and for all."

"Permanently" is a word the meaning of which will respond to the context. In my opinion in its setting in s 251K(3) the meaning adopted by Jenkins J. is completely satisfying.

On reference to the precise terms of s 251K(3) one is immediately struck by the feature that registration must be cancelled on death or bankruptcy. In both of these cases the purpose of registration is frustrated, in one case forever and in the other by a commercial event effectuating, at least for an indefinite period, a complete frustration of the purpose of registration. It would appear a sound view that the same notion should be seen to underlie the third criterion by reference to which registration is, according to the section, to be cancelled. That notion is that registration should be cancelled if the contemplated business for which registration was granted has come to an end in a permanent way, or if the contemplated business has never eventuated and there is such lack of intention to commence business that it is to be inferred that the business which was contemplated and the contemplation of which was the foundation of the application for registration has been abandoned. In either event the purpose of registration is fundamentally frustrated. When that situation arises, then, by virtue of the section, the registration must be cancelled but only then. The mandatory nature of the statutory provision is apparent from the decision of the President of the Tribunal, Brennan J., in Re Adams and the Tax Agents ! Board (1976) 12 A.L.R. 239.

In a case like the present the ascertainment of the necessary facts by the Board is not easily achieved. For practical reasons the Board normally does not, and possibly cannot examine and cross-examine the holder of the registration in person. Before this Tribunal by the evidentiary processes available to it the proper conclusion appears to be that the applicant has certainly not given up all intention of engaging in business as a tax agent, he has not given up the intention once and for all of engaging in such business. His activities to this date have been minimal but it cannot be said that before 19 September 1978 when the decision was taken to cancel his registration, that there had not been some slight stirring. He is and has been employed by a well known firm of public accountants, but has the right of private practice. That there has not been more action on his part in engaging in business as a tax agent has been due partly to the circumstance that his current employment rather limited the extent to which in accordance with the by-laws of the Institute of Chartered Accountants c Australia he could so engage. He was thereby limited to earning $500 per year as a tax agent on his own account. Inactivity was also due to the lack of appreciation by the applicant that his registration might be in danger if he failed to conduct business with some reality, and to the circumstance that the state of his income from his current employment did -not make it necessary for him to be more active in the matter. However, I belie that the applicant has always intended to operate as a tax agent on a greater or lesser scale, according to circumstances. It cannot be said that the business which was in contemplation when he sought registration, namely, one to be commenced thereafter at some indefinite time, had been abandoned as at last September. Indeed he had made some slight efforts to acquire clients, albeit with little success. He had actually submitted only one return on behalf of one client whose income was not taxable and in respect of which only a modest fee was received by him. Nevertheless the intention to do business for which he needed the registration was always present. Although the time when real vigour would be applied remained uncertain, I do not consider it is shown that as at last September, there had been in recent times no current activity in the business which was contemplated, when registration was sought, or that the degree of intention to apply real vigour to the development of that business was so minimal or remote that an abandonment of intention to apply such vigour was to be inferred. When asked what in last September his answer would have been to the question when was he going to develop a tax agent's business, he replied:

"Your Honour, my answer would have been that I am doing it now. This Is back in September. I was cultivating the contacts - the relationships - I had with people and I was doing it at that time and am still doing it."

This answer puts upon his past activities the best complexion from his point of view, but I am satisfied that as an expression of a continuing intention to engage in the business of a tax agent to a material extent it is to be accepted.

It is only where the business has permanently ceased to be carried on, in the sense discussed above, that the registration must, or indeed under s.251K(3) can, be cancelled. It is only then that the purpose of registration can be said to have been frustrated.

Some assistance in relation to the kind of problem now before this Tribunal is to be found in the remarks of Cotton L.J. in In re Middleborough Assembly Rooms Company (1880) 14 Ch. D. 104 . In that case a petition to wind up a company on the ground that it had suspended business for more than a year and that it was just and equitable that it be wound up had been granted. In 1876 the company had ceased taking steps to carry on its undertaking and had suspended business for more than a year. In May 1879 the Directors laid the position of the Company before the shareholders, who resolved to leave the whole matter in the hands of the directors. In those circumstances the Court of Appeal set aside the order for winding up which had been made on the ground that "not one single thing had been done in performance of the purposes for which the company was incorporated". Lord Justice Cotton said at p.111:-

"If the petitioner could satisfy the Court that the business of the Company had been suspended on account of inability to carry it on, or by reason of an intention to abandon the undertaking the case would be very different. It is true there is no evidence that the Company will proceed with their building within any given time, but I cannot look upon the delay as arising from anything but a wish to wait for a time when business can be carried on more profitably than at present."

Compare per Chitty J, in In Re Tomlin Patent Horseshoe Coy Ltd. (1886) 55 LT 314 and s 222(1)(c) of the Companies Act 1961 (Vic.).

Accordingly it is the view of this Tribunal that the. decision of the Board be reviewed and that that decision be set aside and that the applicant's registration as a Tax Agent No. 32612 be restored and not be cancelled pursuant to s.251K(3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 on the ground that he has permanently ceased to carry on business as a tax agent.

It will be apparent from the above that in the application of this statutory provision, the criterion by reference to which cancellation of the registration on the ground mentioned above is required, namely the existence of the permanent cessation of carrying on business, always involves a question of fact. Accordingly n formula can be devised as a test for all cases. Guidelines may well be devised which would assist in the determination of the question of fact in many cases, but in every case the ultimate conclusion depends upon a correct assessment, individually made in the particular case of the relevant facts in the light of the principles discussed above.
