"AA" and Commissioner for Superannuation [1985] AATA 525 (6 November 1985)

Last Updated: 19 February 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

SUPERANNUATION - jurisdiction of Tribunal - communication of information relating to calculation of entitlements following Tribunal's decisions - whether constitutes a reviewable decision in relation to matters other than the calculation of entitlements whether enables a challenge to existence of benefit classification certificate before subsequent Tribunal - functus officio - whether decisions under s66(2) of Superannuation Act separate decisions.

Superannuation Act 1976, s66(2), 154

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s3

Re : "AA"

And : COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION No A85/37

AAT Decision No 2399

Tribunal : Mr Ewart Smith (Senior Member)

Dr DJ Howell (Member)

Mr DRS Craik (Member)

Date : 6 November 1985

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No A85/37 GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: "AA"

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Mr Ewart Smith (Senior Member)

Dr DJ Howell (Member)

Mr DRS Craik (Member)

Date : 6 November 1985

Place : Canberra

  1. In this matter, the applicant has sought review of what are described respectively as decisions of the delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation made on 22 February 1985 -

to confirm the decision notified to the applicant in a letter dated 28th May, 1984 to the effect that the applicant's entitlement to invalidity benefit under the Superannuation Act 1976 continued to be determined in accordance with s66(2) of the Act;

to confirm the decision notified to the applicant in a letter dated 28th May, 1984 that the applicant's entitlement to invalidity pension had been assessed having regard to his annual rate of salary on retirement being a salary which did not include an allowance of a prescribed kind.

  1. For the reasons set out in the accompanying Reasons for Decision, the Tribunal decides that it does not have jurisdiction to review the first matter referred to in paragraph 1 above.

EWART SMITH

Presiding Member



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No A85/37

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: "AA"

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The applicant has sought review by this Tribunal of what he has described as decisions of the delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the Commissioner") made on 22 February 1985-

”to confirm the decision notified to the applicant in a letter dated 28th May, 1984 to the effect that the applicant's entitlement to invalidity benefit under the Superannuation Act 1976 continued to be determined in accordance with s66(2) of the Act;

to confirm the decision notified to the applicant in a letter dated 28th May, 1984 that the applicant's entitlement to invalidity pension had been assessed having regard to his annual rate of salary on retirement being a salary which did not include an allowance of a prescribed kind."

2. The second decision referred to above is not an issue for present purposes and, at the request of both parties, further argument on that matter has been deferred and will be the subject of subsequent proceedings. However, the request for review of the first '"decision" raises a threshold question which the parties wished to have determined before proceeding to call evidence. The threshold question is whether this Tribunal has jurisdiction to deal with that matter.

3. In order to understand the present application it is necessary to refer, briefly, to the history of the superannuation questions that have arisen in relation to the applicant. We take as a starting point the proceedings in applications Nos A83/56 and A83/75 before this Tribunal, decisions in which were given on 1 May 1984 by Deputy President Hall, Mr Prowse and Dr Garlick. The first application (A83/56) related to a decision made on 16 May 1983 refusing to direct that the election which the applicant made on 31 January 1978 for lump sum benefit in lieu of a pension be cancelled under s157 of the 1976 Act. The second application (A83/75) related to a decision made on 17 June 1983 refusing to extend the prescribed period in which the applicant could request reconsideration of decisions taken by the Commissioner under ss 184(2) and 66(2) of the 1976 Act on 23 June 1977 and 25 January 1978 respectively. As a consequence of those decisions, the applicant's benefit entitlements (whether for a pension or a lump sum payment) were more limited than they might otherwise have been.

4. The decisions in those proceedings were:

(1) to set aside the Commissioner's decision not to direct that the applicant's election for a lump sum in lieu of a pension be cancelled and to direct that the election be cancelled (A83/56); and

(2) to affirm the decisions of the Commissioner not to extend the time in which the applicant may request a reconsideration by the Commissioner of the decisions taken on 23 June 1977 and 25 January 1978 respectively (a) to issue a Benefit Classification Certificate ("BCC") specifying a history of nervous disorders and (b) deciding that the condition causing invalidity retirement was substantially contributed to by. The certified decision (A83/75).

5. The applicant's solicitor stated that he was not wishing to canvass the decision of the Tribunal in proceedings A83/75, but sought to re-open the decision by attacking the legal validity of the BCC. For there to be a valid decision under s66(2) of the 1976 Act, he said, the following condition specified in paragraph (b) of s66(2) had to be met, namely:

"(b) there was in force in respect of the person immediately before the person's retirement, a benefit classification certificate;".

He claimed that this factual basis did not exist and in fact had never existed. The basis for this submission was that at no time was the applicant a member of the Provident Account established under the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the superseded Act") and therefore s184(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the 1976 Act) did not deem a BCC to be in force. This situation arose, he claimed, because the applicant had in fact become a member of the Superannuation Fund (in the narrow sense of the term) in 1966, having been accepted as a member of the Fund and having had deductions made from his salary for that purpose and having been allocated a number in the Fund. Once a person became a contributor to the Superannuation Fund under the 1922 Act, he said, the purported decision made in April 1967 to accept him as a contributor to the Provident Account was of no legal effect. He then argued that there was no provision in the Act for removal of a contributor from the Superannuation Fund to the Provident Account and that, in purporting to so transfer the applicant, the Commissioner had acted beyond his powers.

  1. The applicant not having been a member of the Provident Account immediately before the commencement of the 1976 Act, it was argued that the provision in that Act deeming a BCC to have been in force in relation to a member of the Provident Account could not apply and the applicant's rights therefore had to be determined pursuant to s66(1) of the 1976 Act, not s66(2) as had been decided.
  2. It should be said, without going into all the details relating to the applicant's appointment, that in a letter dated 18 March 1966 sent to the applicant by the Public Service Board, (T9/1), he was advised that the "Board's medical advisers in the Department of Health had recommended that the question of a final decision on your medical fitness for permanent appointment should be deferred and that' you serve an extended provisional period". That letter also stated that it was "expected that the certification will be for Provident Account, rather than Superannuation Fund, benefits". It should be also observed that, by letter dated 14 January 1966 (T7/1), the applicant was offered permanent appointment as a research officer grade 1, Public Service Board, Canberra. It was indicated in that letter that his appointment would commence on 1 February 1966.

8. The applicant, who was then resident in the United Kingdom, had been examined by Dr Cox, a Commonwealth Medical Officer, on 29 October 1965 in London, who recommended acceptance for the Superannuation Fund. It was not until April 1967, however, that a decision was made to accept the applicant into the Provident Account (T12/1). It might also be observed that on 25 May 1971 the applicant wrote to the Superannuation Board stating the following:

I am contributing to the Provident Account at present but I would like to apply for a review with the aim of transferring to the Superannuation Fund.

I presently hold a Senior Research Officer, Grade 2 position in this Department. (My personnel No. is 66/85402R). I joined the Public Service on 1.2.66 and initially I contributed to the Superannuation Fund. However, early in 1967 as a result of a deferred medical examination I was transferred to the Provident Account and I have been contributing to it ever since."

This application was not, however, approved by the Superannuation Board (T/14).

9. We now quote paragraphs 4 and 7 of the Reasons for Decision given in respect of the decisions of this Tribunal referred to in paragraph 3 above:

"4. On 17 February 1977, following the commencement of the Superannuation Act 1976, Mr AA wrote to the Commissioner through the Inspector (Personnel) seeking the revocation of the benefit classification certificate that by virtue of s184(2) of the 1976 Act (as it then stood) was deemed to be in force in relation to him. Mr AA was no doubt aware that because he had been a contributor to the Provident Account under the superseded Act there was a benefit classification certificate that was deemed to be in force in respect of him specifying the physical or mental conditions by reason of which the Superannuation Board had decided that his health and physical fitness were not such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund established under the superseded Act. By letter 23 June 1977, which was recorded as having been sent by certified mail (Exhibit T4/34), the Commissioner replied to Mr AA's request indicating that it had been decided that the certificate should remain in force in his case. A copy of the 'certificate' was enclosed specifying 'history of nervous disorders'. Mr AA, however, had no recollection of having received this letter or the attached certificate."

"7. As earlier mentioned, it was on 27 April 1967, following a further medical examination by a Commonwealth Medical Officer on 29 March 1967, that a decision was finally taken to accept Mr AA into the Provident Account. In our view Mr AA must have realised from the outset and certainly by no later than the end of January 1978, that his acceptance into the Provident Account was primarily due to his own history of nervous disorder. He knew that he was on probation for an initial period of six months and that the probationary period-had been extended. He claims not to have been notified of the eventual decision to accept him into the Provident Account, but whether that be so or not, he could not have been unaware of the significance of his having commenced psychiatric treatment in December 1966, some three to four months before the date of his final entry medical examination on 29 March 1967."

10. The Reasons for Decision went on:

"9. On 30 August 1977, after what his psychiatrist Dr RC McDonald described as 'more sober reflection', the applicant wrote to the Commissioner for Superannuation requesting that his resignation be treated as retirement on the grounds of invalidity and that' he be granted retirement benefits accordingly.

"10. On 25 January 1978 a delegate of the Commissioner, being satisfied that at the time Mr AA ceased to be an eligible employee, he was, by reason of anxiety, depression and personality disorder, unfit to perform his duties, made the requested direction. However, pursuant to s66(2) of the 1976 Act, the delegate of the Commissioner also formed the opinion that:

'... the conditions (namely Anxiety Depression and Personality Disorder) that have been accepted as the reason for Mr AA's being deemed to have been retired on 30 June 1977 on the ground of physical or mental incapacity were substantially contributed to by the condition (namely, History of Nervous Disorders) specified in 'the Benefit Classification Certificate deemed by virtue of the operation of section 184 of the Act to have been in force on the commencing day in respect of Mr AA and which was still in force immediately before Mr AA's deemed retirement on 30 June 1977

These decisions which were made on 25 January 1978 were notified to Mr AA under cover of a letter of that date.

"11. Pausing there for a moment, it is appropriate to observe that the decisions that Mr AA now wishes the Commissioner to reconsider and which are the subject of the second application for review (A83/75) before the Tribunal are:

(i) the decision notified on 23 June 1977 refusing to revoke the benefit classification certificate deemed to be in force in respect of the applicant; and

(ii) the decision made on 25 January 1978 pursuant to s66(2) of the 1976 Act that the conditions (namely anxiety, depression and personality disorder) that had been accepted as the reason for Mr AA's being deemed to have retired on 30 June 197 7 on the ground of physical or mental incapacity were substantially contributed to by the condition (namely history of nervous disorders) specified in the benefit classification certificate deemed by virtue of the operation of s184 of the Act to have been in force on the commencing day in respect of Mr AA and which was still in force immediately before Mr AA's deemed retirement on 30 June 1977.

"12. Neither decision was challenged at the time (although, as earlier noted, Mr AA claims not to have received notice of the first decision until the end of January 1978). Pursuant to s154(2) of the 1976 Act a person affected by a reviewable decision who is dissatisfied with the decision may, by notice in writing given to the Commissioner within the period of thirty days after the day on which the decision first comes to the notice of the person or within such further period as the Commissioner allows, request the Commissioner to reconsider the decision. It was not until 8 October 1982, that Mr AA first wrote to the Commissioner requesting reconsideration of the decision made on 25 January 1978 pursuant to s66(2) of the 1976 Act. The Commissioner appears to have treated this request as extending as well to the decision taken on 23 June 1977 not to revoke the benefit classification certificate deemed to be in force in respect of the applicant. However, the Commissioner declined to extend the time in which to request reconsideration of either decision.

  1. As already noted, the Tribunal set aside the election that the applicant had made but declined to grant the extension of time to seek reconsideration by the Commissioner of the decisions relating to the BCC.
  2. Following these decisions of the Tribunal, the respondent wrote to the applicant the letter dated 28 May 1984 referring to the two decisions of the Tribunal and cribbing their consequence in the following terms:

"Decision No A83/56 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal set aside the Commissioner for Superannuation's decision not to cancel your original election to take your superannuation benefit in a lump sum following your termination of employment having been deemed to be invalidity retirement for Superannuation purposes. The effect of the cancellation of the election is, in accordance with the legislation, as if the election had not been made. This means of course that you no longer have any entitlement to the lump sum of 3.5 times your accumulated basic contributions which was previously paid to you. You are now entitled to a Superannuation benefit in the form of total pension or in the form of an annual government financed pension together with a refund of accumulated contributions.

2. As a result of the Tribunal's further decision No A83/75, your benefit entitlements are, in accordance with the relevant provisions of the Superannuation Act 1976, as follows:-

(i) in the event that you desire to take your benefit in total pension form, a pension of $6,628.55 pa with effect from 1 Jul 1977, increasing by virtue of the annual pension increases to $10,142.32 pa with effect from 24 Jun 1983, or

(ii) a government financed pension of $4,734.68 pa with effect from 1 Jul 1977, increasing by virtue of the annual pension increases to $8,248.45 pa with effect from 24 Jun 1983, together with a refund of your accumulated contributions to the Superannuation Fund amounting to $7,511.28.

"

The letter went on to deal with detailed calculations of pensions available to him and enclosed an election for him to complete for whichever form of Benefit he required. Other details relating to the payment of pension were included in the letter.

13. The applicant sought reconsideration of the decisions notified to him in that letter and, on reconsideration the delegate confirmed - "The following decisions:

1. A decision notified to AA in a letter dated 28 May 1984 to the effect that his entitlement to invalidity benefit under the Superannuation Act 1976 continued to be determined in accordance with sub-section 66(2) of the Act;

2. A decision notified to AA in a letter dated 28 May 1984 that his entitlement to invalidity pension had been assessed having regard to his annual rate of salary at retirement, being a salary that did not include an allowance of a prescribed kind." It, of course, the first "decision" that is the subject of the present proceedings.

14. Section 154 of the- 1976 Act, so far as material, provides as follows:

154. (1) In this section -

"decision" has the same meaning as in the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975;

"reviewable decision" means a decision of the Commissioner, or a delegate of the Commissioner, under this Act, under the superseded Act or under the regulations made under either of those Acts, and includes a decision of the Superannuation Board, or a delegate of the Superannuation Board (other than a decision under section 141 of the superseded Act); "Superannuation Board" means the Superannuation Board established by the superseded Act.

(2) A person affected by a reviewable decision who is dissatisfied with the decision may, by notice in writing given to the Commissioner within the period of 30 days after the day on which the decision first comes to the notice of the person, or within such further period as the Commissioner allows, request the Commissioner to reconsider the decision.

(3) There shall be set out in the request the reasons for making the request.

(4) Upon receipt of the request, the Commissioner shall reconsider the decision and may confirm or revoke the decision or vary the decision in such manner as he thinks fit.

(5) Where the Commissioner confirms, revokes or varies a decision, he shall, by notice in writing either personally or by post on the person who made the request, inform the person of the result of his reconsideration of the decision and his reasons for confirming, revoking or varying the decision, as the case may be.

(6) Applications may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of reviewable decisions that have been confirmed or varied under sub-section (4) and for review of decisions of the Superannuation Board, or delegates of the Superannuation Board, made under section 141 of the superseded Act.

15. Under that section, the function of this Tribunal is to review reviewable decisions that have been confirmed or varied under sub-section (4) by the Commissioner. It is therefore the original decision that is the subject of review by this Tribunal, but that decision must, of course, have gone through the process of reconsideration by the Commissioner [s154(4)]. What the applicant seeks to do, in effect, is to take paragraph 2 of the letter of 28 May 1984 as a further decision that his benefits are to be calculated on the basis of the existence of a BCC (which decision A83/75 required) and challenge not only the calculations, but the existence of the BCC. It is perhaps unfortunate that the first so-called "decision" - the subject of the present proceedings - purported to be a decision confirmed on reconsideration by the Commissioner's delegate. But it was a "decision" unnecessary both in point of fact and of law. The only fresh decision open to the Commissioner or his delegate to make, and to reconsider, following the Tribunal's decisions was that relating to the calculation of the applicant's benefits.

16. The applicant's request for reconsideration in relation to the matter was stated in his letter of 29 May 1984 in the following terms;

"(2) Your decision that, because of AAT decision A83/75, my entitlements are as you state (para 2).

Grounds: Your decision is not supported by the AAT decision quoted, because it was merely to do with an extension of time not another substantive issue.

Furthermore, Dr RW Cox recommended on 29-10-65 that I be accepted for the Superannuation Fund. Deductions were made from my salary at the Superannuation Fund rate as from commencement of duty on 1-2-66.

Therefore, by virtue of s5 of the Superannuation Act as it then was I must have been accepted into the Superannuation Fund on that date. Once so accepted, I could not subsequently be transferred to the Provident Account. It follows that no BCC should ever have been issued and it also follows that my benefits on retirement should not be reduced on medical grounds."

17. It can be seen that the applicant is thus seeking to have reviewed, years out of time, albeit on a different ground, the decision that, by reason of the 1976 Act, a BCC was deemed to be in force in relation to him. He had in fact accepted the validity of this BCC on two occasions. The first was when he applied for its revocation on 17 February 1977 and was refused. The second was when he was informed in January 1978 of his limited alternative entitlements following his retirement on the grounds of invalidity that was considered to have been substantially contributed to by the conditions specified in the BCC deemed to have been in force in respect of him and his subsequent acceptance of a lump sum benefit (see paragraphs 9 and 10 above). He did not seek reconsideration (as he was then entitled to) of either of these decisions.

  1. As a consequence of this Tribunal's decisions in the two matters, the applicant had no further right to call into question or to seek to attack the validity of the decision made in 1978 with respect to his limited entitlements following his retirement. The only matter for which the Commissioner had any continuing responsibility was to recalculate the applicant's entitlements based on the Tribunal's decisions and, in particular, on the cancellation of his election. That was precisely what was done and communicated to the applicant in the letter of 28 May 1984. At that point, the Commissioner - and the Tribunal - was otherwise functus officio and, the applicant having failed before the Tribunal to obtain an extension of time to challenge the BCC, the Commissioner had no power to make any other decision. If the calculations are in error, this Tribunal can of course correct them. That is in fact the basis of the second matter before this Tribunal.
  2. We see s66(2) of the 1976 Act as requiring a series of decisions to be taken and not as constituting an indivisible whole. We agree with the respondent's arguments on this point. Thus, decisions under paragraphs (a), (b) and (c) with respect to the basic entitlements to benefits can be taken (and in this case were taken) independently of paragraph (d), under which the consequential benefit entitlements of the applicant were determined. It is quite plain that the only aspect of these decisions that the Tribunal in 1984 allowed to be re-opened was the recalculation of the applicant's pension, or pension and lump sum. To hold otherwise would be to allow that all antecedent-decisions that had been accepted and acted on could be re-opened before the Tribunal whenever a reference was made to them in correspondence. We do not accept that this is the position. In short, the applicant is seeking in this case to. raise again an issue he could have raised in relation to earlier decisions (but did not), at a subsequent stage when the results of the decisions are conveyed to him. "Decision" of course is widely defined in s3 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975; but it does not, in our view, extend to a mere statement of the consequences of a decision given by this Tribunal so as to allow a re-opening of that decision on some ground other than that put to the Tribunal.

20. In view of the approach we have taken to the question, we do not find it necessary to consider specifically the authorities quoted by the applicant's solicitor. Suffice to say that we are not persuaded that any of them alter our view of the matter. We also mention we have not found it necessary to consider the arguments submitted by the respondent based on estoppel.

21. For these reasons, we find that the Tribunal has no jurisdiction to review the first matter raised by the applicant. The second matter will be considered at the first convenient date.
