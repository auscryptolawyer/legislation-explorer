Re Rosalie Anne Taxis and Secretary, Department of Social Security [1989] AATA 258 (18 December 1989)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: ROSALIE ANNE TAXIS

And: SECRETARY, DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL SECURITY

No. Q87/305

AAT No. 5555

Social Security

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

D.P. Breen(1) (Deputy President), Mrs H. Pavlin(1) (Member)

and J.D. Horrigan(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Social Security - reduction in Supporting Parents' Benefit pursuant to subsections 63(2) and 64(1) of the Social Security Act 1947 - whether lump sum payment made on behalf of applicant was "income" under subsection 6(1) of the Act.

Words and Phrases: "income", "derived".

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

Social Security Act 1947 ss. 6(1), 63(2), 64(1)

Social Security and Veterans' Affairs (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act No. 106 of 1986

Read v. Commonwealth of Australia 1988 78 ALR

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Clarke 1927 40 CLR

HEARING

BRISBANE

18:12:1989

DECISION

In this matter the decision under review was one of a delegate of the respondent whereby the rate of supporting parents' benefit paid to the applicant pursuant to subsections 63(2) and 64(1) of the Social Security Act 1947 (Cwth) (as amended) was reduced. The basis for that decision was that a sum of $5,460 paid to a finance company by the applicant's ex-husband, though it was paid in a lump sum from the proceeds of the sale of a capital asset, was nonetheless income, as that term is defined in the Act, in the hands of the applicant, the payment by the husband being on her account. The contention for the respondent was that, though the payment had the appearance of being a capital one, it was in fact in lieu of maintenance as part of the divorce settlement arrangements struck by the parties.

2. At the hearing, the applicant gave some brief evidence but substantially the facts in the case were contained in the "T" Documents which were Exhibit 1. The submissions by the respective Advocates, Mr. Stephens of Counsel for the applicant and Mrs. Green for the Department, were based upon the "T" Documents.

3. The issues can be summarised with brevity. Until October 1986, the applicant resided in the State of Victoria. Following the breakdown of her marriage the applicant, who had legal custody of the single child of the marriage, a daughter Chantelle, applied for and was granted supporting parents' benefit on and from 30th May 1985. These payments continued for approximately one year being then cancelled and reinstated on 10th July 1986. In October of that year she and her daughter shifted to Queensland.

4. Though the process of acquisition had begun prior to her leaving Victoria, it was not until her arrival in Queensland that she completed the purchase of a home unit at Maud Street, Caloundra.

5. Finance for this purchase was to be by way of loan through Defence Service loans. However, the applicant was advised that any such loan would take a year to finalise. She negotiated bridging finance from Network Finance Limited (the owner of the Maud Street home unit) but was subsequently advised that any such year-long bridging loan would require an upfront lump sum payment of interest. This lump sum payment of interest amounts to $5,460.

6. The former matrimonial home in Victoria was sold, as part of the property settlement aspect of the divorce. Mrs. Taxis received the nett proceeds of that sale, a sum of approximately $40,000, subsequently settling upon her husband 50% of the nett of that sum in terms of the settlement agreement as it then stood.

7. She found herself in the position of not having sufficient funds at her disposal to pay to Network Finance Limited the required lump sum interest of $5,460. Prompted by that shortfall, she undertook further negotiations with her former husband with a view to final settlement of the financial aspects of their separation and divorce with the result that a consent order was made in the Family Court in consequence of which the husband paid that sum to the finance company. We record the relevant paragraphs of the Family Court consent order:

"3. That the husband pay to Network Finance

Limited, Bundilla Branch, the sum of FIVE THOUSAND FOUR

HUNDRED AND SIXTY DOLLARS ($5,460.00) by bank cheque

being interest payable for a period of twelve (12)

months as and from the 26th day of September 1986 on

finance obtained by the wife from Network Finance

Limited.

4. That payment by the husband of the said sum of

FIVE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY DOLLARS ($5,460.00)

be in lieu of maintainance for the wife and child of the

marriage for a period of twelve (12) months as and from

the 26th day of September 1986 to and including the 25th

day of September 1987."

8. The case for the applicant (as initially indicated by letter dated 26th May 1987 from the applicant's Solicitors to the Regional Manager of the Department of Social Security) is that the payment of the five and a half thousand odd dollars was a re-adjustment of the nett proceeds of the sale of the matrimonial home so as to indicate a 60% division in the settlement of capital assets common in such situations where a parent with limited income earning capacity has custody of a child of the marriage and financial responsibility for that child. This correspondence, together with an earlier correspondence between the same two parties is set out below.

"16th January 1987

We act for the abovenamed who has recently appealed

against a decision of your department to reduce her

supporting parent's benefit from $270.40 per fortnight.

Our client has handed us a letter she has written to the

Appeals Tribunal which outlines accurately her

situation.

We recently acted for Mrs. Taxis in the purchase of her

home unit at Maud Street, Caloundra. The property was

financed other than borrowings from our client's share

of the net proceeds of the sale of she and her husband's

former matrimonial home situated in Victoria. Mr. Taxis

took approximately $15,000.00 of the net proceeds and

Mrs. Taxis received $25,000.00. Initially Mrs. Taxis

received approximately $20,000.00 and the balance of

$5,460.00 is the amount referred to in the agreement

between the parties set out as minutes of consent

orders, a copy of which agreement, we understand is in

your possession."

"26th May 1987

We refer to our letter of 16th January 1987, a copy of

which is enclosed.

We confirm the information contained in our letter in

that the sum of $5,460.00 received by Mrs. Taxis was

part of her share of the net proceeds of the sale of her

matrimonial home situated in Victoria.

The said sum of $5,460.00 was paid by the husband by

bank cheque payable to Network Finance Limited to assist

Mrs. Taxis in the purchase of a home unit. The funds

were not paid direct to Mrs. Taxis.

The total amount received by Mrs. Taxis was $25,460.00

which is slightly in excess of 60% of the net proceeds

of sale of the home. A 60% division of assets by way of

property settlement is common in situations such as Mrs.

Taxis' situation, where a parent with limited income

earning capacity has custody of a child and financial

responsibility for the child. In these circumtances the

sum need not be characterised as maintenance, but simply

as part of a disproportionate adjustment of property,

taking account of the different financial circumstances

of the parties.

We are instructed by Mrs. Taxis that she has been

separated from her husband since 30th May 1985 and has

not received any maintenance either for herself or the

child of the marriage since that date."

9. Conversely, the line of argument put forward by the Department was that the payment of the interest owed to Network Finance Limited was, in fact, a lump sum payment of maintenance paid on behalf of Mrs. Taxis and the daughter pursuant to the consent order. In support of this, the Department has stressed the importance and wording of the consent order processed in the Family Court.

10. This matter is to be determined pursuant to the Social Security Act 1947, prior to the amendments introduced by the Social Security and Veterans' Affairs (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act No. 106 of 1986 with the applicable sections being 6(1) (providing the definition of "income"), section 63(2) and 64(1).

11. The definition of "income" provided in subsection 6(1) is as follows:

"'income' in relation to a person means personal

earnings, monies, valuable consideration or profits

earned, derived or received by that person for the

person's own use or benefit by any means from any source

whatsoever within or without Australia and includes a

periodical payment or benefit by way of gift or

allowance but does not include - "

12. A number of specific exclusions were then mentioned by the Act, none of which are pertinent to this present matter. The rate of supporting parents' benefits and the income of the parent in question with respect to the above definition of "income" are determined by subsections 63(2) and 64(1) respectively. A simplified effect of these sections is that any ascertainable increase in the income of the supporting parent in question effects a reduction in benefits received.

13. Mr. Stephens has essentially put forward a three-pronged argument. This is, firstly, that capital payments are not included in the applicable definition of "income". Secondly, in natural furtherance of the first prong that the $5,460 lump sum payment was a capital payment and therefore not income by any definition. Finally, regardless of whether the payment is deemed to be capital or maintenance, Mrs. Taxis had not "earned, derived or received" the payment pursuant to the subsection 6(1) definition.

14. In considering the parties submissions, for and against those propositions, we find that we are unable to go past the reasons for decision of the majority (consisting of Mason C.J., Deane and Gaudron JJ.) and Brennan J. (dissenting) in the High Court of Australia in the recent decision of Read v. Commonwealth of Australia (1988) 78 ALR. In that decision, Their Honours, in determining the status of units in a capital growth trust were called on to determine whether payments of a capital nature came within the definition of "income" defined as it was at the relevant time by Section 18 of the Social Security Act 1947. The Section 18 definition was the precursor to the definition we are concerned with (subsection 6(1)), and was in identical terms.

15. The majority said at page 660 of Volume 78 of the Australian Law Reports:

"We are prepared to assume for present purposes that the

words 'profits earned, derived or received ... by any

means from any source whatsoever' as used in s 18 of the

Act, as it stood in 1984, are wide enough to include

capital profits...."

16. However, the point is put more succinctly by Brennan J. at page 662 in his dissenting judgment where he remarks:

"The definition is exhaustive: the term 'income' means

what it is defined to mean; it does not mean what

'income' would be understood to mean if the definition

were not in the Act. The definition is couched in the

widest terms, presumably to ensure that public

expenditure is directed to those who stand in actual

need of the periodic support which income-related

pensions provide. The definition is wide enough to

embrace receipts of a capital nature as well as receipts

of income, for 'income' is defined to mean, inter alia,

any moneys, valuable consideration or profits

irrespective of the means by which or the source from

which those moneys, etc. are received."

His Honour continues further down that page when discussing the merits of construing the definition of "income" narrowly.

"Unless the definition is read down, so the argument

runs, occasional receipts of capital might work an

unintended destruction of pension entitlements. Thus it

was suggested that it could not have been intended to

include within the definition of 'income' a profit

realised on the sale of a pensioner's home. The

difficulty is illusory."

17. From Read's case it is quite certain that the definition of "income" contained in subsection 6(1) even prior to the 1986 amendment (No. 106 of 1986) was wide enough such that payments of capital could be seen as inclusive. Therefore, the semantics of whether the payment in question is one of a capital nature or one in lieu of maintenance becomes an irrelevancy.

18. Conclusive to this case will therefore be whether or not such payment has been earned, derived or received by the applicant. Mr. Stephens' argument is to the effect that the money has clearly not been earned or received in a strict definitive sense so the only possibility is that it was derived by the applicant, Mrs. Taxis. He referred the Tribunal to the 1927 High Court case of the Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Clarke (1927) 40 CLR where the Acting Chief Justice, Sir Isaac Isaacs as he then was, said at page 261:

"'derived' only means 'obtained' or 'got' or 'acquired'.

All income is derived from something and by someone."

19. Counsel for the applicant submitted that each of those words has or contains a concept or notion of receipt. Mr. Stephens continues at page 6-7 of the transcript where he says:

"That is, that it is my submission, although it is a

narrow interpretation of the section, but it is my

submission it ought to be so narrowly interpreted and

although it may - there are general words in the

section, it is my submission that it cannot be said that

my client earned, derived, or received that capital

payment, and as a consequence it is a payment that is

outside the definition of section 6(1) of the Act."

20. However, Mr. Stephens' argument fails. We have already recorded the passage at page 662 in the dissenting judgment of Mr. Justice Brennan in Read's case, that passage, though his judgment overall was a dissenting one, being consistent with the majority judgment so far as the matters with which it dealt were concerned.

21. The payment by the former husband pursuant to the terms of the consent order was a payment, the benefit of which she derived, and a receipt of "income" within the applicable definition of that word under the legislation.

22. The decision under review is therefore affirmed.
