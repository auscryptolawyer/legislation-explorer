Re Donald Thomas Brown and Commissioner of Superannuation [1987] AATA 64 (27 February 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: DONALD THOMAS BROWN

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. W86/132

AAT No. 3494

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.D. Nicholson (Deputy President) Dr J.G. Billings (Member)

Dr P.A. Staer (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - period of contributory service - administrative formula - alternative formula proposed

Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Act 1973

Superannuation Act 1976

Becker and the Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs [1977] AATA 12; (1977) 1 ALD 158

Drake and Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs (No. 2) [1979] AATA 179; (1979) 2 ALD 634

HEARING

PERTH

27:2:1987

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

Decision Under Review

The Applicant, Mr. Brown, seeks review of a decision represented by a document T8, the effect of which is to apply to his transfer into the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme in application of subsection 128(5) of the Commonwealth Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") a formula determined and used by the Commissioner in the application of that sub-section.

Jurisdiction

2. Jurisdiction arises in this Tribunal pursuant to sub-section 154(6) of the Act. The application is within extended time allowed. The Tribunal is constituted in accordance with sub-section 154(7) of the Act.

Facts

3. The facts, as they are set out in three of the opening paragraphs of document T2 are not in dispute, that is that Mr. Brown became a contributor to the Superannuation Scheme established under the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Act 1973 ("the DFRDB Act") on 27 January 1977, on joining the Army as a captain. He was promoted to major on 27 January 1983 and was discharged from the Army on 2 December 1983 having served six years and 310 days. The Applicant then commenced employment as a science 2 (pharmacist) with the Department of Health on 5 December 1983. On 6 December 1983 he elected to preserve his rights under the scheme under the DFRDB Act.

4. The transfer value payable to him was $22,995.20 comprising an employee share of $8,282.08, and an employer share of $14,713.12. His commencing salary as a science 2 (pharmacist) was $24,396. it was calculated that the credit of additional service to which the Applicant was entitled pursuant to sub-section 128(5) of the Act was four years and 301 days. It is that calculation of which the Applicant seeks review. In making the calculation the Respondent applied a formula as follows:

"(12. In his administration of sub-section 128(5) of

the Act, the Commissioner has adopted a formula -)

A = B

_

C x .125

where A is the credit of additional service in years;

B is the amount of the employer component of the

transfer value;

C is the person's rate of salary at the end of

12 months employment (excluding general

increases)."

5. While no sworn evidence was given as to the manner in which this formula was adopted by the Commissioner, it was stated from the bar table by Mr. fisher representing the Respondent that a number of conferences involving personnel of the Commission and the Department of Finance had evolved the formula as the best method of reconciling the different interests concerned in the calculation of additional service for the purpose of the application of the sub-section on transfer. He also told the Tribunal that the sub-section has been applied in hundreds of cases and that in none of them has the Commissioner taken into account other factors pursuant to his ability to do so by the nature of the discretion in the sub-section and in particular, the authorization given to him in paragraph 128(5)(b). Mr.Fisher has been with the Respondent for a period of approximately six years, and even if these statements are taken to be only relating to that period they still have significant strength for the purposes of the decision the Tribunal is requested to make.

6. There has been made available to the Tribunal at the hearing as exhibit 1 an extract from the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Superannuation circular dated 2 August 1978 which in effect on page 3, paragraph 12(a), sets out the provisions in sub-section 128(5), but refers also to attachment A in which examples of a method of calculation are set out. It is apparent from the two examples in attachment A that a person on a high salary at the end of 12 months as required by application of the formula may have a significantly less period of additional service to be added to the period of contributory service, or included in it, than a person on a lower salary at the same date.

7. It is also apparent from that attachment that the Commissioner does exercise a discretion in a specific situation, namely where a person who has previously been a contributor to the Commonwealth scheme and elected for preservation and returns to Commonwealth employment and again becomes a contributor. That, however, is the sole example before the Tribunal of the Commissioner not invariably applying the formula. It does, however, make apparent the fact that the Commissioner does have a discretion and that a variation is possible if it lies within the law.

8. As the form entitled, "Credit of Additional Contributor Service - calculation sheet" (document T5/1) makes apparent in paragraph E, if the application of the formula results in the period of additional service exceeding the period of prior contributory service the matter must be submitted to a delegate of the Commissioner to determine the actual period of additional service to be granted. We are assured on behalf of the Respondent that any such references have not resulted in variation to application of the formula.

9. The Applicant submits that in applying the formula without variation to his situation the Commissioner has not had regard to all relevant facts and that an injustice has occurred. He contends that the only fair way to calculate an amount of prior service would be to compare the employer content over the period of prior immediate contribution with an amount which the Commonwealth would have contributed over the same period for an officer employed by the Commonwealth at the point in the salary scale (in his case, basic grade science 2). Applying this reasoning as set out in exhibit C, to his case, he calculates as appropriate by application of the formula with these requirements in mind an additional period of service of six years, 77 days contrasting with the period of four years, 301 days resulting from application of the formula in the form applied by the Commissioner.

Review of policy by a Tribunal

10. A useful summary of the powers of this Tribunal to review Ministerial and departmental policy is contained in paragraph 227 of Pearce, Commonwealth Administrative Law (1986) at pages 38 to 40. It is apparent that the Tribunal may more readily review departmental policy than it may review Ministerial policy. Nevertheless, the considerations relating to review of policy generally in both categories are similar. They were stated in particular in the decision in re Becker and the Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs [1977] AATA 12; (1977) 1 ALD 158, and in re Drake and Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs (No. 2) [1979] AATA 179; (1979) 2 ALD 634. In the latter case, Mr. Justice Brennan, who was then dealing with a case of review of Ministerial policy, made the following points:

"Inconsistency is not merely inelegant: it brings the

process of deciding into disrepute, suggesting an

arbitrariness which is incompatible with commonly

accepted notions of justice."

11. However, he also pointed out that the Tribunal must reach the decision with independence and that it should not follow the policy where it is apparent that the policy is unlawful or produces an unjust decision in the circumstnces of the particular case. He added also that:

"it does not follow that ... formulated by an

adjudicative.........(?) ...."

12. We consider that these considerations are the ones which we should follow in taking up the Applicant's request that we examine the justice of the application of a formula representing considered policy to his position.

Justice of the decision

13. In determining whether the Applicant's position represents an unjust application of the policy we note that this Applicant had a choice whether or not to enter the Commonwealth fund. We note also that the moneys were not paid across to the Commonwealth fund until he knew that the calculation of the period of additional service would be four years, 310 days, although he did not necessarily accept that as a just calculation. We note in addition that this policy has applied invariably to persons transferring in to the Commonwealth superannuation arrangements. We are told by Mr. Fisher, and we consider as reasonably likely, that the consequences of adopting the basis of calculation suggested by the Applicant, Mr. Brown, would be uncertainty, complexity, artificiality and an underestimation of Commonwealth liability under the scheme of the Act. In Mr. Brown's submission, the formula overprotects the Commonwealth liability and under-rates the factors which he considers should be taken into account.

14. We have in mind also that this Tribunal is an appellate tribunal. In the words of Mr. Justice Brennan, "not well equipped for formulating policy". Where we meet a situation in which a policy has been well considered and thoroguhly developed and invariably applied, as it were, at departmental level we find it extremely difficult to detect injustice of the type which would require us, in accordance with the decisions which bind us, to set aside that policy.

15. In particular, the Tribunal is ill-equipped itself to conduct an inquiry into all the factors appropriate to formulating a new formula, or the consequences of varying its application in one particular case.

16. Furthermore, it has no evidence before it that would enable it to carry out that task which would be akin to the task carried out by the consulting group which devised the formula now used by the Commissioner. The Tribunal is aware that injustice must never be muffled by an application of policy. However, in the light of all those circumstances referred to, the Tribunal does not consider that this is a case where the facts disclose an injustice of the type which would permit it to set aside the policy hitherto invariably applied.

17. It does recognize that the Applicant may be disadvantaged by the formula not taking into account the matters which he seeks to have included. It recognizes it would be more advantageous for him if they were taken into account. But in the view of the Tribunal neither of those factors amount to the type of injustice which permits a Tribunal at this appellate level to interfere with the application of the formula applied under sub-section 128(5) of the Act.

18. The Tribunal notes also the importance of consistency in decision-making, again noting it must yield to injustice of the requisite type.

Conclusion

19. While the Tribunal recognizes the discretion in the Commissioner pursuant to sub-section 128(5) is one which may be exercised without regard to the formula, it nevertheless considers that the application of the formula to this Applicant does not give rise to the type of injustice which would permit this Tribunal to set aside the policy represented by the formula and applied to the calculation of the period of additional service. Taking that view of its function as it does, in the light of the established authorities, the Tribunal will affirm the decision under review.
