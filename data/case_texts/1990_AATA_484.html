WT88/25 and Commissioner of Taxation [1990] AATA 484; (1990) 21 ATR 3434; 90 ATC 413 (29 May 1990)

Last Updated: 1 June 2009

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION



INCOME TAX - deduction disallowed - Trust acquired a portfolio of warehouses for long term investment - economic decline - property sold - personal guarantee of rental undertaking - trustee to pay $133,000 providing lessee relinquished existing lease and entered into new lease - whether amount claimed as deduction an expense incurred gaining future assessable income and/or reducing future revenue expenses - whether payment is of a capital nature.



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 S51

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 S37

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Ilberry 81 ATC 4661

W Neville and Co Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1937] HCA 9; (1937) 56 CLR 290

Magna Alloys Research Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1938] HCA 72; (1938) 61 CLR 337

BP Australia Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1965] UKPCHCA 2; 1965 112 CLR 386

REASONS FOR DECISION



29 May 1990 Deputy President G L McDonald



The applicant, a Trust, is applying for the review of a decision of the Commissioner disallowing an amount of $133,000 as a deduction under the provisions of S51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") for the year ending 30 June 1985. The applicant claims the amount is deductible as an "incentive payment re lease".



Documents prepared for the purposes of S37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and other documents were admitted into evidence. The respondent called no evidence. The Tribunal heard sword evidence from one of the directors of AN Pty Ltd (Mr GC) and from an officer of the State Superannuation Board.



The circumstances surrounding the claimed deduction start in 1981. On 27 February 1981 AN Pty Ltd became the Trustee of the WIE Unit Trust. The purpose of the Trust was to acquire a portfolio of industrial warehouses for long term investment purposes for the production of rental income. The Commissioner concedes that the applicant is in the business of renting industrial properties. He also concedes that the applicant does not carry on the business of trading in industrial or any other properties.



The WIE Unit Trust made distributions through another Trust to the applicant Trust. During the relevant year in question 500 of the 1,000 units issued in the WIE Unit Trust were held by GC & Co Pty Ltd in trust for the applicant and 500 by AC Nominees Pty Ltd as Trustee for the C Family Trust. GC & Co Pty Ltd and AC nominees Pty Ltd are unrelated although Mr GC and Mr C, the businessmen behind the companies, are business associates of longstanding.



The first property purchased by the WIE Trust was at 48 T Way, Wattle Grove. At that time land was vacant and the property was purchased with the intention of involving adjoining land owners in seeking to have the zoning of the land changed from "industrial" to "serviced industrial". This was not initially proceeded with because of the lack of cooperation of the adjoining land owners.



In 1981 the WIE Trust purchased lots 75, 77, 78 (all of which were adjoining lots) in F Street, Welshpool. In July 1981 those lots were amalgamated and became Lot 200 F Street.



In 1980 lot 100 F Street was purchased by the WIE Trust in partnership with another. In the same year the WIE Trust and interests connected with Mr C purchased lot 101 F Street. Lots 100-101 are adjoining.



The WIE Trust purchased lot 74 F Street in April 1983. Lot 74 adjoins lot 200. Lots 100 and 101 are located opposite lots 200 and 74. Loans for purchase of the above properties were arranged and secured by mortgages over the properties.



In 1980/1 buildings were erected on lots 100 and 101 and leased to B Ltd. In mid 1982 a warehouse of approximately 3,000 sq.m was erected on lot 200 F Street and leased to A Ltd for 12 months with an option for a further year. At the same time B Ltd required a further 3,000 sq.m storage space and it was decided to erect a warehouse of 4,500 sq.m on the remainder of lot 200 F Street. B Ltd was to take 3,000 sq.m of the new 4,500 sq.m warehouse.



The 3,000 sq.m leased by B Ltd at Lot 200 F Street was for the period 1 September 1982 to 15 December 1984 at an annual rental of $54,900. B Ltd also took an option, exercisable before 15 December 1985, to lease the additional 1,500 sq.m. Upon the exercise of the option B Ltd was to pay $18.30 per sq.m plus 1% thereon for each month from the 1 September 1982 until the exercise of the option (exhibit F).



Since by February 1983 B Ltd had not exercised the option the WIE Unit Trust arranged to lease 600 of the 1,500 sq.m to an unrelated firm M Pty Ltd for one year with a six month option (exhibit G). As the correspondence in exhibit H shows M Pty Ltd was to pay the rent directly to B Ltd and B Ltd was to reimburse WIE Unit Trust for the difference between the higher rent being paid by M Pty Ltd compared to that payable by B Ltd had they exercised the option with respect to the 1,500 sq.m area. M Pty Ltd stayed for one year but did not exercise the option to renew for the further six month period.



Mr GC told the Tribunal that he was anxious to bring in other investors because he felt the property portfolio was over exposed because of the high proportion of the total portfilio rented by B Ltd. This however was opposed by the other unit holder and was not proceeded with.



Mr GC told the Tribunal that the economy declined through 1982 and he approached the State Superannuation Board in November 1982 offering to sell lot 200 for $1.22 million and to purchase from the State Superannuation Board one of their industrial properties (which was fully leased on a long term basis) for $560,000 (exhibit J). This approach was unsuccessful.



Also in November 1982 the tenant of a property owned by ACN Pty Ltd (the holder of half the units in the WIE Unit Trust) did not renew a lease on an industrial property owned by ACN in the same area. Mr GC told the Tribunal that this put further financial pressure on ACN Pty Ltd and this in turn flowed over to the financial pressures being experienced by WIE Unit Trust.



By February 1983 the rental property market had further deteriorated and the applicant commenced looking for other tenants (as evidenced by the approach to M Pty Ltd). Despite experiencing financial pressure the Trust purchased lot 74 which was immediately adjacent to lot 200. The reason given for this was that B Ltd's middle management said more space would be required despite the general financial downturn. At the same time the pressure to sell lot 200, in relation to which at that stage interest was being paid at 22-24% on a loan of $890,000, increased. On 7 April 1983 lot 200 was put on the market (see correspondence contained in exhibit K). The intention was for lots 100, 101 and 74 to be retained and a compatible owner for lot 200 found in order that B Ltd may continue to utilise the entirety of the space which was located in the one area.



Exhibit M is a letter from the State Superannuation Board dated 11 May 1983 to the applicant in which the Board agrees to purchase lot 200 for $1,250,000 subject to, inter alia, AN Pty Ltd leasing the property back for a term of three years at a rental of $148,500 per year. Since B Ltd's tenancy of the 4,500 sq.m was due to terminate on 15 December 1984 and the A Ltd lease of the 3,000 sq.m was due to terminate in August 1983, there was no certainty from AN Pty Ltd's point of view that a tenancy lasting until 1986 could be secured.



The applicant wrote suggesting a number of different rent and condition scenarios to B Ltd on 12 April 1983 (exhibit L). An offer and acceptance for the sale and purchase of lot 200 was completed in June 1983 (exhibit P). The terms of the contract provide inter alia:



. An acknowledgement that the property is sold subject to the lease made 29 October 1982 to B Ltd as lessee and a convenant of the vendor as to the validity of the lease (exhibit P clause 4(1)). .

The vendor to deliver to the purchaser an adjustment of rent and outgoings as at settlement date (exhibit P clause 4(2)).

. A requirement that the vendor deliver to the purchaser

the original executed and stamped lease;

a notice requiring a lessee to pay all future rent and other outgoings under the terms of the lease to the purchaser;

the duplicate key to the property;

the architectural and design plans relating to the property (exhibit P clause 4(3)); .

An assignment and transfer of all the benefits and interests of the lease from the vendor to the purchaser (exhibit P clause 4(4)). Clause 5 should be stated in full and is in the following terms:



"5. RENTAL UNDERTAKING (1) Following settlement the Vendor shall use its best endeavours to procure that B Ltd agrees to take on lease from the Purchaser additional premises within the Property at the best market rental possible. (2) Notwithstanding Condition 5(1) the Vendor covenants that the Annual Net Income (computed for this purpose on a monthly basis) during the period of three years from the settlement date shall be not less than ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY EIGHT THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED DOLLARS ($148,500.00). The Vendor agrees that if at any time and from time to time during that period the Annual Net Income (computed for this purpose on a monthly basis) falls below that figure, the Vendor will forthwith pay to the Purchaser (on a monthly basis) the amount of shortfall. Such shortfall shall be calculated each month during that three year period. The first such calculation shall be made by the Purchaser on 15th June 1983 and subsequent calculations shall be made by the Purchaser on the 15th day of each successive month. The Purchaser shall advise the Vendor of any shortfall so calculated and the Vendor will pay the amount of that shortfall within three days of receipt of that advice. If the Vendor disputes the calculation it will nevertheless pay the amount claimed but may refer the matter for decision in the manner mentioned in Condition 5(3).

(3) In the event of any dispute as to the manner of calculation or the amount of Annual Net Income it shall be referred for the decision of a qualified valuer appointed by the Purchaser who shall be acting as an expert not an arbitrator (and therefore the Arbitration Act shall not apply) and whose decision shall be final and binding upon both parties.

(4) For the purposes of this clause "Annual Net Income" means the net return from moneys (paid as rent not as outgoings) payable under the Lease or other leases entered into by the Purchaser after the Settlement Date relating to the Property. Clause 6 provided a personal guarantee of rental undertaking signed by Mr GC, his wife and Mr C.



An additional term of the contract provided for a bond in the sum of $100,000.00 to be retained by the purchaser to secure the performance of the rent undertaking given by clause 5.



Clause 8 should be quoted in full and is as follows:



8. RELEASE OF RENTAL UNDERTAKING, GUARANTEE AND BOND The Purchaser will release the Vendor from the rental undertaking contained in Condition 5 and the Guarantors from the Guarantee contained in Condition 6 and will repay the Bond referred to in Condition 7 (or so much thereof as remains) on that date being the later of - (1) 9th June 1986; (2) the day on which B Ltd executes such additional lease or leases in respect of the Property (or part thereof) in form satisfactory to the Purchaser (in its absolute discretion) whereby B Ltd agrees to pay rental which when aggregated with the rental under the Lease (referred to in Condition 4) amounts to a net rental (exclusive of outgoings) of not less than ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY EIGHT THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED DOLLARS ($148,500.00) per annum for a period expiring not earlier than 9th June 1986. Mr GC conceded that the above stated conditions did not, on their face, constitute a situation in which WIE Unit Trust held a head lease. However he said since the conditions imposed obligations on the WIE Unit Trust to meet a rental base of $148,500.00 per month, then the WIE Unit Trust had the right to use that part of lot 200 not used by B Ltd or to arrange rental of that part of the property to another party. He told the Tribunal that it was his understanding if the part of the property unused by B Ltd was rented at a higher amount (ie. exceeding the guaranteed rent of $148,500) the Trust would retain the excess.



On the basis of that understanding Mr GC on behalf of the applicant attempted to obtain other lessees for the unused part of the warehouse on Lot 200 F Street (see correspondence contained in exhibit U) but was unsuccessful.



Despite the assignment of the leases to the State Superannuation Board at the time of the transfer of the property M Pty Ltd notified Mr GC rather than the new owner, (the State Superannuation Board) by letter dated 13 December 1983 that it did not propose to exercise the option to renew the lease of the 600 sq.m (exhibit V).



On 11 December 1983 Mr GC wrote to B Ltd suggesting rent adjustments with respect to lots 100 and 101 and further suggesting that the lease on lot 200 be extended to 30 June 1987 with rent adjustments (exhibit Y).



Exhibit AA is a letter of confirmation from GC & Co Pty Ltd binding AN Pty Ltd as Trustee for the WIE Unit Trust to pay $133,000.00 to B Ltd on 15 June 1984 providing B Ltd relinquishes the existing lease and agrees to enter into a new lease with the State Superannuation Board. The letter proposed B Ltd would pay $148,560.00 per annum rent for the 4,500 sq.m warehouse until 15 May 1987 and $12 per sq.m per year ($1 per sq.m per month) to AN Pty Ltd for any or all the space it (ie. B Ltd) occupied in the 3,000 sq.m building on lot 200 until 30 June 1986. $133,000 represented the future lump sum rental value for 2 1/2 years on the 3,000 sq.m warehouse discounted by 12%. The total which AN Pty Ltd could recoup if B Ltd occupied the 3,000 sq.m area would therefore be 2 1/2 x 12 x 3,000 = $90,000. Those figures indicate that in any event AN Pty Ltd would sustain a loss by the arrangement.



A new lease dated 29 June 1984 (exhibit CC) between Superannuation Board and B Ltd evidenced the above agreement being put into effect as from 1 May 1984 (clause 1.1 and item 4 of the Schedule). The lease is expressed to apply to the whole of the land contained in lot 200 (exhibit CC (clause 1.1 and item 5 of the schedule)) but makes no reference to the 3,000 sq.m area mentioned in the correspondence (exhibit AA) (supra).



B Ltd in fact occupied at least part of the 3,000 sq.m and paid the $1 per sq.m per month in April, May and June 1984 (exhibit EE page 2). Exhibit EE page 3 confirms B Ltd paid the rates in relation to the 3,000 sq.m part of the block for the period during which it rented the premises but called upon AN Pty Ltd to pay the balance of rates for the year October 1984 to June 1985 which are noted as being paid. Further adjustments for land tax, water and insurance were made with respect to 3,000 sq.m (exhibit EE page 17) for the year in question. The applicant points to those payments as confirming its ongoing involvement with respect to receiving rent from lot 200 even although there was no apparent mention of any such arrangement in the documentation.



The investment manager of the Superannuation Board gave evidence that after acquiring lot 200 F Street the Board did not actively seek tenants for any of the available space in the 3,000 sq.m warehouses on lot 200 F Street. He told the Tribunal that the applicant had the right "to do with the property as he will" except subject to the usual covenants with respect to safety and not damaging the property. The Manager could give no explanation as to why in exhibit M he wrote to Mr GC that:



"The Board's offer will be subject to formal valuation and subject to AN Pty Ltd leasing the property back from the Board for a term of 3 years at a rental of $148,500.00 per annum". yet no such clause was contained in the sale agreement (exhibit P) other than to say he relied on the Board's solicitors to ensure the Board's intentions were adequately protected. The Manager agreed he would have instructed the Board's solicitors with respect to the preparation of the offer and acceptance. He told the Tribunal that it was his view that the Board would not have have purchased the property without a rental undertaking or guarantee.



The $133,000.00 is claimed as a deduction under the provisions of S51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") which is in the following terms:



"51(1) (Deductions for losses and outgoings) All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income". The applicant maintains that the claimed deduction was not of a private, domestic or capital expense nor was it incurred in the production of exempt income but it served the dual purposes of:



. gaining future assessable income, and reducing future revenue expenses. The respondent argues:



(a) the payment of any shortfall arising as a result of the agreement for sale is not deductible being, as part of the consideration for the sale of the property of a capital nature (see CTBR case 75) (b) in the event of (a) not being upheld then the incentive payment is a payment of a capital nature and is therefore excluded under the exception contained in S51(1) (c) in any event the incentive payment is not deductible against the the reimbursement stream. S51(1) excludes as deductible any expenditure which is of capital or of a capital nature. The aggregation of non capital expenditure into a lump sum does not necessarily change the character of that expenditure which would otherwise be recurrent expenditure (Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Ilberry 8 ATC per Toohey J at 4669). If the expenditure is not of a capital nature then the question for the Tribunal is whether the $133,000 is outlayed as part of the applicant's business operations directed or to the production of income (W Neville and Co Ltd v The Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1937] HCA 9; (1937) 56 CLR 290 per Latham CJ at 301) and the purpose of incurring the expenditure will be relevant (Magna Alloys Research Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 80 ATC 4542 per Brennan J at 4547).



In determining whether the payment is capital or of a capital nature Dixon J in Sun Newspapers Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1938] HCA 72; (1938) 61 CLR 337 set out the following test at 363:



"There are, I think, three matters to be considered, (a) the character of the advantage sought, and in this its lasting qualities may play a part, (b) the manner in which it is to be used, relied upon or enjoyed, and in this and under the former head recurrence may play its part, and (c) the means adopted to obtain it, that is, by providing a periodical reward or outlay to cover its use or enjoyment for periods commensurate with the payment or by making a final provision or payment so as to secure future use or enjoyment". That test was approved by the Privy Council in BP Australia Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1965] UKPCHCA 2; (1965) 112 CLR 386 at 394.



The applicant relies on the terms of the agreement of sale in order to justify its assertion that there was a head lease in favour of the applicant. It is not said that the agreement was part oral or part written.



Clause 5 of the agreement is headed "Rental Undertaking" and by sub-clause 2 the vendor agrees to pay the purchaser a shortfall in any rental income below $148,500.00. Sub-clause 1 is also an undertaking by the vendor to try to procure B Ltd Ltd as lessee of 4,500 sq.m in the building to take further space at the highest rent possible. Sub-clause 4 emphasises in the definition of "annual net rental" that that sum needs to be calculated by reference to the "net return from monies... payable under the lease or other leases entered into by the purchaser after the settlement date...". There is no suggestion in the clause that there is a head lease or that the vendor has any power to use and/or lease any part of the premises. In my view the lease document upon which the applicant relies does not reflect an agreement that the WIE Unit Trust would be head lessee. No such arrangement is apparent on the face of the lease which evidences a sale to the purchaser and allows the purchaser to enter into whatever arrangements it will subject to the existing lease to B Ltd.



The rental undertaking was to be personally guaranteed by the three named parties. The guarantee looks to the individuals behind the WIE Unit Trust to ensure the unit trust commitment is met and there is in my view no duplication in a rental undertaking given by the WIE Trust supported by personal guarantees.



If, within the time limits set out in clause 8, B Ltd entered into an agreement to take additional space at an aggregated rental not less than $148,500.00 then WIE is relieved from the rental undertaking and the guarantors from the guarantee.



It is obvious from the agreement that the vendor's (applicant's) best interests are secured by B Ltd reaching an agreement with the purchaser to take the remainder of the rental space available at 200 F Street.



The negotiations subsequent to sale led to the applicant Trust, as well as the individuals who had given the guarantee, being relieved of their obligations to make up any shortfall of rental below the $148,500 p.a. mark. In my view it was the obtaining of that relief which formed the basis of the negotiations between the applicant, the Superannuation Board and B Ltd resulting in the lease document of 29 June 1984 (exhibit CC). Those negotiations and their resolution are not connected with the applicant's income earning stream but relate to rearranging the terms of the sale to reduce the liability of the applicant vendor as well as that of the guarantor's. That rearrangement relating as it does to the sale of the lot 200 F Street is of a capital nature. Any subsequent arrangements between the applicant, the Superannuation Board and B Ltd are in respect of a licence arrangement between the owner, the lessee and the applicant and in my view must be looked at as a separate issue from consideration connected with the sale and subsequent agreements which relieve the applicant Trust of its rental undertaking and the guarantors of their guarantee.



For those reasons the decision under review is affirmed.
