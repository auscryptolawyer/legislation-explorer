ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - deductions - whether carrying on business of primary-production

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s51(1)

Blockey v FC of T [1923] HCA 2; (1923) 31 CLR 503

FC of T v Martin [1953] HCA 100; (1953) 90 CLR 470

Case P14, 82 ATC 62

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION Nos. AT86/292-295

AAT Decision No 3795

Deputy President RK Todd

25 September 1987

) Nos. AT86/292-295 GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

DECISION

Tribunal : Deputy President RK Todd

Date : 25 September 1987

Place : Canberra

Decision : The Tribunal decides:

  1. To affirm the objection decisions of the respondent in respect of the first applicant.
  2. To vary the objection decisions of the respondent in respect of the second applicant in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1981 by allowing a deduction of $477 for motor vehicle expenses and $312 for entertainment expenses.
  3. To otherwise affirm the objection decisions of the respondent in respect of the second applicant.

Second Applicant

REASONS FOR DECISION

25 September 1987 Deputy President RK Todd

  1. The applicants in this reference are both teachers who, since 1967, have owned a property of 182 ha located approximately one hour's drive from Canberra.
  2. In partnership returns for the year of income ended 30 June 1981 and 1982 they claimed to be entitled to deduct various expenses relating to this property. The claims were rejected, as were the objections lodged by the applicants. These claims have now been referred to this Tribunal for review.
  3. Claims for deductions by the second applicant for entertainment and travel expenses in the year ended 30 June 1981 which were partially disallowed on both initial assessment and objection were conceded by the Commissioner at the commencement of the hearing. These expenses were in relation to his employment at a school.
  4. The Tribunal will give effect to these concessions, and therefore varies the objection decision of the respondent for the year ended 30 June 1981 in respect of the second applicant by allowing further deductions of $789.
  5. Due to this concession, and to agreement that quantum of the primary production claims not be in issue, the only question before the Tribunal was whether or not the applicants were engaged in the carrying on of a business of primary production in the years of income 1981 and 1982.
  6. Evidence was given at the hearing by the second applicant and by an Australian Taxation Office valuer.
  7. It is clear from authorities such as Blockey v FC of T [1923] HCA 2; (1923) 31 CLR 503 and FC of T v Martin [1953] HCA 100; (1953) 90 CLR 470 that whether or not someone is carrying on a business is a question of fact. In Case P14, 82 ATC 62 Dr. P. Gerber (now a Senior Member of this Tribunal) at p.63A emphasised again that "whether a man is or is not carrying, on a business is, ultimately, a question of fact ...". He went on to set out the factors to be considered as follows:

"9. Doing the best I can with such tried old warhorses as Thomas 72 ATC 4094, Martin [1953] HCA 100; (1952-53) 90 CLR 470 and Ferguson 79 ATC 4261, the following indicia seem to emerge:

(i) 'A man may carry on business although he does so in a small way' (Thomas)

(ii) 'The test is both subjective and objective: it is made by regarding the nature and extent of the activities under review, as well as the purpose of the individual engaging in them ... the determination is eventually based on the large or general impression gained' (Martin).

(iii) 'If the transactions which go to make up the activity or operations of the taxpayer have an element of regularity or repetitiveness, this factor assists in concluding that the taxpayer is carrying on a business rather than indulging in a recreational or hobby activity' (Ferguson)."

8. While it is clear that the applicants have prepared a "Development Proposal", which was tendered at the hearing, the Tribunal is unable to find that a business of primary production had commenced in the years in question. In the Tribunal's view it may become a business at some future date, but during the years in question it was still in a preparatory stage.

  1. In coming to this conclusion I have taken into account the general state of the property as described by witnesses and as shown in photographs tendered at the hearing (Ex.7); the numbers of stock on the property; and the stock trading figures for the years in question,
  2. In his report the Taxation Office valuer concluded, in part:

"(d) Whilst it is of larger than normal size for part time farming it is largely undeveloped with only approximately 20% cleared and the. balance comprising green timber and underbrush with little potential for clearing due to light soils and current costs.

(e) Stock management is not of a satisfactory standard with virtually no handling facilities and a practice of in-breeding from stock on the property as well as failure to undertake normal husbandry practices as required.

(f) Current carrying capacity could be increased with further pasture improvement and a continuing fertiliser maintenance programme.

(g) Nett profit from current stock breeding activities is unlikely to cover property running expenses.

(h) The activity has been established in only a partly successful manner."

11. The stock trading accounts for the two years in question showed that at the start of the 1981 financial year there had been 11 sheep and 8 cattle on the property. During that year, 3 sheep and 3 cattle died and 3 cattle had been born. No cattle or sheep were bought or sold. In the 1982 years, 1 cow and 2 sheep died with no natural increase occurring. There was however a purchase of 24 sheep.

  1. Apart from that purchase, the only purchase between 1978-79 and 1983-84 was 1 sheep and 1 cow in 1978-79. The only sales to occur in that period were 18 cattle in 1979-80 and 4 cattle in 1983-84.
  2. While it is true that during the period in question a drought affected the area, a lack of regularity in the taxpayer's operations is indicated in the trading operations carried on which supports the finding that a business of sheep or cattle breeding was not being carried on on the property in 1980-81 or 1981-82.
  3. For the reasons given above, the Commissioner's decision upon objection is affirmed in respect of the claim for deductions related to primary production. In accordance with the concession detailed above, the second applicant's objection decision is varied by allowing deductions of $477 for motor vehicle expenses and $312 for entertainment expenses in the year of income ended 30 June 1981.
