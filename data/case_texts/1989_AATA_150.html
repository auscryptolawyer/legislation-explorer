Re Ilona Bujna and Commissioner of Housing [1989] AATA 150 (22 June 1989)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: ILONA BUJNA

And: COMMISSIONER FOR HOUSING

No. A89/30

AAT No. 5163

Housing Assistance Act

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.K. Todd(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Housing Assistance Act - decision pursuant to Clause 19(1)(b) Housing Assistance Program - whether reviewable

Housing Assistance Act 1987, ss.12, 13, Schedule 1

HEARING

CANBERRA

22:6:1989

ORDER

The Tribunal will proceed no further in this matter it having no jurisdiction therein.

DECISION

The issue here is whether the Tribunal has jurisdiction to hear the purported application for review lodged herein.

2. The decision sought to be reviewed is that of the Acting Commissioner for Housing that the applicant be required to vacate a three bedroom Housing Trust House. The decision was conveyed to the applicant by a letter dated 18 January 1989 sent to her solicitors.

3. On 23 November 1987 a three bedroom house at Calwell was allocated to the applicant and a Ms De Pasquale as joint tenants for the accommodation of both women and of one Steven Bujna, who is the applicant's son and the de facto spouse of Ms De Pasquale. On 23 April 1988, Ms De Pasquale vacated the premises leaving only the applicant and her son in occupation thereof.

4. The situation was then considered in accordance with the "Policy Statement for Government Assistance in the ACT" which provides that where a joint tenancy in a three bedroom home breaks down by one tenant leaving, the tenancy will be terminated or transferred to more appropriately sized accommodation. Thus, it was recommended that it was in the interests of good administration of public housing that the applicant and her son be required to transfer to a two bedroom flat. Pursuant to clause 19(1)(b) of the gazetted "Public Rental Housing Assistance Program" the then Acting Commissioner for Housing approved the transfer proposal. The applicant was notified of this decision in a letter to her solicitors dated 18 January 1989. It is in respect of this decision that the present application was made on 15 March 1989. A preliminary conference was held on 4 May 1989 with no resolution of the matter.

5. In letters of 8 May 1989 and 9 May 1989 the respondent submitted that the Tribunal may not have any jurisdiction to review a decision of the Commissioner under clause 19(1)(b) of the Program. Accordingly a directions hearing was set down to resolve this issue.

6. Mr. P. Walker, for the respondent, submitted that there was no power to review the decision because clause 21(1) of the Program provides that a reference to a "decision of the Commissioner" shall be read as a reference to a "decision of the Commissioner on an application made under this Program". Here the decision was taken unilaterally under clause 19(1)(b), and not as a result of an application.

7. Section 12 of the Housing Assistance Act No.36 of 1987 ("the Act") allows the Commissioner, for the purposes of the Act, to prepare a housing assistance program, implementation occurring only with the Minister's approval and taking effect only after publication in the Gazette (s.13(2)). On 16 December 1987, a delegate of the Minister approved the "Public Rental Housing Assistance Program" which was notified in the Gazette on 23 December 1987.

8. Schedule 1 of the Act sets out the principles of the Program, one of which is to ensure that every person in the ACT has access to adequate and appropriate housing at a price within his or her capacity to pay. The Program also seeks to reflect the principles of providing general assistance according to need and achievement of maximum social benefit; allocation of housing on a non-discriminatory basis having regard to access to community services; and the provision of income-related assistance to tenants.

9. Clause 4 of the Program sets out eligibility requirements for assistance; Clause 8 provides for allocation of assistance; Clause 11 for immediate assistance; Clause 15 relates to tenancy agreements, and Clause 16 sets out the rental provisions. Clause 19 refers to the transfer of tenants to other commission housing either upon application by a tenant (Clause 19(a)) or where it is reasonably required for the purposes of good administration (Clause 19(b)). Clause 20 sets out the form of applications made under the Program. Clause 21 provides for review of decisions, "decision of the Commissioner" being defined in Clause 21(1) as referring to such a decision "on an application made under this Program ...". Clause 21(3) allows the Commissioner to reconsider a decision if the Commissioner believes sufficient reason to exist but Clause 21(4) allows an applicant, within 28 days, to request the Commissioner to reconsider that decision. Clause 21(6) provides for the Commissioner to undertake such a reconsideration and to affirm, revoke or vary the decision. Under Clause 21(8) applications may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal ("the AAT") for review of a decision made under Clause 21(6).

10. In this matter there seems to have been a purported reconsideration under Clause 21(6). In a letter of 19 September 1988 the applicant's solicitors requested the Commissioner to reconsider the decision to transfer the applicant to alternative accommodation. The Acting Commissioner by letter dated 18 January 1989 affirmed the decision, informed the applicant of the result of his reconsideration, and further informed the applicant's solicitors that application might be made to the AAT for review of this decision. It is from this reconsideration that the application to the AAT was sought to be brought.

11. A request for reconsideration pursuant to Clause 21(4) is however limited to "a decision of the Commissioner", defined in Clause 21(1) to mean "a decision of the Commissioner on an application made under this Program". At no point was there any application made by the applicant to transfer her to alternative accommodation, so that the decision to which the letter of 18 September 1988 pertains was not a "decision on an application". This was rather a unilateral decision taken under Clause 19(1)(b). Therefore there could be no request for reconsideration under Clause 21(4), and there could neither be valid reconsideration pursuant to Clause 21(6) nor anything from which to bring an application for review to the AAT. It is nothing to the point that reconsideration did occur, apparently on the assumption that the request was validly made.

12. The reference to "an application" in Clause 21(1) is to any application made under the Program and not merely to an application made under Clause 19(1)(a). For example, if an application for assistance were made pursuant to Clause 4(1) and denied by the Commissioner, Clause 21 would be activated, the denial being a decision of the Commissioner on an application. For the applicant Mr Pappas however went further, suggesting that the "application" referred to in Clause 21(1) could, in this case, encompass the applicant's original application for assistance in 1987 and that any decision of the Commissioner subsequent to that application was a decision on that application. In my opinion this would, with all respect to the argument, strain the words in Clause 21(1) too far. In deciding to transfer the applicant to alternative accommodation, the Commissioner was not making a decision on the original application for assistance. That application was spent once housing was provided to the applicant. In any case the original application is too far removed from the decision now made to transfer the applicant. Clause 19 supports this view in that Clause 19(1)(a) allows applications to be made for a transfer, a different application from the original application for assistance. Clause 21 sets out the review mechanism pertaining to such an application.

13. Mr. Pappas indicated that at least three applications had been made by the applicant under the Program, two of them concerning rental rebate. Such applications do not, however, in my opinion carry through to the situation where a person is transferred to alternative accommodation any more than does a Clause 4 application.

14. This result points to an anomaly in the Program as it presently stands. While a person who applies for a transfer to alternative accommodation under Clause 19(1)(a) may have recourse to the review procedures set out in Clause 21, a person who is transferred as a result of the Commissioner's decision that such a transfer is reasonably required for the purposes of good administration has no such right. It does not seem to be in the interests of proper and just administration that such a result occur, and it may be that the wording of Clause 21(1) of the Program should be amended to enable persons such as the applicant, who have been transferred as a result of a unilateral act of the Commissioner, to have such action reviewed.

15. The only order that I can make is that the Tribunal will proceed no further in the matter, it having no jurisdiction therein. I may add that it is to be hoped that an agreement may nevertheless be reached. There appear to be some medical grounds to support the applicant's need for a house rather than a unit, even if the need for a three bedroom house cannot be supported. There may be other possibilities.
