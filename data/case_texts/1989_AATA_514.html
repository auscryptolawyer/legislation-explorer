ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - payment for initial repair made to body corporate whether of a capital nature

Strata Titles Act 1967 (Vic) ss.15, 16

Case V83 (1988) 88 ATC 580

Case T108 (1986) 86 ATC 1196

Conveyancing (Strata Titles) Act 1961 (NSW) First Schedule

W. Thomas & Co Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1965] HCA 54; (1965) 115 CLR 58

Re Steel and Others and the Conveyancing (Strata Titles) Act 1961 (1968) 88 WN (Pt 1) NSW 467

Respondent REASONS FOR DECISION

11 August 1989 Mrs R.A. Balmford, Senior Member

  1. This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent disallowing an objection by the applicant, Mrs C, to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of her taxable income for the year ended 30 June 1985 of an amount of $5372 derived from the C Family Trust ("the Trust").
  2. The applicant requested on 25 June 1987 that the decision on the objection be referred to this Tribunal, and that request was complied with by the respondent. By virtue of sub-section 189(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") that referral is. deemed to constitute the making of an application to this Tribunal for review of the decision on the objection.
  3. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 as modified by section 14ZG of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 and numbered T1 to T10, together with other materials lodged at the hearing. Evidence was given at the hearing by Mr C, a director of C Pty Ltd, and Mr H, an estate agent with experience in property management. The applicant was represented by Mr M, an accountant, and the respondent by an officer of the Australian Taxation Office.
  4. By a resolution made on 28 June 1985 the directors of C Pty Ltd as trustee of the Trust resolved to distribute the income of the Trust for the year ending 30 June 1985 as to specified amounts to three named beneficiaries (none of whom was the applicant) and as to the residue to or for the benefit of the applicant.
  5. The profit and loss account of the Trust annexed to its income tax return for the year ending 30 June 1985 included an amount of $5502.19 for "Repairs and Maintenance (Buildings)". An attached note read as follows (with identification omitted):

"REPAIRS & MAINTENANCE

Included in this figure is $5397.19 paid to Body Corporate Services - special levy re 7-8 S Street, P, being for:

- repairs to the building

- burst cold water pipe.

Taxpayer acquired a unit at 7-8 S Street, P, in April, 1985, settling in May, 1985, but subsequent to having signed the contract, discovered that major expenditure was required as above. The vendor had not disclosed this fact, either to the Estate Agent, nor on the Vendor's Statement, pursuant to Section 32 (d). The Vendor subsequently claimed that he had been unaware of the problem at the time of signing the contract.

It is contended that this is an allowable deduction as, in the unusual circumstances, it could not be categorised an initial repair. Taxpayer paid $97,000 for the unit on the basis that there were no major faults nor payments due to the Body Corporate, and that the property was tenantable as was."

6. The respondent added the sura of $5372 forming part of the amount of $5502 to the net income of the Trust as shown in its return. By virtue of the resolution described in paragraph 4 supra, that amount was included in the taxable income of the applicant.

7. Having considered the evidence before me, I find the sequence of events relating to the purchase of the unit to be as follows, so far as can be ascertained:

  1. February: sale note signed for purchase by C Pty Ltd of Unit 31 at 7-8 S Street, P. The property had been inspected on behalf of the parties and was not obviously in need of repair. The purchase price was $97,000. The property was purchased as an investment.

13 March: extraordinary general meeting of Body Corporate Strata Plan No __ resolved;

  1. to appoint a specified builder to carry out a sample repair, the cost of the sample repair to be deducted from the total tendered price of $125,173 if that builder was later instructed to carry out repairs to the whole building;
  2. to authorise the manager to raise a levy, in accordance with unit liability, to meet the cost of the repairs.

The nature of the repairs in question does not appear from the evidence.

10 May: purchase settled and possession given. Half-yearly body corporate fees adjusted. No reference to levy on adjustment statement.

  1. May: extraordinary general meeting of Body Corporate Strata Plan No __ resolved;
    1. to appoint the builder to carry out the repairs at a total estimated cost of $118,550;
    2. to raise a levy to meet the cost of those repairs and of repairing a burst water pipe.

24 May: Notice of special levy sent to C Pty Ltd showing $5397.19 due and payable in respect of Unit 31.

3 June: levy paid by C Pty Ltd.

Later in June: unit let to tenant.

Later: structural repairs to the building were carried out with the moneys raised by the levy.

8. The undertaking of the repairs and the raising of the levy were authorised by paragraphs 15(1)(f) and 16(c) and (d) of the Victorian Strata Titles Act 1967 ("the Strata Titles Act") which read as follows, so far as relevant:

" 15.(1) The duties of the body corporate shall include the following:-

...

(f) To keep in a state of good and serviceable repair and properly maintain the common property;

...

16. The powers of the body corporate shall include the following:-

...

(b) To establish and maintain a fund -

(i) for administrative expenses sufficient in the opinion of the body corporate for the control, management and administration of the common property and for the payment of any premiums of insurance; and

(ii) for the discharge or the performance of any other obligation of the body corporate;

(c) To determine and record in writing from time to time the amounts to be raised for the purposes aforesaid and the time and manner of payment thereof ...;

(d) To raise amounts so determined by making demands in writing for and receiving payment of them from members in proportion to the unit liability of their respective units or in such other proportions as may from time to time be determined by unanimous resolution;"

Section 17 provides for the recovery of amounts due and payable under paragraph 16(d).

9. It was argued for the applicant that the amount in question was deductible under the second limb of sub-section 51(1) of the Act, as being an outgoing "necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing [the assessable] income" and not of a capital, private or domestic nature. The expenditure on the repairs by the body corporate in respect of the unit in question, had it been paid directly by C Pty Ltd, would have been expenditure of a capital nature as an "initial repair". However the payment was not a payment for the repairs: it was the payment of a levy to the body corporate. As such, it was indistinguishable from the regular half-yearly fee to the body corporate. An analogy could be drawn from the financial statements of the Australian Society of Accountants. That body owned several freehold properties, no doubt purchased in part out of members' subscriptions: but that did not render any portion of the subscriptions of a capital nature so as not to be deductible by the members.

10. The authorities relating to "initial repairs" are succinctly considered by the Tribunal, constituted by Mr Trowse, Member, in the following passage from the decision in Case V83 (1988) 88 ATC 580, where the matter arose in the context of sub-section 53(1) of the Act:

"10. A determination as to whether or not a particular outgoing comes within the meaning of being a repair will be assisted by reference to various judicial decisions, see for example, comments made by Windeyer J. in W. Thomas & Co. Pty. Ltd. v F.C. of T. [1965] HCA 54; (1965) 115 C.L.R. 58 at p. 72:

'Repair involves a restoration of a thing to a condition it formerly had without changing its character.'

11. To decide whether a particular amount expended on premises ought to be charged against revenue or capital may in some circumstances present difficulties. However, it is clear that where the expenditure does substantially more than meet a need for restoration and results in an asset with considerable advantage over the old one, that expenditure is of a capital nature - see comments of Kitto J. in F.C. of T. v Western Suburbs Cinemas Ltd. [1952] HCA 28; (1952) 86 C.L.R. 102 at p. 106.

12. The decision in the case of Thomas (supra) is, in my opinion, most relevant to this reference, in as much that it is authority for the proposition that amounts expended to remedy faults in existence at the time of acquisition, sometimes referred to as 'initial repairs', are to be regarded as part of the original cost. As such they are to be classified as capital expenditure. The following statement taken from the judgment of Windeyer J. at p. 72 is apposite:

'Expenditure upon repairs is properly attributed to revenue account where the repairs are for the maintenance of an income-producing capital asset. Maintenance involves the periodic repair of defects that are the result of normal wear and tear in operation. It is an expense of a revenue nature when it is to repair defects arising from the operations of the person who incurs it. But if when a thing is bought for use as a capital asset in the buyer's business it is not in good order and suitable for use in the way intended, the cost of putting it in order suitable for use is part of the cost of its acquisition, not a cost of its maintenance.' (emphasis added)

13. In my view the 'initial repair' test is no more than an application of the well-recognised principles that have been developed over the years by courts in determining what is capital. More specifically, I see it as an extension of the 'profit-yielding structure' principle enunciated by Dixon J. (as he then was) in Sun Newspapers Ltd. and Associated Newspapers Ltd v FC of T [1938] HCA 72; (1938-1939) 61 CLR 337. In terms of that reasoning, expenditure relating to the establishment, replacement or enlargement of the profit-yielding structure, as opposed to those incurred in the operation of that structure, will be of a capital nature."

  1. I accept, as I have indicated, the evidence that the property appeared to be in good order when inspected before the purchase. However, the resolutions to undertake the repairs and raise the levy were passed by the members of the body corporate, that is to say the owners of the units in the building (see section 14 of the Strata Titles Act), who thereby each bound themselves to pay a substantial sum of money towards the repairs. The only possible inference from that decision is that the repairs were necessary.
  2. Whether they were necessary in order to "put the building in good order and suitable for use in the way intended", to use the words of Windeyer J, or to forestall the building's becoming out of order and unusable is a question of degree, and the answer to that question does not appear from the evidence. However, a necessary expenditure of five and a half per cent of the cost of the property, immediately after purchase, in my view comes within the concept of an "initial repair" in terms of the authorities cited, so as to render that expenditure of a capital nature.
  3. The applicant, as I have said, conceded that the expenditure on the repairs by the body corporate was expenditure of a capital nature, but submitted that the payment of the levy by C Pty Ltd to the body corporate had a different character, and that this matter was "on that ground, distinguishable from the "initial repair" cases.
  4. In Case T108 (1986) 86 ATC 1196 the payment of a levy to a body corporate for significant repairs to a building constituting a block of separately owned units was held to be a non-deductible capital outgoing. The taxpayer had owned her unit and lived in it for some time, but before the levy was imposed she had moved out and let the unit. The Tribunal, constituted by Mr Stevens, Senior Member, found that the expenditure did not arise out of the income-producing activities of the applicant, on the basis (inter alia) of the decision in W. Thomas & Co Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (supra). It does not appear that any question was raised as to the payment having been made by the taxpayer's paying a levy to the body corporate, rather than paying directly for the repairs.
  5. The essential part of the argument for the applicant was the submission that the amount in question, once paid to the body corporate, was money in the hands of that body which could be used for whatever purpose the body corporate deemed fit, and was not in any way earmarked for the purposes of the repairs. There was no relevant distinction, Mr M argued, between the levy for repairs and the regular half-yearly fee payable to the body corporate, the amount of which had been adjusted between the vendor and C Pty Ltd on settlement of the purchase, and appears to have been subsumed under the heading "rates and taxes" in the profit and loss account referred to in paragraph 5 supra.
  6. In Re Steel and Others and the Conveyancing (Strata Titles) Act 1961 (1968) 88 WN (Pt 1) NSW 467 Else-Mitchell J was concerned with an application to appoint an administrator under New South Wales legislation corresponding to section 30 of the Strata Titles Act. His Honour said (at page 470) after referring to the failure of the respondents to hold a general meeting of the body corporate in 1967, to maintain a separate set of books or a separate bank account for the affairs of the body corporate, or to provide for an annual audit of the body corporate:

"In these respects, as in some others which emerged from the oral evidence of Mr. Jacombe but which it is not necessary to recount, I consider that the respondents have failed to appreciate the nature of the duties cast on them as members of the council of a body corporate under the Conveyancing (Strata Titles) Act. Such persons are at least in a position analogous to company directors; they may even have a higher fiduciary duty, and when they are promoters as well this duty has a dual basis. It is plain that the respondents have failed to recognize that it is their duty to manage the affairs of the body corporate for the benefit of all the lot holders, and that the exercise of any of their powers in circumstances which might suggest a conflict of interest and duty requires them to justify their conduct, and that the onus lies on them to prove affirmatively that they have not acted in their own interests or for their own benefit. Mr. Jacombe's evidence shows that he does not understand this duty, though he claimed to have studied law at London University and, indeed, he said that the reason for appointing his own company as administrator under his supervision to conduct the affairs of the body corporate in the manner of which complaint is made stemmed from his desire to save expenses, not in the interests of the lot holders, but in the interests of his own company which promoted this venture and was proposing to sell other lots to the public.

There is no doubt in my mind that in these respects there have been breaches of the fiduciary duty which flows from membership of a council of a body corporate under the Conveyancing (Strata Titles) Act, 1961. That Act does not specify the grounds on which the court in its discretion may appoint an administrator but uses the expression 'on cause shown'. Such cause may be found in a wide variety of circumstances and situations entailing nonfeasance or misfeasance by the council of a body corporate, which it would be impossible to categorize exhaustively. For present purposes it is, I think, sufficient to say that in the absence of cogent explanation or general agreement, a clear and continuing failure to observe the statutory obligations arising under the by-laws in the First Schedule will constitute ground for seeking the appointment of an administrator. The Act contemplates that this remedy is to be a summary one, as indeed it must be for the adequate protection of proprietors of strata title lots as well as purchasers, mortgagees and others who claim derivative interests in the strata title lots which usually confer titles in fee simple. It is important that those titles should be protected by the law to no less an extent than estates in fee simple in land simpliciter, and since the council of the body corporate is the statutory agent for lot holders it should be astute to recognize the rights of lot holders and not give the appearance of oppression which has been evident in this case."

17. While the decision in that case is not directly in point, His Honour's statement of the nature of the duties of the governing body of the body corporate encourages me to reject the submission of Mr M that moneys raised for a specific purpose from the owners of units on a strata plan may be expended for a different purpose.

18. I note that by-law No 2 in the First Schedule to the Conveyancing (Strata Titles) Act 1961, being the legislation with which His Honour was concerned, required the body corporate "to control, manage and administer the common property for the benefit of all proprietors". It is to be presumed that the power to amend or repeal that by-law which is provided for section 13 of that Act had not been exercised. No words corresponding to the passage underlined appear in the Strata Titles Act or in the by-laws provided for by that Act, and I was not provided with a copy of the by-laws which had been adopted in the case of the building with which I am concerned. However, in my view, such an obligation, in general terms, must be implicit in the conferral on the body corporate by the Strata Titles Act, of the duties and powers therein set out; and the implication of such an obligation leads necessarily to the view expressed by His Honour. His Honour did not, in his judgment, refer to the by-law in question.

19. For the reasons given, I find that the payment in question is properly characterised as payment for an "initial repair" and thus of a capital nature. The decision under review will be affirmed.
