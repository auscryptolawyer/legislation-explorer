NT86/4035 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 5; 87 ATC 207 (14 January 1987)

Last Updated: 8 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Allowable deductions - Prospector – expenses incurred in relation to gold and in relation to proving viability of other mineral resources - not allowable.

Income Tax Assessment Act (Cth) 1936, S51(1).

Sun Newspapers Ltd v FCT.and Associated Newspapers Ltd v FCT [1938] HCA 72; (1938) 61 CLR 337).

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISIO

NT86/4035

Applicant

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

No. NT86/4035

AAT Decision No 3155

MR PM ROACH (Senior Member)

14 January 1987

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

)

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re: NT86/4035

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

Mr PM Roach (Senior Member)

1. The Applicant in this reference is an elderly gentleman distinguished by his geniality and optimism. That optimism caused him to become a prospector in the fashion of the present times. His career to an age at which most have retired from full-time employment was in an unrelated industry and his endeavours continued in that field throughout the year of income ended June , 1923 He was employed the year in that other industry in the City. The demands of that employment were not sufficient to occupy his time fully and he spent considerable effort in documentary research and some field work in his search for mineral wealth. In those endeavours he associated with an old friend, a person somewhat older than himself, who even at his advanced age was still interested in mining exploration and development. The basis of that association was that the friend would provide some finance to assist the Applicant in his investigations with a view to possible development. By reason of that financial assistance the friend required that prospecting licences and similar rights be registered in his name or in the name of companies he controlled, although assuring the Applicant of a substantial beneficial interest in any mining development which might emerge.

  1. The principal areas of prospecting interest were searching for gold in payable quantities under economically feasible conditions in the Northern Territory and in seeking to appraise zircon and rutile deposits in areas known to contain those minerals. Much of the 'prospecting' was done by way of investigating the geological and mining records held by authorities in Sydney. Endeavours to advance the appraisal of the zircon and rutile deposits were substantially hindered by the restrictions imposed by public authorities opposed to mineral exploration and development. No assessable income was derived during the year of income. Further, short of establishing mining operations, no assessable income was in prospect.
  2. The Applicant incurred substantial expenses which were wholly or in part related to the foregoing activities and which were wholly claimed as income tax deductions in the year of income ended 30 June 1983. They were claimed to be as follows:

Mining leases and costs 443.00

Travelling expenses 49.00

Hire of equipment 299.00

Research and testing costs 119.00

Bank charges 100.00

Interest paid 200.00

Technical magazines and journals 47.00

Club subscription (library and writing facilities) 25.00

Telephone - business only 275.00

Licences 35.00

Printing, stationery and postages 48.00

$1,944.00

  1. In addition the Applicant claimed a deduction for $303.00 for "accountancy fees", said to have been incurred as expenses "as mining prospector". It is unnecessary to consider that issue as the Commissioner conceded that for other reasons the claim is allowable. To that extent the Applicant will succeed upon his objection.
  2. Having said that, I am of opinion that in all other respects the claim of the Applicant must fail. Insofar as the expenses incurred related to gold-prospecting activities the claims are to be excluded under Section 51 of the Income Tax Assessment Act because they were directed to the generation of "exempt income" rather than assessable income. Insofar as the activities of the taxpayer were not directed to the derivation of exempt income, assuming that they were directed to the derivation of income at all, they were in my view directed towards an endeavour to establish a new "profit-yielding subject" (cf Dixon, CJ in Sun Newspapers Ltd and Associated Newspapers Ltd v FCT [1938] HCA 72; (1938) 61 CLR 337 at 360). In my view the objective of the Applicant's endeavours in relation to zircon and rutile was to "prove up" a viable mine. Had he succeeded in that, then and only then would there have been a new "profit-yielding subject", the working of which would have generated assessable income. The expenses of operating that "profit-yielding subject" - whether operated by the Applicant and his friend, or by others to whom the project might have been sold - would have been allowable as provided for by the Income Tax Assessment Act. For those reasons the Applicant must fail in substance.

6. Accordingly, I would vary the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection by allowing by consent a reduction in taxable income of $303.00, but in all other respects I affirm the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection.
