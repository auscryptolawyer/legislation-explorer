TT86/499-500 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 29; 87 ATC 262 (4 February 1987)

Last Updated: 9 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax: Additional Tax - Incorrect returns - Taxpayer employed by company he controlled - Wages records altered retrospectively - No personal dishonesty of taxpayer - Remission of additional tax - Commissioner's Guidelines.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Commonwealth) S226 (2) and (3)

Re: TT86/499-500

Applicant

And: Commissioner of Taxation

Respondent

No: TT86/499-500

AAT Decision No 3182

Date: 4 February 1987

P M Roach (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No TT86/499-500

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re: TT86/499-500

Applicant

And: Commissioner of Taxation

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

Mr P M Roach (Senior Member)

  1. The Applicant in this reference (A) is a panel beater, born in 1946. It seems that he possessed greater aptitude for the practice of his trade than for matters of business. In 1979 at the instigation of an experienced man of business (C), A and C established a company (PBCO) to carry on a small panel beating business. A was to be employed full time in the business. In 1980, possibly because his business experience enabled him to read the signs with foresight lacking in A, C retired from the business. Thereafter A, with the clerical assistance of his wife, carried on the affairs of the company. The business of PBCO did not prosper and the company was finally liquidated.
  2. A was regularly being pressed by bankers to the company to improve the financial performance of the company and his control of its affairs. By June 1981 the company's trading profit was only $117 with accumulated losses carried forward of $1,523. A further $7,008 (goodwill) was written off at the close of that year, with the result that there was a deficiency in shareholders' funds amounting to $8,624 on a subscribed capital of $24. All this occurred at a time when A was maintaining some loan funds interest free with the company. At 30 June 1981 the amount on loan to the company did not exceed $8,418. I am not persuaded that other funds were introduced later. The bank manager suggested that A should be drawing against his loan account rather than continue to draw wages - at least until such time as the company should return to profit. The bank manager advised A that the company couldn't afford to pay wages for A in addition to the other employees. A checked with his then accountant who approved the idea.

3. In evidence A said that, when he spoke with that accountant about the bank manager's recommendation, he asked what he should continue to do about his own weekly drawings and that the accountant "suggested continuing as before (and he) would fix it at the end of the year". It was A's intention to implement that recommendation. Up to that point of time, each week Mrs A had prepared the payroll. Each week she drew a cheque for net wages and a cheque in favour of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation to cover tax withheld from gross wages. The cheques so drawn were written up in a cash book and there identified as "wages" and "tax" respectively. In addition a wages book was maintained recording in one section gross wage entitlements for A and the other employees of the company; and in another section "tax". In most periods there were two other tradesmen, usually paid marginally less than A. That practice in large measures was to continue.

4. The cash book and wages book were produced in evidence, but the bank statements were not. Week by week the cash book and wages book reconciled, although there are pencilled annotations to the effect that five cheques in favour of the Deputy Commissioner drawn between 2 and 30 June 1982 in sums amounting to $997.40 were "cancelled".

The wages book in the period to 30 June 1982 showed that wages "year to date" for A amounted to $13,150 out of a total for wages of $52,352.26. There then appeared an entry "Adj ($9,050)" leaving concluding figures for A and in total of $4,100 and $43,302.26. In the tax section, tax deductions "year to date" for A were shown as $2,999.10 beneath which appeared "less adj ($2,999.10)", leaving a balance of nil. Total remissions for tax deductions were similarly adjusted. Those figures were not reconciled with the cash book. PBCO provided A with a group certificate showing gross salary at $4,100 and tax deductions at nil (sic). Tax agents prepared A's income tax return and, upon assessment, the Commissioner determined that "no tax is payable". On that basis nothing was refundable or payable.

6. In the 1982/83 year the wages book reconciled with the cash hook insofar as it recorded the total of weekly wages payable to A at $13,000 for the year, but there then appeared the entry "adj ($9,000)", leaving a balance of $4,000. However, on this occasion there was no adjustment entry in relation to tax and the recorded figure of $2,743.40, appearing as the sum of all tax deductions week by week during the year, remained unchanged. Once again A was provided with a group certificate by PBCO, on this occasion recording wages of $4,000 and tax instalments deducted at $2,743.40. This time the return came under scrutiny before assessment.

7. That scrutiny resulted in original notices of assessment issuing in relation to years 30 June 1982 and 1983 on 29 August 1985. The taxable income of A was assessed after bringing to account (inter alia) "omitted income (from PBCO)" of $9,050 and $9,000 respectively. By those assessments additional tax was also levied pursuant to s.226 (2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act in sums of $2,475.10 and $1,601.51 respectively - a total of $4,076.61. A disputes the treatment of the so-called "wages" as assessable income; and disputes the imposition of additional tax.

8. The objection lodged by his present accountant said (inter alia):

"The grounds upon which the objection is based are that the Commissioner is in error in including the aforementioned sums as taxable income when it was, in fact, drawings by the taxpayer against his loan account with the company. All of the statutory records of the company record that the amounts were in fact drawings, the annual financial statements of the company (PBCO) Pty Ltd are drawn up after treating the amounts as drawings, were accepted by the shareholders at the annual general meetings and were accepted by the Corporate Affairs Office when the annual returns were lodged. The Commissioner has arbitrarily and erroneously extracted records from a book in which notes were kept of wages paid, drawings made and taxation instalment deductions to be forwarded to the Commissioner, and treated the aggregate as being salary. It is pointed out that this note book is not a statutory book and was used merely for notation purposes.

It is conceded that excessive tax instalment deductions were made from the taxpayer's income and remitted to the Commissioner, however, it is pointed out that it is not an offence to deduct more tax that is required by law and indeed many taxpayers (including the objector) use this as a method of forced savings.

The Commissioner would be aware that declarations have been made by directors of the company in accordance with the provisions of the Companies Act that the amounts in dispute were drawings, that group certificates were prepared and signed as being correct which excluded the drawings, that the taxpayer included amounts in his returns in accordance with these group certificates and has generally acted in accordance with the law."

9. I acknowledge that it is open to persons who control a company to take moneys to their own use either by borrowing from the company or by drawing wages or salary to which they are entitled. It is also possible for such persons to draw moneys on loan in anticipation of a future decision being made as to what part of the moneys so drawn will be determined to be wages non-repayable - and what. amount will remain as a loan to the drawer - repayable to the company. It is also true that there is nothing unlawful about an employee arranging with an employer for the deduction of needlessly high amounts on account of tax and for the remission of such moneys to the Commissioner. It is also true that members of the company, so far as their own interests alone are concerned, may approve a reorganisation of things already done by the company - although they cannot alter the fact of the things which were done - although not doing so to the extent of creating a new order of priorities to bind a future liquidator.

10. Although such things may be done, the first question in this reference is only whether it is more probable than not that what was done in each of the weeks of the years ended 30 June 1982 and 30 June 1983 for which PBCO recorded that it paid "wages" to A, and as it did to others, constituted the payment of wages to A for his services. On the evidence I think the conclusion inescapable that PBCO did so pay wages. What is more, in each week, it recorded deductions of tax for all employees and the evidence indicates that week by week until the latter part of the year ended 30 June 1982 and week by week throughout the year ended 30 June 1983, the tax recorded as deducted was in fact remitted on account of all employees to the Commissioner. I mention the last few weeks to June 1982 because, although the annotations in the cash book provide some indication that some tax was not remitted, they fail to explain how PBCO could later certify that no tax at all had been deducted for A; or how it was, as it seems to have been, that the Commissioner had not received any payments of tax to the credit of A. The cancelled cheques only amounted to $997.40; the tax deducted was recorded as $2,999.10 for the year ended 30 June 1982.

11. It was contended that A and PBCO were entitled to carry out those actions without it necessarily following that the amounts recorded as "wages" actually constituting rewards to A for his personal services. Before such a conclusion could be reached, it would have to be found to flow from the making of some arrangement between A and PBCO which gave a different character to the actions in the tax years 1982 and 1983 to the character the same actions had in the year of income ended 30 June 1981. There is simply no evidence to that effect. I am satisfied that week by week PBCO paid "wages" to A as a reward for his personal services and that A received as a gross wage (less tax deducted) the amounts recorded against his name in the wages book of PBCO. To that extent the assessments of the Commissioner are to be upheld.

12. By reason of the inaccurate representations amounting to $2,865.60 and $2,692.21 for the years ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 respectively. In consequence of the understatements of income A became liable to the imposition of additional tax pursuant to s226(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. To quote the Commissioner's reasons for disallowing the taxpayer's claim as expressed in the statement furnished when the request for reference was transmitted

"(3) As a result of such failure he became liable to pay additional tax under sub-section 226(2) of the Act equal to twice the difference between the tax properly payable and the tax payable on the basis of the returns furnished by him.

(4) Of such additional tax the Commissioner has remitted 66.27% and the circumstances are such that no further remission is warranted."

  1. At the hearing the Commissioner's representative advised that the amounts of $2,475.10 and $1,601.51 levied as additional tax had been calculated by aggregating interest on tax avoided at the rate of 10% per annum to 13 February 1983 and at 20% per annum thereafter until 3 August 1985; and a further 40% of tax avoided as a culpability factor.
  2. Subsequent enquiries through the Registry indicated that the totals were calculated by the Commissioner as follows:

10% pa to 13.2.1983 $ 63.15 -

20% pa to 3.8.1985 $1,330.81 $ 680.23

40% of tax avoided $1,080.64 $ 921.28

Total $2,475.10 $1,601.51

  1. I observe that according to my calculations 40% of $2,865.60 is $1,146.24, not $1,080.64. Similarly in the following year 40% of $2,692.21 is equal to $1,076.88, not $921.28.
  2. In other reasons for decision (handed down today) I considered the principles to be applied in approaching the task of reviewing determinations by the Commissioner as to the remission of additional tax. I seek; to apply those concepts in relation to these references.

17. A had no talent for matters of law, business or accountancy. He utilised the services of a tax agent, an accountant - not his present accountant - and had taken advice from that person on the course of action to be followed in face of pressure from the bank manager. It was, of course, open to A and to the company to act in accordance with the bank manager's advice, at least to the limit of the loan account, subject to any considerations which might have come to be relevant in the event of the company becoming insolvent, a condition which probably already existed. Whether he was incorrectly advised, or whether he misunderstood the advice he was given, I do not know. Nor can I find any basis in the evidence for understanding what appears to have been done after the close of each financial year in "adjusting" downwards the wages of A or in "adjusting" the amounts recorded as having been remitted to the Commissioner in relation to PAYE deductions. Whatever may be the position as to that A, acted openly in disclosing to his accountants the information relevant to his income tax return. It seems probable that the error then made - assuming it to he an error - was that they misunderstood the law, overrating the capacity of accountancy in thinking that accounting entries could retrospectively create events, rather than only record the events of the past. There is no suggestion of any prior avoidance of tax and, in my judgment, no need for the application of any particular deterrent. The error was as much occasioned by PBCO as by A, and presented the Commissioner with an election to recover against either A or, the Commissioner's statutory agent, PBCO. Presumably the Commissioner elected to recover from A because PBCO was in liquidation. On the other hand, in this instance A was the person who had complete control of PBCO, his employer. In that sense A did not involve any other person in the understatement which was made. On the other hand accounting records were altered.

18. In the view I have formed the culpability factor at the normative maximum applied by the Commissioner in the absence of aggravating circumstances (cf my reasons for decision already referred to) is far too high. I consider a figure of the order of 25% appropriate. In addition I make some allowance for the severity of the "compensatory rate" of interest applied by the Commissioner.

19. I would reduce additional tax from $2,475 to $2,000 for the year of income ended 30 June 1982 and from $1,601 to $1,200 in the year of income ended 30 June 1983. Measured against the pre-Guideline standards, the additional tax imposed for incorrect returns amounts to nearly 70% and nearly 45% of tax avoided.
