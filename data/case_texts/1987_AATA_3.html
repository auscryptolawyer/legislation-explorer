WT86/4380 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 3; 87 ATC 192 (9 January 1987)

Last Updated: 8 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - assessable income - dividend issued by way of distribution from capital reserve company to fully pay up shares of director - effect of late attempt to reverse this course of action.

Income Tax - penalty - "culpability factor" - consideration of Commissioner's Guidelines on basic penalty rate.

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) ss 6, 44, 226,251M

FC of T v Trautwein [1936] HCA 48; (1936) 56 CLR 211

DECISION AND REASONS

Re: WT86/4380

APPLICANT And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No. WT86/4380

AAT Decision No: 3152

Date: 9th January 1987

Place: Brisbane

Dr P Gerber, Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

)

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re: WT86/4380

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The applicant, now retired, was formerly a director and shareholder of HR Pty Ltd. On 5th December 1980, an unsigned minute of a (fictitious) meeting of directors recorded the following:

CALL ON SHARES: Resolved that a call of 40 cents per share be made on 9,332 ordinary shares held by Mr HR.

Resolved that a call of 90 cents per share be made on 13,668 ordinary shares held by Mr HR.

DISTRIBUTION OF Resolved that $31,034 of the amount standing to the Capital CAPITAL RESERVE: Reserve be distributed to share holders as follows:

  1. Towards Uncalled Liability –

$3,732.80 to Mr. H.R. in satisfaction of a call of 40 cents per share on 9,332 ordinary shares. $12,301.20 to Mr HR in satisfaction of a call of 90 cents per share on 13,668 ordinary shares. The Secretary was instructed to alter the relevant share scripts.

2. New Share Issue -

By the issue of 3,793 ordinary shares of $1.00 to Mr HR.

By the issue of 6,207 ordinary shares of $1.00 to Mr HR.

The Secretary was instructed to draw up the necessary share scripts.

This document was prepared by the firm's accountants. In due course, it was lodged with the Corporate Affairs Office (WA) together with a Statement Containing Particulars of Shares Allotted Otherwise Than for Cash (Exhibit B) which records an allotment of 15,000 ordinary ($1) shares in HR Pty Ltd to Mr and Mrs HR pursuant to clause 110 of the company's Articles of Association. The applicant, who held a number of shares which were only partly paid, was credited by the company in the amount of $16,034. This had the effect of making his share holding fully paid. Pausing here, what was done was to distribute from the capital profits reserve of the company, a sufficient amount to convert the partly paid shares held by the applicant to fully paid, shares, thus extinguishing what would otherwise have been a liability or debt owed by the applicant to the company. In the company's Profit and Loss Statement attached to its 1981 return, the amount of $31,034 is shown as "transfer from Capital Reserve" with the subsequent notation:-

CAPITAL DISTRIBUTION

During the year, $31,034 was transferred from capital reserve and distributed to members by way of ordinary shares and paying calls on ordinary share capital."

2. If no more were known, one must conclude that the company had paid its shareholder HR a dividend in the sum of $16,034, made up of $3,732.80 (40 cents per share on 9,332 ordinary shares) and $12,301.20 (90 cents per share on 13,668 ordinary-shares). Section 44(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) provides that "the assessable income of a shareholder in a company shall..... include dividends paid to him by the company out of profits derived by it from any source". "Dividend" is defined to include:-

(a) any distribution made by a company to any of its shareholders, whether in money or other property;

(b) any amount credited by a company to any of its shareholders; and

(c) the paid-up value of shares issued by a company to any of its shareholders to the extent to which the paid-up value represents a capitalization of profits. (cf sec 6[1]).

3. It was not until some time in 1994 and as a result of some requisitions made by the respondent relating to the resolution authorizing the transfer from the company's capital reserve, that the resultant tax implications for the applicant were fully appreciated by the company's accountants and an attempt made to "reverse" what had been done. Thus a fresh minute of a director's meeting was prepared, again dated 5th December 1980 and signed by HR as Chairman (who had resigned from the company in 1981) which stated, inter alia:-

CALL ON SHARES: Resolved that a call of 40 cents per share be made on 9,332 ordinary shares held by Mr HR. Resolved that a call of 90 cents per share be made on 13,668 ordinary shares held by Mr HR.

DISTRIBUTION OF Resolved that $15,000 of the amount standing to the Capital

CAPITAL RESERVE: Reserve be distributed to the shareholders as follows:-

1. New Share Issue

By the issue of 8,793 ordinary shares of $1.00 to Mr HR.

By the issue of 6,207 ordinary shares of $1.00 to Mr HR.

The Secretary was instructed to draw up the necessary share scripts.

To complete the attempted "reversal" of what had been done, the company "required" the applicant to repay the amount of $16,034. Meanwhile, the applicant had sold some of the by now fully paid shares to a son and a dividend was declared in 1983 on the basis that the shares were fully paid.

4. What then is the legal effect of all this? At the outset, it should be noted that not even the gods have the power to undo the past. The stark facts are that the company debited the applicant's loan account in the sum of $16,034 as a result: of which it treated his shares as fully paid. In fiscal terms, the crediting of the above amount constituted a "dividend" by definition and had the effect of extinguishing the applicant's liability to the company. This cannot be undone by a later re-payment of a like amount - whether voluntarily or by operation of law. It was argued on behalf of the applicant that the distribution was "unlawful" in that the minute purporting to authorize it recorded a fictitious event - there was no meeting, there were no directors present so that the whole thing was a nullity. Furthermore, it was submitted that the distribution was not in accordance with article 110 of the company's articles of association. I am not persuaded that these arguments have any merit. Even assuming illegality, the illegal nature of a receipt cannot alter its taxability. Again, much comfort was sought from the fact that the sum credited was subsequently repaid under "compulsion". Just on what basis the company could have recovered the amount credited as a matter of law was not made clear. Even assuming that it had a cause of action, this cannot affect the only question before me: Was the sum credited in 1981 a "dividend"? Once that is answered in the affirmative, an inchoate obligation to make restitution cannot alter the character of the receipt.

  1. No evidence was adduced or argument advanced that the amount credited from the company's capital reserve was other than a payment out of revenue profits. In the circumstances, I must conclude that the requirements of Section 44(1)(a) have been satisfied.
  2. The only remaining question relates to penalty. Briefly, the tax position can be set out as follows:-

(i) additional tax due $6,821.80;

(ii) interest (10% from due date of original assessment to 13th February 1983 and thence at 20% until notice of assessment) $2,308.19;

(iii) additional tax pursuant to Section 226 levied at 40% of the tax avoided $2,728.72.

I have no quarrel with the interest charge. On the penalty component, it was frankly conceded that the 40% was levied after much reflection, including the lack of co-operation of the applicant's accountants – in accordance with the guidelines enshrined in IT 2012. This guideline recommends a minimum figure of 40% in all cases of non-voluntary disclosures of assessable income. This is frequently referred to as the "culpability" component. However, I find this term singularly unhelpful. "Culpa" can range from magna culpa to culpa levissima. The guideline suggests that the basic penalty rate (ie 40%) should not be reduced beyond the basic rate merely because a taxpayer claims the understated income was the result of carelessness, ignorance as to liability to tax, or the fault of an agent. The extenuating circumstances which may be pleaded by the applicant in this case are the fact that at the end of the day, he is not one whit better off, that he was wholly guided by the company's (and his) tax agent was ignorant of tax law and had no intention to understate his taxable income. In any event, he could have achieved a comparable benefit by way of a retiring allowance. On the other hand, I am mindful of what Evatt J said in FC of T v Trautwein (No 3) [1936] HCA 48; (1936) 56 CLR 211, 217:-

"The object of the section is to impose a heavy penalty so as to ensure the accuracy of returns, upon which the whole income tax system of the Commonwealth is based."

The above observation, in conjunction with Section 251M, which, while not exonerating a taxpayer from his tax liability, nevertheless provides that where he becomes liable to pay additional tax as a result of the negligence of a registered tax agent, he has recourse against that agent, who "shall be liable to pay to the taxpayer the amount of additional tax, and that amount may be sued for an recovered by the taxpayer in any court of competent jurisdiction." Whether of not this applicant can recover the imposed penalty from his agent is for someone else to decide. However, the fact that a taxpayer who is subject to strict liability for the fault of an agent has a right of indemnity, is a matter which I consider relevant in determining the reasonableness of the amount of the penalty imposed. In all the circumstances, I have not been persuaded to interfere with the amount of the penalty the respondent has imposed in this case.

7. The decision under review is affirmed.

This and the preceding seven pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons for Decision of Dr P Gerber, Senior Member in the matter of WT86/4380.

Date: 9 January 1987
