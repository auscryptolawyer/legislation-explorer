ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - multiple trusts - whether discretion under section 7A should be exercised in favour of trusts not entirely independent of each other

Income Tax (Rates) Act 1976 ss.6E, 6F, 7A

Case U111 (1987) 87 ATC 667

Case U157 (1987) 87 ATC 912

Case V 4 (1987) 88 ATC 123

REASONS FOR DECISION

6 May 1988 Mrs R.A. Balmford, Senior Member

  1. This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent disallowing an objection to an assessment in respect of the year ended 30 June 1979, treating a sum of $783 as income received by Mr B, (as trustee of the B Family Trust), under a separate trust for MB, who was born on 7 November 1975.
  2. The notice of objection, dated 26 June 1980, was disallowed on 24 July 1980. On 18 September 1980 the trustee, by his agent, requested that the decision be referred to a Board of Review. By virtue of section 223(1) of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 and sub-section 189(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") that referral of the decision on the objection is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision.
  3. Evidence was given at the hearing by Mr B, who represented the trust. The respondent was represented by Mrs Moshinsky of counsel. An agreed statement of facts and supporting documents were lodged with the Tribunal.

4. The relevant legislative provisions at the relevant time were sub-sections 6E(4), 6F(1) and 7A of the Income Tax (Rates) Act 1976 which read as follows:

"6E(4) The rates of tax payable by a trustee in pursuance of section 98 or 99 of the Assessment Act are as set out in Schedule 12."

"6F(1) Subject to section 7A, where -

(a) the trustee of a trust estate (other than the estate of a deceased person) is liable to be assessed under section 98 of the Assessment Act in respect of a share of the net income of the trust estate to which a beneficiary under the age of 16 years on the last day of the year of income is presently entitled; and

(b) the amount of that share of the net income does not exceed $1040,

no tax is payable under sub-section 6E(4) in respect of that share of the net income."

"7A(1) Subject to sub-section (2) of this section, sub-section 6C(1) or (2), sub-section 6F(1) or (2) or sub-section 6J(1) or (2) does not apply in relation to a trustee of a trust estate who is liable to be assessed and to pay tax in pursuance of section 98 of the Assessment Act in respect of the share of the net income to which a beneficiary under the age of 16 years on the last day of the year of income is presently entitled if -

(a) the beneficiary was presently entitled to a share of the net income of the year of income of one or more other trust estates of the kind referred to in paragraph (a) of that sub-section; and

(b) the sum of the amounts of the shares of the beneficiary of the net incomes of the first-mentioned trust estate and that other trust estate or those other trust estates exceeds $1040.

7A(2) Sub-section (1) does not apply in relation to a share of the net income of a trust estate to which a beneficiary under the age of 16 years on the last day of the year of income is presently entitled if the Commissioner is of the opinion that it would be unreasonable that that sub-section should apply in relation to that share of the net income.

7A(3) In forming an opinion for the purposes of sub-section (2) in relation to the share of the net income of a trust estate to which a beneficiary under the age of 16 years on the last day of the year of income is presently entitled -

(a) the Commissioner shall have regard to the circumstances in which and the conditions (if any) upon which, at any time, property (including money) was acquired by or lent to the trust estate, income was derived by the trust estate, benefits were conferred on the trust estate or special rights or privileges were conferred on or attached to property of the trust estate, whether or not the rights or privileges have been exercised;

(b) if a person who has, at any time, directly or indirectly -
  • (i) transferred or lent any property (including money) to, or conferred any benefits on, the trust estate; or
  • (ii) (ii) conferred or attached any special right or privilege, or done any act or thing, either alone or together with another person or other persons, that has resulted in the conferring or attaching of any special right or privilege, on or to property of the trust estate, whether or not the right or privilege has been exercised.

has not, at any time, directly or indirectly -

(iii) transferred or lent any property (including money) to, or conferred any benefits on, another trust estate being a trust estate in which the beneficiary is or was a beneficiary; or

(iv) conferred or attached any special right or privilege, or done any act or thing, either alone or together with another person or other persons, that has resulted in the conferring or attaching of any special right or privilege on or to property of another trust estate, being a trust estate in which the beneficiary is or was a beneficiary, whether or not the right or privilege has been exercised,

the Commissioner shall have regard to that fact;

(c) if, in relation to the year of income, the Commissioner has formed the. opinion mentioned in sub-section (2) in relation to a share of the net income of another trust estate to which the beneficiary is presently entitled, the Commissioner shall have regard to the fact that he formed that opinion; and

(d) the Commissioner shall have regard to such other matters (if any) as he thinks fit."

5. In the income year in question, MB received an allocation of the net income of the Trust in the amount of $783. Also paid to her in that year by the Trust was an amount of $874 described as "interest". It is to be presumed, although this was not expressly stated in evidence, that that interest was paid on an amount of $12,533 described in the books of the Trust as an unsecured loan from MB: an amount derived, in part at least, from income of the trust.

  1. Mr B, at the hearing conceded that the latter amount was interest on an amount which was, in the relevant year, held by the trustee on trust for MB under a separate trust. That being so, MB was, in that year, presently entitled to a share of the net income of each of two trust estates. On the basis of the authorities on these provisions, commencing with Case U111 (1987) 87 ATC 667, I am satisfied that that was an appropriate concession.
  2. Thus, subject to the exercise by the Tribunal, standing as it does in the shoes of the Commissioner (see sub-section 43(1) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975) of the Commissioner's discretion under sub-section 7A(2), as the sum of $874 and $783 exceeds $1040, the trustee, in respect of MB's share of the income of the Trust, loses the benefit of sub-section 6F(1), that provision being overridden by sub-section 7A(1).
  3. The matters to which the Commission is to have regard in considering whether to exercise the discretion conferred by sub-section 7A(2) are set out at length in sub-section 7A(3). In Case U111 (supra) in which the Tribunal was constituted by the President, Davies J, his Honour said, at page 671, after noting that he had had regard to the matters set out in sub-section 7A(3):

"The manner in which the affairs of the trusts were managed, that is to say, the obtaining of a deduction totalling $5644 in respect of the two sums of interest for the F Family Trust and a distribution to each of DF and CF of only $1040, the total allowed by section 6F(1), appears to have been selected with a view to obtaining a favourable tax position for the F Family. As to this I offer no criticism, but it does not seem to be unreasonable that the normal taxation rates should apply to a situation so brought about. Furthermore, in a case such as this, it would not accord with the policy of section 6F(1), which limits the non-taxable trust income to a total of $1040 and of section 7A, which is designed to limit avoidance of tax through trust splitting, not to apply the provisions of section 7A(D."

  1. Mr B submitted, in reliance on paragraph 7A(3)(d), that the Tribunal should find that it would be unreasonable that sub-section 7A(2) should apply in respect of the amount of $783. He had considered at the time that it was sound commercial practice to pay interest at commercial rates on funds lent to the trust, particularly as MB was his daughter and was far too young to have any understanding of what was being done. In contrast, the Trust had not paid interest on moneys lent to it by his wife, who was an adult and could look after herself.
  2. He said that the purpose of the legislation was to stop the proliferation of trusts as a means of avoiding tax. However, the payment of interest was not an attempt to avoid tax by the proliferation of trusts, but was the continuation of a practice which he had followed in previous years.
  3. He referred to the Taxation Office memorandum of 7 December 1978 relating to the exercise of the discretion in sub-section 7A(2) and particularly to paragraph 24 thereof, which reads:

"24. In considering whether to exercise the discretion in these cases, regard should be had to the tax that would be payable if the related trust estate income had been derived from one trust estate. The discretion may be exercised in relation to one or more of the related trust estates in a manner that ensures that the aggregate tax payable by the trustees of the related trust estates is the lowest amount that is not less than the amount that would have been payable if the whole of that income were derived from one trust estate."

12. Mr B accepted, in cross-examination, that he had, at all relevant times, been receiving regular advice on taxation matters from his accountant, and that he would have been aware of "the rules of the game".

13. Mrs Moshinsky submitted that the discretion was only intended to be exercised in a case where, the two trust estates in question were entirely independent, for example, where they had been created by the beneficiary's grandparents on different sides of the family. She referred to the effectively identical provisions of sub-section 99A (3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. The Commissioner's memorandum was not binding on the Tribunal. The amount in issue was small, but this legislation was, in its nature, directed to small amounts. The applicant had made his choice as to the payment of the interest, and there was no reason for the Commissioner to waive the application of sub-section 7A(1).

14. Having considered this matter, I have formed the view that the matters relied on by the applicant are not sufficient to distinguish this case from earlier decisions of the Tribunal in which the application of sub-section 7A(2) has been considered, and the discretion has not been exercised. I would refer to Case U111 (supra) and also to Case U157 (1987) 87 ATC 912, Case V4 (1987) 88 ATC 123, and Case V27 (1987) 88 ATC 262. Accordingly, I am of the opinion that it would not be unreasonable that sub-section 7A (1) should apply in respect of the amount of $783, being MB's share of the income of the Trust in the year ended 30 June 1979. The objection decision under review will be affirmed.
