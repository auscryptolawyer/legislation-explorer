Re "S" and Commissioner of Taxation [1989] AATA 145 (16 June 1989)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: "S"

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. W88/120

AAT No. 5160

Freedom of Information

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

G.L. McDonald(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information - documents being part of an audit file currently the subject of an investigation carried out by the Commissioner of Taxation.

Freedom of Information Act 1982 s3, s37(1)(a), s37(2)(b), s40(1)(c),(d),(e), s40(2), s55

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 s35

Attorney-General's Department and Another v Cockcroft (1986) 64 ALR 97

National Companies and Securities Commission v News Corporation Ltd and Others [1984] HCA 29; (1984) 52 ALR 417

Re AB and Australian Taxation Office (1986) 10 ALD N249

Re Z and Australian Taxation Office (1984) 6 ALD 673

Re Mann and Australian Taxation Office (1985) 7 ALD 698

Re Lander and Australian Taxation Office 85 ATC 4674

HEARING

PERTH

16:6:1989

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

The applicant, who is a financial consultant and a tax agent, is applying under the provisions of s55 of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the FOI Act") for a review of a decision of the respondent refusing access to some of the documents which form an audit file relating to an investigation currently being carried out by the respondent into the applicant's tax affairs.

2. The applicant initially applied to the respondent for the release of the documents on 15 April 1988. Access was granted to some of the documents and refused in relation to others. On 20 May 1988 the applicant requested a review of that decision and as a result further documents were released. The applicant has applied to the Tribunal for a release of the remainder of the documents.

3. The applicant represented himself. The respondent was represented by Miss P Harrison.

4. The respondent claimed that the documents were variously exempt under the provisions of s37(1)(a), s37(2)(b), and s40(1)(c)(d)(e). In relation to some of the documents, the respondent claims only a partial exemption and has, after making the deletion, made those documents, save where the deletions may render the documents misleading, available to the applicant.

5. The Tribunal was provided with a copy of the documents which were contained in two files for which exemption was claimed. An order under s35 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 was made with respect to those documents pending the outcome of this hearing.

6. The applicant gave evidence on his own behalf. The respondent filed an affidavit of Mr M Boyd, the Principal Appeals Review Officer of the Appeals and Review Branch of the Perth Office of the Taxation Department. Additionally, Mr Boyd gave evidence at the hearing. The applicant, on 11 April 1989 requested the respondent to call 16 employees and former employees. The respondent declined to comply with that request and, as a result, the Tribunal convened a directions hearing the day before the hearing (14 April 1989). At the directions hearing, the applicant said the number of witnesses he now required the respondent to have present at the hearing was 5. The respondent resisted that application and the Tribunal reserved consideration of this aspect to the hearing. At the hearing the applicant renewed his request that 4 of the remaining witnesses be called, given that Mr Boyd had given evidence and was one of the 5 whom the applicant wished to call. The applicant was unaware of the names of 3 of the 5 witnesses whom he wished called. The Tribunal declined his request for reasons which will become apparent in the course of this decision.

7. An applicant does not need to assert a reason to establish a right for access to documents. The object of the FOI Act is expressed in broad terms in s3(1) as follows:

"S3(1) The object of this Act is to extend as far

as possible the right of the Australian community

to access to information in the possession of the

Government of the Commonwealth by -

(a) making available to the public information

about the operations of departments and

public authorities and, in particular,

ensuring that rules and practices

affecting members of the public in their

dealings with departments and public

authorities are readily available to

persons affected by those rules and

practices; and

(b) creating a general right of access to

information in documentary form in the

possession of Ministers, departments and

public authorities, limited only by

exceptions and exemptions necessary for

the protection of essential public

interests and the private and business

affairs of persons in respect of whom

information is collected and held by

departments and public authorities".

8. An exemption from access is only to be granted where it falls within the categories nominated in the FOI Act. In this case, the relevant sections under which exemption is claimed are (with emphasis added):

S37(1) A document is an exempt document if

its disclosure under this Act would, or could

reasonably be expected to -

(a) prejudice the conduct of an investigation

of a breach, or possible breach, of the

law, or a failure, or possible failure,

to comply with a law relating to taxation

or prejudice the enforcement or proper

administration of the law in a particular

instance;

S37(2) A document is an exempt document if

its disclosure under this Act, would, or could

reasonably be expected to -

(b) disclose lawful methods or procedures for

preventing, detecting, investigating, or

dealing with matters arising out of,

breaches or evasions of the law the

disclosure of which, would, or would be

reasonably likely to, prejudice the

effectiveness of those methods or

procedures;

S40(1) Subject to sub-section (2), a document is

an exempt document if its disclosure under this

Act would, or could reasonably be expected to -

(c) have a substantial adverse effect on the

management or assessment of personnel by

the Commonwealth or by an agency;

(d) have a substantial adverse effect on the

proper and efficient conduct of the

operations of an agency; or

(e) have a substantial adverse effect on the

conduct by or on behalf of the Commonwealth

or an agency of industrial relations.

9. The expressions which I have emphasised are to be given their ordinary meaning and require a decision as to whether it is reasonable as distinct from "irrational, absurd or ridiculous" to expect the effects nominated in the sub-sections to occur if exemption is not granted (re Attorney-General's Department and Another v Cockcroft (1986) 64 ALR 97 particularly Bowen CJ and Beaumont J at pg 106). The reasons of any one applicant are relevant, if at all, only in as far as they may be used to test "reasonableness" under ss37 and 40 of the FOI Act. To be relevant, those reasons are to be viewed in the context that a decision to grant an exemption should be made as if disclosure were to be at large as anticipated by the words used in s3(1) . . . . "right of the Australian community", and 3(1)(b) "general right of access". The reasons of any one applicant, for instance as here, that the department is conducting some sort of vendetta against him will rarely be a relevant consideration for the Tribunal to take into account in determining whether or not an exemption should be granted. There are other more appropriate forums in which this type of complaint can be pursued e.g. to the Ombudsman.

10. The context in which the reasonableness or otherwise of each of the exemptions claimed should be viewed is that of a current and ongoing audit of the applicant's taxation concerns. As Mr Boyd pointed out in paragraph 5 of his affidavit:

"5 The documents have been assembled in the

course of an investigation undertaken by

the Commissioner of the affairs of the

Applicant. The investigation is still in

progress and it is expected to be some

time before it is completed".

11. If disclosure were to occur, Mr Boyd submitted in paragraph 8 of his affidavit:

"8 If the extent and results of inquiries made by

the Commissioner were disclosed prior to the

conclusion of the overall investigation, it is

possible, and indeed could reasonably be

expected, that a person connected with the

investigation may frame responses to queries

which do no more than remain consistent with

information which he knows already to be in the

possession of the ATO. Similarly, the direction

of inquiries would be revealed, enabling a

person to anticipate questions and supply

answers which would frustrate the investigation

by diverting or misleading the investigating

officer. Disclosure would also make a person

aware not only of how much information the

Commissioner possesses but of how much he has

not acquired, thereby facilitating concealment

of relevant facts and implementation of

counter-measures".

12. The Tribunal accepts these as valid concerns. As was said in the National Companies and Securities Commission v News Corporation Ltd and Others [1984] HCA 29; (1984) 52 ALR 417 at 437 (Mason, Wilson and Dawson JJ):

"It is the very nature of an investigation that

the investigator proceeds to gather relevant information

from as wide a range of sources as possible without the

suspect looking over his shoulder all the time to see how

the inquiry is going. For an investigator to disclose

his hand prematurely will not only alert the suspect to

the progress of the investigation but may well close off

other sources of inquiry."

13. The Tribunal has consistently adhered to this view (see Re AB and Australian Taxation Office (1986) 10 ALD N249).

14. Against this background the documents under consideration divide into the following categories each of which will be considered for exemption:

Working papers generated by departmental officers

including file notes, reports and inquiries including searches and records of and into the applicant's affairs;

Documents in which departmental officers are named.

PAPERS GENERATED BY DEPARTMENTAL OFFICERS INCLUDING FILE NOTES, REPORTS AND

INQUIRIES INCLUDING SEARCHES AND RECORDS OF AND INTO THE APPLICANT'S AFFAIRS

15. Exemption has been claimed under s37(1)(a) and s37(2)(b) for documents falling within this category. The exemption is sought in the context of a current tax audit of the applicant's affairs. S37(1)(a) clearly contemplates the type of investigation which is currently being undertaken. The disclosure of the steps taken by the respondent could, for the reasons set out in paragraph 8 of Mr Boyd's affidavit, reasonably prejudice that audit during the course of the audit and, if applicable, to any proceedings which may follow it. In that context, even the disclosure of documents which may otherwise be available either through a public register and/or to the applicant as being documents generated by him to the respondent, may qualify for exemption. In this case, the Tribunal finds that they do qualify for exemption because their disclosure would, or could reasonably be expected to, lead to prejudice by alerting a party of a particular line of enquiry being pursued.

16. Equally, the release of documents individually or in groups would, or could reasonably be expected to, prejudice the effectiveness of the methods or procedures adopted by the respondent in its audit and therefore those documents qualify for exemption under s37(2)(b).

17. The situation in this case is different from that in Re Lander 85 ATC 4674. There, an investigation resulting in amended assessments and the imposition of penalties had been completed along with the hearing before the Board of Review prior to the applicant making a request under the Freedom of Information Act. Mr Boyd, in his evidence to the Tribunal, rightly said differing considerations may well apply to the claims for exemption of documents after the completion of an audit that apply while an audit remains under active consideration and uncompleted.

18. For the above reasons, the documents enumerated in Schedule 1 are granted exemption under either or both of s37(1)(a) or s37(2)(b).

DOCUMENTS IN WHICH DEPARTMENTAL OFFICERS ARE NAMED

19. The names and/or initials which may identify Australian Tax officers (not holding delegations from the Commissioner) appear on a number of the documents. The applicant told the Tribunal that, in his capacity as a tax agent, he knew the names of a number of tax officers employed by the respondent and that his learning of the names of others would not or could not reasonably be expected to lead to the adverse effects alluded to in s40(1)(c), (d) and (e) of the FOI Act. The Tribunal adopts what was said in Re Z and Australian Taxation Office (1984) 6 ALD 673, Re Mann and Australian Taxation Office (1985) 7 ALD 698 at 708-11 and Re Lander and Australian Taxation Office 85 ATC 4674 at 4677. I consider that disclosure of the officers' names or initials which may lead to them being identified would have the substantially adverse effects mentioned in s40(1)(c) and (d) and that exemption from the publication of those names and/or initials should be granted. In large part, the respondent has released the balance of the contents of the documents set out in Schedule 2 in which officers are named or their initials appear save and except where exemption is claimed for those documents under other sections of the FOI Act. The applicant obviously knows the names of some of the officers to whom he has spoken and those from whom he has received correspondence. Those and other names may be contained amongst some of the material which has been released to him. The knowledge of some of those names does not, where there is a claim for exemption, warrant the Tribunal in deciding all names are to be released. Each document must be assessed according to the principles set out in s40 of the FOI Act and a decision reached in relation to each one. I have proceeded to make an assessment on that basis. Exemption is granted to the documents mentioned in Schedule 2.

20. There are no considerations of public interest which would lead the Tribunal to conclude that disclosure of the officers' names would, on balance, be in the public interest pursuant to the provisions of s40(2) of the FOI Act. It has not been necessary for the Tribunal to make any finding under the provisions of s40(1)(e) of the FOI Act.

21. For the reasons expressed above the decision under review is affirmed.
