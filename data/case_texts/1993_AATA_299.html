Eric William Milroy Thomson and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland No: Q93/268 Aat [1993] AATA 299; (1993) 93 ATC 2159 (1993) 27 ATR 1001 (1993) 30 ALD 747 (29 September 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

ERIC WILLIAM MILROY THOMSON v. TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF

QUEENSLAND

No: Q93/268

AAT No. 9015

Number of pages - 5

Stay of Decision

(1993) 93 ATC 2159

(1993) 27 ATR 1001

(1993) 30 ALD 747

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

K.L. BEDDOE (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Stay of Decision: Tax agent - refusal to re-register - whether in public interest to stay decision - application refused

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975: Ss. 41(2)

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936: Ss. 251A, 251BC, 251JC

Re Dart and Director-General of Social Services (1982) 4 ALD 553

Re Dekanic and Tax Agents' Board of NSW (1982) 6 ALD 240, 82 ATC 4560

Re Fitzgibbon and Tax Agents' Board (Qld) 93 ATC 2053

Re Nelson and Tax Agents' Board 93 ATC 2145

HEARING

BRISBANE, 22 September 1993

29:9:1993

Solicitors for the Respondent: Mr M. Belcher

Australian Government

Solicitor

The Applicant appeared in person.

ORDER

The application for an order under section 41 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 is refused.

DECISION

K.L. BEDDOE The applicant seeks an order under sub-section 41(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("AAT Act").

2. Section 41 of the AAT Act provides that the Tribunal, including an authorised senior member, if of the opinion that it is desirable to do so after taking into account the interests of any persons who may be affected by the review, make an order or orders staying or otherwise affecting the operation or implementation of the decision as considered appropriate for the purpose of securing the effectiveness of the hearing and determination of the application for review.

3. Sub-section 251JC(4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ACT") provides in effect that where a Tax Agents' Board makes a decision refusing to re-register an applicant as a tax agent and notice of the Board's decision was served on the applicant at a time after the expiry of the registration, then the applicant shall be taken to have been registered as a tax agent during the period commencing at the time the notice expired and the date notice of the Board's decision was served on the applicant.

4. By letter dated 28 April 1993 the Secretary of the Tax Agents' Board notified the applicant that his application for renewal of registration as a tax agent made on 13 March 1992 had been refused by the Board.

5. The applicant subsequently sought review of that decision in this Tribunal and now seeks a stay of the Board's decision.

6. At the hearing of the stay application the applicant appeared on his own behalf and Mr Belcher of the Australian Government Solicitor's office appeared for the respondent. The hearing was conducted by telephone.

7. The Board refused re-registration under section 251JC of the Act on the basis that the applicant was not a fit and proper person for registration as a tax agent. In the section 37 AAT Act statement the Board gave its reasons for refusing re-registration as follows:

"In reaching its decision the Board considered the reports

(Documents T4 and 5) together with the application. The reports

indicated to the Board that Mr Thomson had been convicted of

serious taxation offences as defined in S251A of the Income Tax

Assessment Act. These convictions were for breaches of Section

8C of the Taxation Administration Act, on 4 February 1988 and

4 May 1989, and convictions for breaches of Section 221F(5)(a)

of the Income Tax Assessment Act on 7 August 1991 and 20 April

1993.

The Board noted that on his application for re-registration Mr

Thomson answered "no" to question 15, which asks whether the

applicant has ever been convicted of any offence (including any

offence under the Income Tax Assessment Act or Taxation

Administration Act), against the law of Australia, a State,

Territory or other country. At the time of applying for

re-registration Mr Thomson was required to advise the Board in his

application of the convictions of 4 February 1988, 4 May 1989

and 7 August 1991, and he failed to do this.

The Board noted that as a result of the conviction of 20 April

1993 for breaches of section 221F(5)(a) of the Income Tax

Assessment Act, Mr Thomson was sentenced to 6 months

imprisonment.

In considering the re-registration application the Board was

required to consider, pursuant to S251JC(1), whether Mr Thomson

was a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and

transact business on behalf of taxpayers.

The Board considered whether Mr Thomson was such a fit and

proper person in terms of S251BC of the Income Tax Assessment

Act.

The Board concluded that Mr Thomson fell within the ambit of

S251BC(1)(e) and (f) in that he:

(e) had been convicted of a serious taxation offence during the

previous five years;

and additionally

(f) was a person under sentence of imprisonment for a serious

taxation offence.

The Board had discretion, under S251BC(3), to disregard the

convictions of 4 February 1988, 4 May 1989 and 7 August 1991,

which did not give rise to custodial sentences, however, the

Board did not find it necessary to exercise this discretion.

Further, the Board did not find it necessary to consider the

implications of the false answer given in relation to his

convictions.

The Board noted that it had no discretion under S251BC(3)(b) to

disregard the conviction of 20 April 1993 which resulted in the

sentence of imprisonment and accordingly refused Mr Thomson's

application."

In my view the Board was wrong in describing the conviction of 4 February 1988 as a serious taxation offence as defined.

8. The facts relied upon by the Board were not disputed before me. Mr Thomson drew attention to the fact that the conviction of 20 April 1993 has been the subject of appeal proceedings with the Court's decision reserved.

9. As to the non-disclosure of the earlier convictions on the application by answering "no" to the question "Have you ever been convicted of any offence.. . against the law of Australia, a State, Territory or other Country?". Mr Thomson said the application for registration, although signed by him, had been prepared by a member of his staff and that he was preoccupied with other matters at the time.

10. In my view Mr Thomson was required to disclose all of his convictions and he did not do so.

11. The question I have to decide is not whether the applicant is entitled to re-registration as a tax agent and it would be wrong to construe these reasons as in any way reflecting on the ultimate decision to be made by the Tribunal. This is not a preliminary trial of the issues (Re Dart and Director-General of Social Services (1982) 4 ALD 553 at 555.

12. In Re Dekanic and Tax Agents' Board of NSW 82 ATC 4560; (1982) 6 ALD 240, Davies J, as President of the Tribunal, dealt with the operation of section 41 in the context of a cancellation of a tax agent's registration. His Honour placed considerable emphasis on the right to be heard on a cancellation of registration, dicta which must apply with equal force on a refusal to renew a registration. Of more relevance to this matter is his Honour's conclusion at ATC page 4561; ALD page 242:

". . . it is the ordinary practice of the Tribunal in the

review of a cancellation of a tax agent's registration to grant

a stay if the Tribunal is asked to do so, and it grants that

stay until the hearing and the determination of the review."

and in the following paragraph his Honour said:

"That, as I see it then, is the basic approach to take in these

matters but, of course, in every particular case the

circumstances of the case must be considered and in not every

case will it be appropriate that a stay should be granted."

13. His Honour then went on to discuss the public interest in relation to the stay of a decision cancelling registration, and, taking public interest factors into account, his Honour stayed the decision to cancel registration subject to certain undertakings to be given by the applicant.

14. The present case is different to the factual basis in Dekanic if only because there was no suggestion in that case of convictions for serious tax offences. Also, this case involves a false statement in a declaration made to the Tax Agents' Board. I have previously dealt with such a case in Re Fitzgibbon and Tax Agents' Board (Qld) 93 ATC 2053.

15. Mr Thomson seeks the stay order so that he can complete outstanding work for clients. He did not say whether he also proposed to take on new work for his existing clients, nor did he say anything about new clients.

16. I have considered carefully whether a stay order should be made upon Mr Thomson undertaking to the Tribunal to limit his work as a tax agent to completion of partially completed work only. On balance I have decided that would not be an appropriate course because I am not satisfied that approach would be in the best interests of those clients. Nor am I satisfied that it would be in the public interest.

17. The material before me does not raise a prima facie case that the respondent Board was wrong in its refusal to re-register the applicant.

18. In these circumstances, given the material placed before the Board as to convictions and also taking into account the non-disclosure of those convictions, Ihave come to the conclusion that the public interest is the overriding factor and that until the Tribunal comes to a different conclusion on the merits of the case the Board's refusal to re-register the applicant should not be disturbed. In coming to that view I have not overlooked the fact that the Board allowed this applicant to continue to practise as a tax agent for over 12 months while it considered his application.

19. For these reasons an order staying the implementation of the Board's decision will be refused (c.f.Re Nelson and Tax Agents' Board 93 ATC 2145).
