Stage Sound Enterprises Pty Ltd and Revenue Control Brisbane [1978] AATA 48 (29 June 1978)

Last Updated: 14 March 2007

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No. 78/14008

Re: STAGE SOUND ENTERPRISES PROPRIETARY LIMITED

And: ASSISTANT COLLECTOR,

REVENUE CONTROL, BRISBANE

AAT Decision No 89

DECISION

The Tribunal decides to review a demand by the Assistant Collector, Revenue Control, Department of Business and Consumer Affairs, Brisbane for the payment of Customs Duty amounting to $140 in respect of amplifiers which arrived in Australia on 29 February 1976 and which were entered for home consumption and determines that the amplifiers were differential amplifiers and entitled to entry free of duty under By-law 7259560.

DATED this Twenty-ninth day of June 1978.

K.S. EDMUNDS

Senior Member

R.L. STOCK

Member

V.J. SKERMER

Member

REASONS FOR DECISION

The applicant imported a quantity of goods which arrived in Australia on 29 February, 1976, and were entered for home consumption. The goods were supplied by Albin Hagstrom Limited of Sweden and amongst the goods were two articles described on the entry for home consumption as amplifiers falling in sub-item 85.14.900 but free of duty under By-law 7259560.

On 9 April, 1976, a post note was issued claiming duty payable on the amplifiers at the rate of 35% under item 85.14.900 and amounting to $140 as the goods were not properly identified as differential amplifiers. This amount was paid by the applicant under protest pursuant to section 167 of the Customs Act.

The effect of the post note was to deny that the By-law referred to applied to the subject amplifiers. The goods in question are apparatus which take electrical impulses from a musical instrument and amplifies and transmits them to a loud speaker or speakers.

Chapter 85 of the Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act relates to "Electrical Machinery and Equipment; Parts Therefore", Item 85.14 reads "Microphones and stands therefore, loud speakers, audio frequency electric amplifiers". (Sub-item 85.14.100 - speech trainers with duty at general rates at 34% and preferential at 21% and 85.14.900 other, with a duty, both general and preferential, of 35%.

Consolidated by By-law reference B.L. 7259560 (which the applicant claimed authorised the admission of the subject goods for home consumption free of duty) allowed admission under Item 19 of Schedule 2 of the Customs Tariff Act of "Differential Amplifiers" under Tariff Reference 85.14.

The applicant claims the amplifiers which he imported and were included in the post note were "differential amplifiers" and following the payment under protest correspondence passed between the applicant and respondent with the applicant submitting opinions it had obtained and extracts from scientific journals which it claimed supported its contention. The respondent also obtained an opinion which it claimed supported its classification of the amplifiers for duty purposes.

On 21 March, 1978, the applicant by its agent applied to the Tribunal to review the decision of the respondent and stated as the grounds for its application -

"The goods in question may be described as 'differential amplifiers' on the terms of Tariff Item 85.14 and should be accorded the treatment offered by the Minister at the time of entry."

On the hearing of the application by the Tribunal in Brisbane Mr Christopher Kinman, the Managing Director of the applicant at the time of the importation of the goods , gave evidence and a number of documents were tendered through him, including on the notepaper of Albin Hagstrom Limited a statement headed "Certificate", which recited "This is to certify that all Hagstrom amplifiers are to be classified as 'differential amplifiers'" and bearing two signatures, one purportedly that of the Chief Engineer and the other the Export Manager. It is noted Mr Kinman stated he obtained this certificate prior to the importation of the goods. Other documents included a brochure with a photograph of the amplifier and an accompanying description of it which the Tribunal notes also lists the technical data of the amplifier without the word "differential" appearing. Another document tendered was the circuit diagram of the amplifier.

Mr Kinman, although not holding any technical qualifications, is engaged in the business of manufacturing musical instruments. He said it is not the practice in the audio equipment trade to describe equipment as a differential amplifier. His understanding of the term differential amplifier is that it is used a lot in high quality audio circuitry where they have differential stages throughout the building structure of the amplifier for reasons of upgrading quality and producing less distortion. The amplifier in question he would regard as a differential amplifier because the whole output is 100% differential. He said in cross-examination that the reason the goods were entered simply as amplifiers and not differential amplifiers was due to a mistake by the customs agent and in his instructions to them he had included the word "diff.".

Mr Kinman said the amplifiers were differential because of the power output stage. In his opinion the differential characteristics are derived from the configuration of the output modules. There is a differential stage before the power output commonly termed the voice splitter, which divides the single ended output into two out of phase components which in turn are fed to the individual amplifier modules which in turn have a single output each. The two of those outputs constitute a differential output. As a result the goods were differential output but not differential input amplifiers.

This witness said if the subject amplifiers did not have a differential function they would have been entered as audio frequency amplifiers and subject to duty at the rate of 35%. If an intending purchaser asked for an audio differential amplifier he would not receive anything different from the ones in question.

Dr Laurie Vaughan Skatterbol, a Senior Lecturer in electronic engineering in the University of Queensland, gave evidence. This witness obtained his Doctorate of Philosophy in electronic engineering and lectures to both undergraduate and post-graduate students. With the assistance of the circuit diagram, this witness illustrated the function of a section of the circuit which, in his opinion, gave the amplifier its differential aspect. He referred in particular to the paraphase amplifier – the paraphase splitter - the purpose of which is to change a single ended input into a differential output. He said pre-amplifiers are relatively easy to design as they work at low level. The biggest problem arises in actually getting power into the loud speaker so the design of the power amplifier is really one of the crucial points in amplifier design. Pre-amplifiers are used in home audio amplifying equipment. Differential types are used in very high quality equipment. Most home "hi-fi" units would be single ended output and double input.

Considering the circuit as a whole, the witness estimated that in terms of difficulty of construction and cost, the output section of the subject amplifier would, in terms of the whole, represent at least 30%.

Dr Skatterbol was referred to the definition appearing in the book written by Professor Giacoletto, titled "Differential Amplifiers". The witness said Professor Giacoletto was a well known writer on transistor circuitry. The book referred to is a text book and the writer of it was speaking in generalities. The writer said there are three types of differential amplifiers with differential inputs and differential outputs.

In answer to a question Dr Skatterbol said without the two differential type outputs that had been illustrated in the circuit, the balance of the circuit would be of a single-ended type and could be used as such.

The normal reason for a differential set like the one in question is for linearity, which reduces distortion.

In cross-examination, Dr Skatterbol explained in greater detail the function of the various stages in the circuit and the operation of some of its component parts.

The witness said that without reservation and after close study of the circuit, he considered the subject goods as imported are definitely within the category of differential amplifiers. Their common use would probably be as electric guitar amplifiers.

A witness, Mr John Crago, gave evidence. He is a technical officer with Telecom Australia with a background of employment for a number of years with technical, remote control scientific instrumentation equipment and general communication equipment and with a long time interest in musical equipment, electronics, tone audio electronics, constructing amplifiers and designing equipment to do with musical amplifiers.

Mr Crago said the subject goods had a differential output and fell within the classic textbook definition of a differential amplifier. His description of a differential amplifier corresponded with the descriptions of those instruments in Dr Giacoletto's book referred to above, namely that a differential amplifier can have a differential input and a single ended output or a differential input and a differential output or a single ended input as in the present case and a differential output. On a consideration of the circuit diagram, the differential stage is expensive by comparison with the rest of the circuit and is a significant part of the entire amplifier and the amplifier could not function without it. The practical effect of the inclusion of differential section is the reduction of distortion and of any interference carried into the power supply to the power amplifier.

Mr Crago also explained in some detail by using the circuit diagram the function of the various stages illustrated in the diagram and the function of the various components. It is only in the last few years that differential amplifiers have started to be applied to the audio amplifier field. Until a few years back, the only place a differential amplifier was found was in the scientific context.

The last witness called on behalf of the applicant was Mr Gregory Ball, a design engineer and manufacturer of audio equipment. He holds a science degree in physics and applied science and has lectured in electronics. He said that probably once a week he was asked to design a differential amplifier of one type or another. He had seen and studied the circuit diagram and would class a piece of equipment represented by that diagram in the category of a differential amplifier having a differential output. The fact that it has a differential output is enough to classify it as a differential amplifier. The power amplifier stage is internally complex and the cost of it on a percentage basis to the total cost of the amplifier would be approximately 30%. This witness also described the function of some of the components parts of the amplifier.

The respondent called one witness, Professor Morris William Gunn, the Professor of electrical engineering at the University of Queensland. Professor Gunn, amongst other academic distinctions, holds a Doctorate in Philosophy in Electrical Engineering and has held the professorial appointment in electrical engineering in Queensland since 1968, and before that was a Professor of Electrical Engineering in the Gill University in Canada. Professor Gunn was asked to define the essential features of a differential amplifier and he said:

"An amplifier is an electronic device to amplify signals. To make them larger or make them greater and a differential amplifier in the way I would normally view it is an amplifier that amplifies the difference between two signals, the differential between two signals. So, my definition of a differential amplifier is an amplifier that in its input stages amplifies the difference between the signals. Its function is to make great the difference between two signals at its input."

In a comment on the description of differential amplifiers as given by earlier witnesses, Professor Gunn said: ".... All that I could comment on there is that if you look at any modern textbook in electronics and look in the index under differential amplifier, you would invariably be led to a circuit diagram that deals with the input stage of the amplifier. It is an amplifier that amplifies the difference between two input signals."

Later he said, "Again it is a matter of definition. We have the three definitions that have been raised today. My definition - I would certainly put the two inputs as the key point of a differential amplifier." In relation to the subject amplifier, a question was put to Professor Gunn and his answer was as follows:

"If you define the amplifier as the complete circuitry on the sheet from the input terminal to the output terminal, yes, there is no doubt about it. Again what has been said before by Dr Skatterbol with this output stage with the two input leads to it, that is working differentially and that is working as a differential amplifier. Mr Skermer: In your view it is not a differential amplifier as a whole? As a whole, if one defines the whole circuit from input terminals to the output terminals in my view, no, because again, defining a differential amplifier, is one that amplifies a difference in voltage in the input."

This witness said he did not know what would be accepted by people in the trade, by people who manufacture those items and who deal in them wholesale and retail and whether they would accept Professor Giacoletto's broader understanding of the term or the restricted definition adopted by him. He agreed with other witnesses that the stage in the circuit which they had accepted as being the stage giving the differential output is a most important stage on the question of cost and would represent 30% of the total cost.

In cross-examination Professor Gunn's attention was drawn to pencil alterations to the circuit diagram and was asked if in fact a differential input at that stage would conform to his definition. The Professor answered yes - that that would then be a part of the whole circuit and you could apply voltage and amplify the difference. It would be differential amplifier that you could attach an input to and amplify the difference between the two inputs.

In connexion with this last answer, the Tribunal notes that a consideration of the transcript of the proceedings makes it uncertain if the witness knew the exact state of the subject amplifier and if the pencilled additions formed part of the circuit diagram. A reference to Mr Kinman's evidence indicate that the original circuit drawings were prepared in 1972. The pencilled additions shows an external connexion to gain access to the power amplifier section of the whole unit and that external connexion was in fact on the goods when imported.

The Tribunal finds that the subject goods were clearly amplifiers and the question is do they meet a description of a differential amplifier within the meaning of the By-law reference 7259560. When the applicant's custom agent entered the goods for home consumption, he did not include the word "differential" as a description of the amplifier and it was this omission which probably led to the issue of the post note.

The consolidated By-law includes references to several other types of amplifiers in addition to "differential amplifiers" all within Tariff Item 85.14, but in each of those other cases the types are described with some particularity - for example, the type listed immediately below "differential amplifiers" are described as:

"85.14: Dual channel amplifiers with total output 420 W rms into 8 ohm and 800 W rms into 4 ohm, slewing rate 8 V/micro sec, hum and noise (20-20 kHz) 100 db below 150 W rms output, frequency plus or minus 0.1 db 0-20 kHz at 1 W into 8 ohm plus or minus 0.6 db 0-100 kHz (Op. 1.1.71 - BL 7259561 - W2362/69)." The By-law relating to differential amplifiers had effect from 1 January, 1971, and although Professor Gunn's evidence is that modern writers consider differential input voltage signals are essential for an amplifier to be described as "differential", it would appear that in 1970 Professor Giacoletto's three definitions had validity even if two of them may today have lost significance in some academic circles. The inference from this is that the By-law when made was intended to cover any type of amplifier which could be described as a differential type.

Whatever may have been meant by the description when the By-law was made, the Tribunal's function is to ascertain if amplifiers of the type imported in this case fell within the By-law description on the date of importation.

Reference to standard english dictionaries are of no assistance in a case such as this as "differential amplifiers" are not defined. The Brussels Custom Co-operation Council Explanatory Notes under the heading of Audio-Frequency Amplifiers do not refer to differential amplifiers.

The certificate given by the manufacturers that the goods are classified as differential amplifiers while of some value is not to be preferred to the direct evidence of expert witnesses.

Professor Giacoletto defines a differential amplifier as "a collection of passive and active components structured to preferentially amplify differential signals while suppressing accompanying common signals". This definition would appear more restrictive than the three types the Professor described later in his book and which were adopted by all witnesses other than Professor Gunn.

There is further reference to definitions of differential amplifiers which was before the decision-maker and supplied with documents to the Tribunal by the respondent under section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act. The definitions were noted as being from a transcription of a written opinion given by Mr J South of Telecom. This person was not called as a witness and his opinions therefore were not tested in evidence. His first opinion was;

"A differential amplifier is one which has 2 inputs and one output. In phase signals on the input leads are amplified to a much lesser degree than are out-of-phase signals. Generally a diff amp is very small (1/2 inch wide)."Then came the following statement:

"Larger amplifiers may incorporate differential amplifiers as individual components in a multi-component structure but the amplifier as a whole should not be classified as a differential amplifier unless
(a)The basic requirements of suppressing in-phase input signals is present at the external terminals of the amplifier;
(b)That part of the amplifier associated with the differential function is a significant (30%?) part of the entire amplifier.".

From the photographic illustration of the subject goods the first of the above definitions would not apply as the amplifier is considerably larger than 11/2 inches wide. As to the second definition, said to be of a larger amplifier, the evidence is that witnesses said the amplifier in question incorporates a differential amplifier and all witnesses agreed that the part associated with the differential function is a significant part of the amplifier.

Counsel for the applicant referred to the well known case of Herbert Adams Pty. Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1932] HCA 27; 1932 47 CLR 222.

The problem with applying the principles ennunciated in that case is that Mr Kinman in his evidence said people do not ask for differential amplifiers and said, in effect, more expensive amplifiers would be differential amplifiers. Some witnesses said they were actively engaged in design or manufacture of differential amplifiers but did not refer to how such amplifiers were described in the trade. The witnesses, with the exclusion of Professor Gunn, did say the goods in question had incorporated in their constituent parts a section which gave the goods the character of differential amplifiers. The other case referred to, namely Landau & another v. Goldwater & another 13 ALR 192, is also of limited application to the facts of this case.

Although Professor Gunn agreed that the inclusion of the pencilled additions to the circuit diagram would result in the amplifier conforming to his definition of differential amplifier, some doubt was subsequently raised by a statement made to the witness that the pencilled additions were not included. However, the evidence is clear that the pencilled additions did form part of the circuit diagram of the subject goods. The Tribunal also notes, Professor Gunn said that although it is a matter of definition, the key to a differential amplifier is what goes on at the end of it. On this aspect we find little disharmony in definition in all the witnesses and from the earlier opinion of Mr South of Telecom.

Professor Gunn referred to the goods as "the combination of those two integrated circuits". The second of these he recognised as a differential amplifier but because the first was incorporated in the unit as a whole, and because the input to the whole unit was single, he considered that the goods were thereby taken outside what he considered to be the preferred definition. Nevertheless, when the proposition was put to the witness:-

"One accepts that in the careful study of electronics science the kind of distinctions we have been discussing are important. In the case before the Tribunal what we have to decide is the practical question of, ‘Here is a piece of equipment: what should it be described as, and as such where does it fit in the classification?'

Would it be fair to say that in this piece of equipment we do have something which takes what I think is described as a single-ended input and does produce a differential output for coupling up with a loud speaker or loud speakers? This is what this particular unit does in fact do? Yes."

The Tribunal was fortunate in the consideration of this request in the quality of the witnesses. Mr Kinman with his trade knowledge, Mr Crago who has had considerable practical experience, Mr Bali who has combined academic qualifications with practical experience in active design of transformers and Dr Skatterbol and Professor Gunn, both with impressive academic achievements in the area with which we have been concerned. It is also of interest that with the exception of Mr Kinman, none of the witnesses had financial or other interests in the outcome of the decision.

We find that the goods in question were differential amplifiers which, in the meaning of By-law 7259560, on the day of importation and accordingly were entitled to entry for home consumption free of duty.

The decision of the Assistant Collector, Revenue Control, Department of Business & Consumer Affairs, Brisbane, is set aside and in substitution for that decision the Tribunal decides the subject goods are entitled to entry free of duty.
