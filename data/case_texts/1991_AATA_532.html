Andreatta and Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 532 (29 May 1991)

Last Updated: 4 July 2008



Superannuation - applicant failed to meet Commonwealth Public Service general medical standard and was retired on invalidity grounds - specification of conditions on Benefit Classification Certificate - Ryan and Fry followed - Re Rohde considered



Practice and Procedure - on death of applicant, application should be made by applicant's personal representative in accordance with s.30(1A) of AAT Act to be made a party to the proceedings

REASONS FOR DECISION



29 May 1991 Deputy President R.K. Todd

Dr D.B. Travers (Member)

Mr N.J. Attwood (Member)



1. This was an application for review of a decision taken on reconsideration confirming a decision of a delegate of the respondent to issue in respect of Giacinto Andreatta a Benefit Classification Certificate ("BCC") pursuant to s.16(11) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") specifying the conditions "Backache and Leg Pain", "Essential Hypertension" and "Diabetes".

2. After the hearing of the matter had concluded and the decision of the Tribunal had been reserved Mr Andreatta died. The Tribunal was informed accordingly and the question arose as to what procedure the Tribunal should follow. The Australian Government Solicitor, acting for the respondent, lodged a submission with the Tribunal which invited the Tribunal not to follow certain earlier Tribunal decisions which had held that the Tribunal may, upon the death of an applicant, proceed to complete the review without the substitution of any legal personal representative.



3. With some alterations of a formal nature the submission, which is worth setting out in full, was as follows:



The Tribunal should not follow earlier decisions of the Tribunal in which it has been held that, notwithstanding the death of the applicant after the conclusion of the hearing, the Tribunal has, without more, the power to deliver a decision: see Re Davis and Director-General of Social Security (1984) 6 ALN N332; Re Doublet and Secretary, Department of Social Security (1985) 8 ALN N124; Re Wiseman and Department of Transport (No.2) Decision No.2621, 11 April 1986; Re Miller and Secretary, Department of Social Security (1987) 12 ALD 431; Case U171, 87 ATC 975; cf. Re Garraffo and Secretary, Department of Social Security (1988) 15 ALD 676.



The judgment of the High Court in Ryan v Davies Brothers Limited [1921] HCA 53; (1921) 29 CLR 527 esp. at 532-3, which has been relied on in earlier Tribunal decisions in support of the proposition that the Tribunal has the power to deliver a decision where an applicant has died after the conclusion of the hearing, is of no relevance to proceedings before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal ("the AAT"). That case is authority for the proposition that where there has been an appeal from a judgment on an actio personalis and a party to the appeal has died after the hearing of the appeal, then, the right of action for the original wrong having merged into the judgment and having taken its place to create a new and different right, the maxim actio personalis moritur cum persona (which would have applied to the original cause of action) has no application to the appeal. Thus the appeal before the High Court in Ryan v Davies Bros Ltd involved a fundamentally different species of right (viz. the right created by a judgment of a Court) from that in an appeal from an administrative decision to the AAT.



A distinction should be drawn between the entitlement that is the subject of a proceeding before the AAT and the person in whose name the proceeding is brought. Whether or not a statutory entitlement devolves upon the death of a person depends on the language of the statute under which that right arose: Peebles v Ostwaldthistle Urban District Council [1896] 2 QB 159; Darlington v Roscoe [1907] 1 KB 219; United Collieries Ltd v Simpson [1909] AC 383 esp. at 391 and 397; Smith v Williams [1922] 1 KB 158; Dean v Wiesengrund [1955] 2 QB 120; D(J) v D(S) [1973] 1 All ER 349; Schleneret v Watson Contracting Co . Ltd [1979] 1 NSWLR 140; Executor Trustee & Agency Co. of S.A. Ltd v General Motors-Holdens Ltd (1981) 29 SASR 166; Rickless v United Artists Corp. [1988] QB 40 esp. at 56. Part II of the Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1955 (A.C.T.) has no operation in respect of proceedings before the Tribunal.



Where the statutory entitlement that is the subject of the proceeding does not devolve upon the death of an applicant, then the death of the applicant will extinguish the availability of that entitlement and, with it, the power of any decision-maker (which, by ss.43(1) Administrative

Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (the "AAT Act"), includes the power of the AAT upon review) to decide that that entitlement is properly payable to the applicant: Marvel Skate Co Pty Ltd v Bright, ex parte Reid (1952) 52 SR(NSW) 277; Goodman v Jones [1909] VicLawRp 52; [1909] VLR 307.



Where the statutory entitlement that is the subject of the proceeding does devolve upon the death of an applicant, then the person to whom the statutory entitlement devolves must make application pursuant to ss.30(1A) AAT Act to be made a party to the proceeding. Unless and until such application is made the Tribunal will have no jurisdiction to review the decision. The position of the Tribunal where no such application is made is analogous to that of an arbitrator confronted with a like situation (prior to the enactment of specific statutory provisions, for example, ss.52(1) Commercial Arbitration Act 1986 (A.C.T.); ss.2(1) Arbitration Act 1950 (U.K.); cf Arbitration Act 1902 (N.S.W.) in its application to the A.C.T., reprinted in Part 3, Schedule 2, New South Wales Acts Application Act 1984 (A.C.T.)): viz., the authority of an arbitrator was, unless the order of reference specifically provided otherwise, determined if either party died before an award was made: Toussaint v Hartop [1817] EngR 514; (1817) 7 Taunt 571, 129 ER 227; Cooper v Johnson (1819) 2 B & Ald 394, 106 ER 410; Rhodes v Haigh [1823] EngR 194; (1823) 2 B & C 345, 107 ER 413; cf Brooke v Mitchell [1840] EngR 32; (1840) 6 M & W 473, 151 ER 498.



The respondent would not oppose any application pursuant to ss.30(1A) AAT Act made by the "spouse" (as defined by the Superannuation Act 1976) of the applicant. If there is no such "spouse" and no such "eligible child" of the applicant, the respondent would not oppose any application pursuant to s.30(1A) AAT Act made by the applicant's personal representative.



4. We considered that, with all due respect to the previous decisions of the Tribunal, the submission's distinguishing of Ryan v Davies Brothers Ltd was compelling. Our reasons for forming this view are those set out in the submission, in particular those set out in the second paragraph. There is nothing that we can usefully add to them.



5. The solicitors for the late Mr Andreatta were accordingly invited to make the application under s.30(1A) of the AAT Act to which the solicitor for the respondent had proleptically consented.



6. Subsequently the solicitors for the estate, who had acted for Mr Andreatta in these proceedings until his death, informed the Tribunal that a grant of probate had been made to Mr Michael Joseph Higgins who sought to become a party to the proceedings. On 15 March 1991 the Tribunal, being satisfied that the personal representative of Giacinto Andreatta deceased was a person whose interests were affected by the decision of the respondent of which the applicant had sought review, ordered pursuant to s.30(1A) of the AAT Act that Michael Joseph Higgins be made a party to the proceedings. For convenience, we shall however continue to refer to the late Mr Andreatta as "the applicant".



7. The relevant legislative provisions, in the form in which they stood for the purposes of this case, are s.16(11) and (11A) of the Act:



"(11) Where -



(a) a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of death or retirement on the ground of invalidity;



(b) his period of contributory service is less than 20 years and, on the day on which he ceases to be an eligible employee, he has not attained his maximum retiring age;



(c) a benefit classification certificate is not in force in respect of the person immediately before his death or retirement or, if a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of him at that time, the Commissioner is not of the opinion that the death or incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions of the person specified in the certificate, or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions; and



(d) the Commissioner is satisfied -



(i) that, at or in connection with a medical examination which the person was required to undergo under this section, the person failed to furnish any information required to be furnished by him or furnished false information; and



(ii) that, if the person had not failed to furnish that information or had not furnished that false information, there would have been in force in respect of the person, immediately before his death or retirement, a benefit classification certificate in which there would have been specified the physical or mental condition or conditions which caused, or substantially contributed to, the death or retirement, or a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions,



the Commissioner shall issue in respect of the person a benefit classification certificate in which there is or are specified the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person which, in the opinion of the Commissioner -



(e) in a case where a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of the person at the time he dies or retires - would have been the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person specified in the certificate at that time; or



(f) ...,



if the person had not failed to furnish that information or had not furnished that false information and, for the purposes of this Act, the certificate shall be deemed to have been in force in respect of the person immediately before his death or retirement.



(11A) Where a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of a person in respect of whom a benefit classification certificate is issued under subsection (11), the first-mentioned certificate shall be deemed to have been revoked by the second-mentioned certificate with effect from the time immediately before the death of the person or the retirement of the person by reason of which the second-mentioned certificate was issued."



8. By way of introduction, we now set out relevant portions of the statement of reasons supplied pursuant to s.37 of the Act, making some formal changes only:



2. (a) The applicant was born on 1 Oct 1928. He became an eligible employee for the purposes of the Act on 2 Sep 1980 by virtue of his employment by the ACT Electricity Authority ("ACTEA") as a meter reader, having commenced as a temporary employee on 10 Apr 1978. In connection with his permanent appointment to the Commonwealth Public Service, and pursuant to subsection 16(2) of the Act, he was required to undergo a medical examination, which was conducted on or before 13 Nov 1980 by Dr F S D Thompson, a Commonwealth medical officer ("CMO") and an approved medical practitioner for the purpose of the subsection.



(b) In connection with that examination the applicant provided a statement of his personal medical history by completing a questionnaire, in which he answered "No" to the following questions, inter alia:



"Are you now suffering or have you ever suffered from any of the following disabilities -

....



5.(a) Lumbago, paralysis or disorder of the limbs or spine?

(b) Poliomyelitis, flat feet or injury or fracture of bones?

....

12.(b) Have you had any complaints, illness or injury not mentioned above?"

(c) In his report of his examination of the applicant, Dr Thompson made the following comments against the following items in the medical report form:



'1. General appearance of candidate: physique, mobility, presence of deformities, etc - Good physique and mobility.

....

8. Is there any abnormality in use of legs and arms. Any restriction or pain in movement of the spine? - No [and] No.'



(d) On 13 Nov 1980 Dr Thompson stated that he considered that the applicant did not meet the general medical standard (GMS), due to the condition 'essential hypertension'. (The GMS is contained in the Handbook for Commonwealth Medical Officers and is used by CMOs and delegates of the Commissioner as a guide in assessing the likelihood of applicants remaining fit for employment until attaining their maximum retiring age.) On 14 Nov 1980 Dr H S Turner, as adviser to the Commissioner, stated that she also considered that the applicant did not meet the GMS (sc. for the reason stated by Dr Thompson).



(e) Pursuant to subsection 16(3) of the Act, the report of the result of the examination was furnished to the Commissioner. Subsequently, in a memorandum from the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office ("AGRBO"), dated 13 Oct 1981, the attention of the Department of Health was drawn to the fact that the applicant had not provided an answer to a question in the statement of personal medical history questionnaire concerning diabetes, and to the result of a urine test conducted at the examination. The applicant was subsequently medically re-examined, on or before 22 Oct 1981, by Dr M Rajasingham, CMO, who, on that date, stated that she was of the opinion that Mr Andreatta did not meet the GMS due to the conditions 'Hypertension' and 'Diabetes'. On 26 Oct 1981 Dr Turner, as adviser to the Commissioner, also recommended that the applicant did not meet the GMS.



(f) The report of the result of the medical re-examination was furnished to the Commissioner. On 10 Nov 1981 a delegate of the Commissioner, having considered the reports of both medical examinations and such other matters as were considered relevant, pursuant to subsection 16(4) of the Act issued in respect of the applicant a BCC in which the conditions 'Essential Hypertension' and 'Diabetes' were specified.



(g) The applicant was notified of the issue of the BCC in a letter dated 10 Nov 1981.



3. (a) On 30 Apr 1985 the applicant was medically examined by a CMO to determine whether he was fit for continued employment or should be retired on medical grounds. In his undated report of the examination the CMO stated that he considered that the applicant was suffering from the following condition, with the following percentage degree of contribution to incapacity for work:



'Spinal cord stenosis with 2o [secondary] claudication: 100%'.



(I note in this regard the following definitions provided in Dorland's Illustrated Medical Dictionary:



'Claudication ... limping or lameness intermittent c., a complex of symptoms characterised by absence of pain or discomfort in a limb when at rest, the commencement of pain, tension, and weakness, after walking is begun, intensification of the condition until walking becomes impossible, and the disappearance of the symptoms after a period of rest.')



The CMO provided the following brief history of the condition:



'[History of] pressure and pain in legs which first started in 1979. Due to walking prolonged distances or time, especially up hills. Gradually deteriorating. Diagnosed as spinal cord stenosis with 2o claudication [leading to] pains on exercise.



Suffers from a number of other conditions which are not presently affecting his ability to work viz. diabetes, hypertension, varicose veins, (L) varicocele, multiple lipomas, psychiatric problems.



Prognosis

Fair.'



The CMO recommended that the applicant should be redeployed to duties of the following nature:



'Those not requiring prolonged walking or heavy lifting and carrying.'



On 31 May 1985 Dr B C Bromhead, as adviser to the Commissioner, oversaw the CMO's report. In an attachment to the report Dr Bromhead stated,



'Dr Gavranic's hand written report contains an excellent longitudinal history'.



(b) A report, dated 11 Apr 1985, by Dr E J Cassar, a consultant physician, was provided to the Secretary of the ACTEA.



(c) In a letter from the Secretary of the ACTEA, dated 11 Jul 1985, the applicant was informed that it had not proved possible to redeploy him in accordance with the CMO's recommendation.



(d) On 4 Sep 1985 the applicant was retired on invalidity grounds with effect from 11 Oct 1985.



4. (a) Since, at the date of his retirement, the applicant had a period of contributory service of less than 20 years and had not attained his maximum retiring age, consideration was given as to whether subsection 16(11) of the Act had application to him. For that purpose the following additional reports and other documents were obtained:



. reports of the entry medical examination and re-examination, dated 13 Nov 1980 and 22 Oct 1981 respectively;



. report by Dr Cassar dated 24 Jan 1986;



. report by Dr D Jarvis, general medical practitioner, dated 28 Jan 1986.



(b) On 19 Jun 1986 a delegate of the Commissioner reached the following conclusions:



. that, pursuant to paragraph 16(11)(c) of the Act, he was not of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement (the effect of 'spinal cord stenosis with secondary claudication') was caused, or substantially contributed to, by a condition or conditions specified in the BCC currently in force in respect of the applicant i.e. by either 'Essential Hypertension' or 'Diabetes', or a connected condition;

. that, pursuant to paragraph 16(11)(d), he was satisfied that, at or in connection with his entry medical examination, the applicant failed to furnish information required to be furnished by him or furnished false information (in relation to a history of back ache and leg pain); and



. that he was satisfied that, if the applicant had not failed to furnish that information or had not furnished that false information, there would have been in force in respect of him, immediately before his retirement, a BCC in which there would have been specified the physical or mental condition or conditions which caused, or substantially contributed to, his retirement, or a condition(s) connected with such conditions (namely 'Backache and Leg Pains').



The delegate accordingly issued in respect of the applicant a BCC in which were specified the conditions which, in the opinion of the delegate, would have been specified in the BCC if the applicant had not failed to furnish information or had not furnished false information (namely 'Backache and leg pains', 'Essential Hypertension' and 'Diabetes'). By the operation of subsection 16(11), the BCC was deemed to have been in force immediately before the applicant's retirement. The same delegate determined, on the same date, pursuant to subsection 66(2) of the Act, that he was of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a condition or conditions specified in the BCC, or by a condition or conditions connected with such a condition or conditions. As a result of the delegate's decisions, the benefit payable to the applicant was a lump sum rather than a pension.



9. We now proceed to set out our own findings in the light of the evidence before the Tribunal. The applicant had been seen by numerous doctors. Written evidence and reports were tendered by a number of CMOs namely Drs Thompson, Delivera, Rajasingham, Turner, Bromhead and one other who was unidentified; by three general practitioners: Drs Jarvis, Foo and Gavranic; and by a number of specialists: Dr Dhall, a vascular surgeon; Dr Mitchell, a consultant physician; Dr Newcombe, a neurosurgeon; Dr Cassar, a consultant physician; Dr Utley, a radiologist; and by Dr Vance, an orthopaedic surgeon. Of these, four were called as witnesses, namely Drs Dhall, Thompson, Jarvis and Vance.



10. Dr Thompson, CMO, conducted the initial medical examination for superannuation purposes on 13 November 1980. In his report he noted that the applicant was suffering from essential hypertension and he recommended that this condition be included in a Benefits Classification Certificate ("BCC"). Dr Thompson made this diagnosis on clinical examination and the taking of a history although the applicant had answered "No" to question 2(c) on Form 66 that is to say "Are you now suffering or have you ever suffered ...(c) high blood pressure". During questioning the applicant indicated that he had suffered from hypertension since 1978.

11. Dr Thompson did not make any note regarding diabetes although the applicant had failed to answer the question 8(a). A record was made on the form stating "super ++ 1/2%". Subsequently this was drawn to the attention of the CMO by a delegate of the Commissioner (13 October 1981) and the condition "Diabetes" was recommended for inclusion in the BCC.



12. Dr Thompson was questioned at length regarding the answers given by the applicant to the questions on Form 66. In evidence the applicant had stated that the doctor had filled in all the answers.



13. Dr Thompson categorically denied this, pointing out that the answers were not in his handwriting and that it would have been impossible for the CMO to operate with any reasonable throughput if this was so. We are in agreement with Dr Thompson on this and are of the view that none of the answers to section B of the form were recorded by him. Counsel for the applicant did not dispute this fact.



14. Question 13(a) "Date you last received medical or surgical treatment and for what condition?" was answered "1979". Dr Jarvis in evidence showed that the applicant had been seen eleven times in 1980 prior to the CMO examination. The last recorded visit was on 12 November 1980 for a blood pressure check. The Tribunal was satisfied that the answer given to question 13(a) was incorrect and that the applicant would have been well aware that he had received medical treatment in 1980.



15. Under examination Dr Thompson stated that had he been aware that the applicant had suffered from backache and pain in the legs he would have sought more information from the applicant's general practitioner and possibly sought specialist opinion. In response to questioning Dr Thompson stated that pain in the legs might have indicated a number of conditions. When asked, he elaborated on "intermittent claudication" and stated that this normally caused pain on walking relieved by rest. The usual cause was atherosclerosis (thickening of the arteries). Dr Thompson also commented on the difference between the ache in the legs usually caused by varicose veins and the pain of intermittent claudication.

16. During the years 1980-85 the applicant was treated by general practitioners: Dr Jarvis until 1983, then by Dr Gavranic. The latter reported on 29 April 1985 to the CMO that, among other complaints, he had treated the applicant for hypertension, diabetes mellitus, low back pain and claudication pain left leg. Dr Gavranic's record showed that low back pain had first been noticed in 1972.



17. Mr Andreatta had in 1984 been referred to Dr Dhall, a vascular surgeon, who could find no clinical evidence of vascular disease. The applicant had also been referred to Dr Cassar, a consultant physician, in 1983. A presumptive diagnosis of "spinal cord stenosis with secondary claudication" was made although there was no report of any X-ray confirming this.



18. Following complaints by Mr Andreatta that he was unable to continue working without severe disabling leg pain relieved by rest he was examined by the CMO on 30 April 1985. A recommendation that Mr Andreatta be redeployed on duties not requiring prolonged walking or heavy lifting and carrying was made. The CMO listed the diagnosis as "spinal cord stenosis with secondary claudication " 100 per cent.



19. We noted that the senior medical officer, in commenting on the examination, stated: "continuation of hypertension and diabetes may affect arteries and produce claudication".



20. Dr Dhall in evidence stated that he did not consider the applicant to have symptoms of a vascular nature and suggested referral to a rheumatologist. His diagnosis was based on clinical examination only, and he stated that he did not consider that it warranted undertaking further investigations at that stage as they would have been of an invasive nature.



21. Dr Jarvis gave evidence that he had treated the applicant from July 1976 until June 1983. He had been treated for numerous complaints including hypertension and diabetes. The first record of low backache was in February 1977. His last record of "backache and leg pain" was on 30 September 1980. Treatment for these conditions included various anti-inflammatory drugs.



22. On 11 April 1985, Dr Cassar, consultant physician, after initially seeing the applicant on 20 July 1983 gave a presumptive diagnosis of "spinal cord stenosis with claudication as manifestation of this".



23. The CMO who examined the applicant on 31 May 1985 had access to this information and listed the condition as contributing 100 per cent to the incapacity of the applicant to follow his normal duties. The recommendation that he be redeployed on duties not requiring prolonged walking or heavy lifting and carrying was made. It was however noted by the CMO that symptoms of pain in the legs had started in 1979.



24. On 11 July 1985 ACTEA notified the applicant that no positions were available for which he might be suited, and a recommendation was made that he be retired on the grounds of invalidity. The reason given was the diagnosis given by the CMO in his report (see paragraph 18 above).



25. Dr Vance saw the applicant in 1989. A CAT scan reported on by Dr Utley was available to Dr Vance. The scan showed bulging of L3/4 discs but this was below the level of the nerve roots that might produce pain in the leg. There was no evidence of nerve root compression. Dr Vance stated in evidence that the applicant did not have spinal cord stenosis although he did have spondylosis not inconsistent with a man of his age. Dr Vance could not attribute the leg pain and backache to any other condition.



26. On 19 June 1986 a delegate of the Commissioner noted that if all information had been available in November 1980 a BCC listing "backache and leg pains" would have been issued. A BCC was accordingly issued listing these "complaints".

27. We were, in summary, required to consider the following:

(i) Were the answers given by the applicant on the medical examination form for entry correct and did he comprehend the nature of the questions?



(ii) Had he answered correctly, would a BCC have issued specifying "backache and leg pains"?

(iii) Did the inclusion of "backache and leg pains" on the BCC satisfy the requirements of the Act?

(iv) What was the relationship of any condition on entry to the cause of retirement?



28. We are satisfied with Dr Thompson's evidence, and consider that the applicant did not make adequate disclosure of his medical history in November 1980 as required by the Act and in the terms of the construction of the Act adopted by the Federal Court in Fry v Commissioner for Superannuation [1984] FCA 211; (1984) 6 ALD 229. We are of the view that had the disclosure of pain in the leg been made at the initial medical examination Dr Thompson would have made further investigation. Having regard however to our other findings as appearing below, we need not elaborate our reasons on this point further.



29. Medical opinion varied as to the relationship of a particular condition that might have been present prior to the entry medical examination. The diagnosis "spinal stenosis" did not exist either before or after entry.



30. The decision of the respondent to include "Backache and Leg pain" in the BCC is not in our view in accordance with s.16(b)(i) of the Act. Both are symptoms that could be indicative of numerous "physical conditions". The Tribunal considers "a physical or mental condition" to mean one that can be defined in terms of some pathology. The reasons for this view should be expanded.



31. The application of s.16 of the Act can be a matter of considerable complexity in factual and logical terms. So much appears from previous cases, but it appears very strongly from the statement of reasons provided pursuant to s.37 of the AAT Act in the present case. The portion of that statement dealing with the question of what situation would have obtained had the applicant not failed to furnish required information etc. occupied 21 carefully prepared and closely reasoned pages of text. The difficulty with that reasoning is, with respect, that it suffers from a failure to stand back from the minutiae of many medical reports and to consider instead a broader view of the case. What we have here is a BCC condition expressed in a very broad form, namely "Backache and Leg Pain". The first point to make is that we are not satisfied that these are proper or adequate statements of conditions for the purposes of the Act. No doubt a precise diagnosis is not requisite, but simply to speak of the existence of pain in a broadly stated way, limited only by reference to two parts of the body, is not to speak of a physical condition in the manner contemplated by s.16(11)(d). The section looks forward to the possibility of a linkage between a condition specified in a BCC and a retirement condition, or, significantly for this analysis, a condition connected with such a condition. Will any pain in the leg, or ache in the back, of whatever cause, be enough to create that linkage? That surely cannot be, yet to permit so broad a statement to stand as a BCC condition opens up that prospect. Any pain in the leg at entry would then suffice, given retirement on the ground of pain in that limb, notwithstanding that the origin of the later pain was entirely different from, and entirely unrelated to, the earlier pain. The source of one might be vascular, and the other caused by an ailment of the nervous system or by some malignancy. If such a statement of a condition is adequate, why not just "The applicant suffers pain", without any attribution to any particular part of the body?



32. There are no doubt some areas in the field of mental health in relation to which it may be difficult to describe the relevant mental condition other than by reference to symptomatology: see Re Rohde and Commissioner for Superannuation (1988) 18 ALD 791. But in relation to most such cases, and certainly in relation to physical conditions, we are confident of the view that we have expressed.

33. To reject the BCC condition as stated is not however enough to decide the case. If some other physical or mental condition should be specified, having regard to the evidence before the Tribunal, we should decide accordingly. We do not consider that it should.



34. The only other option possibly available would be spondylosis. There is however quite insufficient evidence to conclude that had the applicant made adequate disclosure a delegate could have concluded that a condition "spondylosis" should have been specified. The most likely conclusion would have been that there were unexplained back and leg pains, or back and leg pains due to fatigue. Dr Jarvis was of the view that as at 3 September 1980 the applicant was not suffering from the effects of any spondylitic degeneration of the spine. Dr Jarvis was of the view that the leg pain at that time, which was bilateral leg pain, was fatigue. After September 1981 however, the position appears to have changed, and in fact the applicant had by then had a fall at work. Dr Dhall's evidence was that the applicant's later leg pain, in 1984, which was unilateral was an indicator that what by then was troubling the applicant had a neurological basis. This analysis is, we consider, important because it tends to show how unsafe it would have been to specify, based on what would have been known had full disclosure been made, any conditions other than essential hypertension and diabetes each of which were undoubted and each of which are irrelevant to the present discussion.



35. Having regard to the foregoing, the decision of the Tribunal will be to vary the decision under review by deleting from the BCC the words "Backache and Leg pain". It will be up to the respondent to recalculate the entitlements that would have been payable to the applicant in his lifetime had this decision been substituted for the decision under review.
