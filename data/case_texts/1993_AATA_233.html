Re Commissioner of Taxation No: Vt92/138 Aat [1993] AATA 233; (1993) 26 ATR 1254 (27 July 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No: VT92/138

AAT No. 8873

Number of pages - 9

Income Tax

[1993] AATA 233; (1993) 26 ATR 1254

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION DIVISION

P. GERBER (Deputy President), W.G. McLEAN (Member) AND B.H. PASCOE (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Primary producer - Whether clause 2 or clause 3 of Part I of Schedule 7 of Rates Act applies - Whether Division 16 of Part III of Assessment Act applies to income.

Income Tax Rates Act 1986 Schedule 7

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 Division 16 Part III

Batagol v FC of T [1963] HCA 51; (1963) 109 CLR 243

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 11 February 1993

27:7:1993

Solicitors for the Applicant: Briglia and Co.

Mr C. Stock and Mr F. Graham appeared for the applicant.

An officer of the ATO appeared for the respondent.

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

P. GERBER, W.G. McLEAN AND B.H. PASCOE This application is for the review of the decision to disallow an objection against an assessment of income tax in respect of income for the year ended 30 June 1990.

2. The issue in this case is whether Clause 2 or Clause 3 of Part I of Schedule 7 of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 was the correct Clause under which the amount of income tax payable should have been calculated.

3. The case proceeded on the basis of agreed facts as follows:

1 The applicant, with other persons, had been carrying on

businesses of primary production and had derived assessable

income therefrom in each of the income years ended 30 June 1986

to 1990 inclusive.

2 In 1979 the applicant and her sister came into possession of

a farm on the death of their mother. In 1987 on the death of

their father, they came into possession of a number of

residential properties from which they derived rental income.

3 In August 1989, the applicant and her sister agreed that the

applicant would acquire her sister's half share in the farming

property in return for the applicant's half share in a

residential properties, plus $56,000 in cash, representing one

half of the difference of $112,000 in the values of the

respective properties.

4 Arising from the disposal of her interest in the rent

producing properties, a capital gain of $66,389 accrued to the

applicant during the year of income ended 30 June 1990 ("the

1990 year"). That amount was included as a net capital gain in

her assessable income for that year under section 160ZO of the

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Assessment Act").

5 The applicant's taxable income for the 1990 year was $59,001

comprising:

Net capital gain $66,389

Net loss-primary production (43,155)

Income-other sources $35,767

Taxable Income $59,001

6 By an assessment for the 1990 year, notice of which was dated

22 March 1991, the Commissioner of Taxation ("the

Commissioner") assessed the applicant to tax on a taxable

income of $59,001. The Commissioner calculated the primary tax

of $28,106.35 on the taxable income by applying the formula in

Clause 3 of Part I of Schedule 7 of the Income Tax Rates Act

1986 ("The Rates Act") to determine the rate of tax. The

Commissioner applied the formula in Clause 3 on the basis that

the applicant's average income was $45,727, calculated over a

three year period. In the applicant's assessment for the 1990

year, the Commissioner did not allow any rebate in respect of

averaging for primary producers (section 156(4)), nor was any

liability for complementary tax imposed under section 156(4A).

7 By a notice of objection against the 1990 assessment dated 22

May 1991, the applicant objected to the assessment on the basis

that, inter alia, the amount of income tax payable should have

been calculated in accordance with Clause 2 of Part I of

Schedule 7 of the Rates Act. The objection was accompanied by a

notice of election under section 158A. Grounds 4, 5 and 5 of

the objection related to that notice of election.

8 By a notice of decision dated 15 June 1992, the Deputy

Commissioner of Taxation notified the disallowance of the

applicant's objection.

9 On 27 July 1992, the applicant applied to the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal for review of the Commissioner's decision on

the objection.

10 In a document entitled "Statement of Issues" which was

furnished to the Tribunal on 1 December 1992, the applicant

advised inter alia, that she wished to amend her grounds of

objection by withdrawing grounds 4, 5 and 6 of the notice of

objection dated 22 May 1991.

4. The two relevant clauses of Schedule 7 of The Rates Act are as follows:

2. where -

(a) the taxable income of a resident taxpayer consists of or

includes a special income component; and

(b) Division 16 of Part III of the Assessment Act does not

apply to the income of the taxpayer,

the rate of tax for every $1.00 of the taxable income is the

amount ascertained in accordance with the formula ............

3. where -

(a) the taxable income of a resident taxpayer consists of or

includes a special income component; and

(b) Division 16 of Part III of the Assessment Act applies to

the income of the taxpayer,

the rate of tax for every $1.00 of the taxable income is the

amount ascertained in accordance with the formula ...........

5. Under section 3 of the Rates Act, a "special income component" in relation to the taxable income of a taxpayer of a year of income in relation to which there is a capital gains amount, an abnormal income amount or both, means:

(a) if there is a capital gains amount but no abnormal income

amount:

(i) if the taxable income is equal to or less than the capital

gains amount - the whole of the taxable income; or

(ii) in any other case - so much of the taxable income as

equals the capital gains amount.

6. The formula under each of the Clauses of Schedule 7 has the effect of taxing the capital gains component at a rate based on the inclusion of 20% of the gain. The basic difference between the two formulae is that under Clause 3 the average income is the base to which the 20% of the capital gain is added to arrive at the rate of tax, whereas under Clause 2, the base is the taxable income of the year reduced by the capital gain. 7. The question which is the appropriate clause under which the tax payable for the relevant year is to be calculated is to be resolved by determining whether or not Division 16 of Part III of the Assessment Act applied to the income of the taxpayer and this was the question to which the representatives of both parties addressed themselves.

8. Interestingly, there is no specific section within Division 16 which makes a positive statement of either when or whether the Division applies to the income of a taxpayer.

9. The essence of submissions made by Mr Stocks, who represented the applicant, was that Division 16 can only be said to apply to the income of a taxpayer where an assessment results in either a rebate or complementary tax as a result of income from primary production being included in taxable income of the year. It was further submitted that Division 16 did not apply to income of the 1990 year, because the taxpayer had a reduced taxable income of nil pursuant to section 149A of the Assessment Act, so that there could be no assessment of a reduced taxable income.

10. The Commissioner's representative submitted that Division 16 applied to the income of the taxpayer because she derived income and carried on a business of primary production, and no provision operated to prevent the Division from applying to her income. It was submitted that Division 16 simply constitutes a stage to be worked through, which may then have an impact on the amount of tax payable under the Rates Act. Its application may result in a rebate, a complementary tax or in no tax payable.

11. The research of neither party was able to find any case in which this matter had been previously considered. However, Mr Stocks submitted that the decision in Batagol v FC of T [1963] HCA 51; (1936) 109 CLR 243; 9 AITR 207 supported his view that there was no assessment as there was no taxable income once one applied section 149A to exclude the capital gain from assessable and taxable income. However, Batagol was concerned primarily with the question of what constitutes an assessment and whether the issue of a refund advice was an "assessment".

12. We are unable to see how this case assists the taxpayer. It is clear that the taxpayer received an assessment and, irrespective of the appropriate Clause to be used in the calculation, income tax was payable and was assessed in respect of the particular year.

13. Other references provided by the parties were to cases primarily concerned with whether the particular taxpayer was carrying on a business of primary production in the relevant period. It was common ground that this taxpayer did carry on a business of primary production during the year, and it is with no disrespect to the parties that we see no need to deal with those decisions.

14. As mentioned previously, there is no positive statement in any section contained in Division 16 as to when and whether the Division applies to the income of a taxpayer. However in our view, it is possible to arrive at that answer by considering the purpose and function of the Division, and to consider, applying that purposive test, whether this taxpayer has derived income to which the Division does not apply.

15. Section 157 states:-

(1) In respect of income derived during the year ending on 30

June 1938 and during any subsequent year or during any

accounting period adopted in lieu of any such year. The

foregoing provisions of this Division shall not apply except in

respect of income derived by a primary producer.

(2) For the purposes of this section, "primary producer" means

a person who carries on in Australia a business of primary

production.

16. Section 158 provides that:-

This Division shall not apply in any case where there are not

at least 2 average years or where the taxpayer is assessed in

accordance with section 99A in respect of the year of income,

and shall not apply to the taxable income of a company except

income in respect of which it is assessable as trustee.

17. Section 158A allows a taxpayer to elect that the Division shall not apply in relation to income of the taxpayer of a year of income and of all subsequent years of income.

18. Sections 150 to 155 are all concerned with identifying which years are capable of being average years and which year constitutes the first average year. Further indication of what is meant by the words "Division applies" can be found in the words of sections 151 and 152.

151(1) For the purposes of the first application of this

Division in determining the tax payable by a taxpayer, the

first average year shall be the first year which is otherwise

capable of being an average year, and in which the taxable

income is not greater than that of the next succeeding year. No

year prior to that first average year shall, for the purposes

of any application of this Division in determining the tax

payable by a taxpayer, be capable of being an average year.

151(2) Any year in which the taxpayer was not carrying on

business and was not in receipt of a taxable income shall not

be counted as a first average year for the purposes of the

first application of this Division in determining the tax

payable by a taxpayer.

151(3) This section shall not apply to a taxpayer whose income

has been or is liable to be assessed at an average rate of tax

determined under the provisions of the previous Act.

152 Any year in which the taxpayer was not carrying on business

and was not in receipt of assessable income shall not be

counted as an average year, and the provisions of this Division

shall apply to the income thereafter derived by him as if he

had never been a taxpayer before that year.

19. Section 156 is the operative section which establishes whether a taxpayer will be entitled to a rebate or liable to complementary tax by reference to notional rates under the Rates Act. It is a condition precedent to the operation of this section that "this Division applies to the income of a taxpayer of a year of income". Consequently, it is necessary to determine whether or not the Division applies prior to considering whether section 156 will produce any differing result in tax payable from that produced by the remainder of the Assessment and Rates Acts.

20. Mr Stocks placed heavy reliance on the wording of section 149A (which was inserted in 1986 and amended in 1987 to exclude capital gains for the purposes of Division 16). This section provides:-

149A(1) For the purposes of this Division (including the

purpose of determining whether this Division applies to the

income of a taxpayer):

(a) references in this Division to the assessable income of a

taxpayer shall be read as references to the amount that would

have been the assessable income if the assessable income did

not include any net capital gain within the meaning of Part

IIIA; and

(b) references in this Division to the taxable income of a

taxpayer shall be read as references to the amount that would

have been the taxable income if:

(i) the assessable income did not include any net capital gain

within the meaning of Part IIIA; and

(ii) the taxable income were reduced by so much of the taxable

income as consists of an abnormal income amount as specified in

section 158L.

21. In our view, this section does no more than ignore the capital gain for the purpose of ascertaining whether the Division applies in the relevant year, whether the year is capable of being an average year and the calculation of average income.

22. Consequent upon this review of the provisions contained in Division 16, we are satisfied that the purpose of Division 16 is to determine for specific taxpayers the quantum of an average income for the purpose of calculating under section 156 and the Rates Act the amount of tax payable in a year in which the relevant taxpayer has a taxable income. In other words, it applies to income of all years in respect to a taxpayer where:-

(i) the taxpayer carries on in Australia a business of primary

production;

(ii) the relevant year is an "average year" and there are at

least 2 average years, and

(iii) the taxpayer has not elected that the Division shall not

apply in relation to the relevant year

23. We are therefore unable to agree with Mr Stocks that the Division must have some positive effect on the calculation of net tax payable for it to be said to apply to the income of the year. Any such effect can be calculated after a determination of whether the Division applies. It is a condition precedent to any calculation of the tax payable. For example, in a year in which a taxpayer incurs a loss, we take the view that, provided the criteria set out above are satisfied in respect of that year, the Division applies to income of the year even though no tax will be payable. If the Division did not apply in that year, it is difficult to see how the results of that year can be included as an average year.

24. We therefore find that Division 16 of Part III of the Assessment Act applied to the income of the taxpayer in the year ended 30 June 1990, and Clause 3 of Part I of Schedule 7 of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 was the appropriate clause under which the amount of income tax payable was calculated.

25. The decision under review is affirmed.
