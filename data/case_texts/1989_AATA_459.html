ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - Allowable Deductions - Interest on Moneys borrowed - Borrowings used in part to repay existing interest-free loan from and in part on-lent interest-free to parent company - parent foregoing claim to rent - whether interest deductible - apportionment.

REASONS FOR DECISION

23 June 1989 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. Following an investigation into the affairs of the Applicant company (Work-co), on 22 June 1987 the Commissioner issued amended assessments to income tax against Work-co for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 1986 inclusive. The adjustment sheet which accompanied the notices of amended assessment indicated that the original assessments had been amended with the following effect:

Year ended 30 June 1984 1985 1986

Taxable income originally assessed $ 786 $51,971 $191,017

Disallowed -

Motor vehicle expenses $1,500 $ 1,750 $ 2,000

Travelling expenses .- -

Interest and bank charges $2,536 $11,346 $ 32,676

Amended Assessment $4,822 $65,067 $227,623

Tax previously assessed was

increased by: $1,856 $6,024 $16,836

a total of $24,716

2. Additional tax was also imposed. Instead of additional tax being imposed at 200% of tax avoided ($3,712, $12,048 and $33,672: a total of $49,432) the Commissioner exercised his discretion as to the matter of remissions and remitted additional tax to sums of $1,553, $3,868 and $6,910: a total of $12,331.

3. Work-co objected to the amended assessments but so expressed its objection as to only put in issue the disallowance of "interest and bank charges". It did not dispute the adjustments in relation to motor vehicle expenses or travelling expenses. Neither did it express any objection to the manner in which the Commissioner had exercised his responsibility to consider remissions of additional tax for incorrect returns. The significance of that omission will be considered in due course. To understand the issues to be resolved it is necessary to have regard to the history of the total business enterprise.

4. Bob and Bert had been associated in business for several years. Each controlled his own family company: (Bob-co and Bert-co respectively). Those two companies in equal shares held all of the issued capital in a third company (Top-co). Both Bob-co and Bert-co held other interests than their holdings in Top-co. Top-co had as its wholly owned subsidiary the Applicant company (Work-co). The other principal interest held by Top-co was a freehold property recorded in its accounts at cost: a figure substantially less than its market value. That property was occupied by and wholly given over to the purposes of Work-co. Work-co occupied the premises on a rent-free basis without any security of tenure. Bob and Bert were the principal executives of Top-co and Work-co. Neither derived salaries from the companies. Instead their services as "consultants" were provided on the basis of fees being paid by Work-co to Bob-co and Bert-co. Two employees of Work-co, one of whom was Allen, had had allotted to them 1,000 redeemable preference shares in Work-co to provide something in the way of an incentive for them.

5. As December 1983 approached, Bob was anxious to retire from the entire enterprise. To achieve his ambition he needed to arrange for the loans which had been made by Bob-co to Top-co to be repaid and for someone to purchase the shares in Top-co held by Bob-co. Persons interested to arrange the purchaser of the shares were found in the persons of Bob and Allen.

6. The three men consulted the accountant to the business. I accept that they looked to him to provide sound financial and fiscal advice and that they also hoped for fair advice particularly as to the value of the interest of Bob-co. The accountant confidently went about the tasks assigned to him and a ... value for the interest to be disposed of was agreed. The question to which the accountant then had to direct his attention was how to arrange the necessary finance. To understand the advice given it is necessary to understand the inter-relationships between the individuals, their family companies, Top-co and Work-co, The company which carried on the principal trading activity and the company which had the largest and strongest cash flow was Work-co. However, its profitability was dependent, not only upon its capacity to produce successfully and profitably, but also upon continued availability of the right to enjoy rent-free occupation of the premises owned by Top-co. On the other hand, one of the two principal assets of Top-co was its shareholding in Work-co and, thereby, its capacity to benefit from the profitability of Work-co. The other was the property occupied rent-free by Work-co. As the businesses were operating early in 1983, that meant that, to the extent to which Top-co was prepared to forego rent from its freehold premises occupied by Work-co, its profits from Work-co would be increased. Conversely, to the extent to which it might to recover rent from Work-co, the profits of Work-co would be reduced and the benefits to Top-co from Work-co diminished.

7. A further significant element of the inter-relationships was that it had long been the practice for Bob-co and Bert-co to maintain monies on loan to Top-co interest free, repayable on demand; and for funds surplus to the immediate requirements of either Top-co or Work-co to be made available to the other of those companies interest free and repayable on demand. Such was the inter-relationship in the activities of Top-co and Work-co that the commercial practicality of the matter was that the risk of Work-co being dispossessed by Top-co was as insignificant as the prospect of Top-co being able to repay on demand the substantial interest free loans made available to it from Bob-co and Bert-co was illusory. The fortunes of all were substantially inter-dependent.

8. I am well satisfied that in these circumstances the efficacy of all the transactions depended on the one hand largely upon a mutual trust between Bob and Bert as controllers of the shareholdings and. of loan funds; and on the other hand upon the financial inter-dependence between the companies.

9. Having agreed upon a sale price; having agreed that Bob-co would sell 60% of its half-share in Top-co to Bert-co and the remaining 40% of its half interest to a newly incorporated company (Allen-co) controlled by Allen and his wife; having agreed that Top-co should arrange repayment of the amounts due on loan account to Bob-co so that the investment of Bob-co in the business would be wholly terminated; and having agreed that Top-co would repay what it owed to Bert-co to assist Bert-co with the funds it would need to purchase the shares in Top-co, the question which then had to be addressed was how Top-co could raise the necessary funds to discharge its obligations on loan account to Bob-co and Bert-co. In particular, the difficulty which had to be addressed was that, although Top-co had the resources to provide adequate security for the borrowing, under existing arrangements it would not have had a cash flow sufficient to enable it to pay interest, or to effect payments on account of the loan principal. It followed that, if Top-co were to borrow the monies from a third party, it would have to increase its revenues. The most obvious way - and probably the only way - open to it to do so was to charge rent at an appropriate rate to Work-co. That would have had the consequence that the profitability of Work-co would have declined pro tanto.

10. An alternative approach to the problem which was considered was that Work-co, as the company with the greatest cash flow, should be the borrower and that it should use the monies borrowed to repay what it then owed on loan account (interest free repayable on demand) to Top-co and that, over and above that, it should lend the additional monies borrowed to Top-co. Top-co could then use the monies so lent to it to repay what it owed on loan account to Bob-co and Bert-co. There were basically two ways of organising such a loan. The first would be for Work-co to on-lend the monies to Top-co at interest. Were it to have done so the same problem would have arisen for Top-co as would have arisen upon borrowing from any other party: it would have needed to establish a cash flow to enable it to meet interest obligations, or suffer its interest obligations to be accumulated and, presumably, capitalised. As with a borrowing from any third party, the necessary funds could have been generated by responding to Work-co's demands for interest by making a demand for rent.

11. The accountant considered all these matters and took a sensible, sound, practical view of the matter: that a simple, arrangement was to be preferred to a more complicated arrangement. He advised, and the advice was accepted, that there was a second way of organising matters. He recommended that Work-co should borrow from the bank at interest; that the entirety of the monies borrowed should be advanced to the use of Top-co, partly in repaying the existing debt of Work-co to Top-co and partly by way of an advance of monies on loan; and that, as to the latter amount, the loan should be interest free repayable on demand. Just as Work-co had enjoyed a borrowing interest free repayable on demand in relation to the amount now to be repaid to Top-co, now Top-co would be debtor on loan account to Work-co on the same basis. Meanwhile Work-co would continue to have the use of the premises of Top-co without liability to rent. The advantage of the arrangement was judged by those concerned to still lie with Work-co. Later events were to confirm their judgment.

12. When the co-operation of the bank was secured Work-co, at the instance of Top-co, drew funds and remitted them to Bob-co and Bert-co in satisfaction of the obligations of Top-co to Bob-co and Bert-co on loan account; and it debited its loan account with Top-co with the amount of the advance. Contemporaneously Bert-co and Allen-co borrowed other moneys from the same bank which enabled them to make payment to Bob-co of the amounts due respectively by Bert-co and Allen-co as purchasers of the shares formerly held by Bob-co. In the case of Allen-co the amount borrowed was, for all practical purposes, the total purchase price. For Bert-co it was something less because of the other resources of Bert-co, including the monies which had come to it upon collecting the amount due to it on loan account from Top-co.

13. The accountant gave evidence before me which I completely accept. It was that it did not then occur to him, and at date of hearing had still not occurred to him, that the arrangements so made were in any way such as to place in doubt the claims of Work-co to income tax deductions for interest subsequently paid to the bank, upon its increased borrowings.

14. That thought was only first suggested to him when an "audit" was undertaken by officers of the Commissioner. The suggestion that Work-co should not be entitled to such interest deductions was first made after November 1986. It was initially made by one officer and later confirmed by his superior. Further, they suggested that the interest outgoing would be allowable to Work-co if Work-co charged either a commercial or higher rate of interest to Top-co on the monies Top-co had borrowed even if it meant that Top-co, in order to provide for the interest payment, in turn charged a sufficient or commercial rate of rent to Work-co. As a precaution Top-co and Work-co responded by accommodating the suggestion. A valuation was obtained, no doubt at some significant expense, to confirm what all concerned in the interests of the companies already knew: that any disadvantage there might be to Work-co in foregoing any claim to interest on the monies due to it by Top-co would be more than outweighed by Top-co foregoing any claim it might make to rent in relation to the premises owned by it and occupied by Work-co.

15. Having no doubts about the efficacy of the arrangements made, when the accounts of Work-co were prepared to 30 June 1984 and later years, claims were made to deduct all interest interest incurred as follows:-

Year ended 30 June 1984 1985 1986

Interest 6,944 12,689 36,579

Later those claims were partially disallowed. The Balance Sheets showed that the loan moneys due from Top-co to Work-Co changed as follows;-

Year ended 30 June 1984 1985 1986

Loans to (Top-co) (7,248) 50,307 70,161

I am satisfied that the balance due on loan account varied from time to time during the year, but I am not able to trace the timing of the changes precisely.

16. When the Commissioner's officers moved to disallow "interest and bank charges", as I understand it they sought to disallow all of the interest paid attributable to the amount borrowed and initially paid to, or to the use of, Top-co. To the extent to which they did so, they failed to deal with two facets of the problem. First, a substantial part of what was advanced by Work-co only effected a repayment of moneys borrowed. Secondly, thereafter the balance of the loan account fluctuated as it always had done.

17. It is therefore probable that, even if the Commissioner was correct in principle, the amounts disallowed ($2,536; $11,346; and $32,676) may be excessive. Fortunately it is not necessary to make a finding in that regard. Neither party clearly addressed any evidence to such an issue.

18. From that point commercial operations continued "under new management". In the period the companies had clearly prospered. For a period the former practice of providing for the services of Bob and Bert and their spouses by the payment by Work-co of consulting fees to Bob-co and Bert-co continued, with Allen and his wife substituting for Bob and his wife and Allen-co for Bob-co. The sums so paid were sufficient to enable those two companies to pay salaries to Bert and to his wife and to Allen and his wife, and also to enable those companies to pay the substantial amounts of interest to the bank for which they were liable upon the monies borrowed to finance the purchase of the shares. As will appear from the figures hereafter, it was a near run thing for Allen-co, because it had borrowed all that it had invested in buying shares in Top-co.

19. In November 1985 there was a further revision in the financial arrangements. It was recommended by the accountant and may be conveniently referred as an "amalgamation" of three accounts into one. After Bob had stepped out of the business in December 1983 there were three borrowings from the bank; that by Allen-co to fund its purchase of shares; that by Bert-co to partly fund its purchase of shares; and that by Work-co to enable it to move from being a debtor on loan account to Top-co to a creditor. It was proposed that for convenience and ease of administration the three accounts should be made one. This was achieved by Work-co increasing its borrowings from the bank to a point sufficient to enable it to lend monies to Bert-co and Allen-co sufficient to enable them to fully satisfy their respective indebtedness to the bank on the original borrowings. This was done and, as had been the case with other borrowings within the group, it was done on the basis that there would be no liability in the borrowers to pay interest to Work-co. It was effected at a time when the consulting fee arrangements of the past were being altered so that Bert and Allen and their respective wives came to be remunerated directly by Work-co, but in my view that can provide no explanation for the transaction. Rather it was contended that, just as with the earlier transaction so too with this: the nett result was the same whether or not Work-co lent at interest. The effects of the arrangement, it was contended, was that by having Work-co pay the interest the return to Bert-co and Allen-co by reason of their shareholding in Top-co, would be diminished to the same extent. It was noticeable during the hearing that the accountant, who had expressed such complete confidence in the efficacy of the arrangements originally contemplated did not express any such firm confidence as to the fiscal efficacy of the "amalgamation".

21. As has already been indicated, in relation to the original loan, any view that interest on money borrowed with a view to being on-lent is not to be deductible if the moneys are on-lent at no interest, or at a lesser rate of interest than borrowing rate, is too simplistic a view of entitlement to tax deductibility. In particular, such a view does not accurately express the principles underlying the decision of a full bench of the Federal Court of Australia in Ure v F.C.T. (81 A.T.C. 4100). Whether the Applicant is entitled to the deductions it claims depends upon its capacity to satisfy the requirements of Section 51(1) of the Act. The provisions of that sub-section (as presently relevant) state:

"All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are .... necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such Income, shall be allowable deductions "

22. The term "necessarily" is not to be strictly construed. As a full bench of the High Court of Australia said in Ronpibon Tin W.L. v F.C.T. [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 C.L.R. 47:

"The word 'necessarily' no doubt limits the operation of the alternative, but probably it is intended to mean no more than 'clearly appropriate or adapted for'."

Again, as Dixon C.J. said in F.C.T v Snowden & Willson Pty Ltd [1958] HCA 23; (1958) 99 C.L.R. 431 (at 436 & 437):

"Clearly the expression ('necessarily') is used in relation to business. Logical necessity is not a thing to be predicated of business expenditure. What is meant by the qualification is that the expenditure must be dictated by the business ends to which is directed, those ends forming part of or being truly incidental to the business."

23. I hold that, in so far as the Applicant made the original borrowing and used the moneys in part to repay the interest-free loan it had enjoyed from its parent company and in part to advance moneys on interest-free loan to its parent company thereby avoiding any claim being made against it for rent, the requirements of the Section are satisfied.

24. The same principles are to be applied in assessing the deductibility of interest payable by Work-co on the "amalgamation" loan. However, in relation to the "amalgamation" loan it is necessary to more closely consider a further question: whether the entirety of the interest payable by Work-co on the borrowings should be so deductible. The question arises because Section 51(1) of the Act only allows for deductibility "to the extent to which" the criteria of the Section already quoted have been satisfied. As the evidence stands, I accept that to the extent to which the loan was commercially effective to off-set any claim that there might have been to rent, the deduction is allowable. Upon the evidence I am not persuaded that there was any other profit-making purpose of Work-co or any profit-making objective of Work-co which occasioned the making of any loan in a sum in excess of the amount appropriate to off-setting such a claim. To that extent I am not persuaded that liability in Work-co to interest arose in the course of the profit-making activities and endeavours of Work-co. The evidence does not suggest, let alone establish, that the loan was made interest free as a cost incurred, for example, in securing the services of employees by providing them with "fringe benefits". As to the additional moneys lent, it was merely a means of making funds available interest-free from Work-co to its holding company and whereby Work-co suffered itself to bear the interest burden in doing so. That was not an income-producing purpose of Work-co in any relevant sense.

25. In an endeavour to effect the apportionment which must be made in accordance with the principles expressed in Ronpibon (ante) I have regard to the evidence, such as it is, as to the rental value of the premises made available to Work-co by Top-co. On 9 December 1987 a registered valuer fixed on $40r000 per annum as the value in the market place of a tenancy of those premises at that date. To accommodate the standards said to have been (... acceptable to the Commissioner, Work-co made provision for payment of $32,000 by way of rent to Top-co in the year of income ended 30 June 1987 and a figure of $29,900 for the year of income ended 30 June 1986. If the evidence had been more fully developed it might have persuaded me that those figures were commercially unrealistic or unreasonable in that they were too low. I am conscious that in adopting them Work-co and Top-co were not so much concerned to fix upon a full and fair rent for those years such as would have been appropriate to parties dealing at arms length, but rather only to fix the rent sufficient to satisfy the "requirements" of the Commissioner.

26. But as the evidence was not so developed I must make a judgment upon the evidence available. I conclude by finding that for the year of income ended 30 June 1986 - a year during the second half of which only the "amalgamation" loan had been on foot - interest claimed at $32,676 was deductible to the extent only of $27,900.

27. Additional Tax

There are several aspects to be considered in relation to the problem of reviewing the Commissioner's exercise of discretion in the matter of remitting additional tax for incorrect returns. First, in presenting the formal documents to the Tribunal the Commissioner conveniently disclosed that the amounts fixed upon for additional tax had been calculated in accordance with his then current practice as follows:

Year ended 30 June 1984 1985 1986

Culpability component at the

rate of 40% of tax avoided 743 2,409 6,735

Interest component at the

rate of 20% per annum 810 1,459 175

Total penalties 1,553 3,868 6,910

28. The practice is to be commended. It is a convenient way of informing the Applicant and the Tribunal as to just how it is that the Commissioner claims to have gone about his duty. It would be even better if it showed the dates from which and to which the interest component was calculated. However, it would be of even far greater advantage to the community if the same information could be provided to taxpayers at the point of assessment.

29. The second thing to note is that no reference was made in the notices of objection to any challenge being instituted to the manner in which the Commissioner went about discharging his duty to consider remissions. Nonetheless, the Commissioner's representative conceded at the outset that it was not contended on behalf of the Commissioner that that circumstance would preclude the Tribunal from granting such relief as it thought fit. Some such relief might follow upon a reduction in taxable income. In view of the concession made I will proceed to consider the possibility of further remissions.

30. Next, not only were the lesser two elements in the adjustment not disputed in so far as they went to the calculation of taxable income, but no evidence was placed before the Tribunal in the course of the hearing as to the circumstances giving rise to the "errors". It is not possible to say whether the items adjusted were occasioned by simple error or deliberate deceit. However, as to that the Commissioner's representative has conceded that, should any further remission be considered appropriate in relation to the "interest" issues, the Commissioner accepts that there should be further remissions at the same rate in relation to the other items also. The concession will only avail the Applicant if the Applicant is unsuccessful in relation to all, or some part, of the interest issues.

31. Further, only shortly prior to the hearing the Commissioner issued Income Tax Ruling 2517: a ruling in which he substantially revised his former rulings and the directions to his staff. The new Ruling establishes norms which will substantially mitigate the harshness of previous practice. The Commissioner's representative immediately conceded that questions of remission should now be considered in the light of that Ruling rather than in the light of practices and rulings current at the date the assessment was raised.

32. For that reason, the Commissioner's representative proposes that there should be further remissions in that a rate of 14.026% per annum should be substituted in the calculation for the rate of 20% per annum; and that a "culpability factor" at the rate of 30% of tax avoided should be substituted for a rate of 40% of tax avoided. The Applicant went to the high point of optimism in responding to that proposal by suggesting that there should be a remission not only of the "cupability factor" but also of the compensatory factor. The latter proposition was abandoned promptly and, in my view, correctly so.

33. Three questions remain to be considered. The first of them can be simply dealt with. In so far as I have held that the interest claims arising in consequence of the original borrowing and on-lending are allowable, additional tax for incorrect return will be wholly remitted. Secondly, in so far as I have held that there was no entitlement to an interest deduction in relation to the gratuitously assumed interest burdens in the interest of Bert-co and Allen-co, I have to form a view as to the appropriate rate for the "cupability factor". The Commissioner's representative contended that the claim was made "recklessly", but the same submission was put in relation to the claim correctly made in relation to the original borrowing. I think the term is too strong. But, on the other hand, in relation to the "amalgamation", I do not consider it to be anything so simple as a misunderstanding of a difficult point of law. In my view there was no substantial basis for making the claim as it was made, and it should not have been made. I accept that Bert and Allen were not conscious of the relevant principles but Work-co, through them, did take the advice of its accountants and it must accept the consequences of doing so. In my view justice will he done if the culpability factor is fixed at 20%.

34. The third and final question is also now capable of being simply resolved. I think it proper to apply the concession offered by the Commissioner in relation to the other adjustments and with those also the culpability rate will be reduced to 20%.

35. In reviewing penalties I take into account reductions in taxable income; reduction of the culpability rate from 40% to 20%; and reduction of the compensatory rate from 20% p.a. to 14.026% p.a. I also have regard to the circumstance that the period of avoidance was greater than seems to have been allowed for.

Conclusions:

37. For the foregoing reasons the order of the Tribunal will be that for the years of income

(a) ended 30 June 1984 taxable income will be reduced by $2,536 and additional tax to $370;

(b) ended 30 June 1985 taxable income will be reduced by $11,346 and additional tax to $320;

(c) ended 30 June 1986 taxable income will be reduced by $27,900 and additional tax to $800.
