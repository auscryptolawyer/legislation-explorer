VT85/270-272 and Ors and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 219; (1987) 18 ATR 3619; 87 ATC 743 (4 June 1987)

Last Updated: 20 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - assessment under ITA Act before taxpayer company dissolved - amended assessment under s15(3) of T(UCT) Act after dissolution - amended assessment under s15(3) referred to as notional assessment - whether proviso to s. 185 of ITA Act applicable - effect of s15(9) and (12) of T(UCT) Act discussed

Income Tax - a 166 ITA Act assessment made when taxpayer company already dissolved - assessment invalid

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 166, 170, 173, 190(b)

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ss. 5, 15(3), (4), (9), (12), 18(1)

Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 s. 223

1. The decision under review in respect of all the objections to which the applications in these proceedings relate, except the decision in respect of the objection to which application No. VT85/270 relates, are affirmed.

2. The decision under review in respect of the objection to which application No. VT85/270 relates, that is to say the objection against the assessment made in respect of company A of ordinary company tax payable for the 1974 tax year, is set aside and the matter is remitted to the Commissioner of Taxation for reconsideration in accordance with a direction that the assessment was invalid because it was issued in respect of a company which had been dissolved and was not issued in pursuance of the provisions of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982.

(sgd I. R. Thompson)

Detupy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) Nos. VT84/49-51

) VT85/270-272, 1732

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION ) VT86/5414-5416, 5419

VT87/1457, 1492

REASONS FOR DECISION

  1. In 1974 Z was the Managing Director of five private companies which formed a group. They were respectively companies A, B, C, D and E. At least two of those companies, C and E, were trading also in the taxation year ended 30 June 1973. Companies A, B, D and E have all been dissolved. A, B and E were dissolved on 23 June 1976 and D on 19 September 1975. Company C is still listed.
  2. On 5 July 1982 an assessment was issued in respect of company A for ordinary company tax for the 1974 tax year. It was issued pursuant to section 166 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITA Act"). On 31 March 1983 another assessment was issued in respect of company A, on that occasion of tax payable under Division 7 of Part III of the ITA Act in respect of undistributed profits of the company in the 1974 tax year. That assessment was issued pursuant to section 15(4) of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("the T(UCT) Act") and served on X as vendor shareholder. An objection against it was lodged by X as nominated vendor shareholder on 27 May 1983.

3. In July 1982 an assessment was issued in respect of company B relating to ordinary company tax for the 1974 tax year. It was issued pursuant to section 166 of the ITA Act; as the company had already been dissolved, it was invalid. However, on 30 March 1983 another assessment was issued, on that occasion pursuant to section 15(4) of the T(UCT) Act in respect of ordinary company tax. On the same day an assessment was issued pursuant to section 15(4) in respect of Division 7 tax on undistributed profits in the 1974 tax year. Both assessments were served on Y as vendor shareholder. His objections as nominated vendor shareholder against those assessments were lodged on 27 May 1983.

4. On 5 July 1982 an assessment was issued in respect of company C relating to ordinary company tax for the 1974 tax year. Subsequently, an amended assessment was issued under section 166 of the ITA Act; but pursuant to section 18(1) of the T(UCT) Act it was served on Y as vendor shareholder. On 31 March 1983 an amended assessment in respect of company C relating to ordinary company tax for the 1974 tax year and an assessment relating to Division 7 tax on undistributed profit in the 1974 tax year were also issued under section 166 of the ITA Act. Those assessments were served pursuant to section 18(1) of the T(UCT) Act on Y as vendor shareholder. He lodged objections against them as nominated vendor shareholder on 27 May 1983.

5. On 17 August 1983 assessments in respect of company D relating to ordinary company tax for the 1974 tax year and Division 7 tax on undistributed profits tax in the 1974 tax year were issued pursuant to section 15(4) of the T(UCT) Act and served on Z as vendor shareholder. He lodged objections against them as nominated vendor shareholder on 11 October 1983.

6. In respect of company E an assessment of Division 7 tax on undistributed profits in the 1973 tax year was issued pursuant to section 166 of the ITA Act in 1974; an objection was lodged by the company. On 22 August 1983 an amended assessment was issued pursuant to section 15(3) of the T(UCT) Act, the objection being allowed and the amended assessment being in terms of what had been sought by the objection. It was served on Z as vendor shareholder. An objection against that assessment was lodged by Z as nominated vendor shareholder on 11 October 1983.

  1. On 1 July 1985 assessments in respect of recoupment tax under section 5 of the T(UCT) Act were issued to X and Z. In each case they related to tax unpaid by company E and in particular to ordinary company tax for 1973 and 1974 and undistributed profits tax for 1974. Objections against those assessments were lodged on 22 August 1985.
  2. All the objections were disallowed by the Commissioner. The persons objecting then requested referral to a Board of Review or, depending on the date of the request, the Administrative Appeals Tribunal; they were referred accordingly. Objections referred to a Board of Review and not determined by the Board are required to be heard and determined by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (section 223, Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986). It was agreed by the parties that the Tribunal should hear all of the references together. At the hearing the objectors were represented by Mr G. Bigmore, a solicitor, and the respondent by Mr G. Nettle, of Counsel. Various documents were tendered in evidence. Oral evidence was given by Mr A. T. Snowsill, an officer of the Australian Taxation Office, who was called as a witness by Mr Bigmore.
  3. At the commencement of the hearing Mr Nettle conceded that the assessment issued under section 166 of the ITA Act on 5 July 1982 in respect of company A and relating to ordinary company tax for 1974 was invalid because before that date that company had been dissolved. We accept that that concession was properly made and our decision reflects that fact. Mr Nettle informed the Tribunal that an assessment in respect of ordinary company tax for 1974 would be issued shortly pursuant to section 15(4) of the T(UCT) Act.
  4. Although the objection against the assessment issued on 22 August 1983 in respect of company E in relation to Division 7 tax for the 1973 tax year had been referred to the Tribunal in accordance with Z'3 request, Mr Nettle submitted that it was not a valid objection. That was because in 1974 an assessment in respect of the 1973 tax year had been issued and an objection lodged. The assessment issued on 22 August 1983 was an amended assessment issued under section 15(3) of the T(UCT) Act; it was for a smaller amount than the original assessment, imposed no fresh liability on the company and did not increase its existing liability in respect of any particular. It is apparent from the notice of assessment that the reduction in the liability of the company made by that assessment was the result of the objection lodged in 1974.
  5. The significance of the possible invalidity of the objection lodged in respect of the 1983 assessment for the 1973 tax year is that, by virtue of section 5(1) of the T(UCT) Act, a prerequisite of the issue of a notice of assessment in respect of recoupment tax to vendor shareholders of a company that has ceased to exist is that the period for objecting against the assessment in respect of the company has expired and any objection against the assessment has been finalised.
  6. Mr Nettle submitted that on 1 July 1985, when the notices of assessment of recoupment tax were issued to X and Z, the objection against the assessment made in 1974 had been finalised and that by reason of the proviso to section 185 of the ITA Act neither the company nor a vendor shareholder had any right to object to the 1983 assessment. No fresh liability had been imposed on the company and its existing liability had not been increased in respect of any particular. Mr Bigmore referred us to section 15(3) of the T(UCT) Act, which provides that an amended assessment issued under that sub-section is referred to in section 15 as a "notional assessment", and to the fact that the same expression is used in section 15(4) in respect of assessments issued under that sub-section where the company has ceased to exist before any assessment has been made. He then referred to sub-sections (9) and (12) of section 15. Those two sub-sections are as follows:-

"15. (9) Where notice of the notional assessment is served under sub-section (6) or (7) -

(a) if the notice was served under sub-section (6) or sub-paragraph (7)(a)(i) - the person on whom the notice was served; and

(b) if the notice was served under sub-paragraph (7)(a)(ii) - one person who -

(i) is included in the representative class in relation to the recoupment tax referred to in paragraph (3)(c) or (4Kb), as the case may be, that is vendors recoupment tax; and

(ii) is nominated, by notice signed by more than one-half of the persons included in that class and lodged with the Commissioner,

has the same rights under Division 2 Part V of the Assessment Act in respect of the notional assessment as the company would have had if it had continued to exist and the notional assessment were an assessment of ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax, as the case may be, payable by the company in relation to the relevant year of income and, for the purpose of the exercise of those rights by the person, the provisions of that Division apply in relation to notional assessment in like manner as those provisions apply in relation to an assessment under the Assessment Act.

(12) For the purposes of ascertaining the notional company tax that is applicable, at a particular time, in relation to the company in relation to the relevant year of income by virtue of the notional assessment, the provisions of the Assessment Act apply in relation to the notional assessment and the notional ordinary company tax or notional undistributed profits tax, as the case may be, to which the notional assessment relates as if -

(a) the notional assessment were an assessment of the ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax, as the case may be, payable by the company in relation to the relevant year of income; and

(b) the notional ordinary company tax or notional undistributed profits tax, as the case may be, were ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax, as the case may be."

13. Mr Nettle submitted that, because section 15(9) confers on a nominated vendor shareholder the same rights under Division 2 of Part V of the ITA Act in respect of a notional assessment as the company to which it relates would have had if it had continued to exist and the notional assessment were an assessment issued under the ITA Act, the proviso to section 185 was applicable. Mr Bigmore submitted that to construe section 15(9) in that manner would be contrary to natural justice and would deprive the vendor shareholders of a right to object to an assessment in circumstances where they might not have been in a position to cause an objection to be lodged against the original notice of assessment which was issued before the company was dissolved.

14. We are satisfied that the provisions of section 15(9) are quite clear. Although the amended assessment issued under section 15(3) is referred to in the T(UCT) Act as a notional assessment, that does not change its character. Section 170 of the ITA Act provides for the amendment of assessments issued pursuant to section 166 of that Act; section 173 then provides that, except as otherwise provided, every amended assessment is to be an assessment for all purposes of that Act. Section 185, under which objections against amended assessments issued under section 173 are made, is in terms of dissatisfaction with an assessment and in terras of lodging an objection against the assessment. There is, therefore, in our view nothing significant in the fact that the amended assessment issued under section 15(3) of the T(UCT) Act is referred to as a notional assessment. It is clearly required by section 15(9) to be treated for the purposes of objection against it as though it were an amended assessment issued under section 170 of the ITA Act. We have no doubt that, where section 185 of the ITA Act was applicable as in force before 1 July 1986 (when it was amended by section 79 of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986), a vendor shareholder did not have a right to make an objection against an amended assessment issued under section 15(3) of the T(UCT) Act which did not impose fresh liability on the company or increase its existing liability in respect of any particular.

15. We dealt with that question as a preliminary issue at the hearing and ruled that the purported objection lodged by Z against the amended assessment issued in October 1983 was invalid. Although the Commissioner had referred it to the Tribunal, that fact of itself could not give it a validity which it would not otherwise have had. When the recoupment tax assessment was issued, the only valid objection against the original assessment of Division 7 tax had been finalised by the issue of the amended assessment in the terms sought by that objection. We gave our reasons for that ruling orally and undertook to set them out in these reasons for our decision on the objections. We are satisfied that the finality which was prerequisite for issue of the assessments of recoupment tax to X and Z on 1 July 1985 had been reached before that date.

16. So far as the assessments in respect of companies A, B, C, D and E are concerned, no evidence of the accounts of the companies for the tax years to which they relate was presented to the Tribunal, except for a combined trading and profit and loss statement for the group of five companies for the 8 months ended 28 February 1974. That was annexed to an auditor's report prepared in 1974 for the purpose of the sale of the companies' shares by their shareholders. It showed that the group had a net operating profit of $261,811.00 and that provision of $122,229.00 was made for income tax. That provision is broadly consistent with the total of the amounts of ordinary company tax assessed in respect of the companies (including the invalid assessment in respect of company A). So, far from the evidence establishing that there was error in any of the assessments, it tends to support them. At the end of the hearing Mr Bigmore conceded that, on the basis of the evidence before the Tribunal, the applicants had not discharged the burden, imposed on them by section 190(b) of the ITA Act and 15(9) of the T(UCT) Act, of proving that the assessments were excessive. Accordingly, we have affirmed them.

17. Liability of vendor shareholders to the payment of recoupment tax arises where the circumstances set out in section 5 of the T(UCT) Act exist. Mr Snowsill gave evidence of investigations which he made into matters relating to the sale of shares in the five companies by the vendor shareholders. In these proceedings we are concerned only with recoupment tax assessed in relation to company E. Documents were tendered in evidence which established the manner in which the sale of the shares in all the companies in the group was arranged, the amounts paid to the shareholders for those shares, the assets of the companies, the "cashing up" of the companies and the stripping of them of their cash assets by the purchasers of the shares. They established, in respect of those companies which had been dissolved, that immediately before they ceased to exist they would have been unable to pay to the Commissioner all the company tax that would have been due and payable by them then if the company tax due and payable by them after the commencement of the T(UCT) Act had been due and payable by them at that same time. Again, therefore, far from the evidence tending to establish that the assessments in respect of X and Z in relation to vendors recoupment tax are excessive, they tend to establish that they are not excessive. Mr Bigraore again conceded that, on the evidence before the Tribunal, X and Z had not discharged the burden of proving that the assessments were excessive. We are satisfied that that is so and we have, therefore, affirmed those assessments.
