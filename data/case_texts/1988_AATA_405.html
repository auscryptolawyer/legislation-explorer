Van Noord and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 405 (18 February 1988)

Last Updated: 26 February 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

FREEDOM OF INFORMATION - request for documents relating to taxation affairs - legal professional privilege - sole purpose test applied exemptions claimed on other grounds upheld.

Freedom of Information Act 1982 - ss 37(1)(a), 38, 42(1), 64(1)

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s.16

Grant v. Downs [1976] HCA 63; 11 ALR 577.

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Swiss Aluminium Australia Limited 66 ALR 159.

REASONS FOR DECISION

18 February, 1988. Deputy President R.C. Jennings Q.C.,

The applicant seeks to review a decision whereby he was refused access to a volume of documents which related to his financial and taxation affairs between 1978 and 1986. They included many documents in respect of which access was refused in part only.

The Tribunal received the T documents lodged pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. The respondent lodged an affidavit by a senior officer of the Australian Taxation Office ("ATO"), Mr. E.A. Brett. An application made pursuant to s.35(2)(c) of that Act that the contents of paragraphs 5 to 9 inclusive be not disclosed to the applicant was granted. The applicant's counsel was permitted to cross-examine Mr. Brett but adduced no evidence.

At the request of the Tribunal all the documents to which access was sought were produced for inspection pursuant to s.64(1) of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the Act"). During the hearing the Tribunal expressed the view that several passages from some of the documents which had been partially released should be also released to the applicant. Counsel for the respondent agreed and accordingly the Tribunal read those passages aloud. It is assumed that copies of those extracts have been or will be supplied to counsel for the applicant and that it is accordingly unnecessary to refer to them further. Liberty to apply will be reserved in due course should it become necessary to seek any further directions in relation to those passages.

The grounds upon which exemption was claimed by the respondent were described in paragraphs 11 to 19 inclusive of Mr. Brett's affidavit and fully detailed in six schedules to the s.37 statement referred to above.

The applicant's counsel said that he did not pursue the claim for release of documents which clearly related to the personal affairs of other persons. Nor did he seek disclosure of the names of ATO officers which had been deleted from documents to which access had been otherwise permitted. The Tribunal is satisfied after careful inspection of all the documents listed in the fifth and third schedules that the basis for exemption described in paragraphs 16 and 17 respectively of Mr. Brett's affidavit is established upon the grounds referred to in s.41(1) and s.40(1)(c),(d) and (e). It is unnecessary in view of counsel's concession to deal with these claims any further.

Section 37(1) (a) of the Act provides as follows:-

"37.(1) A document is an exempt document if its disclosure under this Act would, or could reasonably be expect to -

(a) prejudice the conduct of an investigation of a breach, or possible breach, of the law, or a failure, to comply with a law relating to taxation or prejudice the enforcement or proper administration of the law in a particular instance".

The documents to which the applicant sought access were all documents relating to the income tax investigation of his affairs. They were divided into several groups marked as follows:-

Audit File (Copies of unedited versions of edited and exempt documents)

Audit Working Papers (Purchases)

Audit Working Papers (Bank accounts)

Audit Working Papers (Gross Sales)

Returns of Income

Appeal Files

Recovery File

Those paragraphs of the affidavit (5 - 9) marked by direction "For Tribunal Eyes Only" relate specifically to the circumstances by reason of which the applicant's affairs became the subject of investigation, the results of those investigations and subsequent developments.

The affidavit then proceeds as follows:-

"10. Amended assessments were issued in respect of years ended 30 June 1978 to 30 June 1984 inclusive. Objections to these assessments were disallowed and have been referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal. These actions before the Tribunal have been put aside pending a decision in the FOI matter. Recovery proceedings for the collection of outstanding amounts have commenced in the Supreme Court of Tasmania.

  1. The documents have been assembled in the course of an investigation undertaken by the Commissioner into the affairs of the applicant. The investigation is still in progress and it is expected to be some time before it is completed. Those documents listed in column 1 of the schedule accompanying the section 37 statement are the subject of a claim for exemption under paragraph 37(1)(a) of the FOI Act.
  2. In the conduct of such investigations, the Commissioner obtains information from various sources in response to official queries and by independent enquiries. The information thus obtained in this case is contained in the investigation working papers, as are file notes and other internal records prepared from time to time in the course of the investigation. If the extent and results of enquiries made the Commissioner were disclosed prior to the conclusion of the overall investigation, it is possible, and indeed could reasonably be expected, that a person connected with the investigation may frame responses to enquiries which is known to be already in the possession of the ATO. Similarly, the direction of enquiries would be revealed, enabling a person to anticipate questions and supply answers which would frustrate the investigation by diverting or misleading the investigating officer. Disclosure would also make a person aware not only of how much information the Commissioner possesses but of how much he has not acquired, thereby facilitating concealment of relevant facts and implementation of counter measures.

13. For the reasons outlined above, I consider that the release of documents listed in column 1 of the section 37 statement, to the extent that they contain such material, could reasonably be expected to have a prejudicial effect on the conduct of an investigation of a failure or possible failure to comply with a law relating to taxation or on the proper administration of the law in this instance within the terms of paragraph 37(1)(a) of the FOI Act."

Having examined the documents produced and considered the material in paragraphs 5 to 9 of the affidavit, the Tribunal is satisfied that all the documents in respect of which an exemption is claimed under s.37(1)(a) are properly so claimed. This is because their disclosure could reasonably be expected to prejudice the conduct of an investigation of a breach, or possible breach, of the law, or a failure to comply with a law relating to taxation.

Mr. Brett admitted under cross-examination that he could give no indication as to how long the investigation might continue, but the Tribunal does not consider that this uncertainty affects the possibility of the kind of prejudice described in the Act.

In view of these findings it is unnecessary to consider the alternative claim for exemption under s.37 (2) (b) advanced in respect of some of the documents the disclosure of which could prejudice the conduct of an investigation.

Section 38 of the Act provides as follows:-

"38. A document is an exempt document if there is in force an enactment applying specifically to information of a kind contained in the document and prohibiting persons referred to in the enactment from disclosing information of that kind, whether the prohibition is absolute or is subject to exceptions or qualifications."

Paragraph 15 of Mr. Brett's affidavit states:-

"Certain segments of documents listed in the Fourth Schedule attached to the section 37 statement are the subject of a claim for exemption under section 38 of the FOI Act. These documents are internal memoranda, letters from third parties and banking documents. The information contained in these documents was obtained or acquired by officers of the ATO in the course of their employment in accordance with the provisions of the ITAA, and relates to the affairs of parties other than the applicant. It is not uncommon for the affairs of other persons to be involved with those of a person subject to investigation in such a way that section 16 of the ITAA applies precluding release of information relating to them to that person. In other cases, records relating to another person in whose affairs similar issues arise might be included on a person's file for reference purposes and as a step in assembling arguments for use in defence of any assessments ultimately raised. The disclosure to the applicant of the material so acquired is prohibited by the secrecy provisions of section 16 of the ITAA. It is considered that section 16 of the ITAA is a secrecy provision of an enactment to which section 38 of the FOI Act applies, and that the documents listed in the Fourth Schedule attached to the section 37 statement are to the extent that they contain such information exempt documents pursuant to section 38 of the FOI Act."

A longstanding difference of opinion amongst members of this Tribunal as to the proper interpretation of s.38 of the Act and in particular whether s.16 of the Income Tax Assessment. Act ("ITAA"} is a secrecy provision of an enactment to which s.38 applies was resolved in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Swiss Aluminium Australia Limited 66 ALR 159, a decision of a full Federal Court which was affirmed by the High Court when an appeal to it was dismissed because it was incompetent [1987] HCA 43; (73 ALR 584).

However the High Court also held as follows:

"The effect of the decision of the Full Court was not finally to determine the rights of the appellant. The effect of the decision was to establish that D79 was a document to be classified as an exempt document. But the decision did not determine that the appellant had not right of access to it. The making of such a determination depended on a decision that the document contained no matter other than exempt matter. Until the Tribunal answered the question whether all the matter was exempt the matter and determined that the appellant had no right of access to the document, finality was not achieved."

The majority in the Federal Court (Bowen CJ and Jackson J) had held that the document marked D79 was exempt on the ground that 3.16 of the ITAA is an enactment applying specifically to information of a kind contained in the document and prohibiting persons referred to in the enactment from disclosing information of that kind.

In this case the Tribunal is satisfied that the documents in question contain no matter other than exempt matter.

Section 42 (1) of the Act provides:-

"42.(1) A document is an exempt document if it is of such a nature that it would be privileged from production in legal proceedings on the ground of legal professional privilege."

Paragraph 18 of the affidavit states:-

"The documents itemised in the Sixth Schedule attached to the section 37 statement are letters to and from the Australian Government Solicitor's office and internal memoranda concerning legal advice on the course of the investigation and debt collection procedures. It is considered that the information contained in these documents would be privileged from production in legal proceedings on the ground of legal professional privilege and accordingly are considered exempt in terms of subsection 42(1) of the FOI Act."

The Tribunal required counsel to reveal that Folios 151-152 were a letter from the respondent dated 10 August 1983 and Folios 153-154 was the reply to that request dated 16 August 1983.

Counsel for the applicant quite properly argued that this claim could only be sustained if the sole purpose of the request was to procure legal advice and that it was the responsibility of the Tribunal to satisfy itself that it did not have a twofold purpose.

The sole purpose test was settled by the High Court in Grant v Downs [1976] HCA 63; 11 ALR 577. It was stated by Stephen, Mason & Murphy JJ in the following terms at page 588:-

"All that we have said so far indicates that unless the law confines legal professional privilege to those documents which are brought into existence for the sole purpose of submission to legal advisers for advice or for use in legal proceedings the privilege will travel beyond the underlying rationale to which it is intended to give expression and will confer an advantage and immunity on a corporation which is not enjoyed by the ordinary individual. It is not right that the privilege can attach to documents which, quite apart from the purpose of submission to a solicitor, would have been brought into existence for other purposes in any event, and then without attracting any attendant privilege. It is true that the requirement that documents be brought into existence in anticipation of litigation diminishes to some extent the risk that documents brought into existence for non-privileged purposes will attract the privilege but it certainly does not eliminate that risk. For this and the reasons which we have expressed earlier we consider that the sole purpose test should now be adopted as the criterion of legal professional privilege."

The letter of 10 August describes at some length the history of the applicant's tax returns since 1978, the objections he made to assessments and his failure to pay tax assessments issued to him. It proceeds to describe the results of some of the investigations made into his affairs and to discuss various courses of action which might be taken. It then concludes:-

"Please advise of your recommendations on this matter and the necessary steps to be taken if you agree that..... (etc)."

There is no doubt that the request sought no more than legal advice and is accordingly entitled to exemption from disclosure.

The reply which is a direct response to the request deals specifically with a number of legal alternatives and is accordingly entitled to the same exemption.

The decision under review is affirmed, but there will be liberty to apply for the reasons already referred to.
