Last Updated: 4 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION - Whether proceeds from sale of certain "strata titled units" fall within the provisions of ss.25(1) or the second limb of ss26(a) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - discussion of applicable principles including Myer Emporium case - method of calculating quantum of interest deductible in terms of s51(1) method of calculating quantum of s.80 loss to be carried forward discussed - whether taxpayer liable to pay additional tax-pursuant to ss226(1) and (2) of the Act.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss 25(1), 26(a), 26AAA, 51(1), 80, 80(2), 95, 99A, 223, 224, 225, 226(1), 226(2).

Taxation Board of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986

Decision: The determinations of the Commissioner of Taxation under review be set aside and in lieu thereof, a decision is substituted that:

  1. The proceeds resulting from the sale of the apartments do not represent assessable income under subs 25(1) nor does the profit from their disposal come within the ambit of the second limb of s 26(a) as it then existed.
  2. Of the amount of interest paid by the company during the year of income ended 30 June 1981 no greater amount than $46,401 is allowable as a deduction under subs 51(1).
  3. No greater deduction than $63,422 is allowable to the company in terms of subs 80(2) for losses claimed to have been incurred in the years of income ended 30 June 1979 and 1980.
  4. No income was omitted from the returns of Mr & Mrs S and thus they are not liable to pay additional tax in terms of subs 226(2).
  5. The taxpayers not having furnished returns of income for the year ended 30 June 1981 as and when required by the Act each of them is liable under subs 226(1) for additional tax to the extent of $2.

(Sgnd) Mr Justice FR Fisher

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) Nos. ST85/34,35 and 36

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

RE:

Applicants

AND: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

14 December, 1987 Mr Justice FR Fisher

Deputy President RA Layton

Mr DJ Trowse

In this matter three objections, which had earlier been disallowed by the Commissioner of Taxation, were referred to a Taxation Board of Review in accordance with requests dated 12 August 1985. By virtue of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 they are directed to be heard by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal ("the Tribunal"). The taxpayers comprise two natural persons, husband and wife (Mr & Mrs S) and a company (J Nominees Pty Ltd hereinafter called "the company"). Mr S gave his occupation as that of a builder and the company was trustee of a family trust.

By consent the three applications for review were heard concurrently by the Tribunal although at all times during the hearing attention was directed primarily to the assessment raised by the Commissioner against the company as such trustee. The Commissioner relied at the outset upon the provisions of subs25(1) and both limbs of s26(a) of the Income Tax subs25(1) and both limbs of s26(a) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 as amended ("the Act") to support the company's assessment which related to the year of income ending 30 June 1981. However at the time of opening his case in defence of that assessment, his counsel abandoned any reliance upon the first limb of s26(a).

The amount of deduction allowable to the company in respect of outgoings incurred in earlier years, pursuant to subs80(2), also requires our attention, as does the quantum of additional tax imposed by the Commissioner for the late lodgement of returns by the company and Mr & Mrs S Another matter originally in issue was the action taken by the Commissioner in imposing additional tax for incorrect returns. However, in a letter forwarded to the Tribunal after the conclusion of the hearing, the Commissioner's representative indicated that his client intended to remit those penalties in full and cited the decision of the Full Federal Court in North Coast Grazing Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 87 ATC 4553 as authority for that proposed action.

With respect to the assessments based on subs25(1) and s26(a) however, the ultimate conclusion on the assessability or otherwise of receipts under these sections is essentially a question of fact or at least of mixed fact and law. These references raise difficult and contested questions of fact, and recent decisions of the High Court in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Whitford's Beach Pty Ltd [1982] HCA 8; (1982) 150 CLR 355 and Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Myer Emporium Ltd [1987] HCA 18; (1987) 71 ALR 28 indicate that the relevant law is not always easy to identify or apply in circumstances as complex as the present. It is therefore necessary to set out in some detail and at length the relevant facts particularly because there were some conflicts of evidence and because of the inability of many witnesses to recall relevant happenings.

First of all it is appropriate to identify briefly what is involved in the references. In the return of the family trust for the year of income ending 30 June 1981 the company included an amount of $487,682 which it claimed to be a capital profit produced by the sale of certain "strata titled units" (hereinafter called "apartments"). The Commissioner's contention, which only became apparent at the time he filed his statement to the Board of Review in accordance with subregulation 35(1), was that this sum, being portion of a larger amount, namely $581,922 which he then claimed to be the relevant profit, should be included in the net income of the company, as trustee of a trust, under subs25(1) or s26(a) of the Act. This profit arose from the sale of apartments which had been constructed by the company in a building which it had purchased as a concrete and steel shell a few years earlier. The sale of the apartments occurred during the year of income and the proceeds of sale and the costs of construction were all brought to account in the return for that year.

We will deal in first instance with background circumstances and subsequently fill in the detail in respect of happenings which we see as significant topics. Certain background information was contained in a statement supplied to the Commissioner in 1982 which did not always coincide with oral evidence, although ultimately this divergence is not in our opinion of any great significance.

Mr S was born in Germany in February 1934 and came to South Australia in 1955 with his father who was a building engineer. In Germany the father had operated a construction company which had been engaged in building apartments or repairing building blocks which had been damaged during the war, and had specialized in high rise buildings. Mr S was apprenticed to his father's firm as a carpenter. On completion of his apprenticeship Mr S worked with his father in a managerial position and for a period of two years undertook architectural studies.

On arriving in South Australia Mr S and his father worked in partnership sub-contracting as carpenters and joiners to the South Australian Housing Trust. They then established a joinery workshop producing general joinery products.

At the same time as the establishment of the workshop Mr S and his father commenced general building particularly in the area of speculative housing, flats and home units. They mainly built on land provided by sub-divisional developers with payments therefor deferred until the completion and sale of particular buildings. This partnership between Mr S and his father continued for a period of 12 years in which partnership Mr S provided the labour and his father controlled and provided the finance.

Mr S and his wife, whom he had married in 1961, then decided to carry on their own business. Land, near the Adelaide Airport, was purchased by them in 1966 on which Mr S, using his building experience, constructed a 14 unit motel and manager's flat on the site. Mr S's evidence was that the land was purchased because he wanted to build a motel and that throughout the period he and his wife operated the motel as a partnership. At that time Mr S did not have a builder's licence and the work was performed in his father's name.

This business prospered by virtue of its proximity to the airport and Mr S's entrepreneurial skills in establishing a courtesy car service to and from the airport. Mr S said that his father provided him with funds to the extent of 3,000 pounds and thereafter the business was financed by borrowings guaranteed by his father and by the funds produced by the business. After some 5 years he built a further 20 motel units, a kitchen and a licensed restaurant. He and his wife managed the business. In or about 1976, 10 years after its purchase, they sold the motel for $500,000. The motel was sold because in the opinion of Mr S it was too small for his future motel ambitions and further his wife and child were eager to live in Germany. After repayment of borrowings the operation produced a cash surplus of approximately $380,000.

Mr and Mrs S then travelled to Germany seeking to establish a hotel or motel operation in Europe. They returned after 8 months bringing with them a container load of furniture. They purchased a unit in which they lived for a short time and ultimately sold. Then a residential property was acquired in circumstances which will ultimately be related in greater detail. Suffice to say that after signing a handwritten agreement for purchase with the owners of the property, they arranged for the incorporation of the company as a trust company. Mr. S's sister then settled $100 on complex trusts in favour of Mr & Mrs S and their child or children and remoter issue nominating the company as trustee. The company was incorporated on 8 November 1976 and the settlement was dated the same day. On 10 November 1976 Mr & Mrs S as directors of the taxpayer company held a meeting with their solicitor Mr H at which a formal agreement for sale and purchase by the taxpayer company of the residential property was executed. It was also resolved at that meeting that the taxpayer company, as trustee of the S family trust carry on the business of a financier and that a Credit Providers Licence should be obtained with Mr S as manager. The funds for the purchase of the residential property were provided by way of a loan by Mr and Mrs S.

In September of the following year the residential property was sold together with the furniture brought in from Germany. Mr and Mrs S then travelled to the United States. Again they investigated the prospects of acquiring a hotel or motel operation. Mr S said he was particularly interested in the Sheraton Hotel in Michigan which was for sale for US.$2,000,000. However after 3 months they returned to South Australia and resumed the search for a business proposition, in particular, a hotel or motel.

Very shortly after returning Mr S became aware of an unfinished 14 story building. It consisted of an empty shell constructed some years earlier as a motel of 14 floors with a revolving restaurant and penthouse on the top floors. Most of the floors were divided by walls into motel rooms. As reported by consultants subsequently engaged by the South Australian Development Corporation the building had "a lengthy and somewhat sorry history" in which construction commenced in 1970 but ceased because of "bankruptcy". During the final weeks prior to work ceasing on the project, concrete was poured at the upper level without professional supervision, and as a result, many rumours circulated concerning the adequacy of the concrete strength, correctness in the placement of steel reinforcement and the vertical trueness of the lift shafts.

At the end of December 1977 Mr S approached real estate agents in Adelaide and a Mr M in particular with a view to making an offer to purchase. Mr M had previously conducted three unsuccessful auction sales of the site over the past 4 years. His evidence was that he enquired of Mr S what he proposed to do with the premises to which Mr S replied that he intended to use them "as a motel or maybe strata titling the individual motel units into strata units". Mr S denied that he said anything to Mr M concerning strata title apartments. The contract was dated 29 December 1977 and settlement took place on 15 February 1978.

Prior to settlement Mr S approached the relevant City Corporation ("the City Council") and received on 12 January 1978 a letter indicating that building work could continue on the shell in accordance with amended plans which had been submitted to Council in September 1970 by the previous owner. However any further amendments to plans and specifications would require further approval from the City Council. Subsequent paragraphs of the letter indicated that the City Council would be pleased to see the completion of the building. He also received on 24 January 1970 a letter from consulting engineers indicating the work required to be done to ensure the building would carry the loads for which it was originally designed and constructed. A certificate to this effect was received on 6 February 1978. An approach was also made to consultants concerning the work to be done on mechanical, electrical and lift services. Two days before settlement a letter was written to the Premier of South Australia by Mr S's accountants indicating that he wished to complete the construction and provide accommodation to the extent of 76 motel type units, 10 executive suites and one presidential suite together with revolving restaurant, coffee shop and 4 specialty shops. The Premier was asked to assist with the finance for the project by way of a loan of $3 million from the State Bank, or alternatively a guarantee from that Bank or the Savings Bank or in the further alternative a guarantee from the State Government of outside borrowings. In each of the last two alternatives it was contemplated that the funds would be obtained from an overseas source. This letter also contained a budget of profitability of the proposed motel together with estimates of the rate of occupancy of the motel rooms and other facilities.

After settlement Mr S and his accountants and solicitors comprehensively canvassed numerous lending institutions within and outside Australia in an attempt to obtain finance to complete the building. It is not necessary to identify each of the bodies approached as it was not suggested that any possible avenue was overlooked, and evidence given by Mr S, his accountant and his solicitor was not challenged in this regard. The proposals to these institutions were at all times on the basis that Mr S intended to complete and run the building as a motel which included a restaurant and we have taken Mr S to have included a restaurant as part of his concept of a motel. Ultimately however, the only offers of finance received were contingent upon obtaining a guarantee from the State Government, the State Bank or the Savings Bank.

At the same time as efforts were being made to obtain funds to complete the project, Mr S was in touch with a large local building firm from which firm quotations were obtained for the cost of completion. These quotations were obtained for the purpose of presentation to financial institutions and not because Mr S specifically proposed to employ a builder. He made contact with the architect engaged by the original owner of the motel. Over the next year quotations were also obtained for air conditioning and mechanical services, electrical services and the revolving floor, laundry equipment and requirements, the installation of windows and lift equipment.

In June 1978 Mr S obtained an offer of finance, which offer was subject to obtaining a government guarantee and was passed to the Industries Assistance Corporation to which corporation the Premier had referred the request for financial assistance. On 4 August 1978 the SA Development Corporation (successor to the Industries Assistance Corporation), was given the information which it had requested on 27 July 1978 concerning compliance with fire protection requirements, the cost to complete the building and the terms of the borrowings arranged. Notwithstanding the fact that an offer to provide finance had been obtained, approaches continued to be made to further lending institutions with the hope of obtaining the finance he sought without government guarantees being required.

By letter dated 11 September 1978 the Development Corporation indicated that it wished to employ, at the expense of Mr S, consultants to advise on structural and economic aspects of the proposal to complete and run a motel and restaurant. The cost of the consultancy was $7,860. This was agreed. The Development Corporation advised Mr S by letter of 17 November 1978 that the consultants had presented to it a report on the feasibility of the project. The report's estimate of profitability and rate of return on investment as well as the predicted demand was more conservative than that relied on by Mr S Subsequently a revised application seeking a lesser guarantee of $2.5 million was submitted, and shortly thereafter Mr S indicated to the Development Corporation that a guarantee of $2 million would be sufficient. These requests for guarantees of lesser amounts reflected the applicant's endeavours to obtain acceptance for his proposals as well as his modified cost structures for a less luxurious motel. It appears from the correspondence that a meeting was held with the Deputy Premier on 4 April 1979 when a question was raised concerning the amount of equity which the company could contribute to the project.

It was not until 31 May 1979 that the Development Corporation finally advised Mr S that it was unable to recommend to the Premier that a guarantee be granted because of the inability of the company to contribute at least 30% of the funds required for the project. By this time Mr S was desperately in need of finance to complete the building and was unable to meet running expenses such as rates and taxes and his finances were such that he sold his motor vehicle in order to meet commitments.

At about this same time, Mr S developed an alternative plan that the motel units on floors 2 to 8 be converted to apartments and sold to fund the overall cost of completion of the building and that the remainder be retained and conducted as a motel/restaurant business.

Arrangements were made for appropriate architectural alterations be made to plans and on 10 September 1979 Mr S made further application to the City Council for consent under the Planning and Development Act to change the use of the land. The proposed use was described as "Residential Units, Restaurant (200 persons) and offices". The application was to convert all but the restaurant area into apartments on the assumption that the City Council would not approve a change of use on a piecemeal basis, namely conversion only of floors 2 to 8. Consent for this change of use was given by instrument dated 7 November 1979 but such consent was conditional upon the premises not being used for the proposed purpose until all work had been completed. By letter dated 22 November 1979 Mr S's architects informed the City Council that such conditional consent was tantamount to a refusal as the residential units, hereafter called apartments, would have to be sold floor by floor to enable working capital to be available to complete the building. On 21 December 1979 the Corporation gave its consent to a variation of the requirements of this particular provision.

On 13 September 1979 Mr S's accountants reported to the Development Corporation by letter that in consequence of the "sale of 20 units previously shown for occupancy" the cash equity of the taxpayer company was $725,000. The accountants said that additional finance to the extent of $300,000 had been arranged from this sale, but that "most of these units will be leased back to the motel which in turn will be let out as motel units". The evidence established that the 20 units comprised two floors of the structure and that prepayments, primarily from relatives, had been made in respect of the purchase of these units. By letter dated 19 October 1979 the Development Corporation indicated it would not consider further Mr S's proposal.

At about the time the application was made to the City Council on 10 September 1979, Mr S obtained a licence enabling him to operate as a builder. He proposed that the work of completion would be performed by him as the builder employing sub-contractors and under the supervision of architects and engineers. He gave evidence to the effect that he had been in touch with a number of persons who were interested in purchasing apartments and then leasing them back to the company for use as motel units. This evidence was not challenged on cross-examination. By February 1980 finance to the extent of $850,000 had been obtained from a finance company for the purpose of constructing "all 14 stories of residential apartments motel and revolving restaurant" and those funds had been made available on the basis that the proceeds of the apartments sold would be used in the repayment of indebtedness to the finance company.

On 15 April 1980 the plans and specifications for this form of development were approved by the City Council.

Later that year on 13 August 1980 an application was made to change the use of the 9th, 10th and 11th floors from apartments to motel suites, being implementation of the plan conceived by the applicant in September 1979. It was proposed that these floors be constructed as 16 motel units. Consent to this change was given on 23 October 1980.

Subsequently on 3 May 1982 application was made to reconvert the motel units on these floors to residential apartments because retention of these floors as motel units was not viable, but such consent was refused on 4 August 1982. However this application was made after the end of the year of income of the company, namely the year ending 30 June 1981.

During that year Mr S completed the construction of the apartments on floors 2-8 of the building and all but one of these apartments were sold quite indiscriminately. Mr S had made no calculations as to what money he sought for their sale, he took what was offered as he could not afford to do otherwise. Application to obtain strata titles to these apartments had been lodged on 26 November 1980 and the new Certificates of Title were issued on 18 December 1980. It was obviously not possible to sell the apartments until such time as strata titles were obtained and such titles could not as a matter of law be obtained until construction of the apartments had been completed. Additionally, the balance of the building was completed in the same year and the motel/restaurant business commenced operations in April 1981. Such business was managed by Mr & Mrs S, who during that period resided in the penthouse.

The income tax return for the year ending 30 June 1981 of the company as trustee of a trust estate disclosed a loss of $137,699 from the motel/restaurant business operations, "income" from sale of property $487,682 and losses carried forward under s80 of the Act totalling $126,844. The net income of the trust estate in accordance with the return was $223,139. However later in the return the amount previously shown as "income" from sale of property, namely $487,682 was claimed to comprise "capital profits" in consequence of which the net income of the trust estate could be said to be nil.

This capital profit was identified in an annexure to the tax return as having arisen from the sale of the strata titled units as follows:

"J NOMINEES PTY LTD

AS TRUSTEE FOR THE S FAMILY TRUST

Capital Profits Reserve

Balance 1/7/80 Nil

Add: Capital Profit on sale of Strata Titled Units 487,682

Balance 30/6/81 $487,682

Capital Profit

J Nominees Pty Ltd purchased the property situated at ... on behalf of the S....Family Trust for the development of a Shopping Arcade, Motel units and Revolving Restaurant.

Due to financiers, Government Departments and money lenders in general, being skeptical as to the soundness of the existing structure it became impossible for building finance to be obtained.

Rather than trying to sell the land and structure as is without doing any building work on it the Trustees looked for alternative means of financing the project.

This was achieved by dividing floors 2 to 8 into strata titled units. By constructing the building in this manner finance to a limited extent and for a specified period was obtained and the construction of the building was finalised. The strata titled units were sold and used to repay the finance obtained. A capital profit resulted on the sales and amounted to $487,682."

On 24 June 1982 the Commissioner wrote a letter to the company seeking information concerning the profit arising from the sale of the apartments. It is not necessary to set out the terms of this letter.

The Commissioner had not received a reply to that letter by 15 December 1982 on which date he issued a notice of assessment to the company wherein the taxable income of that taxpayer as trustee was stated as $223,139 (in fact as returned by the taxpayer in the initial part of its return prior to claiming that the amount of $487,682 was a capital profit). Income tax was assessed on the net income of the trust estate as $139,018.65. An adjustment sheet attaching to and forming part of the Notice of Assessment indicated the adjustments which had been made as follows:

‘’ $

Net loss as returned 137,699

Adjusted to include profit on the sale of Strata Titled Units in the absence of a satisfactory- reply to the query dated 24 June 1982 - 487,682

These adjustments and the end result thereof may be more easily understood if set out in the following form.

In assessing the Trustee, the Commissioner took the view that no beneficiary was presently entitled to the above surplus and thus raised the assessment under S99A of the Act.

On 16 December 1982 a letter was sent by the accountants of the company in reply to the Commissioner's letter of 24 June 1982 supplying the information sought. For the purpose of these proceedings it is only necessary to refer to certain information supplied in the schedules to the accountants' letter.

This schedule showed details of units sold, names of purchasers of units, dates of sales and the sale proceeds after charges. The sale proceeds, which included $71,469 being the proceeds of sale of vacant land, totalled $1,121,693. Also attached to the letter was a schedule setting out the calculation of the profit on sale of the apartments as follows:

On 17 December 1982 the company lodged a notice of objection to the assessment, contending inter alia that the income assessed was incorrect and excessive not being income under s25 of the Act and that the amount of $487,682, being the surplus on disposal of the units, was not assessable under either limb of s26(a). The taxpayer also objected to what was incorrectly seen as the deletion of the s80 losses, the assessment based on s99A of the Act and the penalty for late lodgment of the return. On 20 April 1983 a notice of disallowance of objection was sent to the company which then on 21 April 1983 requested that the matter be referred to the Board of Review.

In a letter dated 13 May 1985 addressed to the accountants for the company and Mr & Mrs S, the Commissioner indicated a new mathematical approach to the determination of the net income of the trust estate. However as the Commissioner conceded at the hearing that this new approach was inappropriate it is unnecessary to set out the text of the letter. It is pertinent to note though that the Commissioner resiled from his earlier allowance in the assessment under review of $126,844 as s80 losses, and reduced the amount of these losses to $35,000.

At about the same time the Commissioner took the view that the net income of the trust estate was divisible between and should be included in the personal returns of Mr & Mrs S. The resultant amended assessments issued on 21 June and 1 July 1985 respectively and included substantial amounts of additional tax for incorrect returns and late lodgement. Objections to these assessments were couched in terms similar to that used in the objection raised against the assessment of the trust estate.

The hearing of the reviews extended over a period of seven days and there was no dispute that if the decision of the Tribunal was that there was "net income of the trust estate" as defined by s95 of the Act, each of them Mr & Mrs S was obliged to include in his or her assessable income one half thereof as beneficiaries presently entitled thereto and not under any legal disability. As between the parties there was also agreement that the reference involving the assessment raised in terms of s99A should be put to one side in the first instance.

As was stated at the outset, the decision whether the profit produced by the sale of the apartments was net income of the trust estate is a question of fact or at most of mixed fact and law. The Commissioner contended that the company commenced carrying on business as a property developer at the time of acquisition of the property and thus the proceeds of sale of the apartments comprised income assessable under subs25(1). Alternatively the company had entered into a profit making scheme at this time, or in August/September 1979, which scheme involved the completion and sale of the greater part of the building as strata titled apartments. Finally it submitted that in accordance with the recent decision of the High Court in the Myer Emporium Case supra the proceeds of sale were income because they were produced by a commercial undertaking entered into for the purpose of profit or gain.

The crucial question of fact, stated simplistically at this stage, is whether we can accept Mr S's assertion that at all relevant times his purpose was to complete the building and to operate it as a motel. He strongly denied that at any time he intended profit making by sale of the whole or any part thereof. In this regard the Commissioner's contention was that from the outset Mr S, and the company, contemplated profit making by constructing and selling strata title apartments. The resolution of this issue was, counsel said, "a straight question of credit between Mr S and Mr M."

Mr S was adamant and asserted vigorously at all times that he wished to complete the building and operate it first as an International class motel and later as a less luxurious motel when finance became difficult. He said that it was only severe financial difficulties, occasioned by his inability to obtain funds, which compelled him to change his original plans and convert portions of the building to apartments for sale. The apartments had to be built and sold so as to obtain funds to enable him to complete the building.

Notwithstanding certain unsatisfactory aspects of the matter, we are prepared to accept the evidence of Mr S in this regard. It is however necessary to refer to the aspects of the matter which give concern.

Although he was unshaken on his assertion that he at all times wished to complete and use the building as a motel, Mr S's evidence was very short on detail. He was unable to recollect or explain to our satisfaction many happenings, saying that these were matters which he had left to his solicitors or accountants. Many of these matters the solicitor and two accountants who gave evidence were equally unable to remember or satisfactorily explain. However with the exception of Mr M's evidence there was no occasion on which there was a conflict of evidence.

As previously related Mr M stated that on the occasion of their first meeting, Mr S said, in reference to his enquiry, that he had in mind using the "property either as a motel or maybe strata titling the individual motel units into strata title units". Mr M said that he made this enquiry as some newspaper publicity suggested that the property could be used as the first casino in the State, as a motel with a revolving restaurant or as rental apartments. He explained that he was interested in what Mr S had in mind because of the possibility of further business should Mr S propose strata titles and therefore he would be happy to obtain listings on the apartments.

The evidence of Mr M however, is not without its difficulties. In examination-in-chief, Mr M indicated that he did not speak with Mr S about the Council approval for use, but later in questioning he said that he advised Mr S that he would need to obtain Council approval for the altered use to strata title apartments. However, even if Mr S did make the comments of which Mr M gave evidence, the conversation between them was casual and further, at this time Mr S understood, probably incorrectly, that he was not committed to the purchase until various enquiries, such as to permitted use and the doubts concerning the strength and adequacy of the shell were made. Therefore the evidence of Mr M when taken in conjunction with the totality of other evidence before us, which clearly indicated an intention at the time of aquisition to purchase the property to construct and run a motel, does not persuade us otherwise.

This other evidence includes the following significant matters.

Prior to seeing the building, Mr S had been looking to purchase a motel for he and his wife to run.

Mr S sought finance for the completion of building as a motel.

Mr S paid $7,860 for consultants to assess the structural and economic aspects of completion and running of a motel.

Mr S ran into economic difficulties due to failure to obtain suitable finance, eg sold his motor vehicle and tried to defer land tax and rates.

Mr S in December 1978 refused an opportunity to sell the property for $600,000 in spite of urgings by his accountant and solicitor to accept this offer which would have resulted in a profit.

Mr S when converting and subsequently selling apartments, contemplated the leaseback or repurchase of apartments for conversion to motel units.

Mr S sold the apartments on an indiscriminate basis for whatever he was offered as he was not in a financial position to bargain or make the terms.

The evidence given by his accountant Mr G, his solicitor Mr H and his architect Mr B all support the evidence of the applicant that he purchased the property for the purpose of completing and running a motel and that he only subsequently decided to convert part of the building to apartments for sale in order to obtain finance.

An unsatisfactory aspect of the evidence concerns happenings in relation to the purchase and the sale of the residential property. The Commissioner emphasized these matters as indicating that Mr S was prepared to manipulate fiscal matters to his advantage to avoid the impact of taxation. He said that such a "stigma" attached to his conduct of these transactions that we should not accept him as a witness of credit.

The relevant circumstances are as follows. At some time prior to 10 November 1976 Mr and Mrs S initialled a hand written contract note wherein they agreed to purchase a residential property together with certain specified domestic articles, but significantly excluding any boat, for $170,000. On 10 November 1976 a meeting of Mr & Mrs S as directors of the company, which had shortly prior thereto been incorporated, resolved "that the company as trustee of the S Family Trust purchase from..." (the residential property) "together with all improvements thereon in consideration of the sum of $125,000". The funds for the purchase were to be borrowed from Mr & Mrs S, the seal of the company was to be affixed both to an acknowledgement of debt and to an agreement for purchase of the property and the solicitor who was present at the meeting was directed to take all necessary steps to give effect to the agreement. The "purchaser's statement" prepared for the purpose of settlement, indicated that settlement subsequently took place on 15 November 1976. This document stated the purchase price of the house as $125,000 and that of a boat as $45,000. The total amount payable by the purchaser was $176,170.94 which included the boat. Mr S told us that there was a boat in the house. He also said that his solicitor or his accountant could explain the transaction but neither of them could remember or give any acceptable explanation thereof. It was contended by the Commissioner that the inference which should be drawn was that the purchase price of the property had been manipulated for the purpose of avoiding stamp duty on the conveyance of the property.

This property was sold the following year and the income and expenditure statement of the S Family Trust as at 30 June 1978 stated that it had been purchased by the Trust for $176,271 and had been sold on 9 September 1977. The sale price of the house property was stated to be $148,620 and $3,000 for the boat. A note was to the following effect:

"Note: The house property was sold on 9 September 1977; the settlement statement showing a net price of $183,620. However, of this amount, $35,000 related to furniture."

The vendor's settlement statement indicated that the sale price of the property was $190,000 and made no reference to furniture or a boat. Because it was sold within 12 months of purchase, the Commissioner understandably asked us to infer that the figures had been manipulated by Mr S for the purpose of avoiding the impact of s26AAA of the Act. Once again Mr S's professional advisors had no recollection of the transaction or the circumstances thereof and could give no reasonably acceptable explanation. Likewise Mr S who acknowledged that he at some time had become aware of the provisions of s26AAA, could not explain the discrepancies and again referred us to his advisors.

We have carefully and critically taken these happenings into account for the purpose of determining whether we should accept the evidence of Mr S in relation to the construction and sale of apartments. Notwithstanding the unfavourable impression that they tend to create, both of Mr S and his advisors, we have to acknowledge that this impression arises in respect of peripheral happenings. It does not necessarily require us to disbelieve Mr. S. in all the circumstances of the matter on the main issue. We are ultimately prepared to accept his evidence that at the time of the acquisition of the property he proposed to construct and conduct a motel, and that the later conversion of part of the property to apartments and their subsequent sale was an endeavour to obtain funds to enable him to proceed with the motel, albeit on a diminished scale.

Counsel for the taxpayers contended that at no stage had the company acquired the property for property development and sale nor had it purchased the property as part of a profit-making undertaking or scheme. Alternatively if it was found that in deciding to convert part of the building to apartments for sale in September 1979 certain floors were then committed to an undertaking or scheme, such was the proper value of these floors at that time that the scheme or undertaking did not produce a profit.

Counsel for the Commissioner identified the relevant authorities as Californian Copper Syndicate Ltd v Harris (1904) 5 TC 159, Whitford's Beach supra and Myer Emporium supra and on this aspect there was little dispute. Counsel then contended, in respect of his submission that the company was from the outset carrying on business as a property developer, that the law to be applied was as stated by the former Chief Justice in Whitford's Beach. At page 367 his Honour said:

"The question whether the profits were income within ordinary concepts depends on the application of the tests laid down in Californian Copper Syndicate v Harris (1904) 5 Tax Cas 159) and in the cases that have followed that decision. Was what was done merely a realization of the taxpayer's asset, or was it something done in what was truly the carrying on or carrying out of a business? In other words the question is 'whether the facts reveal a mere realization of capital, albeit in an enterprising way, or whether they justify a finding that the' (taxpayer) 'went beyond this and engaged in a' (business of profit-making) 'in land albeit on one occasion only see McClelland v Federal Commissioner of Taxation ((1970) 120 CLR at p496) where however the words used are 'a trade of dealing in land'; the words which I have ventured to substitute seem more consonant with the Australian authorities. The words 'merely' and 'mere' in these statements seem to me to be an important part of the definition of the line between profits that are taxable and those that are not."

Counsel also referred to a like statement in the reasons of Mason J (as he then was) at page 383. He said:

"The principal, if not the essential, question under the second limb of s26(a), as under s25(1), is whether more is involved than the mere realization of an asset. As Deane J noted in his dissenting judgment in the Federal Court, we must not overlook the importance and the scope of the word 'mere'. To bring this case within the second limb the Commissioner does not need to show that the respondent was carrying on a business. As we have seen, it is enough to answer the statutory description that there was a profit-making undertaking or scheme which exhibited the characteristics of a business deal, even though it did not amount to the carrying on of a business."

These statements of the law were also accepted by counsel for the taxpayers as applicable to determine the assessability or otherwise of the profits. However the fact that the application thereof produced on their respective submissions a different result indicates that the question for determination is essentially a question of fact. Each however accepted that the Californian Copper case supra was the appropriate starting point.

Counsel for the Commissioner also referred to the recent decision of Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Myer Emporium Ltd supra and the emphasis which was also there placed on the word "mere" in the Californian Copper case. On page 35 the following passage appears in the joint judgment of the High Court.

"The proposition that a mere realisation or change of investment is not income requires some elaboration. First, the emphasis is on the adjective 'mere' (Whitford's Beach at p 383). Secondly, profits made on a realisation or change of investments may constitute income if the investments were initially acquired as part of a business with the intention or purpose that they be realised subsequently in order to capture the profit arising from their expected increase in value."

Whitford's Beach case supra was also relied upon by counsel for the Commissioner as supporting his alternative submission, namely that the second limb of s26(a) required the profits to be included in the assessable income of the company. His contention was that the building as a whole, or alternatively the apartments, were committed to a profit making undertaking or scheme on August 1979 or alternatively from the outset. On page 383 of Whitford's Beach Mason J said:

"All that I have said indicates that the second limb does not affect the principle enunciated in Californian Copper except to emphasize that an undertaking or scheme may be a profit-making one even if it lacks the characteristics of repetition or recurrence supposedly essential to the carrying on of a business. It is possible that the second limb applies when the taxpayer's activities amount to more than the mere realization of an asset but do not constitute the carrying on of a business because they lack the characteristics of repetition or recurrence. The distinction made in Californian Copper (1904) 5 Tax Cas 159 between a mere enhancement in value by realization of a security and a gain made in an operation of business in carrying out a scheme of profit-making remains a valid distinction for the second limb of s26(a)."

Counsel contended that whatever approach we took to the evidence of Mr S, it had to be accepted that in constructing and selling the apartments he set out to make a profit.

We were also referred to certain dicta in the Myer Emporium case, which counsel for the Commissioner contended lent support, on another basis, to his submission that the profits were assessable as gross income under s25(1). He particularly relied upon the following passage on page 32 -

"Generally speaking, however, it may be said that if the circumstances are such as to give rise to the inference that the taxpayer's intention or purpose in entering into the transaction was to make a profit or gain, the profit or gain will be income, notwithstanding that the transaction was extraordinary judged by reference to the ordinary course of the taxpayer's business. Nor does the fact that a profit or gain is made as the result of an isolated venture or a 'one-off transaction preclude it from being properly characterised as income."

In our opinion however, for reasons subsequently stated, it is necessary to add a reference to the next succeeding sentence, namely

"The authorities establish that a profit or gain so made will constitute income if the property generating the profit or gain was acquired in a business operation or commercial transaction for the purpose of profit-making by the means giving rise to the profit. [We have added the emphasis,)

Counsel for the Commissioner asked us to apply these statements of principle in the light of various alternative findings of fact. In the first instance he asked us to find that Mr S had from the outset the purpose or intention of building and developing apartments as an alternative to the building and conduct of a motel. By the use of the word "developing" we assume that it entailed development for the purpose of sale. The second alternative was that we should find that in August 1979 Mr S had the purpose of building and developing the whole building except the top two floors as apartments. He asked us not to accept the evidence of Mr S that his purpose was "simply to construct them to get enough money to keep on with" the construction of the motel. In this regard he contended that the only reason the concept of building a motel was kept on foot was to assist the company to obtain an Establishment Payment Scheme Grant from the Department of Trade and Industry. Finally he asked us to find that inherent in all the company's transactions was the purpose of profit making. If this was the case the profit was, he submitted, in accordance with the Myer Emporium case, assessable income.

It is our view that we should not make any of these crucial findings of fact. It is our view that at the time of acquisition of the land and building the purpose of the company and Mr S was to erect and conduct an international motel. The subsequent conversion of portion of the building to apartments and their sale was not a profit making but a financing exercise to enable the building including a modified motel to be completed. Mr S had only two alternatives at that time, to abandon the project and sell the property or to obtain finance to complete the structure by selling off apartments. We accept the evidence of Mr S in this regard, supported as it was by the unchallenged evidence, on this topic, of his solicitor, accountant and architect.

In these circumstances our finding of fact is that the company was not engaged in any form of profit making by the construction or sale of the apartments from the outset or from August 1979.

It only remains to deal with the submissions based on Whitford's Beach and Myer Emporium, namely that the proceeds are assessable under s25(1) because they arise out of transactions which were more than "mere" realizations and which arose in the course of a commercial undertaking. The Commissioner relied upon the broadly stated principles set out above in the Californian Copper case.

It would seem that the question whether the transactions in relation to the construction of the apartments went beyond a mere realization is a question of fact. It is pertinent to contrast the position in Fox's case [1956] HCA 63; (1956) 96 CLR 370 and Whitford's Beach with the facts in this case. In Fox's case and Whitford's Beach the land could have been sold as broadacres instead of the large and complex exercise involved in that subdivision. By contrast in this case, no sale of a portion of the building could have been achieved until consents and strata titles were obtained and the apartments constructed. In the circumstances, no more was done than had to be done to enable portions of the building to be sold and we would conclude that what was done did not go beyond "mere realization".

But if the transactions did go beyond "mere realization" and arose in the course of construction of a motel by a project builder we do not accept that the Californian Copper, Whitford's Beach and Myer Emporium require us to find that the proceeds constitute assessable income. A careful consideration of dicta in these authorities satisfies us that this is only the case where the profits arise from operations which operations can accurately be labelled as profit making operations. They require that the taxpayer has a purpose of profit making by the particular operation.

In Californian Copper supra we find the statement of general principle explained at page 166 as follows:

"What is the line which separates the two classes of cases may be difficult to define, and each case must be considered according to its facts; the question to be determined being - Is the sum of gain that has been made a mere enhancement of value by realising a security, or is it a gain made in an operation of business in carrying out a scheme for profit making?"

In the present case the operation which allegedly produced the gain was not, on our findings of fact, a scheme of profit making, albeit, ex'hypothesi, an operation of business.

We have already referred to the Californian Copper test which test the former Chief Justice of the High Court stated in his own words in Whitford's Beach at page 367

"'or whether they' (the facts) 'justify a finding that the [taxpayer] went beyond this and engaged in a [business of profit making] in land...'"

At page 381 Mason J. said:

"...it is the existence of the purpose of profit making by sale, the dominant purpose, which is critical to the character of the profit as income under the general law and under s26(a)."

The Myer Emporium case turned on totally different facts but there are significant statements which indicate the significance which the High Court attached, when referring to general principle, to the purpose of profit making. At page 32 -

"The authorities establish that a profit or gain so made (from an isolated venture) will constitute income if the property generating the profit or gain was acquired in a business operation or commercial transaction for the purpose of profit making by the means giving rise to the profit."

On page 33 the Court said:

"The important proposition to be derived from Californian Copper and Pucker is that a receipt may constitute income, if it arises from an isolated business operation or commercial transaction entered into otherwise than in the ordinary course of the carrying on of the taxpayer's business, so long as the taxpayer entered into the transaction with the intention or purpose of making a relevant profit or gain from the transaction." (emphasis added).

In the present case our findings of fact preclude the conclusion that the proceeds of sale of units are income as it cannot be said that the company entered into the transactions necessary to sell apartments "with the intention or purpose of making a relevant profit or gain from the transaction."

In considering our decision in this matter we have noted the decision of the Full Court of the Federal Court in Bowden v Commissioner of Taxation 87 ATC 4630, in the facts of which we see similarity to those before us, in that there was a subsequent change from the original purpose. However the essential business of the taxpayers to which the relevant land was committed on the change of purpose was that of developing and selling ie dealing in land. It followed that the proceeds of sale were income in accordance with ordinary concepts.

In our opinion the Commissioner erred in determining that the proceeds of sale were assessable under either subs 25(1) or the second limb of s26(a). If we are wrong in this and the proceeds are assessable under either section in that a portion of the building was committed to a business or profit making in August 1979, difficult questions of valuation arise. Should we have been required to determine these questions we should have required further evidence of value than the somewhat limited evidence before us.

We are now obliged to address ourselves to the problem of the quantum of the s.80 loss to be carried forward from 1979 and 1980. We accept that a division of the indirect items of expenditure incurred in those years, is required. That portion relating to the motel/restaurant business will represent the s.80 loss, whereas the balance will refer to the apartments sold, forming part of their overall capital cost. These indirect outgoings, details of which are contained in the 1979 and 1980 trust returns, total $126,844.

Whilst it seems reasonable to accept the Commissioner's proposal that the allocation be expressed as a percentage of the direct costs involved in the completion of the building, we have some doubts as to the correctness of the method applied. As between the parties, it was agreed that the following percentages represented a breakup of the direct outgoings incurred in the completion of the project:

Floors 2-8 44

Motel/Restaurant 34

Penthouse 9

Ground and 1st Floor 13

100%

These proportions appear to be reasonable.

Our problem relates to the Commissioner's contention that the figure of 34% should be used in determining the s80 loss. In the first instance, we see no reason for a distinction between motel/restaurant and the penthouse. Recognising that the penthouse was occupied by Mr & Mrs S who were employed by the company to manage the motel/restaurant business, we take the view that, for apportionment purposes, these areas should be combined. Additionally, we have difficulty in accepting the private use concept where the property was owned by the company and not the individuals. Secondly, it seems that the ground and 1st floors were regarded as common areas, ie they were used by inhabitants of all apartments as well as staff and patrons of the motel/restaurant,and thus in all the circumstances we are of the opinion that the figure of 13% should be shared equally between the two remaining components.

In our view the final division should be:

Apartments sold or to be sold 50

Motel/Restaurant and Penthouse 50

100%

  1. the interest charge of $92,801 which was incurred during the 1981 year and which related to monies borrowed to complete the entire building. All of this interest was claimed as a deduction in calculating the net result for the motel and restaurant.
  2. the expenses incurred during the 1979 and 1980 years and which total $126,844. The final calculation will be:

Finally we turn to address the question of whether the amounts of additional tax imposed by the Commissioner in the assessment of Mr & Mrs S for incorrect returns, and those imposed for late lodgement of the returns of Mr & Mrs S and the company. Having regard to our decision on the substantive issue, the returns as lodged by Mr & Mrs S are not incorrect and thus the penalties imposed for those alleged breaches are to be remitted in full.

There was no dispute that the returns of Mr & Mrs S and the company as trustee had not been lodged until 26 May 1982 and that such returns should have been lodged with the Commissioner by 31 December 1981. In reliance on the provisions then contained in ss226(1) of the Act the Commissioner imposed on each of the personal taxpayers additional tax to the extent of $4,944 and $5,135 in the case of the company. At that time the subsection read as follows:

"226 (1). Notwithstanding anything contained in sections 223, 224 and 225, any taxpayer who fails to duly furnish as and when required by this Act or the regulations, or by the Commissioner, any return or any information in relation to any matter affecting either his liability to tax or the amount of the tax, shall be liable to pay as additional tax an amount equal to the tax assessable to him or the amount of $2 whichever is the greater."

Our finding on the surplus resulting from the sale of the apartments leaves Mr & Mrs S and the company in a position where they have no "tax assessable to" them. In that circumstance the penalties should in each case be reduced to $2.

For the reasons stated above, our conclusion is that the determinations of the Commissioner upon the objections be set aside and that the following decisions of this Tribunal be substituted in lieu thereof:

(1) The proceeds resulting from the sale of the apartments do not represent assessable income under subs 25(1) nor does the profit from their disposal come within the ambit of the second limb of s 26(a) as it then existed.

(2) Of the amount of interest paid by the company during the year of income ended 30 June 1981 no greater amount than $46,401 is allowable as a deduction under subs 51(1).

(3) No greater deduction than $63,422 is allowable to the company in terms of subs. 80(2) for losses claimed to have been incurred in the years of income ended 30 June 1979 and 1980.

(4) No income was omitted from the returns of Mr & Mrs S and thus they are not liable to pay additional tax in terms of subs 226(2).

(5) The taxpayers not having furnished returns of income for the year ended 30 June 1981 as and when required by the Act each of them is liable under subs 226(1) for additional tax to the extent of $2.
