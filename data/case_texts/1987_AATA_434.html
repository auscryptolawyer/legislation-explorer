Re Taxation Appeals [1987] AATA 434 (7 December 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

Nos. WT85/548, 559-561

AAT No. 3977

Taxation Appeals

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

R.D. Nicholson (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation Appeals - income and deductions - whether Applicants engaged in business - "one-off" purchase and sale of spaghetti manufacturing machinery - whether Applicants' evidence discharges onus

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 25, 26, 51, 52, 92, 170, 190

Blockey v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1923] HCA 2; (1923) 31 CLR 503

Edwards (H.M. Inspector of Taxes) v. Bairstow & Harrison [1955] UKHL 3; (1956) AC 14

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. The Myer Emporium Ltd. 87 ATC 4363

Jones v. Leeming (1930) AC 415 and (1930) 1 KB 279

HEARING

PERTH

7:12:1987

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the objection decisions under review and, reserving liberty to each party to apply for further directions generally, remits each decision to the Respondent with the following directions:

1. The Applicants were engaged in a business of

selling the spaghetti manufacturing machinery.

2. With respect to the 1981 year of income: the

Applicants' liability to tax is to be determined on

the basis that

(a) the sum of $750 received by the Applicants on

sale of the water purifying plant was not profit

arising from the sale and therefore did not

constitute income;

(b) they have failed to discharge the onus of

establishing the following payments by the

partnership formed by each of them:

(i) $1,100 for casual labour in dismantling

the machinery;

(ii) $1,600 for travel, accommodation and

entertainment;

(iii) $1067 for utility expense;

(c) the sum of $27,750 is a capital outgoing and not

a deductible expense.

3. In respect of the 1982 year of income: the

liability of the Applicants to tax is to be

determined on the basis that

(a) they have not discharged the onus upon them of

establishing the following payments by the

partnership formed by each of them:

(i) $686 entertainment;

(ii) $1,544 utility expenses;

(iii) such part of $3,286 claimed for travel and

accommodation as relates to travel and

accommodation expenses after 15 July 1982;

and

(b) they have not established that the sum of

$11,850 from sales of the spaghetti

manufacturing machinery other than the plant was

derived in the 1982 year of income or that it

was profit derived in that year.

DECISION

Objection Decisions Under Review

The Applicants seek review, with respect to the 1981 year of income, of disallowance of their objections to amendment of their assessments seeking in respect of each of their returns to have an amount of $375 earned by the taxpayer on sales resulting from a spaghetti machine venture included as assessable income and an amount of $13,680 allowed as a deduction for expenses relating to the venture.

2. With respect to the 1982 year of income the Applicants seek review of the disallowance of their objections to amendment of their respective returns so as to disallow a claim for a loss of $4169 on the spaghetti machine venture.

3. The Commissioner's reasons for disallowing the taxpayers' claims in each of the years were as follows:

"1. During the years ended 30 June 1981 and 1982 the

taxpayer was not carrying on a spaghetti machine

venture either on his own account or in partnership

with his spouse and for that reason the proceeds of

$750 and $11,850 respectively from the disposal of

machinery do not constitute income within the

meaning of sub-section 25(1).

2. Under the provisions of section 28, sub-section

51(1), sections 52, 53, 54, 59 and 90 and

sub-section 92(2) of the Act no part of the

deductions of $27,360 and $20,189 claimed to have

been incurred during the years ended 30 June 1981

and 1982 respectively by the partnership between

the taxpayer and his spouse in respect of the

spaghetti machine venture is allowable to the

taxpayer either directly or as a share of his

interest in the partnership loss.

3. The amended assessment issued in respect of the

year ended 30 June 1982 was authorized by

sub-section 170(2)."

Facts

4. Spaghetti, as the Shorter Oxford English Dictionary (1973) at p 2060 states, is an Italian variety of alimentary paste made in solid cords intermediate in thickness between macaroni and vermicelli. The male Applicant (he alone is described as "the Applicant") has had a long business acquaintance with spaghetti. Until 1975 he was a partner in a family pasta business. It had a small plant so he went to Italy and visited spaghetti machine manufacturers and acquired 13 containers of machinery. Shortly thereafter the business was sold together with the newly acquired machinery.

5. Some time around 1977 the Applicant, who had no business connection with the new owner, went with him to Italy to purchase further spaghetti machinery which was placed in premises belonging to the new owner in Perth. The Applicant continued to be on friendly terms with the new owner.

6. The Applicant's business involved him in buying and selling real estate. In the course of that business he met representatives of C Pty. Ltd. who were promoters and entrepeneurs. The Applicant, having made a profit on the sale of a unit, suggested to C Pty. Ltd. that it put together a spaghetti restaurant project and he would contribute his expertise and a spaghetti manufacturing machine (he having formed the intention to buy the equipment then on the new owner's Perth premises). The Applicant anticipated being issued with shares to the value of $200,000 in a company to be formed to conduct the project in return for his input of expertise and the machine, which he thought he could acquire for around $30,000.

7. C Pty. Ltd. proceeded to take steps to implement the project, describing itself as a representative of the consortium formed to undertake the restaurant concept. In October 1980 it applied to register "spaghetti factory" as a business name; it applied for a lease of premises and instructed a firm of licensed surveyors to prepare preliminary plans. On 12 November 1980 the proposed lessor advised C Pty. Ltd. that it had decided to postpone the decision on future development of its land and premises until such time as a master plan had been completed, estimating this would take approximately 3 - 4 months.

8. On 12 November 1980 ("the date of payment") payment was made (apparently from an account styled in the name of a nominee company as trustee for the Applicant's family trust) by way of first payment on purchase of the equipment comprising the spaghetti manufacturing machinery. The machinery comprised two P300 Pavan presses (extruders); a water purifying plant ("the plant"); an oil-fired burner; a packaging machine; an overhead automatic feed gantry; a wavy spaghetti packaging machine; a Braibanti macri and micro press; stainless steel tubs and two flour silos (all included in the description "the spaghetti manufacturing machinery" or "machinery").

9. The Applicant's evidence was that a couple of weeks prior to the date of payment C Pty. Ltd. had told him it would not proceed with the proposed venture and he had thereupon made up his mind to purchase the spaghetti manufacturing machinery to develop a business supplying and installing such machinery for Australian manufacturers. His explanation of correspondence which was sent by C Pty. Ltd. on 12 November 1980 (that is, after the date of payment) in furtherance of aspects of the project was that it let the "paper work" go on. Despite this he said it had advised him of its intention not to pursue the proposed spaghetti restaurant project prior to payment for the spaghetti manufacturing machinery.

10. In December 1980 the Applicant made an offer to sell the spaghetti manufacturing machinery to R Company and to T Company. In January 1981 he travelled to Adelaide and offered it for sale to S Company.

11. On 4 February 1981 the second and final payment for purchase of the spaghetti manufacturing machinery was made and one week later or thereabouts, the Applicant took possession of it. The machinery was dismantled because it had to be moved from the former owner's premises. The result was that when representatives of T Company visited Perth to inspect it in February they saw it in a dismantled state and did not take up the Applicant's offer to sell it to them for $50,000.

12. In February 1981 the Applicant caused an advertisement to be placed in the West Australian newspaper reading:

"SPAGHETTI manufacturing plant ready to be installed,

ideal for (word unreadable) businessmen, tremendous

potential in W.A. only State in Australia that does not

have its own local spaghetti co. Ph. (the Applicant and

his telephone number) for further facts and

particulars."

13. Close to 18 February 1981 the Applicant appointed a used machinery company as his agent to sell the machinery for $50,000. The Applicant said he did this because his direct approaches to manufacturers had not been successful. He said that at that time he still believed if he could make a sale he would succeed in approaching other manufacturers to sell further machinery.

14. In his endeavours to sell the machinery, the Applicant had also contacted a dog food manufacturing company in Perth (the manufacture of dog food sometimes making use of machinery such as the Applicant owned). He received an offer of $25,000 from a company in Melbourne contingent upon establishment of a new factory by them but nothing came of that because it purchased equipment direct from Italy. The Applicant also said he spoke to businessmen around Perth in an endeavour to find a buyer for the machinery.

15. On 5 April 1981 the Applicant wrote to the Town Clerk of the City of Perth concerning the availability of certain suburban land for use for car parking in relation to adjacent land being studied as a potential site for a "spaghetti production and restauranteering concept". This was followed by a further letter on the same subject on 15 April 1981. On 20 May 1981 the Council invited him to submit a formal application regarding the spaghetti and restaurant concept for the land in question.

16. On 11 August 1981 the Applicant made formal application to the Perth City Council to set up a spaghetti production plant and restaurant on a near city site. The application was approved in October 1982 but the project did not proceed.

17. In May 1982 the Applicant visited companies in Sydney and Melbourne presenting photographs of the machinery and data to representatives as part of a continuing attempt to sell it.

18. On 12 June 1982 the Applicant left Australia to visit Italy and while there visited a major pasta company trying, on his evidence, to sell the machinery to it. In a letter dated 13 July 1982 that Company stated that it did not wish to purchase the equipment, stating in Italian (in an interpretation accepted on behalf of the Respondent) that "it is not possible to confirm what was said yesterday in person through our office" (otherwise interpreted in the documents before the Tribunal as "we cannot confirm what we told you yesterday personally at our office"). The Applicant's evidence was that when the Italian pasta company declined to purchase the equipment he thereupon decided to accept the offer from a company in Western Australia which manufactured pellets for live sheep transportation to purchase the machinery for $10,000 and telephoned his wife and told her to accept that offer. In his evidence he said that had occurred at the end of June. In cross-examination he said he sold it to the Western Australian company after 12 July 1982 (the day prior to the letter containing the above translated sentence). On re-examination he said that the sale had occurred before 30 June 1982. Payment for the equipment took place on 15 July 1982. The Applicant returned to Australia on 3 August.

19. In undertaking the above action the Applicant says he acted on behalf of a partnership comprising himself and his wife. There was no written partnership agreement between them and the partnership name was not registered as a business name. The accounts maintained for the partnership show receipt of income jointly so that, if such receipt be proven, the existence of the partnership will be established.

20. Other relevant facts will be stated in the course of addressing the objections raised by the Applicants.

Objections in 1981 year of income

- sales income

21. The first objection is that the income earned on sales made in the machinery venture is income assessable under section 25 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). The amount in question is $750 ($375 in respect of each of the Applicants) and it arose from sale of a water purifying plant ("the plant") for which that amount was deposited in a Town and Country Building Society account in the name of the Applicants on 11 May 1981. The Applicant's evidence was that carbonated water in Western Australia is highly chlorinated and is not very good for making pasta so that the plant was not required to sell the remainder of the spaghetti manufacturing machinery.

22. Whether any income earned on this sale is income assessable under section 25 will be determined by the view which the Tribunal takes on the evidence as to whether the Applicants were engaged in the business of marketing spaghetti manufacturing machinery, of which the plant formed part.

23. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. The Myer Emporium Limited 87 ATC 4363, the High Court of Australia considered whether a certain amount was income under sub-section 25(1) of the Act or alternatively, whether it was assessable under section 26(a) in the form which it was in the year of income relevant here. The Court said that a gain made otherwise than in the ordinary course of carrying on a business which nevertheless arises from a transaction entered into by the taxpayer with the intention or purpose of making a profit or gain, may well constitute income and it will do so where, generally speaking, the circumstances give rise to the inference that the taxpayer's intention or purpose in entering into the transaction was to make a profit or gain. The Court continued:

"Nor does the fact that a profit or gain is made as the

result of an isolated venture or a "one-off"

transaction preclude it from being properly

characterized as income (F.C. of T. v. Whitfords Beach

Pty. Ltd. 82 ATC 4031 at pp 4036-4037, 4042; (1982)

[1982] HCA 8; 150 CLR 355 at pp 366-367, 376). The authorities

establish that a profit or gain so made will constitute

income if the property generating the profit or gain

was acquired in a business operation or commercial

transaction for the purpose of profit-making by the

means giving rise to the profit."

24. After referring to Jones v. Leeming (1930) AC 415 from which it appeared that a profit on an isolated sale not being an adventure in the nature of trade was a capital accretion - said to be the reason for the enactment of section 26(a) in its original form - the Court referred to Edwards (H.M. Inspector of Taxes) v. Bairstow and Harrison [1955] UKHL 3; (1956) AC 14 where joint venturers who engaged in an isolated transaction of buying and selling a complete spinning plant, with a view to making a profit, having no intention of using the plant or deriving income from it, were held liable to income tax on the profit made on resale. At p 4368 it cited the observations of Lord Radcliffe (p 38) in rejecting the argument that the profit was not income because it arose from an isolated transaction:

"... that circumstance does not prevent a transaction

which bears the badges of trade from being in truth an

adventure in the nature of trade. The true question in

such cases is whether the operations constitute an

adventure of that kind, not whether they by themselves

or they in conjunction with other operations,

constitute the operator a person who carries on a

trade. Dealing is, I think, essentially a trading

adventure, and the respondents' operations were nothing

but a deal or deals in plant and machinery."

25. After stating that the judgments in some of the English decisions naturally reflect the language of the United Kingdom statutory provisions (which involve an enquiry as to whether or not there was "an adventure ... in the nature of trade"), the High Court said that it had, together with the Privy Council, accepted that profits derived in a business operation or commercial transaction carrying out any profit-making scheme are income, whereas the proceeds of a mere realisation or change of investment or from an enhancement of capital are not income.

26. In elaboration of the reference to realisation the High Court said:

"The proposition that a mere realization or change of

investment is not income requires some elaboration.

First, the emphasis is on the adjective "mere"

(Whitfords Beach, at ATC pp 4046-4047; CLR p 383).

Secondly, profits made on a realization or change of

investments may constitute income if the investments

were initially acquired as part of a business with the

intention or purpose that they be realized subsequently

in order to capture the profit arising from their

expected increase in value - see the discussion by

Gibbs J. in London Australia (Investment Co. Ltd. v.

F.C. of T. 77 ATC 4398 [1977] HCA 50; (1977) 138 CLR 106), at ATC pp

4403-4404; CLR pp 116-118. It is one thing if the

decision to sell an asset is taken after its

acquisition, there having been no intention or purpose

at the time of acquisition of acquiring for the purpose

of profit-making by sale. Then, if the asset be not a

revenue asset on other grounds, the profit made is

capital because it proceeds from a mere realization.

But it is quite another thing if the decision to sell

is taken by way of implementation of an intention or

purpose, existing at the time of acquisition, of

profit-making by sale, at least in the context of

carrying on a business or carrying out a business

operation or commercial transaction."

27. Here the Tribunal is called upon to decide whether the Applicant had the intention or purpose at the time of acquisition of the spaghetti manufacturing machinery and water purifying plant of profit-making by sale (as contended by the Applicant) or whether the Applicant had no such intention at the time of acquisition so that the decision to sell was not taken by way of implementation of that intention (as contended by the Respondent).

28. The Respondent argues that the chronology of events involving as it did the application for registration of the business name "Spaghetti Factory"; an application for a lease on certain premises; the preparation of plans for those premises; the purchase of equipment; the ascertainment that a lease of the premises could not be secured; and then the decision to sell the equipment establishes that the equipment could not have been purchased with the necessary intention at the time of acquisition of profit-making by sale.

29. The Applicant's argument was that the facts in this proceeding lend themselves to the same interpretation as that given by Lord Radcliffe to the facts in Bairstow's case (supra) where he said at pp 36-37:

"Here are two gentlemen who put their money, or the

money of one of them, into buying a lot of machinery.

They have no intention of using it as machinery, so

they do not buy it to hold as an income-producing

asset. They do not buy it to consume or for the

pleasure of enjoyment. On the contrary, they have no

intention of holding their purchase at all. They are

planning to sell the machinery even before they have

bought it. And in due course they do sell it, in five

separate lots, as events turned out. And, as they

hoped and expected they make a net profit on the deal,

after charging all expenses such as repairs and

replacements, commissions, wages, travelling and

entertainment and incidentals, which do in fact

represent the cost of organizing the venture and

carrying it through.

This seems to be, inescapably, a commercial deal in

second-hand plant. What detail does it lack that

prevents it from being an adventure in the nature of

trade, or what element is present in it that makes it

capable of being aptly described as anthing else?"

30. The question before the Tribunal is, therefore, whether the Applicants have discharged the onus upon them pursuant to section 190(b) of the Act of establishing that at the time of their acquisition of the spaghetti manufacturing machinery and water purifying plant they had the necessary intention of profit-making by sale.

31. The difficulty before the Tribunal in deciding the central question of intention is that it has before it only the evidence of the Applicant, not only as to his subjective intention, but as to the order in which the events referred to on behalf of the Respondent occurred. In its opinion, despite pressing questioning on behalf of the Respondent, the Applicant's evidence remained consistent on the order in which the events occurred, namely that the decision to purchase was made after the Applicant had been told the consortium project would not proceed. On his evidence, therefore, the purpose of the acquisition would have been formed when the possibility of use of the machinery as capital in the consortium project was precluded. In any event, and although the matter was not argued on behalf of the Applicant, even if the project had not then been precluded (as the Tribunal does not find) the evidence establishes that the Applicant could have purchased the machinery for the purpose of selling it to the company formed to conduct the project in consideration for the issue of shares.

32. The Applicant's evidence should be tested against all the evidence in the proceeding. There is nothing therein which makes inappropriate the acceptance by the Tribunal of the Applicant's evidence. Such evidence, however, is both consistent with an intention to sell formed at the time of acquisition as with an intention to sell formed some time thereafter, however short. Having considered the evidence in relation to these matters, the Tribunal considers the Applicant's evidence discharges the onus upon him on behalf of the Applicants in establishing the sequence of events at the time of acquisition and the intention with which the acquisition was made.

33. Accordingly, the Tribunal considers the correct conclusion is that in purchasing the machinery the Applicant, acting for the partnership of the Applicants, had the necessary intention so that they were engaged in a business in relation to the spaghetti manufacturing machinery. The consequence is that any profit from realisation is properly treated as income.

34. The Applicant was put to proof that he received $750 from sale of the plant. He could not recall the name of the purchaser and could only describe it as a chemical company in a Perth suburb. His accountant produced evidence of the deposit. The Applicant's oral evidence establishes the sale occurred. In the view of the Tribunal the Applicants have discharged the onus upon them to establish the receipt of the item from sale of the plant.

35. There is, however, a further difficulty. The accounts prepared for the Applicant bring to account the total receipts for the plant ($750) as income. There is no evidence that $750 was the profit on the sale. Nor could it be given that the plant formed part of machinery acquired as a "lot sale" all of which was ultimately sold at loss in the 1981 year of income. The proceeds of sale of the plant do not therefore constitute income to be brought to account.

- deductions

36. The second objection in the 1981 income year is that expenses of $27,360 as claimed ($13,680 in the return of each of the Applicants) should be deductible under sub-section 51(1) of the Act.

37. Included in the amounts sought to be deducted is the sum of $27,750 being the purchase price for the spaghetti manufacturing machinery. In the Tribunal's view that is a capital item precluded from deduction by the express words of sub-section 51(1).

38. In relation to the remainder of the deductions claimed, the Tribunal considers that those outgoings are not of a capital nature nor otherwise excluded by sub-section 51(1).

39. However, the Applicants were put to proof with respect to certain parts of those amounts as follows:

(a) $1100 (part of $3424 claimed) casual labour

The Applicants seek a deduction for payments by cash to

casual labourers said by the Applicant to have been engaged

to dismantle the machinery. They are unable to produce any

records of substantiation of the payment which therefore

cannot be allowed.

(b) $1600 travel, accommodation and entertainment

The Applicant stated these expenses relate to his travel to

Adelaide, accommodation and taxi fares. He produced no

proof to discharge the onus upon him to establish the

entitlement to a deduction for the Applicants as partners.

(c) $1067 utility expenses

40. The Applicant adduced no proof in support of these.

41. The Applicants contend the remainder of the expenses claimed should remain allowable deductions. This was conceded in respect of certain items at the hearing.

Objections in respect of the 1982 year of income

42. The Applicants first object that the Commissioner is not entitled to issue the amended assessment because there was not, as required by sub-section 170(2) and (3) of the Act, a failure to make full and true disclosure of all material facts. In the view of the Tribunal the taxpayer has not established that he did make a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment. It was not otherwise proved to the Tribunal that in any other respect the amended assessment was not authorised by section 170.

43. It follows from the acceptance by the Tribunal of the trading nature of the spaghetti-manufacturing machinery venture that (subject to what is said in relation to proof of particular items on which the Applicant has been put to proof by the Respondent) the Applicant is in principle entitled to an amount of $4169 by way of deduction as his share of the loss of the partnership venture pursuant to section 92(2) of the Act or section 52 of the Act.

44. While the Tribunal accepts that the particular expenses claimed are not excluded by the exception to sub-section 51(1), it has not been satisfied in discharge of the requisite onus by the Applicant that certain of the outgoings there referred to were incurred namely, $686 entertainment; $1544 utility expenses; $231 telephone; part of $3286 for travel and accommodation. In respect of this latter item the evidence establishes that after the date of sale the expenses of travel and accommodation in New York and eastern Australia are not allowable deductions.

45. It follows from earlier reasoning that if sale of the machinery took place in the 1982 year of income, any profit arising from the sale should be returned as income in the returns of each of the Applicants. The evidence adduced, however, leaves the Tribunal unsatisfied that the sale did in fact take place in the 1982 year of income. The Applicant was clear in his evidence that he telephoned his wife to instruct her to effect the sale after the conference with the Company in Italy. While the translated version of the letter from that Company does not establish definitely that he was at that company the previous day, it heightens the probability that he visited the company after 30 June. That probability is strengthened by the date of payment. The company which purchased the machinery (other than the plant) no longer exists so that no corroborative evidence could be obtained from its records other than a letter from it dated 20 June 1982 to the male Applicant asking for a response to the offer to purchase by 27 June 1982. Furthermore, it is apparent the machinery was sold at a loss so that no profit arose.

Conclusion

46. The Tribunal will set aside the objection decisions in this matter and, reserving liberty to either party to apply for further directions generally, remit each decision to the Respondent with the following directions:

1. The Applicants were engaged in a business of selling the

spaghetti manufacturing machinery.

2. With respect to the 1981 year of income: the Applicants'

liability to tax is to be determined on the basis that

(a) the sum of $750 received by the Applicants on sale of

the water purifying plant was not profit arising from

the sale and therefore did not constitute income;

(b) they have failed to discharge the onus of establishing

the following payments by the partnership formed by each

of them:

(i) $1,100 for casual labour in dismantling the

machinery;

(ii) $1,600 for travel, accommodation and

entertainment;

(iii) $1067 for utility expense;

(c) the sum of $27,750 is a capital outgoing and not a

deductible expense.

3. In respect of the 1982 year of income: the liability of the

Applicants to tax is to be determined on the basis that

(a) they have not discharged the onus upon them of

establishing the following payments by the partnership

formed by each of them:

(i) $686 entertainment;

(ii) $1,544 utility expenses;

(iii) such part of $3,286 claimed for travel and

accommodation as relates to travel and

accommodation expenses after 15 July 1982; and

(b) they have not established that the sum of $11,850 from

sales of the spaghetti manufacturing machinery other

than the plant was derived in the 1982 year of income or

that it was profit derived in that year.
