Re Peter William Taylor and Commissioner of Superannuation [1981] AATA 169 (14 December 1981)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: PETER WILLIAM TAYLOR

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. D80/2

Superannuation Act 1976

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

The Hon. J.B.K. Williams (Senior Member)

Dr M. Glick

Mr I. Prowse

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation Act 1976 - issue of benefit classification certificate under Section 16(4) - objection by application - question whether review by respondent made under Section 16(6) or Section 154(2) - certificate amended on review to specify a condition of "history of back injury and back pain" - lack of medical evidence defining nature of back problem - matter remitted to respondent for reconsideration with direction that specialist orthopaedic examination be made of applicant.

HEARING

CANBERRA

14:12:1981

ORDER

The Tribunal decides to set aside the decision under review and to remit the matter for reconsideration by the respondent in accordance with the directions contained in the attached reasons.

DECISION

The applicant in this matter, Mr Peter William Taylor, became an "eligible employee" for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 (the "Act") on 19 June, 1979 by virtue of his permanent appointment to the Commonwealth Public Service. He underwent a medical examination by a Commonwealth Medical Officer who reported that the applicant suffered from migraine, injury to his coccyx and a stiff neck. As a result, he did not consider that the applicant met the general medical standard but was fit for permanent appointment. He also commented that the applicant was not suitable for jobs involving heavy lifting.

2. Following consideration of the medical report, the respondent formed the opinion that the applicant, by reason of the physical disabilities previously mentioned, was not likely to continue to be an eligible employee until he attained his maximum retiring age, and accordingly issued a benefit classification certificate pursuant to the provisions of Section 16(4) of the Act specifying the physical conditions earlier referred to.

3. The applicant was dissatisfied with the issue of the certificate. He sought a reconsideration of it in a letter to the respondent which is undated and which appears at pages T5 22/23 of the papers before us. In it he claimed that the tests in relation to migraine had never been conducted nor had it ever been diagnosed that he had suffered from migraine. As to the reference to injury to his coccyx, he stated that this was an assumption as no tests or x-rays had been made or taken. He also said that not long before his medical examination he was treated for a neck complaint "that is most commonly referred to as a chill or cold in the neck". The respondent treated the applicant's letter as a request under Section 154(2) of the Act and following reconsideration of the matter decided to delete from the benefit classification certificate the words "migraine, injury to coccyx and stiff neck" and to substitute therefore the words "history of back injury and back pain".

4. Following this reconsideration of his case by the respondent, the applicant is still dissatisfied and seeks a review of the matter by the Tribunal. In his written application for a review by the Tribunal, the applicant stated as his reasons for seeking the review, the following:-

"failure to seek 'specialist' medical opinion regards

(1) cause of back pain

(2) long-term diagnosis of complaint

(3) handing down of decision applciable to the case."

He gave evidence before us from which it emerges that he contends that the words "history of back injury and back pain" are too wide and general and should be replaced by the more specific words "lumbar back disease". In the submission of the applicant, a specification in the certificate in these terms would localize his problem and would properly limit the application of the certificate to the circumstances of his case.

5. As has been said, the applicant gave evidence before us. He told us that he first experienced back problems in 1967 while serving as a member of the R.A.A.F. He said that he had a fall which resulted in his being paralysed "from the waist down in both legs for a couple of days, and then the left leg, from memory, for approximately five days and I was in hospital for a period of approximately fourteen days". He also said that he went to Darwin with the R.A.A.F. in 1967. There, whilst employed in spreading sand and soil in the rifle range, he pulled a muscle in his back which, he assumed, was an aggravation of his previous injury. He was hospitalized "for a couple of weeks". He added that he was discharged medically fit from the R.A.A.F. on 20 April, 1979 and almost immediately commenced his employment with the Commonwealth Public Service.

6. There was also evidence before us that on 9 January, 1980 the applicant, whilst entering his place of employment with the Public Service, fell on a stairway and injured his back. In ensuing months he had a number of absences from work as a result of this injury.

7. It also appears that the applicant has had accepted by the Department of Veterans' Affairs a claim arising from his service with the R.A.A.F., which acceptance relates to lumbar disc disease.

8. The Tribunal is now reviewing, under Section 154(6), the original decision taken under Section 16(4) as varied and in so doing is empowered to exercise all the powers and discretions conferred by the Act upon the respondent.

9. As has been said, the respondent treated the applicant's objection to the original certificate as a request under Section 154(2) of the Act for a review of the decision to issue the certificate. It appears from the Section 37 statement in the papers before us that the decision of the respondent following the applicant's objection was to vary the original decision by deleting the conditions "migraine", "stiff neck" and altering the description of "injury to coccyx" to "history of back injuries and back pain", and that the variation was made pursuant to powers conferred upon Section 154(4) of the Act.

10. Subsequent to the hearing of this matter and when consideration was being given by the Tribunal to its decision in the matter, it was thought a question may arise as to whether the matter was properly dealt with under Section 154 or whether the request should have been dealt with under Section 16(6) of the Act. That Sub-section is in the following terms:-

"Where a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of an eligible employee, he may, on grounds specified in the request, request the Commissioner to revoke or vary the certificate and, where an eligible employee makes such a request, he shall, within such period as the Commissioner requires, undergo such further medical examination or examinations by an approved medical practitioner or practitioners as the Commissioner requires."

As the question was not canvassed at the hearing, the parties were invited to furnish written submissions on it. The applicant, who was not legally represented, understandably furnished no submissions on the question. Submissions by the respondent were furnished by a letter dated 11 November, 1981.

11. In general terms the respondent's submissions are that two alternative avenues of review were open to the applicant. The first was pursuant to Section 154(2) of the Act under which a person dissatisfied with the decision of the Commissioner or his Delegate may request the Commissioner to reconsider it. Upon receipt of a request, the Commissioner is required (Section 154(4)) to reconsider the original decision and to confirm, revoke or vary it in such manner as he thinks fit. A decision so reconsidered may be the subject of further review by the Tribunal (Section 154(6)).

12. The second avenue of review, in the respondent's submission, was pursuant to Section 16(6) of the Act under which an eligible employee with respect to whom a benefit classification certificate issued pursuant to Section 16(4) is in force, may request the Commissioner to revoke or vary the certificate. Upon receipt of a request, the Commissioner is empowered to require the employee to undergo further medical examination and, after consideration of the matter, the Commissioner is empowered to either revoke the certificate, delete from it a mental or physical condition specified in it, or to refuse the request (Section 16(8)).

13. The respondent's submission is that the avenue provided by Section 154(2) is the proper one in cases where the request for a review is concerned with a primary decision to issue a certificate under Section 16(4) and which raises the question of the correctness or otherwise of that decision. On the other hand, the avenue provided by Section 16(6) is appropriate to those cases in which, during the currency of a certificate, a person's mental or physical condition may have altered, justifying either revocation of the certificate or the deletion of a specified condition from it. As pointed out in the respondent's written submission, a revocation or variation of a certificate under Section 16(8) takes effect on or from the date on which the revocation or variation is made. The rationalization of the avenues of review provided by Section 16(6) and Section 154(2) put forward by the respondent receives support in the decision of the Tribunal, apparently given in a directions hearing, in the case of Begaud and the Commissioner for Superannuation (No. N81/50).

14. We are informed that the form of benefit classification certificate has endorsed on it references to the review conditions contained in Section 16(6) and Section 154(2). Whether the applicant in the present case directed his mind to them does not appear. The applicant's immediate reaction to the issue of the certificate was contained in his undated letter appearing at pages T5 22/23 of the papers. This letter opened with the following words:-

"Further to previous discussions by telephone on 11 January 1980, I hereby given notice of appeal against the decision to issue a benefit classification certificate against me, my defence being as follows:"

The applicant then went on in his letter to contest that he suffered from the physical conditions stipulated in the certificate. He attached to his letter a certificate by Dr Nerang, to which previous reference has been made.

15. At the hearing before us, the applicant accepted that he had a disability affecting his lumbar area, the nature of which had not been determined. Thus, it may be said that, at the time of the hearing, he was not then contending that a benefit classification certificate should not have been issued; what he was challenging were the physical and medical conditions specified in it, his contention being that they should be replaced by a condition localizing his back complaint to the lumbar area.

16. However, when consideration is given to the terms of the applicant's letter, the view taken by the respondent with the applicant's then complaint was that the decision to issue the benefit classification certificate was an incorrect one, was one which in our opinion was properly open to the respondent as was his view that the decision was one reviewable under Section 154(4).

17. A feature of this case which has caused us concern is the paucity of the medical evidence available. As has been said, the applicant was examined by a Commonwealth Medical Officer. That officer's report, however, appears to have been based upon statements made by the applicant in answer to questions put to him rather than as a result of any physical examination made of the applicant. Oral evidence was given before us by Dr Stewart, another Commonwealth Medical Officer. It does not appear that Dr Stewart has ever examined the applicant, his evidence being largely confined to comment upon the medical history revealed by the applicant. There was also before us the medical certificate from Dr Nerang. This certificate is dated 14 January, 1980, a short time after the issue of the original certificate. So far as it related to the applicant's back condition, it stated that the applicant did have a history of low back pain for a few years. X-rays of the lumbo-sacral area revealed no injury. It was also stated that the applicant was referred to an orthopaedic specialist for his recurring low back ache, the result of which would be known later. However, there was no evidence before us of the orthopaedic specialist's findings.

18. It is evident from the foregoing that the applicant has suffered several accidents resulting in injury to his back. The available evidence suggests that his problems relate to his lower back, the nature and extent of which remain unexplained.

19. As has been stated earlier, the respondent, upon his reconsideration of the matter, deleted from the certificate the conditions originally appearing therein and substituted therefore the words, "history of back injury and back pain". Whether these words can, in the circumstances of this case, properly be described as a "condition" within the meaning of Section 16(4) is, we think, open to doubt as the word "condition", in its ordinary meaning, denotes the existence of a state of being. However, we think it should be kept in mind that a condition specified in a benefit classification certificate can have an important effect upon the legal rights and obligations of an employee and the respondent which extend over a long period of time. It thus seems to us that it is plainly necessary that a condition be expressed with as much precision as possible. The words presently appearing in the certificate, even if capable of being construed as a condition, are, in our view, too indefinite and uncertain. It appears to us that the applicant should be further examined by an orthopaedic specialist with a view to defining the problem relating to the applicant's back. This further examination would appear to be authorized by the provisions of Section 16(2) of the Act.

20. Our consideration of the matter leads us to the conclusion that the decision under review should be set aside and that the matter be remitted for reconsideration by the respondent in accordance with the views we have expressed.
