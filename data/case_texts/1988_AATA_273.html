ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX- allowable deductions - investment allowance "tooling" – whether "extruder heads" used in production of plastic pipes constitute "tooling".

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - ss. 62, 62AA, 62AB, 82AA, 82AF, 170.

Case 14 14 C.T.B.R. (N.S.) 66;

Burt v. Commissioner of Taxation [1912] HCA 49; (1912) 15 C.L.R. 466.

Applicant

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

Nos. NT36/6241-49

AAT Decision No 4610

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

9 September 1988

Sydney

) Nos. NT86/6241-49

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Applicant

DECISION

Tribunal : Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

Date : 9 September 1988

Place : Sydney

Decision : The Tribunal decides that in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1983 the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection under review is affirmed. In relation to all other years of income ended 30 June 1976 to 1985 inclusive, the determination of the Tribunal is that the decisions of the Commissioner upon the objections under review be varied and that the taxable income of the company be reduced by $2,503, $38,296, $5,112, $6,712, $10,346, $11,829 and $4,844 respectively.

(sgd.) PETER M. ROACH,

Senior Member.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) Nos. NT86/6241-49

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re :

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

9 September 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. The Applicant is a company which I shall refer to as "TUBE-CO". It is a manufacturer of PVC piping. It produces hollow pipes by the production technique known as the extrusion process. That process results in the conversion of granular plastic powder into stable hollow piping suitable for all manner of industrial and commercial uses. In all, the company could produce twenty-four sizes of pipe on the one production line. The pipes so produced had certain characteristics. In cross-section they were all round and all hollow. They differed in internal diameter; in external diameter; and in the thickness of the pipe wall. The pipes so produced also differed in colour - a circumstance I attribute to the plastic powder or additives. Ordinarily, pipes so produced were of uniform dimensions throughout their length but there was a capacity to enlarge an end-section of any standard pipe by a "belling" process so that, as enlarged, it could receive an un-enlarged section of otherwise identical pipe. Production was by a straight-line process which, for present purposes, involved three stages of production. The issues for determination in this reference relate to the second stage.

  1. In the first stage the granular plastic powder was introduced to a mixing chamber or barrel in which rotating feed screws pushed the raw material forward under considerable pressure. While being pushed forward, heat was applied to convert the granular powder into a flowing stream of homogenized plastic.
  2. In the second section ("the extruder head"), the flowing plastic stream passed around a torpedo-shaped metal tube which was held within the casing in the middle of the flowing plastic stream by a "spider". The spider was held in position within the extruder head by a set of vanes. The outer casing of the extruder head contained heating elements. As the flowing plastic was pushed forward under pressure it was diverted around the vanes or arms to be thereafter re-integrated into a single stream of material passing on to a die and pin at the forward end of the second chamber. I find that, in the second stage, the process of homogenization which commenced in the first stage, was continued in minor degree under the influence of heat and pressure. The pin was so designed that it was of diminishing diameter with a diameter matching that of the torpedo at their meeting point but being least at the forward end of the production line: that diameter being equal to the intended internal diameter of the pipe to be produced. The die is of matching configuration and so designed that its least diameter is also at the forward end and that diameter is equal to the external diameter of the pipe being produced.
  3. In the third stage the now wholly homogenized flow of plastic was received under pressure and passed cooling and polishing dies which determined the external diameter of the pipe. The plastic was thereby cooled under controlled conditions to produce a solid-walled, durable pipe ready for use.
    1. By selecting particular combinations of die and pin for use within the extruder head and of dies for the third section it was possible for TUBE-CO to produce some twenty-four models of pipe, each of which was capable of being belled by the belling process. Each segment of any particular size of un-belled pipe which was manufactured in accordance with specification using any particular standards and colour of plastic powder was identical in all. respects. Further, although each segment so manufactured to any particular specification was identical, each of the twenty-four models of pipe which TUBE-CO was equipped to produce was quite distinct from the other twenty-three models.
  4. 6. What is at issue in this reference is whether the purchase of the components of the extruder head (but not of dies or pins) qualifies the Applicant for "investment allowance" deductions for income tax purposes in addition to its undisputed claim for depreciation on capital equipment. The issue arose when, following an investigation, the Commissioner raised amended assessments in which he disallowed claims to investment allowance previously made and allowed in relation to components of the mid-section and of the end-section. The company's claim to investment allowance in relation to the first section as originally made and allowed was not disputed upon the issue of the amended assessments. The company's claim in relation to the dies and pins having been disallowed they were objected to but, upon the commencement of the hearing, the claims were abandoned.

7. It is common ground that all save one of the requirements necessary to satisfy the investment allowance provisions have been satisfied. That one point of difference arises in that s.82AF(2) provides:-

"This Subdivision does not apply in relation to-

................................................................................................

(g) plant or articles referred to in paragraph 62AA(3)(h) or (i)...;

.................................................................................................

Section 62AA(3) provides: •

"This section does not apply in relation to-

........................................................................................................

(h) ... tooling (including work-holding fixtures, working heads and tool-holders), and articles of a description, or having a use, similar to that of any of those articles; or

.......................................................................................................

8. Counsel for the Commissioner argued that the question to be determined can be resolved simply by considering the meaning of the term "tooling". One can agree, but so diverse and varied are the meanings which may be attributed to that term and the related term "tool" (cf. some of the meanings to be found in the Macquarie Dictionary) that it is especially important to consider the term in the context in which it is used. Only then can a judgment be reasonably made as to the sense in which the Parliament intended the term to be understood. That in turn requires that both S.82AA and S.62AA should be considered in context.

  1. In 1962 the Parliament amended the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") to provide a special incentive to manufacturers to encourage investment in plant and equipment. S.62AA provided for the allowance of a deduction of 20% of capital expenditure incurred in certain circumstances by a manufacturer on new manufacturing plant. The special deduction was to be over and above deductions allowable under other pre-existing provisions of the Act. (In 1963 S.62AB was introduced to provide for a similar special deduction of 20% on capital expenditure incurred by taxpayers on new plant and equipment for use wholly and exclusively for the purpose of carrying on the business of primary production in Australia). The scheme remained in operation for nearly a decade.
  2. In 1976 a new incentive scheme was introduced to the Act. It was in the form of Division 3: Sub-Division B -Investment Allowance (S.82AA and following). The objectives were similar to the earlier scheme for investment allowance and to some extent the draftsman borrowed on the earlier provisions. So it was that s.82AF(2)(g) referred to "plant or articles referred to in paragraph 62AA(3)(h) or (i)" of the earlier scheme. That being so, in order to construe the relevant provisions of S.62AA, it is necessary to have regard to the scheme as enacted in 1962 rather than the scheme enacted in 1976.

11. S.62AA(5) provided:-

"Subject to this section, where the taxpayer has incurred expenditure of a capital nature on new manufacturing plant for use by him in Australia for the purpose of producing assessable income, there shall be allowed as a deduction from his assessable income of the first year of income during which that manufacturing plant is either used, as manufacturing plant, for the purpose of producing assessable income an amount equal to one-fifth of expenditure."

"Manufacturing plant" was defined by s.62AA(l) to mean –

"A. unit of property in relation to which this section applies."

S.62AA(2) provided that –

"Subject to sub-section (3), this section applies in relation to any property being plant or articles owned by the taxpayer that is for use by the taxpayer primarily and principally, and directly

(a) in any part of the operations by means of which -

(i) manufactured goods are derived from other goods (including other manufactured goods) by the taxpayer...; or

(ii) manufactured goods manufactured by the taxpayer or by other persons are (otherwise than by packing, placing in containers or labelling) brought into or maintained in the form or condition in which they are sold or used by the taxpayer or those other persons, as the case may be;

..........................................................................................

(The emphases are mine).

12. It will be seen immediately that, "subject to sub-section (3)", the concept of "manufacturing plant" embraced not the collection of manufacturing equipment commonly referred to as a "plant" (cf. "the fixtures, implements, machinery and apparatus used in carrying on any industrial process..." S.O.E.D.). It also extended to each and all of the components of such a "plant" provided that they were used "primarily and principally, and directly in any part of the operations by means of which manufactured goods are derived... or brought into or maintained in the form or condition in which they are sold..."

  1. S.62(4) of the Act was so cast as to ensure that "plant or articles" used in particular industries - petroleum, wool, timber, primary products, freezing, printing, meat curing, fish curing, milk pasteurization, food canning and power generation would be considered to be "manufacturing plant".
  2. S.62AA(3) established another parameter. It excluded from consideration as "manufacturing plant" a diversity of items including -

    • (a) plant or articles for use in mining or quarrying operations;
(b) road vehicles;
  • (c) the manufacture of goods to be used in road, bridge, dam and building construction;
  • (d) cooking appliances and other plant or articles used in preparation of food or drink;


(e) office equipment;
  • (f) containers, spools or other articles in or on which goods are to be delivered;
  • (g) plant or articles for use in power production in the circumstances specified; and

(h) the two provisions imported by s.82AF(2)(g): "plant or articles referred to in paragraphs 62AA(3)(h) or (i)".

15. The terms of those two provisions are such that, for reason which shall become obvious, I shall refer to them as the "Roget clauses". Neither Counsel could advance any suggestion as to the sources drawn on by the draftsmen in compiling the clauses. The Roget clauses are of such particularity that I shall set them out in full. They are in the following terms:-

"62AA(3) This section does not apply in relation to-

.......................................................................................................

(h) blocks, bolsters, core boxes, dies, driers, flasks, gauges, jigs, lasts, matrixes, moulds, patterns, saggars, stereotypes, templets and tooling (including workholding fixtures, working heads and tool holders), and articles of a description, or having a use, similar to that of any of those articles; or

(i) hand tools and other loose tools, including anvils, arbors, augers, bits, blow-lamps, braces, brushes, buckets, buffers, callipers, chisels, chucks, clamps, countersinks, cramps, crow bars, cutters, dial indicators, die-stocks, dividers, drills emery wheels, explosive tools, face-plates, files, forks, funnels, gimlets, grease guns, grindstones, hammers, jacks, knives, levels, mallets, mandrels, measuring equipment, micrometers, nail-pullers, needles, oil cans, oil guns, picks, pincers, planer-knives, planes, pliers, portable electric or pneumatic hand tools, protractors, punches, rasps, reamers, ropes, saw-sets, saws scales, scissors, scrapers, screwing tools, scribers, shears, shovels, sieves, soldering irons, spanners, spokeshaves, spray guns, stapling machines, stencils, strainers, taps, tap holders, thimbles, tin snips, tongs, torches, tube benders, vee blocks, vices and wrenches, and articles of a description, or having a use, similar to that of any of those articles."

16. Some guidance in understanding the meaning of the term "tooling" in the context of the section may be had by considering some of the definitions which appear in the Shorter Oxford English Dictionary. Many of the terms are capable of many meanings (cf. "block") but the phrases to be now quoted do exhibit a common characteristic: they are all about the definition of the final form of things produced.

Phrases which appear in the Shorter Oxford English Dictionary in relation to the terms in sub-section 62AA(3)(h) include the following:-

BLOCK : a piece of wood or other substance on which something is moulded or shaped;

BOLSTER : the plate or block of a punching machine on which the metal to be punched is laid;

CORE BOX : (core) an internal mould filling the space intended to be left hollow on a hollow casting;

DIES : an engraved stamp (often one of two) for impressing a design or figure upon some softer material...;

DRIER : there does not seem to be any relevant definition;

FLASK : a frame or box used to hold a portion of the mould for casting;

GAUGE : a contrivance to regulate the penetration of a cutting tool;

JIG : to shape an earthern vessel with a jigger;

LAST : a machine for turning wood, metal, ivory etc. in which the article to be turned is held in a horizontal position by means of adjustable centres and rotated against cutting tools;

MATRIX : a piece of metal, usu. copper, by means of which the face of a type is cast, having the letter stamped on it in intaglio with a punch. Also in stereotyping, the mould of plaster, etc. into which stereotypers' metal is cast;

MOULD : (1)a pattern by which something is shaped; e.g. the

templet used by a shipbuilder, mason, bricklayer, or plasterer; (2) a hollow form or matrix into which fluid or plastic material is cast or pressed and allowed to cool or harden so as to attain a particular shape or pattern;

PATTERN : a matrix, a mould; a figure in wood or metal from which a mould is made for a cast;

SAGGAR : a protecting case of baked fire-proof clay in which the finer ceramic wares are enclosed while baking in the kiln;

STEREOTYPE: something continued or constantly repeated without change; to fix or perpetuate in an unchanging form;

TEMPLET : (a) an instrument used as a gauge or guide in bringing any piece of work to the desired shape; usu. a flat piece of wood or metal having one edge shaped to correspond to the outline of the finished work; also, used as a tool in moulding;

(b) a flat plate or strip perforated with holes used as a guide in marking out holes for riveting or drilling.

Those definitions form part of the relevant background against which the term "tooling" has to be construed.

17. Of all those terms, the one most nearly appropriate to describe the torpedo is "core" or "core box", but there remains one major distinction. The extruder head only "forms" the flowing plastic while it is in the head. If the material passed through the extruder head without being passed through the die and pin into the third section, the extruded material - the extrudite - would be form-less.

  1. It is contended by Counsel for the Commissioner that there was nothing in the terms of the Act to indicate that any of the terms in the Roget clauses were being used in any special sense distinct from their ordinary meaning; and that, in any event, there was nothing in the evidence placed before to indicate that any of the terms were used in the plastics industry in any special sense distinct from their ordinary meaning. I agree.
  2. In resolving the matter I take as my starting point the concept that the Parliament was taking steps to encourage taxpayers to incur "expenditure of a capital nature on new manufacturing plant". I am quite satisfied that in 1962 the Parliament was well aware of the industrial revolution; that the Parliament had it in mind that manufacture by machinery using mass-production techniques was the norm in industry; and that that was more the subject of its concern than manual production carried out principally by individuals working with their hands aided by "hand-tools".

20. Just as "manufacture" once meant "man-made" and was used principally in the sense of being manually made, so too "tooling" in earlier times was most commonly used as a term to describe the means for giving a specific shape or form to an object, be it a stone or a book, so as to give it distinct characteristics only capable of being repeated precisely with great difficulty. I find the term "replication" helpful - not in the sense used by pleaders in litigation, but in the sense of making precise copies ("replicas") and, in doing so, producing not only a "duplicate" (or one copy) but in the way of repeated production of identical items. With the advent of mass-production replication came to be easily achieved. The predominant characteristic of mass-production was identity of form achieved by moulds, matrixes, templets, dies, lasts, and the like. The use of such articles served the function of definitively setting the final form of the article which was to be the product of the manufacturing process.

  1. That is how the matter was viewed in Case 14 [4 C.T.B.R.(N.S.) 66] which concerned the lasts which welded together the sole and upper of shoes in a particular configuration which was constant for each last but which varied from last to last. The claim in relation to the lasts was disallowed. Similarly, in this case, the dies and pins defined the final form and dimensions of the pipe. But the torpedo, the spider pin and the extruder head did not.
  2. I find that the function of the extruder head in the manufacturing process was to contribute to the finalisation of homogenization process and to ensure that the homogenized plastic was so presented to the die and pin as to enable the plastic to be moulded in the form defined by the die and pin to a consistency, strength and quality as required by the pipe's specification. The function of the extruder head, like that of a vat from which molten metal is poured into a mould, is to present the material to be formed into product in a condition, quality and volume suitable for use by the final, form-defining machine in the production process.

22. I find that the term "tooling" is to be construed eiusdem generis with the terras preceding it. In that context it is not to be construed in the much broader sense of referring to either any machine which contributes in any way to the production of the end product or as referring to the collection of machines which together comprise a manufacturing plant or production plant. Further, I hold that neither the inclusion of words in parenthesis to provide:-

"(including workholding fixtures, working heads and toolholders),"

nor the final words:-

"and articles of a description, or having a use, similar to that of any of those articles."

persuades me to any different view whatever those particular earlier terms may mean - a matter of considerable doubt according to the experts experienced in production who gave evidence before me - they did not in my view either enlarge the concept of "tooling" or, by their own force, constitute the extruder head as falling within any of the categories of "workholding fixtures, working heads and toolholders".

23. I am confirmed in that view by the fact that s.62AA(5) speaks of "manufacturing plant", although s.62AA(l) defines "manufacturing plant" to mean "a unit of property" which, clearly by reference to other provisions of the section, includes single articles which, in conjunction with other components, can go to make up a "plant".

24. I also find support in the circumstance that, although s.62AA(2) provides that the section applies "in relation to any property being plant or articles", and although s.62AA(3) excludes from the application of the section "plant or articles" [as described in sub-paragraphs 3(a), (c), (d), (e) and (g)], that phrase is not used to effect the exclusion from the operation of the section of

"road vehicles, wherever or however used, or the kinds ordinarily used for the transport of persons or the delivery of goods" [s.62AA(3)(b)]; or

"containers, spools or other articles in or on which goods are to be delivered by the taxpayer Cs.62AA(3)(f)3; or

in relation to the Roget clauses Cs.62AA(3)(h) and (i)]. In particular, the Roget clauses refer only to "articles". That all supports the view which I have formed that the Roget clauses are limited in scope to "tooling" which defines the final form of the item to be produced [s.62AA(3)(h)]; and to "hand tools and other loose tools", being particular articles having an ancillary function in relation to the operation of productive plant [s.62AA(3)(i)].

24. I have reached that conclusion upon an analysis of the provisions of the Act considered in light of the objectives of the Parliament in introducing, or re-introducing, the concept of special concessions for those who were prepared to incur "expenditure of a capital nature on new manufacturing plant". I find that that conclusion is consistent with a principle of construction whereby it is appropriate to construe narrowly words of exception which would deny to taxpayers the benefits of concessions or advantages clearly intended to be conferred on them but for the words of exception [cf. Burt v. Commissioner of Taxation [1912] HCA 49; (1912) 15 C.L.R. 466 at p.482].

  1. For those reasons I am of opinion that the Applicant is entitled to succeed on this reference. That being so, it is unnecessary to consider the question as to whether the Commissioner was authorised, pursuant to s.170(3) to issue the amended assessments which issued in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1976 and 1977. Had it been necessary to consider that question it would have been important to determine whether there was an obligation upon the taxpayer to not only accurately describe the equipment in questions as "extruder heads" but whether, in order to effect a "full and true disclosure" to the Commissioner, it would have been necessary for the Applicant to explain the nature and function of the "extruder heads" as used by the Applicant so fully and precisely as to make known to the Commissioner and bring to his mind concepts such as would have been effectively conveyed to persons familiar with the industry by the use of the term "extruder heads".
  2. As the claims of the Applicant in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 have been abandoned, the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection under review in relation to those years shall be affirmed. In relation to all other years of income ended 30 June 1976 to 1985 inclusive, the determination of the Tribunal will be that the decisions of the Commissioner upon the objections under review be varied and that the taxable income of the company be reduced by $2,503, $38,296, $38,528, $5,112, $6,712, $10,346, $11,829 and $4,844 respectively.
