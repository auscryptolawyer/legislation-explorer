VT87/2561 and Commissioner of Taxation [1990] AATA 509 (15 June 1990)

Last Updated: 4 June 2008

INCOME TAX - deductibility - whether transaction entered into with the intention of making a profit - whether a hedge contract



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.51, 52, 80



Ronpibon Tin NL v FCT [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47

Amalgamated Zinc (De Bavay's) Ltd v FCT [1935] HCA 81; (1935) 54 CLR 295

Lunney v FCT (1958) 100 CLR 478

FCT v Myer Emporium Ltd [1987] HCA 18; (1987) 163 CLR 199 Case X47 (1990) 90 ATC 382



D E C I S I O N & R E A S O N S



Re:



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION



No VT87/2561

AAT Decision No 5978



Tribunal: Mrs R.A. Balmford, Senior Member



Date: 15 June 1990



Place: Melbourne



DECISION



Decision: The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.



....................... Senior Member



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL) ) No VT87/2561

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION)



Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



REASONS FOR DECISION



15 June 1990 Mrs R.A. Balmford, Senior Member



1. This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent disallowing an objection by the applicant, H Pty Ltd, to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of its taxable income for the year ended 30 June 1984 of an amount of $295,319 which had been claimed by it as a deduction by way of loss carried forward pursuant to section 80 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").



2. On 11 March 1986 the applicant requested that the decision on the objection be referred to a Board of Review, and the decision was referred to this Tribunal. By virtue of section 223(1) of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 and sub-section 189(2) of the Act, that referral of the decision on the applicant's objection is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision.



3. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 as modified by section 14ZG of the Taxation Administration Act 1953, together with extensive other material lodged at the hearing. Evidence was given by Mr WF, who was described as the chairman and managing director of the F group of companies. The applicant was represented by Mr de Wijn of counsel and the respondent by Mr Maxwell of counsel.



4. In its return of income for the year ended 30 June 1983 the applicant recorded a loss available to be carried forward of $360,761. In his assessment of the applicant's taxable income for the year ending 30 June 1984, the respondent allowed only $65,442 of that loss, leaving $295,319, the amount the subject of the present proceedings. It is not in issue, and I find, that that amount constitutes the loss incurred by the applicant during the year ended 30 June 1983 on a forward buying contract to purchase Japanese yen against US dollars. The question before the Tribunal is whether that amount is deductible pursuant to section 51 or section 52 of the Act, so as to be included in the applicant's losses carried forward in 1984 and subsequent years.



5. The submission of the applicant was that amount was deductible under either limb of sub-section 51(1). The transaction in question was entered into with the intention of making a profit, and that profit would have been assessable either under section 25 of the Act or under section 26(a) as it then stood. The loss which was in fact incurred was clearly not of a private or domestic nature, or related to exempt income. It was incurred in the ordinary course of business or in the carrying on or carrying out of a profit-making undertaking or scheme and thus was not a loss of capital or of a capital nature.



6. On the basis of the evidence of Mr WF and the documentary material before the Tribunal, I make the findings set out in this and the following four paragraphs. Identifying material has been removed or appropriately altered. Mr WF joined his father, Mr PF, in the F group of companies in the late 1970's, and since the death of his father in 1985 has been in effective control of the group. Prior to 1982 H Pty Ltd, a member of the group, was a share trader: most of the trading decisions were made by Mr PF or by Mr C, financial controller of the group.



7. In the early 1980's Mr WF became interested in speculative trading in commodity futures, and began to direct some of H Pty Ltd's activities to dealing in futures contracts in precious metals, cattle and currencies through the Sydney Futures Exchange ("SFX"), which was the only method available. The only overseas currency transactions which could be entered into through the SFX were in Australian dollars against US dollars. Transactions were carried out through a merchant bank, B Ltd, which had a subsidiary which was a member of the SFX. The motive in these dealings was to make a profit. The following passage from the respondent's Ruling No IT 2228 explains the nature of these dealings sufficiently for present purposes:



"A futures trade involves a taxpayer entering into a futures contract. In substance it is simply an agreement between two parties to purchase and sell to each other some goods at a specified time in the future. At law there is created an executory contract. In the formal futures market all contracts are standardised designating a specific quantity and quality of the commodity to be delivered at a specified future date at a price agreed upon at the time the contract is entered into. There is not a purchase and sale of the actual commodity. Title does not change hands. Money does not change hands at the time the contract is entered into. The commodity that the contract relates to may but need not exist at the time the contract is made."



8. Throughout the year ending 30 June 1982, H Pty Ltd dealt in 232 separate futures contracts, at a total nominal purchase price of nearly $47 million, for a total loss of $38,000. Share trading activity also took place in that year. No documentation was available in respect of earlier years. Mr WF said that H's activity in futures trading had commenced prior to the 1982 financial year. In the year ending 30 June 1983 H Pty Ltd dealt in only fifty futures contracts, and dealing ceased in late 1982. If the loss the subject of the present proceedings is left out of the account, the company made a profit on those dealings of just under $1000. In September 1982 the company closed off its account with B Limited. Almost all the company's shareholdings were disposed of in that year.



9. At all relevant times O Pty Ltd was "an entity used for borrowing funds on behalf of the F group of companies and the X group of companies". Administration of O Pty Ltd was handled by the X group, and its commercial decisions were made on a basis of consultation between the two groups. The F group had a 50 per cent interest in O Pty Ltd, and decisions in respect of that interest were made by Mr PF. In 1981 and 1982 O Pty Ltd borrowed large sums in yen, US dollars and deutschmarks, to be repaid in 1982 and 1983. Funds borrowed by O Pty Ltd were used for the general purposes of the F group and the X group. Officers of the F group of companies, Mr A, an account, and Mr C, financial controller, were in regular contact with the Reserve Bank in connection with these borrowings. Neither of those officers is still with the F group, although Mr WF is aware of the whereabouts of Mr C and possibly of Mr A.



10. The principal banker of the F group of companies was the ANZ Banking Group ("ANZ"). L Pty Ltd, a member of the F group, had given a registered charge to ANZ to secure advances to members of the F group, including H Pty Ltd. Borrowings by O Pty Ltd were channelled into the F group through L Pty Ltd, which operated as a "finance and administration" company.



11. On 23 March 1982 Mr A sent the following memorandum to Mr PF with copies to Mr WF and Mr C:



" Purchase of Japanese Yen - H Pty Ltd. Today we purchased Yen 721,350,000 at a rate of Yen 240.45 to the U.S. dollar for delivery on 24th June 1982 (i.e. $U.S. 300[0]000)."



12. On 31 March 1982 Mr A signed on behalf of H Pty Ltd an application to ANZ under the Banking (Foreign Exchange) Regulations for authority to make the purchase described in the preceding paragraph. The section of the form requiring "description and details of the related commercial transaction" with the direction "Please specify type of transaction and give brief details including reference to any related Exchange Control letter of approval" was completed as follows: "To cover L group share of yen borrowings by O P/L - reference No 15622".



13. The Reserve Bank subsequently notified ANZ that the sections of the form requiring "Date on which payment or receipt is due" and "Name and country of residence of overseas party" should be completed. They also required details of how the amount and due date of the contract had been calculated, and the relationship between H Pty Ltd and O Pty Ltd. The ANZ's request to H Pty Ltd for this information was replied to by a letter dated 4 May 1982 and signed by Mr A on behalf of L Pty Ltd, which read:



"RE: Foreign/Foreign Contract No. 7000-32476. We advise answers to questions 3(b) and 3(c) on the Foreign/Foreign application form are as follows :- 3(b) O Pty. Ltd. has borrowings in Yen due as follows:

5 June 1982 Y 203,196,500

11 June 1982 Y 402,416,804

16 June 1982 Y 199,538,543

14 Aug. 1982 Y 158,925,690

7 Jan. 1983 Y 246,800,000

3(c) Citibank - Tokyo

The company took out the Hedge Contract to cover Yen borrowings by O Pty. Ltd. O Pty. Ltd, is a company in which the F Family holds a 50% interest. It was thought prudent to hedge the F Group share of the foreign currency exposure in O and accordingly this hedge was taken out in H Pty. Ltd., a fully owned F Group company."



14. On 3 June 1982 the first three loans listed in the preceding paragraph were extended to 6th, 12th and 15th July 1982 respectively.



15. On 18 June 1982 Mr C sent the following memorandum to Mr PF with a copy to Mr WF:



"Subject: Hedge Roll Please note that on the 17th June, 1982 the purchase of Japanese Yen in the name of H due on the 24th June, 1982 of 721,350,000 was rolled for an additional 32 days now bearing a maturity of 26th July, 1982 at a new conversion rate of 238.40 giving a U.S. dollar equivalent of $3,025,796.98."



16. On 26 July 1982 Mr C sent the following memorandum to Mr WF with a copy to Mr PF:



"Subject: Hedge Contracts

On the 23rd July, 1982 I arranged with the ANZ for the extension of the H purchase of Japanese Yen of 721,350,000 from the 22nd July, 1982 to the 27th September, 1982. The cost of the extension was Yen 2.6 (i.e. 6.6% p.a.) giving a new conversion rate of 235.80 and a new U.S. equivalent of $3,059,160.31."



17. On 11 August 1982 Mr C sent the following memorandum to Mr WF and Mr PF:



"SUBJECT: FORWARD EXCHANGE CONTRACTS

The Yen hedge in the name of H Pty. Ltd. due to mature on the 26th July, 1982 was cancelled with settlement effected on the 11th August, 1982 as follows:- Yen 720,350,000 @ hedge purchase rate - 235.80 = U.S. $3,059,160.31

Yen 720,350,000 @ cancellation rate - 260.45 = U.S. $2,769,629.49

Loss U.S. $ 289,530.82 Australian equivalent @ .9804

A. $ 295.319.07 The above loss will be debited to the account of H Pty. Ltd. on the 11th August, 1982. To avoid the necessity of going to the Reserve Bank on an opposite contract, it was decided to effect the cancellation as of the 11th August, 1982.

At forward cover rates falling at the 10th August, 1982 the above method resulted in the minimum loss."

The figure "721 350 000" has been written in on the document above "720 350 000" where first appearing.



18. Mr WF said in evidence that the transaction evidenced by the documents set out in paragraphs 11-17 supra was entered into by H Pty Ltd in the ordinary course of its business of futures trading. He was the person substantially in charge of that aspect of the company's business. He saw the possibility that the US dollar would weaken and the yen strengthen, and there was an opportunity for profit. There was "a general view around among people who follow these things that the US dollar had been too strong over a period of time and was due to weaken off". It was not possible to enter into a contract in respect of yen against US dollars on the SFX. He initiated enquiries with ANZ as to whether it would be possible for H to do so on the basis of the exposure to yen of O Pty Ltd, and he obtained approval to buy yen and sell US dollars and trade the contract.



19. He said that all decisions with regard to the futures trading business were essentially made by him, except for the final decision to close off the yen contract. Mr C and Mr PF had considered that the loss was sufficient and had eventually persuaded him that closing it off was the prudent thing to do. Asked why the contract was not entered into through the Chicago Mercantile Exchange where it would have been possible, he was initially uncertain, and subsequently said that he had believed that the only way to do it was through a bank. The contract had been entered into in the expectation that it would make a profit, and that was its sole purpose. It was never intended to take delivery of the yen. When he was asked about the inconsistency between that statement and the documents cited in paragraphs 11-17 supra, his answers were equivocal. He agreed that a hedge contract necessarily involved an intention to take delivery at a future date.



20. On the basis of the evidence set out in paragraphs 11-17 supra, and in particular the document extracted in paragraph 13 (Exhibit 5), Mr Maxwell submitted that the transaction with which these proceedings are concerned was a hedging loan intended to cover the borrowings by O Pty Ltd and not to make a profit. The contemporaneous documents were more to be relied upon than the recollections of Mr WF in giving evidence almost eight years later. The transaction bore no relationship to any other futures transaction in the two years as to which information was before the Tribunal. It related to yen: it was not made through B Ltd: it was not made on the SFX: and the amount of the loss was hugely different from any other loss incurred in the two years, and larger by a factor of 10 than any other loss incurred in the 1983 financial year.



21. He drew attention to the fact that neither Mr C nor Mr A had been called to give evidence, although Mr WF's evidence had been that he knew the whereabouts of Mr C and had a starting point for locating Mr A. Their evidence as to their instructions, their communications with ANZ, and the reasons why Exhibit 5 was written as it was, would be crucial. The Tribunal was entitled to draw the inference that their evidence would not assist H Pty Ltd. Mr WF, who had given the evidence, was at one remove from them.



22. He pointed out that the first two internal memoranda concerning the loan (set out in paragraphs 11 and 15 supra) were directed to Mr PF, who was the director principally concerned with the affairs of O Ltd, and not to Mr WF, who made the decisions about futures trading.



23. If the transaction was not a hedge, he submitted, Exhibit 5 was an outright falsehood. It seemed improbable that Mr A, an employed accountant, would put his name to a falsehood. He had a continuing relationship with the Reserve Bank, and it would not be worth his while to deceive it. The language of the internal memoranda, which were not prepared for the Reserve Bank, was consistent with Exhibit 5 in describing the transaction as a hedge.



24. Mr Maxwell concluded by submitting that the transaction was intended as a hedge against rather more than one half of the yen liabilities of O Pty Ltd, a company in which the F group had a half interest. There was no intention of gaining or producing assessable income. He referred to well-known passages from Ronpibon Tin NL v FCT [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47, Amalgamated Zinc (De Bavay's) Ltd v FCT [1935] HCA 81; (1935) 54 CLR 295 and Lunney v FCT (1958) 100 CLR 478, which it is not necessary to reproduce here, and on the basis of those authorities submitted that the loss was not deductible under either limb of sub-section 51(1).



25. Mr de Wijn submitted that the transaction in question was inherently speculative. The only possible outcomes were a profit or a loss for H Pty Ltd. The Tribunal should rely on the evidence of Mr WF. When the substantial loss was incurred on the transaction the company abandoned the business of futures trading (see paragraph 8 supra). This indicated that the transaction was a dealing in the ordinary course of that business.



26. The transaction could not be a hedge for three reasons, he submitted. It was not entered into by the entity which owed the debt: it did not eliminate risk as between US dollars and Australian dollars: and the settlement date did not match the due dates of the loans to O Pty Ltd.



27. Mr de Wijn summarised as follows his client's evidence with regard to the documents:



"Mr F has given a perfectly explicable, believable explanation to those documents. He said quite frankly that this company, that is H Pty ltd, wanted to speculate on the US yen currency movement. It had an opportunity to do so, because as he understood it, the Reserve Bank would give approval because there was some sort of underlying transaction, albeit that in his own mind, unrelated. He said that his understanding of the Reserve Bank situation, was that they were only concerned with what would happen if one took delivery of the currency and that appears (a) to make sense; and (b) to be consistent with the fact that the Reserve Bank approval apparently was not required, it certainly was not asked, on the closing out of the contract. So in his mind it is quite feasible and explicable that, as far as the Reserve Bank was concerned, the explanation given to them was an explanation only to do with what might be done with the moneys if they took delivery of the yen, and although with the benefit of hindsight, many years after the event, one might be able to say it does not clearly indicate what was intended, one can see the reasoning in Mr F's mind, this was a commodity that we were not going to take delivery of, the Reserve Bank is only concerned if we take delivery and really that is the end of the matter."



28. He submitted that either the transaction was an ordinary transaction as part of the company's business, or it was an isolated transaction intended to make a profit. In either case a profit would have been assessable and the loss was accordingly deductible under sub-section 51(1). He referred to FCT v Myer Emporium Ltd [1987] HCA 18; (1987) 163 CLR 199.



29. Having considered the evidence, I prefer to rely on the contemporaneous documents rather than on the evidence of Mr WF at the hearing in 1990 as to the purpose of the transaction. Both cannot be true. It may be that Mr WF disguised the true nature of a speculative transaction in order to obtain Reserve Bank approval. If so, he must now bear the consequences of that deception. However, I accept the submission of Mr Maxwell as to the evidence, and in particular the internal memoranda, indicating that the contemporaneous documents describe the transaction accurately.



30. In reaching that view, I have taken into account the submissions of Mr de Wijn set out in paragraph 26 supra. It is clear from the documents and the evidence of Mr WF that the F group of companies operated as a group. No evidence is before me as to shareholding and formal control. However, on the evidence, it seems entirely probable that one member of the group would enter into such a transaction in respect of the debt of another: Mr Maxwell suggested various possible reasons why this should have been done. The possible risk as between Australian dollars and US dollars, and the non-alignment of the settlement dates raise questions, but are not of themselves enough, in my view, to counterbalance the evidence which has led me to my conclusion.



31. Mr de Wijn referred me to the decision of the Tribunal, constituted by Mr Beddoe, Senior Member, in Case X47 (1990) 90 ATC 382. However that decision, like the decision in the present matter, turned on the particular facts there before the Tribunal.



32. I am satisfied on the evidence, and I find, that the purpose of the transaction in question was to provide a hedge against the F group's share of the yen borrowings of O Pty Ltd, and not to make a profit. That being so, it is not deductible under either limb of section 51, or under section 52 as it stood at the relevant time. Accordingly, the applicant has not discharged the burden imposed on it by section 190(b) of the Act of proving that the assessment is excessive. The objection decision under review will be affirmed.
