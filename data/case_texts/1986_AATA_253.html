Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - superannuation fund - investments - ratio of public securities - failure to comply - exercise of discretion.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s.121C.

Case 28 22 CTBR (NS) 283, 78 ATC 87

F.C. of T. v Wade [1951] HCA 66; (1951) 84 CLR 105

Re Aston and Secretary, Department of Primary Industry (1985) 8 ALD 366

Re Drake [1979] AATA 179; (1979) 2 ALD 634

DECISION AND REASONS

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

AAT No 2883

Date: August 29, 1986

Mr KL Beddoe, Senior Member



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL No B



TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION



Re:



APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION



RESPONDENT



REASONS FOR DECISION

Tribunal: Mr K L Beddoe, Senior Member

Date: 29 August 1986

Place: Brisbane

The question at issue in this application is whether the Commissioner of Taxation made the correct decision when he refused to exercise a discretion in favour of the trustee. The discretion in question is to be found in terms of subsection 121C(4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

2. This applicant is the trustee of a non-contributory superannuation fund which would be exempt from income tax under section 23F except that it failed to comply with section 121C and therefore became taxable within the terms of section 1210. There was no issue before the Tribunal about the exemption of the Fund under section 23F.



3. To put the matter in context sub-section 121C provides, so far as is relevant, that the investment income of a superannuation fund, such as this fund is not exempt from income tax by virtue of section 23F unless the Commissioner is satisfied that, at all times during the year of income:-

(a) the assets of the fund included public securities the cost of which was not less than 30% of the cost of all the assets of the fund; and

(b) the assets of the fund consisting of public

securities included Commonwealth Securities the

cost of which was not less than 20% of the cost

of all the assets of the fund.

Sub-section 1210(4) provides that the Commissioner shall disregard any failure of the assets of a superannuation fund to include at all times during a particular year of income, assets as provided by any provision of section 121C if he is satisfied:-

(a) that the trustee of the fund made a genuine and bona fide attempt to ensure that the assets of that fund included, at all times during that year of income, assets as so provided; or

(b) that the failure was by reason of a temporary delay in investment, and that, in all the circumstances, it would be reasonable to disregard the failure.
  1. Although not in issue in the application before the Tribunal it is also necessary for a full understanding of this matter to have an appreciation of the operation of section 82AAE. This section is concerned with the maximum deduction allowable to employers in respect of contributions to superannuation funds for the benefit of employees. Funds approved for exemption under section 23F are such funds and it is beyond dispute that the applicant was trustee of such a fund.
    1. Section 82AAE contains another discretionary power vested in the Commissioner of Taxation. The section reads as follows:-

"82AAE. The deduction, or the sum of the deductions, allowable under this Subdivision in an assessment or assessments of a taxpayer or taxpayers in respect of income of the year of income in respect of amounts set apart or paid by the taxpayer or taxpayers as or to a fund or funds for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, any one employee –

(a)shall not exceed whichever is the greater of the following amounts:

(i) $400; and

(ii) 5% of the total remuneration paid to the employee by taxpayers during the year of income of the employee that, in the opinion of the Commissioner, corresponded to the first mentioned year of income in respect of his employment by those taxpayers; or

(b) if the Commissioner is of the opinion that there are special circumstances that justify the allowance of a greater deduction, or of deductions of a greater sum, than the amount ascertained in accordance with paragraph (a) - shall be such amount, being greater than the amount so ascertained, as he considers reasonable."



  1. A B C and D Services Pty Ltd ("trustee") is trustee for the A B C and D Partners Pty Ltd Superannuation Fund ("Fund"). The Fund was established 1 June 1980.
  2. The trustee lodged a return of income for the year ended 30 June 1982 about March 1983 having previously lodged a return for the year ended 30 June 1981.
  3. The 1982 return of income disclosed that total assets of the Fund were $109,725. These assets were made up as follows. (I have reproduced only the relevant part of the balance sheet):- Balance sheet omitted.

It will be apparent that the Australian Savings Bonds represented less than 4% of the cost of all the assets of the fund.

  1. A separate schedule attached to the return indicated that the interest bearing loan had been made to C Pty Ltd at 13.5% p.a. It was stated that the term of the loan was "payable on demand" but evidence before the Tribunal indicated that it was payable on demand after three months notice. C Pty Ltd is a company associated with A B C and D Partners Pty Ltd which carries on business as architects. It was established that the business of C Pty Ltd was that of providing services to the architectural practice carried on by A B C and D Partners Pty Ltd. It is what is euphemistically described as a "service company".
  2. The accounts revealed that although contributions received from the employer during the 1981 year of income had amounted to $63,666 the contributions received during the year of income ended 30 June 1982 had amounted to $100.
  3. A profit and loss account of the Fund attached to the return (Document T14) is set out in full as to details:-Table omitted.

    1. There was also evidence before the Tribunal of a bank statement in respect of the Fund's bank account (Document T6). The bank statement revealed that an amount of $12,907 was credited to the Fund's account on 30 June 1982. This amount represented interest paid by C Pty Ltd in respect of the moneys on loan from the Fund.
    2. The bank statement also revealed that the Fund's bank account was debited on 30 June 1982 with two amounts - both drawn by cheque - $1,000.00 and $11,907.00. Evidence was given by the trustee that the amount of $1,000 was invested in Australian Savings Bonds Series 21 and that the amount of $11,907 was paid to C Pty Ltd as a further loan.
    3. These investments by the Fund need to be considered in the light of Exhibit A which is the signed minutes of a meeting of Trustees of the Fund held on 21 June 1982. For a full understanding of this matter it is appropriate that I set the minutes out in full making adjustments to protect confidentiality only:-

"The chairman stated that the fund's advisers had advised that the Australian Taxation Office had made an announcement regarding the investment of superannuation fund assets. Apparently 30 per cent of a fund's assets must be invested in Commonwealth Government Securities, in future.

The Chairman also stated that there is some confusion regarding this announcement as to whether or not these changes will be implemented or take effect in the year ended 30th June, 1982 or 1983.

The Chairman advised that the fund has purchased $3,000 in Australian Savings Bonds on 30th June 1981. The Chairman also advised that the principal employer had requested that the trustees agree to make a loan to the employer as in previous years.

The advisers to the fund had suggested that the trustee consider borrowing the moneys necessary to purchase sufficient Government Bonds to meet the requirement. It seems that the Taxation Office will treat the fund as tax exempt if this is achieved. Furthermore it appears that a promoter of a scheme to enable the fund to borrow has advised that some time will be allowed after the 30th June 1982 to meet this.

The meeting considered whether this option would be appropriate. The Chairman suggested that it seemed peculiar that the Taxation Office would, in effect, be encouraging superannuation funds to borrow money to invest in Government securities. After all, the interest rate on the loan would be more than that earned by the investment. The Chairman expressed his failure to see how this could help the fund.

It was decided to retain the present investment strategy of the fund and to ensure that the appropriate amount of Government Bonds would be purchased as soon as possible. It was also decided to press for full repayment of the loan to C Pty Ltd and to invest the funds elsewhere.

This course of action was felt to be the best for the members of the fund. There certainly appeared to be no justification to borrow moneys to buy bonds.

It was RESOLVED that the Chairman's suggestions be accepted."



15. It is apparent that the respondent Commissioner's policy as to the exercise of his powers was an overriding factor in all of this. It is relevant to consider Exhibit C in this context. Exhibit C is a copy of a letter from the Commissioner of Taxation dated 22 December 1981 signed by Mr B Taylor, Senior Assistant Commissioner. The letter is addressed to a person who was at that time in partnership with the actuary who was advising the applicant trustee. There was no evidence before the Tribunal to establish whether the applicant's particular circumstances were being considered in Mr Taylor's letter. The text of the letter reads as follows:-

" I refer to your letter of 24 November 1981 wherein you raised several matters concerning superannuation funds .which have been subject to income tax under section 121D of the Income Tax Assessment Act.



Firstly, it is confirmed that in determining the contribution to a superannuation fund which will be allowable as an income tax deduction for the year ended 30 June 1982, the Commissioner's discretion under section 82AAE(b) of the Act will not be exercised because of the fact that the fund is subject to income tax under section 121D of the Act.

However, if the trustees of a fund take all necessary action to comply with the '30/20' ratio at the earliest practicable opportunity, but not later than 30 June 1982, the discretion under section 121C(4) of the Act will be exercised to disregard the failure of the fund to comply with the ratio at all times during the year of income. In these circumstances the income of the fund will be exempt and the discretion given under section 82AAE(b) of the Act will be exercised to allow the contribution necessary to provide the approved retirement benefit.

Turning now to the alternatives expressed in your letter it is considered that the first of these could be acceptable to this office. It would be necessary, however, to examine the particular facts of each case before a final decision could be given.

If the trustees of a fund adopted the course of action outlined as option 2 of your letter it is doubtful that special circumstances warranting the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion under section 82AAE(b) could be said to exist. Consequently, the allowable income tax deductions for contributions to the exempt fund would be limited to the statutory maximum.

Finally, I might add that, where, because of the nature of the investments held by the fund, it is not practicable for the fund to comply with the 30/20 ratio by 30 June 1982, it is open to the trustees to advise the Deputy Commissioner at whose office the fund return is lodged of the full facts of that particular case. In these circumstances every effort will be made to assist in any genuine attempt to comply with all relevant requirements."

16. The applicant's representative, who was the actuary advising the trustees, sought to place particular emphasis on the final paragraph. He did this in the context of evidence from the trustee that C Pty Ltd, and the architectural business with which it was associated, was suffering severe liquidity problems at or about 30 June 1982 and was not in a position to repay loans made. As has already been shown a further loan was made to C Pty Ltd on 30 June 1982.

  1. Exhibits A and C also support evidence by the actuary that there was considerable confusion among professional advisers in 1982 concerning the Commissioner's policy in relation to the application of section 121C. Evidence was given by the actuary that he had had meetings with officers of the Commissioner and also with a Second Commissioner in relation to the official policy. The uncertainty appears to have had its genesis in a change of policy in relation to section 82AAE as set out in Mr Taylor's letter (Exhibit C).
  2. The applicant's representative also tendered a copy of a letter dated 24 November 1982 addressed to a company not related to the Fund or its professional advisers. The letter was issued by the Australian Taxation Office, Brisbane, in the name of a Deputy Commissioner. The tender was made on the basis that the letter set out the official policy of the Commissioner at that time. The Commissioner's representative objected to the tender because the letter referred to the affairs of other taxpayers and the policy applicable to those taxpayers was not relevant to consideration of the liability of the Fund. Stated in that way the objection suggested that the Commissioner was selective in the application of his discretionary powers so that more favourable consideration is given to the clients of certain professional firms. I rejected the proposition. Such a proposition is outrageous. I also ruled that to the extent the letter set out the Commissioner's general policy it was relevant to the application. Certainly the letter (which became Exhibit B) establishes that the Commissioner was prepared to favourably exercise the discretion under sub-section 121C(4) where a fund which had previously relied upon the favourable exercise of discretion under section 82AAE, and had not therefore sought to comply with section 121C, was having difficulty complying with the change in the Commissioner's policy. The Fund's position at 30 June 1982 can only be properly comprehended in the light of this change in the Commissioner's policy.

19. The text of Exhibit B reads as follows:-

"Reference is made to your letter of November 1982 wherein you requested that the Deputy Commissioner exercise his discretion with respect to funds failing to comply with the 30/20 ratio and with the new guidelines as at 31 July 1982.

A separate request should be lodged on behalf of each fund that requires an extension of time to meet the requirements of the 30/20 ratio.

Generally the discretion under section 121C(4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, as amended, will be exercised for the years ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 provided that each fund acquires the public securities as proposed in your letter and achieves the. 30/20 ratio no later than 30 June 1983. Undertakings to this effect, signed by the trustees of the fund are to be lodged with the 1982 income tax return as well as the superannuation fund return for that year.

The discretion under section 82AAE(b) will be exercised for the year ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 also, subject to the requirements being met, and subject to a reasonable rate of interest being paid on any loan by the fund to the sponsoring employer. For the year ended 30 June 1982 a rate of not less than 13% per annum would have been regarded as being reasonable in this context.

Full details of the special circumstances in respect to each fund applying for an extension beyond 30 June 1983 should be forwarded. Consideration can then be given to the individual request and a limited extension may be granted subject to the following conditions:

  1. The total contributions and net income of the fund attributable to the years ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 are invested in public securities or life assurance policies if required by 30 June 1983;
  2. The 30/20 ratio will be achieved no later than 30 June 1984; and
    1. Undertakings to this, signed by the trustee, are lodged with the 1982 income tax return and the superannuation fund return for that year.

The discretion under section 82AAE(b) will be exercised for the years ended 30 June 1982, 1983 and 1984 provided that:-

  1. The requirements set out above are met; and
  2. A reasonable rate of interest is paid on loans by the fund to the sponsoring employer. For the year ended 30 June 1982 a rate of not less than 13-1/2% per annum would have been regarded as being reasonable in this context.

It should be noted that if the above requirements are not met, the income of the fund will not be exempt from income tax and the deductions for contributions to the fund will be limited to the statutory maximum in terms of section 82AAE(a), i.e. the greater of $400 and 5% of the employee's remuneration."

20. Exhibits A, B and C establish the context in which a letter dated 16 June 1983 was sent to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation by the applicant Fund's consultants. A copy of the letter is Document T17. The text of the letter reads as follows:-

"We act as superannuation consultants to the Trustees of the above Fund and have been instructed to request you to exercise your discretion to disregard the non-compliance of the Fund with Section 121C(1) of the Act for the years of income ended 30.6.82 and 30.6.83.

For your information we advise that the Fund was established on 1.6.80 and the Trust Deed governing the Fund satisfies the statutory requirements of Section 23F of the Act.

The Trustees have in the past made loans to the employer party in accordance with your guidelines, with the intention of seeking repayment of sufficient loans to allow the Fund to comply with the 30/20 ratio by 30.6.82 so as to take advantage of the discretion you exercised in that year.

However, the employer being an architectural practice experienced a tight liquidity position at that time reflecting the poor economic conditions of the building industry. Further, the Trustees were advised that the employer was experiencing great difficulty in recovering fees from certain of its larger developer clients and had been incurring expenditure on projects that did not proceed.

During recent months the employer group has been able to increase its cash flow through a major project and this has enabled the repayment of loans so that the Trustees have been able to purchase $20,000 Australian Savings Bonds on the 18.3.83. Further, loan repayments will be made by 30.6.83 so that the Fund will comply with the 30/20 ratio at that date.

Enclosed you will find a copy of the Balance Sheet of the Fund as at 30.6.83 which shows a nominal holding of Australian Savings Bonds.

Your early reply would be appreciated so that we may advise the Trustees of your decision."

The reference in the penultimate paragraph to 30.6.83 should be read as 30.6.82.

21. There was no evidence before the Tribunal that the Deputy Commissioner replied to or even considered this letter. By notice of assessment dated 9 August 1983 the Deputy Commissioner assessed the applicant on the basis that section 121D of the Act applied to the Fund and tax was levied at the rate applied by the Income Tax (Companies, Corporate Unit Trusts and Superannuation Funds) Act 1981 (Document T18). I infer that the Deputy Commissioner exercised the discretion in sub-section 121C(4) against the trustee.



22. By notice of objection under cover of a letter dated 7 October 1983 the trustee lodged an objection to the assessment (Documents T19-21). I will set out the grounds of objection in full because they effectively set out in summary form the arguments put before the Tribunal. The grounds relied upon in the objection are as follows:

“1 The Trust Deed governing the Fund has been approved as satisfying the statutory requirements of Section 23F of the Income Tax Assessment Act as per your letter dated 25th May, 1982.

  1. The Fund was established on 1st June, 1980 and in accordance with your guidelines in each successive year so as to ensure that it complies with Section 23F.
  2. The Trustees of the Fund have made loans to the employer party to the Trust Deed in accordance with your guidelines in each year and in particular the minimum accepted rate of interest of such loans has been enforced.
  3. It was the intention of the Trustees to seek repayment of sufficient loans to meet the requirements of Section 121C(1) by 30th June, 1982 so as to take advantage of the discretion exercised by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in deeming the Fund to have complied with Section 121C(1) for the whole year of income if it so complied on 30th June, 1982.
  4. The employer party advised the Trustees that it was unable to repay sufficient loans as described in 4 above as it was experiencing a tight liquidity position at the time reflecting the poor economic conditions of the building industry. By way of explanation the employer is an architectural practice.
    1. On 16th June, 1983 a letter was issued to the Australian Taxation Office by Consultants Pty Ltd, Superannuation consultants to the Trustees seeking that the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation exercise his discretion as referred to above to disregard the non-compliance of the Fund with Section 121C(1) of the Act for the years of income ended 30th June, 1982 and 30th June 1983. At the time it was not known if sufficient loans could be repaid to comply with Section 121C(1) but certainly it was the intention to do so. A copy of the letter is enclosed.
  5. As it transpired, as at 30th June, 1983 the employer has been able to repay all loans so that the Trustee has been able to comply with Section 121C(1) as at 30th June, 1983. A copy of a draft balance sheet at that date is enclosed.
  6. It can now be shown that during the year of income ended 30th June, 1983 all receipts into the Fund be they interest payments, new contributions or loan repayments have been applied by the Trustees in the first instance to ensure that the Fund complies with Section 121C(1).
  7. These above grounds have been prepared on the basis that income tax has been imposed on the income of the Fund for the year of income ended 30th June 1982 as a consequence of the Fund not complying with Section 121C(4) of the Act."
    1. Attached to the notice of objection were copies of the letter dated 16 June 1982 and attached balance sheet (Documents T22 and 23 - T23 being a copy of T17) and a draft balance sheet as at 30 June 1983. That draft balance sheet (document T24) disclosed the following in relation to the assets of the Fund:- Balance Sheet omitted.



  1. It will be apparent that the Fund had recalled the loan to C Pty Ltd and had complied with sub-section 121C(1) by 30 June 1983 (but not at all times during the year of income).
  2. The objection was disallowed and on 26 April 1984 the trustee requested that the decision to disallow the objection be referred to a Board of Review. On 17 June 1985 the Commissioner referred the matter to Taxation Board of Review No 3. That Board had not commenced to take evidence in the matter prior to 1 July 1986 when pursuant to the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 the reference became an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision.
    1. It is apparent from the evidence that the change of policy by the Commissioner provoked a change of investment policy by the trustee but that change in investment policy did not impress the Commissioner sufficiently to cause him to favourably exercise the discretion in sub-section 121C(4). In its early stages of its existence the Fund preferred to rely upon a favourable exercise of discretion under paragraph 82AAE(b), because, I infer, that resulted in the employer obtaining additional deductions for contributions to the Fund. Those deductions being allowable deductions in the hands of the-employer and the possibility of the Fund being able to obtain exemption in respect of income derived from loans back to an associated company in the form of interest which was an allowable deduction in the hands of that company constituted an almost irresistible investment package for the group including the trustee. It is not difficult to understand why the Commissioner came to the conclusion that his policy in respect of section 82AAE was having the effect that it did and should be changed. It is also not difficult to understand the trustee's position. This is brought out by what happened in the 1981 year of income when contributions of $63,666 were made to the Fund by comparison in the 1982 year of income (no doubt due to the difficult liquidity problems and, I infer, a decline in profitability on contracts) the contributions amounted to $100.
    2. It is clear enough that by 21 June 1982 the trustee had fully realised that the Commissioner had changed the general policy in relation to superannuation funds. It is also clear enough that the trustee had (by 21 June 1982 when the matter is formally recorded) decided to alter its investment policy so that it complied with the requirements of sub-section 121C(1). Clearly the trustee could not comply with that sub-section in the sense of maintaining a ratio of 30% of public securities to the total cost of all assets at all times during the year ended 30 June 1982.
    3. This non-compliance arose out of the following:-
      • (a) the deliberate failure to comply in the past (Document T15),
      • (b) the fact that the Fund's professional advisers apparently only became aware of the changed policy of the Commissioner during the year of income (Exhibit C and page 25-28 of transcript);
      • (c) the difficult liquidity problems of the business which resulted in it not being possible for C Pty Ltd to repay the loan made by the Fund immediately (Exhibit A and page 7 of transcript);
  • (d) the deliberate decision to lend $11,907 to C Pty Ltd on 30 June 1982 (Exhibit A and Document T6);
  • (e) the apprehension by the Fund's professional advisers that compliance as at 30 June 1982 or some later date would result in a favourable exercise of the discretion for the year ended 30 June 1982 (Exhibit C); and
  • (f) the trustee's refusal to enter into a scheme to enable the fund to comply as at 30 June 1982 or a date proximate acceptable to the Commissioner (Exhibit A).
  1. Unlike the Taxation Boards of Review this Tribunal has not been invested with all the powers and functions of the Commissioner as previously set out in now repealed sub-section 193(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act.
  2. Sub-section 43(1) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 provides that for the purpose of reviewing a decision the Tribunal may exercise all the powers and discretions that are conferred by any relevant enactment in the person who made the decision. That power clearly covers the issues arising in this application and I therefore turn now to a consideration of section 121C.
  3. The Commissioner's representative conceded that the Fund is a superannuation fund to which section 23F applies in relation to the year of income. There was no evidence before the Tribunal to suggest that this concession was not correctly made and I will proceed on the basis that the Fund is a superannuation fund coming within the terms of sub-section 121C(1A).
  4. In terms of sub-section 121C(1)it is clear that the Commissioner was correct in forming the view that he could not be satisfied that at all times during the year of income the assets of the fund included public securities the cost of which was not less than 30% of the cost of all the assets of the fund. That was the only view open to the Commissioner on the evidence and the applicant’s representative did not seek to suggest otherwise.
  5. Except for the discretionary provision in sub-section 121C(4) that would be the end of the matter. Turning to sub-section 121C(4) the Commissioner shall disregard any failure of the assets of a superannuation fund to include, at all times during a particular year of income, assets as provided by section 121C if he is satisfied on one of two assets.
  6. The first test is that the Commissioner must be satisfied that the trustee of the fund made a genuine and bona fide attempt to ensure that the assets of that fund included, at all times during that year of income, assets as provided by the section. The reality is that the trustee made no real attempt to comply with the section. I need go no further than note the deliberate decision to lend $11,907 to C Pty Ltd on 30 June 1982 when the holding of public securities was less than 4% of the cost of all assets of the fund. The decision to make this loan was made in the course of a meeting of the directors of the trustee which also discussed the need to comply with section 121C. The trustee’s representative virtually conceded the point in his submissions by arguing that given that the fund could not comply there was no point in trying to comply in part, ie to raise significantly, the public securities holding, albeit to less than 30% of the total cost of assets.
  7. In my view there is no basis to consider an exercise of the discretion arising from the test set out in paragraph 121C(4)(a). The applicant is well outside the paragraph.
  8. Turning then to the alternative test in paragraph 121C(4)(b) which is that the Commissioner must be satisfied that the failure to comply with sub-section 121C(1) was by reason of a temporary delay in investment. No explanation is given in the Act as to what is a temporary delay in investment and no explanation was offered to the Tribunal by the parties to this application although the applicant’s representative sought to rely on the paragraph. Exhibit A establishes that the policy of the trustee was “to ensure that the appropriate amount of Government bonds would be purchased as soon as possible.” It is also evidence of the decision by the trustee to press for full repayment of the loan to C Pty Ltd. I am satisfied that as at 30 June 1982 the failure to comply with sub-section 121C(1) was by reason of a temporary delay in investment. The trustee had resolved to make the required investments, was restrained from doing so immediately because of liquidity problems in the architectural business which had the effect of making it not feasible to recall the loan, and did not make the required investments when the loan was repaid.

37. It is also clear that the temporary delay only occurred in the last month of the year. It seems to be clear that prior to June 1982 the Fund had proceeded on the basis that it would not be complying with the so-called 30/20 rule. Without more this would be sufficient to say that the Fund was so far away from complying with sub-section 121C(1) by its own deliberate decision that there was no issue. But it resolved in June 1982 to so comply and it is at this point that the temporary delay in investment occurred. Given that the Commissioner seemed to have a general policy of favourably exercising the discretion in cases of non compliance throughout the 1982 year by funds which previously took advantage of the favourable exercise of discretion under section 82AAE there is no basis for saying that this fund should not have received the same consideration. It would be grossly unfair if this Tribunal did not take into account the fact that the Commissioner had changed his policy so as to give proper effect to the Act and had undertaken a certain course of action designed to ameliorate the adverse consequences of that change of policy.

38. There was no evidence before the Tribunal as to what material, if any, in addition to the applicant's tax return for the year ended 30 June 1982 was before the Commissioner when he made the assessment. I think I am entitled to infer that the letter dated 16 June 1983 (Document T17) was also taken into account given that it was probably received by the Commissioner seven weeks -before he issued the notice of assessment. Whether the Commissioner addressed the questions raised by sub-section 121c(4) is quite another issue. Apparently no response was made to the letter and I think I am entitled to infer from this that the Commissioner merely treated the letter as confirmation that the fund had failed to comply with section 121c and he made the assessment accordingly.

  1. This is not a case where the Tribunal can merely review the exercise of discretion by the commissioner nor is it a case where the Tribunal should be reluctant to interfere with an exercise of discretion by the Commissioner, especially as there is no evidence that the Commissioner ever applied his mind to the provisions of sub-section 121C(4).
  2. The question to be considered is, whether in all the circumstances it would be reasonable to disregard the failure to comply with sub-section 121C(1)?
  3. Facts which appear to be relevant, given that the Act does not lay down the circumstances to be taken into account, include the following:
    • (a) the change in the Commissioner's policy in relation to the administration of section 82AAE

as set out in the letter of 22 December 1982

(Exhibit C);

(b) the statement by the Commissioner in Exhibit A that "if the trustees of a fund take all necessary action to comply with the "30/20" ratio at the earliest practicable opportunity, but not later than 30 June 1982 the discretion under section 121C(4) of the Act will be exercised to disregard the failure of the fund to comply with the ratio at all times during the year of income"; and later, "Where, because of the nature of the investments held by the fund, it is not practicable for the fund to comply with the 30/20 ratio by 30 June 1982 it is open to the trustees to advise the Deputy Commissioner at whose office the fund return is lodged of the full facts of that particular

case. In these circumstances every effort will be made to assist in any genuine attempt to comply with the relevant requirements";

(c) the statement in Exhibit B by a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation that generally the discretion under section 121C(4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, will be favourably exercised for the years ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 provided that each fund acquires the public securities and achieves the required ratio no later than 30 June 1983;

(d) the Fund did not comply with sub-section 121C(1) at all times during the year ended 30 June 1982 consequent upon a deliberate decision by the trustee not to so comply;



(e) the Fund did not comply with sub-section 121C(1) at all times during the year ended 30 June 1983 but reached the required holdings of public securities during 1983 and maintained the requisite holdings to 30 June 1983;

(f) the trustee was entitled to reply on general rulings given by the Commissioner as to how the Commissioner would exercise the discretion in sub-section 121C(4) and that such general rulings would be applied by the Commissioner on a consistent and even-handed basis;

(g) the trustee made a decision to comply with sub-section 121C(1) at some time on or before 21 June 1982;

(h) on 30 June 1982 the trustee made a further loan of $11,907 to C Pty Ltd;

(i) the loan to C Pty Ltd could not be called in as at 21 June 1982 or immediately thereafter due to adverse financial factors in associated companies.

Taking all these circumstances into account I have concluded that the discretion in sub-section 121C(4) should be exercised in favour of the applicant trustee.



  1. No authorities were brought to the Tribunal's attention in these proceedings. I have obtained some assistance from the reasons for decision of Taxation Board of Review No 1 in Case 28 22 CTBR (NS) 283, 78 ATC 87 which dealt with paragraph 121C(4)(a) in particular.
  2. In relation to (f) in paragraph 41 above I have considered the dicta of Kitto J in FC of T v Wade [1951] HCA 66; (1951) 84 CLR 105 at 117 where his honour stated that no conduct on the part of the Commissioner could operate as an estoppel against the operation of the Act. In that case the question at issue was whether compensation received in respect of condemned livestock is assessable income. The Commissioner had brought the amount in dispute to account as assessable income under the wrong provision of the Act. There was no exercise of a discretion involved so that I do not think I am bound to follow his Honour's dicta in this application, involving as it does, an exercise of discretion.
    1. It seems to me that where the Commissioner is required to exercise a discretion which simply states "that, in all the circumstances, it would be reasonable to disregard the failure" a taxpayer is entitled to rest assured that the Commissioner will exercise the discretion in accordance with his published guidelines. The taxpayer is also entitled to assume that the discretion will be exercised objectively without fear of favour so that clients of one professional adviser will not receive more favourable treatment in the exercise of the discretion than clients of another professional adviser.

45. On this basis I believe it is correct in the circumstances for the Tribunal to have

regard to the Commissioner's rulings as they affect this application albeit that the

Tribunal is not bound to follow those rulings. This is especially so in a case such as

the present where the applicant has been adversely affected by the Commissioner's

change of policy in respect of the exercise of discretion under section 82AAE.

I do no more than cite a change of policy in respect of the exercise of the discretion under section 82AAE. I do no more than cite a paragraph I need do no more than cite a

paragraph from the decision of the Tribunal in Re Aston and Secretary, Department of Primary Industry (1985) 8 ALD 366. The Tribunal was constituted by his Honour the President, Deputy President Layton and Mr Pascoe. At page 378 the Tribunal stated:

"Thus, the function of this Tribunal is to make a decision in the particular case taking into account all relevant considerations, including any policy which has been enunciated by the primary decision-maker. The weight to be given to policy will vary according to the circumstances in which the policy was developed and the appropriateness of its application to the case on hand. The principle was stated as follows by Brennan J in Re Drake

(No 2), supra at 643: 'If the Tribunal applies Ministerial policy, it is because of the assistance which the policy can furnish in arriving at the preferable decision in the circumstancews of the case as they appear to the Tribunal. One of the factors to be considered in arriving at the preferable decision in a particular case is its consistency with other decisions in comparable cases, and one of the most useful aids in achieving consistency is a guiding policy. An appropriate guiding policy should thus be applied, ...'."

The citation for Re Drake is [1979] AATA 179; (1979) 2 ALD 634.

46. The Respondent's decision on the objection is to be set aside.



  1. I have concluded that it would be reasonable to disregard the failure to meet the test laid down by section 121C.
    1. The Applicant's objection in respect of the year ended 30 June 1982 is therefore allowed.
