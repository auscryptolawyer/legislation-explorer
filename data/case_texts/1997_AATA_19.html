CATCHWORDS

INCOME TAX - Director/shareholder of clothing manufacturing company - cheques received from customers converted to cash - failure to include as assessable income - whether a deemed dividend pursuant to s. 108 - whether assessment excessive - penalty tax - decision affirmed.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 44(1), 108(1), 227(3)

Taxation Administration Act 1963 sub-s. 14ZZK(b)

Martin v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 93 ATC 5200

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Dalco 90 ATC 4088

VT96/30-32, AAT Decision No. 11,437

REASONS FOR DECISION

24 January 1997 Deputy President B.M. Forrest

Mr D.L. Elsum, AM, Member

Mr W.G. McLean, Member

This is an application by the taxpayer for review of a decision of the Commissioner of Taxation to disallow an objection against an assessment of income tax issued to her for the year ended 30 June 1991.

The applicant purchased a 50 per cent shareholding in M Pty Ltd (“the company”) a clothing manufacturing company incorporated on 27 June 1990, the other 50 per cent shareholding was held by Mr L.

The company, Mr L and the applicant were the subject of an audit for the year ended 30 June 1991.

The auditor found that the company had laundered cheques totalling $406,855 which it had received from its customers as payment in return for finished garments in the year ended 30 June 1991. The company then endorsed the cheques over to the company’s accountant in return for a discounted cash amount. The cheques were deposited in third party bank accounts and were not recorded as income in the company’s accounts.

The audit also revealed the company had not lodged an income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1991. At the behest of the auditor an income tax return was lodged on behalf of the company on 1 December 1993. The company’s taxable income of $397,342 for the year ended 30 June 1991 was calculated by adding the amount of the cheques said to have been laundered ($406,855) to the loss of $9,513 reported by the company. A company tax assessment was issued on 31 January 1994. It has not been challenged. The company was dissolved on 10 July 1992 and the Commissioner has been unable to recover any of the income tax assessment of the company.

The auditor formed the view the company had paid to the applicant the sum of $197,671 in the year ended 30 June 1991 representing the applicant’s 50 per cent share of the company’s correct operating profit of $397,342 for that year. This amount was considered to be a deemed dividend under s. 108 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 and assessable income under sub-s. 44(1). An amended assessment was issued to the applicant on 30 June 1994.

The applicant objected to the amended assessment in a letter dated 15 April 1995 from her accountant in which it was claimed that 90 per cent of the proceeds of the cheques were paid in cash to outside workers. No records of the cash payments were kept.

On the basis of additional information, the total amount of the laundered cheques was revised from $406,855 to $340,806. The applicant’s objection was allowed in part, the deemed dividend reduced from $197,671 to $165,646 representing a distribution of the company’s profits being her 50 per cent share of the company’s operating profit for the year ended 30 June 1991 of $331,292. The applicant’s income tax assessment was therefore reduced by $33,025. In her income tax return for the year ending 30 June 1991, the applicant had returned salary and wages of $32,726 and claimed a non primary production loss of $14,271 returning a net taxable income of $18,455.

In a statement of Facts Issues and Contentions lodged with the Tribunal by the applicant’s solicitors it was stated:

  1. FACTS

...

  1. At all material times, the Applicant besides having a 50% shareholding in the Company was also employed by the Company as a sewer.
  2. At all material times Mr L had total control of the Company including but not limited to the administrative and managerial aspects of the Company.
  3. At all material times the Applicant had no participation in the day to day decision making of the Company in relation to administration and management, or at all.
  4. At all material times the Applicant’s involvement with the Company on a day to day basis was as an employee and as such acted solely in accordance with the directions and instructions given to her by Mr L.
  5. But for salary and wages disclosed in her income tax return for the year of income ended 30 June 1991 (‘the 1991 year’), the Applicant received no other assessable income for the purposes of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (‘the Act’) either directly or indirectly from the Company for the 1991 year.
  6. During the 1991 year the Applicant did not receive directly or indirectly any advances or loans from the Company.
  7. The Respondent has asserted that the Company failed to include in its assessable income for the 1991 year $406,855 referrable to the value of ‘laundered cheques’ in relation to which it, the Company allegedly received cash (‘the laundered cheques’), and accordingly deemed a dividend of $165,646 in relation thereto to the Applicant pursuant to sections 44(1) and 108 of the Act.

...

CONTENTIONS

  1. During the 1991 year the Applicant did not receive a dividend, a loan or an advance from the Company.

...

  1. During the 1991 year the only income ever derived by the Applicant which is assessable to the Applicant under the Act in relation to the Company is the salary and wages of $32,726 paid to the Applicant by the Company out of which total tax instalments of $9184.10 were deducted by the Company.
  2. The whole or alternatively part of the cash value of the laundered cheques is not properly assessable as income of the Company under the Act.
  3. To the extent that any part or the whole of the cash value of the laundered cheques is properly assessable as income of the Company under the Act (which is denied), the company is properly entitled to a deduction or deductions under the Act in relation thereto.
  4. In all the circumstances the Respondent is not correct in deeming that the Applicant received a dividend from the Company pursuant to sections 44(1) and 108 of the Act.

The respondent’s contentions were as follows:

CONTENTIONS

The taxable income of the Applicant is $184,101 and accordingly the assessment is not excessive.

M Pty Ltd paid or credited the sum of $165,646 or some other amount on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of the Applicant.

The amount of $165,646 or some other amount which was paid to or credited on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of the Applicant represented a distribution of profits of M Pty Ltd which is deemed a dividend paid by M Pty Ltd to the Applicant and therefore assessable income of the Applicant.

The Respondent correctly imposed by way of penalty, additional tax equal to 40% of the tax avoided and the Applicant is not entitled to any further remission pursuant to section 227(3) of the Act.

Sub-section 108(1) of the Act provides:

“108(1) If a private company:

(a) pays an amount to an associated person by way of an advance or loan; or

(b) pays or credits an amount on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of, an associated person;

so much (if any) of the amount paid or credited as, in the opinion of the Commissioner, represents a distribution of profits shall, for the purposes of this Act other than Division 11A of Part III and Division 4 of Part VI, be deemed to be a dividend paid by the company:

(c) to the associated person as a shareholder in the company;

(d) out of profits derived by the company; and

(e) on the last day of the year of income of the company in which the payment or credit referred to in paragraph (a) or (b) is made.”

Sub-section 44(1) provides:

“44(1) The assessable income of a shareholder in a company (whether the company is a resident or a non-resident) shall, subject to this section and to section 128D-

(a) if he is a resident - include dividends paid to him by the company out of profits derived by it from any source; and

(b) if he is non-resident - include dividends paid to him by the company to the extent to which they are paid out of profits derived by it from sources in Australia.”

Mr Rosenbaum, counsel for the applicant conceded that the amount of the cheques identified by the Commissioner were cashed but disputed that the amount assessed was assessable income of the applicant. His principal submissions were directed to issues of whether s. 108 applies and if it does what is the appropriate amount of the deemed dividend and to the further issue of penalty.

Mr Rosenbaum primarily submitted that s. 108 cannot be invoked. He submitted the criteria for the application of s. 108 have not been satisfied. There was no evidence before the Commissioner of an advance, loan or payment to the applicant. It was insufficient to rely on the fact cheques were drawn in favour of the company. There was no evidence the applicant controlled the account into which cheques were paid.

Ms Jasper, an accountant and taxation auditor employed by the Australian Taxation Office since 1988 gave evidence at the request of the applicant’s advisers.

Ms Jasper said she reviewed the audit undertaken by another officer of the Australian Taxation Office, Mr Anthony. In doing so she had a copy of Mr Anthony’s report, his working papers, including notes of interviews with the applicant, statements obtained from drawers of cheques, a list of cheques paid to the company, an extract from the Australian Taxation Office data base recording the accounts into which cheques were paid and spreadsheets, one detailing all cheques in the company bank account, the other all laundered cheques.

As a result of her review the adjustment was made (as detailed at T3) reducing the gross amount represented by laundered cheques from $406,855 to $340,806.

The applicant did not give evidence. The Tribunal was informed that shortly prior to this hearing the applicant pleaded guilty in the County Court, to defrauding the Commonwealth contrary to s. 29D of the Crimes Act 1914 by not declaring to the Australian Taxation Office all assessable income derived by her from the business activities of the company for the year of income 1 July 1990 to 30 June 1991.

The amounts of the cashed cheques are not in dispute. The audit trail stops with the cashing of the cheques and there is no evidence of what happened beyond that. The Commissioner has drawn certain inferences from the audit investigation. What is known is that the amount of the cheques were unaccounted for by the company; that the applicant has admitted her involvement in the laundering of the company cheques, although to a minor degree. Her accountant in his letter of objection (T7) refers to the applicant laundering cheques “several times” and also implicates her co-director Mr L in the scheme. No books or records of the company were produced and there is no evidence where the money went.

We think it is clearly open to the Commissioner on the known facts to draw an inference that the applicant (as a director and as a 50 per cent shareholder of the company) received 50 per cent of the moneys that were unaccounted for by the company and that such amount represents a dividend to her out of the profits of the company. The fact the applicant’s return included salary and wages is not in the circumstances conclusive of anything else.

The applicant carries an onus of proof: sub-s. 14ZZK(b) of the Taxation Administration Act 1963. We are satisfied she has not discharged that onus. There has not been put to the Tribunal any facts which might suggest that the inferences drawn from the known facts were not open to be made.

In Martin v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 93 ATC 5200 Davies J. after referring to the judgment of Brennan J. in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Dalco 90 ATC 4088 (at 4090-4091) said at 5203:

“It follows that the task of the Court on an appeal from a decision on an objection is to determine whether the taxpayer has satisfied the burden of proof of showing that the amount of taxable income assessed is excessive, a burden which the taxpayer cannot dispel merely by showing that there was an error in the assessment. The taxpayer must go on to establish what is the amount which should be substituted therefor.”

On the evidence and material before the Tribunal, the applicant has not provided any evidence which would permit a finding to be made that the amended assessment is excessive or what should be the correct amount.

Specifically on the question of payments to outside workers we note that labour costs claimed by the company for outworkers, $171,238 were allowed as a deduction in the company return. There is nothing before us to suggest this figure is too little.

Turning to the question of penalties the Commissioner imposed an additional tax penalty on the applicant of 40 per cent. Reference was made by Mr Rosenbaum to a decision of the Tribunal (Mr Pascoe Senior Member) in VT96/30-32 delivered 28 November 1996, AAT Decision No. 11,437, where the penalty upon individual applicants’ was reduced from 75 per cent to 25 per cent. He submitted a similar percentage decrease should be made in the case of the present applicant.

Case VT96/30-32 concerned three applications for review, one by a company and by two individuals. The Tribunal in that case did not disturb the additional tax penalty imposed on the company of 75 per cent but on the individual applicants pointing out the practical effect of the application of s. 108 on the same amount of income.

There is a significant difference in the present case where the company has been dissolved and there is no prospect of recovery of the company tax liability for the year ended 30 June 1991.

We are unable to detect any grounds pursuant to s. 227(3) of the Act to justify a remission of additional tax below 40 per cent.

In saying that we have noted that the culpability component was reduced from 50 per cent to 40 per cent on the basis of certain admissions made by the applicant and as an incentive for prompt payment.

The decision under review is affirmed.
