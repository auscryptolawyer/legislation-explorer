Gwynn-Jones and Commissioner for Superannuation [1978] AATA 31 (18 April 1978)

Last Updated: 14 March 2007

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No. 77/115

Re: BRIAN GWYNN-JONES

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

AAT Decision No 70

DECISION

The Tribunal affirms the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation made on 18 July 1977 varying and confirming the decision taken on his behalf on 18 February 1977.

Dated this eighteenth day of April 1978.

K.S. Edmunds (Senior Member)

R.B. Hutchison (Member)

W.J. Smith (Member)

REASONS FOR DECISION

This is an application to the Tribunal by Mr Brian Gwynn-Jones to reconsider a decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation relating to the level of contributions properly payable by the Applicant following his transfer and consequential reduction in salary from the position of Journalist Grade Al with the Australian Wool Board to the position of Journalist Grade A with the Department of Interior. The transfer took effect on 2 February, 1965, and the contribution effected by the decision are contributions that were payable under the Superannuation Act 1922-1963 ("the superseded Act").

The application has been made under S 154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 (the present Act) and covers a decision made by the Commissioner in the exercise of his powers under the superseded Act.

The relevant facts are that the Applicant became an employee for the purposes of the superseded Act on 26 November, 1953, and became a contributor to the Superannuation Fund established under that Act on that date. Immediately prior to 29 January, 1965, he was employed by the Australian Wool Board as a Journalist Grade Al, but on that date he ceased his employment with the Wool Board and on 2 February, 1965, without any break in his rights to contribute to the Fund, he commenced employment with the Department of the Interior as a Journalist Grade A. The change in his employment status from Grade Al to Grade A resulted in a reduction of salary. As the Applicant had not made an election under s22 of the superseded Act at the time of his transfer, his contributions were continued at the rate payable for the higher salary he had been receiving as a Journalist Grade A1.

For the purposes of the application of Section 22, reference is made to salary groups and from time to time the group salary range of a Journalist Grade Al was increased and at these times the Applicant's contributions were also varied to accord with the contributions payable under the corresponding increased salary of a.Grade A1. From 6 July, 1967, the salary which he had been receiving on 29 January, 1965, as a Grade A1 had been exceeded by the salary payable to him as a Grade A.

In December, 1975, the Applicant was transferred to the Department of Health and on 12 August, 1976, the Department wrote to the Commissioner and sought advice whether the Applicant's superannuation contributions were at the correct level. The commissioner replied by letter dated 18 February, 1977, reading as follows:-

"I refer to your letter of 16 December 1976 concerning Mr Gwynn-Jones and wish to advise that no provision exists in the Superannuation Act whereby the salary applicable to a Journalist Al can be regarded as Mr Gwynn-Jones' salary for superannuation purposes.

2. Section 20B of the Superannuation Act 1922 reads as follows:

(1) "Where the number of units of pension for which an employee is required to contribute, would but for this section, be reduced by reason of a reduction in his salary or in the prescribed amount, the employee shall, subject to the next succeeding sub-section, continue to contribute for the number of units for which he was a contributor immediately before the reduction.

(2) An employee to whom the last preceding sub-section applies may, subject to section twenty-two of this Act, elect to reduce the number of units for which he is required to contribute to a number not less than the number of units ascertained in accordance with ... sub-section (2) of section twenty of this Act, as it applies to him at the time of the election."

3. In Mr Gwynn-Jones' case when he transferred to the Department of the Interior on 2 February 1965 as a Journalist Grade A he was, in the absence of an election under the provision of Section 20B(2), eligible to continue contributing for the number of units applicable to the salary of Journalist Al at that time. However eligibility to contribute for additional units would not occur until the salary of a Journalist Grade A exceeded that of Journalist Al at the date of reduction in salary, and from that point Mr Gwynn-Jones' entitlements should be based on salary of Journalist Grade A."

The information in this correspondence was forwarded to Mr Gwynn-Jones and he, on 16 March, 1977, applied to the Commissioner to reconsider his decision. The Commissioner reconsidered his decision and Mr Gwynn-Jones was informed of the final decision in a letter dated 28 July forwarded to him from the office of the Commissioner.

The decision and reasons of the Commissioner are as follows:

"I, Robert Clyde DAVEY, Commissioner for Superannuation, acting pursuant to sub-section 154(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 VARY the decision taken on my behalf and set out at paragraph 3 of the memorandum addressed to the Director-General of Health and dated 18 February 1977 (folio 26) by changing the reference to "section 20B(2)" to refer to "section 22 of the Superannuation Act 1922-1963" which was the provision of the Act which applied to Mr Brian Gwynn Jones following his reduction in salary on 2 February 1965; AND in all other respects CONFIRM that decision.

2. My reasons for this decision are:
(i) In the absence of an election by Mr Brian Gwynn Jones, section 22 of the Superannuation Act 1922-1963, as it applied to Mr Brian Gwynn Jones following his reduction in salary on 2 February 1965, allowed Mr Brian Gwynn Jones to pay contributions to the Superannuation Fund in respect of the number of units applicable to the salary of a Journalist Grade Al on 1 February 1965 until such time as the salary applicable to a Journalist Grade A exceeded that salary.
(ii) The Act does not provide me with the power or the discretion to permit Mr Brian Gwynn Jones to pay contributions to the Superannuation Fund in respect of the number of units applicable to the salary of a Journalist Grade Al as was payable from time to time on and from 2 February 1965.
I also note that there is no provision in the Superannuation Act 1976 under which Mr Brian Gwynn Jones is permitted to pay contributions under either sections 46 or 47 of the Act on the basis of a rate of salary higher than his actual rate of salary as Journalist Grade A.

3. In reaching this decision I have also had regard to the reasoning in paragraphs 19 and following of the reconsideration submission of 18 July 1977."

Subsequently Mr Gwynn-Jones applied for and was granted a stay of the operation of the Commissioner's decision until the application to the Tribunal had been determined.

The grounds shown in the application to the Tribunal are:

"The decision is in conflict with written and verbal undertakings and actions of the Commonwealth on and after 2 February 1965. The decision has seriously disadvantaged me and my family and has resulted in a loss of income and superannuation benefits.

Following a request on 16 March for reconsideration of the matter, the Commissioner for Superannuation confirmed his original decision on 26 July 1977." Before the Tribunal the Applicant gave some details of the loss the Commissioner's decision, if implemented, would cause him. He produced pamphlets issued by the Superannuation Board explaining the operation of s22 of the superseded Act and also some documents prepared by staff members of the Departments in which he had worked which he claimed supported his submission that he was obliged to continue contributing for units of pension based on a Grade Al salary.

Because of the view we take of the legal effect of s22 of the superseded Act, of the obligation of a contributor under that Act and under the Superannuation Act 1976 to make contributions based upon salary received and the absence of any discretion in the Commissioner to permit contributions by the Applicant on any other basis, we consider no purpose would be served in the ultimate determination of this matter by the Tribunal in referring further to this material.

It is our opinion that if s22 is construed as permitting contribution by a contributor whose salary has been reduced, to be continued on the basis of the amount applicable to the salary received at the time of or just prior to the salary reduction, then it applies only while he is receiving a salary less than that actually paid to him before the reduction. The salary group of a Journalist Grade Al or Grade A or indeed of any Public Service classified office in itself has no significance in either section 22 or indeed any other section in the superseded Act or in the present Act.

Although s22 was not without its problems, of which its subsequent repeal and replacement by s20B is some evidence, bearing in mind the scheme of the superseded Act and of the present Act and the relation between salary at any given time, and the units of pensions to which a contributor was entitled and the amount of contribution payable, therefore s22 cannot properly be interpreted to justify any level of contribution greater than that relating to the salary paid to the Applicant at or just prior to his transfer on 29 January, 1965. Once the salary received by the Applicant as a Journalist Grade A had, by adjustments of whatsoever kind, advanced beyond this salary the section had no further application and his contributions must be governed by the general provision of the superseded Act or the present Act as the case may be.

For these reasons we consider that the decision under review should be confirmed.
