Re John Allan Steel and Commissioner of Superannuation [1981] AATA 8 (19 February 1981)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JOHN ALLAN STEEL

And: COMMISSIONER FOR

SUPERANNUATION

No. S80/12

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mr R.K. Todd (Senior Member)

Sir Ernest Coates (Member)

Mr I. Prowse (Member)

CATCHWORDS

superannuation _ election of contributor to contribute for additional units previously rejected _ effect of 1976 amending Act _ retrospective operation of s.2(6) and s.9(c) of the amending Act

Superannuation Amendment Act 1976 s.2(6), s.9(c)

Superannuation Act 1922 s.20A(3)

HEARING

CANBERRA

19:2:1981

ORDER

The Tribunal decides to affirm the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation conveyed to the applicant by letter dated 17 June 1976 and later confirmed by the Commissioner upon reconsideration.

DECISION

1. In this application the applicant seeks review of a decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation that he was not entitled to elect to contribute for a number of additional units of pension which he had previously rejected. It is common ground that upon retirement the applicant's entitlement to pension would be advantaged were his right so to elect acknowledged.

2. The problem arises in this way. Sub-sections (1), (2) and (3) of s.20A of the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the 1922 Act") provided:

'(1) If the salary of an employee who has attained the age of forty years is increased and, by reason of the increase, the employee would, but for this sub-section, be required to contribute for an additional unit or units of pension, he may, subject to section twenty-two H of this Act, not later than six months after the date upon which his salary is increased, elect not to contribute for all or any of the additional units attributable to the increase.

(2) If the prescribed amount is increased and, by reason of the increase, an employee who had attained the age of forty years before the date of the increase would, but for this sub-section, be required to contribute for an additional unit or units of pension, he may, subject to section twenty-two H of this Act, not later than six months after that date, elect not to contribute for all or any of the additional units attributable to the increase.

(3) Where an employee who has attained the age of forty years is a contributor for a number of units of pension less than the difference between _

(a) the number of units of pension ascertained in accordance with sub-section (2) of the last preceding section as it applies in relation to him; and

(b) the number of non-contributory units of pension, if any, applicable in relation to him,

he may elect to contribute for such number of additional units of pension as does not exceed the number by which that difference exceeds the number of units of pension for which he is a contributor.'

Sub-section (4) of the same section required the Superannuation Board which then administered the Act to be satisfied as to the medical condition of a person seeking to make an election pursuant to sub-section (3).

3. As part of the processes relating to the introduction of the new Superannuation Scheme provided for by the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the 1976 Act"), a number of statements were made and circulars issued. One was a circular sent out by the Superannuation Board to all Departments and Approved Authorities dated 2 February 1976 which stated, inter alia, that the Government had decided that superannuation contributions would be 'frozen' on and from 4 February 1976 at the level applying as at that date, but which also acknowledged (in paragraph 3(iv) thereof) that Staff Clerks could still 'make adjustments and report variations in contributions' in respect of 'elections to take up non-contributory units in respect of units effective before the commencement of the 'freeze' or in respect of previously rejected units'. A Ministerial statement, issued as a press release, dated 12 February 1980 set out a number of matters in relation to the introduction of the new scheme. It was stated, inter alia, that certain provisions in the 1922 Act would be suspended immediately pending introduction of the 1976 Act, including provisions that allow 'pension scheme contributors' to convert rejected units to contributory units. This statement was sent out under cover of a Superannuation Board Circular dated 13 February 1976. The Circular itself stated, inter alia, that:

'It will be noted from the press release that the following options available under the Superannuation Act 1922-1974 will no longer apply on or after 13 February 1976 _

...

Election under s.20A(3) to take up neglected (scil. rejected) units on a contributory basis.

...

Any election or application relating to the abovementioned sections which has been made before 13 February 1976 should be processed in the normal manner, however, staff clerks should not process any election or application made on or after that date.'

4. By a circular dated 10 February 1976 the Department within which the applicant was employed sent out an extract from the Superannuation Board circular of 2 February, but this document did not come to the notice of the applicant until 18 February. He thereafter wrote various letters and made various representations, but the most important of these was a letter dated 6 May 1976 addressed to the Secretary, AGRBO (Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office). In substance, he complained therein of a lack of information about the proposals for a new Superannuation Scheme and about the reduced pension benefit that he was likely to receive if he could not elect to take up the non-contributory units. He said that it was his opinion that he had been grossly uninformed and would be unjustly and unfairly penalised. Accordingly, he said, 'I hereby request permission to take up my previously rejected units'. This letter appears to have been received by the AGRBO on 10 May 1976 (a Monday). In the meantime, on 7 May 1976, assent had been given to the Superannuation Amendment Act 1976 ("the Amendment Act") which, by s.9(c) thereof, amended s.20A of the 1922 Act by adding at the end of s.20A(3) the words: '...but a person is not entitled to make an election under this sub-section after 12 February 1976'.

Section 2(6) of the same Act provided:

'2(6) Paragraph 9(c) and sections 12 and 46 shall be deemed to have come into operation on 13 February 1976'.

5. There is certainly room for doubt as to whether the letter of 6 May constituted an election as required by s.20A(3). We are, however, not aware of there being any prescribed form for making an election, although an official form described as a Form SB8 was available for the purpose, and for the purposes of the argument it may be assumed that the letter of 6 May did constitute a purported election. The next difficulty is that the letter of 6 May did not reach the AGRBO until 10 May 1980, being after the commencement of the Superannuation Amendment Act 1976. We should have thought that an election could not be regarded as effective unless somehow communicated to the AGRBO or the Superannuation Board. Despite these difficulties, we shall for the purposes of discussion assume that the letter of 6 May constituted an election and that it was communicated to the Superannuation Board on that day. Normally, an election would have been made on a Form adopted by the Board, but not apparently prescribed by Regulations, which would have been handed to the staff clerk or equivalent officer in the person's personnel section.

6. Having made these assumptions, the question is whether the effect of the Superannuation Amendment Act 1976 was to put an end to any right to make an election pursuant to s.20A(3) of the 1922 Act once 12 February 1976 had passed. We consider that there is no doubt that it was. If the words added to s.20A(3) had stood alone, then given that the Amendment Act did not receive the Royal Assent until 7 May 1976, it might have been possible to contend that an election in fact made prior to that date would have to stand since at the date when it was made there was no legislative bar to it being made and accepted. Indeed s.20A(3) would have still permitted it, notwithstanding the various administrative pronouncements that had been made and which have been set out above. But the provisions of s.2(6) of the Amendment Act make it clear that the provisions of s.9(c) of the Amendment Act, which added the words referred to in s.20A(3) of the 1922 Act, were to be deemed to have come into operation on 13 February 1976. We consider that the effect of this provision is to place a retrospective ban upon the legal effectiveness of any election purporting to have been made after 12 February 1976: See R. v. Kidman [1915] HCA 58; (1915) 20 CLR 425; Millner v. Raith [1942] HCA 21; (1942) 66 CLR 1.

7. It follows that in terms of this legislation it was not open to the Commissioner to accept the election purported to have been made by the applicant in his letter of 6 May 1976, even making the assumptions that we have been prepared to make, viz that the letter constituted such an election and that it had been made before 7 May 1976.

8. The applicant contended that on 4 February 1976 the Superannuation Board, by depriving contributors of their rights under the Superannuation Act 1922 acted without legislative authority; that what he described as 'monetary penalties' subsequently retrospectively imposed were unjustly harsh and severe, having the effect of excluding him from obtaining the optimum benefits of the new Superannuation Scheme; that the hardship which he saw as already imposed was exacerbated by the provisions of the Commonwealth Employees (Redeployment and Retirement) Act 1979; that the Superannuation Fund created by the Superannuation Act 1922 constituted a contract between the Commonwealth as an employer and its employees which was breached by the administrative action taken by the Superannuation Board in its circulars; and that contributions to the Superannuation Fund are a form of taxation.

9. The contentions referred to in paragraph 8 contain assertions of fact and of law. We do not wish in any way to suggest that we agree with any of them, but we do say that even if each of them was entirely accepted by the Tribunal they would have to bow to the effect of the amendments retrospectively made by the Superannuation Amendment Act 1976. Not surprisingly, the applicant was quite unable to suggest any way in which the consequences of the amendments could be overcome. In all the circumstances, we find it unnecessary to canvass the various contentions which he put forward.

10. The Tribunal's decision will be that the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation conveyed to the applicant by letter dated 17 June 1976 that he could not then elect under s.20A(3) of the Superannuation Act 1922 to take up any rejected units be affirmed.
