McLinden and Acting Commissioner for Superannuation [1979] AATA 82 (28 June 1979)

Last Updated: 12 March 2007

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Jurisdiction - s154 Superannuation Act 1976 -what is a "reviewable decision"? - Regulations No 282 of 1978 regs 4 to 6 - officer simultaneously a delegate of Commissioner pursuant to s.25(1) of Superannuation Act 1976 and authorised to exercise the powers under reg 6 - whether decision made pursuant to reg 6 reviewable - circumstances when certificate pursuant to reg 6 would be properly given considered.

DECISION AND REASONS

Re: MERVYN FELIX McLINDIN

And: ACTING COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. 78/12017

AAT Decision No 215

Davies, J., R.B. Hutchison, W.J. Smith

Date: 28 June 1979.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No.78/12017

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re : MERVYN FELIX McLINDIN

And : ACTING COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

DECISION

The application to review the decision made on 30 May 1978 by the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation is refused.

DATED this 28th day of June 1979.

(J.D. Davies, J.)

(R.B. HUTCHISON)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No.78/12017

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re : MERVYN FELIX McLINDIN

And : ACTING COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

This is an application to review a decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation made on 30 May 1978 confirming a decision that an additional duties allowance not be taken into account in calculating the salary at the time of his retirement of the applicant, Mervyn Felix McLindin.

Section 5(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 defines salary to include :

"(a) any allowance, or the value of any allowance, or any fee, that is an allowance or fee of a kind that, under the regulations, is to be treated as salary for the purposes of this Act;".

When this application first came on for hearing, no regulations had been proclaimed providing that a higher duties allowance may be taken into account as salary. However, s168 of the 1976 Act and s70 of the Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978 authorised the making of retrospective regulations. Anticipating that such regulations would be made, the Commissioner for Superannuation, by circular dated 12 May 1976, stated the circumstances in which a higher duties allowance would be taken into account as part of salary. The circular described its purpose as follows:

"The purpose of this Circular Memorandum is to request Departments and Approved Authorities to nominate a position within their Organisation, the occupant of which can act as a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation in:-

(i) .....

(ii) declaring that contributors to the new Superannuation Scheme will be in receipt of higher duties allowance for a likely period of not less than 12 months so that the higher duties allowance can be regarded as salary for superannuation purposes.",

and stated :

"6. Under the new scheme, where a contributor is in receipt of Higher Duties Allowance and the Commissioner or his delegate is satisfied that the allowance is likely to continue for a minimum period of 12 months, the allowance may be regarded as salary for superannuation purposes. If on the commencement of the new scheme a contributor has already been on continuous higher duties allowance, that prior period may be taken into account in determining the period of 12 months. Before any higher duties allowance may be regarded as salary however, a declaration to the effect that the allowance has continued or is likely to continue for at least 12 months will need to be completed by the delegate of the Commissioner.".

To implement the scheme, a circular memorandum giving approval of appointment of delegates purported to give additional authority to each delegate in the following terms:

"3. Approval has also been given for the occupant of the above position or any person for the time being occupying that position to declare, for the purpose of determining salary for superannuation purposes, that an eligible employee has been in receipt of higher duties allowance for a continuous period of not less than 12 months or that an eligible employee is in receipt of higher duties allowance and is likely to continue to receive higher duties allowance for a total continuous period of at least 12 months.".

Nevertheless, when the application first came on for hearing, the regulations had not been proclaimed. Under the law as it then stood, the applicant was not entitled to have a higher duties allowance taken into account as part of his salary. Accordingly, the Commissioner's representative suggested that the proceedings be adjourned until the proposed regulations were proclaimed. With the applicant's consent, the Tribunal adjourned the hearing of the application.

Regulations No 282 of 1978 were proclaimed on 22 December 1978. Regulation 2 provided that the Regulations be deemed to have taken effect from and including 1 July 1976. Accordingly, there are now regulations on which this Tribunal may act which set out the circumstances in which a higher duties allowance may be taken into account as part of a salary for the purposes of s5(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976.

The relevant provisions of the Regulations read as follows:

"4. In this Part, a reference to an allowance of a prescribed kind shall be read as a reference to an allowance of one of the following kinds:
(a) an allowance that is payable to an eligible employee by reason that he temporarily performs the whole or a part of the duties of an office of a higher classification than the classification of he office occupied by him;

...........
5. Each of the following kinds of allowance is a kind of allowance that is to be treated as salary for the purposes of the Act:

.............

(1) an allowance of a prescribed kind that is payable on a regular basis to an eligible employee.
6.(1) An allowance of a prescribed kind shall not, for the purposes of paragraph 5(1), be taken to be payable to an eligible employee on a regular basis unless the allowance is payable in respect of duties or work performed by the eligible employee for a continuous period that commences -
(a) .........

(b) where a certificate in respect of the eligible employee has been given under sub-regulation (2) - on the day specified in the certificate.
(2) If a person authorized by the Commissioner for the purposes of this regulation is of the opinion that there is a likelihood that, from and including a particular day –
(a) an eligible employee will, for a continuous period of not less than 12 months, perform duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of a prescribed kind will be payable to him;

.............
the authorized person shall give to the Commissioner a certificate to that effect specifying the day (not being a day earlier than the day on which the certificate is given) on which the period referred to in paragraph (a) or (b), as the case may be, in relation to the eligible employee will commence.".

We accept, for the purposes of these reasons, that the delegates who were authorised in the manner described above to give certificates in relation to a higher duties allowance, were persons "authorized by the Commissioner for the purposes of" reg 6. However, in the present instance, no such certificate of a person authorised by the Commissioner has been given. It necessarily follows that neither the Commissioner nor this Tribunal may take the higher duties allowance into account as part of the applicant's salary at the time of his retirement. The provisions of reg 6 have not been satisfied.

In our opinion, this Tribunal is not authorised to review a decision of a person, authorised to act under reg 6, who has refused to grant a certificate or has granted a certificate which is not satisfactory to the claimant. The section giving authority to the Tribunal to review a decision is

s154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 which reads, inter alia:

"154.(1) In this section –

'decision' has the same meaning as in the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975;

'reviewable decision' means a decision of the Commissioner, or a delegate of the Commissioner, under this Act, under the superseded Act or under the regulations made under either of those Acts, and includes a decision of the Superannuation Board, or a delegate of the Superannuation Board (other than a decision under section 141 of the superseded Act);

'Superannuation Board' means the Superannuation Board established by the superseded Act.

(2) A person affected by a reviewable decision who is dissatisfied with the decision may, by notice in writing given to the Commissioner within the period of 30 days after the day on which the decision first comes to the notice of the person, or within such further period as the Commissioner allows, request the Commissioner to reconsider the decision.

.......................

(4) Upon receipt of the request, the Commissioner shall reconsider the decision and may confirm or revoke the decision or vary the decision in such manner as he thinks fit.

...........................

(6) Applications may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of reviewable decisions that have been confirmed or varied under sub-section (4) and for review of decisions of the Superannuation Board, or delegates of the Superannuation Board, made under section 141 of the superseded Act.".

These provisions empower the Tribunal to review a decision of the Commissioner in which the Commissioner has reconsidered a decision of his own or of one of his delegates. Section 25(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 provides for delegation as follows:

"25.(1) The Commissioner may, either generally or as otherwise provided by the instrument of delegation, by writing signed by him, delegate to a person any of his powers under this Act or the superseded Act, other than this power of delegation.".

A person who acts as delegate exercises the powers of the Commissioner in relation to the matters to which the delegation extends. Although the Commissioner has authorised all the persons to whom he has delegated power under s25 of the Superannuation Act 1976 also to exercise the powers conferred by reg 6, the fact that a person so authorised is also a delegate does not make his decision under reg 6 a decision of a delegate for the purposes of s154. The person who considered the applicant's claim was a person to whom powers had been delegated under s25 but, when considering the applicant's claim, he was not exercising a power so delegated but the authority conferred on him pursuant to reg 6 to consider and, if proper to do so, to grant the higher duties certificate. Moreover, the decision not to grant to the applicant a higher duties certificate was not a decision of the Commissioner himself. Regulation 6 does not confer authority upon the Commissioner himself to grant the certificate but empowers him to authorise a person to form the specific opinion and to grant the certificate. Thus, the decision not to grant the certificate was not a "decision of the Commissioner" for the purposes of s154 and was not a decision subject to the review procedures laid down in ss(4) and (6) of that section. It follows that the subject decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation was not a decision under s154(4) and this Tribunal has no authority to review it. For this reason, the applicant's application for review must be refused.

However, it may be useful if we make some comment upon the merits of the application. On the facts established before the Tribunal, when the applicant took up his new position on 2 August 1976, in respect of which he was entitled to a higher duties allowance, he expected to remain in that post, either by virtue of his acting in that position during the absence of the permanent holder of the post or by virtue of an anticipated subsequent permanent appointment to the post, for at least twelve months after the date of his taking up duties. Moreover, the person who had general control of the employees in that post and who was the applicant's superior also expected the applicant to remain in the post for at least twelve months. Neither person expected the permanent holder of the post, who was on sick leave, to return. Both expected that the permanent holder of the post would remain on leave for at least twelve months and both expected that, if perchance a vacancy occurred during the twelve months, the applicant probably would be appointed permanently to the post.

On these facts, if a decision had been made on 2 August 1976, or as at 2 August 1976, the conclusion should then have been drawn that the applicant would hold the post for a continuous period of at least twelve months. But such conclusion would not of itself have determined the matter in the applicant's favour.

First, the representative for the Commissioner submitted in argument that a decision should not on or as at 2 August 1976 have been made that the applicant would, for a continuous period of twelve months, perform work in respect of which the allowance would be payable to him if it had then been anticipated that, during the twelve months' period, he would be appointed permanently to the post. We express no view on that argument. Secondly, the Regulations do not require that the certificate be given on the date of the officer's appointment to the acting post or that the facts be looked at as at that date. Rather, reg 6 requires that the facts be examined as at the time when the certificate is given and requires that the certificate shall specify a date, not being a day earlier than the day on which the certificate is given, from which date the period of twelve months will commence. The applicant commenced his acting duties on 2 August 1976. He had been appointed to the post hurriedly and the facts do not suggest that it was the duty of any person to consider on 2 August 1976 the issue to the applicant of a higher duties certificate. In any event, had the matter come up for consideration on that day, we think that the authorised person would not then have issued his certificate. The permanent occupant of the post had been on a month's sick leave expiring on 31 July 1976. The person having general control of the section had been advised orally by the Commonwealth Medical Officer that he did not expect that the permanent holder of the office would return to employment. But no medical certificate to that effect had issued. The permanent holder of the position was again examined by the Commonwealth Medical Officer on 4 August 1976 and, on that day, a medical report issued stating that it was unlikely that the permanent holder of the position would return to his post. That report was received by the person in charge of the section on 6 August 1976. In our view, it is unlikely that a person authorised to give a certificate under reg 6 would have been prepared to give a higher duties certificate to the applicant until he had received that report on or about 6 August. In the meantime, the applicant himself had gone on sick leave. After working on 2 and 3 August, he saw a medical practitioner on the evening of 3 August and, on 4 August, went on three months' sick leave. When the granting of the higher duties certificate thereafter came up for consideration, the certificate could not be granted. After the applicant had gone on sick leave, a certificate could not have been given that it was likely that he would thereafter, for a continuous period of not less than twelve months, as calculated by sub-regs (2) and (3) of reg 6, perform duties or work in respect of the performance of which the higher duties allowance would be payable to him.

For these several reasons, the application for review must fail and the application is refused. We do not confirm the decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation for, in our view, his decision confirming the decision not to grant to the applicant the certificate for the higher duties allowance was not a decision subject to review by this Tribunal. The fact that the Superannuation Act and all regulations made under it did not come into simultaneous existence and effect as from 1 July 1976 has obviously caused practical problems. It can happen, as it has in this case, that what were regarded as practical administrative steps to deal with the multitude of circumstances which arose in the transition from the old scheme to the new one come under challenge.. However, the Tribunal has no option, despite any personal and administrative difficulties which might follow, but to interpret the legislation in accordance with the normal rules of statutory interpretation.
