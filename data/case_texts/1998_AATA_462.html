DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No QT97/264

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re McNEVIN CESSNOCK PTY LTD

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION

Tribunal Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date 21 May 1998

Place Brisbane

Decision The Tribunal decides that the objection decisions relating to the superannuation guarantee charge are affirmed.



Decision No 12911 (Sgd) K L Beddoe

(Senior Member)



CATCHWORDS

TAXATION - Superannuation Guarantee Charge - shortfall in payment of superannuation payments by employer - failure to lodge statements - Motel Industry Award - inconsistency between Award and legislation - charge imposed.



Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 ss 18, 19, 20, 23, 33, 49, 59, 62

Taxation (Interest on Overpayments) Act 1983 s 10(1)(b)

Taxation Administration Act 1953 s 14ZZC

REASONS FOR DECISION

21 May 1998 Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



  1. The applicant seeks review of an objection decision notified by the respondent on 21 July 1997. The respondent disallowed the objection against an assessment of superannuation guarantee charge for the years ended 30 June 1993, 30 June 1994 and 30 June 1995.
  2. At the hearing Mr McNevin represented and gave evidence for the applicant and Ms Oliver represented the respondent. An officer of the Australian Taxation Office, Ms Angela Day, also gave oral evidence.
  3. Section 16 of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (“the Act”) provides that a superannuation guarantee charge imposed on an employer’s superannuation guarantee shortfall for a year is payable by an employer.
  4. A superannuation guarantee shortfall for the year ended 30 June 1993 is the sum of the employer’s half yearly shortfalls in respect of an employee for the year (s 18(1)). The formula for calculating the shortfall is provided for in s 18(2) and depends upon the “charge percentage for the employer for the half year” ascertained after the application of ss 20 to 23 of the Act as the case may be.
  5. In this case s 18 operates to apply a 3% charge percentage for the two half years in the year ended 30 June 1993. The calculations relied on by the respondent have not been disputed by the applicant.
  6. Crucial to this case, however, is the formula in s 18 which applies as follows:
Total salary or wages paid by the employer x 3

to the employee for the half year 100


  1. The shortfall has been calculated on a basis which includes all salary and wages paid to an employee. The applicant says it cannot make superannuation payments to the industry fund in accordance with the Award until eligibility for admission to the fund has been determined. Delay occurs because of “lead” times under the award, some employees are slow in making application to the fund and the fund has to determine eligibility. Similar provisions differing in detail apply for the years ended 30 June 1994 and 30 June 1995 (s 19).
  2. The applicant says it has complied with the terms of the industry award (the Motels and Resorts Award 1992 “the Award”) and that it should not be penalised because it has complied with the terms of the Award, albeit that the company failed to comply with the Act.
  3. Exhibit A is a copy of the Award. It is not necessary for the purpose of making a decision in this matter that I analyse the Award in relation to employer superannuation contributions. I note that Clause 56 of the Award prescribes that each employer shall contribute to the scheme of which the eligible full time, part time and casual employee is a member an amount equal to 3% of the “ordinary time earnings” where that employee earns $276.70 or more in a four week period.
  4. “Eligible Casual Employee” is defined by clause 56 of the Award to include an employee employed on a “casual basis at or after 1 February 1990 at completion of 8 weeks continuous service from date of employment”.
  5. It will be apparent therefore, as the applicant submits, that there is a conflict between the Act and the Award in relation to contributions for new casual employees. It is the time lag between commencing employment and date of eligibility to join the scheme that is the main cause of the shortfall in this case.
  6. Section 23(3) of the Act relevantly provides for variation of the charge percentage calculated under s 20 if in a contribution period:
    • (a) an employer is required by an occupational superannuation arrangement to contribute for the benefit of an employee to a superannuation fund; and
    • (b) the requisite contribution is a specified percentage of the employee’s earnings base; and
    • (c) the employer contributes to a complying superannuation fund for the benefit of the employee in accordance with the arrangement.

On a superficial view it might be thought that this provision would assist the applicant (he did not claim that it would). However, I am satisfied that the shortfall claimed in this case by the respondent is in respect of periods when the applicant was not required to make contributions for the benefit of its employees by the Award arrangement. I am not satisfied therefore that s 23 assists the applicant in this case.

  1. Similar but not identical provisions apply in respect of the years ended 30 June 1993 and 30 June 1994.
  2. There is nothing in the material before me to suggest that excluded wages have been taken into account in calculating the shortfall. In particular the applicant does not claim that wages paid to those employees who were paid less than $450.00 in a month have been incorrectly included in the calculation of the shortfall (s 27(2)).
  3. The applicant's real complaint in this matter is that the company has been penalised notwithstanding that it intended to and did comply with the provisions of the Award. It has a shortfall because the requirements of the Act are not consistent with the requirements of the Award. This inconsistency resulted in the company not making the minimum level of contributions for each employee in the respective contribution periods as required by s 23(2) of the Act. That failure resulted in the contributions shortfall. On the respondent's submission the shortfall in relation to lead times could have been avoided if the applicant lodged the amounts of contributions in question with the Australian Taxation Office for the periods when employees were not members of the superannuation fund.
  4. It is beyond argument, in my view, that the inconsistency between the Act and the Award must be resolved on the basis that the Act prevails. A fair reading of the Act shows that the Parliament did not express any intention that the Act was to be subject to requirements of industrial awards in its operation. Section 23 expresses the opposite intention, i.e., the charge will apply where contributions are made under an industrial award but those contributions could result in a “nil” charge after application of the formula.
  5. The Act imposes late payment of penalties of unpaid charges (s 49). The penalty payable by way of additional superannuation guarantee charge is payable at the rate prescribed for the purposes of s 10(1)(b) of the Taxation (Interest on Overpayments) Act 1983 subject to adjustment of the amount unpaid by deducting for the administration component and nominal interest component calculated in accordance with ss 31 and 32 of the Act (s 49(2)).
  6. That additional charge is imposed by the Act and on my reading of the Act is not subject to any power to remit it in whole or in part.
  7. A different situation pertains to penalties imposed by Part 7 of the Act. Section 62(3) confers on the respondent a general discretion to remit such penalties in whole or in part.
  8. Section 59(1) of the Act imposes additional superannuation guarantee charges equal to double the amount of superannuation guarantee charge payable by the employer for the year. Under s 59(1) the liability is imposed, inter alia, where an employer fails to provide a superannuation guarantee statement relevant to assessing the employer’s liability to pay superannuation guarantee charge for a year.
  9. Section 33(1) of the Act states that an employer who has a superannuation guarantee shortfall for a year (as determined by s 17) must lodge a superannuation guarantee statement for the year on or before:
(a) 14 August in the following year; or

(b) any later day allowed by the Commissioner.


Section 6(1) defines “year” to mean financial year which itself is not defined but I think is clearly intended to mean the 12 months ending on 30 June each year.

  1. In this case the applicant concedes it failed to provide a superannuation guarantee statement for each of the years ended 30 June 1993, 30 June 1994, and 30 June 1995. That failure occurred in the context that the company believed it had fulfilled its obligations by complying with the requirements of the Award. While it appears some minor errors may have occurred in seeking to comply with the Award I am satisfied on the explanations given to the Tribunal that those mistakes were relatively insignificant.
  2. While none of that in any way excuses compliance with the Act I am satisfied that the company thought it was complying with those obligations.
  3. Put at its highest the company failed to comply with the Act because those controlling the company failed to understand its obligations under the Act. In particular I am satisfied there was not a deliberate or reckless disregard of the company’s obligations. If its obligations under the Award had matched its obligations under the Act then the default would have been relatively minor.
  4. I am satisfied, and find, that the applicant failed to provide a superannuation guarantee statement as required by s 33(1) for each of the subject financial years. It follows that the Act imposes by way of penalty additional superannuation guarantee charges equal to double the amount of the superannuation guarantee charge payable by the applicant for each financial year.
  5. This is not a case of failure to keep records, refusal to produce, the making of false or misleading statements or an arrangement entered into to avoid the charge.
  6. Copies of the notices of the assessments in dispute are not before the Tribunal. However, Document T21 sets out the details of remission of the additional superannuation guarantee charge payable by the respondent. Those details may be summarised as follows:

Charge avoided 6,222.70 5,846.49 3,827.21

“Non lodger” (10%) 622.27 584.64 382.72

“Culpability” (25%) 1,555.67 1,461.62 955.80

“Recurring Offence” 292.32 382.72

  1. It will be apparent that the total charge avoided including nominal interest imposed by s 31 and administration charge for each year imposed by s 32 of the Act is $15,896.40. Of this the shortfall totalled $11,999.21, nominal interest $1,347.19 and administration charge $2,550.00.
  2. By letter dated 27 April 1997 (T22) the applicant objected against the decision to only remit portion of the additional charge imposed by the Act as set out in folios 77-79 of the T documents. By letters dated 27 August 1997 (T24) the respondent notified disallowance of those objections. The company’s application for review (lodged on 4 August 1997) in this Tribunal refers to objection decisions notified on 21 July 1997. The reasons for decision on the objections are not before the Tribunal although referred to in the application for review, nor are copies of assessments made and notified by the respondent in the material before the Tribunal. After considering the matter carefully I must accept the respondent's submission that the question of additional superannuation surcharge is not before the Tribunal. That submission was included in the respondent's written submission. The applicant was given leave to make a written submission in reply and did so in a letter lodged in the Tribunal on 11 December 1997.
  3. Section 14ZZC of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 applies in relation to a reviewable objection decision so that the prescribed time whereby an application for review must be lodged with the Tribunal is “within 60 days after the person making the application is served with the notice of the decision”. That 60 days period may be extended by the Tribunal upon application in writing by the applicant (s 29(7) Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975).
  4. The decisions on objections relating to the additional charge imposed by s 59 of the Act were notified on 27 August 1997 (T24). That is 23 days after the application for review was lodged in the Tribunal and 12 days after the Tribunal Registry advised the applicant's representative to the effect that no fee was payable on the application. A Deputy Registrar of the Tribunal issued a notice pursuant to s 29(11) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 on 20 August 1997. It is clear therefore that the application for review had been accepted as a valid application before the respondent notified the objection decisions on 27 August 1997. I am not satisfied that the application for review can be said to have been lodged within 60 days after service of the objection decision notices dated 27 August 1997.
  5. I am not satisfied that the applicant may have pre-empted these notices of objection decision because, as is asserted in the application for review, the notice of decision on objections dated 21 July 1997 (T1) only is referred to. That notice apparently had reasons for the decision attached but a copy of the document containing the reasons is not before the Tribunal. The respondent's s 37 statement (T2) makes it clear that the assessments of “Part 7 penalties” were notified (“issued”) on 4 April 1997, i.e., after the applicant's objection date 15 March 1997.
  6. As already indicated I need to be satisfied that the issue of the additional superannuation guarantee charge is properly before the Tribunal. On the material before me I cannot be so satisfied. It is, of course, open to the applicant to make an application for review of the objection decisions in relation to the Part 7 penalties as the applicant was advised to do in a directions hearing held on 15 October 1997 (paragraph 9.5 of respondent's submission).
  7. The objection decisions under review will be affirmed.

I certify that this and the eight (8) preceding pages are a true copy of the decision and reasons for decision herein of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)
