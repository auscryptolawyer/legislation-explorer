Frank Anthony Oreb and Commissioner of Superannuation [1997] AATA 211 (20 June 1997)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

FRANK ANTHONY OREB v. COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No.

Q95/431

AAT No. 11976

Number of pages - 7

Superannuation



COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

D.W. MULLER (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - productivity benefit - permanent incapacity - whether unlikely to ever be able to work again

Defence Act 1903: S.52(1)

The Defence Force (Superannuation)(Productivity Benefit) Determination Clause 6

HEARING

BRISBANE, 24 March 1997 (hearing), 20 June 1997 (decision)

20:6:1997

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr. Turnbull

Solicitor for the Applicant: Sarinas and Associates

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr. Dube, departmental advocate

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

D.W. MULLER

1. This is an application to review a decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation dated 7 November 1994 that Frank Anthony Oreb, the applicant, was not entitled to immediate receipt of a productivity benefit.

2. This matter was heard in Townsville on 24 March 1997. The applicant was represented by Mr. Turnbull of Counsel. The respondent was represented by Mr. Dube.

3. Section 52(1) of the Defence Act 1903 empowers the Minister to make determinations providing a superannuation productivity benefit in respect of continuous full-time service by members of the Defence Force who are contributors to the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits (DFRDB) Scheme.

4. The relevant Determination in this case is The Defence Force (Superannuation)(Productivity Benefit) Determination. In particular clause 6 reads (relevantly) as follows:

"6 (2) Where on a member's retirement, a productivity benefit is payable in respect of the person and: (a) the person: (i) ........; or (ii) has not reached the age of 55 years and is retired from the workforce on the ground of permanent incapacity or permanent invalidity; or (iii) ......; or (b) ...... the benefit is payable to the person at the time when the benefit becomes payable in respect of the person. (2A) A benefit is not payable to a person in the circumstances described in subparagraph (2)(a)(ii) unless the Commissioner is satisfied that the person is unlikely ever to be able to work again in employment for which the person is reasonably qualified by education, training or experience or for which the person could reasonably be qualified after retraining. (2B) In making a decision under subclause (2A), the Commissioner is to have regard to at least 2 certificates, each signed by a registered medical practitioner, giving the opinion of the practitioner of the likelihood of the person being ever able to work again in employment for which the person is reasonably qualified by education, training or experience or for which the person could reasonably be qualified after retraining."

5. (i) Mr. Oreb was born on 21 May 1962.

(i) Mr. Oreb entered the Defence Force on 6 July 1982.

(ii) Mr. Oreb was retired from the Defence Force as medically unfit on 27 May 1994.

(iii) The reason for Mr. Oreb's retirement from the Defence Force was described as "Low back pain - facet joint degeneration and osteoarthritis of lumbar spine".

6. On 22 April 1994, Mr. Oreb applied for early release of his productivity benefit.

7. Mr. Oreb's education, training and work history is as follows:

(i) He left school at the end of Year 10, Perth Modern School, Western Australia, in 1976.

(ii) Upon leaving school he became an apprenticed cabinetmaker in Perth.

(iii) He also worked as a cleaner in his spare time, assisting his father in his industrial cleaning business.

(iv) He then had labouring jobs such as truckies offsider and brickies labourer.

(v) He spent three months in the Army Reserve.

(vi) He joined the Defence Force as a recruit trainee on 6 July 1982.

(vii) Army courses done by Mr. Oreb (as collated by Ms. Nina Williams, rehabilitation/vocational consultant) include the following:

"Army Trade Qualified Courses included: * 31/03/92 Clerk Administrative; * 18/06/84 Rifleman Grade 2; * 18/12/82 Rifleman Grade 1;

Other Army Qualifications: * 06.05.91 (Pass) 2/91 Subject 2 for promotion to the rank of Sergeant Course; * 18/06/90 (Pass) 3/90 Subject 1 for promotion to the rank of Sergeant Course; * 09/04/89 (Pass) 3/86 Subject 3 for promotion to the rank of Sergeant Course; * 21/08/89 (Pass) 1/93 F88 Steyr Conversion; * 26/11/87 (Qualified) for Promotion to the rank of Corporal; * 21/10/85 (Pass) Reconnaissance and patrolling course

Course certificates sighted included: * 15/02/94 Certificate in Human & Physical Resource Supervision, Australian Army; * 15/02/94 Certificate in Personnel Administration, Australian Army; * 15/01/94 Certificate in Search and Rescue Techniques, Australian Army; * 26/11/993 Introduction to DOS 5.0 Defence Centre, Perth; * 17-18/11/93 Lotus 1-2-3- (intermediate) " " " * 29/09/93 Windows Familiarisation " " " * 02/07/93 Ausmis (Army Standard Manpower Information System) - database for data input/retrieval of Army personnel functions; * 17/02/81 Med/Nurse Assistant Course;"

(viii) Since leaving the Defence Force, Mr. Oreb has not been successful in finding suitable work. He has applied for numerous positions which he thought that he might be able to handle, eg. security, service station driveway attendant, manager of a cinema complex and clerk in the Army Reserve. He believes that he has a duty to prospective employers to mention to them his back problems and that this fact has prevented him from being accepted for work.

8. In his statement to the Tribunal Mr. Oreb described his back problems thus:

"I currently suffer from pain in my lower back. It feels like a knife is being twisted in my back. The pain is constant and is aggravated with activity. I have pain in both legs. Pain also radiates down both my legs. I also get occasional headaches. The pain prevents me from sitting or standing for long periods, I constantly have to adjust my position in the chair when sitting down to accommodate the pain. I have to get up and walk around. This annoys me and those that I am around as well because I am forever altering my position and adjusting myself to accommodate the pain. Lying in bed also causes numbness and a pins and needles sensation in my legs as well as increasing my lower back pain."

9. Mr. Oreb has been assessed by a number of medical practitioners and occupational therapists. The following excerpts from the various reports is a representative sample of the opinions expressed by the experts.

(i) Dr. G.R. Stokeld, 20 April 1994 "It is difficult to accurately predict his eventual level of disability but I would expect a gradual improvement to the point where he would be able to perform sedentary duties. This may take a year or more and I would anticipate his employment prospects would not be helped by his history of back pain."

(ii) Dr. David Lewis, orthopaedic surgeon, 18 July 1994 "Mr. Oreb is unable to work as a labourer, or trades assistant. He would be capable of part-time work as a clerk/administration officer, or security guard."

(iii) Amanda McDougall, occupational therapist, 20 June 1994. "Mr. Oreb has indicated an interest in obtaining a position within the field of security. I believe that his capacities would allow him to perform such a position, as assessment results indicate that he would be suitable for work that: - allows regular changes in posture from sitting to standing - does not involve constant or repetitive heavy lifting - involves walking short distances only all of which can be accommodated in a position such as a mobile security officer. Mr. Oreb should avoid tasks involving: - prolonged sitting - repetitive lifting, especially from floor level - walking long distances - excessive stair climbing - working with ladders I believe Mr. Oreb would also benefit from a Worksite Visit to assess workstation set up and seating and assistive equipment needs, when applicable."

(iv) Dr. David Lewis, orthopaedic surgeon, 6 June 1994 "X-rays: A CT scan shows a bulge at the Lumbosacral level, and a Bone Scan suggests that there is degenerate facet arthropothy at the L5-S1 level.

All in all I would consider that he's an L5-S1 Internal Disc Derangement, in the unstable phase.

Mr. Oreb has been sent for an assessment of his degree of disability, and in response to your questions:

Mr. Oreb suffers from an L5-S1 Internal Disc Derangement. This makes it difficult for him to sit or stand for prolonged periods, or bend or lift.

He has some difficulty with sleep, and would almost certainly be required to take painkillers. These could of course affect his judgment, depending on the level of affectiveness of the painkillers.

Given that the kind of employment in which he may be engaged is clerk/administration officer, security guard, labourer or trades assistant. In order his degree of disability is: clerk/administrator officer 30% to 60% or moderate disability security guard 30% to 60% or moderate labourer 60% to less than 100% or large trades assistant 60% to 100% or large The condition from which Mr. Oreb suffers usually stabilizes in two to five years, with four years being average. I suggest that a review of Mr. Oreb's disability be undertaken in some two years."

(v) Dr. Bruce Low, orthopaedic surgeon, 6 July 1995 "I do not believe Mr. Oreb is totally incapacitated for employment.

I believe he is partially incapacitated for employment. I believe he is capable of performing light skilled and semi-skilled and un-skilled work. I will go through his qualifications. He is a qualified cabinetmaker. I do not believe he will be able to perform that sort of work. He was a sergeant in the Army. He is qualified in man-management. If the man-management skills that he possesses can be utilised in a sedentary capacity, he could be employed in those areas. He is qualified in Air Sea Rescue. He is not medically fit for that sort of work. He is qualified as a medical assistant. He may be suitable for that sort of work. He is trained in basic computers. These skills could be upgraded and he could be re-trained into the computer industry, however he does have trouble sitting for long periods of time and operating a computer usually involves sitting for long periods of time. He has tried security work. That could be classified as front-line security or behind the scenes security. For example, he could be trained in operating a large security central switchboard that over-sees all security operations in a Town. That would be a sedentary sit-down occupation that he could perhaps perform.

There is no physical reason why he cannot be retrained into work of a lighter nature, however I am unsure as to how much his back pain will interfere with his ability to be employed on the open work place.

His medical restrictions are that he will need pain relief, anti-inflammatories and sedentary work of a lighter nature. No surgery is likely to be appropriate.

I believe he could be employed in behind the scenes security, or he could be trained into clerical and he could be retrained into work of a sedentary nature that does not involve repeated stressful use of his lumbar spine."

(vi) Dr. David Lewis, orthopaedic surgeon, 6 November 1995. "Mr. Oreb's condition is likely to stabilise, over a period of two to five years. Four years is the considered average.

I would suggest Mr. Oreb be reviewed in some two years.

It would appear to me that Mr. Oreb would be capable of limited sedentary type work."

(vii) Mr. D.R. Ryland, CEO, Townsville Employment Training Inc., 15 May 1996. "The limitations placed on Mr. Oreb because of his physical condition, would discourage an employer from placing him. The reasons would include his need to have special ergonomic equipment in place, and his limited capacity to remain in one position for extended periods. When considering the type of work Mr. Oreb would be capable of performing it must be considered that - * Clerical positions require long periods of sitting, standing and in many circumstances bending. This would aggravate Mr. Oreb's condition. * Security work would entail sitting, walking and perhaps, depending on the position running or detaining physically individuals. An employer would also need to be advised of Mr. Oreb's physical condition prior to employment. The majority of employers would find it daunting to place Mr. Oreb as he would be at high risk of injuring himself, and therefore they would attempt to avoid the claims.

With regard to the present employment situation in the Townsville/Thuringowa Region, it must be remembered that we have over 7 000 people registered with the Commonwealth Employment Service as unemployed and actively jobseeking. Mr Oreb would therefore be competing with many physically capable and fit people for any position he applied for.

Once againt this organisation would not state that Mr Oreb could not be placed in employment, but it would be extremely difficult and Mr Oreb himself has stated that he would require retraining before he could commence actively jobseeking."

(viii) Dr. John Monro, specialises in rehabilitation medicine, 23 August 1996. "In July 1996, he had his left ankle operated on and he is having some difficulty with standing on his left ankle with this.

His tolerances for activities involving sitting, standing or walking would appear to be quite limited and to each there is a 10 minute limit before pain increases in his lower back area.

He is presently using Naprosyn for pain control and is apparently using an anti-depressant medication to help with sleep at night. He is not receiving any other active treatments at present.

An Impairment Assessment was carried out according to the Guides To The Evaluation of Permanent Impairment according to the American Medical Association Guidelines, there would appear that his observable impairment rating using this could accumulate to a 17% Whole of Man figure for his thorocolumbar spine. He is neurologically intact to his lower limbs.

Frank Oreb leads a very restricted life because of the degree of back pain that he is experiencing. His formal education background is quite limited and his work training and experiences are such that he has no skills that he could be likely to use for the future. As such then, his ability to undertake office type employment is not predicted. He has limited physical tolerances so his ability to move as required to in any security situation would also not be likely.

It is my opinion that he is unlikely ever to be able to work in employment for which his education, training and experience would enable him to compete in the job market situation and he has not the ability for educational rehabilitation as well. As such then, it is my opinion that he is unlikely ever to be able to work again."

(ix) Nina Williams, rehabilitation/vocational consultant, 5 March 1997 "On reviewing all presented information:

It is my opinion that Mr. Oreb is not currently competitively employable in his former and proposed occupations, and would only obtain and sustain employment in the unusual (an non-competitive situation) of an employer allowing him to work as and when he is able to.

He is likely to experience difficulty in attempting further learning which will enable him to access occupations which require higher skill levels;

His physical restrictions lessen his competitiveness in a commercial or industrial capacity.

I therefore conclude that Mr. Oreb does not meet the criteria of being able to work in positions for which he is reasonably qualified by education, training or experience, or for which he could reasonably be qualified after re-training."

10. The weight of medical evidence favours the proposition that Mr. Oreb is capable of limited sedentary work. Dr. Munro is more pessimistic about Mr. Oreb's future but I believe that Dr. Monro has misconceived the degree of Mr. Oreb's experience and capacity. I do not accept that Mr. Oreb has no useful work related skills. The courses which Mr. Oreb did in the Army would stand him in good stead. There is a wide range of occupations that he could fill if he were fully fit.

11. There is no doubt that Mr. Oreb will have difficulty in competing in the present labour market. The extent to which the availability of suitable positions should be taken into account in reviews of this type of case is a question of fact and degree. It may well be that Mr. Oreb would have difficulty in securing a job in the present climate even if he were fully fit. On the other hand a person who is severely handicapped may have a special skill which makes them very employable in a specific niche.

12. Mr. Oreb's position is somewhere between the two extremes postulated above. The question is where to draw the line.

13. I am not satisfied that Mr. Oreb is unlikely ever to be able to work again in employment for which he is reasonably qualified by education, training or experience, nor am I satisfied that it is unreasonable to expect that he could be retrained to improve his qualifications and prospects for employment.

14. The decision under review is affirmed.
