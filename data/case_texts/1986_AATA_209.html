Re DJ Moran Managements Pty Limited and Minister of Community Services and Woy Woy Community Nursing Home Limited; Peninsula Homes of the Aged Limited; Jelisu Pty Limited Trading As Avoca Beach Nursing Home and NSW Baptist Homes Trust Trading As Oran [1986] AATA 209 (6 August 1986)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: D.J. MORAN MANAGEMENTS PTY LIMITED

And: MINISTER FOR COMMUNITY SERVICES and WOY WOY COMMUNITY NURSING HOME

LIMITED; PENINSULA HOMES FOR THE AGED LIMITED; JELISU PTY LIMITED trading as

AVOCA BEACH NURSING HOME AND NSW BAPTIST HOMES TRUST trading as ORANA NURSING

HOME

No. N85/378

AAT No. 2902

Health - Practice and Procedure

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

J.D. Davies J. (President)

CATCHWORDS

Health - Commonwealth benefits payable in respect of approved nursing home care - approval in principle by Minister for Community Services refused - request for summonses to produce evidence directed to all nursing homes in area - immense amount of detailed information required

Practice and Procedure summones to produce documentary evidence - failure to use correct form - issue of summonses to strangers to proceedings - failure to limit summonses to relevant issues

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth) s. 40(1A) National Health Act 1953 (Cth) s. 39A

Trade Practices Commission v. TNT Management Pty Ltd CCH Australian High Court and Federal Court Practice 1981-1983 Cases 65-064

Hughes v. Western Australian Cricket Association (Inc) (unreported, 30 May 1986)

Jet Corporation Pty Ltd v. Petres Pty Ltd (unreported, 3 May 1985)

Commissioner for Railways v. Small (1938) 38 SR (NSW) 564

Freemason's Homes of Southern Tasmania v. Minister for Community Services (unreported, 20 June 1986)

HEARING

SYDNEY

6:8:1986

ORDER

1. The application for a direction to issue the summonses sought directed to the following -

(i) The Matron, Killarney Vale Nursing Home,

(ii) The Matron, Toukley Lakeside Nursing Home,

(iii) The Matron, Gosford Nursing Home,

(iv) The Matron, Norah Head Nursing Home,

(v) The Matron, Wyong Nursing Home Pty Ltd,

(vi) The Home Care Service of New South Wales,

Wyong Shire Branch,

(vii) The Home Care Service of New South Wales,

Gosford Shire Branch,

(viii) The Home Care Service of New South Wales,

Main Branch, Parramatta,

(ix) The Matron, Adelene Nursing Home,

(x) The Matron, Nareen Gardens Nursing Home,

is refused.

2. I shall direct the issue of the summonses sought in respect of the

following -

(i) The Matron, Orana Nursing Home,

(ii) The Matron, Peninsula Home for the Aged,

(iii) Woy Woy Nursing Home Association,

(iv) The Matron, Avoca House Nursing Home,

(v) The Secretary, The Federal State

Co-ordinating Committee for Nursing Home

Accommodation for the State of New South

Wales,

when summonses in the correct form are lodged with

the New South Wales District Registry of the

Tribunal and the date for commencement of the

hearing of these proceedings has been determined.

3. Consideration of the issue of summonses to the Minister for Community

Services and the Minister of Health for New South Wales will be deferred until the next directions hearing.

4. I appoint Thursday the seventh of August 1986 at 4.15 pm as the date of the next directions hearing.

DECISION

These are my reasons with respect to the applicant's request that, pursuant to s. 40(1A) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth), I should direct the issue of summonses directed to persons to produce documents before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal at a date to be set out in each of the summonses.

2. The applicant is seeking the review of a decision made by the respondent, the Minister for Community Services, pursuant to s. 39A of the National Health Act 1953 (Cth), refusing to grant to the applicant approval in principle with respect to a nursing home proposed to be constructed by the applicant and granting approvals in principle to four nursing homes, each conducted by one of the parties joined in these proceedings.

3. Section 39A provides, inter alia,

"39A. (1) Upon application made in accordance with

the appropriate authorized form by a person who

is, or proposes to become, the proprietor of a

nursing home, the Minister may, in his discretion,

by notice published in the Gazette, invite

interested persons to apply, by such date, being a

date not less than 28 days after the date of

publication of the notice, as is specified in the

notice, under whichever of sub-sections (2) or (3)

is specified in the notice, for a certificate in

respect of premises situated in an area specified

in the notice.

(2) Upon application made in accordance with

the appropriate authorized form in response to an

invitation under sub-section (1) by a person who

is, or proposes to become, the proprietor of a

nursing home, the Minister may, in his discretion,

grant to the applicant a certificate in writing -

(a) stating that if the applicant applies under

sub-section 40AA(1) of this Act within the

period of 12 months after the grant of the

certificate for the approval of premises

specified in the certificate as an approved

nursing home and the premises comply, at the

time of that last-mentioned application,

with the specifications (if any) set out in

the certificate, that last-mentioned

application will not be refused under

sub-section 40AA(3) of (3A) of this Act;

(b) stating that if that approval is granted,

the number of beds determined in relation to

the nursing home for the purposes of

paragraph 40AA(6)(a) of this Act will not be

less than the number of beds specified in

the certificate; and

(c) in a case where the Minister considers it

appropriate to do so - stating that if that

approval is granted, the admission of

persons to the nursing home as qualified

nursing home patients will be in accordance

with a special purpose of the nursing home

specified in the certificate."

4. Section 39A(6) provides for the formulation of principles and states:

"39A. (6) The Minister may, by instrument in

writing, formulate principles to be complied with

by him with respect to any of his powers under

sub-sections (1), (2), (3) and (4)."

Once principles have been formulated, the Minister is bound to comply with those principles for s. 39A(5) provides:

"39A. (5) The Minister, in exercising his powers

under sub-section (1), (2), (3) or (4), shall

comply with any relevant principles in force under

sub-section (6)."

Likewise, the Tribunal would be bound to do so.

5. Current principles formulated under s. 39A(6), which were published in the Commonwealth of Australia Gazette of 19 May 1986, provide, inter alia,

"3. The provision of nursing home care in a

designated area shall be considered within the

context of the characteristics of the community in

the area and of its needs for community care

services generally.

4. Applications for the grant of a certificate in

respect of premises in a designated area shall not

be invited under sub-section 39A(1) of the first

Act nor shall a certificate under sub-section

39A(2) or (3) of that Act be granted in respect of

those premises if -

(a) the nursing home care that would be provided

in accordance with the certificate if it

were granted is not likely to satisfy the

needs of the area as effectively as the

provision of some other form of community

care;

(b) there is adequate provision in the area for

the nursing home care that would be provided

in accordance with the certificate if it

were granted; or

(c) any perceived need for nursing home care in

the area can be satisfied through a

re-organization of the care or accommodation

provided by existing nursing homes in the

area.

. . . . .

6. The actual need for general purpose beds in a

designated area shall be assessed and satisfied,

on the basis, however, that, except in areas where

a need for a greater number of beds can be clearly

identified, the number of beds required to satisfy

the need for such accommodation in any area will

not exceed such number as bears to the number of

persons aged 70 years or more in the area a ratio

of 44:1000.

7. In ascertaining, for the purpose of assessing

the need for general purpose beds, the number of

persons aged 70 years or more in a designated area

-

(a) current statisstics of population are to be

used based on projections made from the

latest census data available from the

Australian Bureau of Statistics; and

(b) persons in the area aged 70 years or more

that were included in statistics used in

connection with the provision of special

purpose beds are, as far as it is

practicable, to be excluded."

Those principles were stated in lieu of the principles current at the time when the Minister made the decision which is under review, but the only material change to which I need refer is that paragraph 6 of the earlier principles stated the ratio to be 80:1000. The material presently before the Tribunal suggests that, at the time of the Minister's decision and at the present time, the ratio of nursing home beds to the number of persons aged 70 years or more in the relevant area was and is in excess of 80:1000.

6. Accordingly, the task of the Tribunal will be to ascertain whether "a need for a greater number of beds can be clearly identified" and, if so, whether that need can best be satisfied by the grant of approvals to the four parties joined or to the applicant and whether the nursing home care that would be so provided would be as effective as the provision of some other form of community care.

7. Dr G.A. Flick, counsel for the applicant, has sought the issue of summonses directed to The Matron, Killarney Vale Nursing Home, The Matron, Toukley Lakeside Nursing Home, The Matron, Gosford Nursing Home, The Matron, Norah Head Nursing Home, The Matron, Wyong Nursing Home Pty Limited, The Home Care Service of New South Wales, Wyong Shire Branch, The Hone Care Service of New South Wales, Gosford Shire Branch, The Home Care Service of New South Wales, Main Branch, Parramatta, The Matron, Adelene Nursing Home, and The Matron, Nareen Gardens Nursing Home. The nursing homes mentioned, plus the nursing homes conducted by the four parties joined, constitute, I was informed, all the nursing homes in the relevant area, the Gosford/Wyong area, including the Woy Woy Peninsula.

8. Each of the above draft summonses was in the following form:

"To: . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

YOU ARE SUMMONED to bring and produce the

documents and things set out in the Schedule below

before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal at

on or before 25 day of 50.

SCHEDULE

1. All documents, notes, records or other

memoranda recording the numbers of beds

available during the period January 1985 to

date.

2. All documents, notes records or other

memoranda recording rates of occupancy of

beds available during the period January

1985 to date.

3. All documents, notes, records or other

memoranda recording fees charged of all

residents during the period January 1985 to

date.

4. All documents, notes, records or other

memoranda recording:-

(i) persons waiting for admission; and

(ii) the length of time persons have been

waiting for admission during the

period January 1985 to date.

5. All documents, notes, records or other

memoranda submitted to the Commonwealth

Department of Health seeking fee

determinations during the years:-

(i) 1984;

(ii) 1985;

(iii) 1986.

6. Any awards or commendations or like

documents received in respect to:-

(i) architectural merit;

(ii) care of patients.

7. All documents, notes, records or other

memoranda recording:-

(i) the names of all residents;

(ii) the addresses of all residents prior

to admission;

(iii) the present address of all surviving

partners of residents (if any);

(iv) the present addresses of all recorded

next of kin

during the period January 1985 to date.

8. All documents, notes, records or other

memoranda recording the Nursing Home's

policy and priority as to admission."

9. I should first observe that the draft summonses sought do not accord in their terms with the provisions of s.40(1A) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth) or with Form 7 of Schedule 1 to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Regulations. The Registrar, or Deputy Registrar, may, if directed to do so, summon a person to appear before the Tribunal at a hearing to give evidence and to produce such documents, if any, as are referred to in the summons. The draft summonses do not require any person to attend to give evidence but simply to attend on a date to be specified to bring and produce the documents referred to in the schedule to the summons.

10. The power which is conferred upon the Tribunal is a power to summon a person to appear before the Tribunal at the hearing of a proceeding, to give evidence and to produce such documents as are required. Although many courts have power to issue a subpoena requiring documents to be produced for inspection prior to the hearing of a proceeding - see Trade Practices Commission v. TNT Management Pty Limited, CCH Australian High Court and Federal Court Practice 1981-1983 Cases 65-064, Hughes v. Western Australian Cricket Association (Inc) & Ors (unreported, 30 May 1986) and Jet Corporation Pty Ltd v. Petres Pty Ltd (unreported, 3 May 1985) - the Tribunal does not have either the inherent power of a court or its power to make an appropriate rule of court which may confer such authority upon a court.

11. It appears from the draft summonses that the applicant is seeking to require each nursing home in the area to attend before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal on a date to be fixed and to produce an immense variety of material to the Tribunal which the applicant then proposes to inspect. In my opinion, this course should not be followed. I do not believe that the power to direct the issue of a summons was intended to be used for any such purpose, or that it authorises such a procedure.

12. Further, the ambit covered by the schedule to the summonses is so wide that I believe the summonses would infringe the principle stated by Jordan CJ in Commissioner for Railways v. Small (1938) 38 SR(NSW) 564 at 573,

"A writ of subpoena duces tecum may be addressed to

a stranger to the cause or to a party. If it be

addressed to a stranger, it must specify with

reasonable particularity the documents which are

required to be produced. A subpoena duces tecum

ought not to be issued to such a person requiring

him to search for and produce all such documents

as he may have in his possession or power relating

to a particular subject matter. It is not

legitimate to use a subpoena for the purpose of

endeavouring to obtain what would be in effect

discovery of documents against a person who, being

a stranger, is not liable to make discovery. A

stranger to the cause ought not to be required to

go to trouble and perhaps to expense in ransacking

his records and endeavouring to form a judgment as

to whether any of his papers throw light on a

dispute which is to be litigated upon issues of

which he is presumably ignorant : Lee v. Angas LR

2 Eq.59; Burchard v. Macfarlane <1891> 2 QB 241 at

247; A-G v. Wilson 9 Sim.526; Newland v. Steer 13

LT 111; 13 WR 1014. And if a subpoena duces

tecum is issued to such a person in an

objectionable form, the witness may apply to the

Court to have it set aside."

13. Thirdly, it is apparent from the width of the draft summonses to the nursing homes and of the other draft summonses which I shall hereafter mention and also from Dr Flick's announcement that he anticipates that the hearing of the matter will take one month, that the applicant intends at the hearing of this proceeding to enter into a general inquiry into the nursing home industry in the Gosford/Wyong area. Such a proceeding seems to me to be inconsistent with the principles which the Tribunal will have to apply when the matter comes up for decision. Those principles require the need for a greater number of beds to be clearly identified. If such an inquiry is required, I do not presently see that there is a need which can be clearly identified. Of course, once a need has been established, the question will remain as to what is the most effective means of serving that need. But again, it does not presently seem to me that a consideration of that issue will be greatly assisted by having brought before the Tribunal all the documentary material that the applicant seeks to have brought before it.

14. I am encouraged in this view by the sparseness of the information that the applicant itself has given with respect to the case which it proposes to put. The information given at the present time reads as follows:

"The Applicant contends that the material facts are

as follows:

. The aged population of the Gosford/Wyong

area is unique within Australia. It will be

contended that this uniqueness derives

primarily from a migration to that area by

persons moving there for retirement.

. the need for nursing home beds cannot be

accurately calculated by simply looking at

the aged population of that area without

also considering such factors as, but not

limited to, the needs of those persons who

are in nursing homes in the metropolitan

area of Sydney because of the unavailability

of nursing home beds in the Gosford/Wyong

area; the number of persons in the area who

have been approved by the Respondent for

admission to nursing homes; the erroneous

rejection of approval for persons seeking

admission to nursing homes in the area.

. there are no alternative means within the

area catering to the needs of the elderly

other than the provision of more nursing

home beds. It will be contended that, at

the time of making his decision, the

Respondent either was in fact aware or

should have been aware that community based

services, such as community nursing and

Meals on Wheels, could not meet the current

demand for services.

. a significant number of persons are

improperly forced to be admitted to

hospitals for varying lengths of time by

reason of the failure of the Respondent to

approve a proper number of nursing home

beds.

. the correct or preferable location of

nursing home beds in the Gosford/Wyong area

is within the immediate area of Gosford

township, North Gosford and Wyoming.

. on the statistics available to the

Respondent, and in fact considered by the

Commonwealth/State Co-ordinating Committee

for Nursing Home Accommodation in New South

Wales, there should desirably be up to

approximately 185 Nursing Home beds in the

Woy Woy Pennisular (sic) (if that be the

appropriate location for Nursing Homes) and

up to 1400 Nursing Homes in the

Gosford/Wyong area. In fact, the number of

Nursing Home beds, including those for which

approval were given in the second decision

is 100 for the Woy Woy Pennisular (sic) and

931 for the Gosford/Wyong area as a whole."

Directions have been given in this matter for statements of facts matters and contentions to be supplied by the parties. The above information given by the applicant is so brief that it is of little assistance in enabling the Tribunal or the parties to understand what are the factual matters which have to be investigated at the hearing. I am reluctant to direct the issue of summonses to other nursing homes in the area for the production of their records until I am satisfied that there is a relevant issue of fact to be ascertained and that that fact is best ascertained by requiring other nursing homes in the area to produce their records.

15. Finally, I would add that the function of the Tribunal in a matter such as this is to form its own view of the correct or preferable decision to be made under s. 39A and, in an appropriate case, to exercise the primary decision-making power. It is to be remembered that the Tribunal is an administrative body exercising an administrative function. It seems to me to be quite inconsistent with the proper performance of such function for the Tribunal to enter into a general inquiry of nursing homes in the Gosford/Wyong area. The legislation and the principles formulated by the Minister do not provide for the holding of lengthy inquiries in which all nursing homes in a particular area are examined with respect to the details of their operation and of their patients. The first step in a matter such as this is that there should be a clearly identified need for additional nursing home beds. As I said in Re Freemasons' Homes of Southern Tasmania and Minister for Community Services (unreported, 20 June 1986) such a need is best established by the evidence of qualified people working in the field who are able to assess the health care needs of elderly persons and of the means by which that care can be best provided.

16. For these reasons, I refuse to direct the issue of the summonses requested in respect of the above-mentioned nursing homes, but I shall list this matter for further directions with a view to then discussing with the applicant's representatives and with the respondent's representatives what is the best means by which statistical information such as the number of nursing home beds in the area, the occupancy rate thereof and like matters can be established.

17. Summonses in like form to the above were requested in respect of the four parties joined. A summons directed to the Secretary of the Federal/State Co-ordinating Committee for Nursing Home Accommodation for the State of New South Wales, concerning documentary information relevant to the issues in this proceeding, was also requested. I shall direct the issue of such summonses. However, each summons, when issued, must be in the correct form and the date inserted must be the date for the commencement of the hearing of these proceedings. That date has yet to be established.

18. I was also requested by Dr Flick to direct the issue of a lengthy summons to the Minister for Community Services and of a lengthy summons to the Minister for Health, New South Wales. I shall consider this matter further at the next directions hearing.
