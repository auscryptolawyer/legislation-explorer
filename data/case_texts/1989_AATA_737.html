ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - allowable deductions - bad debts - whether deposits with Cambridge Credit Corporation deductible as bad debts.

The Tribunal decides that the objection decisions under review be affirmed.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 : Ss.51, 63

Fairway Estates Pty Ltd v. F.C. of T. 70 ATC 4061

Case 2B 13 CTBR Case T91 B6 ATC 1136

Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 15 December 1989

Place: Brisbane

Two questions arise for decision. The first is whether losses arising from unsecured notes in Cambridge Credit Corporation Ltd. ("Cambridge") are bad debts deductible in terms of section 63 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1536 ( "the Act"). The second question is whether certain expenses paid to solicitors in respect of conveyancing costs on sale of a home unit are allowable deductions in terms of section 51 of the Act. The applicants did not seriously contend for the legal expenses. During the hearing the Tribunal decided that the legal expenses were of a capital nature and therefore not allowable deductions within the terms of sub-section 51(1).

2. These matters involving applications by a husband and wife were heard together on a telephone hookup taking most of a normal day's hearing time. This arrangement was at the specific request of the applicants; the Tribunal having previously made arrangements to travel to a town proximate to the applicants' place of residence. Hearings by telephone place the parties and the Tribunal at a considerable disadvantage. Although copies of documents and an agreed statement of facts had been exchanged prior to this hearing, during the course of the hearing both parties referred extensively to the applicants' books of account with which they were obviously both familiar but which have not been made available to the Tribunal. Furthermore, the Tribunal has not had the usual advantage of seeing the witness who gave oral testimony before the Tribunal. While no issue of creditability arises in these proceedings it is apparent that issues of creditability would be most difficult where the hearing is by telephone. Bearing in mind that the applicants specifically requested a hearing by telephone and that it does not appear to the Tribunal that any injustice will occur to the parties in these cases I have proceeded to determine the matter which remains in dispute.

  1. Sub-section 63(1) of the Act relevantly provides that debts which are bad debts and are written off as such during the year of income, and are in respect of money lent in the ordinary course of the business of lending money by a taxpayer who carries on that business shall be allowable deductions.
  2. The applicants are members of a partnership deriving assessable income by way of interest and rent. The interest was derived during the year ended 30 June 1976 in respect of loans made by way of vendor finance in respect of house and land packages sold by the partnership in earlier years of income. Interest was also derived from loans made to various finance companies, building societies and a well known retailer.
  3. No interest income was derived during the year of income from a loan made to Cambridge in an amount of $1,000 being an unsecured deposit made by the partnership some time prior to the year ended 30 June 1976. That amount of $1,000 is the subject of a claim for deduction as a bad debt by the partnership.
  4. The male applicant claims a further deduction of a bad debt of $980 being 20% of the male applicant's deposit of $4,900 loaned to Cambridge. The female applicant claims deduction for a bad debt in an amount of $40 also being 20% of a deposit with Cambridge, in this case $200.

  1. The applicants in their own right were not carrying on a business of the lending of money. There is a significant and vital difference between a person lending money as a money lender and a person lending money as an investor. The latter is an accepted way of investing money to generate income and does not constitute the carrying on of any business when conducted by individuals on an intermittent basis as was the case with these applicants.
  2. On the other hand the partnership was carrying on a business. The question then is whether that business included a business of lending money.
  3. Exhibit A establishes that the partnership derived its income principally from interest but also derived some rental income during the subject year. Of an amount of $7,069 interest derived $5,207 was derived from housing loans made to seven private borrowers and secured by mortgage over properties originally sold to the borrowers by the partnership.
  4. The balance of the interest income was derived from building societies and finance companies in respect of money on deposit or lent.
  5. In the light of the decision of the High Court in Fairway Estates Pty Ltd v. F.C. of T. 70 ATC 4061 and also Case 28 13 CTBR I am bound to hold that the business of the partnership was a business of lending money. Having come to that conclusion I can see no basis for differentiating between a loan made to an individual as a housing loan and a loan made to a finance house, which at first blush might be thought to be an investment of capital. I am therefore satisfied that the partnership was carrying on a business of lending money in the ordinary course of its business and the bad debts of such business may be deductible within the terms of sub-section 63(1). Therefore such debts which are bad debts and are written off as such during the year of income are allowable deductions.
  6. Two questions remain. Were the debts bad debts and if so were they written off as such during the year of income.
  7. In Case T9I 86 ATC 1136, involving a claim for bad debts in respect of interest derived from but not paid by Cambridge, the factual material set out in the reasons for decision make it clear that although there was a problem with repayment of loans made to Cambridge it could not be said in 1976 that the debts were wholly bad. Section 63 requires that the debt be objectively bad, not simply that the debt has been written off as a bad debt. I have not been satisfied as a matter of fact that the debt was a bad debt in the year ended 30 June 1976.
    1. Furthermore, I am not satisfied that the debt was written off as a bad debt during the year of income. Given that the partnership was a business required to keep, and did keep, books of account the failure to write off the debt during the year ended 30 June 1976 must be fatal to the claim.
    2. I have carefully considered the written submission of the applicants in these proceedings (Exhibit B). I am, however, compelled to the view that the objection decisions should be affirmed.
