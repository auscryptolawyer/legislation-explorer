Re Rosario Meilak v the Commissioner of Superannuation NoV G315 of 1990 Fed [1991] FCA 96; 99 ALR 559 13 Aar 186/22 ALD 32/28 FCR 315 (26 March 1991)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: ROSARIO MEILAK

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No.V G315 of 1990

FED No. 110

Statutory Construction

[1991] FCA 96; 99 ALR 559

13 AAR 186/22 ALD 32/28 FCR 315

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Sheppard(1), Jenkinson(1) and Ryan(1) JJ.

CATCHWORDS

Statutory Construction - applicant answered questions at medical examination held in 1974 - whether the correctness of his answers to be judged according to the standard provided for in an amending Act subsequently coming into force - discussion of principles applicable where amending Act purports to affect accrued rights.

Superannuation Act 1922

Superannuation Act 1976 ss. 16, 16AC, 184

Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act 1990 ss. 21, 91

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 s. 45

HEARING

MELBOURNE

26:3:1991

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr J. Lenczner

Solicitors for the Applicants: Gill Kane and Brophy, Melbourne

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr R. Tracey

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

1. Declare that the provisions of subs. 184(8) of the Superannuation Act 1976 inserted into that Act by para. 91(1)(e) of the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act 1990 apply in relation to the answers made by the applicant in writing to questions asked him at his medical examination held on 28 October 1974.

2. No answer be made to the questions of law referred to the Court by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal on 29 August 1990.

3. There be no order as to costs.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

To be determined are two questions referred to the Court by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal pursuant to s.45 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. The questions are intended to raise for consideration the question whether amendments made to the Superannuation Act 1976 by the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act 1990 applied to statements made for the purposes of the Act by a person before the amending Act came into force. In our opinion the questions are not apt to raise the point which is in issue and it is not useful to set them out at this stage. We shall refer to the detail of them after we have dealt with the point of substance which is involved.

2. There have been three principal Acts providing for superannuation to be paid to members of the Commonwealth Public Service. The first of these was the Superannuation Act 1922, the second the Superannuation Act 1976 and the third the Superannuation Act 1990. The 1922 Act is hereafter referred to as "the superseded Act" and the 1976 Act as "the Act". The provisions of the 1990 Act do not arise for consideration.

3. The applicant was born on 7 November 1931. He commenced work at the Naval Dockyard at Williamstown on 18 November 1955. His employment records show that he suffered from a number of conditions in the years between 1965 and 1974. Amongst the conditions complained of were "bad nerves", "lumbago", "anxiety factor" and "lumbar fibrositis". In 1974 the applicant had five days sick leave for what was described as an "anxiety neurosis".

4. On 28 October 1974 the applicant underwent a medical examination for the purposes of an application for permanent appointment to the Public Service and for superannuation. At the time of the medical examination he was required to complete a questionnaire which asked a number of questions about his medical history. Amongst other things he was asked whether he had ever suffered from any mental or nervous disorder or anxiety state. He answered this question in the negative. He was also asked whether he had ever suffered from lumbago or any disorder of his spine. That question was also answered in the negative. On 20 January 1975 he was accepted for superannuation into what was known as the Provident Account under the superseded Act.

5. The 1976 Act came into force on 1 April 1976. Part X11 of that Act was headed "Transitional Provisions" and contained Division 4 which was entitled "Existing Contributors". The relevant provisions of the 1976 Act have been the subject of amendments in 1978, 1986 and 1988 as well as in 1990. Except for the amendments made in 1990, it is immaterial, for the purposes of this case, whether one refers to the Act in one of its earlier forms or in the form it took after the 1988 amendments. For convenience we propose to refer to the Act as it was after the 1988 amendments but before the 1990 amendments.

6. Division 4 of Part X11 of the Act contains ss. 181 to 185. Section 184 provides that the section applies to a person who is or at any time has been, an existing contributor other than certain categories of contributor not relevant to be considered in the present case. "Existing contributor" is defined in s. 3 to mean a person who is, or has at any time been, a person referred to in para. (a) of the definition of "eligible employee" in subs. 3(1) and who, immediately before the commencement day, was a contributor for the purposes of the superseded Act. Paragraph (a) of the definition of "eligible employee" provides that an eligible employee is a person who, immediately before the commencement of the 1976 Act, was an employee for the purposes of the superseded Act and has continued to be, and is such an employee.

7. Section 16 of the Act makes provision for the issue of benefit classification certificates. The section is to be found in Part 1 of the Act entitled "Preliminary" and provides that the Commissioner for Superannuation may, for the purposes of the section, require a person who proposes (after the commencement of the 1976 Act) to become or becomes an eligible employee to undergo such medical examination or examinations as the Commissioner determines. The Commissioner is to consider the report or reports of any such examinations and such other matters as he considers relevant and, if he is of the opinion that there is a real risk that the person, by reason of or for a reason connected with a physical or mental condition or conditions, will not continue to be an eligible employee until the person attains his maximum retiring age, the Commissioner shall issue a benefit classification certificate to that effect, being a certificate in which the relevant condition or conditions is or are specified.

8. Section 16 does not apply to a person to whom s. 184 applies; see subs. 16(2). That is because it is s. 184 which makes provision in relation to benefit classification certificates issued or to be issued in respect of employees to whom the superseded Act applied.

9. Central to the question which arises for determination in the present matter are the provisions of subsec. 184(5) of the Act which are as follows:

"(5) Where the Commissioner is satisfied, in respect

of a person who is a relevant person-

(a) that, at or in connection with a medical examination

that the person underwent for the purpose of the

superseded Act, the person failed to give information

that the person was required to give or gave false or

misleading information; and

(b) that a physical or mental condition of the person or

physical or mental conditions of the person (in this

sub-section referred to as an "unrecorded condition"

or as "unrecorded conditions") that was not or were

not relevant for the purposes of the superseded Act,

would have been relevant for the purposes of that Act

if the person had not failed to give the information

the person was required to give or had not given that

false or misleading information,

the Commissioner shall-

(c) if there is a benefit classification certificate in

force in respect of the relevant person - revoke that

certificate and issue a new benefit classification

certificate in respect of the person in which the

unrecorded condition or the unrecorded conditions is

or are specified either in addition to or in

substitution for the physical or mental condition that

was, or any or all of the physical or mental

conditions that were, specified in the first-mentioned

certificate;

(d) if there is no benefit classification certificate in

force in respect of the person but a physical or

mental condition of the person or physical or mental

conditions of the person was or were relevant for the

purposes of the superseded Act - issue a benefit

classification certificate in respect of the person in

which the unrecorded condition or the unrecorded

conditions is or are specified either in addition to

or in substitution for the physical or mental

condition that was, or any or all of the physical or

mental conditions that were, relevant for the purposes

of the superseded Act; or

(e) if there is no benefit classification certificate in

force in respect of the person and no physical or

mental condition of the person was relevant for the

purposes of the superseded Act - issue a benefit

classification certificate in respect of the person in

which the unrecorded condition is or the unrecorded

conditions are specified."

Also of relevance is subs. 184(6) which provides:-

"(6) The Commissioner shall not, in a benefit

classification certificate issued under subsection

(5), include a physical or mental condition in respect

of which the Commissioner was of the opinion under

subsection 16(8) that there was not a real risk that

the person would, by reason of or for a reason

connected with that condition, not continue to be an

eligible employee until the person attained his or her

maximum retiring age unless the Commissioner is

satisfied that, but for the failure of the person to

furnish any information required to be furnished by

the person or the furnishing of false information at

or in connection with a medical examination that the

person was required to undergo under subsection 16(6),

the Commissioner would not have been of that opinion.

10. The Act has been the subject of consideration by this Court on other occasions. In McMullen v. Commissioner for Superannuation (1985) 61 ALR 189 the Court gave a comprehensive explanation of how the provisions of the superseded Act and the transitional provisions of the Act operated; see pp 192-202. In Fry v. Commissioner for Superannuation [1984] FCA 211; (1984) 6 ALD 229 the Court considered a number of matters including the use of the expression "false information" in subs. 16(11) of the Act. Subsection 16(11) is in substance similar to subs. 184(5) of the Act. The principal judgment was given by Sweeney J whose judgment was agreed in by Blackburn and Neaves JJ. The problem in the Fry case was that the applicant there had had periods when he was admitted to hospital. He did not disclose this to the medical officer examining him but he was never asked the question whether he had previously been admitted to hospital. Sweeney J said (p 237):-

"There must be a requirement made of the employee to furnish

information before any obligation arises. The extent of

that obligation depends upon the questions asked of him and

the actual state of his knowledge in relation to them.

The obligation upon him is to "furnish information".

"Furnish" means "to provide or supply with (something

necessary, useful, or desirable)" and "information" is

something "of which one is apprised or told." (See the

Shorter Oxford English Dictionary.)

To fall within the scope of the section an employee must

have failed to provide or supply something required of him

of which he has been apprised or told.

In my opinion the section does not support the hybrid test

for which the Commissioner contends. There is no

justification for approaching the obligation placed upon the

employee on the basis of "the knowledge which might fairly

have been attributed to him" or upon what "he should have

known".

An employee complies with the section if he answers the

questions truthfully according to his actual knowledge and

state of mind."

11. On 22 June 1981 a delegate of the Commissioner issued a benefit classification certificate under s. 184(3) of the Act containing a number of specific conditions but making no reference to any condition of anxiety or disorder of the spine. On 9 September 1981 the applicant retired on grounds of invalidity, namely, "post-traumatic anxiety depression neurosis and low back injury". On 29 April 1982 a delegate of the Commissioner issued a fresh benefit classification certificate which specified as physical or mental conditions a history of anxiety and a history of lumbar pain. The conditions earlier specified were also specified in the new certificate. On 30 April 1982 the Commissioner decided that the retirement was caused or substantially contributed to by the previously existing anxiety and lumbar pain conditions.

12. The applicant was advised of the decision on 3 May 1982 and, on 10 May 1982, requested, pursuant to s. 154 of the Act, that the decision be reconsidered. The decision was reconsidered and, on 18 January 1985, almost three years after the decision had been made it was decided that the previous decision should be confirmed.

13. On 2 March 1989 the applicant lodged an application with the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for the review of the 1985 decision to confirm the 1982 decision. We were informed that the Tribunal had granted an extension of time to enable this to be done.

14. The hearing of the appeal to the Tribunal commenced on 29 August 1990. In the meantime the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act had come into force on 1 July 1990. Section 21 of the amending Act inserted a number of sections after s. 16. These included s. 16AC. Subsections (9) and (10) of s. 16AC are as follows:

"(9) For the purposes of this section, a person answers a

question properly if, and only if, he or she gives in answer

to the question all the information that he or she could

reasonably be expected to give, on the basis of:

(a) his or her knowledge about the relevant matters; and

(b) the knowledge that, having regard to his or her

knowledge about the relevant matters, he or she could

reasonably be expected to have about those matters;

if he or she answered the question fully and truthfully on

the basis of the knowledge referred to in paragraphs (a) and

(b).

(10) In subsection (9):

'relevant matters', in relation to a question asked of a

person, means:

(a) in any case-the matters that the person could

reasonably be expected to regard as relevant to

answering the question; and

(b) if the question is asked of the person:

(i) at or in connection with a medical

examination that the person was required

under subsection 16(2) or (6) or 16AB(3)

to undergo; or

(ii) in connection with a request by the person

under subsection 16(6); or

(iii)in connection with an application

under subsection 16AB(2) for the

revocation of a certificate issued in

respect of the person;

the person's medical history and past and present

state of physical and mental health."

15. Section 91 of the amending Act made amendments to s. 184. For relevant purposes the principal amendment was the addition to s. 184 of an additional subsection, subs. (8), which is as follows:

"(8) For the purposes of determining, for the purposes of this

section, whether or not a person answered a question

properly, subsections 16AC(9) and (10) apply as if:

(a) the provisions of this section were provisions of

section 16AC; and

(b) the following subparagraph were inserted before

subparagraph (b)(i) of the definition of 'relevant

matters' in subsection 16AC(10):

(ia) at or in connection with a medical examination

that the person underwent for the purposes of

the superseded Act;"

16. There were also amendments made to paras. 184(5)(a) and (b) and to subs. 184(6). The amendments to para. 184(5)(a) substituted the words "answer properly a question asked of him or her" for the words "give information that the person was required to give". The amendment made to para. 184(5)(b) was to a similar effect. The amendments made to subs. 184(6) involved the deletion of all words after the word "satisfied" and the substitution of the following:-

"that the Commissioner would not have been of that opinion but for

the fact that:

(a) at or in connection with a medical examination that

the person was required to undergo under subsection

16(6) or 16AB(3); or

(b) in connection with a request by the person under

subsection 16(6);

the person failed to answer properly a question asked of the person or gave false or misleading information."

17. In due course it will be relevant to mention the provisions of subss. (2) and (3) of s. 91 of the amending Act but we shall come to those a little later.

18. The purpose of the new subs. 184(8) was to apply to the answers to questions made by a person to whom s. 184 applied the same standard or approach as was to be applied to answers made to questions asked pursuant to s. 16. It may be thought that subs. 16AC(9) was intended to substitute for the standard decided upon in Fry's case a standard which was more exacting and which required the provision of an answer based not only on actual knowledge but also on constructive knowledge.

19. The question which the Tribunal and the parties require answered is whether the answers made by the applicant in the document which he signed on 29 October 1974 should be assessed according to the standard provided for in subs. 16AC(9) or according to the standard which would have prevailed had that subsection not been enacted. In the submission of counsel for the applicant the standard provided for in the amending Act should not be applied because the amending Act does not evince an intention on the part of Parliament to give to its provisions a retrospective operation. Counsel submitted that the applicant had an accrued right and said that it was a well recognised principle of statutory construction that Acts of Parliament which purported to affect accrued rights should not be accorded a retrospective operation unless the language of the statutes evinced a clear intention that this should be the case.

20. Counsel for the applicant was pressed by the Court to specify precisely the nature of the accrued right upon which he relied. He said that it was the right of a person in the position of the applicant here to have the question of the falsity of his answers to questions about his medical condition decided according to the standard to be discerned from the provisions of the Act as it stood prior to the 1990 amendments. We have some reservation whether the right upon which counsel relies is in reality a right of the kind which is referred to in the authorities which illustrate the principle which counsel invoked; compare Maxwell v. Murphy [1957] HCA 7; (1957) 96 CLR 261. In the view we take of the matter, however, we do not feel it necessary to reach a conclusion on this matter. That is because we are clearly of opinion that the amending Act evinces an intention to apply the standard or approach provided for in subs. 16AC(9) to all cases which arise under s. 184.

21. The only operation which subs. 184(5) can have is in respect of the past. It is concerned with applications which were made under the superseded Act before the 1976 Act came into force. If one were to construe the amendment to it as applying prospectively, the amendment would be of no effect. It would have no work to do.

22. Earlier we said that we would in due course make reference to subss. 91(2) and (3) of the amending Act. Subsection 91(2) provides that, subject to subs. (3), the new subs. 184(6) of the Act applies in relation to a question asked of a person, or information given, whether before or after the commencement of s. 91. There is no similar provision made in relation to subs. 184(5) and, at first sight, it may be thought that subs. 91(2) provides some support for the applicant's submissions. But a consideration of the provisions of subs. 184(6) shows that, unlike subs. 184(5), it will in most circumstances have a prospective operation. Subsection 184(5) looks to the question of the truth or falsity of answers made in the past at the time the answers were given. It is true that the Commissioner may from time to time issue fresh benefit classification certificates, but, if the question is whether an employee has failed to disclose a pre-existing condition, it is incumbent on the Commissioner to consider the matter on the basis of the employee's knowledge at the time the questions were answered. The provisions of sub para. 184(8)(b)(ia) of the amending Act reinforce this conclusion.

23. On the other hand, subs. 184(6) deals with what the Commissioner himself may or may not do. The certificates are issued by the Commissioner himself. Ordinarily one would accord to the provision a prospective operation only. One would not think that its provisions were to operate in relation to certificates issued before the amending Act came into force. But the amendments made to subs. 184(6) and the provisions of subs. 91(2) of the amending Act show that the legislature intended the Commissioner to have power to review earlier certificates given by him and, in doing so, to apply the standard provided for in the amending Act.

24. Subsection 91(3) provides a clear indication that the amendments to subs. 184(5) are to operate retrospectively. The subsection provides that where, before the commencement of the section, the Administrative Appeals Tribunal or the Federal Court made a decision or order setting aside or varying a decision by the Commissioner to issue a benefit classification certificate in respect of a person under subs. 184(5) of the Act "as then in force", then "in spite of" the amendments to s. 184, subss. 184(5) to (7) inclusive of the Act as in force before the commencement of s. 91 continue to apply in relation to requirements and information on the basis of which the Commissioner decided to issue a certificate or to specify in it a particular physical or mental condition.

25. It follows that the submissions relied upon by the applicant should be rejected. The standard provided for in subs. (9) of s. 16AC is the standard to be applied in determining whether or not the answers made by the applicant to the question asked him on 28 October 1974 were false.

26. The questions asked by the Tribunal pursuant to s. 45 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act are as follows:

"1. WHEN determining the application, is the Tribunal bound to

give effect to the amending Act and in particular Sections

21 and 91 thereof.

2. IF yes to Clause 1 hereof, does Section 184 as

amended -

(a) require or permit the Tribunal to

have regard to knowledge reasonably

expected to be had by the Applicant;

(b) require or permit the Tribunal to

have regard to knowledge actually

and subjectively had by the

Applicant;

(c) require or permit the Tribunal to

have regard to any other matter.

27. We are not prepared to answer these questions because they are far too widely expressed. The only matter that has been argued before us is whether the standard provided for in s. 16AC(9) is the applicable standard. We have decided that it is. We propose to give effect to our decision by making an appropriate declaration. We shall return the questions to the Tribunal unanswered.

28. Before we conclude, we should refer to another matter. The questions were stated in a document entitled "Questions of Law". The document contained five paragraphs of recitals which made almost no reference to the facts of the matter. Although we sit as a Full Court, we sit at first instance. None of the facts of the matter was before us until we pointed out to counsel that we had been provided with no factual basis upon which to make our decision. This led to the file from the Tribunal being obtained and the tender of the relevant documents from which the facts can be discerned. We wish to emphasise for future cases that if questions of law are to be asked the Court, the Court must be provided with a proper factual basis upon which to make its decision. This may be done by the parties agreeing upon a statement of facts which can be tendered. If appropriate, relevant documents can be annexed to it. If this is not done, the Tribunal itself must find the primary facts and its findings upon those facts must be tendered in evidence before the Court will proceed.

29. In the result we declare that the provisions of subs. 184(8) of the Superannuation Act 1976 inserted into that Act by para. 91(1)(e) of the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act 1990 apply in relation to the answers made by the applicant in writing to questions asked him at his medical examination held on 28 October 1974. We were informed by counsel for the respondent that, in the event of the Commissioner being successful, he did not ask for costs. In those circumstances there will be no order as to the costs of the argument which has taken place.
