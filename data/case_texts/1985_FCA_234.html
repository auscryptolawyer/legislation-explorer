Re Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia v Terrule Pty Ltd and Geoffrey George Manners [1985] FCA 234; (1985) 7 FCR 98 (4 July 1985)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

And: TERRULE PTY LTD. and GEOFFREY GEORGE MANNERS [1985] FCA 234; (1985) 7 FCR 98

No. VG 147 of 1985

Practice & Procedure

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Woodward J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

PRACTICE & PROCEDURE - application for leave to appeal - objection to competency of application for review under the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 - whether final or interlocutory order - whether inability to challenge important jurisdictional question may amount to substantial injustice - order staying proceedings in Supreme Court - injunctions to stand in lieu of undertakings offered at interlocutory stage - trial judge's consideration of whether substantial question to be tried.

Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936.

Practice and Procedure - Application for leave to appeal - Whether important jurisdictional questions justify the grant of leave - Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth). Leave to appeal will be granted to permit the consideration by the Full Court of important issues of jurisdiction not previously considered by a Full Court.

HEARING

1985, July 4. 4:7:1985

D R Meagher QC and P R Hayes, for the applicant.

J W De Wijn, for the respondent.

Solicitors for the applicant: Australian Government Solicitor.

Solicitors for the respondent: Herbert, Geer and Rundle.

GFV

ORDER

1. The applicant have leave to appeal against the decisions of Jenkinson J. of 18 February, 1985 in matters VG 307 and VG 326 of 1984 within twenty-one days of the delivery of the final judgments in those matters.

2. The application for leave to appeal against the decisions of Jenkinson J. of 24 May, 1985 in matters VG 307 and VG 326 of 1984 be refused.

3. The applicant pay the respondents' costs of this application.

(Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with by 0.36 of the Federal Court Rules.)

Orders accordingly

DECISION

This is an application for leave to appeal, first, from decisions of Jenkinson J. given in matters number VG 307 and VG 326 of 1984 on 18 February 1985, in which his Honour overruled an objection to competency taken by the respondent in those applications, the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia, against proceedings under the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977, brought by Terrule Pty Limited in the first place, and Geoffrey George Manners in the second place.

I am informed that the matter was argued in relation to the Terrule application, on the understanding that any decision made in those proceedings would apply equally in the Manners application.

The decision which Terrule is challenging is a decision to institute proceedings in the Victorian Supreme Court for the recovery of income tax and certain additional tax alleged to be due and payable by Terrule. The objection to competency was based on arguments that the decision to institute proceedings was neither a decision of an administrative character, nor a decision made under an enactment, within the meaning of those expressions in the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977.

Mr Meagher QC, for the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, has said that it does not matter whether these decisions of his Honour were of a final or interlocutory nature, because the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation is out of time and must seek leave in any event and, give the nature of the case, similar consideration would apply in the exercise of the Court's discretion.

Mr De Wijn, for Terrule, concedes that the judgments were in essence final, and relies on the delay which has in fact occurred as sufficient reason for refusing leave. I think Mr De Wijn is probably right in his concession; certainly if the decisions had gone the other way they would have constituted final judgments. But I prefer not to decide the question without argument or citation of authority.

It is sufficient for my purposes to say that the judgments, dealing as they do with important questions of jurisdiction, have many of the hallmarks of final judgments, and should not be considered in the same light as judgments concerning, for example, interrogatories or discovery.

The delay in instituting an appeal is explained in the first place by the fact that his Honour's considered reasons for judgment were not available until 16 April 1985, and in the second place by the almost constant litigation between the parties in the Supreme Court and this Court, which occupied the month of May and required much detailed preparation in April. The application for leave to appeal was filed on 14 June 1985.

Since the significance of any appeal was closely related to the outcome of the Supreme Court proceedings, and I cannot see that Terrule has been prejudiced in any way by the delay, I think the conduct of the Deputy Commissioner and his legal advisers has not been unreasonable, and if he is ultimately unsuccessful in the substantive actions, which are presently part-heard before Jenkinson J, he should be allowed then to challenge his Honour's decisions on competency.

With the part-heard cases about to resume next week, I do not think it would be appropriate to delay those proceedings for the hearing of an appeal which, if the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation succeeds in his other defences to the applications, would be redundant.

On the other hand, to deny the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation the right to challenge very important jurisdictional decisions on issues which, as I understand it, are yet to be considered by a Full Court, would in my view amount to a substantial injustice.

Accordingly I grant leave to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, if so advised, to appeal against Jenkinson J's judgments of 18 February 1985, within 21 days of the delivery of judgments following the hearing of these respective matters.

The Deputy Commissioner of Taxation has also sought leave to appeal against two interlocutory judgments of Jenkinson J. delivered on 24 May 1985 in the same matters, number VG 307 and VG 326 of 1984. In those judgments his Honour granted injunctions against the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation which had the effect of preventing him from entering judgment against Terrule in Supreme Court proceedings, or levying execution against Geoffrey George Manners in related Supreme Court proceedings. Manners, of course, is the applicant in the proceedings in this Court, VG 326 of 1984, which run parallel to the Terrule matter.

The substance of Mr Meagher's argument on this application for leave is that, if Jenkinson J. had properly considered the merits of the two actions, he would have found there was no serious question to be tried, and so dismissed the applications for injunctions without ever having to consider the balance of convenience between the parties. As to these being the relevant tests, see Epitoma v AMIEU [1984] FCA 202; (1984) 54 ALR 730 at 734, and cases there cited.

Mr Meagher argued that his Honour did not deal with the merits of the case at all; but he conceded that if there had been a serious question to be tried, the balance of convenience must have been decided against the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation.

It is true that his Honour did not canvass the merits of the action in his judgment, but it must be recalled that the application came at a time when the matter was part-heard before his Honour and the evidence appeared to have been completed. No doubt his Honour would have been reluctant to go into the issues at that stage. He did however make it clear in one passage that the prospects of success for the applicants were present in his mind, although he did not attempt to assess them.

It is also important to note that the injunctions sought were designed to take the place of qualified undertakings which had forestalled the grant of earlier interlocutory injunctions. Thus his Honour, when he granted the injunctions, was merely maintaining the existing position for what then appeared to be a few weeks.

In these circumstances, applying the general principles relating to appeals in interlocutory matters, I am unable to see any special circumstances which would justify the interruption of the continued hearing of these matters to enable the grant of these injunctions to be challenged.

It is not alleged that any property of Terrule or Manners needed to satisfy Supreme Court judgments will be lost to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation by the injunctions. I have to be satisfied that substantial injustice could follow from a refusal of leave to appeal, (Niemann v Electronic Industries [1978] VicRp 44; (1978) VR 431 at page 439), and all Mr Meagher can point to is the cost and delay involved in the continuation of the hearings before Jenkinson J.

Whatever the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation's prospects of ultimate success in these proceedings may be, I am by no means persuaded that it would be in the interest of either party to take questions to the Full Court at this interlocutory stage. I think there is a real risk that such an appeal would determine few, if any, of the serious issues arising between the parties, and would only serve to delay a final result.

Accordingly, leave to appeal from the judgments of Jenkinson J. of 24 May 1984 is refused. Since the only issue on which he has succeeded is one in which an indulgence is sought, the applicant should pay the costs of the application.
