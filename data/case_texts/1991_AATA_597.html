Brown and Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 597 (28 June 1991)

Last Updated: 7 July 2008

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION



SUPERANNUATION - Applicant appointed to Australian Public Service - Appointment on probation - Upon taking up appointment applicant became "eligible employee" for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 (Cth) - Probationary appointment annulled following medical examination - Whether applicant a person whose services had been terminated otherwise than by retirement - Whether the ground upon which the applicant's services had been terminated was her physical or mental incapacity to perform her duties.

1. The applicant, Coral Ann Brown, has applied to the Tribunal for the review of a decision described in the application as having been made on 17 October 1989 by one Peter James Gunther, a delegate of the respondent, the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the Commissioner") appointed under the Superannuation Act 1976 (Cth). The effect of that decision was to reject a claim by the applicant that she was, within the meaning of sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act, a person who had been retired, or whose services had been otherwise terminated, on the ground of physical or mental incapacity to perform her duties.



2. The applicant had been a member of the Australian Public Service. At some time after the annulment, pursuant to s.47 of the Public Service Act 1922 (Cth), with effect from the close of business on 15 August 1986, of her appointment as a Clerical Assistant Grade 1, Department of Defence, in circumstances to which it will be necessary to refer later in these reasons, the applicant claimed to be entitled to invalidity benefit under s.66 of the Superannuation Act on the basis that she was, within the meaning of that section, a person who had ceased to be an eligible employee "by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity" before attaining her maximum retiring age. Section 7 of the Superannuation Act bears upon the meaning of the words "retirement on the ground of invalidity" in s.66. At all relevant times that section provided:



"7 (1) A reference in this Act to the retirement of an eligible employee on the ground of invalidity shall be read as a reference to his having been retired, or his services having been otherwise terminated, on the ground of physical or mental incapacity to perform his duties.



(2) Where a person has, before attaining his maximum retiring age, ceased to be an eligible employee otherwise than by reason of death or by reason of his having been retired, or his services having been otherwise terminated, on the ground of physical or mental incapacity to perform his duties, but the Commissioner is satisfied that, at the time he ceased to be an eligible employee, he was, by reason of physical or mental incapacity, unfit to perform his duties, the Commissioner may direct that the person shall, for the purposes of this Act, be deemed to have been retired on the ground of that physical or mental incapacity."





The applicant claimed that her case fell squarely within the language of sub-s.7(1) with the consequence that her entitlement to invalidity benefit under s.66 did not depend upon a direction being given by the Commissioner under sub-s.7(2).



3. By letter dated 11 April 1989 addressed to the applicant on behalf of the Commissioner, the applicant was informed that written representations made on her behalf had been accepted as an application under sub-s.7(2) of the Superannuation Act. No express reference was made in that letter to sub-s.7(1) but it is implicit that the view had been taken that the applicant's case did not fall within that sub-section. As a result of further correspondence, the question whether the applicant's case fell within sub-s.7(1) was reconsidered pursuant to sub-s.154(4) of the Superannuation Act. It is the decision made upon that reconsideration that the applicant seeks to have reviewed. That decision is expressed as -



"a decision that the circumstances of Ms Brown's annulment of appointment do not constitute a retirement, or a termination of services, on the ground of physical or mental incapacity to perform her duties and that she therefore does not meet the definition of invalidity retirement posited by subsection 7(1)".



4. Reference must be made to the history of the applicant's employment in the Australian Public Service. Before doing so, however, those provisions of ss.34 and 47 of the Public Service Act that are relevant for present purposes should be set out. At all times material to this matter, they provided:



"34. (1) Subject to this section, a person is not eligible for appointment to the Service unless -



(a) he is an Australian citizen; and



(c) the Board is satisfied that he is a fit and proper person to be an officer of the Service.



(2) Except as otherwise provided in this Act, a person is not eligible for appointment to the Service unless the Board is satisfied, after he has undergone a medical examination approved by the Board, as to his health and physical fitness.



(3) Sub-section (2) does not apply to an appointment of a person to the Service that is an appointment on probation.



..."



"47. (1) Subject to sub-section (14) and any other provision of this Act, the appointment of a person to the Service as an officer (other than a Secretary) shall, in the first instance, be an appointment on probation.



(2) A person appointed to the Service on probation remains a probationer until his appointment is confirmed or annulled in accordance with this section.



...

(6) The Board may, at any time during the period of 6 months from the date on which a probationer commenced duties in pursuance of his appointment, annul his appointment.



(7) As soon as practicable after the expiration of the period of 6 months from the date on which a probationer commenced duties in pursuance of his appointment, the Board shall either -



(a) confirm the appointment of the probationer;



(b) annul his appointment; or



(c) direct, subject to sub-sections (7A) and (7B), that he continue on probation for such further period as the Board thinks fit.



(7A) Subject to sub-section (7B), the Board shall not direct that a probationer continue on probation for a further period if the total period of his probation would exceed 1 year.



...



(8) Where the Board directs that a probationer continue on probation for a further period, it may confirm or, subject to sub-section (8AA), annul his appointment at any time during that further period and, if it has not confirmed or annulled his appointment before the expiration of that period, shall do so as soon as practicable after the expiration of that period.



...

(8A) The Board shall not confirm the appointment of a probationer unless the Board is satisfied, after he has undergone a medical examination approved by the Board, as to his health and physical fitness.



(9) The Board shall not -



(a) confirm the appointment of a probationer;



(b) except on the ground that it is not satisfied as to his health, annul the appointment of a probationer; or



(c) direct that a probationer continue on probation,



unless the Board has received -



(d) if he has performed duties as a probationer in a single Department only - a report from the Chief Officer with respect to his service as a probationer; or



(e) in any other case - relevant reports covering the whole of the period of his probationary service.



...



(10) The annulment of the appointment of a probationer takes effect from and including such date as the Board specifies.



(11) Where a probationer has ceased to perform his duties before the Board annuls his appointment, the date specified for the purposes of sub-section (10) may be any date after the date on which he ceased to perform his duties, but shall not in any other case be a date earlier than the date on which the Board annuls the appointment.



..."

5. On 6 February 1985, a delegate of the Public Service Board, acting under s.42 of the Public Service Act, appointed the applicant "as Typist Grade 1, Unattached, Australian Capital Territory ... with effect from the date of this instrument or the date of commencing duty, whichever is the later". The applicant commenced duty in the Department of Defence on the following day, her appointment being, pursuant to s.47 of the Public Service Act, an appointment on probation. Upon her appointment becoming effective, the applicant became an "eligible employee" for the purposes of the Superannuation Act and she paid contributions under, and in accordance with, the relevant provisions of that Act.



6. On 24 April 1985, the applicant, while still occupying a position of Typist Grade 1, was medically examined by Dr I.R. Pryor, a Commonwealth Medical Officer. On examination, the applicant was found to have recently exhibited mild symptoms and signs which were seen as making it probable that she was suffering from "repetition strain injury". Dr Pryor was not satisfied that the applicant met the medical fitness standard for appointment to the Australian Public Service or confirmation of such an appointment but was of opinion that she was likely to be medically fit for confirmation of appointment within 12 months of the date of her appointment but that she should be re-examined in 6 months to enable a final decision to be made.



7. Although the material before the Tribunal does not include a complete statement of her absences from work, it appears that the applicant was absent continuously, or almost so, from 29 April 1985 until 15 August 1986. Much, if not all, of this period was, it seems, covered by medical certificates which nominated "RSI" as the medical reason justifying her absence from work. It also appears that, in respect of these periods of absence from work, the applicant received payments of compensation under the Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees) Act 1971 (Cth) though on what basis does not appear from the material before the Tribunal.



8. On 4 June 1985, a delegate of the Public Service Board, Dr G. Quinn, determined that the applicant should be allowed to continue in her position subject to medical re-examination in 6 months to determine whether the fitness standard was met. It appears that he reached that decision on the basis that the symptoms were mild and that the applicant had not found it necessary to seek medical treatment.



9. On 23 August 1985, the applicant was again examined by a Commonwealth Medical Officer. This examination was conducted by Dr Travers who concluded that the applicant was not medically fit for appointment as she could not be regarded as medically fit to perform efficiently the duties of the position of Typist Grade 1 for a minimum period of three years. Dr Travers identified the condition from which the applicant suffered as "RSI" and expressed the opinion that she might be fit in 3-6 months for employment as a Clerical Assistant. On 26 August 1985, Dr A.P. Delivera, a delegate of the Public Service Board and an adviser to the Commissioner, determined that the applicant -



"is not medically fit for appointment, i.e. cannot be regarded as medically fit to perform efficiently the duties of the position for a minimum period of three years as a typist Grade 1. Defer 6 months and re-examine to determine fitness for appointment as a clerical assistant."



10. On 14 November 1985, with the applicant's consent, Milen Curtis White, a delegate of the Public Service Board, annulled, pursuant to s.47 of the Public Service Act, the applicant's appointment as Typist Grade 1, with effect from the close of business on 21 November 1985, and re-appointed her, under s.47B of that Act, as a Clerical Assistant, Grade 1, Department of Defence, unattached, with effect from 22 November 1985. That appointment was also, pursuant to the provisions of s.47, on probation (see sub-s.47(15)).



11. On 29 January 1986, a delegate of the Commissioner, pursuant to sub-s.16(4) of the Superannuation Act, issued, in respect of the applicant, a benefit classification certificate stating that, in the delegate's opinion, the applicant was not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with the physical or mental condition, namely repetitive strain injury, to continue to be an eligible employee until she attained her maximum retiring age.



12. On 16 May 1986, the applicant was again medically examined, on this occasion by Dr P.M. Purcell. For the purposes of that medical examination a document headed "Medical Report Form - Superannuation Act 1976" and known as a Form S6A was completed. Part of the form was completed by the Department of Defence and part by the applicant. The form comprised a number of sections, sections 1 and 2 (which were completed by the Department) setting out the applicant's name, postal address, date of birth, date of appointment and her current position in the Department. Section 1, as completed, indicated that the purpose of the examination was "Public Service Act Appointment/Superannuation". The other sections of the form (which were completed by the applicant) had the following headings -



Section 3 Applicant's statement of family medical history



Section 4 Applicant's statement of personal, medical, superannuation and other history



Section 5 History of medical examinations, advice or treatments



Section 6 Authority to exchange medical information



Section 7 Applicant's declaration



13. The applicant, in section 4 of that form, answered "Yes" to questions which asked whether she was suffering from, or had ever suffered from or received treatment, for -



. Nervous fatigue or feelings of weakness or tiredness



. Arthritis, rheumatism, gout or stiff joints.



She also answered "Yes" to the following questions -



. Are you aware of any circumstances regarding your health which would interfere with the satisfactory discharge of the duties of the position you occupy or have applied for?



. Have you been absent from work or full time education through illness or injury within the past 2 years?



In response to a further question seeking details of her affirmative answers, the applicant simply stated "RSI". On examination, Dr Purcell added the following comment in the space provided:



"RSI since late 4/85. Had symptoms for 2/52 before going off work - Pain in R arm, shoulder, wrist, neck. Saw Dr Chandran 2/12 ago. Had manipulation and physiotherapy."



14. As the examination was for the purpose of appointment under the Public Service Act as well as for superannuation purposes, the examining medical officer was required to complete, in conjunction with the Medical Report Form S6A, both parts of a form known as Form S6B. The parts of that form were respectively headed "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Superannuation Act 1976" and "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Appointment". The form contained the following:



"PURPOSE OF THE EXAMINATION



For appointment. The Public Service Board or other employing authority determines whether the applicant meets the appropriate medical standard. For the Australian Public Service (except for special occupations) the requirement is that the applicant is of sufficiently sound health to allow him/her to serve efficiently for at least three years without excessive sick leave; see Part II of Handbook for CMOs.



For Superannuation. The Commissioner for Superannuation determines whether a Benefit Classification Certificate is to be issued. A Benefit Classification Certificate specifies any physical or mental condition(s) because of which the Commissioner for Superannuation forms an opinion that the applicant is not likely to continue to be an eligible employee until attaining the maximum retiring age (usually 65 years).



To assist the Commissioner for Superannuation in making a determination, the General Medical Standard (outlined in Part IV of the Handbook for Commonwealth Medical Officers) has been designed to test whether or not the applicant can be expected to remain in employment until the maximum retiring age. Section 9 of the form requires that you take this fact into account when judging the applicant's present state of health.



Where you consider that the applicant meets the General Medical Standard but is unlikely to continue to be an eligible employee until attaining the maximum retiring age, or that the applicant does not meet the General Medical Standard but is likely to continue to be an eligible employee until attaining the maximum retiring age, you should provide the reasons for your view in paragraph 16 of Section 8 of the form."

15. Part II of the Handbook for Commonwealth Medical Officers, in the form in which it stood at the relevant time, was headed "Medical Standard and Procedures for Appointment/Employment". Section 3 within that Part dealt with "Permanent Appointment". Within that section the handbook relevantly provided:



"A. MEDICAL STANDARD



(1) An applicant may be considered to be medically fit for appointment to the Australian Public Service where:



(a) he meets the medical standard in Part IV, or



(b) in the opinion of the CMO, the applicant is of sufficiently sound health to allow him to serve efficiently for at least 3 years or, in the case of a prospective trainee or apprentice, for the length of the training period (including work on the job during training) plus a further 3 years. It is intended that this guideline be administered with flexibility. In making judgments in such cases it should be borne in mind that the applicant:



(i) should be able to carry out all the duties of the position; and



(ii) should not require more than normal periods of sick leave."



Then followed various paragraphs containing information which might assist medical officers to assess an applicant's capacity to serve efficiently for at least 3 years.



16. Part IV of the handbook was headed "The General Medical Standard for Superannuation Purposes". The introduction to that Part (section 8) included the following:



"The Standard is used to assist the Commissioner in forming an opinion as to whether a person is or is not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with a physical or mental condition or conditions, to continue to be an eligible employee until the person attains his maximum retiring age. That is, the Standard is related to the risk that an applicant for superannuation may retire on invalidity grounds or die before reaching maximum retiring age."



Then followed various sections dealing with such matters as height and weight, blood diseases, cardio-vascular system, central nervous system, eyesight, malignant diseases, respiratory system, skeletal and muscular system and miscellaneous disorders.



17. The "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Superannuation Act 1976" contained three sections, the numbering of which consecutively followed the numbering of the sections in Form S6A. Those sections and their respective headings were -



Section 8 CMO's record of examination



Section 9 Conclusion of the examining medical officer (CMO)



Section 10 Advice by the medical adviser to the Commissioner for Superannuation



18. The "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Appointment" contained three sections with the following headings -



Section 11 Applicant's details & signature



Section 12 Examining medical officer's conclusion



Section 13 Decision/determination of delegate/health adviser



19. In relation to limb movement, one of the matters referred to in section 8 of Form S6B, Dr Purcell noted -



"Weak extension of R 4th & 5th fingers. Tender over exterior [extensor] aspect R wrist and distal forearm."



To the question in section 9 of the form -



"As a result of your examination and consideration of the applicant's answers and other material available to you do you consider (having regard to the General Medical Standard as set down in Part IV of the Handbook for Commonwealth Medical Officers) that the applicant is NOT LIKELY TO CONTINUE to be an eligible employee until attaining the relevant maximum retiring age by reason of, or for a reason connected with, any physical or mental condition(s) referred to in this form or the Medical Report Form S6A?"



Dr Purcell answered "Yes". The condition or reason why Dr Purcell considered that the applicant was not likely to continue to be an eligible employee until attaining maximum retiring age was expressed as "Tenosynovitis". Section 10 of the form records that Dr D. Zonta, a Commonwealth medical officer acting as medical adviser to the Commissioner, agreed with Dr Purcell's conclusion.



20. In relation to the applicant's fitness for appointment to the Australian Public Service, Dr Purcell recorded, on a medical record card separate from Form S6B, that the applicant was not fit for appointment and did not meet the General Medical Standard. Section 12 of the "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Appointment" required the examining medical officer to state his conclusion by ticking the appropriate box. So far as public service applicants were concerned, the form provided:



"

Meets the medical fitness standard for appointment or confirmation of appointment;





OR FOR REASONS STATED BELOW EITHER:





is likely to be medically fit for confirmation of appointment within 12 months of the date of appointment (see Section 2 of S6A) but should be re-examined in .... months to enable a final decision to be made;



or





is considered medically unfit for appointment or confirmation of appointment."



Dr Purcell endorsed the form to show that the applicant was considered medically unfit for appointment or confirmation of appointment and made the following additional notation:



"Tenosynovitis (Has been off work continuously for over 12/12 without any redeployment)".



21. Section 13 of the form required the delegate of the Public Service Board to place a tick in the appropriate box. The relevant printed part of that section of the form was as follows:



"

I determine that the health and physical fitness of the applicant justifies his/her acceptance for appointment or confirmation of appointment under the Public Service Act;





OR





I consider that the applicant is likely to be medically fit for appointment but should be re-examined in ... months to determine whether the fitness standard is met;



OR





I consider that the health and physical fitness of the applicant does not justify his/her acceptance for appointment or confirmation of appointment."



That part of the form was completed by Dr Zonta who signed it as a Commonwealth medical officer and as a delegate of the Public Service Board. A tick was placed in the third box and the following comment made:



"On continuous sick leave since April 1985 for RSI."



22. The applicant was informed by letter dated 28 May 1986 that in the opinion of the Commonwealth medical officer she did not meet "the medical fitness requirements for appointment". She was afforded an opportunity to provide written comments or additional medical information before a decision was made whether her appointment should be annulled. The applicant did not take advantage of that opportunity.



23. On 31 July 1986, Raymond Walker Gill, a delegate of the Public Service Board, acting pursuant to s.47 of the Public Service Act, annulled the applicant's appointment as Clerical Assistant Grade 1, Department of Defence, with effect from the close of business on 15 August 1986.



24. At the request of the parties and before hearing any oral evidence, the Tribunal heard argument upon a preliminary question of law raised on behalf of the Commissioner that the language of sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act, in so far as it referred to an eligible employee whose services had been terminated otherwise than by retirement, was not apt to refer to an eligible employee whose probationary appointment had been annulled pursuant to s.47 of the Public Service Act. The Tribunal rejected the Commissioner's contention, ruling that an eligible employee whose probationary appointment was annulled pursuant to s.47 was not, by reason of that circumstance alone, to be treated as outside the purview of sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act. We set out in the schedule to these reasons the substance of the reasons on which the Tribunal relied in giving that ruling.



25. Upon the resumption of the hearing, the documentary material to which reference has already been made was supplemented by the oral evidence of Mr Gill, the delegate of the Public Service Board who made the decision on 31 July 1986 to annul the applicant's appointment.



26. At the material time, Mr Gill occupied the position of Assistant Director, Recruitment Operations Section within the Public Service Board. He said that in that position he dealt, on average, with about 10 annulments a week. He could not recall specifically making the decision to annul the applicant's appointment but had refreshed his recollection from the relevant file. He gave evidence as to the procedure that was followed in such cases and stated that, although he had access to the whole of the applicant's file at the time, the decision to annul her appointment was taken solely on the basis of the "recommendation" of the Commonwealth medical officer that the applicant "did not meet the general medical standard for appointment". In cross-examination and in answer to questions asked of him by the Tribunal, Mr Gill agreed that the recommendation of the Commonwealth medical officer to which he had referred was that recorded in section 13 of the "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Appointment". He said that he did not have before him the medical report card completed by Dr Purcell to which reference has already been made. He said that he had treated Dr Zonta's opinion as recorded in section 13 of that form that "the health and physical fitness of the applicant does not justify his/her acceptance for appointment or confirmation of appointment" as amounting to a conclusion that the applicant "did not meet the general medical standard for appointment". In answer to questions from the Tribunal, he gave the following evidence:



Q. Mr Gill, to what extent did you have any regard to the statement underneath that box that was ticked that you refer to that the applicant here had been on continuous sick leave since April 1985 for RSI?



A. I do not think I would have taken any regard to it, you know.



Q. Did you form any view as to whether the applicant was able to do the duties of a Clerical Assistant Grade 1?



A. No.



Q. Did you have any regard to the earlier part of that form which said that the applicant was suffering from tenosynovitis and had been off work continuously for over 12 months without any redeployment? Did that take any part in your decision-making process?



A. No. I was aware of it, but it did not take any part in the decision.



27. Mr Gill agreed that he would have read the "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Superannuation Act 1976" and the "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Appointment" for the purpose of satisfying himself that the conclusion expressed in section 13 of the latter form was not inconsistent with the material recorded in other parts of the document. He agreed in cross-examination that he would have read what appeared in section 9 of the document, a section relevant for superannuation purposes, and would have had no reason to question what was there stated. He denied that he had himself formed any medical opinion concerning the applicant.



28. Mr Gill also stated that, in making the decision to annul the appointment, he did not have regard to, or form any opinion about, the applicant's capacity to perform the duties of the position she occupied. He explained that, as the applicant had been absent from work for some 12 months prior to the annulment, there were no reports as to her work performance available to him and, therefore, no material upon which he could assess her capacity to perform those duties.



29. Neither Dr Purcell nor Dr Zonta gave evidence before the Tribunal.



30. It is convenient to deal first with an argument advanced on behalf of the Commissioner. It was submitted that a person could not properly be regarded as having been retired, or having had his services otherwise terminated, on the ground of physical or mental incapacity to perform his duties within the meaning of sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act unless, where the retirement or the termination of services took place under and in accordance with a statutory provision, that statutory provision mirrored the language of sub-s.7(1). The relevant criterion of operation of s.47 was said to be not the physical or mental incapacity of the person to perform his duties but the lack of satisfaction by the Public Service Board as to the person's "health and physical fitness". The absence of the requisite identity of language between the two provisions was said to be fatal to the applicant's claim.



31. We are unable to accept that submission. Reference has been made, in the reasons previously given by the Tribunal for its ruling on the preliminary question argued before it, to the diverse classes of persons whom the provisions of s.7 of the Superannuation Act encompass. We then concluded that it would accord with the perceived intention of the legislature not to construe the language of the Superannuation Act narrowly but to give it an ambulatory meaning and application, applying it, so far as its language will properly allow, to a variety of authorities and bodies and to diverse factual situations. We remain of that view. To confine the provisions of sub-s.7(1) in the manner contended for by the Commissioner would, in our view, subvert that perceived legislative intention and should not be adopted unless the language of the provision compels that result. We can discern no sufficient reason to read the sub-section in the manner suggested. In our view, what is required in any particular case is to examine the relevant circumstances in order to determine, as a practical matter of fact, whether the ground upon which the retirement or the termination of services was effected was the person's physical or mental incapacity to perform his duties.



32. We turn, therefore, to the question whether it can properly be said that the termination of the applicant's services effected by the annulment by Mr Gill of her probationary appointment as a Clerical Assistant Grade 1, Department of Defence was on the ground of her physical or mental incapacity to perform her duties. In considering this question it is important to keep well in mind that the scope of the inquiry which sub-s.7(1) requires is confined to the identification of the ground upon which the applicant's services were, in fact, terminated. It does not require, or indeed permit, an independent examination to be made of the applicant's medical condition at the date of the annulment of her appointment with a view to determining whether, at that date, she was unfit by reason of physical or mental incapacity to perform her duties. By contrast, an examination of that kind is required by sub-s.7(2) when a claim falls to be considered under that sub-section.



33. Counsel for the applicant submitted that, upon an examination of the material contained in the forms completed by Dr Purcell and Dr Zonta and in the light of the applicant's employment history between 7 February 1985 when she commenced duty as a Typist Grade 1 in the Department of Defence and 15 August 1986 when the annulment of her appointment as a Clerical Assistant Grade 1 took effect, the Tribunal should infer that Dr Purcell and Dr Zonta had concluded that the applicant was, by reason of her medical condition of tenosynovitis, incapable of performing the duties of her position of Clerical Assistant Grade 1. It was further submitted that, on that material, the Tribunal should reach the conclusion that Mr Gill, notwithstanding his evidence to the contrary, is to be taken to have annulled the applicant's appointment on the ground stated in sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act, namely on the ground of her physical incapacity to perform her duties.



34. The medical examination which the applicant was required to undergo clearly had more than one purpose. It was for the purpose of enabling the Public Service Board to make a decision upon the appointment of the applicant under the Public Service Act and to enable the Commissioner to make any necessary decisions concerning the applicant's membership of the superannuation fund administered under the Superannuation

Act.



35. So far as the question of the applicant's appointment under the Public Service Act was concerned, the relevant question for the Public Service Board was whether it was satisfied, after she had undergone a medical examination approved by the Board, as to her "health and physical fitness". It may be assumed that the Handbook for Commonwealth Medical Officers was designed to ensure, so far as possible, that the examining medical officer would report in such a way as to assist the Board in determining in a particular case whether it was so satisfied. The Handbook provided, in section 3 of Part II, that a person was to be considered to be medically fit for appointment if the person met the medical standard in Part IV of the Handbook, a standard prescribed for superannuation purposes, or if, in the opinion of the Commonwealth Medical Officer, the person was of sufficiently sound health to allow him to serve efficiently for at least 3 years. The Handbook also provided that, in forming an opinion on the latter question, the medical officer was to bear in mind that the person should be able to carry out all the duties of the position and should not require more than normal periods of sick leave.



36. Form S6B emphasised the second of the two alternatives mentioned above. It recorded that, for appointment to the Australian Public Service (except for special occupations), the requirement was that the applicant for appointment be of sufficiently sound health to allow him or her to serve efficiently for at least three years without excessive sick leave.



37. It seems to us that we may safely assume that Dr Purcell and Dr Zonta approached the matter in accordance with the guidelines provided by the Handbook for Commonwealth Medical Officers.



38. We are satisfied, from an examination of the forms completed by Dr Purcell and Dr Zonta, that in the opinion of those medical officers the applicant did not meet the medical standard in Part IV of the Handbook and was not of sufficiently sound health to meet the alternative requirement set out in section 3 of Part II thereof. It may, we think, be accepted that section 12 of the "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Appointment" where it refers to "the medical fitness standard for appointment or confirmation of appointment" reflects, in shortened form, what is said in section 3 of Part II of the Handbook. It follows that Dr Purcell, by ticking the third box in section 12 and adding the notation concerning the applicant's condition of tenosynovitis and her period of absence from work, was expressing the opinion that the applicant did not, for that reason, meet the medical standard in Part IV (that being an opinion also clearly expressed in section 9 of that part of the form relating to superannuation) and the further opinion that, for the same reason, the applicant was not of sufficiently sound health to allow her to serve efficiently for at least 3 years. Dr Zonta, by his notation in section 13 of the form may be taken to have been expressing similar conclusions.



39. It may well have been open to Dr Purcell and Dr Zonta, on the material before them, to conclude that the applicant was, in terms of sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act, incapable, by reason of her physical condition, of performing the duties of a position of Typist Grade 1 or, possibly, of a position of Clerical Assistant Grade 1. They did not, however, express themselves in those terms and, in any event, the ultimate issue is not whether they may have reached that conclusion, but what was the ground upon which Mr Gill acted in annulling the appointment.



40. Except in so far as it may be gleaned from the reference to s.47 of the Public Service Act, the instrument by which the applicant's appointment was annulled does not state the ground upon which that action was taken. However, we accept Mr Gill's evidence that his decision to annul the applicant's appointment was taken solely on the basis of Dr Zonta's conclusion expressed in section 13 of the "Examining Medical Officer's Report Form - Appointment" that the health and physical fitness of the applicant did not justify her acceptance for appointment or confirmation of appointment. We are satisfied that Mr Gill gave no consideration to the question whether the applicant was incapable, by reason of her physical or mental condition or otherwise, of performing the duties of a Clerical Assistant Grade 1.



41. The course of the argument before the Tribunal reflected some confusion concerning the medical standard to which Mr Gill was referring when he said in his oral evidence that the decision to annul the appointment was taken solely on the basis that the applicant "did not meet the general medical standard for appointment". At the time, it was not made entirely clear whether he was referring to the general medical standard set out in Part IV or to the provisions of section 3 of Part II of the Handbook. On reflection, however, we are satisfied that it was to the latter that he was referring. That is, we think, made clear by his reference to the standard as being referable to the process of appointment. It is not inapt to refer to the standard set out in section 3 of Part II of the Handbook as a "general" standard as it is made clear in the extract from Form S6B to which we have referred that that standard was to apply to all appointments to the Australian Public Service except for special occupations.



42. It may have been open to Mr Gill, on the material before him, to have assigned as the ground upon which the applicant's appointment was to be annulled the ground of her physical incapacity to perform the duties of the position of Clerical Assistant Grade 1 in the Department of Defence to which she had been appointed on probation. However, on the whole of his evidence and, in particular, in the face of his specific denial that that was the ground upon which he acted, evidence which we accept, there is no sound basis upon which we could draw the inference which would be necessary if the applicant were to succeed in having the decision under review set aside.



43. For these reasons the Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

17 The Tribunal expressed some concern as to the desirability of considering, in advance of the main hearing, the question as framed by the parties as it required a meaning and effect to be ascribed to the concept of an eligible employee having been retired, or his services having been otherwise terminated, divorced from the ground upon which such retirement or termination was effected. Put another way, the Tribunal was being asked to assume for the purposes only of deciding the preliminary question, but not for the purposes of the matter as a whole, that the applicant's probationary appointment as a Clerical Assistant, Grade 1 had been annulled on the ground of physical or mental incapacity to perform her duties and to determine, on that assumption, whether the applicant is, by reason of that annulment, properly to be described as a person who has been retired or whose services have been otherwise terminated within the meaning of those words in sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act. The justification for determining the question as a preliminary issue was said to be that to answer it could make any further hearing of the matter unnecessary with a consequent saving of time and expense as, if the matter were to proceed to a full hearing, the Commissioner wished to adduce evidence as to the applicant's medical condition at the time her appointment as a Clerical Assistant, Grade 1 was annulled, involving, so it was said, a hearing time of several days. It would appear that the purpose in calling the foreshadowed evidence would be to satisfy the Tribunal that, at the date of the annulment of the applicant's appointment as a Clerical Assistant, grade 1, she was not, by reason of physical or mental incapacity, unfit to perform her duties. It is unnecessary, for present purposes, to consider whether that evidence would be relevant to any issue arising upon the review by the Tribunal of the decision made on 17 October 1989.



18 In the event, the Tribunal decided to hear argument upon the question, reserving for further consideration whether it should be answered in advance of the main hearing. Having given the matter further consideration, the Tribunal is prepared to express its view as to the scope and application of the relevant words in sub-s.7(1) though, as will appear, it considers it appropriate to express its view otherwise than in terms of the question as framed by the parties.



19 It is significant, in considering the preliminary question, to observe that the language of s.7 is apt to encompass the whole class of persons who cease to be eligible employees before attaining their maximum retiring age. Sub-section 7(1) deals with a group of persons falling within that class, namely those who were retired, or whose services were otherwise terminated, on the specified ground. All other persons within the class fall within the purview of sub-s.7(2). Such a person will, however, only become entitled to receive invalidity benefit if the Commissioner is satisfied that, at the time the person ceased to be an eligible employee, the person was, by reason of physical or mental incapacity, unfit to perform his or her duties. The criterion of operation of sub-s.7(1) is, thus, clearly different from that of sub-s.7(2). The former depends upon an identification of the ground upon which the person was retired or had his or her services otherwise terminated. The latter depends upon an independent finding as to the medical condition of the person at the date the person ceased to be an eligible employee, independent that is of the circumstances in which such cessation came about.



20 Other significant matters, matters to which reference has already been made, are the wide variety of authorities and other bodies that are approved authorities for the purposes of the Superannuation Act and the fact that persons employed in a permanent capacity by those authorities or other bodies are covered by the superannuation scheme embodied in that Act. The terms and conditions under which such persons are employed may have their source in statute or in industrial awards or in contract or in a combination of those sources. In such circumstances, it would accord with the perceived intention of the legislature to construe the Superannuation Act as ambulatory in its meaning and application, applying it, so far as its language will properly allow, to a variety of authorities and bodies and to diverse factual situations. Clearly the language should not be construed narrowly.



21 Yet another significant matter to notice concerning sub-s.7(1) is that it identifies the group of eligible employees to whom its provisions may apply by reference to the circumstance that their services have been terminated. Although the provision refers to eligible employees who have been retired, it is clear from the word "otherwise" which qualifies the word "terminated" that action taken to retire an eligible employee is regarded by the legislature as but one method by which that person's services may be terminated.



22 In support of the submission on behalf of the Commissioner that an eligible employee whose probationary appointment has been annulled pursuant to s.47 of the Public Service Act can, under no circumstances, be regarded, by reason of such annulment, as a person who has been retired or whose services have been otherwise terminated, the Tribunal was referred to various provisions in the Public Service Act where the words "retire", "terminate" and "annul" or their derivatives respectively appear. We do not find it necessary to refer in detail to those provisions in these reasons. It was submitted that, because the various sections of the Public Service Act drew a distinction between the annulment of an appointment, the retirement of an officer and the termination of the appointment or employment of an officer or employee, the reference in sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation to a person having been retired or having had his services otherwise terminated was to be read in the same sense and with the same limitations as the corresponding words bore in the Public Service Act. The consequence was said to be that sub-s.7(1) did not encompass within its terms a person whose probationary appointment had been annulled.



23. We are unable to accept this approach for a number of reasons. It fails to take into account the circumstance, to which we have already adverted, that the Superannuation Act is not confined to providing benefits for officers and employees appointed or employed under the Public Service Act. Secondly, it tends to treat sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act as dealing with two separate groups, namely eligible employees who have been retired and eligible employees whose services have been terminated whereas, as noted above, the provision is dealing with one group only, namely those whose services have been terminated whether that termination is effected by retirement or otherwise. Thirdly, it fails to recognise that, when s.47 of the Public Service Act speaks of a probationary appointment being annulled, it means no more than that the appointment has been brought to an end or terminated. Finally, and most importantly, it fails to give to sub-s.7(1) the flexible and broad meaning which we think the legislature intended that provision to have.



24 In our opinion, it would not be an inapt use of language to refer to a person who had been dismissed from his office or his employment as a person whose services had been terminated and thus as a person falling within the language of sub-s.7(1). Equally, it is, in our opinion, not an inapt use of language to refer to a person whose probationary appointment under s.47 of the Public Service Act has been annulled as a person whose services have been terminated otherwise than by retirement. In this latter connection, it may be noted that the draftsman of sub-ss.87M(8) and 87N(9) of the Public Service Act appears to have proceeded upon such a view. Those sub-sections refer to a person whose employment has been terminated by reason of the annulment of his appointment while he was a probationary employee.



25 For the reasons set out above, we are of opinion that an eligible employee whose probationary appointment is annulled pursuant to s.47 of the Public Service Act is not, by reason of that circumstance alone, to be treated as outside the purview of sub-s.7(1) of the Superannuation Act. We need hardly add that, before sub-s.7(1) can be said to apply to such an eligible employee, it is necessary to answer in the affirmative the question whether the termination of his or her services was on the ground of physical or mental incapacity to perform his or her duties. As we understand it, that question remains open to debate in the present review.
