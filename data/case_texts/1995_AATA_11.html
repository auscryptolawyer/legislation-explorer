Maria Keller and Commissioner of Superannuation [1995] AATA 11 (27 January 1995)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

MARIA KELLER v. COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. V94/914

AAT No. 9972

Number of pages - 8

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

H.E. HALLOWES (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - jurisdiction - whether reviewable decision confirmed or varied - whether applicant ceased to be an eligible employee

Superannuation Act 1976 ss.7, 54E, 54H, 154

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 s.3

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 28 November 1994

27:1:1995

Counsel for the Applicant: MARIA KELLER

Counsel for the Respondent: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Solicitors for the Respondent: MR A HARDING

OFFICER (of the Review and Legal

Services Division)

ORDER

The Tribunal has no jurisdiction to decide this application.

The Tribunal therefore directs that the matter be removed from the list of matters to be heard and determined by the Tribunal.

DECISION

H.E. HALLOWES Under subsection 154(6) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") application may be made to the Tribunal for review of a reviewable decision that has been confirmed or varied. Subsection 154(6) provides:

"Applications may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal

for review of reviewable decisions that have been confirmed or

varied under subsection (4) and for review of decisions of the

Superannuation Board, or delegates of the Superannuation Board,

made under section 141 of the superseded Act."

The other relevant subsections of section 154 of the Act for present purposes are as follows:

"154(1) In this section:

'decision' has the same meaning as in the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal Act 1975;

'reviewable decision' means a decision of the Commissioner, or a

delegate of the Commissioner, under this Act, under the superseded

Act or under the regulations made under either of those Acts, and

includes a decision of the Superannuation Board, or a delegate of

the Superannuation Board (other than a decision under section 141

of the superseded Act);

'Superannuation Board' means the Superannuation Board established

by the superseded Act.

(2) A person affected by a reviewable decision who is dissatisfied

with the decision may, by notice in writing given to the

Commissioner within the period of 30 days after the day on which

the decision first comes to the notice of the person, or within

such further period as the Commissioner allows, request the

Commissioner to reconsider the decision.

(3) ...

(4) upon receipt of the request, the Commissioner shall reconsider

the decision and may confirm or revoke the decision or vary the

decision in such manner as the Commissioner thinks fit.

(5) ..."

2. On 5 October 1994 the applicant lodged an application with the Tribunal in which she stated that she was applying:

"...in pursuance of section 5 of the Administrative Decisions

(Judicial Review) Act, 1977 (Cth) and sub-section 21 (1A) of the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 to have the decision

described in paragraph 5 below reviewed by the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal.

. . .

5. The decision to be review is:

The decision made by the Commissioner for Superannuation dated 29

June 1994 not to retire the Applicant on the grounds of

invalidity."

The Tribunal has no jurisdiction under section 5 of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977. Subsection 27(1), not 21(1A) of the Administrative Appeals Act 1975 provides for applications to the Tribunal. On 16 June 1994 the applicant had written to the Secretary of the Commonwealth Superannuation Board of Trustees No 2 ("the Board") applying for invalidity retirement benefits under "section 7 of the Superannuation Scheme". She stated:

"I refer to my injury of 17th June 1992 which made me permanently

incapable to work and would like to apply for a payment of the

total and permanent disablement benefit (invalidity retirements

benefit consisting of a pension and a lump sum according to the

Section 7 of the Superannuation Scheme) from the Fund. ..."

3. The Tribunal advised the respondent of the applicant's application. By letter dated 21 October 1994 the respondent advised the Tribunal it was submitted that the Tribunal did not have jurisdiction to hear the application for the following reasons:

"2. The records of this Office indicate that no decision under

s.7(2) (of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") as amended)

with respect to Ms Keller has been taken by the Board. Subsection

7(2) of the 1976 Act provides as follows:

'(2) Where a person has, before attaining his maximum retiring

age, ceased to be an eligible employee otherwise than by reason of

death or by reason of his having been retired, or his services

having been otherwise terminated, on the ground of physical or

mental incapacity to perform his duties, but the Board is

satisfied that, at the time he ceased to be an eligible employee,

he was, by reason of physical or mental incapacity, totally and

permanently incapacitated within the meaning of Part IVA, the

Board may direct that the person shall, for the purposes of this

Act, be deemed to have been retired on the ground of invalidity

because of that physical or mental incapacity.'

3. Thus subsection 7(2) enables a former eligible employee whose

employment has been terminated on grounds other than invalidity

to have that termination deemed to be retirement on the grounds

of invalidity. The provision, therefore, has no application

whilst a person remains an 'eligible employee'. The records of

this Office indicate that Ms Keller has not yet ceased to be an

'eligible employee'.

4. I would add that even if a decision has been taken by the Board

and was therefore a reviewable decision within the meaning of

s.154 of the Act, it is submitted that the AAT, by operation of

s.154(6) of the Act and s.25(3) of the AAT Act, would not have

jurisdiction until such time as the decision was reconsidered

pursuant to s.154(4) of the Act.

5. It is also submitted that even if a decision had been made by

the Board under s.7(2) the AAT would not have jurisdiction to

review Ms Keller's application. Subsection 154(2) of the Act

permits a person affected by a reviewable decision who is

dissatisfied with the decision to request the Commissioner to

reconsider the decision. Subsection 154(6) of the Act provides

that an application may be made to the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal (AAT) for review of a reviewable decision after it has

been confirmed or varied by the Commissioner in the exercise of

powers conferred by ss.154(2) and 154(4) of the Act. A reviewable

decision is defined, inter alia, as a decision of the Commissioner

or the Superannuation Board created under the Superannuation Act

1922. However, s.154 of the Act does not extend to decisions of

the Board and hence it is submitted that the AAT does not have

jurisdiction to review Ms Keller's application.

6. Consideration has also been given as to whether a reviewable

decision has been made pursuant to section 54H of the 1976 Act.

Section 54H(1) provides that where the Board is satisfied that,

because of her mental or physical condition, an eligible employee

is totally and permanently incapacitated, it may decide to approve

the retirement of the eligible employee. Section 54E of the 1976

Act provides that an 'eligible employee' may make a request to the

Board for approval by the Board of the retirement of that person.

7. Mrs Keller wrote to this Office by letter dated 16 Jun 1994

requesting 'payment of the total and permanent disability

benefit'. This Office responded by letter dated 29 Jun 1994

advising Mrs Keller that if she considered that she should be

retired on the ground of invalidity then she needed to apply for

the issue of an invalidity retirement certificate pursuant to

s.54H of the 1976 Act. It is submitted that this letter did not

constitute a decision pursuant to s.54H of the 1976 Act."

4. At the hearing the Tribunal had before it the material already referred to above, together with further material lodged by the applicant including a copy of the letter from the respondent dated 29 June 1994 in which she was advised:

"I refer to your letter dated 16 Jun 1994 in which you applied for

'a payment of the total and permanent disablement benefit

(invalidity retirements benefit consisting of a pension and a lump

sum according to the Section 7 of the Superannuation Scheme) from

the Fund'. You also requested that your application be treated

urgently and requested "prompt payment".

Records held by this Office indicate that you have not yet ceased

to be an eligible employee of the Commonwealth Superannuation

Scheme and Australia Post has verbally confirmed that you are

still an employee of that organistation (sic), and are currently

on sick leave.

Consequently you do not yet have any entitlement to a

superannuation benefit as your superannuation benefit is payable

only upon your cessation as an eligible employee and is determined

by your mode of termination.

In this regard, if you consider that you should be retired on the

grounds of invalidity, you need to apply for the issue of an

invalidity retirement certificate, pursuant to section 54H of the

Superannuation Act 1976 (the Act). The issue of such a

certificate would then allow your employer to retire you on

invalidity grounds. Application for a certifcate (sic) is made to

Commonwealth Superannuation Board of Trustees No 2 (The Board) via

your employer.

The Board, before approving invalidity retirement, must be

satisfied that the eligible employee is 'totally and permanently

incapacitated' (TPI). This term is defined in Section 54B of the

Act which reads as follows:

'For the purpose of this part a person is totally and permanently

incapacitated if, because of a mental or physical condition, it is

unlikely that the person will ever be able to work in any

employment or hold any office for which the person:

(a) is reasonably qualified by education, training, or experience;

or

(b) could become reasonably qualified after retraining.'

Please note that the Board's primary consideration is whether or

not you are totally and permanently incapacitated for any

employment - as opposed to being unfit to perform the full duties

of your nominal position.

The medical evidence provided with such an application must be as

comprehensive as possible and must include current reports from

your treating doctors and a completed Form SM2 in which a

Commonwealth Medical Officer (CMO) or a Medical Officer approved

by ComSuper indicates that he or she has examined the person and

expresses an opinion that the person is, or is likely to become,

totally and permanently incapacitated.

For your information I have enclosed a procedures document which

outlines the invalidity retirement process.

Subsection 7(2) of the Act, to which you refer in your letter,

enables a former eligible employee who has terminated their

employment on grounds other than invalidity, to subsequently

request to have that termination deemed to be retirement on the

grounds of invalidity. It should be noted that subsection 7(2) is

a discretionary benefit and an applicant must not only satisfy the

Board that he/she is totally and permanently incapacitated (as

defined above) but also that discretion should be exercised in

their favour."

5. The applicant represented herself at the hearing. The respondent was represented by Mr A Harding an officer of the Review and Legal Services Division of the respondent's office. It was the applicant's contention that the Tribunal has jurisdiction to consider her application. The applicant told the Tribunal that she did not receive a response to her letter dated 16 June 1994 from the Board (paragraph 2 above). Instead she received the letter dated 29 June 1994 from the respondent (paragraph 4 above) and she felt that the purpose of that action was to confuse her. She contended in a letter to the Tribunal dated 2 November 1994 that, as her letter to the Board had been received by the Board on 20 June 1994, plenty of time had elapsed within which the Board could have made a decision. The applicant's letter dated 2 November 1994 continued:

"3. ...According to provisions of Australia Post Conditions of

Service, Australia Post (Postal Workers) Award 1985, Australia

Post General Conditions of Employment Award and Union of Postal

Clerks and Telegraphists -v- Australian Postal Commission (in

relation to wages and conditions) 'leave with and without pay

shall not exceed 78 weeks' after which period an officer

(permanent employee) should be automatically retired on the

grounds of invalidity (as leave can not exceed 78 weeks). It is

clear that I am no longer an 'eligible employee', but a 'former

eligible employee' as I was on sick leave and pension for more

than 78 weeks. The records kept by the Comsuper should be

therefore amended accordingly. If Comsuper will not do it I will

be forced to seek a Court order compelling them to do so.

4. It is again irrelevant. The Comsuper had plenty of time to

reconsider their decision and their failure to do so shows that

they failed to fulfill their statutory duties. I therefore would

like to ask the Tribunal to make an order that as the Respondent

failed to reconsider the decision in a reasonable time (more than

a month) therefore the AAT has a jurisdiction according to the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

5. It is a very clumsy attempt to pervert (the) course of Justice.

The Respondent refers to 1922 Act (without explanation why not to

1976 Act) but forgets about recent changes (like on 1 July 1990)

which transferred some powers of the Commissioner to the Board.

Anyway, letter of 29 June 1994 (Enc. 4) was signed by the

Commissioner so my appeal is against the decision of the

Commissioner and therefore the Tribunal has clearly a jurisdiction

to review my application.

6. It is clear that even if I was an 'eligible employee' (which I

am not), I could make a request to the Board for approval by the

Board of my retirement. My letter of 16 June 1994 (Enc. 1) is

clearly addressed to the Board and therefore should be accepted as

an application under Section 54H of the 1976 Act.

7. It is not clear which letter 'did not constitute a decision

pursuant to s. 54H of the 1976 Act'. My understanding is that

reference was made to the Commissioner's letter dated 29 June 1994

(Enc. 4) and therefore I submit that the latter constitute a

decision pursuant to Section 54H of the 1976 Act. It does not

matter if the latter Section of the 1976 Act was mentioned in the

letter of 29 June 1994 or not. What is important that this letter

consisted of decision(s) made by the Commissioner (either in his

own capacity or as an agent of the Board) and therefore those

decisions should be regarded (at least by default) as decisions

pursuant to Section 54H of the 1976 Act."

6. Mr Harding suggested that a letter sent by the applicant to her employer, Australia Post, dated 26 November 1994 could now be treated by the Board as an application under section 54E of the Act for approval of her retirement which may in turn be dealt with under section 54H. The applicant's letter dated 16 June 1994 had been presumed to be an application under section 7 of the Act but it had not been referred to the Board for decision as the respondent's records indicated that the applicant had not ceased to be an eligible employee, a pre-condition before the Board may exercise its discretion to deem the person to have retired on the grounds of invalidity under subsection 7(2) of the Act. Mr Harding said that the letter to the applicant from the respondent's office dated 29 June 1994 explained to her that subsection 7(2) of the Act had no relevance to her circumstances as she remained an eligible employee. He therefore contended that no reviewable decision had been made by the Board which, after being confirmed or varied under subsection 154(4) of the Act, could be reviewed by the Tribunal. The Tribunal, having considered the material before it, including subsection 154(6) of the Act, and having listened to the parties submissions, is satisfied that it has no jurisdiction to consider the applicant's application. Even if the letter to the applicant dated 29 June 1994 could be taken to be a decision of the Board under subsection 7(2) of the Act, and it is clear that it was not a decision of the Board but rather a letter of advice from an officer of the respondent's office, the decision has not been confirmed or varied by the respondent.

7. Subsection 154(6) of the Act also provides that the Tribunal may review decisions of the Board or delegates of the Board made under section 141 of the "superseded Act", that is the Superannuation Act 1922 (see section 3 of the Act). No such decision has been made by the Board under section 171 which was repealed by Act No 17 of 1978 the Superannuation Acts Amendment Act.

8. The Tribunal does not have jurisdiction to hear this application as there is no decision which has been made within the provisions of subsection 154(6) of the Act and, although the applicant may feel that there has been "plenty of time to reconsider their decision" (paragraph 4 of her letter dated 2 November 1994, reproduced paragraph 5 above) in fact, no primary decision has been made which could be reconsidered and the Tribunal is satisfied that the respondent has not refused, at this stage, to make a decision which could be determined to be a decision under subsection 3(3) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

9. During the course of the hearing the applicant requested that the hearing be adjourned pending action she has taken in the Federal Court of Australia, possibly under the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act. Mr Harding opposed the request for an adjournment and the Tribunal was satisfied that nothing was to be gained with respect to the issue of jurisdiction before the Tribunal by awaiting the outcome of the Federal Court action. The applicant remains concerned because she has written to the respondent and she has still not received what she considers to be her entitlement by way of superannuation payments having been on continuous sick leave for two years and four months, particularly in light of the Australian Postal Corporation's conditions of service (paragraph 3 of the applicant's letter dated 2 November 1994, paragraph 5 above). The applicant considers that the Australian Postal Corporation should retire her, thus making her a former eligible employee, and that it has been convenient for both her employer and the respondent to treat her as an employee because neither the respondent nor the Australian Postal Corporation are currently paying her anything. The Tribunal notes that the applicant is now in receipt of disability support pension under the Social Security Act 1991 and she advised the Tribunal that she is pursuing a claim for worker's compensation. The respondent's records indicate that the applicant is still a contributor to a superannuation scheme although the applicant considers that this is not so.

10. Mr Harding further explained to the applicant that, when she originally wrote to the Board seeking a decision under "section 7 of the Superannuation Scheme", she had received a reply (the letter dated 29 June 1994) from an officer of the respondent's office because the Commissioner's office provides administrative support to the Board. Instead of taking up the invitation in the letter to apply for the issue of an invalidity retirement certificate, as suggested in the letter, the applicant has complained to the Superannuation Complaints Tribunal. The applicant noted a document she had received from the Retirement Benefits Office which included a reference to "section 7 of Your Super Scheme", a reference to a section in a booklet entitled "Your Super Scheme" rather than to section 7 of the Act. The applicant understood, in making her application in her letter dated 16 June 1994, that she was doing all which was required of her by referring to "section 7" as suggested in the document she received. She had not looked at section 7 in the booklet and the advice contained therein. The applicant said that she had asked the respondent for the booklet but it was not sent to her. The respondent's officer, on the other hand, had understood the applicant's reference in her letter to be a reference to section 7 of the Act. The applicant's matter was not been placed before the Board because it appeared from the respondent's records that she had not ceased to be an eligible employee. Instead, she was sent the letter of advice dated 29 June 1994.

11. This application highlights the care which must be taken by officers in providing clear and concise information to employees or members of the public in pamphlets or booklets which paraphrase information and entitlements and outline procedures to be followed. It is also confusing for applicants who seek to have a number of issues resolved before a tribunal of limited jurisdiction. As the applicant was no longer receiving a salary it was her contention that the respondent should investigate whether or not she remained an employee. The applicant has clearly lost confidence in the administrative system with respect to compensation and superannuation payments and she has had to seek income maintenance under the Social Security Act. Mr Harding undertook to assist the applicant with further action she must take to have her entitlement under the Act considered. It has not been easy for the applicant to pursue any payment to which she may be entitled under the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988, the Act, the Social Security Act, and her conditions of service.

12. It is for these reasons that the application will be removed from the list of matters for hearing by the Tribunal. If the applicant is dissatisfied with any future decision made by the respondent under the Act and if the provisions of section 154 are satisfied, the Tribunal will then be able to consider her application.
