Whigham and Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner [1993] AATA 732 (3 December 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

SUPERANNUATION - whether eligible termination payment exceeds reasonable benefit limits - effect of rolled-over death benefit - calculation of reasonable benefit limits and eligible termination payment - whether an excessive component - whether special circumstances - Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987. REASONS FOR DECISION

(1) This is the determination of the Application for Review by Carol Mary Whigham of the decision of the respondent pursuant to the provisions of Sec. 15R of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 not to treat any part of an eligible termination payment made to Mrs Whigham as if it were within the reasonable benefit limits.

(2) In May 1989 the applicant's husband died. At the time of his death he was employed by the Queensland State Government. The applicant was entitled to her husband's death benefit through the Queensland State Superannuation Board.

(3) At the time of her husband's death Mrs Whigham was aged 45. She had two dependent children. She was in fact born on the 17 December 1943.

(4) The death benefit was payable to the applicant either as an annually indexed pension, as a lump sum, or as a combination of both these options.

(5) The applicant sought financial planning advice from Shellco Consultants. By document dated 15 August 1989 (Exhibit 2 in these proceedings) the Insurance and Superannuation Commission published an Information Paper entitled "Review of Administration of Reasonable Benefit Limits." This document set out the Government's decisions for changes to Reasonable Benefit Limits and the application thereto. This Paper also gave notice that amendments to the relevant statutes and regulations would be introduced to give effect to its decisions, and also announced that the date of effect of the new arrangements would be 1 July 1990.

(6) In October 1989 Shellco Consultants advised the State Superannuation Board that the applicant wished to roll over the lump sum death benefit due to her into the Oceanic Approved Deposit Fund. This wish was then put into effect. It would appear that the amount of the benefit so rolled over was $178,858.86.

(7) Later in 1989 the applicant consulted The Women's Investment Network, and on the advice of this organization Mrs Whigham transferred the whole of her funds from the Oceanic Approved Deposit Fund to the Macquarie Approved Deposit Fund.

(8) By undated letter sent in late December 1989 The Women's Investment Network (W.I.N.) wrote to the respondent's office. It is necessary to set out the text of this letter in full. It reads as follows:

"Attention: Mr Michael O'Neill

Subject: Request for clarification of Carol Whigham RBL requirements

and associated tax implications.

Dear Sir,

My client is in receipt of a reversionary eligible termination payment of $178,858.86 in her name, made up of 7486 pre i.7.83 days and 2240 post 30.6.83. This was paid to her in October 1989 by the Government Superannuation Office from her husband's superannuation fund after his death in June 1989 at the age of 46.

Mr. Whigham had worked for the education department since 1959 paying into the government Superannuation Scheme.

From information supplied to us and on current interpretation of ISC's guidelines, the service period applicable is that of her husbands (approx 1/12/59) whereas the salary for RBL purposes is to be hers (from 1/7/90 $25000).

Based on figures available to me this will make her liable to excess benefits tax implications from 1.7.90. Thus if she maintains the funds for retirement, as originally planned (until age 65), her total benefit could be $2,170,025 of which $1,537,132 would be taxed as income.

However, I have since spoken with Christine Gordon of your Sydney office, who is of the understanding that as a reversionary benefit, over which she had no control. it would not be included in her RBL calculations.

It would appear to me that this latter ruling is more alined to the aims of the present Government to encourage members to maintain retirement monies for the purpose intended.

I request on my Client's behalf, that a favourable assessment be considered to allow her to maintain the funds in a rollover situation for retirement purposes.

Mrs Whigham is currently not working, due to the emotional strain of her husband's death, however, she will be returning to the workforce in the new year to support herself and her family.

Should your[sic] have any further queries re the above, please contact the writer on 07 8314307.

Thanking you in advance for your prompt assistance in this matter.

Yours sincerely

Financial Planning Consultant

The Women's Investment Network"

(18) This letter dated 24 July 1990 should be set out in full. It reads as follows:

"I refer to the letter of 28 December 1989 from Ms Cobbin and your letter of 4 April 1990 concerning the superannuation benefits of your client, Mrs Whigham. I apologise for the delay in replying.

As you may be aware, the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 and the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations were recently amended to implement the new arrangements for the administration of the reasonable benefit limits (RBL).

The amendments to the Act provide that a payer of an eligible termination payment, superannuation pension or annuity is not required to advise the Insurance and Superannuation Commission of the payment where it is made as a result of the death of a person and is paid to the spouse or a child of that person within 6 months of the death of the person or 3 months of the granting of probate of the will or letters of administration of the estate whichever is the later.

The Regulations provide that where a payment is made to a person as a result of the death of another person and the benefit is a reversionary superannuation pension or annuity (that is, a pension or annuity which is the reversion of a pension or annuity which was in payment to the deceased person), none of the benefit is to be counted towards the RBL of the recipient. Where the benefit is not a reversionary pension or annuity, the whole of the payment will be treated as if it were reasonable even where it is in excess of the RBL of the person receiving the benefit.

Where a person has rolled-over a benefit to an approved deposit fund (ADF) which was originally paid to the person as a result of the death of another person, a withdrawal from the ADF is to be treated as a repayment of an amount to a depositor. Such a payment would not be covered by the provisions outlined above and would be assessed against the RBL of the person. This is because the amount has been deposited in the name of the recipient and has earned income in a concessionally taxed environment in the name of the recipient. Accordingly, if Mrs Whigham has deposited the amount received as a result of the death of her husband in an ADF, any withdrawal will be assessed against her RBL.

I would point out, however, that mere are transitional arrangements for persons who had amounts held by ADFs, like assurance companies or registered organisations at 15 February 1990. Under these transitional arrangements, the person's RBL is the greater of their RBL and the rollover amounts held by that person in all ADFs, life assurance companies or registered organisations at mat date. Where the person was 50 or more at 15 February 1990, the rollover amount includes any subsequent earnings on 15 February 1990 amounts in that ADF, company or organisation. or in another ADF, company or organisation or superannuation fund to which the amount has been rolled-over after 15 February 1990.

I trust you find these comments of assistance."

With future part time employment very uncertain, i believe I have no alternative but to withdraw from rollover investments so that if necessary I could at least maintain myself and my family through until I reach pension age.

(31) Apparently in compliance with sec.16 (2) of the Act the letter dated 13 October 1992 set out the following:

" In support of our request we would like to add the following comments to the case submitted by Mrs Whigham.

  2. On page 35 of the same information paper (copy attached), there was a statement made to the effect that a benefit paid on the death of a member would not be counted for RBL purposes and would not preclude further concessionally taxed benefits.

It was not until your publication of August 1990 that it was clear that the act of rolling over a death benefit would change die nature of the payment, ie. the act of rolling over the benefit converted it from a death benefit to a personal benefit of the spouse.

(32) Sections 16(3) and (4) of the Act provide as follows:

"(3) Upon receipt of the request, the Commissioner shall reconsider the decision and may, subject to subsection (4), confirm or revoke the decision or vary the decision in such manner as the Commissioner thinks fit.

(4) Where the Commissioner does not confirm, revoke or vary a decision before the expiration of the period of 21 days after the day of which the Commissioner received the request under subsection (1) to reconsider the decision, the Commissioner shall, upon the expiration of that period, be deemed to have confirmed the decision under subsection (3)."

(33) The respondent did not confirm, revoke or vary the decision within 21 days after he received the request to reconsider it. Thus he was deemed to have confirmed it.

(34) Sections 16(6), (7), and (8) of the Act provide as follows:

"(6) Applications may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of decisions of the Commissioner that have been confirmed or varied under subsection (3).

(7) Where a decision is deemed, by reasons of the operation of subsection (4), to be confirmed, section 29 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 applies as if the prescribed time for making application for review of the decision were the period commencing on the day on which the decision is deemed to be confirmed and ending on the twenty-eighth day after that day.

(8) Where a request is made under subsection (1) in respect of a reviewable decision, section 41 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for a review of that decision."

(35) By document dated 14 January 1993 Mrs Whigham lodged an Application with the Administrative Appeals Tribunal to Review the respondent's decision.

(36) The hearing of this proceeding took place in private (see sec. 16 (9) of the Act) in the Administrative Appeals Tribunal on the 16 November 1993. The Tribunal reserved its decision.

(37) The payment made by the Macquarie Approved Deposit Fund to the applicant of $205,050 on 14 November 1991 was clearly an "eligible termination payment" as that term is defined in Sec. 27A (1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. 1936 in (c) of that definition, which reads:

"eligible termination payment", in relation to a tax payer, means -

(c) any payment made by the trustee of an approved deposit fund in request of the tax payer by reason that the taxpayer is or was a depositor with the fund, not being a payment that is income of the taxpayer;

.................”

(38) One then should note the provisions of sec. 27B (3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 which provides:

"where an ETP is made in relation to a tax payer in a year of income, the assessable income of the tax payer of the year of income includes the excessive component. "

(39) One should then note the definitions in sec. 27 A(1) of "ETP" being "an eligible termination payment" and "excessive component" in relation to an ETP as "meaning the sum of:

(a) so much of the ETP as the Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner has determined exceeds the reasonable benefit limits; and

(b) so much of the ETP as represents the commutation to a lump sum of the whole or a part of an annuity or pension in compliance with an advice or request of the Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner under section 15S of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987".

(40) One next turns to certain provisions of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 ("the Act"). "Tax Act" is defined in sec 3(1) as the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. Part 111A of the Act is entitled "REASONABLE BENEFIT LIMITS" and in the Interpretation Section of that Part being Section 15 E (1), "eligible termination payment" is defined as having "the same meaning as in section 27A of the Tax Act", "reasonable benefit limits" is defined "in relation to an eligible termination payment, a Superannuation pension or an annuity, means the limits calculated in accordance with the regulations", and "payer" is defined as meaning "any person or other entity (other than a continuously non-complying ADF) that makes or is liable to make, an eligible termination payment, a payment of a Superannuation pension or a payment of an annuity."

(41) Regulation 4ZA(1) of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations provides as follows:

"(1) Where:

(a) the Commissioner is to calculate a person's reasonable benefit limits in relation to a benefit paid, or commencing to be paid, to the person; and

(b) paragraph 15F (1) (a) or (b) of the act applies to the person;

the person's reasonable benefit limits are to be the greater of the person's lump sum RBL or pension RBL (whichever of those is to be applied under regulation 4X or 4Z) and:

(c) if the person was at least 50 year old on 15 February 1990 - the sum of:

(i) the aggregate of the rolled-over amounts held in approved deposit funds, life assurance companies or registered organisations on behalf of the person on that day; and

(ii) the amount of earnings that have accrued after that day on those amounts while held in those funds, companies or organisations or in:

(A) other approved deposit funds, life assurance companies or registered organisations; or

(B) superannuation funds; and

(iii) the amount of earnings on the amount referred to in subparagraph (ii); or

(d) in any other case - the aggregate of the rolled-over amounts held in approved deposit funds, life assurance companies or registered organisations on behalf of the person on that day."

(42) Paragraph 15 F(1) of the Act provides:

"(1) For the purposes of determining the reasonable benefit limits of a person in respect of whom there was on 15 February 1990 an amount deposited with an approved deposit fund or a rolled-over amount held by a life assurance company or a registered organisation, where, on 15 February 1990:

(a) an approved deposit fund (other than a continuously non-

complying ADF) held an amount in respect of an amount deposited with it on or before that day in relation to a person;

or

(b) a life assurance company or registered organisation held an amount in respect of a rolled-over amount deposited with it on or before that day in relation to a person;

the approved deposit fund, life assurance company or registered organisation must, within such reasonable period after 1 July 1990 as is prescribed, give to the Commissioner, in such form as is approved in writing by the Commissioner, a notice containing such information as is prescribed concerning the amount held and the person's identity. "

(43) As paragraph 15 F(1)(a) applies to Mrs Whigham and as she was less than 50 years old on 15 February 1990 then the provisions of regulations 4ZA(1)(d) apply to her.

(44) The Macquarie Approved Deposit Fund duly complied with the provisions of Section 15 G(1) and 15 G(1A) of the Act by providing details of the payment of the eligible termination payment made to the applicant on the 14 November 1991. Such provision of details is evidenced by the documents T5 in these proceedings.

(45) In determining the applicant's reasonable benefit limit at 15 February 1990 as $184,433 being the aggregate of the rolled-over amounts held by Mrs Whigham in approved deposit funds, as at that date the respondent correctly applied regulation 4ZA(1)(d).

(46) Clearly the respondent was correct in determining the amount of the applicant's ETP at $205,050. Further as mentioned earlier it is not in dispute that apart from the provisions of sec 15R of the Act the excessive component amounted to $36,635.

(47) Section 15R of the Act provides:

"15R. Where:

(a) part of an eligible termination payment, a superannuation pension or an annuity would, apart from this section, exceed the reasonable benefit limits; and

(b) the Commissioner is satisfied that, because of the special circumstances of the case, the whole or a part of the payment, superannuation pension or annuity should be treated as if it were within the reasonable benefit limits;

the Commissioner may make a determination under subsection 15K(1), or an interim determination under section 15L, accordingly."

(48) It is the submission by and on behalf of the applicant that because of the special circumstances of the case the whole of the eligible termination payment should be treated as if it were within the reasonable benefit limits.

(49) As to the test to be applied in determining whether circumstances are special the Tribunal has been referred to the decision of Beazley J. in Tefonu Pty Ltd v I.S.C. (1993) 93 ATC 4727. This was an appeal from a decision of the AAT which had inter alia rejected an argument by the trustee of a superannuation fund that under sec. 13(1)(b) of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 "because of special circumstances that existed in relation to the fund during the year of income, it would be reasonable for the fund to be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions."

(50) At pp.4736,7 of the report of Tefonu Her Honour sets out the following:

"Senior counsel for the applicant accepted that the Tribunal had correctly identified the test to be applied in determining whether circumstances were special: See Re Beadle and Director-General of Social Security (1984) 6 ALD I and Minister for Community Services and Health & Anor. v. Chee Keong Thoo [1988] FCA 54; (1988) 78 ALR 307 at 324 where Burchett J said:

"Bearing in mind the care shown by the draftsman of c1 8 to avoid laying down any binding rules, it is particularly important that the broad discretions, created to give a lively flexibility to the administration of the scheme, should not by the gradual deposition of judicial decisions become fossilised into rigidity. Those discretions are intended to be applied to a great variety of situations. In such a context, the core of the idea of 'special circumstances' is that there is something unusual or different to take the matter out of the ordinary course, according to which the presumptions set out in the clause would be expected to apply. As a result, the ordinary course appears less appropriate or fair: of Crabtree v Hinchcliffe (Inspector of Taxes) [19721 AC 707 at 731 per Lord Reid; Jess v Scott (1986) 12 FCR 187 at 195; 70 ALR 185; R v Secretary of State for Home Department: Ex parte Mehta [1975] 1 WLR 1087; Re X and Adoption of Children Ordinance 1965 [1984] FCA 193; (1984) 2 FCR 533; Cortez Investments Ltd v Olphert & Collins [1984] 2 NZLR 434 at 437, 439. 441..."

However, it was submitted that the Tribunal had erred in law in the application of this test by failing to take into account the retrospective and transitional nature of the regulation, by failing to have regard to the reasonableness of the trustee's conduct, once it ascertained that there was a breach of the regulation, and in the manner it dealt with the trustee's ignorance of the proposed introduction of the standards and their eventual implementation."

(51) Her Honour went on to discuss in detail various factors which it was submitted amounted to "special circumstances". This discussion is recorded at pp. 4737 to 4740 of the report.

Her Honour concluded in that case that there was no error of law in the Tribunal's decision that there were no "special circumstances."

(52) This Tribunal is mindful that Tefonu was dealing with a different section of the Act than the sec. 15R which is the subject of the present application. However, we perceive no reason to distinguish the sections so as to prompt us to apply a different test to determine whether the circumstances are special than that applied by Burchett J. as approved by Beazley J. in Tefonu.

(53) The Tribunal notes generally the ISC Guidelines issued in June 1991 being Guidelines for the exercise of the "special circumstances" discretion in sections 13(1), 15(1), and 15C(1) of the Act. A copy of these guidelines is Exhibit 6 in these proceedings. One notes that section 15R is not specified in these guidelines the express purpose of which is to assist officers in determining whether "special circumstances" exist.

(54) In submitting that special circumstances existed pursuant to sec. 15R, Mr Clelland for the applicant relied on certain portions of the Information Papers published by the respondent to support his contention that confusion and misunderstandings were caused. It is alleged that these portions are ambiguous and unclear.

(55) Mr Clelland firstly refers to p 23 of Ex 2. He points to the following information:

"The Government recognises, however, that some people have retired and have made personal plans on the basis of the existing arrangements. Others are near retirement and have made similar plans.

To ensure these people are not retrospectively affected:

(a) all amounts withdrawn from rollover funds after 1 July 1990 by those who are aged 50 or more and who have benefits in rollover funds as at 15 August 1989; and

(b) any amounts held in rollover funds as at 15 August 1989.

will not be subject to the RBL.

This means that these amounts can be received after 1 July 1990 on a concessionally taxed basis regardless of whether they are less than or greater than the person's RBL.

Where those amounts are in excess of the person's RBL, no further concessionally taxed benefits can be received by that person."

Mr Clelland submits that this information would suggest that any amounts held in roll-over funds as at 15 August 1989 (later amended to 15 February 1989 as previously stated) will not be subject to RBL.

(56) However the whole section must be read. It begins on p.22 under the heading "Benefits in Rollover Funds." The fifth paragraph reads:

"The Government announced in the May 1988 Economic Statements that benefits provided by rollover funds would be subject to the RBL. All rollover benefits will therefore be subject to the RBL irrespective of whether a person is also a member of a superannuation fund or not."

The portion referred to by Mr Clelland clearly refers to people who have retired or are near retirement and refers to "these" people. The wording is clear and unambiguous.

Question 15 and the Answer thereto on p. 6 of Exhibit 3 is abundantly and simply clear. It reads as follows:

QUESTION 15

Page 23 of the Information Paper refers to two amounts ((a) and (b)) not subject to the RBL. Does (a) include amounts deposited in the rollover fund after 15 February 1990?

No. Only amounts in rollover funds at 15/2/90 are subject to special treatment. The amount in the rollover fund at mat date will be the minimum RBL for that person. For a person aged 50 or over at that date, his or her minimum RBL will be increased by the earnings on the 15/2/90 rollover fund balance."

The Tribunal then rejects Mr Clelland's argument as referred to.

(57) Mr Clelland next refers to p. 35 of Exhibit 2 and points to the following paragraph

as having the effect of creating misunderstanding:

"In the case of benefits payable to a surviving spouse on the death of a member, the benefit will not be counted for reasonable benefit limit purposes. This will ensure that the receipt of a death benefit by a surviving spouse will not preclude that person from further concessionally taxed benefits on his or her retirement."

It is submitted that this information could create the impression that such benefits even if rolled over would not be counted for RBL purposes.

(58) However, once again this appears to be a case of selecting a piece of information in isolation and attempting to use it out of context. The capital letter heading of p.34 of Exhibit 2 is "DEATH AND DISABILITY BENEFITS" and the portion emphasized by Mr Clelland is referable to such benefits. Roll-overs are not mentioned under this heading. Roll-overs are mentioned elsewhere as we have seen (at pp. 22 and 23 of Exhibit 2).

Clearly the document has to be read as a whole. Again Question 15 and its Answer on p. 6 of Exhibit 6 is relevant.

The Tribunal does not consider that the piece of information at p. 35 of Exhibit 2 as criticized by Mr Clelland is then reasonably capable of being misunderstood as he argues.

(59) Mr Clelland then suggests that paragraph 169 on p. 51 of Exhibit 4 is creative of misunderstanding especially in its final sentence. That paragraph reads:

"169. The Act and the Regulations prescribe special treatment in relation to death benefits. As indicated in paragraph 112 where a death benefit is received by a spouse or child of the deceased person within six months of the death of the person or three months of the granting of probate of a will or letters of administration of an estate, a notification of payment of the benefit does not have to be sent to the Commissioner. These benefits are not subject to reasonable benefit limits."

(60) However this once more is an example of selective isolating of a piece of information quite out of context. The paragraph chosen is under the heading "Death Benefits." Clearly the whole document must be read. Paragraphs 96 and 97 in this document in particular clearly have great relevance to Mrs Whigham's position. These paragraphs read as follows:

"Calculation of RBL for persons with Amounts in Approved Deposit Funds, Life Assurance Companies or Registered Organisation at 15 February 1990

96. Where a person had a rolled-over amount held by an approved deposit fund, life assurance company or registered organisation as at 15 February 1990, the reasonable benefit limits of the person will depend on whether or not the person was aged 50 or more at 15 February 1990.

Person Under Age 50 at 15 February 1990

97. Where the person was aged less than 50 at 15 February 1990, the person's reasonable benefit limit is the greater of:

  1. the person's lump sum or pension reasonable benefit limit (whichever is applicable)., calculated as set out in paragraphs 57 to 73; or
  2. the person's 15 February 1990 roll-over balance. For a person aged under 50 at 15 February 1990, this balance is the aggregate of the rolled-over amounts held in approved deposit funds, life assurance companies or registered organisations on behalf of the person on 15 February 1990.

This ensures that those amounts are not affected retrospectively by the application of the reasonable benefit limits to benefits provided by approved deposit funds and deferred annuity funds."

For the same reasons applied to his previous submissions then the Tribunal rejects Mr Clelland's argument relating to paragraph 169.

(61) In dealing with Mr Clelland's argument concerning this paragraph 169 Mr Robertson for the respondent made reference to paragraphs 109 and 110. In his reply to Mr Robertson's submissions Mr Clelland then suggested that paragraph 110 was another example of ambiguity, one to which his mind had not previously adverted. This attitude serves to be indicative of Mr Clelland's approach ie. to seek to find isolated pieces of information which could be construed as ambiguous or creating misunderstanding without having regard to the whole context or to whether or not such a piece of information actually carried ambiguity or misunderstanding to him, his client or to her previous advisers or whether it reasonably cause such difficulties.

(62) Generally and particularly with respect to all the contentions of Mr Clelland dealt with thus far it has to be borne in mind that at all material times the applicant had financial consultants as her advisers. This Tribunal has no reason to doubt the competency of those advisers and no suggestion of incompetency has been raised.

(63) Again it should be stated that there is no acceptable evidence that the applicant or anyone acting on her behalf was actually or reasonably misled or confused by any of the pieces of information criticized by Mr Clelland and above referred to.

(64) It is also suggested by Mr Clelland that the delay of the respondent in replying meaningfully to the letter sent by W.I.N. in late December 1989 should be regarded as constituting special circumstances for the purposes of Sec. 15R of the Act. This letter is set out in Clause (8) of these Reasons.

(65) A reading of p. 1 of this letter indicates that no exception can be taken to any of the views expressed. The first paragraph on p. 2 of the letter is of apparent significance. It reads:

"I request on my client's behalf, that a favourable assessment be considered to allow her to maintain the funds in a roll-over situation for retirement purpose."

(66) The writer of the letter did not inform the respondent in this letter or otherwise it would appear that the applicant's funds were already in a roll-over state. This is apparent from T12 set out in Clause (11) of these Reasons. Further, requests for the application of the ISC's discretionary powers were usually made at or just prior to the time the funds were to be paid as indeed subsequently happened (in November 1991). The respondent was not informed by W.I.N. in their December 1989 letter that Mrs Whigham was or was about to withdraw her funds from a rolled-over fund so that a favourable exercise of discretion was being sought.

(67) Thus it was far from clear just what ruling, assessment, or advice W.I.N. were seeking from the respondent by reason of their letter of late December 1989. The rather all-embracing letter of reply of 24 July 1990 from the respondent tends to corroborate that the respondent was unclear about precisely what was Mrs Whigham's position and what precise ruling, assessment, advice or assistance was being sought.

(68) It is clear enough that in June 1990 W.I.N. advised the applicant to redeem her rolled-over funds. It is also clear that the applicant was not prepared to act on that advice. Presumably when Maree Simpson was provided the telephonic advice from the respondent's office on the 29 June 1990 (see Clause (17) of these Reasons) this was relayed to the applicant. If it were not, one would reasonably expect to have been provided with evidence to this effect. Clearly, the applicant did not redeem her funds immediately following receipt of this advice.

(69) Of course the applicant chose not to redeem her funds after she received the 24 July 1990 letter from the respondent either. She chose to seek a "third opinion" this time from J.C. McIntosh Financial Planning, she chose not to withdraw her funds at any time thereafter until 14 November 1991.

(70) From December 1989 to the end of July 1990 it would appear the applicant was using W.I.N. as her financial advisers in relation to her fund. There is no reason to suppose as earlier stated that W.I.N. were other than competent advisers. These advisers both in their original letter of late December 1989 and in their fax (T 15) expressed a correct view of the law and in June 1990 advised Mrs Whigham accordingly: she chose not to act on their advice. She chose not to act on the written statement of the law as provided by the respondent's 24 July 1990 letter. Instead she chose to seek the third opinion previously referred to. She has been under the advice of this third set of consultants ever since. However, not until 14 November 1991 did she choose to have her funds redeemed.

This Tribunal rather tends to feel then that the applicant was unaffected by the alleged delay by the respondent in responding in any meaningful way to the W.I.N. letter of late December 1989.

(71) What significance in any event should be placed on the alleged delay in responding to W.I.N.'s December 1989 letter? The Information Papers of August 1989 and April 1990 were clear, comprehensive and unambiguous, the proposed amendments were explained fully and clearly, and competent financial advisers should have been able to advise the applicant with certainty as to what the law was to be. Thus any response from the respondent could only have been a form of repetitive confirmation of the position. A delay on the part of the respondent in these circumstances can not in our opinion be regarded as "special" according to the test we seek to apply as previously set out.

(72) Indeed having taken the approach as set out in Clauses (64) to (71) inclusive of these Reasons we this Tribunal are not satisfied that "special circumstances" have been made out by reasons of the alleged delay of the respondent in providing a meaningful response to W.I.N.'s letter of late December 1990.

(73) Over-all then this Tribunal is not satisfied that "special circumstances" have been established pursuant to sec. 15 R of the Act and therefore concludes that no part of the eligible Superannuation payment which exceeds the reasonable benefit limits should be treated as if it were within those limits.

(74) The Tribunal therefore affirms the decision under review.
