REASONS FOR DECISION

8 February 1988 Deputy President R.D. Nicholson

Decision Under Review

The Applicant seeks review of an objection decision made by the Respondent disallowing the Applicant's claims for a deduction in the 1984 year of income in respect of "scheme costs" of $88,223 and "legal expenses associated therewith" of $5,207. The Respondent Commissioner's reason for disallowing the claims was that no part of that expenditure constituted an allowable deduction pursuant to the provisions of sub-section 51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") because the amounts claimed were outgoings of capital or of a capital or private nature. In argument before the Tribunal the Commissioner relied only on the contention that the amounts were outgoings of capital or of a capital nature.

Evidence

In addition to documents and exhibits before it the Tribunal heard evidence from an employee of the Shire of Oceanway and the Secretary of the Applicant company.

Facts

A statement of agreed facts was filed by the parties which, amended to protect the identity of the parties and with additions in brackets where made appropriate by evidence, reads as follows:

"1. ABC Pty. Ltd. ("the Applicant") occupied premises situate at Lot 229 Truro Road, Baysville ("the Site") from August 1973 to April 1987.

  1. The Applicant purchased the Site, which comprised an area of or about 2.06 hectares, in August 1973.
  2. The Applicant sold the Site [but not its business] in April 1987 to XYZ Nominees Pty. Ltd.
  3. The Applicant at all material times carried on business at the Site as a cement tile manufacturer [and owned and occupied the Site for no purpose other than the production of assessable income].
  4. In the course of its occupation of the Site the Applicant made, inter alia, the following capital improvements to the Site in the period 1973 to 1981:

(i) the supply of electric power, namely by the extension of State Electricity Commission mains power to the Site;

(ii) the supply of water, namely by the sinking of a bore and the construction of both bore water and rainwater holding tanks on the Site;

(iii) the provision of sewerage facilities, by the construction of septic tanks and associated piping;

(iv) provision of adequate drainage by the construction of a sump and associated piping;

(v) the construction of a bitumen driveway, and parking area, providing the access to the factory via Vera Road, which carried heavy vehicle and employee traffic;

(vi) the construction of a driveway, and customer parking area, providing access via Truro Road for office traffic.

  1. The Site subsequently came within the Shire of Oceanway Town Planning Scheme No. 8 - Baysville Industrial Development Scheme ("the Scheme") which was a Town Planning Scheme proposed by the Shire of Oceanway ("the Shire") in 1981 and which became law upon Gazettal on ... January 1982.
  2. The General Objects of the Scheme as set out in the Government Gazette were:
    • (a) To plan, facilitate and encourage the progressive subdivision and development of the land within the Scheme Area for industrial and other purposes;
    • (b) To co-ordinate and control development in the Scheme Area;
(c) To plan and make provision for suitable roads and ways within the Scheme Area;

(d) To make provision for the upgrading of the portion of Abercrombie Drive within the Scheme Area;

(e) To make provision for and ensure the proper drainage of the roads and all other parts of the Scheme Area which require drainage;

(f) To make provision for the creation of sewerage and drainage reserves and easements within the Scheme Area;

(g) To make provision for the reticulated supply of water and all works and matters incidental thereto within the Scheme Area;

(h) To make provision for reticulated deep sewerage and all works and matters incidental thereto within the Scheme Area;

(i) To make provision for power supply within the Scheme Area;

(j) To make provision for land to be used for recreation, Local Authority and Scheme purposes, and for the development thereof within the Scheme Area;

(k) To make provision for landscaping and beautification of the land surrounding any compensating basin or drainage sump within the Scheme Area or any compensating basin or drainage sump acquired or used outside the Scheme Area for the purpose of the Scheme;

(l) To establish some standards for the development of land within the Scheme Area;

(m) To make provision within the Scheme Area for such of those matters set out in the First Schedule of the Town Planning and Development Act, 1928 (as amended) (hereinafter called "the Act") as are necessary or incidental to the good and effective planning subdivision and development of an industrial development scheme;

(n) To improve and secure the amenity, health and convenience of the Scheme Area and to make provision for works calculated to achieve those ends.

[Works done pursuant to these objectives were of a capital nature.

  1. In or about the end of 1970 or early 1971 Vera Road and Box Road, both public roads, were sealed to a width of 7.4 metres.
  2. Pursuant to the Scheme, the following public works, inter alia, were done on or in relation to the land within the Scheme Area:-
    • (a) The provision of and access to:
      • (i) reticulated supply of water;
    • (ii) reticulated deep sewerage;

(iii) the Scheme drainage system;

(b) The sealing and upgrading of Truro Road and the upgrading of Vera Road and Box Road and the constructing and sealing of Target Road. The bitumen surfaces of Vera and Box Roads were extended from 7.4 metres width to 10 metres. Truro Road was sealed to a width of 10 metres and kerbing and drainage installed, and these roads were given a 5 metre wide road reserve either side of the bitumen;

(c) The acquisition of land for the purpose of road and drainage reserves;

(d) Earthworks associated with the provision of drainage including the landscaping of public drainage sumps constructed.
  1. The Shire prepared a budget for the cost of the works which were to be accomplished pursuant to the Scheme. The Shire opened an overdraft account with the Westpac Bank to provide the funds to expend in anticipation of the proposed Scheme becoming law and costs being recouped from the landowners.
  2. Pursuant to the Scheme, land was acquired by the Shire and utilised for the construction of a drainage system.
  3. Pursuant to the Scheme, sewer mains and reticulation were constructed upon drainage and road reserves and easements in the Scheme area.
  4. Abercrombie Drive was constructed and sealed in or about the end of 1979 or early 1980 prior to the existence of the Scheme and pursuant to an agreement between the Shire and ... Councils [of adjacent Shires] and the developers of [an] Estate and the costs were shared by those parties.
  5. The junction of Abercrombie Drive and Vera Road was constructed and sealed at the expense of the Shire in or about the end of 1979 or early 1980 prior to the existence of the Scheme.
  6. Part of the funds obtained from the landowners pursuant to the Scheme were utilised by the Shire to recoup part of its outlay in respect of its share of the cost of construction and sealing of Abercrombie Drive and the cost of construction and sealing the Vera Road/Abercrombie Drive junction.
  7. The Scheme provided that the total cost of carrying out the Scheme was to be paid by the owners of land situated within the Scheme Area in proportion to the ratio that the area of each owners subdivisible land bore to the total area of subdivisible land within the Scheme Area, and that each owner was to pay his appropriate portion of the Scheme costs, or such part thereof, as the Shire from time to time required.
  8. The Scheme did not provide for subdivision of the Site however the Applicant's pre-existing right to apply for subdivision, either alone or in conjunction with other owners, continued albeit now regulated by the Scheme.
  9. The proportion of the Scheme costs payable to date by the Applicant is $88,223.42 ("the Contribution"). Although the Applicant was not required to contribute any further to the Scheme costs prior to selling the Site a final accounting for all scheme costs has not yet occurred and the present owner of the Site will be required to meet those costs.
  10. By way of Writ No. ... of 1982 the Applicant contested the proposed Scheme in the Supreme Court of Western Australia, seeking a declaration that the Scheme was invalid. In doing so the Applicant incurred legal expenses of $5,207.00 which were paid in April 1984. The matter was heard by [a judge] who directed that the Applicant's claim for a declaration and an injunction be dismissed.
  11. The Applicant had no proprietary interest in any of the facilities provided by the Shire in the Scheme Area. The Shire effected no direct works on the Applicant's site.
  12. SEC mains power was provided to the Site at the Applicant's expense eight years before the Scheme came into operation, and furthermore Scheme works in respect of power supplies in the Scheme Area were confined to relocation of poles and lines.
  13. The property at all material times operated its own septic system and no connection has been made to the sewer mains.
  14. Bore water was at all material times utilized in the cement tile manufacturing process. From November 1985 mains water was available if required.
  15. At all material times no connection was ever made to the Scheme drainage system and at all material times the property had a sump which adequately accommodated all waste water flows.
  16. In November 1985 the Applicant expended $1,719 in plumbing costs to connect the reticulated supply of water provided pursuant to the Scheme throughout the Scheme area to certain facilities on the Site:

(a) A drinking fountain in the factory canteen;

(b) The factory employees' toilet and shower amenities;

(c) The factory's fire reel service;

(d) The sprinklers located in the factory rafters for use in the manufacturing process, although by the contemporaneous insertion of a bypass valve in the on-site network of water pipes, provision was made to use the bore water or mains water in the sprinkler system with equal facility.
  1. The Scheme costs to date have been paid in four installments as follows:-

(i) 1.2.83 $20,000.00

(ii) 14.1.84 $20,000.00

(iii) 1.4.84 $20,000.00

(iv) 4.5.84 $28,223.42

  1. The Contribution of $88,223 and the legal expenses of $5,207 were treated by the Applicant as expense items in its profit and loss statement for the year ended 30 June 1984, and written off against total income with no asset or asset revaluation appearing in the balance sheet in respect of the expenditures.
  2. No portion of the Contribution was expended by the Shire at the request of or on behalf of the Applicant."

The liability of the Applicant to contribute towards the cost of the Scheme referred to in paragraph 16 of the agreed statement arose pursuant to paragraph 7.1.2(d) of the Scheme which provided that each Owner shall pay his appropriate portion of the Scheme Costs "when called upon by the Council to make payment as provided hereafter in this clause". Clause 7.1.3 of the Scheme provided that an Owner shall be liable to pay his appropriate proportion or part thereof "as the Council from time to time requires as from the date of posting to him by or on behalf of the Council by prepaid post addressed to his last address known to the Council of a notice informing him of the amount of his appropriate proportion or the part then required or calling upon him to make payment".

By letter dated 1 July, 1983 addressed to the Applicant the Shire forwarded to the Applicant an Advice of Estimated Scheme Costs in respect of his land calculated on the basis that payment would be made either by 12 equal monthly installments commencing on or before 31 July 1983; or by 2 equal installments on or before 31 October 1983 and 31 March 1984; or by one single payment on or before 31 December 1983. The letter stated;

"If no payments have been made by March 31, 1984, and arrangements acceptable to Council for repayment of your Scheme costs have not been made by that date, Council will have no alternative but to serve formal notice for payment of such costs, including penalty interest rates, in accordance with the provisions of the Scheme which, based on current interest rates could be 19.75% per annum."

The Applicant did not adopt either of the first two alternative modes of payment and negotiated with the Shire for one single payment in four installments payable in the manner set out in item 26 of the above agreed statement. On behalf of the Applicant it was contended that the letter of 1 July 1983 from the Shire to the Applicant was a formal notice for the purposes of paragraph 7.1.2(d) and 7.1.3 of the Scheme. In the view of the Tribunal, that is correct. The letter informed the Applicant of the amount of his appropriate proportion and called upon him to make payment. The reservation of the alternative to serve formal notice was directed to the mode of payment if the Applicant did not accept one of the 3 modes of payment offered in the letter. The reservation of this further option does not diminish the character of the letter as one which satisfies the requirements of paragraph 7.1.2(d) and 7.1.3.

Advantages which accrued to the Applicant's business (in which his property was employed) as a consequence of the implementation of the Scheme were as follows:

(a) In respect of sewerage -

(i) the potential for the Applicant (as Owner) or a purchaser to connect to reticulated deep sewerage;

(ii) avoidance of pollution of the water control area under the land as a consequence of other land being on deep sewerage;

(b) In respect of drainage -

(i) the potential for connection to public drainage;

(ii) the freeing up of that part of the Applicant's land as was used for the sump;

(c) In respect of mains water -

(i) access to fluoridated water;

(ii) avoidance of the necessity to cleanse the factory roof (from which water was previously collected);

(iii) avoidance of necessity for office workers to bring their own water to work as was previously done;

(iv) avoidance of rationing of water supplies at time of drought;

(v) ability to avoid the danger of staining on light coloured tiles previously reported by the Government Chemical Laboratories as a hazard of use of the bore water as a spray during the curing process;

(vi) an enhanced fire safety service as a consequence of the ability to use mains water or bore water;

(d) In respect of new roads and upgrading of existing roads -

(i) the sealing, curbing and upgrading of Truro Road to industrial standards;

(ii) the increased width of bitumen on Vera Road from 7.4 metres to 10 metres, providing better access for industrial vehicles;

(iii) general improvement in accessibility for customers, employees and suppliers of the Applicant;

(e) In respect of beautification works -

provision of landscaping and beautification of land in the area, enhancing the approach to the Applicant's property;

(f) In respect of subdivision potential -

the likelihood that any reasonable request for subdivision would not be refused by the Shire.

The evidence was that the implementation of the Scheme had not resulted in an increase in land values as originally anticipated by the Shire because of a general recession. However, in the last 6 months there had been a significant increase in land values in the Scheme area.

The Scheme was brought in as a result of pressure upon the Shire from owners other than the Applicant and the Applicant at all times opposed the introduction of the Scheme.

Sub-section 51(1)

The Respondent concedes that the outgoings here in issue come within the second limb of sub-section 51(1) of the Act, namely that they are "necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income". As has been seen, however, the Respondent says that the outgoings fall within the exception which then follows for outgoings "of capital, or of a capital, ... nature". It is agreed by both the Applicant and the Respondent that in the event of the Scheme cost outgoings not being capital, the legal expense outgoings will similarly not be capital: see Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Snowden and Willson Proprietary Limited [1958] HCA 23; (1958) 99 CLR 431; Cliffs International Inc. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation 79 ATC 4059 at 4066? Case B31 70 ATC 148; and Case B74 70 ATC 348.

In the view of the Tribunal, the essential nature of the outgoing in question is correctly described as a capital contribution towards the Scheme costs. Although it was paid in four installments the contribution was nevertheless a once and for all payment and more than that, it was expenditure which brought into existence an advantage for the enduring benefit of the Applicant and its business (British Insulated and Helsby Cables Limited v. Atherton [1926] AC 205). The contributions to the Scheme costs reflect "the distinction between the business entity, structure, or organization set up ' or established for the earning of profit and the process by which such an organization operates to obtain regular returns by means of regular outlay", per Dixon J., in Sun Newspapers Limited and Associated Newspapers Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1938] HCA 72; (1938) 61 CLR 337 at 359.

On behalf of the Applicant it was argued, however, that this is not enough to establish the contribution to Scheme costs as capital and that, in addition, it must be shown that the Applicant had the purpose of attaining the advantage in making that contribution; it being further argued that because the contribution was exacted by compulsion, no such purpose existed in this instance. The argument is founded specifically on words in the above authorities in particular. For example, reference was made to the well-known passage in the judgment of Dixon J. in Sun Newspapers (supra) at 363 reading:

"There are, I think, three matters to be considered, (a) the character of the advantage sought, and in this its lasting qualities may play a part, (b) the manner in which it is to be used, relied upon or enjoyed, and in this and under the former head recurrence may play its part, and (c) the means adopted to obtain it; that is, by providing a periodical reward or outlay to cover its use or enjoyment for periods commensurate with the payment or by making a final provision or payment so as to secure future use or enjoyment." (Emphasis added)

It is said that the use of the word "sought" and the words "so as to" connote the necessity to ascertain the purpose sought by the taxpayer. Likewise, with reference to the equally well-known citation from British Insulated and Helsby Cables (supra) per Viscount Cave L.C. at 213 reading:

"But when an expenditure is made, not only once and for all, but with a view to bringing into existence an asset or an advantage for the enduring benefit of a trade, I think that there is very good reason (in the absence of special circumstances leading to an opposite conclusion) for treating such an expenditure as properly attributable not to revenue but to capital." (Emphasis added)

it was argued that the words "with a view to" emphasised the same necessity for a purpose to be held by the taxpayer. Similarly in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Ampol Exploration Limited 86 ATC 4859 at 4879 Beaumont J. said "the relevant inquiry is to determine for what object or purpose the taxpayer paid the moneys".

To so treat these dicta is, in the view of the Tribunal, to regard them as statutory tests. As was said by Starke J. in Hallstroms Proprietary Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1946] HCA 34; (1946) 72 CLR 634 at 644, none of the so-called definitions or tests or any other definitions or tests suggested by the cases are decisive. In BP Australia Limited v. Commissioner of Taxation [1965] UKPCHCA 2; (1965) 112 CLR 386 at 397 their Lordships said:

"The solution to the problem is not to be found by any rigid test or description. It has to be derived from many aspects of the whole set of circumstances some of which may point in one direction, some in the other

• • •"

"One may approach the problem by considering the first of the matters mentioned by Dixon J. above, namely the character of the advantage sought, and in this both its lasting qualities and the fact of recurrence may play their parts. Under this head one might also take account of the nature of the need or occasion which calls for the expenditure (Dixon J. in Hallstroms' case)."

Looking at "the whole set of circumstances" in this proceeding, it appears to the Tribunal that on payment of the contribution to Scheme costs, the Applicant brought into existence advantages for the enduring benefit of its established business: cf. Sun Newspapers (supra) per Dixon J. at 361. They were lasting advantages of a once and for all nature which procured rights of a permanent nature. To conclude that the contribution to Scheme costs is capital is entirely consistent, not only with the tests as enunciated in the abovementioned classic dicta and discussed recently by the High Court in Cliffs International (supra) and by a Full Court of the Federal Court in Ampol Exploration Limited (supra), but also with the decisions in Case 103 9 CTBR (N.S.) 681 (costs of extending electricity supply); Case M13 (1961) 12 TBRD 82 (obiter dicta concerning costs of road work fronting company's land) and Case U68 87 ATC 435 (contribution to local council for sealing of road near taxpayer's factory). In this latter case the benefit was found to be the enjoyment of the sealed road with its enduring qualities, intended to be used indefinitely in the manner of a fixed asset of a capital nature. Furthermore the outgoings do not have a quality of recurrence cf. Pitt (Inspector of Taxes) v. Castle Hill Warehousing Co. Ltd. [1974] 3 All ER 146 at 152 per Megarry J.

The statement of agreed facts refers to the accounting treatment of the outgoings by the Applicant as expense items. As was said in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. James Flood Proprietary Limited [1953] HCA 65; (1953) 88 CLR 492 at 506 "commercial and accountancy practice may assist in ascertaining the true nature and incidence of the item as a step towards determining whether it answers the test laid down by s. 51(1) but it cannot be substituted for the test"; see also John Fairfax & Sons Proprietary Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1959] HCA 4; (1959) 101 CLR 30 at 46 per Menzies J. citing Broken Hill Theatres Proprietary Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1952] HCA 75; (1952) 85 CLR 423 at 435 and Ward & Co. Limited v. Commissioner of Taxes (1923) AC 145 at 149; and Arthur Murray (NSW) Pty. Limited v. Commissioner of Taxation [1965] HCA 58; (1965) 114 CLR 314 at 320.

The Tribunal being of the opinion that the outgoings constituted by the contribution to Scheme costs are capital, it follows that the outgoings relating to legal expenses in connection therewith are also capital.

Conclusion

For the reasons given the Tribunal will affirm the objection decision under review.
