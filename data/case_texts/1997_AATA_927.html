Pribac and Commissioner for Superannuation [1997] AATA 927 (25 August 1997)

Last Updated: 21 January 2009





Administrative

Appeals

Tribunal



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION NO 12142

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No A97/198

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )



Re HUMBERT VICTOR PRIBAC

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal Senior Member J. A. Kiosoglous, MBE

Date 25 August 1997

Place Canberra

Decision The Tribunal determines that it does not have jurisdiction to hear the application for review.



(Sgd)

J.A. Kiosoglous

(Senior Member)



CATCHWORDS

PRACTICE & PROCEDURE - Jurisdiction - whether AAT has jurisdiction to review decision of the Commonwealth Superannuation Board - no reviewable decision pursuant to s.154 Superannuation Act 1976 - no jurisdiction.



Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 sub-s.25(4)

Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 s.5

Superannuation Act 1976 ss.7, 153AM, 153AD(3), 154

Superannuation Benefits (Supervisory Mechanisms) Act 1990 sub-s.9(9), s.10

Superannuation (Resolution of Complaints) Act 1993 sub-s.14(6A)



Duncan v Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Authority 3ALD 114

Collector of Customs (NSW) v Lawlor Automotive Pty Ltd [1979] FCA 21; 24 ALR 307

REASONS FOR DECISION

25 August 1997 Senior Member J. A. Kiosoglous MBE



  1. This is an application by Mr Pribac for review of a decision of the Commonwealth Superannuation Board (“CS Board”) dated 5 September 1991, which deemed Mr Pribac not to have retired on the grounds of invalidity. At the same time as lodging his application Mr Pribac also sought an extension of time within which to lodge his application. Upon receipt of the application for review of the decision of the CS Board and the application for extension of time the respondent indicated in writing that the Tribunal did not have jurisdiction to hear the matter. Accordingly the Tribunal brought on the matter by way of directions hearing to deal with the issue of jurisdiction, and in the event of finding that there was jurisdiction to then consider the application for extension of time. With the consent of the parties the Tribunal proceeded to deal with the jurisdictional issue.
  2. The Tribunal received into evidence six exhibits, one tendered by the applicant (Exhibit 1) and five by the respondent (Exhibits A-E). The applicant represented himself, and was assisted by Mr T. Bartos. The respondent was represented by Mr B. Dube, a departmental advocate.
  3. The history, briefly, is the applicant was an employee of the Department of Health and Community Services. As such, he was a contributor to the Comsuper scheme from 2 May 1966. On 18 March 1988 the applicant retired, and on 3 December 1989 he applied pursuant to sub-s.7(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 (“1976 Act”) to be deemed to have been retired on the ground of invalidity. This matter was determined by the CS Board on 5 September 1991, which deemed him not to have retired on the ground of invalidity. The decision was reconsidered by the CS Board on 7 March 1996 and affirmed. On 17 May 1996 the applicant applied for review of the decision of 7 March 1996 to the Superannuation Complaints Tribunal (“SCT”).
  4. On 16 June 1997 the SCT notified the applicant and the respondent that it considered it did not have jurisdiction to review the CS Board’s decision, finding that the applicant’s complaint related to an issue of total and permanent disability and as the original decision was made prior to 1 November 1994 it did not have jurisdiction. The SCT made this decision on the basis of sub-s.14(6A) of the Superannuation (Resolution of Complaints) Act 1993 which provides that it cannot deal with a complaint under this section in relation to a decision of the CS Board with respect to payment of disability benefit because of total and permanent disability unless the decision is made on or after 1 November 1994. The applicant then made application to this Tribunal seeking a review of the CS Board’s decision. In issue is whether this Tribunal has jurisdiction to hear this matter.
  5. The applicant in his submissions stated that the Tribunal did have jurisdiction. The respondent, however, submitted that this Tribunal only has jurisdiction to review decisions of the Commissioner for Superannuation and not to consider decisions of the CS Board.
  6. Mr Dube, in his submissions, stated that the respondent believed that the SCT did have jurisdiction to review the decision of the CS Board. The applicant stated that he was not told of any appeal rights from the decision of the SCT, although he has subsequently found that correspondence was directed to the wrong address. It was only at this hearing that the applicant became aware, upon hearing submissions of the respondent, that he had a right to appeal the decision of the SCT to the Federal Court under s.5 of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review ) Act 1977. Notwithstanding this, the applicant indicated that he wished to proceed with the current matter and have the issue of jurisdiction determined by this Tribunal.
  7. Section 153AM of the 1976 Act provides that a person affected by a decision of the CC Board may apply to the Board for reconsideration of that decision. The CS Board, having power to reconsider its decision, did in fact reconsider it and affirmed its decision. Sub-section 154(6) of the 1976 Act empowers this Tribunal to review reviewable decisions by the Commissioner of Superannuation made under sub-s.154(4) and decisions of the Superannuation Board established under the Superannuation Act 1922, but does not confer power to review decisions by the CS Board.
  8. The Tribunal, being satisfied that the decision under review is a decision of the CS Board and not one by the Commissioner, finds that it has no jurisdiction under s.154 to review the decision. The Tribunal, in arriving at this conclusion, considered the authorities. In particular, the Tribunal noted the Federal Court decisions in Duncan v Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Authority 3 ALD 114 and Collector of Customs (NSW) v Lawlor Automotive Pty Ltd [1979] FCA 21; 24 ALR 307, which hold that the Tribunal lacks jurisdiction to review a matter if there has not been a reviewable decision.
  9. Whilst the Tribunal does not attempt to discuss in depth each of the issues raised by the applicant and respondent, it briefly comments on some of the submissions made by the applicant. The applicant, in interpreting sub-s.25(4) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, submitted that this provision provides the Tribunal with jurisdiction to review “any decision in respect of which application is made to it under any enactment” and therefore provides the Tribunal with a discretion to review the CS Board’s decision. The Tribunal points out that this is an erroneous interpretation and the Tribunal’s jurisdiction arises under specific legislative provisions.
  10. The applicant submitted that he has an accrued right arising from sub-s.7(2) of the 1976 Act to have his matter determined by the Commissioner for Superannuation, which if unsuccessful would then provide a right of review to this Tribunal. The applicant claimed that the Commissioner of Superannuation was originally vested with power to review his application under the 1976 Act, but subsequently the power to make decisions with respect to such applications was transferred to the CS Board by the 1990 Act, and the CS Board then made the decision. The applicant therefore alleged that the handing over of his application by the Commissioner for Superannuation to the CS Board constituted a reviewable decision. In support of this contention the applicant relied on sub-s.9(9) of the Superannuation Benefits (Supervisory Mechanisms) Act 1990 (“1990 Act), which provides the following transitional provision:
“(9) Where any proceedings had been instituted under subsection 153AD(3) of the Superannuation Act 1976 before the repeal of Part XA of that Act, but those proceedings had not been completed before that repeal, those proceedings may be continued, after the repeal, as if Part XA of that Act had not been repealed.”


In relation to this line of argument the respondent submitted that there was no decision by the Commissioner for Superannuation handing over carriage of the matter to the CS Board and the matter came into the hands of the CS Board due to legislative changes, namely due to the amendment of s.7 of the 1976 Act by s.10 of the 1990 Act which omitted from sub-s.7(2) the word “Commissioner” and substituted “Board”.

  1. The Tribunal has insufficient evidence before it to indicate whether a decision was made by the Commissioner or a delegate of the Commissioner handing over the matter to the CS Board. The Tribunal was not in possession of documents lodged pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and was not in a position to determine the course of events that led to this matter coming into the hands of the CS Board. The Tribunal does however note that even had this matter been referred by the Commissioner onto the CS Board, such a decision would have been within the powers of the Commissioner. Sub-section 9(9) of the 1990 Act provides that proceedings instituted under sub-s.153AD(3) of the 1976 Act that have not been completed before repeal of Part XA of that Act, may be continued after repeal as if Part XA had not been repealed. This does not prescribe any mandatory requirement that the Commissioner consider such proceedings, and leaves open a discretion to the Commissioner to refer matters onto the CS Board. The Tribunal is of the view that had the Commissioner made such a decision to refer the application to the CS Board such a decision was within the discretion of the Commissioner. The Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant did not have any accrued rights to have the matter dealt with by the Commissioner.
  2. The applicant submitted that there is a legislative vacuum of three years, between 1991 and 1994, being the time from when the CS Board replaced the Commissioner as the appropriate decision maker until the establishment in 1994 of the SCT. On the other hand the respondent submitted that the SCT did have jurisdiction to review the matter, and hence there was in fact no such vacuum. The respondent also submitted that even if such a vacuum did exist, it was not appropriate for the Tribunal to make a decision when it did not have jurisdiction to do so. The Tribunal has stated already that it does not have jurisdiction to review decisions of the CS Board. The Tribunal is therefore not an appropriate forum for review of this matter.
  3. Accordingly, the Tribunal determines that it does not have jurisdiction to hear this matter.
