Re David Earl Pty Limited and Tax Agent'S Board [1990] AATA 211 (11 September 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: DAVID EARL PTY LIMITED

And: TAX AGENT'S BOARD

No. N89/97

AAT No. 6210

Tax Agent

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

K.L. Beddoe(1) (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Tax Agent - Cancellation of registration as a tax agent - registered nominee not a fit and proper person - neglect of the business of a principal - effect of stay order - from what date registration is restored.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975: ss.41,43

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936: ss.6(1), 251A, 251JA, 251JG, 251K(2), 251K(2A)

Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No.3) 1988: ss.55(16), (17) and (18)

Michael David Foley v. Tax Agents' Board (Senior Member Beddoe - 11 September 1990)

R v. Moodie and Ors ex parte Mithen (1977) 17 ALR 219

Re Dekanic and Tax Agents' Board of NSW (1982) 6 ALD 240.

HEARING

SYDNEY

11:9:1990

ORDER

The Court orders:

(a) That the decision under review be set aside;

(b) that the applicant's registration be suspended for a period of three

months from 11 September 1990 to 10 December 1990 inclusive;

(c) that Michael David Foley cease to be a registered nominee of the

applicant; and

(d) that Ian Keith Maloney be registered as nominee of the company with

effect from 26 April 1989 or such earlier date as he may have been so

registered by the respondent - such registration to be dependent upon

payment of the appropriate fee within seven days of this decision.

DECISION

The applicant company seeks review of a decision of the respondent Board cancelling its registration as tax agent. The decision of the respondent Board under review was made at a meeting held on 6 December 1988 (Document T21).

2. Part VIIA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") provides the legislative scheme, for the registration of tax agents. Subdivision H of that part provides for the cancellation or suspension of such registration of tax agents. In particular subsection 251K(2) of the Act provides that a Board may suspend or cancel the registration of any agent upon the Board being satified that -

(a) ...;

(b) the tax agent -

(i) has neglected the business of a principal; or

(ii) ...; or

(c) a registered nominee of the tax agent is not a fit and proper

person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf
of

taxpayers in income tax matters;

(d) ...;

(e) ...; or

(f) if the tax agent is a company - an executive officer of the company;

(i) ...; or

(ii) is not of good fame, integrity and character.

3. A person (person is defined to include a company BY subsection 6(1)) whose registration as a tax agent under Part VIIA was in force immediately before the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No.2) 1988 ("the Amendment Act") came into force on 1 November 1988 is deemed by virtue of subsection 55(167) of the Amendment Act to have been registered under section 251JA, as amended by that Act, immediately after the commencement of subsection 55(16). Subsection 55(18) provides that notwithstanding section 251JG of the amended Act, the registration of a tax agent referred to in subsection 55(16) shall cease to be in force on 1 April 1989, unless sooner:

(a) cancelled:

(i) under the amended Act;

(ii) under subsection 251K(2A) of the Principal Act in its application

by virtue of subsection 55(17) of the Amendment Act.

(b) ...

4. Subsection 55(17) of the Amendment Act provides that notwithstanding the repeal of section 251JA and subsection 251K(2A) of the Principal Act, those provisions continue to apply after 1 November 1988 in relation to a tax agent whose registration was suspended on that date), as if those repeals had not been effected.

5. Section 251JA of the Principal Act provided, in effect, for annual renewal of tax agents registration as at 1 April each year on application by the tax agent within seven days, or such further time as a Board allows, of 1 April. The essential condition to be met is the application for renewal by the tax agent. Subsection 251K(2A) provided for cancellation of the registration of a tax agent when the agent failed to make an application for renewal of registration in accordance with section 251JA. The overall effect was therefore to require tax agents whose registration was in force on 1 April 1988 to apply for renewal of that registration, either seven days after 1 April 1989, or such further time as a Board allows.

6. Subsection 41(1) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act") provides that the making of an application to the Tribunal for a review of a decision does not affect the operation of the decision or prevent the taking of action to implement the decision. That subsection 41(2) which provides, inter alia, that the Tribunal may make an order staying the operation of the decision under review.

7. By virtue of subsection 43(1) of the AAT Act the Tribunal may exercise all the powers and discretions that are conferred by the Act on the Board in relation to the review of the decision.

8. I turn now to the facts. Although not proved I will infer that the applicant is an incorporated company carrying on business as a tax agent. The directors of the company include Ralph John Joseph Melano and Ian Keith Maloney but do not include Michael David Foley. Maloney and Foley were nominees of the company for the purposes of Part VIIA of the Act. Melano is also a registered tax agent. The company conducts its business in association with Foley's practice as a solicitor. The exact extent of the association was not established before the Tribunal but it must be a reasonably close association because Foley's evidence tended to ignore the distinction at law between his own practice and the business of the company. On the evidence before me Foley is the controlling mind of the company although not a director. Nor is Foley a shareholder in the company although it seems that the shares in the company are beneficially owned by the trustee of the Foley Family Trust.

9. As to whether Foley is an employee of the company the following somewhat ambiguous exchange, as recorded in the Transcript, occurred between Foley and the Tribunal, counsel for both parties having ignored the issue in their respective examinations of Foley:

Tribunal

Q. ... are you simply not a shareholder?

A. Somebody else holds shares; I do not know if they are in trust

for me or not. The directors hold the shares.

Q. Well, you are not a shareholder?

A. Not - no.

Q. What other relationship do you have with David Earl?

A. That is about it. It is a - it is ---.

Q. You are not employed by the company?

A. Well technically I am, yes.

Q. You receive a salary, do you?

A. Oh, not really, no. It is the trustee of the Foley Family Trust.

Q. So you say that although you do not receive a salary, you are

technically employed by the company?

A. Yes.

I am satisfied that Foley is a person who is concerned in, or takes part in the management of the applicant company and is therefore an executive officer as defined by section 251A of the Act. I am not satisfied that Foley is an employee of the company in the sense that he is employed by the company, because he is not in receipt of salary. It follows that I cannot therefore be satisfied that there is a contract of employment between the applicant and Foley.

10. By a form notice addressed to the Secretary of the respondent Board and signed by Foley with date 1 April 1988 the applicant applied for renewal of its registration as a tax agent. (Document T5). The copy document in evidence shows a received stamp dated 19 April 1988. That means the form was prima facie lodged late by four days, the Board having previously extended the lodgment date to 15 April 1988.

11. By letter dated 19 May 1988 the respondent Board's secretary wrote to the applicant drawing attention to the statutory provision (s.251JA) and advising the company that as it had not lodged the application by the due date the Board had decided to cancel the registration at its meeting on 19 May 1988. The letter went on to advise the company that the Board would accept an application for registration until 17 June 1988 without the need to provide the usual details of academic and work experience qualifications.

12. On 17 June 1988 the respondent Board received an application in writing by the applicant for egistration as a tax agent. The form disclosed that Ian Keith Maloney, Ralph John Joseph Melano, and Margaret Mary Foley were the Directors, Managers or Administrative Officers of the company. It further disclosed that the company wished to have Foley as registered nominee. No other person was nominated as a nominee although the form disclosed that Maloney and Melano were registered tax agents.

13. By a standard form letter dated 1 July 1988 the Board wrote to a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (the form does not indicate an address) requesting information regarding the income tax affairs of Foley. (Document T11). A response to that letter was received by the Board from the Deputy Commissioner, Sydney, by a standard form letter dated 11 July 1988. A copy of that letter is Document T13. It discloses answers to the first and second questions only to the effect that Foley had not lodged income tax returns since 1979 and that although the 1980 return had been demanded "no follow up action was taken as the taxpayer was a T/L 7". No explanation was given to the Tribunal as to the meaning of "T/L 7". As to whether "any income tax liability which is required to be paid, (has) been paid" the Deputy Commissioner responded "yes". I do not know the answers, if any, given to questions (c) to (f) on Document T11 because those answers have apparently been obliterated on the copy document T13 before the Tribunal. I do not know why the Board withheld that information from the Tribunal but in view of the answers set out above I will infer that the answers to the subsequent questions were favourable to Foley.

14. On 5 July 1988 i.e., prior to receiving the Deputy Commissioner's response of 11 July 1988 the Board wrote to the applicant advising that it had approved the application for registration as a tax agent. It was not explained to the Tribunal as to why the Board took this action in advance of receiving the response from the Taxation Office in relation to Foley.

15. At a meeting of the Board held on 6 September 1988 the Board gave directions for Foley and the applicant to show cause as to why the company's registration should not be cancelled. (Document T14). The applicant company was advised of the Board's direction by a letter dated 19 September 1988. (Document T15).

16. The company responded by letter dated 9 November 1988. That response by itself was unresponsive to the Board's request except that the company sought to avoid the possible cancellation by directing attention to an application said to have been made on 18 October 1988. The application was to have Ian Keith Maloney registered as a nominee of the company so that the applicant's registration did not depend upon Foley as the sole nominee. Foley said in evidence that Maloney was a nominee of the applicant. That evidence was not contested. However, the Secretary of the respondlent Board did say in evidence that Maloney was not a nominee of the applicant. I am not sure whether he meant to say that Maloney was not registered at the relevant time.

17. At a meeting of the respondent Board held on 6 December 1988 the Board cancelled the applicant company's registration on the basis that Foley was no longer a fit and proper person to remain registered as a nominee.

18. That decision was notified to the applicant by the respondent by a letter dated 6 January 1989. Although there is no evidence before the Tribunal as to when that letter was served on the applicant the parties have proceeded on the basis that the application for review dated 6 February 1989 was lodged in time on that date. That application included a request that the decision cancelling the registration of the applicant be stayed pending the review sought. That was followed by a formal application for a stay dated 21 March 1989 and filed in the Tribunal on the same day.

19. The stay application came on for hearing before Deputy President Bannon QC on 26 April 1989. I was told in the hearing before myself that learned Deputy President expressed concern that the stay application amounted to an academic exercise, but was assured such was not the case. In the event he stayed the decision to cancel the registration made on 6 December 1988 until the determination of the review.

20. When the application eventually came on for hearing before myself on 8 December 1989 Dr Woods QC appeared for the applicant and Mr Johnson of counsel for the respondent. Dr Woods sought to have the matter adjourned so as to enable the applicant to file outstanding income tax returns of its clients. That request was supported by undertakings, inter partes, made in the face of the Tribunal, as to when the returns would be lodged. The respondent consented to the adjournment in the light of the undertakings and the Tribunal decided to adjourn the hearing.

21. At the resumed hearing on 1 March 1990 Dr Woods again sought an adjournment on the basis that the applicant had still to complete the lodgment of the returns it had undertaken to lodge, albeit that a significant number of returns of clients had been lodged at that time. In the course of discussion between the Tribunal and counsel specific references was made to the applicant only being in a position to prepare income tax returns for its clients because of the stay order made by Deputy President Bannon. In the event the matter was again adjourned and the hearing resumed on 29 May 1990; Mr K. Ryan of counsel appearing for the applicant and Mr Johnson for the respondent.

22. Late in the hearing at page 238 of the Transcript Mr Johnson raised the question as to whether the applicant's registration as a tax agent had come to an end due to effluxion of time. Subsection 55(18) of the Amendment Act provides:

(18) Notwithstanding section 251JG of the amended Act, the

registration of a tax agent referred to in subsection (16) of this

section shall cease to be in force on 1 April 1989, unless sooner:

(a) cancelled:

(i) under that Act; or

(ii) under subsection 251K(2A) of the Principal Act in its

application by virtue of subsection (17) of this section; or

(b) terminated or surrendered under the amended Act.

23. Both parties have proceeded in this matter on the basis that the stay order had the effect of maintaining the applicant's registration. The only basis for this must be that subsection 55(18) had no effect on the applicant's registration because as at 1 April 1989 the registration was cancelled by the respondent's decision of 6 December 1988. Subsection 55(18) therefore had no operation as at 1 April 1989.

24. The questions which arise are whether the stay order restored the registration until 1 April 1989, or restored the registration without limit as to time, or had the effect of restoring the registration from 26 April 1989 (the date of Deputy President Bannon's order).

25. The effect of the stay order, although vital to the outcome in this case, was not fully argued before me. There is, however, a number of decisions which provide some illumination on the effect of the stay order. Upon the applicant applying to this Tribunal for review of the respondent's decision the question of the cancellation of the registration became a matter for this Tribunal to decide. The respondent Board had performed its functions under the Act and its decision was not affected by the application for review to this Tribunal. This was made clear by the High Court in R v. Moodie and Ors ex parte Mithen (1977) 17 ALR 219. Section 43 of the AAT Act has the effect of placing this Tribunal in the shoes of the respondent Board for the purpose of reviewing the Board's decision but it is also clear from subsection 43(1) that this Tribunal is limited in its jurisdiction to reviewing the decision under review and does not have a wider jurisdiction to deal with matters generally.

26. However, as Davies J (President) said in Re Dekanic and Tax Agent s' Board of NSW, (1982) 6 ALD 240, section 41 of the AAT Act requires the application of different principles to those adopted in other jurisdictions. In that case his Honour attributed to the stay order the effect of putting the applicant back into the position in which he would have been if his registration as a tax agent had not been cancelled.

27. In the present case Deputy President Bannon's order could not put the applicant back into the same position as it was prior to cancellation of its registration. The reason for this is that the registration would have been cancelled by the operation of subsection 55(17) of the amended Act which had the effect of maintaining the operation of section 251JA and subsection 251K(2A) of the Principal Act.

28. Those provisions depend upon the registration of the tax agent being in force on 1 April in any year; in this case 1 April 1989. The facts already outlined show that this was not the case unless the stay order had retrospective operation. Although I have been unable to find any authority directly in point it seems to me that the better view is that the stay order restored the status quo prior to the decision of 6 December 1988 but it did not do so with retrospective effect. Therefore the status quo was restored on and from 26 April 1989. It follows that the applicant's registration as a tax agent was not force on 1 April 1989 so that section 251JA and therefore subsection 251K(2A) had no operation.

29. I am therefore satisfied that the stay order was effective to restore the applicant's registration as a tax agent with effect from 26 April 1989.

30. The essence of this case is whether or not the applicant company neglected the affairs of its principals. That is not the basis on which the respondent Board made its decision as already pointed out. But in the course of the evidence it became clear that there was a backlog of unlodged income tax returns of clients of the company. The extent of that backlog depends upon the criteria adopted to measure it.

31. The officers of the Taxation Office who gave evidence before me referred to a basis which depends upon a client list in the name of the applicant which is apparently maintained by the tax agents liaison centre which is a section of the Taxation Office at Chatswood. That client list apparently formed the basis upon which the Taxation Office "monitored" a tax agents lodgment of income tax returns. The level of compliance with a predetermined lodgment program determined, in some mechanical way which is unclear to me, whether or not a tax agent was granted extensions of time for lodgment in respect of some of its clients' income tax returns.

32. It seems the Taxation office adopts the approach that a taxpayer is a client of a particular tax agent until the tax agent deletes the client from its list or another tax agent advises that the taxpayer is now its client. The compilation of the client list pays no regard as to whether or not the tax agent holds current instructions from the taxpayers on the list to lodge income tax returns on their behalf. In this regard it did not seem to be understood by the officers of the Taxation Office that a tax agent can only lodge an income tax return without those instructions. The result can be, as it is in this case, that the tax agent can be visited with the sins of omission of its clients. However, the solution is to maintain an updated client list, showing only those clients who have or are likely to give instructions for the lodgment of their income tax returns. This the company failed to do. On the evidence of Foley, the company failed to regularly update the client list with the consequence that the Australian Taxation Office apparently became dissatisfied with the applicant's lodgment achievement.

33. It is clear enough on the evidence that the cancellation of the applicant's registration had its genesis in the respondent Board's concern that Foley was not a fit and proper person to be registered as a tax agent and Foley was the sole nominee of the company. It has not been suggested to me that the applicant has been other than a competent tax agent, albeit at times being dilatory in looking after its clients affairs.

34. Evidence was given before me by three clients of the applicant. They were clients of long standing and professional businessmen well able to judge professional competence. They gave evidence to the general effect that the applicant had handled their income tax affairs to their total satisfaction. Each of them made it clear that they had dealt with Foley and their evidence reflected their satisfaction with his work. There is no basis in their evidence for saying that the applicant neglected their affairs. There was some evidence of a dispute in relation to the income tax affairs of a Mr and Mrs Long. However, the respondent chose not to call the Longs to give evidence, after intimating that they would be called, so I must infer that their evidence would not assist the Tribunal. On the evidence before the Tribunal I am unable to come to any conclusion as to whether the applicant neglected the income tax affairs of Mr and Mrs Long.

35. As to the question of whether Foley is a fit and proper person, I have dealt with that issue in my reasons in Michael David Foley v. Tax Agents' Board (decision dated 11 September 1990) and merely adopt those reasons. In the result Foley's failure to lodge income tax returns is irrelevant because Foley is not a fit and proper person for other reasons. He cannot be a nominee of the company in these circumstances.

36. If it had been clearly established that Maloney is a nominee of the applicant there would, in my view, be no basis for cancellation of the registration but the admitted tardiness in lodgment of client returns does justify a lesser penalty. In the circumstances, I propose to set aside the decision under review and direct that Foley cease to be a registered nominee of the applicant, that Maloney be registered as nominee of the applicant in accordance with the applicant's letter of 9 November 1988 (if that has not already been done) and that the applicant's registration be suspended from today until 10 December 1990 inclusive i.e. for a period of three months, on the basis that the applicant has neglected the business of principals because of its admitted dilatoriness in the lodgment of income tax returns. In fixing the period of three months I have taken into account the period prior to 26 April 1989 when the registration was effectively cancelled. I have also taken into account the fact that the applicant undertook significant steps to bring its lodgment program with the Taxation Office upt to date and I have also taken into account the fact that the applicant failed to comply with certain undertakings given to the respondent. The suspension of registration is pursuant to subsection 251K(2) of the Act.
