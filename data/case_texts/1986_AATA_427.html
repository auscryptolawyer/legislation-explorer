NT85/4110 and Commissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 427; (1986) 18 ATR 3142; 87 ATC 171 (19 December 1986)

Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Deductions - Employer contribution to section 23F superannuation fund - contributions exceeding 5% of remuneration - "special circumstances" - non-compliance with commissioner's "guidelines" - status of "guidelines"

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) s 51, 82AAE, 82AAR.

NT 85/4110

AAT Decision No 3113

19 December 1986

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS DIVISION )

) No.

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

And:

Applicant

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The Applicant (Aco) is a company controlled by a married couple (H and W). H is a registered plumber and licenced builder. Aco is operated as a family company providing employment not only for H and W, but also for their children and their spouses; for relatives of the spouses of their children; and for strangers. It has conducted a business trading in plumbing fittings and related goods since 1973. The business was commenced by H and two other registered plumbers, who later disposed of their interest in the business. In common with many businesses of its type, it had no assurance of continued prosperity; it had on-going liquidity problems; and it was frequently pressed by its bankers to reduce its overdraft. (By June 1982 it had an overdraft with its bankers in the order of $120,000 and additional finance provided through the same bankers by way of a "bank bill" in the sum of $40,000). H & W worked long hours, longer than most employees and they were paid less for their services than was paid to strangers rendering the same service. However, H & W were both beneficiaries of a Superannuation Scheme established by Aco. In the year of income ended 30 June 1982 Aco contributed $4,168 to that Superannuation Fund by way of employer contribution. In the same year Aco paid wages of $12,600 to both H and W. Contributions to Superannuation for H and W were also equal. The Superannuation Fund was controlled by H and W and was an approved Fund for the purposes of section 23F of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act"). The principal investment of the Fund was constituted by monies lent to Aco to enable Aco to continue in business in the interests of all. The income of the Fund was the interest paid by Aco to the Fund on monies it had borrowed.

2. The expenses so incurred by Aco constituted losses or outgoings incurred in the course of carrying on its business and would ordinarily have been allowable as deductions pursuant to section 51 of the Act. However, section 82AAR of the Act provides:

"A deduction is not allowable under any provision of this Act other than this Subdivision in respect of an amount set apart or paid by a taxpayer as or to a fund for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, an employee or employees".

That being so Aco would be eligible for deductions in respect of the contributions it made if, and only if, it satisfied the requirements of Subdivision AA ("Contributions to Superannuation Funds for Benefit of Employees") of Division 3 ("Deductions") of the Act. It was common ground that Aco satisfied all requirements of Subdivision AA save that its contributions exceeded the maximum permitted limits for deductibility specified by section 82AAE. For the Commissioner it was contended that Aco did not satisfy the requirements of section 82AAE because of the "Commissioner's guidelines". For Aco it was conceded that the contributions did not satisfy the requirements of those "guidelines", but it was contended that "special circumstances" existed which should make the guidelines ineffective. On both sides it seems to have been forgotten that the question of entitlement was to be determined by reference to the Act as enacted by the Parliament.

3. Section 82 AAE is in the following terms:-

"The deduction, or the sum of the deductions, allowable under this Subdivision in an assessment or assessments of a taxpayer or taxpayers in respect of income of the year of income in respect of amounts set apart or paid by the taxpayer or taxpayers as or to a fund or funds for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, any one employee -

(a) shall not exceed whichever is the greater of the following amounts:

(i) Four hundred dollars; and

(ii) five per centum of the total remuneration paid to the employee by taxpayers during the year of income of the employee that, in the opinion of the Commissioner corresponded to the first-mentioned year of income in respect of his employment by those taxpayers; or

(b) if the Commissioner is of the opinion that there are special circumstances that justify the allowance of a greater deduction, or of deductions of a greater sum, than the amount ascertained in accordance with the last preceding paragraph shall be such amount, being greater than the amount so ascertained, as he considers reasonable."

4. Following application by Aco the Commissioner applied his mind to that problem in relation to H and W and on the 25 November 1980 wrote to the accountant to Aco stating (inter alia):

"Consideration has now been given to the exercise of the discretion under section 82 AAE (b) of the Income Assessment Act to allow contributions by (Aco) on behalf of (H and W).

After careful consideration of the circumstances surrounding the employment of each, as well as the details set out in return of income for year ended 30 June 1978, it has been decided that a reasonable benefit for each on retirement or death would be: -

H 150,950 W 112,000

"Contributions by the employer towards providing a benefit for each employee not exceeding the amount determined as reasonable will be deductible, provided the employer company furnishes a certificate, signed by the Trustees or their Agents, with its claim in each years (sic) return that:-

(a) The benefit provided from the Fund on the retirement or death of each will not exceed the following amounts - H $150,950 - W $112,000.

(b) The contributions by the employer to provide the benefit will be paid annually and, together with any contributions made by the employee to the Fund, will not cause the amount determined as reasonable to be exceeded."

5. Aco failed to respond and the Commissioner was put to the trouble of again writing to the company in May 1981. This time a response was forthcoming. By letter of 1 June 1981 Aco advised the Commissioner:

"Further to yours of the 25 December (sic) 1980 and 19 May 1981, relating to Superannuation Contributions by the abovenamed taxpayer, I certify that:

(a) The benefit provided by the Fund will not exceed the following amounts, H $94,000 - W $94,000.

(b) The contributions paid by the taxpayer company to provide the benefits will be paid annually and together with any contributions made by the employer to the fund, will not cause the amount determined as reasonable, to be exceeded.

(c) The contributions are being maintained at the same level as the year ended 30 June 1980, which was H $2,200 and W $2,100."

As the evidence stands before me there was no further correspondence on the matter or any difficulty arising relating to the assessment of the company or the Fund for the year of income ended 30 June 1981.

6. At this point it is appropriate to consider another aspect of the legislative schema relating to Superannuation. What has been considered to date relates to the assessment of employers. What is now to be considered relates to the assessment of Superannuation Funds. The Parliament provided in 1965 that certain employer sponsored to Superannuation Schemes would be exempt from income tax provided they complied with requirements specified in the Act which were directed towards ensuring that the benefits would be assured to and paid in due course and at an appropriate time to beneficiaries (section 23F). Then in 1973 section 121C(1) was amended to provide (inter alia) that section 23F Superannuation Funds would not be eligible for that exemption unless they complied with a rule specified precisely by the legislation but referred to for convenience as the "30/20 rule": a rule which required that the assets of funds be invested as to 30% in public securities with not less than 20% of the whole being invested in Commonwealth securities.

7. During the year of income ended 30 June 1982 the Aco Fund did not comply with the "30/20 rule". It had forfeited any claim to exemption. That is not a matter in dispute. However the Commissioner contends that the failure on the part of the Fund to comply with the "30/20 rule" carried with it a further consequence, not for the Fund, but for employers who contributed to the Fund. The further consequence contended for by the Commissioner was that the deductions allowable to Aco in relation to superannuation contributions should be limited to 5% of the amounts paid by way of salary to each of H and W. Before me the contention for the Commissioner was that to have allowed more would have contravened what were described during the hearing as the Commissioner's "guidelines".

8. The "guidelines" were evidenced in the form of circulars to Tax Agents issued by a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in December 1981 and in May 1982. The officer of the Commissioner responsible for the assessment of superannuation fund returns and related aspects of employer returns gave evidence that she understood her duty to be to enforce the "guidelines" as proclaimed in those documents and that she had sought to do so.

9. I acknowledge the "guidelines" as proclaimed by those documents as an accurate record of the directions given by the Commissioner to his officers as to how he required them to act in the discharge of their duties and as a accurate record of communications by the Deputy Commissioner to Tax Agents as to the expectations of the Commissioner in that regard. But such "guidelines" are not a statement of the law as proclaimed by the Parliament and they are not authoritive statements as to the principles of interpretation to be followed by this Tribunal in applying the law proclaimed by the Parliament. I also observe that the "guidelines" did not give any directions to, or reflect any directions to, staff of the Commissioner as to how they should act in exercising the discretion of the Commissioner: they merely indicated a condition (non-compliance with "the 30/20 rule") which would not constitute "special circumstance" for the purpose of section 82AAE (b), and which would negate the existence of "special circumstances" for the purposes of that section.

10. In consequence this reference is to be determined by considering section 82AAE of the Act and, in particular, whether "there are special circumstances that justify the allowance" of the deduction claimed. In my view the "special circumstances" to be considered must relate to the employer and the employee; the relationship between them; and the reasonableness of the superannuation benefits proposed. If consideration of those factors established "special circumstances", then employer contributions in excess of 5% could be deductible; and those "special circumstances would still exist even though the Fund was liable to income tax, whether because of the "30/20 rule" or any other consideration.

11. In my view "special circumstances" did exist and I am confirmed in that view by the fact that the Commissioner so found in 1980. Further, I am of opinion that the "special circumstances" which were sufficient in 1980 in the mind of the Commissioner to justify deductions of $2,200 and $2,100 continued - a finding not negated for that year or the previous year by non-compliance by the Fund with "the 30/20 rule".

12. I would uphold the objection of the Applicant.
