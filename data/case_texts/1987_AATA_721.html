ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - deductions - service fee paid to property investment trust – whether capital – either fee for reimbursement for the establishment and promotion of the trust or a management fee management fees provided for separately - fee part of the capital cost involved in the investment.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s.51(1)

REASONS FOR DECISION

30 July 1987 Dr R.A. Hayes (Senior Member)

This is an application for review of a decision by the Commissioner to disallow the applicant's objection against the assessment of income for the year ended 30 June 1983. The applicant had claimed a deduction, pursuant to s.51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") in respect of a "service fee" of $1,300 paid to the Hooker Growth Fund No. 4.

In August 1981, the applicant purchased 20,000 $1 units in the Growth Fund. As part of that transaction he also paid, by a separate cheque, a service fee calculated on the basis of 6.5 cents per unit purchased. In his 1982 tax return, the applicant claimed a deduction of $163, being one-eighth of the service fee. This calculation was made on the basis that the expected life of the fund was eight years. This deduction was allowed in the year ended 1982.

The applicant again claimed the deduction in his 1983 return. This was disallowed on the basis that the outgoing was of a capital nature, and accordingly not deductible under s.51(1) of the Act. The applicant has also claimed the same deduction in 1984 and 1985. These objections were not before me in this review, but it was agreed by the parties that the result of this application should determine the success or failure of the other objections.

At the hearing of this matter, the applicant gave evidence that he believed the service fee was intended to be used for initial management costs in relation to the property investments. Ongoing management costs would be deducted from the income generated by the property.

The applicant tendered a certified extract from the trust deed and a document described as the prospectus. In the prospectus the major aims of the fund are set out as being capital growth and income production. It is further stated that:

"The degree of income for distribution half-yearly will be governed by the extent of mortgage ratio and the rate of interest to be paid. To assure some 'assessable' income, so that the service fee can be claimed as a tax-deductible item (Section 51(1) of the Income Tax Act) it is unlikely that the total fund will be mortgaged beyond 55% of the value.

After a basic 2% is paid, any surplus (income) is shared between the unit holders and the Managers on a 50/50 basis."

All outgoings such as maintenance and management expenses are paid from income before the surplus is distributed to the owners/unit holders. The service fee is described as a "payment in advance for services to be rendered throughout the life of the fund." There is no description of the nature of the services outlined in the prospectus.

It is clear from the trust deed that the service fee is an integral part of the price of the unit. Clause 20 provides a mechanism for the computation of the selling-price of the units, which includes the service fee. Clause 20(b) provides that:

"From the amount of the price obtained or to be obtained consequent on any such sale and in relation to the Property-units so constituted the Managers shall be entitled to receive as the initial service charge the sum of 6c per Property-unit."

Clause 21 provides further details concerning the purpose of the fee.

"The initial service change as determined above shall be the source of the first remuneration of the Managers which is to be a remuneration in advance on account of their services in carrying on the business and gaining and producing the income of the Fund."

The fee is obviously separate from and additional to the "Manager's fee" of 0.5% of the gross value of the fund. This fee is paid from the income of the Fund on an annual basis. In addition the Manager receives 50% of the income in excess of a basic 2% per annum.

The Commissioner referred to the recent Tribunal decision, Case U53, 87 ATC 351, which also concerned the No. 4 Growth Fund. In the course of his reasons, Senior Member B.J. McMahon remarked that "In so far as service fee is a reimbursement for the cost of setting up and promotion of the trust, it is clearly a capital item."

The crucial distinction required to be made is whether the fee was intended to be a reimbursement for the establishment and promotion of the trust or whether it is part of the management fee. In Case U53, Mr McMahon took the view that:

"the percentage concept indicates that if any service was to be rendered, it would not be in the nature of management services (which are provided for elsewhere in the information documents). All expenses upon a day-to-day basis for the running of the properties and of the trusts are a first charge on revenue and are covered by such items as the manager's, trustee's and auditor's fees, and the general postages and stamp duties on cheques which are referred to in the offer document for the No. 4 Fund. The service fee must, therefore, be in a different category."

In that case the provisions of the trust deed outlined above were not before the Tribunal. With this information before me, I too am of the view that the true characterisation of the payment is as part of the initial capital contribution to the trust, to be used for its establishment and promotion.

The expenditure in question bears the traditional attributes of a capital payment. It is a payment made "once and for all". While this is not determinative, it is generally indicative of a capital expense. The real test is probably best expressed in the words of Viscount Cave, in Atherton v. British Insulated and Helsbv Cables Ltd (1925) 10 TC 155 at 192-193:

"But when an expenditure is made, not only once and for all, but with a view to bringing into existence an asset or advantage for the enduring benefit of a trade, I think that there is very good reason (in the absence of special circumstances leading to an opposite 'conclusion) for treating such an expenditure as properly attributable not to revenue but to capital."

I can find nothing in the evidence before me which would make me depart from the concluding remarks in Case U53, that "the service so-called service fee is no more than a thinly disguised addition to the capital cost of the acquisition of the assets."

The other issue raised at the hearing was in what year the expense should have been claimed. As I have found that the expense was not an allowable deduction, it is unnecessary for me to determine this quest.

For the reasons set out, the decision under review should be affirmed.
