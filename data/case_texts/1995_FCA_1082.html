John Kizon v the Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1995] FCA 1082; (1995) 95 Atc 4112 (1995) 30 Atr 196 (22 February 1995)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

JOHN KIZON v THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

NO. WAG69 of 1994

FED No. 60/95

Number of pages - 7

Practice And Procedure - Evidence

(1995) 95 ATC 4112

(1995) 30 ATR 196

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

WESTERN AUSTRALIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

NICHOLSON J

CATCHWORDS

Practice And Procedure - application for order to review - notice of motion having same effect as application - whether applicant entitled to final order on motion.

Evidence - admissibility - documents of respondent's office - whether admissible as admissions against interest by respondent in face of other objections to their admissibility.

Batagol v The Commissioner of Taxation [1963] HCA 51; (1963) 109 CLR 243.

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (WA); ex parte R v Commissioner of Taxation; ex parte Briggs (1986) 12 FCR 301 at 303 and 17 ATR 1031 at 1037-8.

Re Pezzano; ex parte Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (NSW) 20 ATR 423 at 427.

HEARING

PERTH, 10 February 1995

22:2:1995

Counsel for the Applicant: G Beaumont QC and E Power

Solicitors for the Applicant: Pryles and Defteros

Counsel for the Respondent: S Owen-Conway QC and L Price

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

THE COURT ORDERS THAT:

(1) The applicant's motion be set down for hearing together with

the respondent's motion and the application for an order to review

on a date to be fixed after the decision of the High Court on the

case stated in Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v Richard Walter

Pty Ltd.

(2) Within 14 days the parties file drafts of further programming

orders.

(3) The matter then be set down for a further directions hearing.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

NICHOLSON J The applicant moves the Court for an order that certain notices of assessment, the subject of an application herein for an order to review, be referred back to the respondent for amendment according to law.

2. By the application filed on 10 June 1994 the applicant sought orders to review certain decisions of the respondent relating to the issue of notices of amended assessment under the provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") in respect of the applicant in the period 1 July 1987 to 30 June 1992. The application claims that the applicant is aggrieved by the assessments because of decisions and conduct of the respondent having the effect of imposing on him a liability for an amount of income tax which is incorrect in a situation where he has no outstanding tax liability and where he should receive a credit amendment and refund of money. The grounds on which the applicant seeks relief are breach of the rules of natural justice; failure to comply with certain procedural requirements; the taking into account of irrelevant considerations; the failure to take into account relevant considerations; errors of law; and the application of policy without regard to the merits. The relief sought by the applicant is an order quashing and setting aside relevant decisions, further consideration by the respondent of all the matters to which each of the decisions or conduct relate, as well as "orders pursuant to s.39B of the Judiciary Act 1903 if the Court thinks appropriate."

3. Following the lodgment by the respondent of a request for further and better particulars on 12 July 1994, directions were given on 5 July 1994, 15 September 1994, 24 October 1994 and 16 December 1994. In the latter directions the time specified for the applicant to answer the respondent's request for particulars was extended from 23 September 1994 to 30 January 1995 and the time specified for the respondent to file and serve any affidavits upon which he intends to rely was extended from 14 October 1994 to 27 February 1995. The application was adjourned until 6 February 1995 for further directions.

4. On 14 December 1994 Mr Peter Pryles ("the solicitor"), a partner in the firm of solicitors acting for the applicant, swore an affidavit which was filed on 23 December 1994. The applicant was not represented at the directions hearing on 16 December 1994 and the Court was unaware in making programming orders on that date that the applicant intended to file the affidavit. In the affidavit the solicitor deposed to the making of a request for access to information pursuant to the provisions of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 on behalf of the applicant and served on the respondent. He further deposed to the granting of access to certain documentation, to the seeking of an internal review and to the provision of further documentation following that review. He exhibited to his affidavit the documents received. In addition he deposed that the applicant had received from the respondent a notice of the existence of further documentation to which access was denied. The affidavit concluded with a statement that an examination of the documents provided to the applicant by the respondent requires the Court to grant the relief sought in the application.

5. The affidavit was filed contemporaneously with the applicant's notice of motion. In its terms, however, the affidavit purports to be filed in support of the application and seeks the relief sought in the application rather than the motion.

6. In the motion the applicant sought an order "that the Respondent cancel the Notices of Assessment which form the basis of the Application herein". During the course of the argument on the hearing of the motion the applicant accepted that, in view of the provisions of s.170 of the Act, as explained and interpreted by the Court in Batagol v The Commissioner of Taxation [1963] HCA 51; (1963) 109 CLR 243, the respondent did not have power to cancel the notices of assessment and that, if the Court were disposed to make an order on the motion, it should be in the terms that the assessments be returned to the respondent to be dealt with in accordance with the law.

7. In reliance upon the documents to which reference has been made it is contended for the applicant in support of the motion that bad faith is manifested by the documents both in the purpose for which the present notices of assessment were issued and the view purportedly held by the respondent that the applicant should not be told of the incorrectness of the assessments until the hearing of the application.

8. In support of these contentions the reference was made on behalf of the applicant to some of the documents exhibited to the affidavit of the solicitor. The first two of these are submissions which the applicant's case describes as having been made by senior officers of the respondent. The first submission is dated 16 June 1994 and is identified as relating to the applicant. The name of the person from whom the memorandum came does not appear and in its place appears an outline from which counsel for the applicant asked the Court to infer that the name of the author has been obscured. The document originates from "debt collection". It commences by stating that after a thorough examination of the applicant's account "the balance currently on the NTS system is incorrect, and that the balance as shown on all of the assessments issued 5/5/94 were incorrect". The submission proceeds by stating that the amounts included as income in the assessments are correct, that analysis being presently disputed on behalf of the applicant. It then re-calculates the outstanding balance on the assessment for the applicant for the year ended 30 June 1989 and makes other calculations which the applicant says failed to take into account terms of settlement and a request for variation of provisional tax made on his behalf. The submission concludes with a recommendation that the 1991 assessment should be cancelled (again) and re-issued deleting reference to provisional tax credited, thereby reversing the balance to a debit in a certain figure. The recommendation continued by stating that to effect this there was a need to cancel both the 1990 and 1991 assessments issued on 5 May 1991. The submission continues:

"The issue/cancellation of the assessments on 22/2/94

and the subsequent lack of appropriate action taken

across the various accounting systems is responsible

for the incorrect amounts showing on all the notices

subsequently issued. I have not reconciled the

balance shown on each of the notices. Since they are

wrong anyway, and we'll have to admit that, I see a

reconciliation exercise as being a waste of time.

I stress again that my examination of the facts

extends only to balancing the accounts to reach an

amount payable. I do not question the amounts

arrived at in the assessments, and assume that you

will examine the 91 in respect to prov tax before it

is re-issued."

9. The submission also stated:

"...I think that should the client seek remission of

ATLP... he would be justified in expecting the credit

that extinguished the '89 debt to be applied at the

Feb issue date, assuming the delayed re-issue was not

the clients fault".

10. Accordingly the submission recommended the cancellation of assessments and their re-issue.

11. The second submission is dated 20 June 1994 and is identified as relating to the tax liability of the applicant. Neither its author nor recipient is apparent from it and from marks at the place where their names are designated to appear, counsel for the applicant submits that the Court should infer the authors' names have been obscured. There is provision both for signature on behalf of the special audit and the debt collection officer, but neither appears. After two and a half pages of reconciliation calculations the submission shows a figure for the net tax payable as at 5 May 1994 but arrives at a figure different from that calculated in the first submission. The submission reaches the conclusion from its analysis that the amount currently noted in "the NTS system" is incorrect and that remedial action could only be undertaken after the review action sought by the taxpayer is completed, the submission noting that the application has been lodged by the applicant. The submission then continues:

"Remedial action involves the cancellation and issue

of assessments for the 1990 to 1992 years. This

action would further complicate matters as the

taxpayer and/or his representatives have received a

final notice dated 31 March 1994 for an outstanding

amount without firstly receiving assessments in

respect of this final notice and are also in receipt

of assessments that were issued on 5/05/94. These 2

issues are also mentioned in the 'Application for

Order to Review' that was lodged by the taxpayer.

It would be more appropriate to advise the taxpayer

of the impending cancellation of the 1990-1992

assessments at the time of the hearing of this

application, and also provide an explanation

regarding the issue of the new assessments

incorporating the reconciled amounts in order to

avoid any confusion on the taxpayer's part.

...In addition to the cancellation of the 1990 and

1991 assessments, the 1992 assessment also have to be

cancelled in order to effect the adjustment as

detailed.... This submission is based on the premise

that the assessment issued in respect of the 1988

year is correct and is not in contravention of the

terms of the settlement of the taxpayer's 1989

settlement."

12. The documents exhibited to the solicitor's affidavit also include a copy of a handwritten memorandum relating to the applicant dated 29 October 1993 the author of which is not identified. At the foot of the page on which the memorandum appears, appears the notation "second audit compartment A". In the memorandum there appears this passage:

"The T/P has lodged his 1990 return as a NIL return

in order to get a refund of some of the money.

In order to avoid this the 1990 and 1991 returns will

be amended to include this income. Await the 1989

amendments to issue."

13. Counsel for the applicant asks the Court to conclude from reference to this memorandum that it records the improper purpose for which the assessments were issued, that is, to avoid the applicant obtaining a refund to which he was otherwise entitled.

14. On 24 January 1995 the respondent also filed a motion raising in relation to the application the contention that the Court lacks jurisdiction to deal with it; alternatively, that if the application invokes s.39B of the Judiciary Act 1903, it should be adjourned until the decision of the High Court of Australia on the case stated in Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v Richard Walter Pty Ltd and further matters of particularity which are of no present concern. The respondent's motion also contended that the applicant's motion should be dismissed because this Court lacks jurisdiction; the relief sought in the motion is substantially the same as the relief sought in the application so that the motion is seeking final relief by way of interlocutory relief; there is a lack of power in the court to cancel notices of assessment (a matter which the applicant addressed during the hearing in the manner previously referred to); the validity of the notices of assessment was not justiciable before the court, the motion was an attempt by the applicant to have the matter finally determined without the benefit of particulars of the applicant's case and the evidence of the parties; and the affidavit of the solicitor sworn on 14 December 1994 was inadmissible in whole or part in any event.

15. The jurisdictional contentions were not dealt with at the special appointment set down at the applicant's request for the hearing of argument in support of its motion. Even if the respondent were not to succeed on the arguments made on his behalf at that hearing, the applicant's motion could not be finally determined without further argument on the remaining issues taken by the respondent. In view of the impending decision of the High Court in Walter's case (supra), it was agreed on behalf of the applicant that if its motion were unable to be resolved as a consequence of the hearing at the special appointment all issues on both the application and the motions should be held over to be heard together following that decision and reasons being handed down by the High Court.

16. The contentions made on behalf of the respondent in terms of the last two grounds for opposing the applicant's motion make it inevitable, in my opinion, that the Court cannot at this time make any order in terms of the applicant's motion as formulated before the Court. To do so would be to grant final relief in the face of the direction of the Court made on the 16 December 1994 without the respondent having had the opportunity of complying with the direction made to enable it to state his case. It was only during the hearing, that is after the date referred to in that direction for compliance, that there was filed on behalf of the applicant his response to the respondent's request for particulars. At the same time there was also filed a further affidavit from the solicitor for the applicant sworn on 8 February 1995. The respondent has had no opportunity to consider these.

17. For the respondent it is asserted that the challenged notices of assessment are all correct. On his behalf it is further contended that the application does not make apparent the basis on which the validity of the assessments is attacked. It is in this context that it is submitted for the respondent, in my view correctly, that until these matters have the benefit of full hearing, final determination is entirely inappropriate.

18. In relation to the documents relied upon to support the motion for the applicant, objections are made on behalf of the respondent that some of the documents are irrelevant, relating to years of income after the making of the assessments in contention, or are in a form which makes them inadmissible because their authorship is not apparent. There is a further objection that the documents are not shown to have formed any part in the decision-making of the respondent giving rise to the notices of assessment and that it is not open to the Court to infer from the source of the documents that they are the respondent's reasons for acting in issuing the contested notices of assessment. It is also apparent that the abbreviations and references in the documents are unexplained in the absence of further evidence.

19. The only response made by senior counsel on behalf of the applicant to these evidentiary objections at this time is that the documents are admissible as admissions against interest. It is submitted that because all of them came from the office of the respondent they are to be taken as the respondent's documents. In my opinion the fact that the documents were obtained by the applicant as a consequence of the request for information under the Freedom of Information Act to the office of the respondent does not of itself make those documents admissible. The status of the documents as documents received as the result of a response to a request for information under the Freedom of Information Act does not entitle this Court to act on a myriad of assumptions displacing the normal rules of evidence and setting aside the objections made to their admissibility. Furthermore, any quality which the documents may have as admissions is one which flows from them after they become admissible. That putative quality is no answer to objections on the ground of relevance or otherwise.

20. In the course of submissions for the applicant reliance was placed upon what was said by the Full Court of the Federal Court in Re Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (WA); ex parte R v Commissioner of Taxation (WA); ex parte Briggs (1986) 12 FCR 301 at 303 and 17 ATR 1031 at 1037-8 to the effect that the issue of a notice of assessment not reflecting any rational assessment of a liability or with reckless indifference to such was not an "assessment" invoking the operation of s.177(1) of the Act. Reliance was also placed on what was said by Beaumont J in Re Pezzano; ex parte Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (NSW) 20 ATR 423 at 427 that "it may be accepted that a colourable assessment or an assessment made with an improper purpose in view would be regarded as an abuse of power and, as in the case of any act of bad faith in public administration, would be declared invalid by a court in the exercise of the ordinary power of judicial review". In my view this reliance is premature. The evidence is not yet properly before the court to enable this law to be invoked if it is relevant to the case for the applicant.

21. For these reasons it is not open to the Court to make an order in terms of the applicant's motion as pressed at this time. Fresh programming orders should now be made having as their objective the need for a final hearing on all issues arising under the application and each of the motions.
