DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION



TAXATION - capital gains - review of objection decision of respondent relating to assessment - partnership carrying on business of vessel charter - lease - acquisition of asset - whether disposal of previously leased asset was subject to capital gains provisions - date of acquisition of vessel - cost base calculation for determining amount of capital gain - whether lease agreement or hire purchase agreement - Income Tax Assessment Act ss 160(3)(d), 160U(1), 160ZH(1)

REASONS FOR DECISION



21 December 1992 Mr BJ McMahon (Deputy President)



1. The applicant and his brother were mine deputies. In 1982 his brother had been retrenched. Together with his brother's wife and his own fiancee, the applicant and his brother decided to enter upon a new venture. They had in mind acquiring a vessel and chartering it for off-shore party fishing with or without a crew provided. Evidence was given that for some time they were unable to obtain suitable finance from a bank or a finance company, as they had no experience in the field they were seeking to enter. Ultimately they found a vessel which they wished to acquire and enlisted the aid of a finance broker, who was a friend of the vendor.



2. The price of the vessel was $135,000. The broker found a finance company willing to finance the transaction, but only to the extent of $125,000. This meant that $10,000 had to be paid to the vendor by the partnership. The vendor indicated that he was prepared to lend this amount to the partnership and on 22 December 1982 a written agreement was entered into between the vendor and the partnership reciting the agreement to lend and the agreement to repay the advance by 48 monthly instalments with interest. There was no written sale agreement. The advance was paid back to the vendor when possession of the boat was taken.



3. Another condition of the finance company was that a pre-payment of $50,000 should be made, together with stamp duty of $750. This was paid in advance and thereafter payments continued in accordance with the documentation to which I will refer. As documented, the pre-payment was an advance payment of rentals under a lease.



4. On 20 December 1982, (2 days before the $10,000 advance) the partners signed an offer to enter into a lease of the vessel. The offer was subsequently accepted on 22 December 1982 by the finance company. At no time did any of the partners have direct contact with the finance company. The applicant gave evidence, which I have no reason to reject, that the agreement was brought to him by the broker who had made the necessary arrangements. The applicant said in evidence that he read the document carefully and said to the broker "this seems to be a lease". The applicant said that he was under the impression that he was borrowing money from the finance company and that he was paying it off. The broker then said to him "this is the way this particular finance company does business". The applicant then agreed to sign the document. There was no evidence from any of the other partners.



5. The applicant said that he was under the impression that the vessel would belong to the partnership when they had paid it off. He said he asked the broker where that appeared in the document. He was then shown the item "residual value $12,500 being a pre-estimate of the value of the goods at termination of the lease". The broker told him that it was the general custom to purchase any goods in this manner. The applicant accepted his assurance. He did not seek professional help at that time, but told his accountant some 2 weeks later about these events. No attempt was made by the applicant or by his accountant then or later to alter the legal incidents of the arrangements.



6. By an oversight, the first lease payment of $50,000 was not claimed as a deduction in the partnership's tax return for the year ended 30 June 1983. Thereafter however, the partnership claimed as an expense the lease payments of $2,143 per month until the final payment on 14 September 1987. On that date, the partnership terminated the lease and paid $16,652 to the finance company. The applicant said in evidence that there was no difficulty about terminating the lease although it had not run its full course. When he telephoned the finance company he spoke to a young lady who worked out what the future rental payments were and what the residual value was and simply said "you owe us $16,652".



7. The sum of $10,000 was shown in the 1983 balance sheet of the partnership as "cruiser equity". The counter balancing entry was "loan-[vendor]". Interest on the loan was accounted for directly under the same heading, showing the balance owing less interest not then due.



8. On the finalisation of the finance arrangement, the $16,652 together with the $10,000 was capitalised and depreciated from 14 September 1987 until the vessel was disposed of on 12 July 1988 when the depreciation as claimed, namely, $4,397 was duly recouped. Depreciation was claimed in the sum of $4,250 in 1988 and $147 in 1989 up to the date of the vessel's disposal. In July 1988, the vessel was sold for $168,364. When the facts emerged by way of a request for a ruling, the net income of the partnership for the year ended 30 June 1989 was adjusted to include a capital gain of $151,712 being the $168,364 received on the sale of the vessel, less the cost base of $16,652. The applicant's share of the capital gain was $37,928 and his assessment for the year ended 30 June 1989 was amended to include this amount. The disallowance of an objection relating to this assessment is the subject matter of the present review.



9. There are 2 issues involved. The first question to be determined is the date of acquisition of the vessel. If it is found to be after 20 September 1985, the operative date of introduction of capital gains tax, then the second question to be determined is the cost base calculation for determining the amount of the capital gain.



10. The applicant's case is that the partnership acquired the vessel, or an equity in the vessel, in 1982 when the financing arrangement was entered into. This is put on the basis firstly that an amount of $10,000 was paid to give the partnership an equity in the vessel and secondly, that at all times it was agreed between the partnership and the finance company, that the partnership would be entitled to acquire title to the vessel when it had been paid off. As the partnership obtained the use and enjoyment of the vessel from the date of the signing of the lease, the applicant contends that a reading of 2 sections, entitles the partnership to a finding that the acquisition was in 1982 rather than in 1987 when the lease was paid out.



11. Section 160M(3)(d) is in the following terms —



"s160M(3) Without limiting the generality of subsection (2), a change shall be taken to have occurred in the ownership of an asset by —



............



(d) subject to subsection (4), a transaction in relation to the asset under which the use and enjoyment of the asset was or is obtained by a person for a period at the end of which the title to the asset will or may pass to that person."



12. In the implementation of this section some guidance is given by s 160U(7) which is in the following terms —



"s160U(7) Where the acquisition or disposal of the asset occurred as a result of a transaction referred to in paragraph 160M(3)(d), the time of acquisition or disposal shall be taken to have been the time when the use and enjoyment of the asset was first obtained by the person mentioned in that paragraph."



13. The applicant says that on 22 December 1982 the partners took possession of the vessel. Keys were handed over by the vendor. Registration was transferred from the vendor to the partnership. There was no note or plaque on the vessel indicating any interest on the part of the finance company. The vessel was used continuously in the business of the partnership, until it was disposed of. It was tied up at a mooring which the vendor had made available. There was an oral agreement with the vendor that he would stay on for some 12 months, until the partnership came to know the business. There is no evidence however that the vendor, in any way, inhibited their use and enjoyment of the vessel. From these facts and from a submission that title to the asset would or might pass to the partnership at the end of the lease period, the applicant submits that the partnership acquired the asset in 1982.



14. From a legal point of view, however, this evidence is quite inconsistent with the opening terms of the lease. Under that document, the finance company clearly claims ownership. Above the signatures of the members of the partnership are these words —



"As an inducement to you to accept this offer I hereby represent and warrant that I have never had any property or interest whatsoever in the above goods and I HEREBY AGREE TO BE BOUND by the provisions of this instrument including those set out below or on the back hereof."



This is also inconsistent with the submission that payment of $10,000 gave the partnership an equity in the vessel.



15. Relevant terms and conditions of the lease are as follows —



"TERMS AND CONDITIONS OF LEASE



I, THE ABOVE LESSEE, AGREE —



1. (a) Subject as hereinafter provided to pay the rent instalments set out in the schedule together with the stamp duty thereon as stated in the schedule: (b) to obtain delivery of the goods at my expense.



2. ............



(b) to keep the goods in good order and repair and properly operated serviced and housed, observing at all times the manufacturer's recommendations in respect of operation and servicing; provided always that I shall not have or be deemed to have any authority to pledge your credit or create any lien upon the goods:



(c) to indemnify you against loss (the measure of which shall be the then outstanding rent rebated as provided for in clause 6 hereof together with the residual value hereunder, or the value of the goods, whichever is the greater, less the value (if any) of the salvage) arising from destruction or loss (including lawful confiscation) of the goods provided that in the event of such destruction or loss I shall cease to be liable to pay the rent instalments then unpaid or any relative stamp duty:



............



(e) to keep the goods under my personal control and not to attempt or purport to sell dispose of or encumber the same or any interest therein nor without your prior written consent to sell or encumber nor agree to sell or encumber any land to which the goods may at any time be affixed:



............

5. That nothing herein shall confer on me any right or property or interest in or to the goods and I shall be a bailee thereof only.



............



7. (a) If, upon the goods being received into your possession consequent upon the expiration of the period of the lease or any extension thereof, the goods are disposed of by you for the best price you can reasonably obtain at the time, and the net proceeds of such disposal (after allowing for all costs and expenses incidental to such disposal including storage) are less than the residual value stated in the schedule, I agree (subject to sub-clause (b) to pay you on demand (additionally to any rent or other moneys payable by me) the amount of such deficiency,



............



8. In the event of your agreeing to my returning the goods to you prior to the expiration of the period of the lease, the balance of the rent for the whole period of the lease shall forthwith become due and payable (less a rebate calculated in the same manner as provided in Clause 6) together with an amount equal to the stamp duty (if any) on the net rent so payable, and I agree to deliver up the goods to you or your nominee at the time and place nominated by you, and in the event of such return or in the event of your having retaken possession of the goods pursuant to Clause 6, you shall dispose of the goods as soon as reasonably practicable at the best price you can reasonably obtain at the time of disposal, and if the net proceeds of such disposal (after allowing for all costs and expenses incidental to repossession and to disposal including storage) are less than the residual value stated in the schedule, I agree to pay you on demand (additionally to any rent or other moneys payable by me) the amount of the deficiency; if such net proceeds exceed the residual value the excess shall be set off against any unpaid rent in respect of the unexpired portion of the term and/or paid to me to the extent of any rent paid in advance in respect of the portion of the term unexpired at the date of return or repossession.



9. I warrant that before signing this offer I have examined the goods and have satisfied myself as to their condition and ability for my purposes and I agree that no warranty condition description or representation on your part is given and express warranty or condition as to the quality fitness safety or otherwise of the goods prior to or at any time during the continuance of the lease is hereby excluded and except as otherwise provided by law my obligations to pay rent and otherwise hereunder shall continue notwithstanding the occurrence of any defect or breakdown therein or any damage thereto falling short of destruction. And I agree that you will not be responsible for any representation or promise made by any person may have introduced this transaction to me and I agree that you shall not be liable in respect of any statements regarding my rights or position in respect of any law relating to taxation or any other matters."



16. The document is not a hire purchase agreement. It does not contain any option on the part of the lessee to acquire title. I would refuse to read into the written document an implied agreement between the partnership and the finance company that there was such a side agreement for the same reasons that were set out by this Tribunal in Case X81 90 ATC 594. There is nothing, therefore, within the agreement or in the conduct of the parties that points to the possibility of the operation of sub-section 160M(3)(d).



17. The operation of the phrase "may pass" in that sub-section, in my view, can not be viewed in retrospect. The fact that title did in fact pass to the person at a later stage, is not sufficient to bring the transaction within the terms of the paragraph. The question of the possibility of title passing to the person must be looked at at the time that use and enjoyment of the asset is obtained by that person. "May pass" can not mean a possibility at large, in the sense that anything may happen. There must be, in my view, a nexus between the possibility of title passing and the totality of the arrangements. Such a possibility will be found in the option conferred upon a purchaser under a hire purchase agreement. Whether the option is exercised is a matter for the purchaser. Until the option is exercised, it is not known whether title will pass or not but title may pass if the purchaser so requires, both parties having agreed to this arrangement.



18. In the present case, there is no obligation on the part of the finance company, the owner of the asset, present at the time when use and enjoyment of the asset was obtained by the partners to pass title to the applicant and his partners or even to give them a right to acquire title. This may be contrary to the behaviour of the clerk who gave the payout figure without hesitation. It may be contrary to the general understanding of the transaction of the applicant, (although I am not satisfied that he did not appreciate the difference) but it is the only possible interpretation that can be put upon the transaction in the way it was structured.



19. The applicant was advantaged by this structure through the deductibility of lease payments and through the deductibility of depreciation after the lease was paid out. To find now that the transaction was not, after all, one upon which those deductions could lawfully have been based, would necessarily involve re-writing the history of this whole transaction. That is not a course that is open either to the applicant or to this Tribunal. I find, therefore, that the vessel was acquired by the partnership in July 1988.



20. I now turn to the cost base, which must be taken into account in determining the amount to be deducted from the gross sale price for the purpose of calculating the capital profit.



21. The principles to be applied are set out in s 160ZH(1) in terms that were in force at the relevant time —



"s160ZH(1) Subject to the following provisions of this section, for the purposes of this Part, the cost base to a taxpayer of an asset is the sum of —



(a) the amount of any consideration in respect of the acquisition of the asset;



(b) the amount of the incidental costs to the taxpayer of the acquisition of the asset;



............



(c) the amount of any expenditure of a capital nature incurred by the taxpayer to the extent to which it was incurred for the purpose of enhancing the value of the asset and is reflected in the state or nature of the asset at the time of disposal of the asset;



(d) the amount of any expenditure of a capital nature incurred by the taxpayer to the extent to which it was incurred in establishing, preserving or defending the taxpayer's title to, or a right over, the asset; and



(e) the amount of the incidental costs to the taxpayer of the disposal of the asset."



22. The respondent contends that the consideration for the acquisition of the asset was $16,652 being the amount paid to the finance company. This consisted of the pre-arranged residual value of $12,500 plus rental payments yet to fall due. The applicant contends that this amount should be increased by the amount of $10,000 borrowed from the original vendor and paid back to him, together with the first lease payment of $50,000. This last payment certainly can not, in my view, form part of the cost base. Having regard to my finding on the legal structure of the arrangement, the sum of $50,000 can not be considered as anything other than lease rentals. The fact that a deduction was not claimed in respect of this amount, is irrelevant.



23. The applicant says that payment of the $10,000 gave the partnership an equity in the vessel. This is not consistent with the terms of the lease. The finance company purports to have 100 per cent of the ownership of the asset and the partners affirm in the lease that this is so. The situation can be tested by looking to an application of clause 8 of the lease, where the goods are to be repossessed and sold. No credit would have been given to the partnership for the sum of $10,000 in any accounting by the finance company. To all intents and purposes, the sum would have been lost. The applicant contends that the $10,000 would have been found as part of the gross proceeds of sale of the vessel and, to that extent, on a repossession sale the partnership would have received that part of the value represented by the $10,000. This seems to me conceptually flawed. The fact that the finance company paid less than market value for the vessel, rather than the fact that the partnership paid $10,000, would more properly account for any built in financial advantage which the partnership might gain on a repossession sale. Rather than give the partnership an equity in the vessel the $10,000, it seems to me, was an amount paid to the vendor as an inducement to the finance company to purchase the vessel for less than the asking price and thus to reduce the exposure of the finance company. There seems no basis on which it could be said that the partnership thereby acquired a right in rem. The $10,000 could not be considered as part of the cost of acquisition of the asset having regard to the way in which the whole transaction was structured. The cost of acquisition to be taken into account is the cost as between the finance company and the applicant when, for the first time, the applicant acquired title to the vessel.



24. For these reasons the decision under review is affirmed.
