Income Tax - Assessable Income - Superannuation Fund - failure to comply with 30/20 investment rule re public securities -exercise of discretion.

REASONS FOR DECISION

This application once again raises the question as to whether the respondent Commissioner of Taxation made a correct decision when he refused to exercise a discretion in favour of the trustee in terms of sub-section 121C(4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act").

  1. The applicant company is the trustee of a superannuation fund which would be exempt from income tax except that it failed to comply with the so called "30/20 rule" in relation to holdings of public securities (ss121C(1)).
  2. Relevant statutory provisions in section 121C are as follows:

"121C. (1A) In this section, 'superannuation fund' means a provident, benefit, superannuation or retirement fund -

(a) the income of which would, but for this Division, be exempt from income tax by virtue of paragraph 23(JA), irrespective of whether that income would be so exempt by virtue of any other paragraph of section 23; or

(b) to which section 23F applies in relation to the year of income,

but does not include a fund that is primarily and principally a hospital benefits fund, medical benefits fund, sickness benefits fund, dental benefits fund or funeral benefits fund.

(1) The investment income of a superannuation fund derived during the year of income, being a superannuation fund that was established after 1 March 1961, is not exempt from income tax by virtue of paragraph 23(ja), or section 23F, unless the Commissioner is satisfied that, at all times during that year of income -

(a) the assets of the fund included public securities the cost of which was not less than 30% of the cost of all the assets of the fund; and

(b) the assets of the fund consisting of public securities included Commonwealth securities the cost of which was not less than 20% of the cost of all the assets of the fund.

(2) ...

(3) ...

(4) For the purposes of this section, the Commissioner shall disregard any failure of the assets of a superannuation fund to include, at all times during a particular year of income, assets as provided by any provision of this section if he is satisfied -

(a) that the trustee of the fund made a genuine and bona fide attempt to ensure that the assets of that fund included, at all times during that year of income, assets as so provided; or

(b) that the failure was by reason of a temporary delay in investment,

and that, in all the circumstances, it would be reasonable to disregard the failure.

(5) Where the Commissioner is satisfied, upon application made by the trustee of a superannuation fund, that the inclusion in the assets of that fund, at any time or times, of assets as provided by any provision of this section would be likely to endanger the financial stability of that fund and would be unreasonable in the circumstances, the Commissioner may inform the trustee, by notice in writing,' that the exemption from income tax of the investment income of that fund will continue, in whole or in part and in respect of a year or years of income, as specified in the notice, and in that case the exemption continues accordingly.

(6) - (12)...".

4. The applicant trustee lodged a return of income for the year ended 30 June 1983 in March 1984. Prior to the return being lodged with the respondent the trustee wrote to a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation by letter dated 3 February 1984. The text of the letter read as follows:

"The above fund did not comply with the requirement of the 30/20 ratio at 30 June 1982.

It was not possible to arrange for the repayment of various loan monies prior to 30 June 1982 to make the necessary investment in government securities. However, since that date discussions with the principal employer entity have substantially altered this situation, and the Trustees (sic) have had the Fund comply with the 30/20 ratio as at 30 June 1983.

It is therefore requested that the discretion under Section 121C(4) of the Act be exercised in respect of the above Fund for the year ended 30 June 1983 now that the 30/20 ratio has been complied with by 30 June 1983."

5. A profit and loss account for the year ended 30 June 1983 lodged with the return of income revealed the following (cents have been ignored):

Income

Interest Received - Loans $25,532

Interest Received - Bonds 12

25,545 Expenses

Nil

Operating Profit 25,545

Deduct - Provision for Income Tax -

$25,545

6. A balance sheet as at 30 June 1983 in respect of the superannuation fund was also lodged with the return. It may be summarised as follows (cents have been ignored):

Members funds

Accumulated funds at 1/7/82 $144,884

Add Contributions Received

H Pty Ltd 100

K Pty Ltd 20

F Pty Ltd 40

Net Income for year 25,545

Accumulated Funds at 30/6/83 170,590

which are represented by:

Bank Funds 170

Interest Bearing Loans 117,815

Australian Savings Bonds 60,000 177,985

Current Liabilities

Provision for Income Tax 7,395

(carried forward from previous year) -

$170,590

  1. By a notice of assessment dated 20 August 1984 a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation notified the trustee of an assessment made on the basis that section 121D of the Act applied to the Fund and advised the trustee that the "assessment has been raised under S.121D because the fund did not comply with the requirements of S.121C". There is no evidence before the Tribunal as to whether the Deputy Commissioner turned his attention to the applicant's letter dated 3 February 1984 requesting that the discretion in sub-section 121C(4) be exercised in favour of the fund.
  2. By a notice dated 30 August 1984 the applicant objected against the assessment. The representative of the respondent objected to the assertions of fact in the notice of objection being in evidence before the Tribunal. Although I understand the objection by the Commissioner's representative to be a routine one, at the hearing it transpired that facts asserted in the notice of objection were not correct and apparently intended to mislead the Commissioner. The relevant grounds of objection are set out in full as follows:-

"3. The Trustees of the fund have made loans to the employer party to the Trust Deed in accordance with your guidelines in each year and in particular the minimum accepted rate of interest on such loans has been enforced.

  1. It was the intention of the Trustees to seek repayment of sufficient loans to meet the requirements of Section 121C(1) by 30th June, 1983 so as to take advantage of the discretion exercised by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in deeming the fund to have complied with Section 121C(1) for the whole year of income if it so complied on 30th June, 1983.
  2. On 3rd February, 1984 a letter was included with the 1983 income tax return seeking that the Deputy Commissioned of Taxation exercise his discretion as referred to above to disregard the non-compliance of the fund with Section 121C(1) of the Act for the year of income ended 30th June, 1983. A copy of the letter is enclosed.
  3. As at 30th June, 1983 the employer was able to partly repay its loan so that the Trustee was able to comply with Section 121C(1) as at 30th June, 1983. The balance sheet lodged with the 1983 tax return for the fund disclosed that 34% of the fund's assets were held in government securities.
  4. It can now be shown that during the year of income ended 30th June, 1983, all receipts into the Fund be they interest payments, new contributions or loan repayments have been applied by the Trustees in the first instance to ensure that the fund complies with Section 121C(1). From 30th June, 1981 onwards the Trustees followed a programme of investing all monies received in government securities to ensure compliance with the 30/20 ratio by 30th June, 1983 at the latest.
  5. These above grounds have been prepared on the basis that income tax has been imposed on the income of the fund for the year of income ended 30th June, 1983 as a consequence of the fund not complying with Section 121C(4) of the Act."

9. There are two fundamental errors in these assertions. Firstly the Fund did not lend its funds to any of the contributing employers. Almost the entire funds of the Fund were lent to another company (0 Pty Ltd). Secondly, the evidence establishes that the trustee continued to lend the funds of the superannuation fund to 0 Pty Ltd up to 29 June 1983 when 0 Pty Ltd paid the trustee an amount of $59,992 and the trustee purchased the Australian Savings Bonds for $60,000 on that day.

10. The evidence also establishes that contributions to the superannuation fund by the 3 employers for the year ended 30 June 1982 and amounting to $13,467 were loaned to 0 Pty Ltd on 28 June 1982 and not applied to the purchase of public securities. Loan interest of $17,448 received from 0 Pty Ltd was also loaned to 0 Pty Ltd on 28 June 1982.

  1. It can be seen then that the assertions of fact in the notice of objection are positively misleading.
  2. There was no evidence lead before the Tribunal from the directors of the trustee. Having established that facts i asserted by the trustee in the notice of object are incorrect and the failure of the trustee to give evidence before the Tribunal means that the Tribunal must assume that the trustee was not able to give evidence to support the assertions of fact in the notice of objection and relying on the principle in Jones v Dunkel [1959] HCA 8; (1959) 101 CLR 298, I so find.

13. The facts found are that the trustee took no action to invest the funds of the superannuation fund in public securities until 29 June 1983. (Exhibits E and F). Before that date it had been the consistent policy of the trustees to loan the available funds of the superannuation fund to 0 Pty Ltd notwithstanding that the trustee was advised in writing by its advisers on 20 April 1982 and 21 July 1982 (Exhibit G) that it needed to change its investment policy.

  1. The applicant's representative placed considerable emphasis on the policy statements of the respondent and Exhibit D is copies of two documents which set out in verying terms what purports to be the respondent's policy. If it was necessary for me to analyse these documents, I would have considerable difficulty in coming to a rational conclusion as to what was the respondent's policy at that time. However, in view of the state of the evidence no issue as to exercise of the discretion in sub-section 121C(4) arises in this case and I need not consider that policy further (cf Case T70 86 ATC 1029).
  2. In this case the applicant is unable to establish either:-

(a) that it made a genuine and bona fide attempt to ensure that the assets of the fund included public securities as required by sub-section 121C(1); or

(b) that the failure of the assets of the fund to include at all times during the year ended 30 June 1983 public securities as so required was by reason of a temporary delay in investment.

A deliberate failure to invest in public securities does not constitute a temporary delay in investment.

16. Having found that the applicant is unable to meet either of the tests set out in the previous paragraph I do not need to consider whether in all the circumstances it would be reasonable to disregard the failure. However, assuming that I am wrong in my conclusions as to (a) or (b) in sub-section 121C(4) I also find that there were no circumstances, on the evidence before the Tribunal, which would lead me to form the view that it would be reasonable to disregard the failure to comply with sub-section 121C(1).

  1. It was not suggested during the course of the hearing that the financial stability of the fund was other than sound, nor was it suggested that an investment in public securities would endanger the financial stability of the fund. There is therefore no issue under sub-section 121C(5).
  2. The objection decision under review will be affirmed.

This and the preceding eight (8) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT85/1074.

Deputy Registrar
