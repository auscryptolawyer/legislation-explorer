Re Taxation Appeals [1989] AATA 50; 20 ATR 3433; 22 ATR 3042 (8 March 1989)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. QT85/6600

AAT No. 5015

Income Tax

20 ATR 3433

[1989] AATA 50; 22 ATR 3042

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

K.L. Beddoe(1) (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Concessional rebates - dependent spouse - separate net income - whether contributions to superannuation fund taken into account in determining separate net income.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s.159J

Case 60 10 CTBR (NS) 365

Case 3 25 CTBR (ns) 11

HEARING

BRISBANE

8:3:1989

ORDER

The Tribunal decides that the objection decision under review be affirmed.

DECISION

The question at issue in this application is whether "separate net income" for the purposes of section 159J of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act") means the net income after deducting allowable contributions to a superannuation fund.

2. Sub-section 159J(1) provides that where, during the year of income a taxpayer contributes to the maintenance of a dependant including a spouse of the taxpayer who is a resident then the taxpayer is entitled to a rebate of tax ascertained in accordance with the section. The amount of the rebate, on the facts of this case would be $1,030 if allowable in full. There is a consequential issue in relation to the zone allowance rebate. It is not necessary to give separate consideration to the zone allowance rebate.

3. Sub-section 159J(4) provides that the amount of the rebate otherwise allowable under the section in respect of a dependant shall be reduced by $1 for every $4 by which the separate net income derived by the dependant in the year of income exceeds $282.

4. Although "separate net income" is defined in sub-section 159J(6) the definition is both exclusory and inclusive but does not assist with the issue arising in the present application. It follows that the phrase "separate net income" must have its ordinary meaning.

5. The applicant derived income during the year of income ended 30 June 1985 from employment. She was also in partnership with her husband in a business which did not produce a net income within the terms of section 90 of the Act in her hands although her spouse derived an amount from the partnership. Her return of income for the year of income disclosed claims for education expenses in respect of two children then aged 14 years and 11 years and made a declaration for medicare levy reduction purposes that the children were her dependants for the full year and had nil separate net income. One of the children was also declared on Schedule R of the return.

6. Schedule R included a claim for spouse rebate in respect of the applicant's husband for a rebate of $676. The applicant also claimed a zone allowance of $328 making a total rebate claimed of $1,004. The respondent assessed the applicant's return and allowed rebates totalling $1,157. The reason for the additional rebate allowed by the respondent is not apparent to the Tribunal.

7. No evidence was led by either party before the Tribunal. There was no dispute as to the character and the amount of the items in dispute. Those facts are adequately asserted in the notice of objection (Document T3) as follows:

"The said assessment is excessive and should be

amended as follows:

(a) Increase the spouse rebate by $297.

(b) Increase the zone rebate by $59.

The grounds upon which reliance is placed are as

follows:

1. I am entitled to a further rebate of tax pursuant

to section 159H in respect to the net income of

my spouse which was $510 for the year ended

30 June 1985.

2. Under section 82AAT my spouse is entitled to a

deduction from his assessable income of

superannuation contributions paid during the year

of income. The amount paid by him was $1,188 so

that his net income was $1,698 assessable income

less the amount of $1,188 which is a deduction

from his assessable income - net $510.

3. My husband is self employed and the contribution

by him to a superannuation fund is an allowable

deduction against his assessable income to arrive

at his net income ...".

8. It was also accepted that both the applicant and her husband lived within the Zone B for the whole of the year of income.

9. In making her return the applicant did not claim the rebates as claimed in the objection; apparently for the reason that the applicant's advisers feared that the applicant would be penalised for lodging an incorrect return if an admittedly contentious claim had been made in the return.

10. In a well prepared address the applicant's representative sought to establish that amounts which are allowable deductions for outgoings (but not losses) may be deducted from the assessable income to determine the net income. He relied on a number of authorities which reflect in some way, in his submission, on the meaning of separate net income.

11. Not only did he concede that losses could not be taken into account but he made the same concession in respect of gifts and concessional expenditure. Subject to those concessions the essence of the applicant's argument was that if an outgoing was deductible for income tax purposes then it should be taken into account in determining the separate net income of the spouse. The superannuation payment made by the spouse was deductible in terms of section 82AAT of the Act. It therefore followed that the income of the spouse should be reduced by the amount of the deductible superannuation payment to determine separate net income. Because deductions allowable under section 51 could relate to assessable income of future years the fact that deductions allowable under section 82AAT related to assessable income of future years was not determinative against the applicant.

12. It should be said straight away that the deduction allowable under section 82AAT does not depend upon the prospect of assessable income being derived in the future by the taxpayer entitled to the deduction. The section contemplates that assessable income, if any, may be derived by others.

13. In Case 60 10 CTBR (NS) 365 the Taxation Board of Review No 2 dealt with a claim for spouse rebate. The taxpayer claimed that the spouse's income should be reduced by the amount of the zone allowance to determine the separate net income. In the course of its reasons for rejecting the claim the Board said at page 366:

"... the words 'net income' mean the amount of income

remaining after deducting from the gross income the

expenditure incurred in gaining it."

14. In a later decision Taxation Board of Review No 2 decided that losses of prior years cannot be applied to reduce the income for the purposes of calculating the separate net income. That decision is reported as Case 3 25 CTBR (NS) 11. In the course of its reasons the Board said at page 12:

"7. Accordingly, we consider that the ordinary

meaning of the term, separate net income, is the

dependants gross income after deduction of the

expenses which may be regarded, in accordance with

ordinary accounting and commercial principles, as

direct charges against that income. In short,

separate net income means gross income less the

expenses incurred in deriving it - and no other

expenses."

15. The Board then went on to deal with an earlier decision of Taxation Board of Review No 1 in terms which are instructive as follows:

"9. In Case 13 9 CTBR (NS) 104, the No 1 Board, as

then constituted, had cause to examine the meaning of

the term 'separate net income' as contained in

s 82C(3). There, the Board refused to accept the

taxpayer's argument that the inclusion of the word

'net' in the phrase 'separate net income' indicated

deductibility of something over and above the direct

expenses of earning that income, leading to the view

that 'net income' in the context of s 82C(3) should be

the gross assessable income less such deductions as

were allowable under the Act other than possibly the

concessional deductions. On the basis of that

argument, the taxpayer contended that the Commissioner

was in error in not deducting from the amount of gross

income in arriving at his mother-in-law's 'separate

net income' an amount paid by her for rates on the

residence (which was admittedly not income-producing)

in which she and the taxpayer and his wife lived. In

making its finding, the Board pointed out that where

the legislature intended the words 'net income' to

have a special meaning as in s 90 and s 95 of the

Assessment Act, it was at pains to expressly so

provide. Accordingly, by attaching no special

definition to the words 'separate net income' as they

appeared in s 82C(3), the legislature had, in the

Board's view, clearly indicated that the net income of

the dependant for whom the deduction was provided in

the section, was to be ascertained in accordance with

ordinary usages and concepts, and without reference to

the provisions of the Assessment Act relating to

assessable income or allowable deductions. On this

view, 'net income' was used in contradistinction to

'gross income' and was of the same character as the

words net profit, as ordinarily understood."

16. That analysis is, in my view, correct and it would be a strange result if the 'separate net income' of a spouse could be less than the spouse's share in the net income of a partnership as provided for in section 90 where the net income from the partnership was the only source of income of the spouse. To find otherwise would be to give the words 'separate net income' a meaning other than their ordinary meaning.

17. Whether or not the superannuation contributions are deductible for taxation purposes is irrelevant as is the likelihood of those contributions resulting in assessable income at some time in the future. The answer is that such contributions had nothing to do with the income derived by the spouse during the year of income and were not in any sense a charge against that income, albeit that some of the income may have been applied to making the contributions.

18. The objection decision under review will be affirmed.
