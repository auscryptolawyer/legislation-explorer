1519/1985 and Commissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 302; (1986) 17 ATR 1219; 86 ATC 1072 (13 October 1986)

Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax: academic obtaining short term positions only - relief position terminated December 1983 - another twelve month term commencing July 1984 applied for and obtained - unemployment benefits received January to May 1984 - period occupied with research and an overseas trip (May to July 1984) - whether expenses of overseas trip allowable as a deduction in terms of section 51(1).

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) (Cth) section 51(1)

F.C. of T. v Finn [1961] HCA 61; (1961) 106 CLR 60

F.C. of T. v Hatchett 71 ATC 4184

F.C. of T. v Wilkinson 83 ATC 4295 F.C. of T. v Klan 85 ATC 4060

Case R 73 84 ATC 509

AAT No 2928

Re

And

No. 1519 of 1985

Tribunal: Mr H.P. Stevens (Senior Member)

Date : 13 October 1986

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TAXATION ) No.

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION Re:

And :

REASON: FOR DECISION

1. The question for decision in this application relates to overseas travelling expenses of $3,748.00 incurred by a lecturer during the year ended 30 June 1963 and claimed to be allowable in

terms of section 51(1). A further issue of entertainment

expenses was withdrawn at the hearing and is no longer in issue.

Born in 1949 the applicant in due course attended the University of Sydney and in 1974 graduated as a Bachelor of Arts with first class honours in Anthropology and became a research assistant in the Department of History at that University for apparently one year. From 1976 to mid 1980 the applicant was a Research scholar in the Department of Anthropology, Research School of Pacific Studies, Australian National University and from mid 1980 to June 1982 a temporary lecturer, School of

Behavourial Sciences, Macquarie University. A lecturer at the University of Sydney being on leave the applicant next became a temporary lecturer in the Department of. Geography at that

University - terminating December 1983. In 1984 the applicant

was awarded a doctorate of philosophy by the A.N.U. (Anthropology).

2.

3. Prior to December 1983 the applicant became aware that

by virtue of an Australian Aid project a position as a Visiting

Fellow would become available at the Griffith University (actually a secondment as Senior Academic Consultant to the Social Science Field Research Training Station, Hasanuddin

University, Ujung Pandang, Indonesia) and she applied for the position advertised to begin in July or August 1984. Other positions were also applied for whilst a post doctoral position

at the A.N.U. was offered to the applicant. However the "Indonesia" position was really what was wanted and an interview was conducted in December 1983 as a result of which it was

unofficially advised the application was successful. Confirmation depended upon Indonesian approval and although a formal offer of January 1984 was accepted it was not until later

in April that the matter was confirmed. Originally it was proposed to pay the applicant at the rate previously paid at Sydney University (lecturer step 4) but upon representations this was increased to lecturer step 5 - the salary that would have been received if the temporary lectureship had not terminated. Upon completion in August/September 1985 of the Visiting Fellow position a further engagement was obtained at Macquarie University (tenured appointments are difficult to obtain).

4. The Visiting Fellow was a position the applicant had wanted for sometime as it was obviously an advantage in terms of a career to go back to where the applicant had been whilst

studying. Some concern was expressed as to how the applicant

would survive until July 1984 without a paid position but the

applicant considered it would be an advantage. Because a tenured position had not been occupied (only fixed term appointments) the applicant had not had a "sabbatical" and the break could be put to the same use. Papers could be finished, research conducted and field work (overseas) carried out - also it would give a breathing space. As part of the utilisation of this break but in no way connected with the obtaining of the Visiting Fellow appointment it was arranged to go on an extended overseas trip through South East Asian countries from 17 May 1984 to 7 July

1984 - Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia and Singapore. The total expenditure including the costs of a two week holiday in Southern Thailand was $3,968.00 of which $3,748.00 was applicable to the year ended 30 June 1984.

  1. Having determined not to accept any appointment during the break from the end of December 1983 to July 1984, the applicant applied for unemployment benefits and was in receipt of income from such source from 12 January to 21 May 1984 (terminated when left for overseas and appointment taken up shortly after return).
  2. The claim made in the applicant's return of income was disallowed as private expenditure and an objection was lodged claiming that-the amount was properly allowable in terms of section 51(1). In elaboration it was stated:

"Your assessor has disallowed expenditure of $3,748.00 relating to an overseas study trip undertaken by the taxpayer on the basis that

4.

it is private expenditure. Your assessor might not have understood that the taxpayer is an academic anthropologist involved in the study of the peoples of the third world

specifically South East Asia. The trip undertaken was not of a 'touristy' nature as can be determined from the itinerary included

in the return. She travelled to remote rural

villages studying their life style and

religious ceremonies, etc. All of this study was recorded in detailed notes taken during the trip as well as 400 photographs all of

which will be used in the taxpayer's

employment with Universities. Prior to the trip the taxpayer held academic positions in the field of south East Asian studies with Australian Universities and subsequent to the

trip she obtained a position with an

Indonesian University in this area.

We prepare the Income Tax Returns of numerous academics and the concept of overseas study leave as a periodic essential prerequisite of their employment has been well established with your office and we are disappointed that you have departed from this principle in this

assessment. The essential character of the

expenditure in the earning of assessable

income has, we believe, been clearly illustrated in both the return lodged and this objection."

7. There is no dispute that the activities overseas were appropriate to the stated objective nor that the sum of $3,748.00 was incurred in relation thereto (accordingly it has been unnecessary to detail the activities or the expenditure). There is also no dispute that working academics (those in full time employment) who incurred such expenditure would be entitled to a deduction in terms of the section which provides:

"51.(1) All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or

5

producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt home."

The short issue is whether a person who is not in full time employment and is, as it were, between jobs similarly entitled.

• For the applicant it was contended she should be viewed

in the same light as the tenured academics. She was in fact an academic of some standing and there was clearly a "perceived connnection" between the expenditure and her earning of assessable

income. It was not necessary for the trip to have been an

express or implied term of her appointment (F.C. of T. v

Wilkinson 83 ATC 4295) whilst the applicant's case was stronger than the taxpayer in Case R 20 84 ATC 201. Reliance was also placed on F.C. of T. V Finn [1961] HCA 61; (1961) 106 CLR 60 and F.C. of T v Hatchett 71 ATC 4184 whilst it was said the "spend money to earn more" test was satisfied as also the "making advancement more certain" test.

The question of the application of section 51(1) has been a vexed one and in Case R73 84 ATC 509 (in my then, capacity as Chairman, Board of Review No.1) I reviewed some authorities (including Finn and Hatchett (supra)) and said at p.513:

6.

"15. This consideration of some of the subsequent cases reinforces my view that the 'purity' of Finn is to be found in the

attitude of mind of Dixon C.J. It is not to be found in the extraction of a general principle that all professionals are entitled to a deduction in respect of the 'costs of keeping up to date' (for Dixon C.J. went out of his way not to so find) but rather in the reminder that it is the words of the section (and not a statement of broad propositions) that are determinative in relation to the particular facts of a case. The Full Federal Court's recent decision in Martin v F.C. of T. -84 ATC 4513 adds further support to this view."

10. On the facts of this case the Tribunal is satisfied the requirements of the section are not satisfied and that it cannot properly be said that the expenditure was incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income of the applicant - accepting future income is as relevant as current income. It is recognised that this may be said to unfairly differentiate between academics in employment and academics between jobs but the authorities establish that "there is a difference of fundamental importance between obtaining a new appointment and seeking preferment to a higher or different, but better paid, position with the same employer" (per Ormiston J. in F.C. of T. v Klan 85 ATC 4060 at p.4067). In that case His Honour was considering a claim by a

school teacher who went overseas after leaving a teaching post in

Australia with the object of gaining "invaluable teaching experience of a kind" which would demonstrate he was "deserving of appointment to senior and better remunerated positions" back

in Australia. With some reluctance His Honour rejected a claim

for a deduction in terms of S.51(1).

11. In the course of his judgment Ormiston J reviewed all the authorities (Finn and Hatchett and many others) before reaching his conclusion. In the course of his reasoning His Honour said at pp.4067 and 4068 respectively:

"Taking a broad approach, it could be said that teaching was the activity carried on by the

taxpayer over the years for the gaining of

assessable income. However, although Mr Klan taught throughout his period overseas, he did not earn assessable income during that time, and there was, both in planning and execution, a clear break in his employment so that the payments were clearly not incidental and relevant to the gaining of assessable income in the period of the break. This appeal is quite different from Finn's case (supra) where an architect travelled overseas to increase his professional knowledge during his long-service and recreational leave and returned thereafter to the same position.

On the other, hand, if one looks to the gaining and producing of assessable income in the future, there can be little doubt that the motive, object and purpose of the taxpayer was to equip himself the better to carry out his teaching activities on his return and thereby earn a higher assessable income. But was the expenditure incurred 'in' or 'in the course of gaining or producing that future income, or was it incidental and relevant to that end? Relevant it may have been to his new post, but it is difficult to characterise the payments as incidental to the earning of income in that post.

"Although the deduction claimed in Maddalena's case was for rather mundane expenditure, the principles there stated are directly relevant to the present case and they solve the temporal dilemma posed by the requirement that outgoings should be incurred 'in', i.e. in the course of, gaining or producing assessable income. It is one thing to assert that it is unnecessary that the outgoing need produce, as a matter of causation., assessable income in the year in which the outgoing was incurred. It is quite another to say that expenditure incurred which is entirely unrelated to existing activities which produce assessable income can come within the section because its object and long-term effect is better to equip the taxpayer to earn income in the future."

8.



12. It is considered His Honour's judgment supports the conclusion reached that the expenditure in question was not incurred in the course of gaining or producing assessable income. At the stage it was incurred, the applicant was unemployed and in receipt of unemployment benefits. Although the expenditure might have been relevant in a general sense to the subsequent post at The Griffith University it could not be characterised "as incidental to the earning of income in that post." Accordingly it is necessary to affirm the decision of the Commissioner in disallowing the applicant's objection to her assessment for the year ended 30 June 1984.
