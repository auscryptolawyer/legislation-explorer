ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - Income Tax - whether partnership existed - at what price used drums purchased - percentage of wastage of drums -whether gratuities paid - nature of payment in 1982 year of income and whether allowable deduction - whether additional tax should be varied

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 6, 51, 226

Taxation Administration Act 1953 s. 14ZG

Case N56 81 ATC 277

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Wade [1951] HCA 66; (1951) 84 CLR 105

REASONS FOR DECISION

14 April 1988 Deputy President R.D. Nicholson

Decision Under Review

The Applicant seeks review of a decision by the Commissioner disallowing the Applicant's objections made pursuant to the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") against the assessment of income tax on income said to be derived by him during the years of income 1978-1982 inclusive. The notices of objection each sought the excision from assessable income of sums of money relating to sales and purchases and a further sum in respect of additional tax. The issues raised by the Applicant's objections appear in more detail in the reasons which follow.

Evidence

The Tribunal had before it documents provided pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (as modified by section 14ZG of the Taxation Administration Act 1953) and exhibits made available during the hearing. On behalf of the Applicant it heard evidence from the Applicant and his wife; from two former employees of a company with which the Applicant dealt; and from two persons engaged in - the same type of business as that from which the Applicant derived income under consideration in this proceeding. On behalf of the Respondent evidence was heard from an auditor of the Australian Taxation Office.

Outline of facts

In 1976 the Applicant retired as a production supervisor with an oil company. Towards the end of that year or early in 1977 he commenced carrying on the business of buying used drums and selling them to reconditioners. Approximately 12 months later he entered into an arrangement with a reconditioning company whereby the drums were reconditioned on his behalf and sold by him mainly to oil companies.

The success of his business depended upon the acquisition of used drums. Initially these were collected on a small trailer by the Applicant but as the volume of purchases and deliveries increased, he had an arrangement with the reconditioning company whereby they would pick up and deliver drums when their trucks were in the relevant areas.

Upon arrival at the reconditioning company's premises the drums were inspected and drums unsuitable for reconditioning were thereupon rejected. The reconditioning process for the remainder involved washing, reshaping and painting. The rejected drums became the property of the reconditioning company, which disposed of them.

To assist in locating used drums the Applicant toured industrial areas. Later he developed connections with people on industrial premises and they would alert him to the availability of drums.

Existence of partnership

The first issue raised by the objections is whether the Applicant carried on his business in partnership with his wife. The evidence lead for the Applicant establishes that he sought his accountant's advice as to the manner in which he should conduct his business and was advised he should form a partnership with his wife. His wife considered the proposition and agreed to become a partner. As a consequence of that she stayed home each week morning to receive phone calls related to the business and. to relay them to the reconditioning company. On occasions she assisted in entertainment to promote the business. She did not receive a wage. A joint bank account was opened and operated by both parties. Moneys earned from the conduct of the business were paid into the joint account. The Applicant's wife at one time loaned to the business a sum of money unsecured with no interest, the sum being subsequently repaid when the cash flow of the business permitted. In the 1977 year of income the Commissioner had accepted the partnership between the Applicant and his wife.

On behalf of the Respondent it was argued (as it had been established on the evidence) that there was no partnership agreement; that no business name was registered; that the Applicant's wife was not held out as a partner; that a motor vehicle and trailer employed in the business were registered in the name of the Applicant only; that the bank at which the joint account operated had advised in August 1977 that it was not aware of the existence of a partnership and that the account was conducted as a joint account only; and that no capital contribution had been made by the Applicant's wife, the only sum advanced by her being by way of loan. The true position it was said was that there had been no change to the business previously commenced by the Applicant as a sole trader and that what the Applicant's wife did reflected the usual arrangement between husband and wife where a wife was engaged in duties of a minor nature. In particular, it was said that the Applicant's wife had not been in receipt of profits. The evidence established that the profits paid into the joint account were withdrawn by the Applicant, paid into an access account in his name and his wife's name and then invested in his name only. The evidence also established that accounts were rendered in the name of the Applicant only, his explanation of this being that it was he who had the established reputation in the trade. The second hand dealer's licence utilised for the purpose of the business was held in his name only.

In Case N56 81 ATC 277 at 281 it was said:

"... for a partnership to exist the law requires more than both a bona fide intention of the parties to act as partners (see Waddington v. O'Callaghan (1931) 16 T.C. 187 at p. 199) and a general understanding between the parties that they are in business as partners (see I.R. Commrs. v. Williamson [(1928) 14 T.C. 335] at p. 340). ... Also, banks, suppliers and customers having dealings with a partnership have a right to know that they are trading with a partnership, as opposed to dealing with an individual, stemming from the obligation of partners to meet the partnership debts to the full extent of their own resources."

Looking therefore beyond the intention of the parties, regard must be had to the receipt of moneys in the joint account given that, in accordance with the definition of "partnership" in sub-section 6(1) of the Act, receipt of income jointly is sufficient to create a partnership for the purposes of the Act. In the view of the Tribunal the following statement in paragraph 24 of Case N56 (supra) is applicable in this proceeding on this point：

"24. Whilst it would seem that all business receipts of moneys were paid into the joint account, it does not necessarily follow that the husband and wife were in receipt of income jointly. The account was not specifically designated as a business account, and it is of course common practice for husbands and wives to operate joint bank accounts financed solely from the husband's own income. ... However, the overriding reason for ruling that they were not in receipt of income jointly stems from the fact that they never became jointly entitled to the profits. It was only the taxpayer who in a real sense conducted the business and therefore the profits of the business were his, and his only."

That statement remains true in the opinion of the Tribunal even taking into account evidence of a withdrawal of a sum of money in respect of wedding expenses of the daughter of the Applicant and his wife.

Furthermore, the acceptance by the Commissioner of the existence of the partnership in the year of income prior to the years here in question is not an estoppel against the operation of the Act in the years here under consideration - Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Wade [1951] HCA 66; (1951) 84 CLR 105 at 117.

Accordingly, the Tribunal finds that in the years of income with which this proceeding is concerned there was not a partnership between the Applicant and his wife in respect of the business activities in question,

Purchase price of drums

Figures relied upon by the Commissioner (Exhibit A) and described as "Number of drums delivered to [preconditioning company]" showed 54,319 in that category. In the Commissioner's calculations an average estimated cost of $1.50 was allowed for the purchase of each drum. The Applicant contends that the amount allowed should be $2.50. His evidence, together with the evidence of his witnesses, was to the effect that the usual cost of such drums lay between $3 and $5 and that acceptance of $2.50 as an average cost would be more than fair, as it were, to the Commissioner.

Consideration of the matter was handicapped by unavailability of the day book of purchases maintained by the Applicant. This had been delivered to the Taxation Office where it had remained for inspection. It was released to an accountant representing the Applicant and mislaid by him. Only a small portion of it had been photocopied. In addition, in a letter from the Applicant's solicitors in 1983 an average price of $1.50 per drum was proposed.

It became apparent during the course of evidence, however, that the average price of $1.50 per drum was only an average price if the total number of drums (90,340) purchased by the Applicant was taken into account. The difference between that figure and 54,319 is represented by drums not utilised for reconditioning purposes and thus not taken into account in the Commissioner's figures relating to sales of reconditioned drums. This difference also explains the figure advanced by the Applicant's solicitors.

In the opinion of the Tribunal the evidence succeeds in establishing the proposition advanced on behalf of the Applicant, namely that the average cost of the drums purchased for reconditioning purposes should be allowed at $2.50.

Wastage rate

The Applicant next contends that allowance should be made by the Commissioner for purchases of drums which proved to be defective and unsaleable and proposes the allowance of 10% wastage at the average rate allowed for the purchase of each drum. The evidence of the Applicant was that wastage was 10-20%; his other witnesses said 20%; 10-15% and "20 drums out of a load of 120". It became apparent that a letter written by the Applicant's solicitors in 1983 suggesting a 5-10% wastage rate again related to the overall purchases by the Applicant and not only to those used in the reconditioning process (described as A class drums).

The evidence of the taxation auditor was that during the course of an investigation of the Applicant the two employees of the reconditioning company said that no reject rate applied to the Applicant's drums for reconditioning because reject drums were exchanged for drums of the reconditioning company or other customers. This was denied by the Applicant's witnesses. No record of the interview at which the admissions were said to have taken place was produced to the Tribunal or the witnesses. In considering this evidence the Tribunal has weighed the fact that the two employees of the reconditioning company are persons who each admitted receipt of secret commissions from the Applicant.

Having weighed the evidence in that light the Tribunal nevertheless considers that the Applicant has discharged the onus upon him of establishing that allowance should be made of a 10% wastage rate in respect of drums rejected as unsuitable for the reconditioning process.

Gratuities

The Applicant next claims that in making purchases of drums from dealers he gave gratuities to labour for 3 purposes namely, assistance in loading; assistance in the care of the drums prior to collection (by insertion of bungs to prevent corrosion); and assistance in offloading upon delivery. He claims that such gratuities averaged approximately $1 per drum and that this should be taken into account by the Commissioner.

In the opinion of the Tribunal the evidence before it on behalf of the Applicant on this issue fails to discharge the onus of proof. There is an entire absence of record to support the claim for an allowance of $1 per drum. In addition, the evidence which may tend to corroborate the Applicant's evidence is of the most general nature. The Tribunal does not consider the Applicant can succeed in this aspect of his objection.

Disallowance of deduction in 1982 year of income

In the 1982 year of income the Applicant objects to the disallowance by the Commissioner of the sum of $30,000 which he claims was made in the ordinary course of his business. The invoice in respect of which the payment was made was for cartage of 56,000 drums from 1 April 1977 to 31 May 1982 ($11,200) and for supply of 3,760 drums between 1 July 1977 and 31 May 1982 ($18,800). The invoice was paid by cheque to the sole owner of the reconditioning company on 1 June 1982.

It should be said at once that the Applicant cannot succeed in his claim for deduction of the full amount because evidence established that some $8，000 of it was given to him by the former employees of the reconditioning company. The only question is therefore whether he has a deduction in respect of the remainder of it.

In the course of his business dealings with the reconditioning company during the years of income in issue the Applicant paid to those two employees a sum of money which represented the equivalent to what he was drawing from his business. Those employees each subsequently admitted, receipt of such secret commission and paid tax on the sum of $50,000 each. The Applicant made the payments because he considered that if the reconditioning company would not assist him with pick up and delivery of drums and making available its reconditioning process (which it did at a rate advantageous to the Applicant) he would be put out of business. When the owner ascertained what was being done by the company in favour of of the Applicant he terminated the employment of each of the persons who were being paid by the Applicant and rendered the invoice in question to the Applicant.

It had been the Applicant's understanding that the charge for reconditioning which he was paying to the company included cartage so that at the time of paying the invoice he did not consider that that part of it relating to cartage was a justifiable charge. As has already been seen, the Applicant has denied that his reject drums were substituted by drums of the reconditioning company or its customers. The Tribunal has accepted that position so that the second part of the invoice is not sustainable on the evidence.

The true explanation for the invoice and the payment which followed it appears from the Applicant's evidence, namely that if the payment had not been made the owner of the reconditioning company would have called in detectives to investigate "collusion" between the Applicant and the employees of that company. The amount was invoiced and the payment made to avoid such investigation and any legal proceedings which may follow from it.

Accordingly, the Tribunal is not satisfied the Applicant has established that the outgoing (to the extent paid by him) was incurred in gaining or producing assessable income or necessarily incurred in carrying on his business to that end as provided for within sub-section 51(1) of the Act. In any event, even if it was, the Tribunal is disposed to think (although the matter was not fully argued before it) that such outgoing would be in the nature of a capital outgoing directed to defending the capital of the business or (more likely in its view) an outgoing of a private nature directed to protecting the good name and reputation of the Applicant and the two former employees of the reconditioning company. Accordingly, it is the opinion of the Tribunal that the Applicant cannot succeed on this limb of his objections.

Additional tax

The following amounts have been assessed in each of the years of income by way of additional tax: 1978 - $10,813; 1979 - $9,932; 1980 - $62,224; 1981 - $41,659 and 1982 $30,852. The Applicant seeks reduction of additional tax to nil or alternatively, assessment of an amount less than an amount included in the amended assessments. The Applicant requests this occur pursuant to the former section 226(3) which continues to apply in relation to additional tax accrued before 13 December 1984.

Before the Tribunal as attachment 5 to Exhibit A was the Commissioner's calculations of additional tax in respect of which that taxation auditor gave evidence. The starting point he said was the amount of tax avoided as a result of overclaimed deductions and other adjustments.

On behalf of the Applicant it was said that a recalculation of the liability to tax of the Applicant taking into account the position contended for by the Applicant in relation to the purchase price of drums and wastage in particular, resulted in no penalty tax being appropriate in respect of the 1979 or 1982 years; no real cause for imposition of it in respect of the 1978 year of income and acceptance of imposition of the additional and penalty taxes in the 1980 and 1981 years of income. On behalf of the Respondent it was said that it was appropriate that the penalty imposed by the Commissioner be imposed in all years of income given the exaggeration of expenditure; the secret commissions; attempts not to provide information; failure to keep records; the nature of the payment to the owner of the reconditioning company and the failure to disclose all facts concerning the nature of the partnership.

The evidence of the taxation auditor was that there were frustrations in the investigation of the matter. He referred, in particular, to one of the employees of the reconditioning company providing a letter to the Respondent in which the purported rejection rate of the drums was said to be 40%. The letter had been prepared by the Applicant who obtained the employee's signature on it. The employee admitted to the rejection rate being overstated. In addition, the auditor was told by one of the reconditioning company employees that the Applicant had told him to state that the sum of money advanced to enable the payment of the aforementioned sum of $30,000 had been advanced by way of loan when that was not the case.

The Tribunal has had regard to taxation ruling IT2012 (remission under s. 226(3) of additional tax imposed by s. 226(2) of the Act); to the discussion of that guideline in Case U36 87 ATC 266; to North Coast Grazing Pty. Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation 87 ATC 4553; and Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Rabinov 83 ATC 4437. In its view, the facts in this case do not justify setting aside or varying the mode of assessment of additional tax adopted by the Commissioner. The case for the Applicant on this point really comes down to an argument that because the Commissioner did not calculate the purchase price of drums and wastage rate in accordance with the findings of the Tribunal in this proceeding after it has had the benefit of extensive hearing of evidence, the penalties upon the Applicant should be reduced. In the opinion of the Tribunal that is not a reason falling within the guidelines as a basis for variation of the additional tax, nor do other circumstances justify that course.

Conclusion

For the reasons given the Tribunal will set aside the objection decisions under review and remit each of them to the Respondent for assessment of the liability of the taxpayer in the years of income 1978-1982 inclusive in accordance with the following directions:

  1. the Applicant did not carry on business in partnership with his wife;
  2. allowance be made for an average purchase price of drums for use in reconditioning of $2.50;
  3. allowance be made for a 10% wastage of drums purchased for use in reconditioning at the aforesaid average rate;
  4. the Applicant has not established his entitlement to an allowance of $1 per drum for gratuities;
  5. the sum of $30,000 disallowed by the Commissioner in the 1982 year of income was properly disallowed;
  6. the additional tax payable by the Applicant be recalculated following reassessment of his income on the basis previously used by the Commissioner namely,
    • - penalty at 40%
    • - penalty at 20%
    • - penalty at 10% per annum to 13.2.83
    • - penalty at 20% per annum from 14.2.83 to 16.9.83
