Re Brian Austin and Tax Agents' Board (New South Wales) [1985] AATA 50 (8 March 1985)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: BRIAN AUSTIN

And: TAX AGENTS' BOARD (NEW SOUTH WALES)

No. N84/483

Tax Agents' Board

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.K. Todd (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Tax Agents' Board - refusal to grant licence - review by AAT

Administrative Appeals Tribunal - jurisdiction - Tax Agents' Board - review of refusal to grant tax agent's licence

Law reform - Administrative Review Council - amendments to ITAA

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s.25(1)

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss.251J, 251K

Re Chapman and Tax Agents' Board of South Australia (1984) 5 ALN N552 (No.359)

Re Qantas Airways Ltd and Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (WA) (1979) 2 ALD 291

HEARING

CANBERRA

8:3:1985

ORDER

The Tribunal decides that it has no jurisdiction in this matter and will proceed no further herein.

DECISION

In this matter the applicant purported to apply for review of a decision of the abovenamed respondent refusing to grant to the applicant a tax agent's licence pursuant to the power conferred on the respondent in that behalf by s.251J of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITA Act").

2. Section 25 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act") provides so far as relevant as follows:

"25(1) An enactment may provide that applications

may be made to the Tribunal -

(a) for review of decisions made in the exercise

of powers conferred by that enactment; or

(b) for the review of decisions made in the

exercise of powers conferred, or that may be

conferred, by another enactment having effect

under that enactment.

(2) ....

(3) ....

(3A) ....

(4) The Tribunal has power to review any decision

in respect of which application is made to it

under any enactment."

3. The relevant provisions of the ITA Act are s.251J and s.251K. Section 251J provides for the registration of tax agents by a Tax Agents' Board but makes no provision for review by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal or otherwise of any decision in relation thereto, and in particular makes no provision for review of a decision by a Tax Agents' Board refusing to register a person as a tax agent. Section 251K provides as follows:

"251K(1) Registration as a tax agent shall remain

in force until cancelled in accordance with this

Act.

(2) A Board may cancel the registration of any

tax agent upon being satisfied that -

(a) any return which has been prepared by or on

behalf of the tax agent is false in any

material particular; unless the tax agent

establishes to the satisfaction of the Board

that he had no knowledge of the falsity or that

the falsity was due to his inadvertence;

(b) the tax agent -

(i) has neglected the business of a

principal;

(ii) has been guilty of misconduct as a tax

agent; or

(iii) is not a fit and proper person to remain

registered; or

(c) in the case of a partnership or company - a

nominee of the tax agent is not a fit and

proper person to be such a nominee, or that a

person who has become a member of the

partnership, or a director, or manager or other

administrative officer, of the company is under

the age of 21 years or is not of good fame,

integrity and character.

(2A) A Board shall cancel the registration of a

tax agent if the tax agent -

(a) was registered on 1 April in any year; and

(b) has failed to give notice to the Board, and

furnish particulars, in accordance with section

251JA within the time allowed by or under that

section after that 1 April.

(3) A Board shall cancel the registration of an

individual as a tax agent upon his death or

bankruptcy, or his permanently ceasing to carry on

business as a tax agent.

(4) A Board shall cancel the registration of a

tax agent which is a partnership or a company -

(a) if there is no nominee registered in respect of

the tax agent;

(b) in the case of a company, if the company goes

into liquidation or, in the case of a

partnership, if any partner becomes bankrupt;

or

(c) if it permanently ceases to carry on business

as a tax agent.

(5) An application may be made to the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of the

cancellation under this section of the registration

of a tax agent, being a cancellation made after the

commencement of this sub-section.

(7) ....

(8) ...."

4. It is clear from the provisions set out above that the Administrative Appeals tribunal has power to review decisions of a Tax Agents' Board to cancel the licence of a tax agent, and that power has been exercised on a number of occasions, the latest being Re Chapman and Tax Agents' Board of South Australia (1984) 5 ALN N552 (No.359). The reason why the Tribunal has such a power of review is that it has been specifically conferred upon the Tribunal by s.251K(5).

5. There is no specific power conferred on this Tribunal by the ITA Act or by any other legislation to review a decision of a Tax Agents' Board to refuse to grant a licence. The argument put by the solicitor for the applicant was that the Tribunal should imply a power to assume jurisdiction in the matter, such implication to be founded upon presumptions available in the interpretation of statutes. These were said to be, as set out in a written submission from the applicant's solicitor:

"(a) The intention of Parliament should be

implemented.

(b) The interpretation should not produce an

absurdity.

(c) No person should be in peril because of

ambiguity.

(d) The common law can only be replaced by clear

words.

(e) No interpretation should be construed as to

give a person the right to profit from their

own wrong.

(f) The jurisdiction of the Court cannot be

ousted."

6. Various authorities were referred to in support of the existence of these presumptions, and the purposive approach to statutory interpretation was invoked. It was said that in dealing with an Act as an entirety it was necessary to look at the mischief which the Act was designed or intended to prevent. The omission of words which would bring the refusal to grant a licence as well as refusal to renew a licence within the scope of s.251K(5) was said to be a matter which a Court could imply so as to prevent an absurdity. The AAT Act was, it was said, clearly designed to give relief to a person aggrieved by a decision of an administrative body, and it would be too narrow a construction of s.251K to exclude from review a decision refusing to grant a licence and thereby grossly affecting the right of a citizen to earn his livelihood.

7. There is in my opinion no possibility in law that the submission put for the applicant could be correct. The Administrative Appeals Tribunal is a creature of statute. Its powers of review are conferred not generally, with stated exceptions, as are the powers of investigation conferred upon the Ombudsman by the Ombudsman Act 1976, but specifically, as provided by s.25(1) and (4) of the AAT Act. There is no way in which a power of review can be implied. Indeed in my view the very specificity of the way in which s.251K has conferred upon the AAT a power of review only in the case of cancellation of a licence tends rather to the conclusion that it was intended to confer only the power of review as conferred and as so limited.

8. Reference may be made to Re Qantas Airways Ltd and Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (WA) (1979) 2 ALD 291 where it was contended that the words "under any enactment" in s.25(4) of the AAT Act conferred jurisdiction on the Tribunal to review a decision made under any enactment. The position was made very clear in the following passage in the Reasons for Decision of Davies J (now the President of the Tribunal) where at p.292 he said:

"It was put by counsel for Qantas Airways Ltd that,

if this Tribunal has a discretion to do so, it

should exercise the discretion in favour of

reviewing the decision. If the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal is authorized to review the

decision no doubt it should do so. The empowering

of the Tribunal to review a decision ordinarily

confers upon the Tribunal not merely an authority

but also a duty to do so whenever proper

application is made to it. However, the real

question is whether the Tribunal is empowered to

review the subject decision.

In argument, counsel for Qantas Airways Ltd relied

upon the provisions of s 25 of the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, particularly the

provisions of sub-s (4) that, 'The Tribunal has

power to review any decision in respect of which

application is made to it under any enactment'. He

submitted, as a matter of construction, that the

words 'under any enactment' qualify the word

'decision' and not the word 'application'. In my

view, the expression qualifies 'application' so

that the Tribunal has power to review a decision

only in those cases 'in respect of which

application is made to it under any enactment'.

Were there any doubt about this, which I think

there is not, that doubt is resolved by the

context. Section 25(1) provides that 'An enactment

may provide that applications may be made to the

Tribunal ...'. An application for which provision

is so made is an application made under an

enactment."

9. It is to be noted that the Administrative Review Council has recommended that the ITA Act should be amended to confer jurisdiction on the Administrative Appeals Tribunal. See also the comments of Deputy President A.N. Hall in Re Chapman above in paragraph 48 of his Reasons for Decision where he said:

"48. In conclusion, I would recommend that two

aspects of Pt VIIA of the Income Tax Assessment Act

1936 (Cth) warrant reconsideration:

(i) ....

(ii) The limited rights of review pursuant to

s.251K(5) appear to warrant reconsideration. Apart

from the curious anomaly shown up in the present

case with respect to decisions taken under

s.251J(11)(d), there is a question whether

decisions refusing to grant registration as a tax

agent (see s 251J(3)) should be reviewable on the

merits (cf the recommendation to that effect by the

Administrative Review Council in its 1978 Annual

Report, paras 54-56)."

10. In my opinion the Tribunal clearly has no jurisdiction in this matter and I shall order accordingly.
