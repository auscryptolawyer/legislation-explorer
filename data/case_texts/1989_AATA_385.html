INCOME TAX - Additional tax for late return - Nature of obligation - Address for service of notices - Communication of address - Prosecution for late lodgment - Withdrawal of prosecution upon undertaking to lodge return - Whether binding undertaking not to impose penalties for late return.

INCOME TAX - Additional tax for late return - Review of penalties.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 S.226(1), (3) & (4), S.223, S.224, S.225, S.161, S.207

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT87/4261

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

12 May 1989 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. Section 226(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 ("the Act") as in force at all material times provided:

"Notwithstanding anything contained in the Sections 223, 224 and 225, any taxpayer who fails to duly furnish as and when required by this Act or the regulations, or by the Commissioner, any return or any information in relation to any matter affecting either his liability to tax or the amount of the tax, shall be liable to pay as additional tax an amount equal to the tax assessable to him or the amount of $2-00 whichever is the greater."

  1. The Commissioner alleges that the Applicant failed "to duly furnish as and when required" by that sub-section an income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1983.
  2. It is not disputed that the primary tax payable in relation the taxable income derived in that year by the Applicant was other than $15,944-47. It follows that, in the absence of anything else, should the Commissioner be correct the additional tax for which the Applicant became liable by force of Section 226 (1) was $15,944-47. It can be observed at the outset that the amount of that liability was to be fixed independently of any consideration as to monies in the hands of the Commissioner on or before any date at which the Commissioner might have assessed the taxpayer. It would not matter whether the amounts so in hand represented tax instalment deductions from salary; provisional tax paid pursuant to an earlier assessment; or amounts paid to the Commissioner as a voluntary act on the part of the taxpayer to establish a credit. It would not matter that upon being duly assessed to tax (but not additional tax) the Applicant would be entitled to a refund from the Commissioner.
  3. However, two further provisions of Section 226 need to be noted. They are Sub-section (3) which provided:

"The Commissioner may in any case, for reasons which he thinks sufficient, and either before or after making any assessment, remit the additional tax or any part thereof";

and Sub-section (4):

"If in any case in which a taxpayer is liable to pay additional tax under this section a taxation prosecution is instituted in respect of the same subject matter, the additional tax shall not be payable unless and until the prosecution is withdrawn."

  1. Expressed in the simplest terms this was a situation in which the Commissioner did not consider the return of income to be "late" until 31 December 1983 had passed; that no return having been received by 2 February 1984 a "Final Notice" issued that day; that no return having been received, on 4 May 1984 a taxation prosecution was instituted by an Information and Summons in the Court of Petty Sessions at Sydney against the Applicant; that on 23 August 1984 the Commissioner consented to the dismissal of the complaint and received from the Applicant his income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1984; that on 1 November 1984 the Commissioner assessed the Applicant as having derived a taxable income (as returned) of $42,063-00 and assessed the tax thereon at $15,944-47; and that he also levied additional tax for late return at $1,983-20 rather than $15,944.47.
  2. The Applicant objected to the imposition of additional tax and upon his objection being disallowed requested reference of the decision upon his objection to this Tribunal for independent review. That was on 26 June 1985. Nearly two years later, in May 1987 the Commissioner complied with his duty to refer the matter to this Tribunal. The matter was heard in November 1988 and April 1989.
  3. The Applicant objects to the imposition of additional tax on the basis:
    • (a) that by reason of an agreement allegedly entered into on 23 August 1984 the Commissioner undertook not to impose any penalties upon the Applicant in relation to the non-lodgment to that date of his 1983 return provided he lodged the return that day; and, alternatively,
    • (b) that the remissions allowed by the Commissioner in consequence of performing his duty pursuant to Section 226(3) are inadequate in that there should have been either a remission of the whole, or at least of a greater part, of the remissions fixed by Section 226(1) than has been allowed.
  4. The Applicant is a medical practitioner. He was and remains irate at the injustices which he considers himself to have suffered at the hands of incompetent and inconsistent officers involved in the administration of the Act. His self-confidence is considerable, as are no doubt his abilities. But a capacity to be objective in appraising his own actions and those of his representatives and in recognising how he has contributed to his present difficulties is deficient. Those difficulties were compounded by:

changes in his choice of tax agent;

a falling out over the years with both of them;

confusion on his part as to the "address for service" to be used by the Commissioner; and

a practice on the part of the Applicant of dealing personally with some aspects of his taxation affairs, dealings which he was not well equipped to undertake.

  1. I accept that the Applicant's experience had been that his agent had always lodged his income tax return in the February following the close of the relevant year for assessment. However it seems that in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1982 the only "return" ever lodged for the Applicant was one not signed by him; a return not seen by him; a return which purported to have been signed by an agent of the Applicant. It was lodged by the then tax agent (TA1) to the Applicant and by-its terms explicitly nominated the address of TA1 as the Applicant's "address for service" for the purposes of the Act. The return disclosed that the Applicant was a self employed medical practitioner who had derived a gross practice income of $90,988; had in that practice generated a net loss of $29-00 (sic); and, by reason of other income, had derived a taxable income of $3,122-00. On 11 May 1983 the Commissioner issued a Refund Notice and forwarded to the Applicant by letter addressed to him at his then recorded address for service - the address of TA1 - a refund cheque for $8,800-00. On the same day the Commissioner wrote requesting information relating to the Applicant's 1982 return. That query was also addressed to the Applicant C/- TA1.
  2. At some later date though it is not clear what date, TA1 forwarded that letter to the Applicant. At 30 June 1983 another year of income ended and with that came an obligation in the Applicant to lodge a further return of income - that for 1983. However, because of the place the Applicant had on the lodgment program of TA1 the Commissioner took the view - a view continued to the hearing - that the Applicant had until 31 December 1983 to lodge his 1983 return. No evidence was presented to indicate that any later date should have been considered - either as a matter of law or as a matter of practice - as the date sufficient to satisfy the lodgment requirements of the Commissioner.
  3. There was no prompt reply to the Commissioner's queries of 11 May 1983. Perhaps to some extent that delay may have been attributable to TA1. But whatever may have been the position in that regard, by at least early September 1983 the Applicant had consulted a new accountant and tax agent (TA2) and retained his services. By letter of 2 September 1983 TA2 replied on his letterhead to the May letter of the Commissioner. By its terms the letter identified the Applicant by name and by his file number; referred to the Commissioner's "requisition"; and was responsive to the Commissioner's questions of 1.1 May 1983. The letter concluded:

"If we can be of any further assistance or you require additional information please do not hesitate to telephone the writer."

  1. The letter identified the firm of TA2 and its address; identified TA2 as a member of the Australian Society of Accountants; and described the firm as "registered public accountants". It explained that there had been an incorrect claim to have a deduction for interest in relation to monies which really constituted principal and it explained the error. It went on to say that:

"In view of this the taxpayer is quite concerned and has asked us to estimate the amount of tax payable to enable him to pay it immediately."

  1. The letter did not state explicitly or even, as the Applicant seems to have thought, implicitly, that the retainer of TA2 was general; that TA2 was being retained in succession to TA1; or that the address of TA2, or any other address, was to be substituted as the Applicant's address for service for any address for service previously notified.
  2. On 21 September 1983 the Commissioner issued a Notice of Assessment calculating the taxable income of the Applicant at $31,794-00 claiming payment of $22,465-65 on or before 24 October 1983. How the figure of taxable income was determined was not explained, and it is not to be explained by reference to any of the exhibits before me. The Notice of Assessment so made issued to the Applicant directed to him by post at his previously notified address for service; the office of TA1. In light of what followed it is clear that the Notice of Assessment was transmitted to the Applicant by TA1 on or before 12 October 1983. The evidence also establishes that, consistently with the undertaking he had given to the Commissioner, TA2 proceeded to do a calculation of the Applicant's tax liability in accordance with his estimate of matters. He fixed on $26,122-00 as the taxable income of the Applicant to 30 June 1982 - a sum equal to income originally returned ($3,122-00) and interest previously claimed but now conceded as not being allowable ($23,000-00). He calculated $16,947-56 as the amount to be paid.
  3. That sum was paid to and receipted by the Commissioner on 12 October 1983 and a receipt marked in favour of the Applicant "C/- TA2" issued and in due course came to the Applicant. The Applicant considered the receipt and its dispatch to him "C/-TA2" as constituting an acknowledgment by the Commissioner of a change in his address for service. I disagree. It indicated no more than the forwarding to the Applicant CI- the address from which the monies remitted had come, a receipt for those monies. How or in what terms TA2 had communicated his assertions of taxable income to the Commissioner was not evidenced. Nonetheless the 1982 problems were resolved when on 18 November 1983 the Commissioner issued a further amended assessment to the taxpayer again directed to him at the original address for service (C/- TAD assessing his taxable income at $26,122-00. At the same time the Commissioner calculated that a refund of $8-93 was due to the Applicant. It was no doubt paid. For the time being no further action was required. Although the troubles relating to the year 1982 were resolved, nonetheless the experiences of that time laid the foundation for the Applicant's complaints in relation to the imposition of additional tax for the 1983 year.
  4. According to the understanding of the Applicant, based upon his experience, he had an obligation to lodge his 1983 income tax return during February 1984. However, his obligation in that regard did not arise from and was not limited by his understanding. His obligation arose by force of the Act and the regulations thereunder. His understanding was relevant only to questions as to the seriousness of any breach of obligation there might have been. Section 161 of the Act so far as is material provided:

"(1) Every person shall, if required by the Commissioner by notice published in the Gazette, furnish to the Commissioner , within the time specified in the notice, or such extended time as the Commissioner may allow, a return signed by him setting forth a full and complete statement of the total income (...) derived by him during the year of income, and of any deductions claimed by him, ...: provided that ..."

  1. By notice in the Commonwealth of Australia Gazette published 28 June 1983, the Commissioner of Taxation gave notice that:

"I hereby require a. return of income derived during the year ended 30 June 1983 (or the accounting period, if any, adopted under that Act in lieu of that year) to be furnished to me in the prescribed manner at the appropriate place on or before 31 August 1983 by every person described hereunder."

  1. As a person resident in Australia whose total income from all sources was in excess of $4,462-00 the Applicant was obliged to provide such a return. The Gazette notice concluded by providing:

"Failure to duly furnish a required return renders the person concerned liable, upon conviction, to a penalty of not less than $4-00 or more than $200-00, as may be determined by a court of competent jurisdiction. Alternatively, that person may become liable to pay, as additional tax by way of penalty, an amount not exceeding the amount of the tax assessed."

  1. Although Section 161(1) made provision for "such extended time" beyond that specified in the Gazette notice as the Commissioner might allow, no such extended time was provided for by the notice. That being so it becomes necessary to consider the possible relevance of the "Agent's lodgment program", as an administrative practice regulating the matter of lodgment dates both for agents and for the clients whom they represent. No evidence was presented before me as to the precise terms of such arrangements, either generally or with reference to the practices of TA1 and TA2, or with reference to the taxpayer. All that does appear is that the Commissioner took the view that the Applicant was not in default of any obligation to lodge until 31 December 1983 had passed. Whether additional evidence might have established that there was no default until 28 February 1984 had passed, I cannot tell. But as to the question of breach of obligation, it is a matter of no moment: no return was lodged by the Applicant with the Commission on or before 28 February 1984.
  2. The evidence of the Applicant was that, having retained TA2 as "my new accountant" as early as September 1983, the Applicant expected TA2 to attend to the lodgment of his 1983 return. To that end his evidence was that he provided all his relevant records to TA2 in December 1983/January 1984 to enable the return to be prepared. His evidence was that a return was prepared but, due to some confusion, possibly on his part and possibly on the part of TA2, it never came to be lodged. Although I accept that the Applicant has convinced himself that such a return was prepared, I am not persuaded either that it was or that in the weeks immediately following 28 February 1984, he actually thought that it had been prepared.
  3. On 2 February 1984 the Commissioner forwarded to the Applicant a "Final Notice" calling on him to furnish the required return; warning him of the possibility of legal proceedings being instituted without further notice; and advising him of liability to additional tax. That liability was said to lie in:

"Any person who fails to furnish a return by 31 August next following the year of income, or by such later date as is authorised,"

  1. Inasmuch as the obligation of the Applicant to lodge a return did not arise by reason only of that notice it is not significant in relation to the question as to whether there had been a failure to furnish a return. However the Applicant contends that there was one highly material circumstance: the notice was addressed to and, according to the Applicant, incorrectly addressed to him C/- the address of TA1. For reasons I have already stated, I find that he was incorrect in that belief.
  2. The evidence of the Applicant was that late in April 1984 he realised that his return, which according to his understanding should have been lodged with the Commissioner by the end of February 1984, had not been lodged. His evidence was that on Tuesday the 24th day of April 1984, without troubling to refer to TA2 whose responsibility it had been to procure the lodgment of the return, he wrote to the Commissioner in the following terms:

"Due to the fact that my wife's and myself (sic) tax returns for 30/6/83 have been misplaced and are being re-prepared I enclose prepayment in order not to incur fines. Adjustments can be made when returns become available in a few weeks."

  1. The letter went on to identify the taxpayers; their tax file numbers; and the amounts remitted for each. In the case of the Applicant, the amount remitted was $10,000-00. The $10,000-00 was received by the Commissioner and credited to the Applicant on Tuesday 1 May 1984.
  2. I am not persuaded that the explanation then offered, or the evidence presented before me confirming it, is true. If the return had been prepared and delivered by TA2 to the Applicant it would either have been signed and returned by the Applicant - in which case without checking with TA2 the Applicant would have had no reason to suppose non-lodgment – or it would have been retained until lost or misplaced by the Applicant. If the later were the true explanation no question of the returns being "re-prepared" would have arisen. TA2 would have had his file copy which could have been executed and lodged. I am not persuaded that at 24 April 1984 TA2 had prepared any return for the Applicant for 1983, or that he had received sufficient instructions to do so.
  3. The letter of 24 April 1984 was addressed to the Commissioner on stationery of the Applicant sourced from a suburban practice address: the first of two such addresses to be used by the Applicant.
  4. On 4 May 1984 officers within the Prosecution Section of the Commissioner's office, not being aware of any communications from TA2 relating to the 1982 assessment; and not being aware of the Applicant's letter of 24 April 1984, caused an Information to issue under the hands of the Justice, summonsing the Applicant to appear before a Court of Petty Sessions on 31 May 1984 to answer to a taxation prosecution whereby the Applicant was charged:

"That at Sydney aforesaid on the 2nd day of February 1984 one .... (hereinafter called the Defendant) of (residential address) was required by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation at Sydney aforesaid by notice in writing, by posting the said notice by prepaid letter post to the address of service, to wit C/- (TAD, to furnish to him within 14 days of that date, for the purpose of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 at his office, in Sydney, a return in writing on the appropriate form, duly signed by the said Defendant, setting forth a full and complete statement of the total income derived by the said Defendant from all sources in Australia and elsewhere during the year ended 30 June 1983 and did not within the said period comply with such requirement, and the Prosecutor further avers that the time allowed for compliance with the notice was reasonable."

  1. The summons and information was served on the Applicant by post, the posting having been on Monday 7 May 1984. The summons was endorsed to identify the complainant as the person to whom any enquiries might be directed and for that purpose telephone numbers were provided. I find that the summons was served in the ordinary course of post. Further, despite doubts as to the evidence, as it was not seriously challenged, I am prepared to accept that on Thursday 10 May 1984 the Applicant signed his income return to 30 June 1983 as prepared by TA2. I further find that TA2 signed the agent's certificate on Friday 11 May 1.984. By that return of income for the first time the Applicant made provision for the Commissioner to be told that "postal address for service of notices" would be the address of TA2.
  2. But having signed the return which had been prepared for him, the Applicant did not have it lodged. At that point it seems that- the Applicant confused two distinct issues: one, the question as to whether he was guilty of the offence alleged or whether he could procure a verdict of not guilty; the other, the still undischarged duty to deliver to the Commissioner an income tax return for the year of income ended 30 June 1983 satisfying the requirements of Section 161 of the Act.
  3. Believing the accusation expressed in the summons to be unsound he took steps to defend himself. He chose to do so without benefit of relevant professional representation. At what must have been considerable cost and inconvenience to himself he appeared before the Court of Petty Sessions on 31 May 1984 when the matter was adjourned; he appeared before the Court of Petty Sessions on 28 June 1984 when the matter was adjourned; and he appeared on the morning of 23 August 1984, to which date the matter had been adjourned. When the matter was mentioned before the Court in the morning, it was stood down to 2pm. In that interval of time the Chief Prosecuting Officer for the Commissioner was brought into the matter personally. An arrangement was then made which resulted in the Chief Prosecution Officer withdrawing the summons before the Court and in the Applicant handing over immediately thereafter his income tax return to 30 June 1983.
  4. The Applicant alleges that those matters occurred in consequence of a bargain which had been struck that he would only hand over his income return if the Commissioner consented to the dismissal of the summons and if it was agreed that no penalties at all would be imposed in relation to the late lodgment of the Applicant's return. The Applicant's contention was that the agreement was such that by it terms, the Commissioner had bargained not to impose any additional tax such as what was later imposed and that by reason of that* agreement the imposition of the additional tax later imposed should be set aside and, if not set aside, at least substantially reduced.
  5. On 1 November 1984 the Commissioner assessed the taxpayer and gave notice to him that he was assessed as having derived a taxable income of $42,063-00 (the figure returned), giving rise to a liability in tax on that amount of $15,944-47. The Commissioner also levied additional tax for a late return in the sum of $1,983.20. The sum of $1,563.87 was fixed upon by talking into account $15,944.47 as the tax assessed: 31 December 1983 as the due date for lodgment; 23 August 1984 as the actual date of lodgment; 20% per annum as a factor, being the rate of interest at that time payable in relation to amounts unpaid pursuant to Section 207 of the Act by taxpayers who had failed to pay tax assessed on the due date; and 227 days as the number of days from 31 December 1983 to date of calculation (236 was the number of days by which the lodgment was late). It was acknowledged for the Commissioner that the imposition of additional tax was sufficiently high not to preclude the Tribunal from exercising a discretion to further reduce the imposition.
  6. Unfortunately the troubles did not end there. When the Applicant had made his payment of $10,000 on 24 April 1984, he wrote from his suburban practice address and the receipt issued to him at that address. After the summons was issued, the Applicant must have made some contact with the Commissioner because on 28 May 1984 the Commissioner wrote to him at the same suburban practice address acknowledging the payment but advising that:

"Duplicate receipts are not issued. However receipt details shown below may be taken as evidence of payment."

  1. Following the issue of the Notice of Assessment, by letter of 22 November 1984 from the same suburban practice address, the Applicant objected. On 19 December 1984 the Commissioner issued a Final Notice claiming the unpaid amount of $1,983-22 and he addressed the notice "C/- TA2". The Applicant responded on 14 January 1985 by personally writing from the practice address to the Chief Investigation Officer asserting (inter alia) that:

"I did not pay $1,983-22 a fine for late lodgment because, as you had conceded on 23/8/84 the Commissioner had erred in error (sic). "

  1. I am satisfied that there was no foundation for that allegation. The Commissioner replied to that letter on 26 April 1985 addressing it to the practice address as he did the Notice of Disallowance of the objection which was issued 18 June 1985.
  2. On 13 May 1985 the Applicant again wrote to the Commissioner from his suburban practice address, on this occasion asserting that having "sacked" TA1 and "adopted" TA2 as his new accountant and also that "a supplementary tax return was lodged" for 1982 with the letter of 2 September 1983 and with the payment of $16,947-56. This letter also emanated from the same suburban practice address. The Applicant also went on to say that:

"With the changeover of accountants delays were encountered with obtaining records and then I managed to misplace certain records which had to be re-prepared. Thus in order not to incur a late penalty I prepaid what I thought might be my tax liability ..."

  1. I note that inconsistently with his evidence in proceedings before me the misplacement was now attributed exclusively to himself but it was not said to be a misplacement of the 1983 tax return. On 21 June 1985 the Commissioner issued a formal acknowledgment of the letter of 13 May 1985 to the suburban practice address.
  2. Having received the. Notice of Disallowance on 26 June 1985 the Applicant wrote to the Commissioner from the first surgery address requesting that his objection be referred for review enclosing cheques for $2.00 (for the review) and $1,993.22 for the outstanding additional tax. That was followed by an undated formal letter of acknowledgment from the Commissioner addressed to the doctor at his suburban practice address. The Applicant's lack of confidence in the Commissioner was compounded when, by letter of 6 September 1985 addressed to him at his suburban address, the Commissioner stated:

"It is advised that whilst your letter (of 26 June .1985) has satisfied the requirements of s.187 of the Income Tax Assessment Act, there is no record of receipt of the $2.00 fee required in terms of s.188. It follows that in terms of the Income Tax law your request for reference cannot be complied with in the absence of the payment of the prescribed fee.

It has been decided, on this occasion, to treat your request as valid on the understanding that a $2.00 fee is remitted to this office by return mail. If the fee is not paid promptly, the request will be regarded as invalid."

  1. Bearing in mind the date upon which the letter was written, it was of course unsound. If the provisions of the Act as to the lodgment of a request for reference and payment of the fee had not been complied with, there would have been no jurisdiction in the Tribunal to entertain the application. The Commissioner had not been invested by the Parliament with authority to relieve taxpayers from an obligation to comply with the provisions of the Act.
  2. A further letter from the Applicant of 10 September 1985 providing detailed particulars forwarded by certified mail produced an acknowledgment that:

"Receipt is acknowledged of your letter, sent under certified mail, dated 10 September 1985. The matter is being looked into and you will be further advised as soon as possible."

  1. That was quite promptly followed up by a further letter from the Commissioner, on this occasion addressed to his private address. Some dialogue probably followed, but I find that no agreement was reached. As a result it was for the Respondent to carry out his statutory duty to remit the appropriate documents to the Tribunal (or the Board of Review as it then was) for review. However, that was not done until 18 May 1987 at which time the Commissioner gave notice to the Tribunal that the address for service of the Applicant was C/- a third tax agent: (TA3). Later that was to lead to a further complaint that:

"It is to be further noted that the Australian Taxation Office's letter of 5/7/88 still bears the wrong address, an address I have not lived at for in excess of 2 years. This is despite the fact that the Commissioner was re-advised at the preliminary hearing of my address and in accordance with Regulation 29. I have submitted at least 2 tax returns (85/86, 86/87) with the address (of TA3). This is the problem which has given rise to these proceedings."

  1. By the date of that letter the Applicant was also protesting:

"A further amount of $675.48 representing the penalty for late payment of the original penalty. I am appealing against all these penalties."

  1. A hearing ultimately evenuated. At that hearing, despite his obvious anger at the events of the past and despite his seeming inability to acknowledge any fault on his own part, he conducted himself in a pleasant, restrained and generally competent manner. His own confidence that he was not in error induced him to call as a witness on his own behalf the now retired Chief Prosecution Officer, but that officer was unhelpful to his cause.
  2. Having heard the evidence of both parties I accept that, insofar as matters turn on the events on what the Applicant sees as the crucial day, 23 August 1984, the Applicant came away with the belief that he was not to be penalised in any way because of the late lodgment of his 1983 income tax return. However, I am not persuaded that that conviction was soundly based in fact or in law.
  3. The Commissioner has a duty to assess and collect tax in accordance with the prescriptions of the Act. In discharging that responsibility he has a duty to exercise discretions within the fields in which he is empowered to do so. In relation to the Applicant, it was the duty of the Commissioner in assessing his late return of income to calculate taxable income; to assess the tax thereon; and to impose a penalty by way of additional tax for late return in an amount equal to tax assessed, unless in the exercise of a discretion to remit - a discretion which in my view he was bound to consider - he chose to remit additional tax in whole or in part. That obligation would arise upon the determination of the tax assessable. I do not consider it necessary or appropriate that I should consider to what extent the Commissioner may contractually bind himself as to the exercise of such a discretion, particularly by actions taken prior to any determination being made as to taxable income or tax assessable. In my view, before the Commissioner could be so bound contractually it would at least be necessary that the officer by whose actions the Applicant sought to bind the Commissioner either had authority to exercise discretions ordinarily only appropriate to be exercised at assessment, or at least was in some way held out by the Commissioner to the Applicant as a person invested with such authority. Upon my view of the evidence the Chief Prosecution Officer neither had, nor appeared to have, nor held himself out as having such authority. Further, I am satisfied that, despite any misunderstanding there may have been on the part of the Applicant, the Chief Prosecution Officer did not purport to exercise any such authority. I am not persuaded that anything said on the day the prosecution was withdrawn and the income tax return delivered up did anything more than acknowledge that, upon the withdrawal of the summons, that would bring to an end forever that prosecution and all risk of penalties arising from that prosecution.
  4. I also refer to the principle of "estoppel", but only to indicate that there is no foundation for its application. By application of that principle, the Commissioner would not be allowed to act inconsistently with any representation he had made which had led a person to act to his detriment. There is very little scope for the application of the principle in relation to tax matters (F.C.T. v Wade (1951) 84 C.G.R. 105). But whatever argument there might be in favour of the application of the principle in some circumstances, those established by the evidence before me are not among them. The Applicant never did act to his detriment. His obligation to lodge his return with the Commissioner was long overdue. Section 226(4) of the Act only ensured protection from the "double jeopardy" of being liable to pay additional tax assessed by the Commissioner while a prosecution was pending.
  5. The final matter remaining to be considered is the reasonableness of the determination of the Commissioner as to remissions. I do not find the Commissioner's calculation particularly helpful. It is based upon a 20% rate - the rate applicable had the Applicant been assessed correctly at an appropriate date and had he thereafter deferred payment until the due date for payment under the assessment before me. The rate so used is reasonable in itself as a measure of assessing compensation but its application to "tax assessed" rather than to the amount which was withheld from the public revenues is not. The use of "tax assessed" as a factor is no doubt based upon its use in Section 226(1) but that use was only to set an upper limit to the additional tax to be imposed. Of itself it gave no indication of what would be an amount that would be appropriate to be remitted.
  6. In reviewing the Commissioner's exercise of discretion as to remissions, I have first set aside any notion that the Commissioner's actions contributed to the problem. They did not. I am satisfied that the non-lodgment of the return and the consequent delay in assessment were wholly attributable to the actions of the Applicant and, possibly - although I make no finding in the matter - his advisers: but in any event advisers for whom he, and not the Commissioner, was responsible; and advisers accountable to him for their negligence (if any), just as he is accountable to his patients for his negligence (if any). That being so, it is appropriate in considering remissions to have regard to both a compensatory factor (to make good the loss to the community occasioned by delay in payment of the amounts payable under the assessments that should have been) and a culpability factor (to reflect the seriousness of the default).
  7. As to the former, it is appropriate to take into account the amount initially withheld from the community to 1 May 1984 at $25,864-47: a figure which excludes from account the $10,000-00 paid on account on 1 May 1984 and also the additional tax of $1,983-22; and from 1 May 1984 to give credit to the Applicant for the $10,000-00 paid on account that day.
  8. On that basis, if tax had been withheld from the Commissioner only from 28 February 1984 the amount of penalty necessary to compensate the community for that deprivation would have exceeded $2,700-00. Recognising that, and bearing it in mind that on that basis no "culpability factor" has been taken into account, the conclusion I have reached is that, not only was the imposition of additional tax not" unreasonable, but rather the remission was liberal.
  9. The determination of the Commissioner upon the objection under review is confirmed.
