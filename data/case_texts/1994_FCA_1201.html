Re John Spernovassilis Ex Parte: Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1994] FCA 1201; (1994) 94 Atc 4484 (1994) 51 FCR 512 (14 July 1994)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

RE: JOHN SPERNOVASSILIS

Ex parte: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

No. VP298 of 1994

FED No. 497/94

Number of pages - 8

Bankruptcy

(1994) 94 ATC 4484

(1994) 51 FCR 512

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

OLNEY J

CATCHWORDS

Bankruptcy - bankruptcy notice - judgment debtor income tax due by debtor - debtor cannot be found after reasonable inquiry - whether personal service required - application of s 214 of Income Tax Assessment Act. Bankruptcy - creditor's petition - act of bankruptcy - failure to comply with bankruptcy notice founded on judgment for income tax due by debtor - debtor cannot be found after reasonable inquiry - whether personal service required - application of s 214 of Income Tax Assessment Act.

Acts Interpretation Act 1901, s 29

Bankruptcy Act, ss 5(1), 40(1)(g), 41(4), 47(1)(c), 52(1)(b), 309(2), 315(1)

Bankruptcy Rules, Rule 15

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s 214(1)

Income Tax Regulations, regs 36, 37, 38, 39

James v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1957] HCA 36; (1957) 97 CLR 23

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 29 June 1994

14:7:1994

Mr M. Crennan (instructed by the Australian Government Solicitor) appeared for the petitioning creditor.

The debtor did not appear.

DECISION

OLNEY J On 29 June 1994 I made a sequestration order against the estate of John Spernovassilis (the debtor) upon the petition of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia (the petitioning creditor).

2. The debtor did not appear on the petition. The petitioning creditor was represented by Counsel. I was satisfied at the time that the petitioning creditor had established all of the facts necessary to support the petition and a sequestration order was accordingly made. However, as some aspects of the case were unusual and I was required to rule on matters which do not appear to be the subject of direct authority I undertook to publish reasons for my decision in due course. I now publish my reasons.

THE JUDGMENT DEBT

3. The petitioning creditor obtained judgment (the judgment) in default of appearance against the debtor on 30 July 1993 in the Supreme Court of Victoria in proceeding 8017 of 1992. The judgment was for the sum of $517,406.35 together with interest in the sum of $52,271.95 and costs fixed at $1,500.

4. Service of the writ in the Supreme Court proceedings was effected by forwarding by certified mail an envelope containing the writ addressed to the debtor at RSD Finns Lane, Scotsburn, Victoria 3357. The envelope was returned unopened marked "Left Address".

5. In an affidavit sworn 28 June 1993 filed in the Supreme Court proceedings in support of a motion for judgment in default of appearance an officer employed in the Debt Collection Section of the Australian Taxation Office deposed to extensive enquiries made concerning the whereabouts of the debtor over a period extending from February 1991 to June 1993. In the affidavit it is asserted that the last known place of abode of the debtor was RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn and that service of the writ was effected pursuant to s 214(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 by posting the writ to the debtor at that address.

6. Although there is no record of any specific finding of the Supreme Court as to the last known address of the debtor, it necessarily follows that the affidavit evidence as to that matter was accepted as probative of the fact asserted.

THE BANKRUPTCY NOTICE

7. On 1 October 1993 a bankruptcy notice addressed to the debtor was issued by the Registrar in Bankruptcy for the Bankruptcy District of the State of Victoria. The notice required the debtor to pay the amount of the judgment and interest calculated to 29 September 1993 (or to secure or compound the same) within 21 days from the date of service.

8. The debtor's address shown in the bankruptcy notice is RSD Finns Lane, Scotsburn 3357.

9. On 19 October 1993 an officer employed in the Debt Collection Section of the Australian Taxation Office caused to be posted to the debtor by certified mail addressed to RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn 3357 an envelope containing a duly signed and stamped copy of the bankruptcy notice. The envelope was returned to the Moonee Ponds Taxation Office on 25 October 1993 marked "left address".

10. On 19 October 1993 the same officer also posted to the debtor at the same address by ordinary post an envelope containing a photocopy of the bankruptcy notice. The envelope was returned unopened on 22 October 1993.

11. Subsequent to the date of judgment the petitioning creditor has made further extensive enquiries in an attempt to locate the debtor but has not obtained any information concerning the current whereabouts of the debtor. The enquiries made by and on behalf of the petitioning creditor reveal that the debtor formerly resided at RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn and that in or about April 1990 the debtor ceased to reside at that address.

12. The petitioning creditor submits that as at 19 October 1993 the last known place of abode of the debtor was RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn and that service of the bankruptcy notice was effected by posting same to him at that address.

THE PETITION

13. The creditor's petition was presented on 29 March 1994 and was issued on 31 March 1994. It was originally made returnable on 30 May 1994.

14. The petition alleges that the debtor is indebted to the petitioning creditor in the sum of $583,778.65 and that the debtor committed an act of bankruptcy by failing on or before 10 November 1993 to comply with the requirements of the bankruptcy notice.

15. On 11 May 1994 an officer employed by the Australian Government Solicitor caused to be posted to the debtor by certified mail an envelope addressed to him at RSD Finns Lane, Scotsburn 3357. The envelope contained an official copy of the creditor's petition together with the supporting affidavits. The envelope was returned to the office of the Australian Government Solicitor on 19 May 1994, unopened and marked "Return to Sender - unknown at address". On 11 May 1994 the same officer also posted to the debtor by ordinary prepaid post a similarly addressed envelope containing copies of the same documents. This envelope was also returned unclaimed on 19 May 1994.

16. Upon the return of the petition on 30 May 1994 the Deputy Registrar ordered that the matter be adjourned for hearing on 29 June 1994 and further directed that a copy of the order be sent to the debtor by post addressed to RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn. Proof of the posting of the order as directed has been filed.

17. Affidavits sworn 29 June 1994 were filed in Court on the hearing of the petition proving both the continuation of the debtor's liability in respect of the judgment debt and the fact that there is no record of other pending bankruptcy proceedings against the debtor.

18. The only matters requiring resolution on the hearing of the petition were as to whether the bankruptcy notice and the petition were properly served.

SERVICE UNDER THE BANKRUPTCY ACT

19. A debtor commits an act of bankruptcy if a creditor who has a final judgment or order the execution of which has not been stayed serves on the debtor a bankruptcy notice under the Act and the debtor does not within the time fixed for the purpose respond in one of the ways contemplated by the notice or by paragraph 40(1)(g) of the Bankruptcy Act.

20. Section 41(4) provides that service of a Bankruptcy Notice shall be as prescribed.

21. At the hearing of a creditor's petition the Court shall require, inter alia, proof of service of the petition (s 52(1)(b)).

22. Section 47(1)(c) provides that a creditor's petition shall be served as prescribed.

23. In the Bankruptcy Act, unless the contrary intention appears, "prescribed" means prescribed by the Act or by rules under the Act (s 5(1)).

24. The Governor-General may make rules or regulations not inconsistent with the Bankruptcy Act prescribing, inter alia, all matters that by the Act are required or permitted to be prescribed (s 315(1)).

25. Rule 15 of the Bankruptcy Rules provides:

15. Unless otherwise ordered by the Court under sub-section 309(2)

of the Act -

(a) service of a bankruptcy notice shall be effected on the

debtor by delivering to the debtor personally a copy of the

bankruptcy notice signed and stamped by the Registrar, and

(b) service of a creditor's petition shall be effected on a

debtor by delivering -

(i) an official copy of the petition;

(ii) a copy of the affidavit or of each affidavit

verifying the petition and

(iii) where a registered trust has consented, as

referred to in paragraph 12(3)(ba), to act as

the trustee - a copy of the consent,

to the debtor personally -

(iv) if service is effected in Australia - not less

than 8 days before the hearing date for the

petition; or

(v) in any other case - not less than such

reasonable time before the hearing date for the

petition as is determined by the Registrar.

26. Sub-section 309(2) has to do with substituted service under the Bankruptcy Act and provides as follows:

309(2) Where a notice or other document is required by this Act to

be served on or given to a person, the Court may, in a particular

case, order that it be given or served in a manner specified by the

Court, whether or not any other manner of giving or serving the

notice or other document is prescribed.

27. (The sub-section has no relevance in the present context).

SERVICE UNDER THE INCOME TAX ASSESSMENT ACT

28. Section 214 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 provides:

214(1) If a taxpayer -

(a) is absent from Australia and has not to the knowledge of the

Commissioner after reasonable inquiry in that behalf any

attorney or agent in Australia on whom service of process

can be effected; or

(b) cannot after reasonable inquiry be found,

29. service of any process in proceedings against him for recovery of income tax may, without leave of the Court, be effected on him by posting the same or a sealed copy thereof in a letter addressed to him at his last known place of business or abode in Australia.

30. (2) In subsection (1), "income tax" includes interest under section 170AA or 207A and additional tax under section 207 of Part VII.

31. The following provisions of the Income Tax Regulations are relevant for the purpose of identifying a taxpayer's last known place of business or abode in Australia:

36 Every person who furnishes a return shall, in the return, give

an address in Australia for service.

37 Every person who has given an address for service and who

subsequently changes his address shall, within one month after the

change, give to the Commissioner, at the place where he furnished his

return, notice in writing of his new address in Australia for

service.

38 The address for service last given to the Commissioner by any

person shall, for all purposes under the Act and Regulations, be his

address for service, but where no address for service has been given

to the Commissioner, or where the departmental records disclose that

such person has subsequently changed his address and he has not

notified the Commissioner, either in the return or by separate

written advice, of such change, then the address of the person, as

described in any record in the custody of the Commissioner, shall be

his address for service.

39 The address for service as prescribed in regulation 38 shall,

for all purposes under the Act and Regulations, be deemed to be the

last known place of business or abode in Australia of any person.

THE RELEVANT FACTS

32. Upon the evidence adduced in support of the petition I find the following facts;

(a) The judgment obtained by the petitioning creditor against the

debtor on 30 July 1993 was for income tax and interest thereon.

(b) After reasonable inquiry the petitioning creditor has been

unable to find the debtor.

(c) On 19 October 1993 a copy of the bankruptcy notice signed and

stamped by the Registrar was posted to the debtor addressed to

him at RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn.

(d) On 11 May 1994 an official copy of the creditor's petition and

the affidavits verifying the petition were posted to the debtor

addressed to him at RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn.

(e) In an income tax return filed by the debtor for the year ended

30 June 1989, which is dated 16 February 1990, the debtor's

postal address for service of notices is given as "Venn Milner

and Co Pty Ltd PO Box 118 Blackburn", and the residential address

of the debtor is given as "RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn Victoria".

The said return is the last return filed by the debtor.

(f) The departmental records of the Commissioner of Taxation

disclose that since the abovementioned addresses were given to

the Commissioner the debtor has changed his address and he has

not notified the Commissioner of such change.

33. Upon the basis of these facts I am satisfied that for the purposes of the Income Tax Assessment Act the last known place of business or abode in Australia of the Debtor as at both 19 October 1993 and 11 May 1994 was RSD Finns Lane Scotsburn. I am further satisfied that the debtor cannot, nor could not at either of the last mentioned dates, after reasonable enquiry be found.

34. Assuming for present purposes that s 214(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act can be availed of for the purpose of effecting service of a bankruptcy notice and a creditor's petition there are two other matters which call for consideration.

35. First, the section applies with respect to the service "of any process in proceedings against (a taxpayer) for recovery of income tax". The judgment was obtained in respect of the debtor's liability for income tax. The process issued by the petitioning creditor, being first the bankruptcy notice and then the creditor's petition were in my opinion for the recovery of income tax. As to this I rely upon the dictum of Dixon CJ, Fullagar and Kitto JJ in James v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1957] HCA 36; (1957) 97 CLR 23 at p 35 where their Honours said:

"We think that the Commissioner or Deputy Commissioner is empowered

to take proceedings in bankruptcy for the recovery of the tax as a

Crown debt".

36. It is implicit from the facts of that case that in referring to "proceedings in bankruptcy" their Honours were referring to the issuing of a bankruptcy notice and the presentation and service of a creditor's petition.

37. The second matter which arises in the application of s 214 is as to the date when service is to be regarded as having been effected. In my opinion, service is effected upon the posting of the process.

38. Section 29 of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 provides:

29 Where an Act authorizes or requires any document to be served by

post, whether the expression "serve" or the expression "give" or

"send" or any other expression is used, then unless the contrary

intention appears the service shall be deemed to be effected by

properly addressing prepaying and posting the document as a letter,

and unless the contrary is proved to have been effected at the time

at which the letter would be delivered in the ordinary course of

post.

39. In my opinion s 29 does not apply to situations covered by s 214 of the Income Tax Assessment Act by reason of a contrary intention appearing from the latter provision. My reasons for this conclusion are these. First, the words of the section are clear. The words "service may . .. be effected by posting the (process)" indicate an intention that the act of posting is to be the act of service. This is not a case in which the Act simply says that service may be effected by post. The words used are clear, and in my view deliberate. Second, there is the context in which the words are used.

40. The section deals with situations where the taxpayer cannot be found. It provides for service by posting to a deemed last known place of business or abode which by definition is a place at which the debtor cannot be found. There is therefore no purpose to be served by deeming service to have been effected at some date other than the date of posting.

41. I turn now to deal with the question of whether s 214 can be availed of for service of a bankruptcy notice and a creditor's petition.

DOES S 214 OF THE INCOME TAX ASSESSMENT ACT APPLY

42. On a literal construction of the provisions of the Bankruptcy Act to which reference is made above, service of the bankruptcy notice and of the creditor's petition in accordance with s 214(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act is a prescribed means of service in addition to the means prescribed by Rule 15 of the Bankruptcy Rules only if in the definition of "prescribed" "a contrary intention appears".

43. There is nothing in the Bankruptcy Act or in the Bankruptcy Rules which can reasonably said to indicate a "contrary intention" and accordingly, if the prefatory words of s 5(1) are to be construed as meaning "unless a contrary intention appears in the Act or Rules" there would be no scope for service to be effected otherwise than in accordance with Rule 15. There is however, no reason to apply such a narrow construction to the words in question, and if this is so, the provisions of s 214(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act can be regarded as prescribing a method of effecting service in respect of both the bankruptcy notice and the creditor's petition.

44. But even if I am wrong in this approach I am nonetheless of the view that s 214(1) is available to the petitioning creditor.

45. The method of effecting service of a bankruptcy notice and of a creditor's petition was not considered by Parliament to be a matter of such high policy as to warrant specific provision to be made in the Bankruptcy Act to regulate those matters. Rather, the means of effecting service was left to be prescribed by subordinate legislation. The same cannot be said of the Income Tax Assessment Act. In the very specific circumstances contemplated by s 214(1), Parliament has chosen to enact provisions which are designed to assist the revenue collection functions of the Commonwealth in circumstances in which the enforcement of the Act might otherwise be frustrated or rendered more difficult. If there is any inconsistency between the Bankruptcy Rules and the Income Tax Assessment Act it should be resolved in favour of giving effect to the specific remedial provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act in preference to the general provisions of the Bankruptcy Act.

CONCLUSION

46. The petitioning creditor has established that service of the bankruptcy notice was effected on 19 October 1993 and that the debtor did not respond to the notice within the period of 21 days from the date of such service. Accordingly, the debtor committed an act of bankruptcy on 10 November 1993.

47. The petitioning creditor has established that the petition was duly served on the debtor on 11 May 1994. As all other matters referred to in s 52(1) have also been duly proved, a sequestration order was made against the debtor's estate.
