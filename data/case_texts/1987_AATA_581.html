Last Updated: 16 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax – Allowable Deductions – Legal expenses – Litigation between partners in relation to partnership and share-farming agreement with third-party – Apportionment and dissection.

Ronpibon Tin NI v FC of T and

Tongkah Compound NL v FC of T [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47

Ra :

And : COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

AAT Decision NO 3359

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

9 April 1987

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) No. NT85/5510

) TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Before : Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

Date : 9 April 1987

Place : Sydney

The Tribunal varies the decision of the Commissioner under review by reducing the taxable income of the applicant by $2,442 for the year of income ended 30 June 1979.

(Sgd.) Peter M. Roach

Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) No. NT85/55I0

)

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. In this reference the Applicant claims a deduction pursuant to section 51 of the Income Tax Assessment Act for "legal expenses" in the sum of $9,019. The liability for those expenses in pare arose under three accounts rendered in December 1978. They related to services rendered over several years. "They accounted for $8,978. The balance was unexplained and the claim to that extent not supported by evidence at all. Upon assessment her claim for the deduction was disallowed and she objected. Upon her objection being disallowed, on 12 May, 1981 (sic) she requested reference to a Board of Review, but the request was not referred on until 20 December, 1985 (sic). On 1 July 1986, determination of the objection became the responsibility of this Tribunal. In December 1986 the matter was listed for hearing in March 1987. The matter was then heard.
  2. In 1969 the Applicant became joint owner with her sister of a wheat property in New South Wales. The property comprised some 1,300 acres (possibly 1,184 only - 480 to 520 hectares). As joint owners, whether as joint tenants or tenants-in-common, their entitlements to occupy and use the property and to enjoy the profits thereof were equal. The rights they so enjoyed derived from their ownership - not from contract. She and her co-owner determined to apply their capital asset (the land) to income-producing purposes. To that end they entered into an agreement with each other; and into another with a third party ("share-farmer") to work the property and generate income. A written partnership agreement was entered into between the property owners, but the agreement with "share-farmer" was not formalized although the parties in that relationship acted consistently with the terms of a written (but unsigned) share-farming agreement which had been the basis of the relationship between previous owners of the property and "share-farmer". Although the Applicant in her evidence spoke of the share-farmer as being an individual, the document relating to the prior share-farming arrangement identified "share-farmer" as a husband and his wife. Nothing turns on that. That document also related only to an area of about 560 acres - part of the subject property. Nothing turns on that either.
  3. In broad terms the "share-farming" arrangement was that "share-farmer" would be responsible for the entirety of the farming operations; that "share-farmer" was to provide one-third of the seed and of the fuel needed to operate machinery and plant to be found by the owners; that "share-farmer" would be entitled to one-third of the crop and the owners to two-thirds; that all wheat would be consigned by "share-farmer" and sold in the joint names of all parties with net proceeds to be distributed one-third to share farmer and two-thirds to the owner. For several years following 1969 the arrangement continued: crops were sown, harvested and sold; and revenues flowed to the land-owners in partnership, but as parties to the agreement with "share-farmer". Unfortunately, although those arrangements continued, they did not proceed smoothly.
  4. Difficulties between the sisters probably started* as early as 1972 or 1973. The Applicant was consulting her solicitors by 1974. However, upon the evidence before me, litigation did not commence until March 1975. The sister then instituted proceedings in the Supreme Court of Mew South Wales, Equity Division against the Applicant claiming orders that the partnership be dissolved; that accounts be taken of the partnership dealings and transactions from 1 April 1969; and that a receiver and manager of the partnership be appointed in the interim.
  5. In January 1976 the Applicant instituted proceedings against her sister claiming (inter alia) a declaration that title to the land was now held by the sisters as tenants-in-common in equal shares; and that, if the partnership was determined by notice in specified circumstances, that would confer on the partner giving the notice an option to purchase the share of the other partner in the capital and assets of the business. (Why there was no contention that the partnership had been so determined and no contention that such an option had been exercised was not explained). Those 1976 proceedings resulted in terms of settlement being agreed. They were formally documented and signed by the parties and their Counsel in February 1976. The terms of settlement provided that both summonses would be dismissed; that all books of account would be delivered up to a named accountant; that the partnership would be dissolved as from a date nearly 12 months hence; that, should the property not have been sold by that date, title would be vested in trustees upon trust for sale; that a clearing sale of all plant and equipment would be held after harvesting the wheat crop to be grown in the winter of 1976; that the drawings of the sister had exceeded the drawings of the Applicant by over $14,000, and it provided for the distribution of available profits equally between the sisters; for payment to the Applicant of a sum "in equalization of" the rights of the parties in relation to a particular cheque; and for the regulation of the financial operations in future operations of the partnership business in the share-farming arrangement. Before the execution of the typed document there was added to the terms of settlement a clause in the following terms:-

"16) The parties agree that the practical running of the property shall be in the discretion of the share farmer pending the dissolution of the said partnership subject to the right of the parties to give the share farmer joint directions in writing."

  1. Unfortunately, even then matters did not proceed smoothly. Further proceedings were instituted in the Supreme Court of New South Wales, Equity Division in about June 1976.
  2. In proceedings instituted that month the Applicant sought relief against her sister and declarations binding the Bankers to the partnership authorising the Bank to honour cheques "signed by either the Plaintiff or the first named Defendant and countersigned by ("share-farmer") except when such cheques are drawn in favour of "cash" or either the Plaintiff or the first-named Defendant in which case, by virtue of paragraph 15(d) of the said terms (of settlement) the second-named Defendant should honour such cheques only if they be signed by the Plaintiff and the first-named Defendant; an order restraining the sister from interfering with "share-farmer" or any of his servants or agents in his practical running of the property or in his performance of the share-farming agreement; an order restraining the sister from placing furniture or other obstructions in the building known as (on the property) in such a way as to prevent its use and enjoyment as sleeping quarters by ("share-farmer") his servants or agents; an order that the sister pay proceeds of sale of some crops from the property into Court from whence they would be paid out in accordance with the agreement; and an order for costs. Supplementary orders were also sought in terms which will not be unfamiliar to those who have had the unhappy experience of being involved in partnership actions. (Such actions are always difficult and particularly so when bitterness is engendered. The degree of estrangement always seems to be in inverse proportion to the closeness of family or other personal ties out of which the partnership originated).
  3. This is an appropriate point at which to say that there was no attempt during the hearing to consider the rights or wrongs of the issues raised in the litigation. Several affidavits were incorporated in documents tabled before me but I have not had regard to them, let alone have I assumed the assertions made in any of them to be true, even when they might be perceived to be adverse to the Applicant in whose interests they were tendered. Nor do I regard the Orders sought in any of the proceedings as being of relevance, except insofar as they bespeak the disruption of the partnership and the general nature of the issues being raised before the Court by either the Applicant or her sister. However, there is one document upon which I do place reliance. That is an office copy of an Order of the Supreme Court of New South Wales, Equity Division made in the last proceedings in July 1976. As it is properly an "office copy", it bears the imprint of the seal of the Court. It records that the Order of the Court was that the Applicant's sister be restrained from interfering with "share-farmer"; be restrained from interfering with the use of the ... building; be directed to pay into Court the monies specified in the summons Further, it authorised the monies to be so paid into Court to be paid out as to two-thirds to the partnership and one-third to "share-farmer"; and it directed the sister to pay the costs of the Applicant ("including costs for which the Plaintiff is liable to the Defendant (Bank))".
  4. By the following October (1976) a contract had been entered into for the sale of the real-estate, subject to the vendors being entitled to take-off the wheat crop then nearly ripe for harvest. At some time thereafter which is unidentified the crop was taken and sold and the sale of the real-estate was completed. Despite that, it was not until December 1978, that the affairs of the partners touching their common ownership and effecting the dissolution of the partnership between them came to an end.
  5. Fortunately for the Applicant, her solicitor, who had represented her in those proceedings and who was called at probably considerable inconvenience and certainly very short notice, explained the delay. I find that entitlements following the sale of the wheat crop were only satisfied in stages. An attempt to have the entitlement assigned was rejected by the Australian wheat Board and the result was that from time to time monies were paid to him which he received to the credit of his trust account from which he disbursed the monies in due portion to the Applicant and her sister. The final action which marked the end of this prolonged and unhappy litigation was when the solicitor on behalf of his firm rendered three accounts for professional services. Each account was presented in narrative form but was not itemised in such a way as to identify precisely the service rendered. (In so saying, I do not suggest that the accounts were in any way improper. Rather, I merely mean to indicate that, for example, when an account refers to a "conference with you" it does not indicate the date or duration of the conference, and so on.)
  6. The first account is directed to the period May 1974 to February 1976. It starts by reference to "instructions to advise you in respect of the ...... partnership" and, on reading it, one can follow the development of the initial litigation: advising the Applicant as to the partnership agreement and then as to "share-farming" arrangements; dealings with the solicitors to the sister; brief to and attendances on counsel; preparation of affidavits; negotiations as to settlement; matters relating to preparation of records; instituting proceedings in the Court; and drafting terms of settlement. The bill so rendered was made up of :-

Solicitor's costs $3,200.00

Counsel's fees 1,290.00

Incidental disbursements 235.39

$4,725.39

  1. The second account related to the period February 1976 to December 1978 and was entitled "re dissolution of ...... partnership". By its introductory terms it was characterised as being "in relation to the implementation of the terms of settlement filed in the Supreme Court of New South Wales" and by its subsequent narrative it dealt with many aspects of the terms of settlement ranging from considerations as to the position of the "share-farmer"; instructions for the auction sale of the property; aspects of "getting in" the 1970 wheat crop and settlement of partnership accounts. The total account was made up of:-

Solicitor's costs $2,465.00

Disbursements 94.97

$2,559.97

  1. The third account was rendered under the title "Re:- Enforcement of Terms of Settlement ........... Partnership" and all the items which appear are consistent with, and apparently consequential upon, the opening entry relating to "instructions to institute the appropriate proceedings to enforce the terms of the settlement previously arranged with Mrs. ...... and to ensure that the "share-farmer" on ........... was not interfered with in the farming of the property and in particular the planting of the then current wheat crop ......". By its terms it is clearly intended to relate to the third set of litigious proceedings. In consequence it brought to account monies received as costs from the Defendant pursuant to the terms of the Order of July 1976. The account was made up as follows:-

Solicitor's costs $2,500.00

Counsel's fees 990.00

Other disbursements 986.52

total $4,476.52

less received from

the Defendant $2,784.00

$1,692.52

  1. The solicitor was able to recall not only the general history of the matter but also the considerations he applied in the preparation of the accounts. I accept his evidence without hesitation as an honest representation of the views he held and the endeavours he made in December 1978. That is not to say that I accept the accuracy of the concepts he sought to apply in distinguishing capital-related items from income-related items. Nor do I assume that he was so neglectful of the interests of his client as to resolve any doubts which might have occurred to him in favour of the revenue. On the other hand, I reject any notion that he was deceitfully adjusting his accounts to advantage his client substantially at the expense of the revenue.
  2. His view at the time was that the Applicant was entitled to claim deductions against the revenue for all items of his costs which were involved in the protection of the crop; but not his fees in relation to matters relating to the dissolution of the partnership and sale of the realty. His view was that it was only the account for $2,559.97 that did not give rise to an entitlement in full to deductions; and that no part of the account for $2,559.97 was allowable. However, perusal of the narrative to the account indicates that in some measure, perhaps very small, it also related to the revenue-producing activities.
  3. I have a similar difficulty with the account for $4,725. I do not accept is as exclusively related to the farming operations. It covers the period from May 1974 (prior to the institution of any Court proceedings) and extends to February 1976 (when terms of settlement were finalised). The end result was to provide for the ongoing management of the revenue producing business pending its termination; to settle arrangements for dissolution of the partnership between the sisters; and to arrange for the sale of their jointly held freehold. No doubt that result, achieved only after two applications to the Equity Division of the Supreme Court, had its genesis in a breakdown in cooperation and/or confidence in the day-to-day workings of the revenue-producing partnership. But it would be fallacious to conclude that because the expense was incurred in relation to some items of a revenue character that therefore the entirety of the expense was wholly allowable as a deduction. It would be equally fallacious to conclude that because some of the expenses were incurred in relation to items of a capital nature that therefore no part of the total expense should be allowable as a deduction.
  4. As to the third account ($1,692) I am quite satisfied that a deduction is wholly allowable. It covered a period after the terms of settlement had been agreed. In that period the "profit-making structure" was not in danger. That appears clearly from the summary (ante) of Orders sought from and the Orders made by the Court. In my view the claim for $1,692 is wholly allowable. To that extent the Applicant will succeed.
  5. In my view the difficulty in relation to the first two accounts is to be resolved by applying the principles laid down by the Full Bench of the High Court of Australia in Ronpibon Tin NL v FC of T and Tongkah Compound NL v FC of T ((1949) [1949] HCA 15; 78 CLR 47). The Court there identified two kinds of items of expenditure that require apportionment; one, where it is possible to dissect a total expense to identify distinct and severable parts; and the other a situation in which the item consists of a single outlay or charge which serves dual objects (capital and revenue related) indifferently. In each case the Court emphasised that "the result must depend ...... upon a finding by the Tribunal of fact" and directed that the Tribunal of fact had an obligation to "make an apportionment which the facts of the particular case may seem to make just... ".
  6. I am satisfied that the bulk of the efforts in the period to February 1976 were giving over to an attempt to resolve the problems the relationship between the sisters. That exercise was probably largely occasioned, and probably exacerbated by, difficulties between them in their relationships with the "share-farmer" - but that litigation was not primarily or even substantially about those income-producing activities.
  7. Making the best I can of the matter, in relation to the first two accounts, I would allow a deduction of in all $750. To that is to be added $1,692 relating to the remaining account. I have reached that conclusion after considering the accounts in detail and despite the circumstance that the Applicant was prepared to wholly abandon the claim on the $2,559 account; and that both sides were prepared that the other claims should be resolved on an "all or nothing" basis.
  8. Accordingly, my order will be that the decision of the Commissioner under review be varied by reducing the taxable income of the taxpayer by $2,442.
