ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Allowable deductions - retiring allowances - whether paid on retirement - whether paid in good faith and in consideration of past services - whether wholly allowable.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) Ss 51, 78(1)(c) and 109

Yenidie Tobacco Co. Ltd. (1916) 2 Ch 426

Reseck v FCT 75 ATC 4213

No. 4459 and 4460/1985

Mr. P.M. Roach (Senior Member)

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The issues for determination upon these separate references relate to the affairs of a limited liability company TCO (formerly known as UBCO) and its four members J, E, A and C, all five of whom are applicants before the Tribunal. Their applications were heard together by consent.

2. At the commencement of the hearing counsel for TCO consented to an order being made that the Commissioner's determination upon an objection in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1981 be upheld and it was so ordered accordingly.

3. J, E and A were brothers and C was a close personal friend. Their association in business is of long standing and, for convenience, I propose to refer to them as "the partners" in the sense of which that term has been used to identify members of closely held companies (cf. Yenidje Tobacco Co. Ltd. (1916) 2 Ch 426). The partners were the Directors and shareholders in TCO from incorporation and for a period they were also employees of that company. One critical finding of fact to be made is whether they have continued at all material times to he employees of TCO and if not, when they ceased to be employed by TCO.

4. The partners J and B commenced business on their own account in partnership making boxes and containers. That business was known by a name which I identify by the letters UB. Later C and A joined J and B in partnership. In January 1978 TCO was incorporated taking initially the name which I have referred to as TOCO. In doing so it adopted the words of the partnership name. TCO took over the business formerly conducted by the partnership. There was no evidence suggesting that the transfer was attended by any particular formalities, although it was clear upon the evidence that by 1981 TCO had the business bank account and was party to many contracts including contracts for the purchase of raw materials; contracts for sale of products; and contracts for the provision to the business of plant, equipment, the services of employees and other services.

5. The business continued to grow and in growing suffered the difficulties commonly experienced with growth. In particular, growth was attended by recurrent liquidity problems accentuated by some unsuccessful business ventures conducted contemporaneously with the continuing business.

6. I am persuaded by the evidence that all four partners worked long hours and worked sufficiently, skilfully and energetically to bring about the growth of the business. I am not able to measure or assess the extent or quality of their work either individually or collectively, but I am persuaded that their work was not paid as well as it would have been if they had been paid on the same basis of remuneration as other employees. I am also satisfied that the four partners were each paid substantially the same rates of remuneration. Such differences as there were were not explained either in evidence-in-chief or upon cross-examination. I am also persuaded that from time to time the partners had to accept deferment in whole or in part of their wages but I am not persuaded that deferments were either frequent or prolonged. I am satisfied that all four partners accepted that at all critical times no distinction should be drawn between them and that that was appropriately so.

7. TCO conducted a superannuation scheme for its employees including the partners. An insurance salesman, M, who dealt with TCO in matters relating to the scheme was aware to some degree of their liquidity problems and he recommended that 'they consult an accountant, W, who practised in the country. Contact was duly made and J, B, M and D (the regular accountant and accounting adviser to TCO) travelled to the country to consult W. That was in about March 1981.

8. W advised that TCO should transfer its business to a unit trust to be established. He further advised that upon that occurring the partners should retire from their employment with TCO at which point they could be paid a "retiring allowance". He advised that implementation of his recommendations would be advantageous tax wise. The party returned to the city and later at a regular Directors meeting of TCO, which was attended as usual by all four partners (but not by M or D), it was decided to implement Ws advice. The partners entrusted the task of implementing the decision to D. D's evidence was, and I accept it, that thereafter, in consultation with W and with the cooperation of the partners, he set out to discharge that responsibilty. The claim of all applicants is that that decision was implemented when on 30 September 1981, at the close of business that day, the partners resigned from the service of TCO to thereafter take up employment with another company (NUCO); when the business of TCO was transferred to NUCO; and when TCO paid retiring allowances to each of the partners in the sum of $9,500.

9. I find that the partners became the sole members of NUCO from its incorporation on 30 June 1981; that a unit trust was established by Deed on 30 June 1981 with the company nominated by W as trustee and with each partner becoming a unit holder; that on 1 July 1981 TCO under the name of UBCO was appointed trustee of the unit trust in succession to the original trustee; that on 2 October 1981 NUCO was appointed trustee of the unit trust in succession to TCO; that on 23 April 1982 the name of TCO was changed from UBCO to TCO; that on 20 January 1983 NUCO formally changed its name with the Commissioner for Corporate Affairs to UBCO - the same name as TCO had had until 23 April 1982.

10. It follows from the foregoing findings that the business name UB originally established by the partnership came to be the name of TCO and later the name of NUCO, the latter change only occurring a substantial time after it is claimed that beneficial ownership of the business under that name had passed from TCO to NUCO; and that from April 1982 to January 1983 there was no registered company of the name UBCO, although the business continued to use that name throughout.

11. I also find that on 1 October 1981 the banking of the business of UB started to be conducted through a new bank account established in the name of NUCO but operations on that account had virtually ceased by 23 October 1981, although the account was maintained until it was closed in July 1984. Further, apart from operations on the last mentioned account, all banking operations of the business were carried out on the bank account conducted by TCO in the name of UBCO at all material times prior to at least May 1983.

12. A Minute Book was maintained for TCO with the intent that it should comprise a formal record of decisions of meetings of Directors and of general meetings of members and I accept that each Minute was prepared substantially contemporaneously and as an honest attempt to record formally and with accuracy the decisions which had been taken.

13. That Minute Book records that at 5.30 p.m. on 30 September 1981 there was a meeting of Directors attended by the partners and D which was in the following terms:-

"RETIRING ALLOWANCES;

Consequent upon the resignations of Messrs J, E, A and C as employees of the Company, the directors recommended that in consideration of the past services of each of them to the Company generally and expecially [sic] having regard to the great contribution which they and each of them had all made to the profitability of the Company in its various business operations producing assessable income over the previous years and to make to each of them some recompence (sic) for the low salary they had received over the previous years whilst the Company business was being built up, the payment of a retiring allowance because of those past services be made as set out hereunder.

E. $9,500.00

J. $9,500.00

A. $9,500.00

C. $9,500.00

DIRECTORS' LOANS:

Resolved that in addition to the retirement payment each outgoing Director be paid $8,500 on account of Directors loan accounts.

There being no further business the Chairman declared the meeting closed. The minutes having been reduced to writing were thereupon read and confirmed and signed by the Chairman as a true record of the proceedings."

A similar Minute for 2 October 1981 records attendance by the partners (not D) and the following:-

"Change of Name:

It was Resolved that the Company change its name to (TCO) Pty. Limited and that the Corporate Affairs Commission be notified of such change.

Closure:

There being no further business the meeting was closed."

The Minutes of Annual General Meetings said to be held 31 December 1981 (attended by all partners) and 31 December I982 (attended by A and C only) recorded the adoption of unidentified annual accounts and that a dividend of $10,619.00 be declared out of the year's profits (the meeting of 31 December 1981) but that no dividend be declared the next year (31 December 1982).

14. I also find that work, papers prepared in relation to the accounts of TCO to a balance date of 30 September 1981 were prepared following that date; were developed into a Balance Sheet and Profit and Loss Accounts to that date; and record a state of affairs consistent with the entire business assets of TCO having been disposed of to NUCO. That Balance Sheet and those accounts are annexed to a form of Income Tax Return prepared for TCO to 30 September 1981 and dated by the tax agent (D's office) 26 January 1982, but not lodged with the Commissioner until 1983. The individual returns of the partners were all completed by the same tax agent in the period August/September 1982 and all of those were consistent with the sale of the business and the retirements as employees having taken place although none of the partners disclosed receipt of any retiring allowance or acknowledged liability to tax on 5% of any allowance.

15. I am further satisfied that there is no evidence that the sale of business or its subsequent management or the retirement of the partners as employees was effected with any greater formality than has already been mentioned. The bank manager's diary relating to the account of TCO is in terms which suggest that he did not understand what was purporting to be done or even that it had ever been explained to him. There is no evidence that there was an assignment of debtors or any novation in relation to creditors. Nor is there any indication that there was any formal documentation of any agreement for sale. Nor is there evidence that NUCO effected workers' compensation in relation to its employees at 1 October 1981 although there was evidence that the term of TCO's insurance would have had expired "at the end of September".

16. For the Commissioner it is contended that I should find either there was no real reconstruction of the business of TCO; that there was no effective transfer of that business to NUCO; and that there was no real retirement by the partners as employees. In summary, the submission was that the partners after 30 September 1981 continued in the business of box-making as employees of TCO. In support of that submission counsel pointed to the continuity of box-making operations; the substantial continuity and operations of the bank account of TCO; the uninterrupted maintenance of the wages records; the provision of group certificates to 30 June 1982 in the name of TCO only as if that company had been the only employer for the year. He also cast doubts upon the accuracy of the documentary records (Minute Book and Income Tax Return of TCO) especially as to date. The points so made are substantial but the difficulty in face of them is that to give full effect to the submission would point towards a conclusion that the business is still the property of TCO and is still being conducted by it as if NUCO had never come into existence.

17. At this point it is appropriate to record my assessment of the only partner to give evidence. I considered him to be unusually candid and completely honest, but in my judgment he had no sensitivity to the subtleties and concepts which pre-occupy accountants and lawyers as to the identity of legal entities conducting businesses; or the preparation and maintenance of accounting records, formal accounts and secretarial records. He had regard to the "business" as if it were not only continuing hut as if it had characteristics giving it a distinct entity from at least the fictional personalities which owned and controlled it. If anything, he identified the business with the partners at the same time clearly recognising the continuing significance of the name UB. That assessment of J is critical to my evaluation of his evidence because, in my view, when the transcript records the use of particular terms (such as the full registered name of a company) it is not always appropriate to give to J's answer the same measure- of precision in meaning as was given to it by his questioner. When counsel has referred to "TCO Pty. Limited" by that name or by its former name "UBCO Pty. Limited" he may have been referring to that entity to the exclusion of all others but J in responding to a question so framed may have been referring to TCO as he knew it before the change of name; to NUCO as it was after assuming the new name by which TCO had formerly been known; or may only have been referring to the continuing business without regard to the identity of its owner.

18. Having considered all of the evidence I have reached the following conclusions:

1. The business once conducted by TCO has come to be conducted by NUCO, just as the business once conducted by the partnership came to be conducted by TCO;

2. The transition was not attended by all or even by many of the formalities which might have been expected had the transferor and transferee been dealing with each other at arms length. The transition was nontheless effective;

3. Such documentation as did relate to the transfer (the Minutes and the accounts) was honestly prepared with the view to giving effect to the transaction which had been recommended by W;

4. The partners were trying to implement the advice received from W with effect from 30 September 1981; and

5. Despite maintaining business operations, bank accounts and other records in large measure as if there was an ongoing concern of constant identity, the accounts of TCO were prepared on the basis that it had ceased trading and had ceased having the services of the partners as employees.

19. That being so I am persuaded as a matter of fact that on a date, possibly 30 September 1981 but not later than 30 June 1982, the partners ceased to be employed by TCO; and that TCO paid to each and each of them received by way of credit to his loan account $9,500.00 - a total of $38,000. I am also satisfied that in each case the $9,500.00 so paid was paid by way of a retiring allowance on termination of employment with TCO and that accordingly the amount so received in each case is assessable only as to 5 per cent thereof (cf. Reseck v. FCT 75 ATC 4213). In doing so I have considered, and rejected, as appropriate findings of fact: that the payees remain employees of TCO; that they ceased to be employees of TCO only after 30 June, 1982; that it was never intended by TCO or the members that "retirement allowances" should be payable upon their retirement as employees; that the retiring allowances were paid, but only after 30 June, 1982; and that the retiring allowances have yet to be paid and that for that reason no "loss or outgoing" claimable as a deduction has yet been incurred.

6. That being so, I am also satisfied that the amount so paid by TCO was paid as retiring allowances to the partners in the capacity of retiring employees and that it was paid in consideration of their past services so that, subject to having been paid "in good faith" and subject to s 109 of the Act, it constitutes an allowable deduction under s 78(1)(c) of the Act.

20. Section 109 provides that so much of a retiring allowance "as exceeds an amount which, in the opinion of the Commissioner is reasonable,..." shall not be an allowable deduction. Although the evidence as to the work done by the partners and as to its value was slight, I am satisfied that such services as the partners had rendered had been less well remunerated than would have been the case had the same services been rendered by employees at arms length. I am also satisfied that having regard to the Commissioner's guidelines, the company would have been allowed a deduction no less than that claimed if the same amounts had been paid to the four least deserving of employees who had served over the same period for the same remuneration. That being so, I am satisfied that the amount paid is wholly deductible notwithstanding s.109 and being so was an amount paid "in good faith" within the meaning of s 78(1)(c).

21. Accordingly I reverse the decision of the Commissioner upon the objection of TCO in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1982 but vary the decisions of the Commissioner upon the objections of TCO each of the partners in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1982 by reducing taxable income in each case by $9,025 - 95% of $9,500.
