Board of Control of Michigan Technological University and Deputy Commissioner of Patents [1979] AATA 58 (11 May 1979)

Last Updated: 12 March 2007

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL



Jurisdiction - Administration Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 - s.3(3) and s.25(1) - Is a decision by Deputy Commissioner of Patents that s.160(2) of Patents Act 1952 did not empower him to extend time a decision "under" s.160(2) - S.160(2) - reg.7B(8) Patents Regulations - application for extension of time to lodge patent application -advertisement of applications pursuant to s.160(4) directed - content of advertisements discussed.



DECISION AND REASONS

Re: BOARD OF CONTROL OF MICHIGAN TECHNOLOGICAL UNIVERSITY

And: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF PATENTS

AAT Decision No 192

No. 78/12033

Davies, J.

Date: 11 May 1979.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. 78/12033

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re : BOARD OF CONTROL OF MICHIGAN TECHNOLOGICAL UNIVERSITY

And : DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF PATENTS

DECISION

The Tribunal decides that it has jurisdiction to review the decision of the Deputy Commissioner of Patents given 3 November 1978 and

(1) Directs that the Deputy Commissioner of Patents advertise in the Official Journal, as soon as practicable,

(i) the application, dated 3 March 1978, of the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University for extension of time to apply for restoration of the lapsed application No.80103/75 for letters patent;

(ii) his decision, dated 3 November 1978, refusing that application;

(iii) the application, dated 30 November 1978, of the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University to this Tribunal for review of that decision;

(iv) the provisions of s.30 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act which allow a party whose interests are affected by the decision under review to apply to the Tribunal to be made a party to the proceeding for review; and

(2) Reserves liberty to either party to apply within seven (7) days for variation of the above direction or for further or other directions.



DATED the 11th day of May 1979.

J.D. Davies, J.

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. 78/12033

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re : BOARD OF CONTROL OF MICHIGAN TECHNOLOGICAL UNIVERSITY

And : DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF PATENTS

REASONS FOR DECISION

On 14 April 1975, the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University applied for the grant of a patent for an invention entitled "Method for Agglomerating Steel Plant Waste Dusts". The application was accompanied by a complete specification which did not comply with the requirements specified in Part II of the Third Schedule to the Patents Regulations. Acting under reg. 7B(1) of the Regulations, the Commissioner treated the complete specification as having been lodged at the Patent Office and, under reg.7B(3), on 8 May 1975 directed the applicant to supply a substitute specification complying with Part II of the Third Schedule to the Regulations. That direction was not complied with within six months from the date on which it was given and, accordingly, under the provisions of reg.7B(4), the application for a patent lapsed. Pursuant to the provisions of reg.7B(5), the Commissioner advertised that fact in the Official Journal.

Regulations 7B(6) and 7B(8) read as follows :

"7B.-(6) The applicant may, within three months after the date of the advertisement under the last preceding sub-regulation, make an application (in this regulation referred to as 'the application for restoration') to the Commissioner for the restoration of the lapsed application.

.......

(8) If the Commissioner is satisfied that -

(a) the failure to comply with the direction within the prescribed time was unintentional and resulted from an error or omission 6n the part of the applicant or of his agent or attorney or from circumstances beyond the control of the applicant;

(b) the applicant has complied with the direction; and

(c) there has been no undue delay in the making of the application for restoration,

the Commissioner shall restore the lapsed application, but if he is not so satisfied, he shall refuse the application for restoration.".

No application for restoration of the lapsed application was made to the Commissioner within three months after the date on which the lapsing of the application was advertised. However, s.160(2) of the Patents Act 1952 provides : "160.(2) Where, by reason of -

(a)an error or omission on the part of the person concerned or of his agent or attorney; or
(b)circumstances beyond the control of the person concerned,
an act or step in relation to an application for a patent or in proceedings under this Act (not being proceedings in a court) required to be done or taken within a certain time has not been so done or taken, the Commissioner may, upon application by the person concerned, but subject to this section, extend the time for doing the act or taking the step.".

Section 160(3) of the Act provides :

"160.(3) The time for the doing of an act or the taking of a step may be extended under either of the last two preceding sub-sections although that time has expired.".

On 3 March 1978, the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University applied to the Commissioner under s.160(2)(a) of the Act for an extension of time within which to apply for restoration of its lapsed patent application. On the same day, the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University applied to the Commissioner, under the provisions of reg.7B(6), for restoration of the lapsed patent application.

On 17 March 1978, a senior clerk of the Patents Office wrote to the patent attorneys for the applicant stating that, "The provisions of Section 160 are not applicable to the lodgement of an application for restoration under Regulation 7B(6). As the application for restoration was not lodged within the prescribed time it is not now possible to restore the application," However, subsequently, Mr. P.F. Kildea, Deputy Commissioner of Patents, considered the two applications and, on 3 November 1978, issued his written decision refusing them. The decision by its heading makes it clear that it is a decision which considered both applications. The heading to the decision states, interalia,

"In the Matter of Lapsed Application No. 80103/75 for Letters Patent by BOARD OF CONTROL OF MICHIGAN TECHNOLOGICAL UNIVERSITY - and - In the Matter of an• Application for Restoration thereof - and - In the Matter of an Application for an Extension of Time".

On the application for extension of time, the decision concluded that "...sub-section 160(2) in my view refers to an act or step in relation to an application, which application is still subsisting. ...... Excluding sub-regulation 7B(6) which was introduced in 1969, there has never been an act or step in relation to a lapsed application to which section 160 could apply.". I need not mention the many factors which Mr. Kildea discussed in reaching his conclusion. The decision also considered the merits of the application for restoration of the patent and concluded that, had time been extended to permit the application to be made, he would require further evidence to support the application for restoration but would give the applicant an opportunity to provide it. The decision concluded in these terms, "I am not authorised to grant the applications for an extension of time and for restoration of the lapsed application.".

The Board of Control of Michigan Technological University has made application to this Tribunal to review the decision of Mr. Kildea on its two applications of 3 March 1978. When the matter came on for hearing, Dr. Emmerson, counsel for the respondent, submitted that this Tribunal had no jurisdiction to review the decision of Mr. Kildea on the two applications. Mr. Searby, QC, senior counsel for the applicant, contended that the Tribunal had jurisdiction. Accordingly, I indicated that I would first consider the jurisdictional issue and, in the event that I concluded that the Tribunal did have jurisdiction to review the decision of Mr. Kildea, I would give directions as to the further hearing of the review.

Section 25 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 provides, inter alia, that,

"An enactment may provide that applications may be made to the Tribunal -
(a) for review of decisions made in the exercise of powers conferred by that enactment; ...".

Sub-section (4) provides that, "The Tribunal has power to review any decision in respect of which application is made to it under an enactment.". Accordingly, the Tribunal may review any decision in respect of which application is made to it under an enactment which provides that applications may be made to the Tribunal for review of decisions made in the exercise of powers conferred by that enactment. Section 151 of the Patents Act 1952 provides that an application may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for a review of a decision of the Commissioner under s.160 of the Patents Act. Regulation 92 of the Patents Regulations provides that applications may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of decisions made by the Commissioner in the exercise of the powers conferred on him by reg.7B(8).

Dr. Emmerson contended that the decision of Mr. Kildea was not a decision under s.160 of the Patents Act (see s.151 of the Patents Act) or a decision made in exercise of powers conferred by s.160 of the Patents Act (see s.25 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act) and that the decision was not a decision made in exercise of the powers conferred by reg.7B(8) (see regulation 92). In substance, he contended that Mr. Kildea declined to exercise the powers conferred upon him by s.160 and reg.7B(8).

However, when s.160(2) confers authority upon the Commissioner, upon application by the person concerned, to extend the time for doing an act or taking a step, the section confers authority upon the Commissioner to consider the application and to refuse it or, upon the application, to extend time. When Mr. Kildea considered and refused the application of the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University he was exercising or purporting to exercise the authority conferred upon him by s.160(2) so to do and his decision was both a decision under s.160 of the Patents Act and a decision made in the exercise or purported exercise of powers conferred by s.160 of the Patents Act. Similarly, his decision to refuse to restore the lapsed application, being a decision made on an application to him to do so, was a decision made in the exercise or purported exercise of the powers conferred upon him by reg.7B(8).

Any doubt as to jurisdiction is resolved by the provisions of s.3(3) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act which provides that a reference in the Act to a decision includes a reference to "refusing to make an order or determination" and to "refusing to do any other act or thing" and by the decision of the Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia in the Collector of Customs (New South Wales) v. Brian Lawlor Automotive Pty. Limited (not yet reported). In his decision in that appeal, Bowen CJ, in interpreting s.25 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, gave weight to the context of the Act and said,

"The Act is clearly intended to give a person whose interests are affected by an administrative decision an effective appeal, free of technicalities, against that decision on questions of fact and of law (see ss. 25, 27, 28, 31, 33, 42 and 44).".

The decision in that case confirmed that the exercise of jurisdiction by this Tribunal is not dependent upon the existence of a valid decision made by the decision maker and that jurisdiction is conferred in cases where the decision, whether valid or not, was made or purported to be made under the relevant enactment.

For these reasons I am of the view that the Tribunal has jurisdiction to review the decision of the Deputy Commissioner of Patents upon the two applications of the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University.

Section 160(4) of the Patents Act requires that,

"Where an application is made under sub-section (2) of this section for an extension of time for more than three months, the Commissioner shall advertise the application in the Official Journal.".

The application of the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University is an application for an extension of time for more than three months. It has not been advertised in the Official Journal, the view being taken that the advertisement would be purposeless. Mr. Kildea said,

"The purpose of the advertisement of an application for an extension of time is to bring it to the notice of possible opponents. I drew his (Mr. McCaw) attention to a similar situation in regulation 76. I also asked him how could sub-section 160(5), which provides for opposition, and sub-section 160(6), which provides for protection for other persons, operate effectively when the application for a patent is not open to public inspection.".

In my view, it is appropriate that the application for extension of time and the proceeding for review be advertised in the Official Journal. The application for the grant of a patent and the lapsing of that application were both advertised. Some person other than the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University may have acted upon the advice contained in the second advertisement. Therefore the application for extension of time and the application for review should be advertised so that any such person has an opportunity to apply to be made a party to the proceeding before this Tribunal. The procedure for opposing the grant of the application for extension of time, which procedure is prescribed in Part XIII of the Patents Regulations is no longer appropriate. In this review, the procedure to be followed is that laid down by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act and Regulations.

If an appropriate advertisement is now inserted in the Official Journal, I think that the hearing of the merits of the application may proceed in this Tribunal. This is a preferable course to remitting the matter back to the Deputy Commissioner of Patents to be further considered by him after an appropriate advertisement has been inserted in the Official Journal. The Deputy Commissioner of Patents has already given full and detailed consideration to both applications. In this circumstance, remission of the matter to him for further consideration is not appropriate. Therefore, the advertisement which is inserted should be so framed as to satisfy the provisions of s.160(4) of the Patents Act yet bring to the attention of persons affected by the decision of the Deputy Commissioner of Patents and by the application for extension of time that the procedure to be followed is the procedure appropriate to a review by this Tribunal rather than the procedure laid down in Part XIII of the Patents Regulations.

I therefore direct that the Deputy Commissioner of Patents advertise in the Official Journal, as soon as practicable,

(i) the application, dated 3 March 1978, of the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University for extension of time to apply for restoration of the lapsed application No.80103/75 for letters patent;

(ii) his decision, dated 3 November 1978, refusing that application;

(iii) the application, dated 30 November 1978, of the Board of Control of Michigan Technological University to this Tribunal for review of that decision;

(iv) the provisions of s.30 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act which allow a party whose interests are affected by the decision under review to apply to the Tribunal to be made a party to the proceeding for review.

As the terms of the advertisement were not discussed in the hearing before me, I reserve liberty to either party to apply within seven (7) days for variation of the above direction or for further or other directions.

I request the Deputy Commissioner of Patents to advise me as soon as practicable of the date upon which the advertisement will appear in the Official Journal. Having regard to the time specified in reg.48 of the Patents Regulations, I shall fix a date for the further hearing of this review which is more than one month after the date on which the advertisement appears in the Official Journal.
