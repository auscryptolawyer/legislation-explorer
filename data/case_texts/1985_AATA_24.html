Re Kay Fallas Cheatham and Commissioner of Superannuation [1985] AATA 24 (4 February 1985)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: KAY FALLAS CHEATHAM

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. N83/819

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.C. Jennings QC (Deputy President)

Dr. A.H. Marsh (Member)

G.R. Taylor (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - determination of invalidity retirement benefits - benefit classification certificate - whether condition specified was "relevant for the purposes of the superseded Act" - whether incapacity caused by or connected with a condition specified in certificate - osteo-arthritis of knees - motor car injury - overweight problem.

Superannuation Act 1922 s. 5(1)

Harrison and The Commissioner for Superannuation (1.2.79)

Burr and The Commissioner for Superannuation (30.9.80)

Brown and Acting Commissioner for Superannuation (29.5.81)

Richardson and The Commissioner for Superannuation (5.6.81)

Hegedues and The Commissioner for Superannuation (16.4.82)

Re Gardner 2 ALN 164

Re Keith 2 ALN 165

HEARING

CANBERRA

4:2:1985

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the decisions under review and in substitution therefor decides that the benefit entitlement of the applicant under the Superannuation Act 1976 upon her retirement on the ground of invalidity as from 8 May 1981, is to be determined in accordance with Division 4 of Part V of that Act.

DECISION

The applicant, Mrs. Kay Fallas Cheatham, seeks review of the decisions of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation made on 22 September 1983 on re-consideration of earlier decisions by his delegate on 14 May 1982:

(i) to issue a benefit classification certificate

in respect of the applicant specifying the

conditions "Hypothyroidism - overweight - history

of anxiety reaction - poor legs and badly fallen

arches" and

(ii) that in his opinion the incapacity which was the

ground for her retirement, was caused or was

substantially contributed to, by a condition

specified in the certificate or by

(a) condition(s) connected with such (a)

condition(s).

The latter decision was made pursuant to s. 66(2)(c) of the Superannuation

Act 1976 ("The Act") and "the certificate" was issued pursuant to s. 184(2)(c)(i). We shall refer later to these provisions in detail.

In addition to hearing evidence from the applicant and from Dr. Eric Hedberg, an orthopaedic surgeon who gave evidence on behalf of the respondent, the Tribunal had before it the documents furnished in pursuance of s. 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, and a transcript of the evidence in an action brought by the applicant in the District Court of New South Wales against one Abadir (No.5582 of 1979) in respect of injuries she sustained as a result of being struck by a motor vehicle on 24 September 1978.

In May 1972, the applicant, who was then a temporary employee in the Postmaster-General's Department, underwent a medical examination for permanent appointment to the Commonwealth Service. This examination was conducted by a Commonwealth medical officer, Dr. Vout, who is now deceased. The applicant who had undergone a partial thyroidectomy in 1962, told Dr. Vout that she was still taking thyroid tablets. In his report, Dr. Vout described the applicant as being "plump" (her weight then being in excess of 11 stone). He referred to the thyrotoxicosis operation and to the fact that the applicant was still having treatment. He recommended that she should be rejected for permanent employment, observing:-

"Para. 89. Thyrotoxicosis Opn. & Still on

TR".

The reference to "Para 89" is to paragraph 89 in the Handbook for

Commonwealth Medical Officers, which states:-

"Evidence of thyrotoxicosis should disqualify.

If surgical treatment for thyrotoxicosis has

been undertaken, it is essential that the

result be carefully assessed, and freedom from

recurrence of symptoms for at least three

years would be necessary before a candidate

would be suitable for acceptance under

Provident Account conditions".

The applicant's medical records disclosed that she had suffered from

"nerves", and in response to an inquiry from the Department, Dr. O.T. Stanley advised on 11 October 1972 that he had been treating her for anxiety since January 1972; that she had been an in-patient at the Royal South Sydney Hospital for a week and that he had seen her regularly as an out-patient. He stated:-

"In summary. . . . I feel that she was suffering

from an anxiety reaction, which to a large

extent is resolving".

On 24 April 1973, she underwent a further medical examination by a Dr. Rowe

(also now deceased) in connection with her application for a permanent appointment. In his report, Dr. Rowe noted her weight as 13 stone, her height as 5"4" and her blood pressure as 135/80. He stated:-

"Has poor legs and badly fallen arches.

Awaiting Spears test".

That test relates to efficiency, conduct and other aspects of employment.

The result of the Spears test was contained in a memorandum dated 19 April 1973 from the Sydney Mail Exchange where the applicant was employed, to the Superintendent, Personnel Branch, of the Postmaster-General's Department. This memorandum was endorsed by Dr. Rowe on 30 April 1973, as follows:-

"With her history of depressive illness,

unsatisfactory Spears report, I would

recommend rejection".

The Department then referred the applicant to Dr. Innes A. Brodziak, who

furnished a report dated 11 July 1973. After dealing with certain effects of the partial thyroidectomy, Dr. Brodziak stated that because of weight gain she had attended a doctor to reduce weight and that she had lost 1 stone 6 lbs in the nine weeks preceding his examination. He described her as being "a short overweight woman of 13 stone 1 pound" and stated:-

"For some years this lady has had a weight

problem which was added to by some

hypothyroidism following thyroidectomy when

she was given Thyroxine".

He concluded his report as follows:-

"Under the adjusted standards this employee

is, in my opinion, fit for the Provident

Fund".

The reference to "adjusted standards" relates to a Superannuation Board

Circular No.1972/11 dated 19 October 1972, which advised that, following a Government decision to relax the basic medical standards for permanent appointment to the Commonwealth Service, in future a three year minimum period (previously seven) of likely efficient service would be used as a general guideline and that a corresponding variation in medical standards had been made for acceptance of contributors to the Provident Account. The circular concluded with the statement:-

"Therefore, if the employment aspect is clear,

a person may be accepted as a contributor to

the Provident Account if the delegate of the

Superannuation Board, having regard to the

person's state of health, is satisfied that a

further period of three (3) years' efficient

service could reasonably be expected".

Subsequently, on 9 August 1973, the Acting Public Service Inspector

forwarded the applicant's medical papers and clinical details to the Director of Posts and Telegraphs, Sydney and requested that they be examined by the Commonwealth Medical Officer, Mail Exchange. On 14 August 1973, Dr. Rowe minuted this letter as follows:-

"Subject to P.S.I. acceptance of her

hypothyroidism poor work attendance S/L

record she may be medically recommended for

P.A. It can be expected her S/L and absences

will continue".

On 27 September 1973 the Superannuation Board was advised by the Australian

Post Office that the applicant met the requirements of s.34(b) of the Public Service Act and that she was also accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account. This advice was signed by a person described as "Delegate of the Superannuation Board and the Public Service Board".

On 24 September 1978, while returning home from work, the applicant was injured by a car which unexpectedly reversed into her, knocking her to the ground. She suffered a fracture of the right wrist and bruising of both legs, and later developed lower back pain. She returned to work on 1 February 1979 and worked until March when she started to develop pain in her right knee. On 26 October 1979, Dr. Roarty performed an excision of osteo-arthritis of the knee and debridement, but her condition did not improve sufficiently to enable her to return to work.

On 14 October, 1981, she was medically examined by Dr. Perrottet, a Commonwealth medical officer who considered that she was unfit for continued employment and should be retired on the grounds of incapacity for further service. The report form completed by Dr. Perrottet (PSB 14B) makes provision for the medical officer to set out the medical condition(s) from which the examinee is suffering and the estimated percentage each contributes to the incapacity. Dr. Perrottet wrote "Osteoarthritis of knees" - "100%". The report concludes:-

"I consider that the examinee is unfit for

continued employment and should be retired on

the grounds of invalidity".

On 11 November, 1981, the delegate of Australia Post completed Form PNL

219(b) stating:-

"I have retired the abovenamed officer

under Section 59 of the Postal Services

Act as from close of business 8/5/81

due to incapacity as outlined in PSB

14B viz. osteoarthritis of knees".

That section so far as is relevant for present purposes, provides:-

(1) "If an officer appears to the Commission

to be . . . unable to discharge or incapable of

discharging the duties of his position, the

Commission may -

. . .

(c) retire him from the Service."

On 14 May 1982, the respondent, in pursuance of sub-section 184(2) of the

Act issued in respect of the applicant, a certificate specifying the conditions "Thyrotoxicosis", "Overweight", "History of Anxiety Reaction" and "Poor Legs and Badly Fallen Arches" as relevant conditions for the purposes of sub-paragraph (c)(i). Upon a re-consideration pursuant to s.154 of the Act the respondent substituted the condition "Hypothyroidism" for the condition "Thyrotoxicosis". Under s.66(2)(c) of the Act, the delegate of the Commissioner formed the opinion on 14 May 1982 that the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement "was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the certificate or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions". Upon re-consideration, the Acting Commissioner confirmed the opinion so formed by the delegate.

The statutory provisions involved in this case have been fully discussed in previous decisions of the Tribunal: see for example:

Harrison and The Commissioner

for Superannuation (1 Feb. 1979);

Burr and The Commissioner for

Superannuation (30 Sept. 1980);

Brown and Acting Commissioner for

Superannuation 3 ALD 185 (29 May 1981).

Although the relevant statutory provisions are set out extensively in the

latter decision, we will repeat them in shorter form so far as appears to be material for present purposes:-

"184(1) This section applies to any person

who is or at any time has been an existing

contributor other than. . ."

An "existing contributor" means a person who immediately prior to 1 July

1976 was an employee and a contributor for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1922 and has continued to be and is such an employee. That is a paraphrase of the effect of some of the definitions in s.3.

S.184 continues:-

(2) . . ., where -

(a) a person to whom this section applies

who, immediately before the commencing

day, was . . . a contributor to the

Provident Account ceases to be an

eligible employee by reason of . . .

retirement on the ground of invalidity;

(b) his period of contributory service is

less than 20 years and, on the day on

which he ceased to be an eligible

employee, he has not attained his

maximum retiring age; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that

. . . the incapacity which was the ground

for the retirement of the person was

caused, or was substantially

contributed to -

(i) by the physical or mental condition

of the person that was relevant for

the purposes of the superseded Act

or, if there was more than one such

condition, by those conditions or

one or more of those conditions; or

(ii) by a physical or mental condition

or conditions of the person

connected with the physical or

mental condition of the person that

was relevant for the purposes of the

superseded Act or, if there was more

than one such condition, with those

conditions or one or more of those

conditions,

the Commissioner shall issue in respect of

the person a benefit classification

certificate in which there is or are

specified the physical or mental condition

or conditions of the person that was or

were relevant for the purposes of the

superseded Act and, for the purposes of

this Act, the certificate shall be deemed

to have been issued under sub-section 16(4)

and to have been in force in respect of the

person immediately before his death or

retirement.

(7) In this section -

(a) a reference to a contributor to the

Fund shall be read as a reference to

a contributor to the existing Fund

under Part III of the superseded Act;

(b) a reference to a contributor to the

Provident Account shall be read as a

reference to a contributor to the

Provident Account established under

the superseded Act; and

(c) a reference to the physical or mental

condition or conditions of a person

that was or were relevant for the

purposes of the superseded Act shall

be read as a reference to the physical

or mental condition or conditions of

the person that, in the opinion of the

Commissioner, was or were the physical

or mental condition or conditions of the

person by reason of which -

(i) the Superannuation Board was not

satisfied under sub-section 5(1)

of the superseded Act that the

health and physical fitness of

the person were such as to justify

his being accepted as a contributor

to the Fund; or

(ii) . . .

other than -

(iii) a physical or mental condition

that, in the opinion of the

Commissioner, did not exist -

(A) at the time the person

became a contributor to the

Fund or a contributor to the

Provident Account; or

(B) . . .

(iv) . . .

Section 66 provides -

(2) . . . where -

(a) a person ceases to be an eligible

employee by reason of retirement on

the ground of invalidity before

attaining his maximum retiring age;

(b) there was in force in respect of the

person, immediately before the person's

retirement, a benefit classification

certificate; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that

the incapacity which was the ground for

his retirement was caused, or was

substantially contributed to, by a

physical or mental condition or

conditions specified in the certificate

or by a physical or mental condition or

conditions connected with such a

condition or such conditions,

the person is entitled -

. . .

(to specific benefits the nature of which

it is unnecessary to describe).

Section 5(1) of "the superseded Act" (Superannuation Act 1922) provides:-

Except as otherwise provided by this Act, and

notwithstanding anything contained in any

other Act, a person who becomes an employee

after the commencement of the Superannuation

Act 1965 shall not contribute to the Fund

under Part III or to the Provident Account

unless -

(a) he has, before becoming an employee,

or within such period after becoming

an employee as the Board allows,

undergone a medical examination by a

legally qualified medical practitioner

approved by the Board;

(b) a report of the result of the examination

has been furnished by the medical

practitioner to the Board; and

(c) the Board is satisfied, after

considering the report of the medical

practitioner, that the health and

physical fitness of the person are such

as to justify his being accepted as a

contributor to the Fund under Part III

or to the Provident Account, as the case

may be.

On this application, three distinct issues arose out of those provisions:

(1) When the Commissioner issued the certificate (as

amended) did he correctly form the opinion that

"overweight" was a relevant condition for the

purposes of the 1922 Act because it was a condition

by reason of which the applicant was not accepted

by the Board as a contributor to the Superannuation

Fund?

(2) What was the incapacity which was the ground for

the applicant's retirement?

and (3) If "overweight" was a relevant condition, was

that incapacity caused or substantially

contributed to by overweight or by a physical

condition connected with overweight.

It will be noted that in stating the first issue in this form, we have

omitted any reference to the other three conditions mentioned in the certificate. This is because it became common ground during the hearing before us that it could not be argued that any of them fulfilled the requirements of s. 66(2).

In considering the first issue, we were referred to two other decisions of this Tribunal which required interpretation of s. 184(2) and (7):

Richardson and Commissioner for Superannuation

(5 June 1981)

and Heqedues and Commissioner for Superannuation

(16 April 1982).

In Richardson the Tribunal said of s.s. (7) at para 16:

"This definition thus requires a reference not

to a condition of a person that is merely

proved to have existed and to have been a

condition to which reference might properly

have been made, but rather to a condition

that has actually been taken into account by

the Superannuation Board for the purposes of

coming to a conclusion as to the health and

physical fitness of the person".

In Heqedues of the same provision at para 38:

". . . what must be examined is the evidence that

was available to the Board or its delegate

at the time when the decision was made . . . and

not evidence that became available only

subsequently and could not have affected the

decision that was taken at that time".

These principles, which we accept, assumed importance because some of the

evidence produced to us which throws light on the applicant's physical condition at the time she was rejected for permanent employment was not available for the purposes of the decision not to accept her as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund. As we have noticed that decision was automatic once it was accepted that she only qualified for the Provident Account.

The additional evidence, to which the applicant objected, was in the form of entries on clinical notes. It was agreed that this material was never seen by the Board or for that matter by the officer of the Postmaster General's Department who advised the Board of the decision to accept her as a contributor to the Provident Account.

For the reasons stated in Heqedues we uphold the objection to the use of the clinical notes, but if we are wrong in this regard we still find ourselves of the same opinion after taking that material into account.

The material relied on by the respondent included notations about "overweight" which were made by Dr. Vout on 9 May 1972 and Dr. Rowe on 24 April and 14 August 1973.

It is apparent from Dr. Brodziak's report that he regarded any weight problem as under control after a dramatic reduction of 1 stone 6 pounds in 9 weeks. He found mild hypothyroidism and the need to resume thyroxine. The most significant consideration for him was obviously the adjusted standard. He considered that the Board should be satisfied that she could reasonably be expected to give at least three years efficient service.

The question of acceptability for the Superannuation Fund never came under consideration. The issue was always permanence and Provident Account as against total rejection.

Dr. Rowe at first favoured the latter but he did not express his view on the ground of overweight. Dr. Brodziak after discussing the weight question, seemed to be satisfied with the improvement but still described her as overweight at 13 stone 1 pound.

Applying the test referred to in Richardson, it seems to us that the Board must ultimately have acted upon the final observations of Dr. Rowe to whom the "medical papers and clinical details" were referred on 13 August. His observations of 14 August are set out at para 11 above.

No inference that overweight was a factor which influenced Dr. Rowe can be drawn from that statement. But at the time Dr. Rowe was appraising Dr. Brodziak's letter of 11 July 1973, he made this note on the clinical card:-

"Seen by Dr. Brodziak - hypothyroidism

overweight. I think she should still be

rejected but if PSI wishes to accept her with

above record, then P/A".

The reference to overweight is equivocal because it is almost certainly no

more than a brief description of the matters Dr. Brodziak had referred to in his letter.

We therefore conclude that nothing in the clinical notes, if it be proper to include them in our consideration, persuades us that the applicant was rejected as a contributor to the Fund because she was overweight.

Our conclusion that overweight was never taken into account by the Board for the purposes of coming to a conclusion about her health and physical fitness, is strengthened by a submission based on Exhibit B, a document entitled "Examination of Candidates for Admittance to the Commonwealth Service".

The relevant paragraphs are 3, 8, 12(a) and 141 to 144 (inclusive). Their effect may be summarized thus:-

A female candidate who is 25-30% above average

weight should not be accepted for superannuation

without question or for the Provident Account if

she is over 30%. In either event deferment for

a period of up to 11 months with instruction to

try and lose weight, may be recommended to

afford an opportunity to reach the required

standard. Acceptance at the end of the period

of deferment may be approved even although she

is still overweight.

At the relevant time the applicant was only one or two pounds over average

weight plus 25% but she was never given an opportunity to lose the necessary weight to qualify her without question for superannuation benefits.

We accept the submission that these considerations favour a conclusion that her overweight was never in issue at the time the applicant was accepted for the Provident Account.

It seems to us to be far more likely that disqualification occurred by reason of the application of paragraph 89 of the standards which we set out in full at paragraph 5 above.

There was yet another condition apart from the history of "thyrotoxicosis" which may well have been a reason for the applicant's acceptance for Provident Account only. That was a history of depressive illness in 1972. It was mentioned by both Dr. Vout and Dr. Rowe without any particular emphasis. However, paragraph 100(b) of the handbook of medical standards refers to cases of "neuropsychopathy" and provides that consideration can be given to acceptance for superannuation benefits only after deferment for the maximum period if there has been freedom from recurrence of a single episode for 3 years.

Counsel for the respondent, Mr. Whithear, argued that the present case was very similar on its facts to that of Richardson (supra) in which Deputy President Todd affirmed the decision under review notwithstanding the fact that the condition "psoriasis" was not mentioned in the final medical report presented to the Board. In many respects there is similarity between the two cases, but we accept the applicant's submission that Richardson turned on its own facts. For all the reasons referred to above we believe the present application is distinguishable.

The second and third issues which we have outlined in para. 21 only become relevant if we are wrong in our conclusion as to the first.

As to the second, counsel for the applicant relied on the evidence of Dr. Hedberg to establish that it was the condition of the right knee, not the osteoarthritis of both which was the determining factor producing incapacity for work. That knee was worse than the left because of the motor vehicle accident.

We accept that submission which was supported by an analysis of the report of Dr. Perrottet despite the fact that on its face it suggested that the incapacity was due to the condition of both knees. Closer examination reveals that he too found the real problems to be in the right knee.

That we may go behind the stated ground for retirement was established in earlier decisions of this Tribunal:

Re Gardner 2 ALN 164

and Re Keith 2 ALN 165

which we are content to adopt.

In dealing with the third issue, counsel for the applicant disposed of any question of "substantial contribution" to the incapacity by reference to the evidence of Dr. Hedberg. It was his opinion that the condition "genu valgum" (or "knock-knees") and overweight taken together contributed substantially to the development of osteo-arthritis of the knees. But he thought that if the former were subtracted from that equation, the overweight standing alone did not constitute a substantial contribution.

This proposition was further reinforced when account was taken of the opinion, which we accept, that 30 percent of the capacity in the right knee was lost as a result of the accident.

We are therefore satisfied that the incapacity which was the ground for retirement was not substantially contributed to by overweight.

Finally, then, was the incapacity for work which was the ground for her retirement, i.e. osteo-arthritis of the right knee caused by overweight or by a physical condition connected with overweight.

Now there is no doubt that overweight was one of the factors which contributed in some degree to the disabling effects of her osteo-arthritis of the right knee. But it certainly did not cause them. It is equally difficult to find that the disabling effect on her capacity for work was caused or substantially contributed to by any condition connected with overweight.

Counsel for the respondent claimed that in cross-examination, Dr. Hedberg had, in effect, said that overweight was "the underlying basis for osteo-arthritis of the knees by the process of wear and tear". He concluded therefore, that the conditions were linked "on a sustainable medical basis", to use the phrase selected by the Tribunal in re Heqedues (para. 87) and that the link was both "real and demonstrable", a phrase appearing in several earlier decisions adopted in Heqedues (para 86).

But although Dr. Hedberg spoke of an "underlying substratum of osteo-arthritis in both knees", he was not prepared to quantify the extent to which overweight was a factor in determining the onset of the condition. He merely said that the wear and tear in knee joints would be significantly worse in people who are overweight.

That evidence does not satisfy us that the Commissioner was entitled to find that any of the prerequisites of s. 66(2) were fulfilled; in particular that incapacity was either caused or substantially contributed to by a physical condition connected with the condition of overweight specified in the certificate.

Some reliance was also placed on answers given by Dr. Roarty and Dr. Trist in the common law proceedings in an effort to establish the relevance of overweight to the applicant's incapacity. But the issues in question when that evidence was given were so different that we derive no assistance from it.

It follows that both the decisions under review must be set aside.
