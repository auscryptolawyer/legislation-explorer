ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - expense incurred in running air-conditioner -whether an allowable deduction.

Taxation Board of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 s 223

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 51,189

Lodge v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 72 ATC 4174

Lunney v Commissioner of Taxation (1958) 100 CLR 478

Amalgamated Zinc (De Bavay's) Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1935] HCA 81; (1939) 54 CLR 295

Respondent REASONS FOR DECISION

Mrs. Rosemary Balmford, Senior Member

1. This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent disallowing an objection by the applicant to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of his taxable income for the year ended 30 June, 1983, of an amount which was described as follows in his income tax return for that year:

Electricity for Airconditioner

As taxpayer works shiftwork and must sleep during the day for 20 days over a 9 week roster.

5 cycles of roster per year

Therefore 100 nightshifts = 800 hours

=1560KWH @ 6.07& per unit = $94.69

2. The applicant's tax agent lodged a notice of objection on 16 November, 1983, stating:

We have been instructed by above tax payer to object against the disallowance of electricity $94 which was disallowed because you consider it private expenditure.

Taxpayer wishes to stress that he lives in a tropical area and because he is working shifts and his occupation requires sharp concentration when he works, it is impossible to sleep during the day when the humidity and temperature are both very high.

It is therefore essential for the proper carrying out of his occupation that he has an airconditioner and it is for the purpose of running the airconditioner that the electricity is needed and it is therefore requested that $94.00 be allowed under section 51.

3. On 20 December 1983 the applicant requested that the matter be referred to a Board of Review. The material before the Tribunal does not indicate when that request was complied with by the respondent. By virtue of Section 223(1) of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 and sub-section 189(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") that referral of the decision on the applicant's objection is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision.

4. The applicant, who was unrepresented, relied on the first limb of sub-section 51(1) of the Act, which reads:

"All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income ... shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income."

5. He gave evidence as to the facts already stated by his agent and quoted in paragraphs 1 and 2 above. He explained that at the relevant time he was employed by the Bureau of Metereology and stationed in Darwin. He worked night-shifts for which he was paid penalty rates. His duties involved the preparation of weather reports. The work-load imposed upon night-shift workers was particularly heavy as the reports they prepared had to be ready by first light. The reports gave information on which were based, for example, cyclone warnings and flood warnings, and were relied upon by many including the aviation industry. The accuracy of the information conveyed by the reports was therefore of critical importance.

  1. The applicant gave this evidence in support of his contention that it was vitally important for him to be mentally alert at the time he worked the night shift, and that this alertness could only be acquired if he had slept soundly during the day prior to beginning work, on the night shift. The heat and humidity of Darwin, he further asserted, prevented him from sleeping soundly during the day in the absence of an air-conditioner. It was only very rarely that he used the air-conditioner when sleeping at night, and he had made no claim with respect to the electricity costs incurred in doing so. This in substance is the basis of the applicant's claim chat the cost of the electricity for the air-conditioner when used by him during the day is an allowable deduction within the meaning of sub-section 51(1) of the Act.
  2. For the respondent it was submitted that the cost was not 'incurred in gaining or producing' assessable income (the 'first limb' of sub-section 51(1)) and further that, in any case the cost was of a 'private or domestic nature' and thus came within the exception to the sub-section.
  3. It has been clearly established that to fail within the scope of the first limb of the sub-section, it is not sufficient, in the words of Mason J, as he then was, that 'the expenditure was an essential prerequisite to the derivation of assessable income' (Lodge v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, 72 ATC 4174 at 4175, 4176). The applicant sought to distinguish Lodge's case and others similar to it by arguing that here, the cost was incurred not as a prerequisite to the earning of income, but as a means of providing him with the capacity to earn assessable income. It is possible to suggest the nature of the distinction.

In Lodge's case, the expense which was the subject of the application was incurred by the applicant by placing her child in a child care centre; had she not so provided for the care of her child she would have had to perform the task herself, and this would have prevented her from earning income. It was submitted in effect by the applicant in this case that here the expense involved was not incurred in removing some external barrier (a lack of time to both look after the child and work, in Lodge's case) which prevented the tax-payer from earning income, but in making the applicant mentally and physically competent to perform his duties by enabling him to sleep soundly.

9. While the Tribunal accepts that a distinction of the sort outlined may logically be made, it considers that the distinction is not of significance for the purposes of the first limb of the sub-section. The applicant's duty is to compile information and prepare weather reports; he may perform these duties competently or otherwise depending on how well he has slept the day before. But the expenses incurred in helping him sleep and enabling him to perform his duties well cannot be the subject of a successful claim under sub-section 51(1) as the expenses involved were not incurred in compiling information and preparing reports. That a nexus of this nature must exist if the requirements of the sub-section are to be satisfied is implicit in the judgment of Mason J. who said, in Lodge's Case at 4176, in relation to the applicant's expenditure on nursery fees that it "was not incurred in, or in the course of, preparing bills of cost" (this being the work of the applicant). Whether the expenditure is to be characterised as being a "prerequisite" for the earning of income or as affecting the "capacity" of the applicant to earn income, it is this test which must be satisfied and which has not been satisfied in this instance. (See also Lunney v Commissioner of Taxation (1958) 100 CLR 478: Amalgamated Zinc (De Bavay's) Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1935] HCA 81; (1935) 54 CLR 295.)

10. For these reasons it is not necessary to consider whether the expense in question was of a "private or domestic nature". The objection decision under review will be affirmed.
