QT85/3124 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 213; 87 ATC 747 (3 June 1987)

Last Updated: 20 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Assessable Income - Rebates - whether superannuation pension derived - eligibility for rebate in respect of war service pension.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - Ss.19, 160AAA

Superannuation Act 1922 - S.143

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

The Tribunal decides that the objection decision be set aside and the objection allowed in part by reducing the assessable income by an amount of $363.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

)

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Two questions in issue:

(1) whether an amount of $363 was correctly included in the applicant's assessable income; and

(2) whether the applicant is entitled to a rebate in accordance with section 160AAA of the Income Tax Assessment Act.
  1. At the hearing the applicant appeared on his own behalf and the respondent was represented by one of his officers. The respondent's representative advised the Tribunal that the Commissioner conceded that the amount of $363 was not assessable income derived in the year of income ended 30 June 1983 which is the only year before the Tribunal. Because of this concession the hearing was confined to the question of the applicant's entitlement to the rebate under section 160AAA.
  2. As it applied for the' year ended 30 June 1983 section 160AAA is to be read as follows:-

"160AAA. Where the assessable income of a taxpayer of a year of income includes an amount paid by way of pension, allowance or benefit under -

(a) the Interim Forces Benefits Act 1947;

(b) the Repatriation Act 1920;

(c) the Repatriation (Far East Strategic Reserve) Act 1956;

(d) the Repatriation (Special Overseas Service) Act 1962;

(e) the Social Security Act 1947 other than -

(i) Part VII of that Act; and

(ii) section 135D of that Act to the extent to which that section applies in relation to a benefit under Part VII of that Act; or

(f) the Tuberculosis Act 1948,

the taxpayer is entitled in his assessment in respect of income of the year of income to a rebate of tax of -

(g) if the taxable income of the taxpayer of the year of income does not exceed $5007-$167; and

(h) if the taxable income of the taxpayer of the year of income exceeds $5007-$1670 reduced by 12.5 cents for each $1 of the amount of the excess.

4. The applicant's return of income which is before the Tribunal (Exhibit A) disclosed income derived as follows:

Department of Social Security $ 8,460

Department of Veterans Affairs $ 2,052

$10,512

5. The applicant claimed that the amount shown as derived from the Department of Veterans Affairs was overstated by $363. As noted above the Commissioner has conceded that the applicant's claim should be accepted.

  1. For the purposes of these reasons I have assumed that the amount of $1689 (2052-363) is a qualifying amount within the terms of section 160AAA.
  2. Eligibility for a rebate cut out for the year of income in question when assessable income reached $6343 so that a taxpayer who otherwise qualified could only receive a rebate if total assessable income was less than $6343.
  3. On the evidence as set out above the applicant does not qualify for a rebate because of the amount of his assessable income.
  4. The Tribunal also investigated the question as to whether half of the income received from the Department of Social Security had been assigned to the applicant's wife. If effectively assigned the income would not form part of the assessable income of the applicant.
  5. The amount of $8460 was paid on instructions from the Commissioner for Superannuation under the Superannuation Act; the applicant being a retired officer of the Commonwealth Public Service, having retired from that service in the early 1970's. Pension entitlements have been paid to the applicant in accordance with his rights under the Superannuation Act 1922. The pension is paid fortnightly by the Department of Social Security, acting as paying agent for the Commissioner for Superannuation.
    1. These fortnightly pension payments have been paid to a bank account owned jointly by the applicant and his wife. Both have the right to operate the account severally and in fact the account is normally operated by the wife.
    2. As already mentioned the question arose as to whether the applicant had assigned some portion of his pension entitlement to his wife. It seems that the Department of Veterans Affairs regards half of the fortnightly pension as having been derived by the wife when calculating her entitlement to a service pension. The basis for this attitude by that Department appears to be in the relevant legislation and not because of any action on the part of the applicant.

13. There is no evidence of any assignment having been made by the applicant. The applicant did not seek to assert that the right to the pension had been assigned. What did happen is that the applicant directed the then Superannuation Board (predecessor of the Commissioner for Superannuation) to pay the superannuation pension to the joint bank account. That direction was not an assignment of the right to receive the pension and was in the nature of a direction falling within the terms of section 19 of the Income Tax Assessment Act. In any event s.143 of the Superannuation Act 1922 would prevent the Superannuation Board giving effect to any purported assignment by the applicant (see also s118 of the Superannuation Act 1976).

  1. Although I am sympathetic to the situation that the applicant finds himself in whereby the superannuation pension is treated differently by different Commonwealth authorities in a way which results in the applicant and his wife losing both ways, the Tribunal must hold that the pension was derived by the applicant and is therefore correctly assessed to him as assessable income.
  2. The consequence of this is that the amount of the applicant's assessable income disqualifies him from the rebate claimed under section 160AAA.
  3. The objection decision under review will be set aside and the objection allowed in part by reducing the assessable income by $363.

This and the preceding five (5) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT85/3124.

Deputy Registrar
