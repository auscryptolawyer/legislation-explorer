Re Taxation Appeals [1991] AATA 82; 22 ATR 3116 (9 April 1991)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. VT89/516

AAT No. 6812

Income Tax

[1991] AATA 82; 22 ATR 3116

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

P.J.B. Burns(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - whether income derived from a business in the applicant's name and income derived from a business in his wife's name were derived by them in partnership or whether the income of each business was solely income of its registered owner - the valuation of stock on hand - rates of additional tax imposed in respect of late lodgment of returns and false or misleading statements.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 6, 28, 31, 190(b), 193(2), 222, 223 227

Morden Rigg and Co and R.B. Eskrigge and Co v Monks (1923) ATC 450

Booth v Booth [1935] HCA 15; (1935) 53 CLR 1

Trautwein v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1936] HCA 77; 56 CLR 63

Keith Spicer Limited v Mansell (1970) 1 WLR 333

Scallan v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4129

Jolley v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4197 Case N56 81 ATC 277, Case V191 87 ATC 1092, Case V218 87 ATC 1218, Case X22 90 ATC 242

HEARING

MELBOURNE

9:4:1991

ORDER

Affirms the decisions under review in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1970 to 30 June 1985 inclusive.

Varies the decision under review in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1986 by reducing the culpability component in the additional tax imposed to the flat rate of 20%.

In all other respects the Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

Matters at issue in these references are:

(i) Whether the applicant and his wife derived

income in partnership during the years under review.

(ii) The valuation of trading stock on hand.

(iii) The quantum of additional tax imposed on

account of the applicants failure to lodge returns of

income by their due dates and in respect of the making

of false or misleading statements.

2. The applicant conducts an amusement machine business and did not lodge returns of income for many years until 26 November 1986 when he lodged returns of income for the years of income ended 30 June 1970 to 30 June 1986 inclusive. In those returns he disclosed one half of the income of a business conducted in his own name ("TDE") and for the period 13 February 1973 to 30 June 1986 one half of the income of a business conducted in his wife's name ("FI") claiming that the businesses were operated by him and his wife in partnership.

3. The Commissioner of Taxation ("the Commissioner") did not accept the claim that a partnership subsisted between the parties and assessed the whole of the income from TDE for all of the years of income under review to the applicant. For the period 13 February 1973 to 30 June 1984 the Commissioner excised from the applicant's assessable income the one half share of the income returned by him as having been derived from FI. For the years of income ended 30 June 1985 and 30 June 1986 the Commissioner included the whole of the income from FI in the applicant's assessable income.

4. Stock on hand as at 30 June 1986, although none was returned by the applicant was estimated by the Commissioner to amount to $10,000. Additional tax was imposed on account of the late lodgment of the returns of income and also in respect of false or misleading statements contained in those returns.

5. The applicant lodged objections against the assessments and on their disallowance by the Commissioner requested reference to the Tribunal.

6. At the hearing the Commissioner was represented by Mrs A. Richards of Counsel, and the applicant appeared in person.

7. Four witnesses were called by the applicant namely a Hotel Manager ("S") who had business dealings with the applicant, a former employee ("J"), the applicant's present accountant ("G"), and the State Manager ("O") of the firm which supplied the applicant with amusement machines. The applicant also tendered statutory declarations from a former hotel owner with whom he had previous business dealings and from a former employee both of whom now live interstate, a personal friend and from the solicitor who had represented the applicant in proceedings before the Family Court of Australia. Two witnesses were called by the Commissioner, the applicant's former wife ("M") and his former accountant ("W").

8. In addition to the documents provided under sec. 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, various accounting records including cash books, ledgers, pay in books, takings books, machine records, bank statements and cheque butts were tendered. Other documents included numerous letters, business name registrations, agreements, returns of income of M and Family Court documents.

9. The applicant who is aged about 50 years was for several years during the 1960's employed by an hotel chain as Manager of a ten pin bowling alley situated in an outer suburban area adjacent to a capital city. In late 1969 the proprietors decided to relinquish control of the bowling alley. The applicant took over its conduct on his own account and on 26 July 1969 registered a business name TDE. Shortly afterwards, the bowling centre was closed and the applicant decided to go into the business of manufacturing billard tables and the hiring out of coin operated amusement machines which were to be placed on site in hotels and clubs. Premises were leased and the manufacture of the tables commenced.

10. Several of the employees from the bowling centre were initially employed in the business on a "no pay basis". In order to finance the business the applicant borrowed $10,000 from the mother of one of the employees and $6,000 from a finance company. The billard table manufacturing side of the business was not successful and was closed down during 1974 and the factory premises vacated. The amusement machine business which later prospered was transferred to the applicant's residence.

11. During 1969 whilst the applicant was at the bowling centre he employed M as an administrative assistant and a romance developed between them. M was one of the employees who came with the applicant when he established the billiard table manufacturing business and according to both the applicant and M she worked long hours assisting in the repair of tables. She also did some work during this period as a legal secretary. On 27 November 1971 a child was born to the couple and because they were not married M became increasingly worried about her future financial security.

12. At the time the couple was acquainted with a well known sporting identity ("F") and it was proposed that the applicant and M join forces with him to get amusement machines installed in hotel premises.

13. The applicant believed that F's reputation would ensure entry into hotels not available to less well known persons. On 19 September 1972 the business commenced and it was conducted by a partnership formed between M and F. The business name of the partnership FI was registered on 1 October 1972. Through the efforts of the applicant and F sites were obtained in hotels for amusement machines but initially because FI was short of funds it sold those sites to other operators. Later as finances improved FI retained the sites which it obtained and installed its own machines on them. The partnership was dissolved on 12 February 1973 and from that date onwards the business was conducted by M and she was registered as its sole proprietor. There are varying accounts as to the reasons why M was named as a partner and later the owner of FI. M stated that the business was structured in that way to give her financial security and at all times during the hearing maintained that after the dissolution of the partnership the business was hers solely.

14. The applicant rejected that version and claimed that the reason why he was not included in the partnership was because he had discussed the matter with M and it was agreed between them that as he was not lodging returns of income some tax had to be paid, and if the Commissioner made inquiries which disclosed that M was paying tax she would be in the clear and any penalties for non compliance with the taxation laws would be his responsibility. By this means, he said, the total operations were divided into two sections, one FI which would derive income which would be returned by M, and the other, TDE, the income from which would not be returned by either of them.

15. He also stated that it was hoped that if taxation officers made inquiries and it could be shown tax was being paid in respect of income being derived from FI, the fact there was another section of the business in respect of which tax was not paid might not be detected.

16. He said that the arrangement was entered into taking into account the business known as TDE was in his name and it was conducted as a "mutual business". He said that from the time FI was created TDE continued to trade but it did not seek any further clients. All promotional work from that time, he said, was directed towards furthering the activities of FI and little emphasis was placed on his original business. That statement is inconsistent with the fact that the tax depreciated value of machines on hand owned by TDE increased from $4,765 as at 30 June 1970 to $72,007 as at 30 June 1986.

17. During the period in which the partnership between M and F conducted the business of FI it maintained its own books of account which were written up by M. The firm's bank account was kept at a branch of the ANZ Bank and the signatories of the account were the two partners. After dissolution of the partnership M became the sole signatory of the account and the applicant was the sole signatory of the TDE bank accounts. This situation continued until mid 1977 and at that time the applicant negotiated a large order from a major client for the placing of a number of machines on site at various hotels. In order to meet that commitment he arranged the purchase of machines to the value of some $40,000. The purchase was made in the name of FI and the purchase price was payable within 30 days of the making of the order. M stated that she was appalled when she heard this as there were insufficient funds in the FI bank account to pay for the machines. This caused problems between her and the applicant which resulted in his disappearing and she did not hear from him for some weeks. She was left to sort out the situation and acting on advice which she received from the accountant W opened a fresh bank account and deposited takings into that account so enabling her to pay for the machines. Those deposits included takings from machines owned by FI and TDE. The machines were then allocated to either FI or TDE on the basis of which entity paid for them.

18. The relationship between M and the applicant had been a stormy affair and according to M a lot of the problems arose from her insistence that he lodge all of his back income tax returns. On his return home after the absence referred to earlier he told her that he would lodge the returns and together they visited W the accountant. As part of the arrangement M became a signatory of the TDE bank account and the applicant a signatory of the FI accounts. M said in evidence that she never operated the TDE bank accounts and did not believe that she had access to funds in order to deal with them as her own money.

19. Both business continued to be conducted from the matrimonial home until 21 June 1978 when they were transferred to factory premises leased in M's name. The businesses are still conducted from those premises which were purchased on 7 September 1981 for $112,500 and registered in the joint names of M and the applicant. During the period that the premises were leased the rental was paid by FI and no contribution was made in respect thereof by TDE. Both parties contributed to the purchase price of the premises but the precise contribution made by each was not established.

20. That procedure was indicative of a number of others which persisted during the period under review. All staff were employed by FI which was registered for group tax purposes and paid their wages. TDE did not reimburse FI for those charges with the exception of one payment of $7,500 which was made in July 1977 in respect of one employee. In addition FI was registered with the stamp duty authorities for credit and rental business. Rental business returns which were prepared by M included machines owned by both FI and TDE. Also the applicant devoted a considerable amount of time to FI but he was not paid wages in respect of his services.

21. There were situations when FI would pay rent to TDE for equipment owned by the latter. On the other hand a Jaguar motor vehicle was purchased by M and TDE paid rent for its use in the business. Running charges in respect of the vehicle were paid by TDE and reimbursement was not made by FI in respect of vehicle use on its account. Also M said in evidence that in the early stages up until about 1979 FI purchased its own accessories but from that time onwards they were purchased by TDE and FI would reimburse TDE for parts which it used. Reimbursement was also made for small Christmas gifts paid for by FI on behalf of TDE.

22. Early in 1979 the couple separated and on 4 April 1979 the Magistrates' Court ordered the applicant to pay maintenance to M and their three children. In September 1980 a reconciliation took place and two years later in September 1982 the final separation occurred. During the whole of the period up until September 1982 M had remained a signatory of the TDE bank account but did not withdraw any funds from that account for her own use. In September 1982 the applicant withdrew all moneys from the FI account and opened a fresh account of which he was the sole signatory as trustee for FI. In addition he changed the bank accounts of TDE to new accounts of which he was the sole signatory.

23. Despite being denied access to funds from what she claimed was her business M continued to work in the business until the end of 1984 making entries in both the Machine Record Books and the Takings Books. FI still continued to employ all of the staff so in order to pay the wages which she regarded as her responsibility she insisted that the applicant give her the necessary funds which she banked in the original FI account and then withdrew the wages from that account in cash.

24. Amongst the accounting records tendered were the Machine Record book, Collection books and Takings books. The Machine Record book recorded the date purchased, cost price and the name of the supplier of each of the machines purchased and the delivery date. One book was used to record all machines purchased. If a machine was purchased by FI the letter F was entered along side the purchase details and in the case of a TDE machine the letter T was entered. There was also another letter W used in a number of cases. In his evidence the applicant said that W represented an account with a building society and that it was used for electrical machines the proceeds of which were not going to be declared for tax. This was contested by M who asserted that she saw no reason why this distinction should be made. In addition in returns of income lodged by TDE depreciation was claimed in respect of machines purchased from the W cash book and none was allotted to FI.

25. The Collection books were used by employees who collected coins from the machines on site and a separate book was used for recording collections from machines designated F, W and T as discussed.

26. The Takings book was written up from information extracted from the Collection books by M during most of the period under review. All takings were recorded in the book and allocated to F, W or T in accordance with the procedures previously described. At times it became necessary to swap machines from one site to another or to withdraw a machine from service. In those instances, irrespective of whether a replacement machine was an F, W or T machine, the takings were allocated on a site basis. That is if the original machine was designated F and belonged to FI and was located on an FI site the takings would be recorded as income of FI, notwithstanding that the replacement machine was owned by TDE.

27. The extent to which the swapping of machines from one site to another took place was not established. M stated in evidence that these swaps were in the main due to users becoming tired of a particular machine or because of its withdrawal from service because it was malfunctioning. In his evidence the applicant said that there was also a large number of transfers made from FI sites to TDE sites in order to divert income from FI, the income from which was subject to tax in M's hands, to either W or T so enabling the accumulation of income by TDE which was not being returned as assessable income.

28. The first witness called by the applicant was a former employee J. In evidence he stated that he was employed as a serviceman by FI from August 1976 to July 1980 and he visited the machine sites weekly. During that period he had social contact with both the applicant and his wife. In the early years the business was conducted from the applicant's residence which had a very large garage or workshop. He stated that he worked for FI although he was aware there was another business known as TDE. He serviced pin ball machines and video equipment and billiard tables. He thought that the proprietors of the business were the applicant and his wife with M doing the general office work and the applicant the outside work. He received instructions from both parties. He said that the recorded message on the telephone answering system used the words FI. He said that three separate Collection books were used to record takings but this was not his concern. He also said that the only name on the factory was FI and the only business cards he saw were also in that name. He stated that his salary was paid by FI and it issued annual group certificates to him for income tax purposes.

29. Under cross-examination he asserted that his understanding of the meaning of partnership was "a joint venture between two parties". He admitted that he did not know the reason why three separate collection books were used but assumed they were used for an accounting purpose. He also stated in reply to questions from Counsel for the Commissioner that he was not aware a vehicle used by FI was not owned by that organisation but was leased to it by TDE. Similarly he was unaware of the fact that spare parts were owned by TDE and when FI required parts it reimbursed TDE for their cost. He also could not remember having seen in the office a framed registration certificate of the business name of TDE.

30. The second witness O called by the applicant is currently the State Manager of an organisation which it was stated is the largest manufacturer and distributor of amusement machines in Australia. He has occupied that position for about eight years and prior to that time was the company's Sales Manager.

31. In evidence he stated that his company was a member of the National Amusement Machine Operators Association of Australia ("NAMOA") and he attended national trade conventions held by the industry. During the period 1977 to 1982 he was acquainted with both the applicant and his wife and he believed they represented FI only and no other organisation. He met them on a number of occasions at functions arranged by the NAMOA. FI was a major buying force during that time and was well known in the industry and in particular in the hotel trade. O said that after taking up his present position he noted that company records indicated that the applicant and his wife were joint proprietors of FI and from his dealings with it he formed the opinion that the applicant was involved in the day to day purchases for FI and the siting of machines whilst M was involved with office administration and the accounts.

32. Under cross-examination he said that he had never heard the name TDE and expressed surprise when it was indicated to him that from time to time payments for purchases made in the name of FI were made by cheques written by TDE.

33. The third witness S is presently a Hotel Manager. In evidence he stated that he first had contact with the applicant and his wife in 1975 at which time he was the Assistant Manager of a suburban hotel. He said that if he telephoned their premises to have an amusement machine serviced M would normally answer the telephone and all invoices issued were in the name of FI. For a period during 1975 to about 1977 S had worked in another state and on his return took up a promotion with a large hotel group transferring over the years to a number of different suburban hotels as Manager.

34. Amusement machines were installed in each of those hotels and as far as he was aware those connected with the applicant and his wife were owned by FI and no other name was used. He assumed that the business was conducted by them in partnership because they were married with M working in the office and the applicant out in the field. Under cross examination, he admitted that he presumed there was a partnership because it was generally accepted that business people who are married are in partnership but he had no knowledge of the inner workings of the business of FI.

35. The statutory declarations made by the former hotel owner, former employee and friend of the applicant which were tendered in evidence, in a general manner disclosed those persons believed that a partnership existed between the applicant and his wife.

36. W said in evidence that he had been a practising accountant for some thirty years and had first met the applicant early in 1969 and at that time the applicant managed a ten pin bowling alley. The applicant took over the conduct of the alley on 1 July 1969 and on 29 July 1969 registered the trade name TDE. He said that for the period September 1969 to December 1969 he wrote up the books for TDE on a monthly basis. During this period he also assisted the applicant in making loan applications and visited various banks with him. W confirmed that moneys borrowed by the applicant on commencement of the business as a billard table manufacturer were borrowed in the applicant's own name. He also asserted that it was never suggested to him M was in partnership with the applicant. W said that he ceased writing up the applicant's books as at 30 June 1980 but continued to prepare his income tax returns.

37. He said that he was aware of the registration of FI in October 1972 and it was to be a partnership between M and F. He went on to say that he had discussions with the applicant in respect of the partnership because of what he considered to be M's lack of experience in the business. In discussions W said that the applicant told him he thought it was a good idea for M to go into the business as he was not then divorced from his first wife and it would provide protection against any claim she might make against him and secondly, that it was protection for his future wife M and their children.

38. W said that he did not write up the books of account for FI, that being done by M, but he prepared income tax returns for the partnership and after its dissolution he prepared returns of income for M in which he included income derived by FI.

39. W also said that he prepared returns of income for the applicant for the years of income ended 30 June 1970 to 30 June 1984 inclusive. The returns for the years of income ended 30 June 1970 to 30 June 75 inclusive were prepared for signing within their prescribed lodgment periods but those for the later years were prepared by the following dates:

Year ended Date of

30 June completion

1976 - 1978 inclusive

24 October 1983

1979 and 1980 January 1984

1981 and 1982 May 1984

40. The reason why the returns were completed well outside of their lodgment dates is not clear. W stated that he thought it was his duty to prepare the returns although he could not convince the applicant to sign them. In fact he said that the applicant had told him at one stage he would leave the country rather than pay tax. He recalled many instances in which M had begged the applicant to sign and lodge his returns.

41. He said that the accounts of the two businesses were kept separate and the ownership of machines purchased by either FI or TDE were recorded under the name of the entity which paid for them. He also testified that he considered the W account part of TDE and recorded machines purchased with money from that account as being owned by TDE. Depreciation schedules were prepared on the basis of ownership as described.

42. The accountant G who replaced W during 1980 was questioned about matters concerned with the accounts prepared by W prior to his taking over the applicant's books. He prepared returns of income for the years of income ended 30 June 1970 to 30 June 1984 inclusive from information supplied by W and varied only by the claim that the income of FI and TDE was derived by a partnership of the applicant and M.

43. M lodged returns of income in respect of each of the years of income ended 30 June 1970 and 30 June 1972 to 30 June 1984 inclusive by their due dates. A return of income was not lodged for the 1971 year as she stated in evidence that she received no income in that year. In the return of income for the year ended 30 June 1973 she disclosed income from the partnership with F and from FI on her own account for the years of income ended 30 June 1973 to 30 June 1984 inclusive. In returns of income lodged for the years of income ended 30 June 1970 and 30 June 1972 she returned wages received from TDE together with other miscellaneous amounts.

44. M stated in evidence that taxes payable were paid on time until March 1979 when she had an outstanding bill of some $16,000. She was separated from her husband at the time and there was insufficient money in the FI account to pay the bill. She said that the applicant at first agreed to lend her the money but then stopped payment on the cheque. Later he relented and at the eleventh hour he gave her the money. A similar situation occurred in respect of the assessment for the year of income ended 30 June 1984. At that time M had no access to the FI bank account and she stated in evidence that the applicant paid about $2,000 in instalments of a total debt of $5,000 and she borrowed the rest on a bank personal loan.

45. Sec. 190(b) of the Act imposes upon the taxpayer the burden of proving that the assessments are excessive and requires the applicant in this case to establish that a partnership existed in the relevant years. The term partnership is defined in sub-s. 6 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") as follows:

"Partnership means an association of persons in

business as partners or in receipt of income

jointly, but does not include a company."

The definition includes two tests, firstly whether there is a partnership under the general law and alternatively whether the parties are in receipt of income jointly.

46. In Jolley v The Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4197 Burchett and Lee JJ. at p 4199 said:

"The question whether a partnership exists is a

mixed question of law and fact (Keith Spicer Ltd. v

Mansell (1970) 1 WLR 333)"

and then at pp 4204 and 4205,

"Whatever may amount to a partnership is a

subject which has been settled by decision

according to English law, and the incidents of

the partnership simply follow from the

establishment of the fact of the partnership.

If the partnership is established as a fact,

then the liability to creditors is a mere

incident flowing from the establishment of the

fact. But it is a contract of some kind

undoubtedly - a contract, like all contracts,

involving the mutual consent of the parties;

and it is undoubtedly a contract for the

purpose of carrying on a commercial business - that

is a business bringing profit, and

dividing the income in some shape or other

between the partners. That certainly

partnership is. Whether it is anything more

or not has been a question between various

authorities, but it is that certainty."

(Pooley v Driver) (1876 5 Ch D 458 at p 472).

Section 8 of the Partnership Act 1895 sets out

matters to which reference is to be made in

determining whether or not a partnership exists,

but the criteria therein are expressed in a

negative rather than in a positive form and the

principal rule for determination of the existence

of a partnership is the requirement that the true

contract and intention of the parties is to be

ascertained according to all the facts and

circumstances which bear on the relationship

between the parties concerned. (Cox v Hickman

[1860] EngR 1068; (1860) 8 HLC 268)."

47. There was no written agreement between the applicant and his wife and whilst this does not necessarily exclude a claim that a partnership existed it does place a heavy onus on the applicant. M at all times before the Tribunal and also in documents lodged with the Family Court maintained that FI was her business and she had never entered into a partnership with the applicant. The applicant has not identified the time when the alleged partnership commenced and the reasons advanced by him as to why FI was registered in M's name relate to an alleged agreement between them, denied by her, that its purpose was to avoid tax. Even if accepted it does not refer to an agreement to enter into a partnership.

48. It is also significant that in 1978 during one of the couple's disagreements the applicant made handwritten notes on the back of an envelope bearing the name of his wife's solicitors which included the following comments, "No future work to be performed by (the applicant) as of instantaneous in the name of FI, all equipment owned by TDE is to be expressly withdrawn from use by FI, all clients to be advised in writing that there is no connection between TDE and FI and that (the applicant) is not connected and not responsible for the activities of FI". He has not demonstrated that both parties intended to share profits and risks or even that there was a general understanding to that effect (see Morden Rigg and Co. and R.B. Eskrigge and Co. v Monks (1923) 8TC 450).

49. In Jolley's case (supra) at p 4205 Burchett and Lee JJ. referred to case N56 (supra) were the Board of Review stated:

"a business being carried on as a partnership

represents to the world that a contractural

obligation, either expressed or implied, exists

between the parties forming the partnership. As

part of this contract, a partner cannot transfer

his share in a partnership to someone who is not

already a partner unless all co-partners

consent. Also banks, suppliers and

customers having dealings with the partnership have

a right to know they are trading with a

partnership, as opposed to dealing with an

individual, stemming from the obligation of

partners to meet the partnership debts to the full

extent of their own resources".

50. There is no evidence that the banks where the respective bank accounts were kept had any knowledge that there was a partnership between the parties. Each of the witnesses called by the applicant believed that there was a partnership between the applicant and M but this appears in each case to have been based upon the assumption that it was so because they were husband and wife and worked together in the business with M performing the administrative work and the applicant being involved in outside promotion. With the exception of the former employee J none of the other witnesses had heard of TDE and O, the State Manager of the amusement machine supplier, was surprised to learn that some of the machines purchased from his organisation were paid for by cheques from TDE. None gave evidence that he had been told the couple were in partnership.

51. Other evidence given by the applicant in respect of his dealings with outside organisations related to his membership of the NAMOA on behalf of FI and his attendance at functions accompanied by M but here again there was no direct evidence that there was a business partnership between the two of them.

52. In case N56 supra at p 281 when referring to the normal husband and wife relationship and the work performed by the wife as part of an asserted business association the Board of Review stated:

"obviously the two are inextricably intermingled.

In such circumstances the Courts have been wary in

treating the wife's role as indicative of the

existence of a partnership; rather they have tended

to see it as a normal incident flowing from the

ordinary family relationship (ref. Booth v Booth

[1935] HCA 15; (1935) 53 CLR 1 at pp 27 and 28; see also Butler v

Madden (1941) 41 SR (NSW) 245 at p 247".

53. The evidence in the instant case discloses that there was intermingling of the two businesses to an extent which would not be expected in the case of two organisations conducted at arms length to one another, but not so unusual in circumstances involving a husband and wife and I do not accept the proposition that this intermingling establishes the existence of a partnership between the parties.

54. For the foregoing reasons I find that the applicant has not demonstrated to the Tribunal that a partnership within the statutory definition of that term subsisted between him and M.

55. Consideration is now given to the question as to whether the parties were in receipt of income jointly.

56. Prior to the incident in mid 1977 when the applicant placed the large order for machines in the name of FI, separate bank accounts were maintained with M being the sole signatory of the FI bank account and the applicant the sole signatory of the account of TDE. There was intermingling of the businesses and the applicant placed great reliance on his claim that income from machines owned by FI was diverted to TDE in order to avoid it being included in M's assessable income. This claim was strongly denied by M. There was no evidence provided which established its extent or the effect which it had on the respective incomes of FI and TDE. M stated that such transfers of machines between the two entities were based upon malfunction of machines or in instances were clients became tired of a machine at a particular location and these changes would have had little effect on the income of either business. Save for the minor exceptions discussed it is apparent that the takings of each business were deposited into bank accounts of which only one person had control and for the period up until 30 June 1977 the applicant and his wife were not in receipt of income jointly.

57. In mid 1977 a change occurred, and although the same bank accounts were maintained as a result of an agreement reached between the parties M became a joint signatory of the TDE bank accounts and the applicant a joint signatory of the FI account.

58. In her evidence M stated that this was done to avoid her being placed again in the position encountered earlier in that year when she had insufficient funds to pay for the large order for machines placed by her husband. W the accountant in his evidence stated that he advised her to open a new account and deposit takings from TDE into it so that she could pay the outstanding invoices. However, the machines purchased were allocated to each business on the basis of the source of the funds used to pay for them. M asserted that she viewed the bank accounts of TDE as solely under the ownership and control of the applicant and would not have withdrawn funds from the account without his express permission. This is subtantiated by the fact that in March 1979 when she required funds to pay her income tax assessment of $16,000 she found it necessary to borrow the money from the applicant rather than withdraw it from the TDE account. Notwithstanding the change in the signatories of the accounts takings continue to be deposited into the respective bank accounts as described earlier.

59. The couple separated in 1979 and on 4 April 1979 M obtained an order of the Magistrates' Court ordering the applicant to pay maintenance to her and the children. After a reconciliation which took place in September 1980 the previous arrangements prevailed until September 1982 and from that time onwards M did not have access to any funds at all as the applicant closed all accounts of both FI and TDE and opened new ones of which he was the sole signatory.

60. The evidence does not support the proposition that the parties were in receipt of income jointly and the alternative test contained in the definition of partnership has not been satisfied.

61. For the years of income ended 30 June 1985 and 30 June 1986 the whole of the incomes of both FI and TDE have been assessed to the applicant. This assessment is not based upon a presumption of ownership of the FI assets by the applicant but on the premise that he assumed control of them and used them in the production of income which he converted to his own use.

62. The grounds of the applicant's objections do not address the method of calculation of the incomes during those years other than in respect of the partnership issue and the valuation of trading stock on hand as at 30 June 1986 and in any event the point was not argued before the Tribunal.

63. In the return of income lodged for the year of income ended 30 June 1986 the applicant did not disclose any value for trading stock on hand and the Commissioner in calculating the applicant's taxable income for that year included stock on hand at a value of $10,000. This estimate was based on calculations which showed that for the years of income ended 30 June 1980 to 30 June 1984 inclusive the average closing stock for the 2 businesses was $8,275 with $11,406 being returned as being on hand as at 30 June 1984. Gross receipts for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1986 amounted to $257,000 and $159,000 respectively, a decrease of some $98,000 between those 2 years. However, those figures relate in the main to takings from machines and sales of equipment form only a minor part and they are not necessarily indicative of the value of stock held for resale.

64. In his objection the applicant claimed that the correct figure was $500 and strangely although no stock on hand was included as at 30 June 1986 he included that amount as opening stock as at 1 July 1986 in his return for the year of income ended 30 June 1987. He said in evidence that a stocktake was not made as at 30 June 1986. He did provide the Tribunal with figures of a stocktake taken at 21 September 1990 some 4 years after which disclosed stock of a value of $1,105.

65. Although I have some concern that the Commissioner's estimate is too high the applicant has failed to discharge the onus of proof and the Commissioner's decision is affirmed.

66. The final matter for consideration is the imposition by the Commissioner of additional tax imposed on account of late returns and false or misleading statements.

67. As additional tax imposed under sec. 222 of the Act on account of late lodgment of the returns of income for the years of income ended 30 June 1970 to 30 June 1986 inclusive does not exceed 20% per annum the Tribunal does not have the power to review the Commissioner's decision in respect thereof (sec. 193(2) of the Act).

68. Similarily as additional tax imposed under sec. 223 of the Act for the years of income ended 30 June 1970 to 30 June 1985 inclusive does not exceed 20% per annum the Tribunal does not have the power to review the Commissioner's decision in respect of those years. Additional tax imposed under sec. 223 for the year of income ended 30 June 1986 was calculated at the rate of 40% flat and as it exceeds a rate of 20% per annum is subject to review by the Tribunal.

69. The additional tax imposed for the latter year relates to the treatment by the Commissioner of the incomes of FI and TDE as solely income of the applicant, the non disclosure of stock on hand as at 30 June 1986 and the claiming as a deduction of capital payments of $14,007 as rent.

70. The applicant presented his case before the Tribunal with great conviction and despite the many inconsistencies disclosed I am convinced that he believed he and his wife were in partnership although as set out in my reasons the facts do not support that contention. The omission of stock on hand and the claiming of capital expenditure as a deduction are serious breaches and ordinarily a 40% penalty would seem to be appropriate. However when viewed in conjunction with the partnership claim the additional tax imposed under sec. 223 of the Act in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1986 should be reduced to a flat rate of 20%.
