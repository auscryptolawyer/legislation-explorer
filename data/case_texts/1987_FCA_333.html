Re Charlotte Melkman As Executor of the Estate of Nico Melkman Deceased v the Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1987] FCA 333 (30 September 1987)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: CHARLOTTE MELKMAN AS EXECUTOR OF THE ESTATE OF NICO MELKMAN DECEASED

And: THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

No. G43 of 1987

Income Tax - Racial Discrimination

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEW SOUTH WALES DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Fox J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - pensions payable to victims of persecution - pension payable under Benefit Payments to Victims of Persecution 1940-1945 (Netherlands) - whether subject to income tax - whether characterised as income or capital - whether exempt under s.23AD(3)(c) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - Income & Corporation Taxes Act 1970 (U.K.) s.365.

Racial Discrimination - Racial Discrimination Act 1975 s.10 - whether s.23(kc) Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 exempting like payments from Germany confers on persons of a particular national origin a right that is not enjoyed by persons of another national origin, within the meaning of s.10 Racial Discrimination Act 1975.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.23(kc), 23AD(3), 25

Benefit Payments to Victims of Persecution 1940-1945 (Netherlands)

Income & Corporation Taxes Act 1970 (U.K.)

Injuries in War (Compensation) Act 1914 (U.K.)

Injuries in War Compensation Act 1914 (Session 2) (U.K.)

Injuries in War (Compensation) Act 1915 (U.K.)

The Pensions (Naval, Army & Air Force & Mercantile Marine) Act 1939 (U.K.)

The Pensions (Mercantile Marine) Act 1942 (U.K.)

Racial Discrimination Act 1975 s.10

Gehardy v. Brown [1985] HCA 11; (1985) 159 CLR 70

HEARING

SYDNEY

30:9:1987

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr J. Basten

Solicitors for the Applicant: Kingsford Legal Centre

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr S.W. Gibb

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The appeal be dismissed.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

This is an appeal from the Administrative Appeals Tribunal relating to an income tax matter. The taxpayer, the applicant in this Court, seeks to have treated as exempt income under the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") a pension paid to her late husband by the Netherlands Government under the Benefits Payments to Victims of Persecution 1940-1945 (the "Benefits Act") of that country. He was not a member of the armed forces of that country nor, so far as appears, was he engaged in military operations. He was, however, of Jewish extraction and in September 1942, while living in Holland he was arrested by the authorities and sent to a concentration camp. He was moved from prison to prison and in the course of his imprisonment maltreated. He also was a witness to what the learned Senior Member of the Tribunal has described as "extraordinary atrocities". He thereafter served for several years in the Dutch navy and then migrated to Australia with his wife. Taking the description of events from that given by the Senior Member:-

"In Australia from the mid-1950's he supported his

family by working as a kitchen-hand in a public

hospital. Although he was qualified for higher duties

his health was not good enought to enable him to secure

advancement and hold it. From the late 1940's he had

suffered from epilepsy which was accepted by his medical

advisers as having been occasioned by head injuries

suffered whilst in concentration camps. By September

1976, when aged only 54 years, he was unable to continue

the employment of the past 18 years. He resigned on

grounds of ill health and from shortly thereafter did

not work again. The medical practitioner who had

treated him over the years and others who assessed his

condition at that time attested that he was mentally

unfit to continue work and that they were satisfied that

that condition was attributable to the atrocities he had

observed and the injuries he had suffered in

concentration camps."

2. From 1978 the Respondent Commissioner has treated the pension as assessable income. The assessment was objected to but relevantly the objection was disallowed on 10 March 1981. On that date original assessments also issued in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1977, 1979 and 1980. On 26 March 1981, the taxpayer requested that his objection in relation the the 1978 year be referred for review and his later assessments have also been the subject of requests for review, including assessments issued in relation to the 1980 and and 1982 years. The earliest of these objections was not referred for review until 31 May 1985 but the taxpayer died in January 1985 before the matter could be decided. The proceedings before the Board of Review and before this Court have been maintained by his widow.

3. The first submission made is that the pension benefits were not income for the purposes of s.25 of the Act. It is necessary to look at their nature and to take account of the legislation under which they are paid. Translations of this legislation have been put before me. It appears therefrom that the pension payable was related to income derived or presumed to be derived by the taxpayer, stated as being from "their work in occupation or business which the persecuted person would have enjoyed in the Netherlands at the time of the application...", with some adaptation where the applicant's income was derived from an occupation outside the Netherlands. The legislation provides for variation in payments, dependent upon considerations, some personal to the payee. (Arts 7(4)(a), 10(1), 11(1) and 18). It is apparent that the pension accrues from day to day or week to week.

4. It seems to me that there is no scope for regarding the payments as other than income. The fact that they were attributable to a period of persecution of two or three years does not alter their character, having in mind their periodic nature, the fact that the entitlement accrues periodically and that they are calculated by reference to loss of income. They are made because of "persecution" which is defined in the Act to include some considerations and exclude others.

5. The next submission is that the payments were exempt from income tax because of the provisions of s.23AD(3)(c) which is as follows:-

"23AD(3) The following payments are exempt from income tax:-

...

(c) payments of wounds and disability pensions of

the kinds specified in sub-section 365(2) of

the Imperial Act known as the Income and

Corporation Taxes Act 1970, other than

payments that, in the opinion of the

Commissioner, are of a similar nature to

excepted payments; and

..."

6. Section 365 of the Income and Corporation Taxes Act 1970, which is referred to, is as follows:-

"(1) Income from wounds and disability pensions to which this

subsection applies shall be exempt from income tax and

shall not be reckoned in computing income for any of the

purposes of the Income Tax Acts.

(2) Subsection (1) above applies to -

(a) wounds pensions granted to members of the naval,

military or air forces of the Crown.

(b) retired pay of disabled officers granted on account

of medical unfitness attributable to or aggravated

by naval, military or air-force service.

(c) disablement or disability pensions granted to

members other than commissioned officers of the

naval, military or air forces of the Crown on

account of medical unfitness attributable to or

aggravated by naval, military or air-force service.

(d) disablement pensions granted to persons who have

been employed in the nursing services of any of the

naval, military or air forces of the Crown on

account of medical unfitness attributable to or

aggravated by naval, military or air-force service,

and

(e) injury and disablement pensions payable under any

scheme made under the Injuries in War

(Compensation) Act 1914, the Injuries in War

Compensation Act 1914 (Session 2), and the Injuries

in War (Compensation) Act 1915, or under any War

Risks Compensation Scheme for the Mercantile

Marine:

Provided that, where the amount of any such retired

pay or pension to which subsection (1) above

applies is not solely attributable to disablement

or disability, the relief conferred by the said

subsection (1) shall extend only to such part as is

certified by the Secretary of State for Social

Services, after consultation with the appropriate

Government Department, to be attributable to

disablement or disabililty."

7. The paragraph said to apply in this case is paragraph (e). It refers to a series of English Acts which it is necessary to examine in turn. The injuries dealt with in the Injuries in War (Compensation) Act 1914 as giving rise to pension rights are perhaps sufficiently described for present purposes in the short title:-

"An Act to provide for the grant of pensions and other

allowances to certain persons if injured whilst employed

in connection with warlike operations, and to their

dependants and for purposes connected therewith."

In my view the taxpayer was not within its terms. Section 1 of the Act provides for the framing of schemes. I do not have before me any scheme framed in accordance with the Act but their ambit is sufficiently indicated by the short title. Whether the Act had, or has, relation to the war of 1914-1918 only is not apparent, although I notice that orders in council under it were made as late as 1958 (Statutory Instrument No. 1266).

8. The second of the Acts mentioned had a short title as follows:-

"An Act to provide for the grant of Pensions and other

Allowances to certain persons if disabled whilst

employed abroad in connection with warlike operations

and to their dependants, and to amend The Injuries in

War (Compensation) Act 1914."

From the footnotes thereto it seems that schemes under this Act have not related simply to the war of 1914-1918 but apart from other matters the disability affecting the taxpayer was not incurred "whilst employed in connection with warlike operations".

9. The short title of the Injuries in War (Compensation) Act 1915 (which was not relied upon in argument) is in these terms:

"An Act to provide for the grant of pensions and other

allowances to certain persons in respect of disablement

due to causes arising out of the operations of the

present war, whilst they are employed afloat in

connexion with the telegraph and postal services, and to

their dependants, and for purposes connected therewith."

Clearly the pension payable to the taxpayer is not "of a kind" specified in this Act.

10. So far as concerns any "War Risks Compensation Scheme for the Mercantile Marine", the Acts to which I have been referred are the The Pensions (Naval, Army and Air Force and Mercantile Marine) Act 1939 and The Pensions (Mercantile Marine) Act 1942. These Acts, so far as relevant apply to the Mercantile Marine serving on British ships (1915 Act) and are not relevant.

11. The submission has been that a person, such as the deceased taxpayer, who was living in Europe and was persecuted by the conquering power, to the extent of being sent to a concentration camp, and bearing in mind the possibility that he was in some way engaged in "clandestine activities in circulating an underground newspaper" should be treated in some way as a person covered by the legislation in question. I do not think this argument is sound, having in mind the legislation pointed to, but in any event, I should say it proceeds on what to my mind is an incorrect interpretation of the word "kinds" in para (c) of s.23AD(3) of the Act. The reference in the paragraph is to payments of wounds and disability pensions "of the kinds...". The reference is to the kind of pension and not the kind of consideration which might have but did not lead to the grant of a pension. The use of the word "kinds" is probably little more than a shorthand way of summarising the effects of all the English legislation in question. One sympathises very much with the taxpayer, having in mind in particular the treatment he received at the hands of the authorities in Nazi Germany, but exemption from tax under the provisions I have so far mentioned does not extend to the pension he has received, as a result of that treatment, from his own Government.

12. It can be mentioned in this connection that s.23 was amended in 1959 to include para.(kc) to read:-

"Pensions, Annuities and Allowances paid or by way of

compensation by a State of the Federal Republic of

Germany under the laws of that Republic relating to

compensation to victims of nationalist socialist

persecution."

The taxpayer would have qualified from the time of this amendment had he received payments from a State of the Federal Republic of Germany and it may seem strange that a non-resident Dutchman receiving a pension because of similar persecution should not receive his pension exempt from income tax. There are several possible explanations of this which were mentioned in the course of argument, the correct reason apparently being related to the fact that the Federal Republic of Germany paid a large lump sum by way of reparation to Holland. However, in 1984 a further amendment was made by the insertion of para.(kca) under which pension payments made to the taxpayer from the date of application of that paragraph have been exempt, and the payments now in question would have been in this position. Unfortunately, the amendments came too late for the taxpayer. I might add that his notice of objection in this case was lodged in 1981 and did not come before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review until May 1985. The first of the requests for review was made in March 1981. A matter of relevance in this connection is of course that having died in the meantime his direct evidence as to the nature of his activities leading to his disabilities was not available. The Tribunal refers to the possible connection with the underground as being a matter of his widow's "belief".

13. The remaining ground is that the payments made under the Netherlands Benefit Payments to Victims of Persecution 1940-1945 were exempt from taxation by reason of the operation of s.10 of the Racial Discrimination Act 1975 upon s.23(kc) of the Act. Sub-sections (1) and (2) of s.10 are a follows:-

"10.(1) If, by reason of, or of a provision of, a law of the

Commonwealth or of a State or Territory, persons of

a particular race, colour or national or ethnic

origin do not enjoy a right that is enjoyed by

persons of another race, colour or national or

ethnic origin, or enjoy a right to a more limited

extent that persons of another race, colour or

national or ethnic origin, then, notwithstanding

anything in that law, persons of the first-mentioned

race, colour or national or ethnic origin shall, by

force of this section, enjoy that right to the same

extent as persons of that other race, colour or

national or ethnic origin.

10.(2) A reference to sub-section (1) to a right includes a

reference to a right of a kind referred to in

Article 5 of the Convention."

14. The law of the Commonwealth does not give the exemption in s.23(kc) to people of any particular race, colour, or national or ethnic origin. In this regard, it is entirely general. The source of the pensions to which the paragraph refers is a State of the Federal Republic of Germany, but payments might be made to a great variety of persons without any distinction other than a nexus with the States. Payments are not confined to people of any particular national origin, although some connection with the named States of the Federal Republic of Germany is required. The common connection is entitlement to payment under laws of the Federal Republic of Germany relating to compensation of victims of nationalist socialist persecution. I am unable to see scope for the application of s.10 of the Racial Discrimination Act 1975. Whether, in any event, the right to a tax free pension is a "right" in the relevant sense is a different question (cf Gehardy v. Brown [1985] HCA 11; (1985) 159 CLR 70).

15. As I have previously mentioned, amendments were later made to our law which made it plain that a person in the position of the deceased taxpayer was also entitled to have any pension received by him exempt from income.

16. The application must be dismissed.
