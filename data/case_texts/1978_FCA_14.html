Pearce v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1978] FCA 14; (1978) 37 FLR 296 (20 April 1978)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

PEARCE v. FEDERAL COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION [1914] HCA 90; (1978) 37 FLR 296

Courts - Income Tax

COURT

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

GENERAL DIVISION

Bowen C.J.(1), Brennan(2), Deane(3) JJ.

CATCHWORDS

Courts - Federal Court - Jurisdiction - Question reserved for consideration - Whether questions concerning matter with respect to which appeal would lie - Matter in which appeal would lie only with leave - Leave to appeal not granted - No matter with respect to which appeal would lie - No jurisdiction to consider question - Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth.), s. 26 (1).

Courts - Federal Court - Practice and procedure - Special case reserving questions for consideration - Based on disputed and incomplete evidence - Not based on factual situation - Whether must be based on facts found or agreed - Mere advisory opinion - Question not considered - Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth.), s. 26 (1) - Federal Court of Australia Rules, O. 6.

Income Tax - Appeals - Appeal from board of review to Supreme Court - Question of law reserved by Supreme Court for consideration by Federal Court - Circumstances in which Federal Court can or will consider question reserved - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.), s. 196 - Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth.), s. 26 (1). A decision of the Commissioner of Taxation disallowing a claim for deductions by the taxpayer was referred to the board of review under s. 188 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (as amended). The board confirmed the assessment and the taxpayer appealed under s. 196 to the Supreme Court of South Australia.

The appeal raised a question as to the construction of the repealed s. 82j (6) of the Act. The Supreme Court reserved the question of construction for consideration by the Federal Court pursuant to s. 26 of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 which provides by sub-s. (1) that: "A court from which appeals lie to the Court may state any case or reserve any question concerning a matter with respect to which such an appeal would lie from a judgment of the first-mentioned court for the consideration of the Court and the Court has jurisdiction to hear and determine the case or question."

Held: The Federal Court should not answer the question referred to it and the matter should be remitted to the Supreme Court because: (1) Per Brennan and Deane JJ., Bowen C.J. not deciding - The Supreme Court had no jurisdiction to refer the question to the Federal Court and the Federal Court had no jurisdiction to answer it, either under the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 or under the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976. Section 26 of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 conferred no jurisdiction in the circumstances because: (a) pursuant to s. 196 (5) of the Income Tax Assessment Act leave would have to be granted by the Federal Court before there could be an appeal to the Federal Court against any decision of the Supreme Court; (b) leave to appeal had not been granted by the Federal Court; (c) in the absence of leave to appeal, it could not be said that the question concerned "a matter with respect to which . . . an appeal would lie" in accordance with the requirements of s. 26 (1).

(2) Per curiam - Having regard to the form in which the question was reserved for consideration, it should not be considered. The question was reserved without reference to any factual context, no findings of fact having been made by the Supreme Court and no statement of facts having been agreed upon between the parties. Hence any answer of the Federal Court would constitute a mere "advisory opinion", the effectiveness of which would depend on facts yet to be determined.

Whether the provisions of s. 198 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 are exhaustive of the circumstances in which a judge of a Supreme Court hearing an appeal under that Act has power to state a case for the opinion of the Federal Court so as to exclude the operation of s. 26 of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976, referred to by Deane J.

HEARING

Adelaide, 1978, April 19-20. 20:4:1978

SPECIAL CASE.

In the course of an appeal by the taxpayer to the Supreme Court of South Australia (Sangster J.) against a decision of the board of review, the Supreme Court reserved a question of law for consideration by the Federal Court in the terms set forth in the judgment of Bowen C.J. The other material circumstances of the case also appear from that judgment.

M. F. O'Loughlin and R. D. Lawson, for the appellant.

D. J. Bleby, for the respondent.

Solicitors for the appellant: Fisher, Jeffries & Co.

Solicitor for the respondent: Alan R. Neaves (Commonwealth Crown

Solicitor).

J. R. P. LEWISOHN

DECISION

APRIL 20.

The following oral judgments were delivered.

BOWEN C.J. This is a question referred to us by the Supreme Court of South
Australia in a matter which is before it on appeal from a board of review. (at p297)

2. A taxpayer whose objection has been disallowed by the Commissioner of Taxation has two courses open to him if he wishes to contest the disallowance. He may take his case either to the board of review, or may require that his objection be treated as an appeal and be forwarded to the Supreme Court of a State (Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s. 187). (at p297)

3. Where he appeals direct to the Supreme Court, that court is empowered by s. 198 to state a case in writing for the opinion of this Court upon a question of law arising on the appeal. Where the matter is heard in the first instance by a Supreme Court there is an appeal as of right to this Court (s. 200). (at p297)

4. Where he appeals to the board of review the board will hear and determine the matter. There is then a right of appeal to the Supreme Court from a decision of the board that involves a question of law (s. 196). (at p297)

5. When a matter comes before the Supreme Court in those circumstances, although it is described as an appeal, it is in truth a hearing at first instance in the court for the first time. It has to be determined on the evidence presented to the court, which may or may not be the same as the evidence presented to the board. In such cases the Income Tax Assessment Act makes no provision for a stated case to this Court on a question of law. Furthermore, except as provided in s. 196 (5) an appeal does not lie from the decision of the Supreme Court. Section 196 (5) provides that the commissioner or the taxpayer may appeal to this Court by leave. (at p298)

6. I turn now to s. 26 (1) of the Federal Court of Australia Act. This provides: "A court from which appeals lie to the Court may state any case or reserve any question concerning a matter with respect to which such an appeal would lie from a judgment of the first-mentioned court for the consideration of the Court and the Court has jurisdiction to hear and determine the case or question." (at p298)

7. The question immediately arises whether this is a matter with respect to which an appeal would lie from a judgment of the Supreme Court of South Australia. There is a need for leave to be obtained before an appeal may be brought. Such leave may only be sought after a decision in the Supreme Court. The initial question, therefore, arises whether in a case where a Supreme Court is hearing a matter on appeal from a board of review this Court may entertain a question reserved. I have some doubt on this question but in view of what I proceed to say on another aspect of this reference, I prefer to express no concluded opinion on the point. (at p298)

8. I turn to the actual question which is referred to us in what is called the special case. It is as follows: "The question reserved for the consideration of this Honourable Court is: 'Whether on the true construction of s. 82J (6) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936-1973 the expression "expenses necessarily incurred by the taxpayer for or in connexion with full-time education at a school, college or university or from a tutor" therein contained means: (a) Expenses essentially, compulsorily or inevitably incurred by the taxpayer for or in connexion with such education; or (b) expenses incurred by the taxpayer which are clearly appropriate or adapted for, or in connexion with such education; or (c) some other, and what, meaning?'" This question, as framed, seeks from us, in effect, a dissertation upon s. 82 (J) without reference to any facts. No evidence has yet been tendered in the Supreme Court and no facts found or agreed either finally or provisionally have been put before us. (at p298)

9. Indeed, while the parties indicated during the hearing before us that they would be prepared to tender in the Supreme Court the transcript of the evidence taken before the board of review. counsel for the Commissioner of Taxation made it clear that he did not accept the findings of the board and proposed to call supplementary evidence should the matter proceed in the Supreme Court. (at p299)

10. In these circumstances we are, as it were, invited to express our views on the connotation of the words used in s. 82J without any factual situation to which our decision will apply. This appears to me to be tantamount to seeking an advisory interpretation of the section. I call attention to the terms of our rules in O. 6 which require even a question reserved to be stated in the form of a special case setting out the facts giving rise to the question. (at p299)

11. The case before us has been prepared in the form of a special case in pursuance of that Order and it raises a question, but the question is not based on any factual situation stated in the case. One purpose of O. 6 in our rules is to have the factual situation stated which gives rise to the question, so that the court will be dealing with a question the answer to which will take effect in the proceedings. It is desirable, therefore, that O. 6 should be faithfully observed. (at p299)

12. I do not wish to suggest the court would not be desirous of assisting parties in every way or to suggest the court would always technically insist upon performance of the letter of the rules. In an appropriate case it may well be prepared to dispense with compliance with particular rules if justice demands it. I merely draw attention to one of the purposes of O. 6 and suggest that if it had been fully observed perhaps this difficulty may not have arisen. (at p299)

13. In the circumstances as they are it is my opinion that the court should not answer the question that has been referred to it. I think there is no need to express a view on some of the other matters that were raised before us in argument. In my opinion, the matter should be remitted to the Supreme Court and we should make no order as to costs. (at p299)

BRENNAN J. I agree. Sangster J. sat to hear an appeal in the Supreme Court of South Australia from a decision of a board of review pursuant to s. 196 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. The power of the Supreme Court under the Income Tax Assessment Act to state a case for the opinion of this Court is conferred by s. 198. The power so conferred may be exercised only when the appeal to the Supreme Court is instituted under s. 197. It does not arise when the appeal to the Supreme Court is instituted under s. 196. (at p299)

2. However, under s. 26 of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976, there is a general power to reserve a question of law for the opinion of this Court conferred upon a court from which an appeal lies to this Court, but only concerning a matter with respect to which an appeal lies to this Court from a judgment of the court hearing the matter. (at p299)

3. There is no appeal from the Supreme Court of South Australia when it is hearing a matter under s. 196 of the Income Tax Assessment Act (see sub-s. (4) of that section), except in those cases where leave has been granted. The effect of this subsection is to ensure that, except in cases where it is appropriate to grant leave, the Supreme Court is the final court for the determination of matters which arise under s. 196. No leave has, of course, been granted in this case. It follows that this Court has, in my view, no jurisdiction to determine the question reserved. (at p300)

4. The question was reserved by Sangster J. at the request of the appellant before him before evidence was received or argument heard. Necessarily the case contains no finding by his Honour as to any facts to which the question reserved could relate. The question reserved would seek merely an opinion, the effectiveness of which would depend upon the facts that remain to be determined in the future. Courts do not give opinions of that kind (see Swift Australian Co. (Pty.) Ltd. v. South British Insurance Co. Ltd. [1970] VicRp 47; (1970) VR 368 ). There is a particular difficulty when the question reserved seeks in substance a mere judicial exegesis of a statutory provision unrelated to any facts. The court is then invited not to apply the statute but to devise precise synonyms for the statutory language. This is a course on which this Court ought not to embark. Had there been power to reserve the question raised in the stated case we should yet have been constrained to refuse an answer. I agree in the order proposed. (at p300)

DEANE J. I agree. It is arguable that, in the context of the appellate code appearing in Divn 2 of Pt V of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, the provisions of s. 198 are exhaustive of the circumstances in which a Supreme Court judge, hearing an appeal (or a reference of a question of law) under that Act, has power to state a case for the opinion of this Court. If this view be correct the provisions of s. 26 of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 are not applicable in respect of any question concerning such an appeal (or reference). (at p300)

2. It is not, however, necessary, for present purposes, that I express any view on that question. Even assuming that the provisions of s. 26 of the Federal Court of Australia Act are applicable, when otherwise appropriate, in respect of an appeal or reference under the Income Tax Assessment Act, the question referred in the present matter does not, in the view I take, concern a matter with respect to which an appeal would lie, in the relevant sense, from a judgment of the Supreme Court. (at p300)

3. At the time when the Supreme Court delivers judgment in the present matter, no appeal will lie to this Court. That is the result of the express words of s. 196 (4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. The fact that this Court may subsequently grant leave to appeal does not alter the fact that, unless and until such leave is granted by this Court, the present matter cannot properly, for the purposes of s. 26 of the Federal Court of Australia Act, be regarded as one in which an appeal would lie from a judgment of the Supreme Court. (at p301)

4. In the result, I am of the view that the learned judge of the Supreme Court lacked jurisdiction to refer the question to this Court and that this Court lacks jurisdiction to answer it. It is unnecessary for me to deal with the other matters that have been discussed in the course of the helpful submissions of counsel who have appeared before us. However, I would indicate that I agree with the remarks of the Cheif Judge and Brennan J. as to the form of the question which was reserved for our consideration. (at p301)

5. The matter should be remitted to the Supreme Court. There should be no order as to the costs of either party in this Court. (at p301)

ORDER

Orders accordingly.
