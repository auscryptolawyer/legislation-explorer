And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal Mr J. Handley, Senior Member

Date 24 January 1997

Place Melbourne

Decision The decision under review is affirmed.



........Sgd. Mr J. Handley........

Senior Member



CATCHWORDS

Taxation - Whether losses incurred in a jointly owned property can be claimed in full by one joint owner only - whether joint owners in a partnership.

Income Tax Assessment Act s.6, s.51

REASONS FOR DECISION

24 January 1997 Mr J. Handley, Senior Member



  1. The applicant applied to review a decision dated 29 August 1996 with respect to the assessment of his income for the year ended 30 June 1996.
  2. The issue to be determined was whether the applicant was entitled to claim totally the losses resulting from real property jointly owned with his wife at Mansfield in Victoria or whether those losses were deductible from his declared income as to 50% only.
  3. Upon the hearing the applicant was represented by his wife. Mr O’Neill represented the respondent.

THE FACTS

  1. On 12 May 1995 the Commonwealth Bank approved a loan to the applicant and his wife for the purchase of a home at Mansfield. The applicant and his wife were then living in a jointly owned home in Patterson Lakes. It was intended that they would ultimately move to live in the Mansfield property and the Patterson Lakes property would be sold. It was expected that the Patterson Lakes home would be sold rapidly. It was not. It was only sold recently and in the intervening period of 2 years mortgage payments and interest have been made to the Commonwealth Bank.
  2. The property at Mansfield was purchased jointly and has been registered as a jointly owned property. Bank repayments and interest have been made from an account jointly held between the applicant and his wife. The applicant’s salary from his employment is also paid into that account. The applicant’s wife does not work. Rental paid over the property is also paid into that joint account.
  3. Mrs Finnis said that the Mansfield property was intended for purchase as a domestic dwelling only. It was never intended that it be purchased as an investment or as a business. She said she was advised by her solicitor to register it in joint names having regard to probate and family law considerations.
  4. Mrs Finnis also said that the relevant bank account referred to above was jointly owned so as to give her access to it for the purposes of obtaining monies for day to day domestic expenses.
  5. The bank interest and expenses making up the losses were brought into account when the 1996 return was lodged, as also was the rental income received. The applicant has claimed as a deduction the sum of $4,751. The losses and rental income are set out as follows-

$

Bank Interest 3262

other expenses 4735

7997

Rental Income 3246

Amount claimed 4751

THE RESPONDENT

  1. Mr O’Neill submitted that the applicant and his wife should be considered as being in a “partnership” for the purposes of this application. Mr O’Neill relied on section 6(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act (“the Act”) where “partnership” was defined as-
“partnership means an association of persons carrying on business as partners or in receipt of income jointly, but does not include a company”.
  1. Whilst acknowledging that the applicant and his wife were not “partners” as was intended by the concept of a partnership in general law (and to this extent he relied on s.5 of the Victorian Partnership Act where “partnership” was defined as a relationship existing between persons carrying on a business in common with a view of profit) the renting of a jointly owned mortgaged property could not be said to be a business with a view to profit. Accordingly, he submitted that profits and losses in the circumstances of the applicant and his wife must be shared equally.
  2. Mr O’Neill relied on the decision of Beaumont J in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v McDonald reported at 87 ATC 4542. In that application His Honour heard an appeal against a decision of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal concerning a written arrangement entered into between a husband and wife with respect to the income and losses from jointly owned properties purchased with the intention of earing income. The agreement provided that profits would be distributed by 75% to the wife and 25% to the husband and that the husband would absorb all losses.
  3. His Honour, in discussing the definition of partnership under s.6(1) of the Act and concluding that there was in fact no partnership between the taxpayer and his wife in that case insofar as the general law was concerned, said that s.51 of the Act did not permit deductions merely by reason of the agreement that had been struck between the parties. His Honour said that payments made under such an agreement were not losses incurred in gaining or producing assessable income or necessarily incurred in carrying on a business. His Honour concluded that the liability as agreed between the parties was of a domestic arrangement only and that the losses were of a private or domestic nature and were excluded by s.51(1).

CONCLUSION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

  1. The decision under review must be affirmed. There is no merit in the argument submitted on behalf of the taxpayer in this case.
  2. The property was jointly owned. The mortgage and the liability for repayments was jointly held. The bank account into which rental payments were paid, being the same account from which mortgage repayments were made was jointly owned. The applicant and his wife apparently agreed that rental payments over the Mansfield property would be paid to the husband alone yet that would appear to have been some arrangement made to bolster the attempt to permit the husband to deduct losses in full.
  3. An arrangement permitting the husband to be paid rent in full (yet ultimately being deposited in the joint account) does not strengthen the applicant’s case and does not defeat the operation of the concept of “partnership” as found at s.6(1).
  4. I adopt the reasoning of Beaumont J in McDonald’s case in full.
  5. The losses incurred in this application were purely of a domestic nature only with respect to a property over which it was intended that the applicant and his wife would ultimately live when their Patterson Lakes home was sold.
  6. The purchase of the Mansfield property and the subsequent receipt of income can in no way be characterised as being the basis of a business established with the objective of making profit. The income and the losses were of a domestic or private nature only.
  7. Mrs Finnis is as much entitled to the Mansfield property as her husband and just as she would be entitled to equal division of rental income from that property she is also equally responsible for the debts of the property.
  8. Accordingly it follows that Mr Finnis is entitled only to deduct 50% of losses incurred with respect to the Mansfield property and not 100% as he has to date asserted.
