Last Updated: 30 April 2009





Administrative

Appeals

Tribunal



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION NO 11769

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No VT96/361

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal Mr. B.H. Pascoe, Senior Member

Date 11 April 1997

Place Melbourne

Decision Having heard the applicant and the respondent, the Tribunal does not extend the time for the making by the applicant of an application for review of the decision.



(Sgd. BH PASCOE)

Senior Member



CATCHWORDS

EXTENSION OF TIME - income tax - application for review of objection decision - application lodged three years after time required - whether applicant aware of decision - whether acceptable explanation for delay - whether prejudice to respondent.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s.29

Taxation Administration Act 1953

REASONS FOR DECISION

11 April 1997 Mr. B.H. Pascoe, Senior Member



  1. This is an application for an extension of time in which to lodge an application for review of an objection decision by the respondent in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1986.
  2. The respondent had issued an amended assessment based on the applicant's income for that year on 17 May 1989 following an audit of the applicant's affairs. An objection to that amended assessment was lodged on 10 July 1989 although no detailed grounds were included in the letter of objection. By letter of 21 July 1993 the respondent disallowed the objection. An application for review of this decision was lodged with the Tribunal on 20 December 1996 by letter dated 17 December 1996 which also sought from the Tribunal an extension of time in which to lodge such application.
  3. As the assessment to which the objection relates was notified in May 1989 the former provisions of section 187, section 188 and section 188B of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") apply. Although nothing seems to turn on the need to refer to these provisions rather than the provisions of the present legislation, it is appropriate to set them out. As far as relevant to this matter these provisions state:
"187 A taxpayer who is dissatisfied with a decision under section 186 on an objection by the taxpayer may, within 60 days after service on the taxpayer of notice of the decision, lodge with the Commission, in writing, either -

(a) a request to refer the decision to the Tribunal; or

(b) a request to refer the decision to the Federal Court.

188(1) ...

188(2) Where the period for the lodgment by the taxpayer of a request under section 187 has ended, the taxpayer may, notwithstanding that the period has ended, send the request to the Commissioner together with an application in writing asking that the request be treated as having been duly lodged.

188(3) An application under sub-section (1) or (2) shall state fully and in detail the circumstances concerning, and the reasons for, the failure by the taxpayer to lodge the objection or request as required by this Act.

188B(1) Where the Commissioner receives an application under sub-section 188(2), the Commissioner shall, as soon as practicable -


(a) if the application relates to a request to refer a decision to the Tribunal - send the application to the Tribunal; or

(b) ...

188B(2) The sending of an application to the Tribunal under paragraph (1)(a) shall, for the purposes of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, be deemed to constitute the making by the taxpayer concerned of an application to the Tribunal to extend the time within which the request may be lodged with the Commissioner.

188B(3) ...

188B(4) The Tribunal or the Federal Court, as the case may be, may grant or refuse the application.

188B(5) Where an application under sub-section 188(2) has been granted, the taxpayer shall, for the purposes of this Part, be treated as having duly lodged the request to which the application relates."

In this case the application for an extension of time in which to lodge the request for reference to the Tribunal was lodged direct with the Tribunal rather than with the Commissioner. However, the ultimate effect is the same, as the decision whether or not to grant the application rests with the Tribunal. For assessments notified after 1 March 1992, section 29 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 as modified by section 14ZZC of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 applies involving direct lodgement of both applications for review of objection decisions and applications for extension of time with the Tribunal.

  1. At the hearing the applicant company was represented by its current accountant and tax agent and the respondent represented by an officer of the respondent. No evidence was called other than copies of correspondence with the applicant from the previous tax agent tendered by the respondent.
  2. For the applicant it was submitted that the amended assessment resulted from an audit by the respondent of the applicant's affairs for the year ended 30 June 1986 and that the applicant's previous tax agent had never informed the applicant of such audit or the result. It was said that the applicant became aware of the liability when the current tax agent enquired from the respondent the details of outstanding tax showing on a notice of assessment for the year ended 30 June 1994. The current tax agent's first involvement with the applicant was the preparation of a return of income for the year ended 30 June 1995. Upon obtaining details of the audit and the calculations of the amended assessment for the year ended 30 June 1986, the applicant considered that errors had been made such as the inclusion as income of proceeds of sale of a director's residence. It was said that the applicant had not had an opportunity to make submissions in relation to such alleged errors and that it was appropriate that such an opportunity be now provided.
  3. For the respondent it was submitted that the further time for lodgement of an application for review of the objection decision should not be granted. It was said that the year of income in question is now more than ten years ago and that relevant documents may no longer exist, officers of the respondent who dealt with the audit are no longer available or unable to recall the matter and the granting of the request would cause prejudice to the respondent. It was argued further that the applicant had been given every opportunity between May 1989 and July 1993 to provide details and make any submissions. The documents tendered were said to demonstrate that the applicant had been advised of the audit and its results including the disallowance of the objection. An additional indication of the knowledge of the applicant of the audit was said to be the authorisation by the applicant for the respondent to access Health Insurance Commission details of amounts paid to the applicant. The respondent argued that the delay of three years from the required time for lodgement of the application for review was excessive and unreasonable. It was further argued that the delay of some twelve months after the appointment of the new tax agent had not been explained acceptably.
  4. At the hearing, the applicant's representative requested and was granted time to consult the director of the applicant and make a written response relating to the copies of correspondence from the previous tax agent produced by the respondent. Subsequently a letter from the director was provided denying that he had ever before seen such documents. However, the letter did acknowledge that the director knew that the audit had taken place because of the bills from the previous agent citing the audit. The letter reiterated that the outstanding tax arising from the amended assessment had only come to the director's attention after the current tax agent made enquiries in late November 1995.
  5. Unfortunately, the question of whether the applicant was aware of the result of the audit and the objection or not can be considered only on the untested written statements by the previous tax agent and the director of the applicant. Neither were called to give evidence. A further unsatisfactory aspect of this matter is the apparent failure of the respondent to pursue recovery of the tax outstanding since 1989 and, according to the respondent's representative, still outstanding at the date of the hearing and which amounted to $32,738.98. Copies of final notices requiring payment addressed to the applicant at the address for service, the previous tax agent, were produced dated July 1989 but the recovery action threatened did not appear to have taken place. If such action had been taken it would have been clear that the applicant was aware of the liability.
  6. It is difficult to accept that the applicant was not aware of the audit and its consequences. The director accepted that he was aware of an audit but denied knowledge of the result. The previous tax agent provided a statutory declaration that the director acting for the applicant had been kept fully informed.
  7. In any event, it is not necessary, in my view, to establish with any certainty which of the competing statements is correct. Whether the applicant has provided an acceptable explanation for the delay in lodging the application is one factor in considering whether further time should be granted. It is sufficient to say on this point that the amended assessment in dispute was issued by the respondent over seven years ago, the applicant was represented during the course of the audit and consideration of the objection, no sworn evidence and submission to cross-examination was available from the applicant and the applicant took twelve months from the stated date of becoming aware of the outstanding tax before taking any formal steps to seek review of the objection decision. All of these facts lead me to the view that the applicant has not provided an acceptable explanation for the delay.
  8. The more relevant factor indicating that further time should not be granted is the clear prejudice to the respondent if the Tribunal agreed to the applicant's request. The fact that the application was lodged more than three years after the time provided for in section 187 with no indication, at least prior to July 1996, that the applicant was in any way dissatisfied with the objection decision is, of itself, sufficient to find that the respondent would suffer prejudice. When that is added to the fact that the amended assessment was issued over seven years ago and the year of income in question is more than ten years ago with the obvious difficulty of obtaining documents and recalling memories of the persons then involved, it is clear that the respondent would be placed in a very difficult position. It should be said, also, that the length of time which has elapsed would place difficulties in the way of the applicant discharging the onus of proof in disputing the assessment where factual matters will require evidence.
  9. While it may be said that the applicant could be prejudiced by not granting further time if the submission that the applicant was not adequately represented at the time of the audit and subsequent negotiation is accepted, it may well be that the applicant has other civil remedies available.
  10. Little was put to the Tribunal as to the likely merits of the applicant's case if further time was granted. The representative of the respondent submitted that arguments were put to the auditor at the conclusion of the audit and the auditor had noted that these had not been accepted. The Tribunal can make no decision as to whether the applicant's case would have merit other than the observation that, if the assertions that receipts have been incorrectly included as income and that the same income has been included in two income years could be demonstrated the case would have merit. Nevertheless the time which has elapsed will clearly make the task of resolving such assertions of fact very difficult.
  11. In passing, it should be mentioned that the respondent appears to have treated the applicant very favourably in accepting the letter of 10 July 1989 as an objection. The letter from the applicant's tax agent referred to the notice of amended assessment and a notification of a sufficient distribution of the applicant's income of the year ended 30 June 1984 and stated:

"We would like to clarify the above two matters regarding "the applicant", copies enclosed.



1. The taxable income on the amended assessment 1986 has been increased to $64,966, an increase of $35,927 over the original assessment made for 1986. Could you please forward details of the increased amount, as we believe an error has occurred resulting in an over assessment of tax.



2. Sufficient distribution will be paid out as a dividend and amended income tax reforms will be forwarded for the year ended 1983/84."

It may well be argued that this wording did not constitute an objection pursuant to the then section 185 of the Act.

  1. The other factor which should be mentioned is that the further time sought by the applicant may be seen as a means of circumventing the provisions of section 170 of the Act as they applied to this matter. The respondent is unable to further amend the assessment issued in May 1989 except as a result of a review of an objection decision as the three year limit imposed by section 170 has well expired. In considering questions of granting further time it is relevant to have regard to the intention of the legislature to limit the time before finality of assessments is reached. In my view there are no special circumstances of this case which justify a further period in which the question of reopening the 1986 year can be considered.
  2. Given the period of time that has elapsed, the potential prejudice to the respondent and the explanation for delay proffered by the applicant, the extension of time sought in which to apply for review of the objection decision should not be granted.
