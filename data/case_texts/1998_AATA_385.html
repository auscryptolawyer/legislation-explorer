Ben and Denese Vaughan and Official Trustee in Bankruptcy [1998] AATA 385 (29 May 1998)

Last Updated: 4 June 1998

Administrative

Appeals

Tribunal

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) Nos T97/98 and T97/99

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re Ben Vaughan and Denese Elizabeth Frances Vaughan

Applicant

And Official Trustee in Bankruptcy

Respondent

Decision No 12935

DECISION

Tribunal Mr C P Webster (Senior Member)

Date 29 May 1998

Place Hobart

Decision The Tribunal affirms the decisions of the Trustee in Bankruptcy of 27 June 1997 objecting to the automatic discharge from bankruptcy of Ben Vaughan and Denese Elizabeth Vaughan pursuant to s.149B of the Bankruptcy Act 1966.

.......... ...........................

Senior Member

CATCHWORDS

Bankrupty - objection to discharge from bankrupty - adequacy of reasons for objection to discharge from bankruptcy.

Bankrupty Act 1966 - ss.149B and 149D

REASONS FOR DECISION

29 May 1998 Mr C P Webster (Senior Member)

Introduction

1. On 27 June 1997 the delegate of the Official Trustee in Bankruptcy ("the Trustee") objected to the automatic discharge from bankruptcy of both Ben Vaughan and Denese Elizabeth Frances Vaughan ("the applicants"). The objection by the Trustee to the discharge of the applicants from bankruptcy was on the basis of ss.149(1)(d) and 149(1)(n) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 ("the Act"). On 21 July 1997 both applicants applied to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal ("the Tribunal") for the review of the decisions affecting each of them.

2. The Tribunal heard the two reviews on 3, 4 and 5 March 1998. Both reviews were heard together and it is convenient that both reviews be considered together. Unless otherwise stated the term "the review" will refer to both reviews.

The Issues

3. The relevant law is contained in ss.149B and 149D of the Act is as follows:-

"Objection to discharge

(1) Subject to the following provisions of this Subdivision, at any time before a bankrupt is discharged from bankruptcy under section 149, the trustee may file with the Official Receiver a written notice of objection to the discharge, or the Official Receiver may file such a notice on the Official Receiver's own initiative.

(2) The trustee of a bankrupt's estate must file a notice of objection to the discharge if the trustee believes:

(a) that doing so will help make the bankrupt discharge a duty that the bankrupt has not discharged; and

(b) that there is no other way for the trustee to induce the bankrupt to discharge any duties that the bankrupt has not discharged."

The relevant parts of s.149D states:-

"(1) The grounds of objection that may be set out in a notice of objection are as follows:-

....

(d) the bankrupt, when requested in writing by the trustee to provide written information about the bankrupt's property, income or expected income, failed to comply with the request;

....

(n) the bankrupt failed, whether intentionally or not, to disclose to the trustee the bankrupt's beneficial interest in any property."

4. The notices of objection to the discharge from bankruptcy of each of the applicants was similar.

5. Both notices listed the following matters of complaint against each of the applicants namely:-

(a) That both applicants owned a sapphire pendant the whereabouts of which the applicants refused to reveal to the trustee when requested in writing to do so.

(b) That both applicants owned antique furniture the whereabouts of which the applicants had refused to reveal to the trustee when requested in writing to do so.

(c) That both applicants had failed to disclose their interest in a property at Orford owned by the "East Coast Trust".

(d) In the case of Mr. Ben Vaughan there was an additional ground for the objection from the trustee in bankruptcy to discharge from bankruptcy namely that he failed to disclose his interest in vintage wine.

6. The issues to be determined in this review are:-

(a) Whether either of the applicants owned a sapphire pendant or antique furniture and failed to disclose their whereabouts when requested to do so in writing.

(b) Whether the applicants or either of them failed to disclose their interest in land situated at Orford in Tasmania when requested in writing to do so.

(c) Whether Mr. Ben Vaughan owned antique wine, the ownership of which he failed to disclose when requested in writing to do so.

The Evidence

The Land:

7. The respondent alleges that the applicants deliberately failed to disclose to the trustee their joint ownership of one third interest in land at Orford.

8. It is common ground that the applicants did own land at Orford, being a one third interest (between the applicants) in land registered as being owned by DOMA Pty. Ltd. It is also not in dispute that (a) apart from the applicants' home in Launceston this was their only interest in real estate; (b) that the applicants failed to disclose their interest in this land in their statement of affairs; (c) and at the time the applicants completed their statement of affairs that land was for sale; (d) that the value of a block of land of a similar type as that owned by the applicants was valued at approximately $45,000.

9. Mr. Vaughan on behalf of the applicants agreed that the applicants had inadvertently failed to disclose in their statement of affairs their interest in their land at Orford, but says that the inadvertence arose because he was told by a servant of the trustee in bankruptcy words to the effect that "they were to complete their statements to the best of the their ability and to let him know later if there were any changes".

10. Mr. Vaughan gave evidence as to that discussion. His wife did not give any evidence at the hearing and there is no direct evidence as to her state of mind when completing her statement of affairs and why she did not disclose her interest in property at Orford.

11. Mr. Vaughan also gave evidence that he believed that the land at Orford was held in trust for the benefit of his son and for the education of his.

12. Mr. Vaughan argues that as soon as he and his wife realised their mistake in not including their interest in the Orford land in their statement of affairs, they amended their statement of affairs without any prompting by the respondent. The respondent on the other hand argues that the evidence indicates that disclosure by the applicants of their interest in the land came on 6 July 1994, two days after the trustee wrote to the applicants' solicitor requesting details about the land.

The Wine

13. There is no dispute that Mr. Vaughan owned a quantity of wine.

14. The trustee says that the existence of the wine was not disclosed. Mr. Vaughan says that it was disclosed and that the item "wine library" in his statement of affairs refers to that wine.

15. it is not disputed that (a) the term "wine library" is included with other items under the item "tools of trade" to which Mr. Vaughan attributed a value of $1,500; (b) that Mr. Vaughan owned 115 bottles of wine which constituted his "wine library"; (c) that Mr. Vaughan also owned a large quantity of books about wine; and (d) that in addition to the 115 bottles of wine referred to above, Mr. Vaughan owned another 76 bottles of wine which he did not disclose at all in his statement of affairs.

The Pendant

16. It is common ground that neither of the applicants disclosed in their statement of affairs their interest in a sapphire pendant. It is also common ground that: (a) that the pendant was commissioned by the applicants when the applicants personally saw the jeweller; (b) that the cheque for partial payment of the pendant was paid by the applicants; (c) that the sapphire is Mrs. Vaughan's birthstone; (d) that the sapphire pendant was in the possession of the applicants; (e) that the applicants insured the pendant; (f) that the pendant was insured for the sum of $5,000; and (g) that the pendant could not be found amongst the assets of the estate of Mrs. Mather (the mother of Mrs. Vaughan) by her executrix upon the death of Mrs. Mather.

17. The evidence by Mr. Vaughan was that at all times the pendant was owned by his mother-in-law (Mrs. Mather), who loaned it to her daughter and that the pendant was lost during the hospitalisation of Mrs. Mather.

18. The respondent argued that the undisputed evidence leads to the conclusion that the applicants own the pendant. In addition to the undisputed evidence the respondent relies on the evidence of two officers of the respondent who state that following the bankruptcy of the applicants they asked Mr. Vaughan the whereabouts of the pendant and were told words to the effect "it's with my wife".

The Furniture

19. It is common ground that (a) the applicants had been in possession of a quantity of antique furniture at their homes in Launceston and Hobart for approximately 20 years prior to their bankruptcy; (b) that the furniture was insured by the applicants; (c) that the applicants did not disclose in their statement of financial affairs any interest in that furniture; (f) that the furniture is no longer in the possession of the applicants.

20. The evidence of Mr. Vaughan was that the furniture belonged to his mother-in-law and was left by her upon her death to her son who stored it at a place, unknown to Mr. Vaughan, in Hobart. The trustee disputes Mr. Vaughan's evidence and in addition to the undisputed evidence above relies on the undisputed evidence of the executrix of the Will of Mrs. Mather which disputes the assertion that Mrs. Mather's son came to Tasmania and stored the furniture and that she as executrix had no knowledge of any furniture owned by the deceased being in the possession of the applicants.

Findings of Fact

21. Mr. Vaughan did not impress the Tribunal as being a credible witness. He did not appear frank in his evidence, but instead appeared more interested as to where the line of cross-examination of him was leading than to the accuracy of his answers.

22. At one stage during his cross-examination, the following exchange took place between the Tribunal and Mr. Vaughan:

"The Tribunal: that was not a hard question was it?

Mr. Vaughan: no, but I need to know what he's asking, you see ...

The Tribunal: no, you are thinking about - you are trying to think of what reason he is asking it?

Mr. Vaughan: ... I - yes."

Later Mr. Vaughan states:-

"Mr. Vaughan: I'm not trying to be evasive, but I'm aware of Mr. Wood's training and that, you know, there may be reasons why he is, you know, leading me down a garden path."

23. The Tribunal could not accept Mr. Vaughan's evidence unless it was corroborated.

24. Mrs. Vaughan did not appear at the hearing. She gave no evidence on oath. The Tribunal had no explanation from Mrs. Vaughan as to her failure to give evidence.

25. Mr. Vaughan stated that his wife was unable to give evidence as she worked part-time in a "sandwich bar" and would lose money if she attended the hearing. The Tribunal notes from its own knowledge that the sandwich bar to which Mr. Vaughan referred is located less than 5 minutes walk from the Tribunal's hearing room.

26. The Tribunal offered, through Mr. Vaughan, to hear evidence from Mrs. Vaughan at a time during which she did not work, even sitting after normal business hours if necessary. Mrs. Vaughan did not avail herself of the Tribunal's offer.

27. The Tribunal can only infer from Mrs. Vaughan's failure to give oral evidence that her evidence would not add anything to her case or that of her husband.

28. The applicants case therefore rests primarily upon the evidence of Mr. Vaughan whose uncorroborated evidence is not accepted by the Tribunal.

29. The Tribunal finds that the applicants deliberately failed to disclose their interest in land at Orford in their statement of affairs.

30. The Tribunal considers it unlikely that Mr. and Mrs. Vaughan would have inadvertently failed to include their interest in the Orford land in their statement of financial affairs.

31. The Tribunal considers that it is extremely unlikely that the applicants forgot the existence of their interest in the land as (a) it was a significant portion of their assets; (b) it was 1 of only 2 interests in land; and (c) the land was for sale at the time of their bankruptcy and therefore likely to be brought to their recent attention.

32. In the case of Mrs. Vaughan, there is no explanation as to why she did not disclose her interest. The Tribunal does not accept Mr. Vaughan's explanation as to why he did not disclose his interest.

33. It is the view of the Tribunal that the applicants deliberately failed to disclose their interest knowing that the land was registered in another name and only disclosed their interest as a result of the inquiry of the trustee to their solicitor as a result of the applicants becoming aware of the trustee's interest.

34. The Tribunal finds that Mr. Vaughan deliberately did not disclose to the trustee his interest in approximately 190 bottles of wine.

35. It is the Tribunal's view that Mr. Vaughan did not intend to disclose his interest in the bulk of his wine by including it under the item "wine library" which was under the general heading "tools of trade".

36. Under the heading "tools of trade" Mr. Vaughan listed desk, chair, filing cabinet, car phone, and wine library. To this he attributed a total value of $1,500. This does not sit well with his evidence that the 117 bottles of wine used for his business was valued at at least $1,500. The Tribunal does not accept his evidence that $1,500 was only half the value of the assets and that his wife was a half owner as this is inconsistent with how the applicants valued other items in their statement of affairs.

37. The Tribunal accepts the submission of the respondent's counsel that the term "wine library" is more likely to refer to the significant library of books which Mr. Vaughan owned on the topic of wines.

38. The Tribunal does not accept Mr. Vaughan's explanation as to why he did not disclose the existence of the other bottles (i.e. the 76 bottles of wine that Mr. Vaughan does not allege was part of the "library").

39. The Tribunal finds at all material times the applicants were joint owners of a sapphire pendant, the whereabouts of which they have deliberately failed to disclose to the trustee in bankruptcy when requested to do so in writing.

40. The Tribunal considers that the undisputed facts, namely that the pendant was commissioned by the applicants; paid for by cheque by the applicants; insured by the applicants; worn by Vaughan when she wished; was made of Mrs. Vaughan's birthstone; must lead the Tribunal to consider that Mr. and Mrs. Vaughan owned the pendant.

41. The Tribunal accepts the evidence adduced by the respondent that Mr. Vaughan advised the officers of the trustee that the pendant was in the possession of wife.

42. It follows from the acceptance of that evidence that the Tribunal does not accept Mr. Vaughan's evidence that the pendant belonged to his mother-in-law and was lost during her hospitalisation on following her death.

43. The Tribunal finds that the applicant owned antique furniture, the whereabouts of which they have deliberately failed to disclose to the trustee.

44. The Tribunal reaches the conclusion that the applicants owned the furniture from the length of time the furniture was in the applicants' possession, that is over 20 years; that the furniture was used by the applicants for their use as if it was their own furniture; the applicants insured the furniture; and the inherent unlikelihood that Mrs. Mather would acquire substantial furniture when she lived at all relevant times in a unit. (The Tribunal is aware from its own knowledge that the address at which Mrs. Mather lived was a well known block of units); and the failure of the applicant to explain what happened to the furniture which even on their own version they were using for their daily use.

45. The Tribunal also accepts the evidence contained in document T31, that is, the notes of discussion with the executrix of the Estate of Mrs. Mather denying knowledge of the existence of the antique furniture as part of the Estate of Mrs. Mather.

The Law

46. Section 149B(1) of the Act provides that the trustee may file a written notice of objection to the discharge of the bankruptcy of a person, and s.149B(2) provides for the circumstances where the trustee must file or make a note of the objection to the discharge of bankruptcy.

47. Section 149C of the Act provides, amongst other things, that a notice of objection must set out the grounds for the objection being a ground or grounds set out in paragraph 149D(1).

48. In the present the Tribunal is satisfied that the trustee has complied with s.149C of the Act. In the present matter the trustee has relied on grounds under s.149D(1)(d) and (n).

49. Section 149A(1) of the Act provides that if the objection by the trustee is made on the basis of s.149D(1)(d) (amongst other grounds) then the period of bankruptcy is extended to 8 years, or if made under another ground such as s.149D(1)(n) to 5 years.

50. Section 149Q of the Act empowers the Tribunal to review the decision of the trustee to object o the discharge to bankruptcy.

51. In the present matter no objection was raised by the applicants as to the technical form of the notice of objection.

52. The sole question to be determined by the Tribunal is whether or not the trustee had grounds to object to the discharge of bankruptcy under s.149D(1)(d) and s.149D(1)(n).

53. The Tribunal has already found as a fact that the applicants own a sapphire pendant and antique furniture and failed to advise the trustee of the whereabouts of the pendant and furniture when requested to do so in wirting. That the applicants had a a beneficial interest in land at Orford, which interest they failed to disclose the trustee in bankruptcy when requested to do so in writing. In addition the Tribunal has found that Mr. Vaughan failed disclose his ownership of win to the trustee when requested to do so in writing.

54. The trustee had therefore established the basis upon which objection to the discharge of bankruptcy of the applicants under s.149D(1)(d) and (n) of the Act.

Decision

55. That the decisions of the trustee in bankruptcy of 27 June 1997 objecting to the automatic discharge from bankruptcy of Ben Vaughan and Denese Elizabeth Vaughan pursuant to s.149B of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 be affirmed.
