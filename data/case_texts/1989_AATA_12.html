ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - Practice and Procedure - application for adjournment - non-appearance of Applicant at hearing absence insufficiently explained - indifference - application refused.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s.42A(2).

Case V91 (88 A.T.C. 617).

Applicant COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

No. NT86/6165

AAT Decision No 4887

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

20 January 1989

Sydney

Tribunal : Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

(Sgd.) PETER M. ROACH,

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT86/6165 TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re :

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

20 January 1989 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. When this application was called on for hearing before the Tribunal the Applicant failed to appear. However, a young lady, his Secretary, on her own initiative appeared on his behalf to seek an adjournment of the Hearing. From all that then appeared it seems that she was doing the best that could be done in difficult circumstances.
  2. The Applicant, who described himself in income tax return forms as an accountant, first lodged with the Commissioner a form of income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1985 acknowledging a taxable income of $37,768.00. It was unsigned. The only information provided in that document as to how the sum was made up was presented upon a sheet of plain paper on which was recorded:

"INCOME - 1985

This represents a summary schedule of the

items as listed $37,768"

That was said to have been lodged on 6 October 1985 which was said to be within 14 days of the issue of a "final notice" from the Commissioner. Then, on 15 December 1985, the Applicant lodged with the Commissioner what he was to claim to be "a subsequent, or amended 1985 return". It was signed and it was dated 15 December 1985. It was presented with an undated and unsigned letter from what would appear to be the Applicant's firm which stated:-

"We are the auditors for the attached (sic).

This is a second or amended return and is designed to up-grade the first return which was lodged on an estimated basis due to the necessity to comply you're your Final Notice.

Enclosed are the actual income figures for the year.

It would be appreciated if you could issue an amended (sic) assessment."

The return provided details in support of taxable income returned. Those details embraced--

Income from employment - $35,907

A property partnership loss - ($2,926)

Interest - $35

Other Income from

"Audit and Public

Accountancy services (net)" - $4,752

$37,768

No details were provided in support of the last-mentioned figure.

  1. By notice of assessment issued the 19th day of February 1986 the Commissioner assessed the Applicant as having derived a taxable income of $37,768 and, pursuant to that assessment, required payment of $12,201.07 on 24 March 1986.
  2. By an amended assessment of 21 April 1986 the Commissioner modified the previously existing assessment to increase the liability of the Applicant by $752.38 identified in the notice of amended assessment as "additional tax for late return". The amended assessment was accompanied by an adjustment sheet which stated:

"NOTE

This amendment has been raised to impose

late lodgement penalty."

5. By letter bearing date 17 June 1986 the Applicant objected. His letter bore a footnote endorsement

"Please note.. this objection has been posted at R......Post Office at 6.15 pm on Friday 20th June, 1986 and is therefore within the time stipulated for lodgement of an objection."

By the objection, the Applicant protested that:-

* "your right to impose late lodgement penalty is eroneaus (sic) within the substantive law ....";

* that he had been unfairly treated;

* that he had supplied an annual return which constituted "a full and complete statement of the total income derived";

* that that return complied fully with the Regulations;

* that

"3. I did lodge my 1985 Return within fourteen (14) days of the issue of the Final Notice which issued from your office on 19th September 1985 thus complying with the substantive law requirement of the Act and therefore denying you the right to impose Item C - late lodgement penalty in relation to the said 1985 Return in the 1985 assessment which subsequently issued from your office 21st February 1986."

There was no contention that the issue of an amended assessment was beyond power.

6. In due course, on 9 July 1986, the Commissioner gave notice that the objection had been considered and disallowed and, on 23 July 1986 the Applicant requested that the matter be referred to this Tribunal for review "as quickly as possible". Although obviously aware of the transfer of jurisdiction from the Taxation Boards of Review to this Tribunal, he was not aware that the obligation to pay a fee to secure that review had been abolished. He enclosed unnecessarily a fee of $2.00. The Commissioner complied with the request on 8 October 1986.

7. In reply to an enquiry made by the Tribunal on 21 January 1988 the Applicant advised in writing

"I am ready to PROCEED TO HEARING on reasonable notice". The Tribunal gave notice of a telephone preliminary conference and, with some difficulty on the date, established contact with the Applicant. As a result, it was recommended that the matter proceed to a hearing if the application was not withdrawn by the Applicant. In consequence, the Applicant was given notice of a date, time and place for hearing and also called on to be available for a telephone directions hearing to be held some six weeks before the appointed hearing date. Two days before the scheduled telephone directions hearing the Registry received notice of a telephone contact number but, at the appointed time, he was not available.

  1. As a result, a message was left with his Secretary - the person referred to in pa.1 - advising that the appointed hearing date was confirmed and that the matter would remain listed to proceed on that date; that failure to attend upon the hearing would invite the risk of dismissal of the application; and that he should promptly make contact with the Commissioner's representative.
  2. I was informed by the Commissioner's representative that that contact was duly made and the messages, as left with the Applicant's Secretary, confirmed.
  3. Nothing further was heard of the matter until the eve of the hearing when, following a number of communications with the office of the Applicant initiated by the Tribunal, advice came to the Registry that the Applicant would not be appearing and that an adjournment was sought. The Registry advised that the request for adjournment was not approved "at this time" and, by fax, attention was directed to the provisions of s.42A(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.
  4. When the matter came on for hearing the Tribunal was fully constituted and ready to proceed; and the office of the Commissioner was represented and ready to proceed with the hearing; but the Applicant did not appear.
  5. In his place his Secretary appeared. She repeated the application for adjournment and sought to explain as best she could why the adjournment should be granted. It appeared that the Applicant had gone interstate, presumably on business, some days before; that no attempt had been made to communicate with the Tribunal before departure; that no arrangements were made prior to departure for any application for adjournment to be made; that the Secretary had been instructed to seek an adjournment when she spoke with the Applicant on the eve of the hearing; and that she had not been able to communicate to him subsequently the faxed response of the Tribunal.
  6. Whether the application should be granted is a matter calling for a responsible exercise of discretion. In discharging that responsibility it is particularly important to bear in mind that the matters which come before this Tribunal for determination commonly involve a challenge by the individual citizen to an exercise of power by the might of Government: in this instance represented by the Commissioner of Taxation.
  7. In Case V91 (88 ATC 617) I drew attention to .the circumstance that, when the absence of an Applicant at a hearing was unexplained, it might be that his failure to appear was due to ignorance; inadvertence; impossibility; or indifference. From what has been presented before me in this matter I am satisfied that the Applicant was aware of the appointed hearing date so that his absence is not to be explained by "ignorance". I am also satisfied that, as recently as the day prior to the appointed date, he was aware of the appointed date, time and place so that there is no basis to attributing his absence to "inadvertence". Further, in view of the advice the day before that he would not be in attendance, confirmed on the day of hearing by advice that he was in fact interstate, I am satisfied that his absence was not be explained by "impossibility" in the sense of factors beyond his control preventing his attendance.
  8. This was not a situation in which nothing was known as to why the Applicant failed to appear such as was the problem faced in Case V91 (ante). In all the circumstances, I am satisfied that the explanation for his absence is "indifference" in that he has been insufficiently concerned to move responsibly in the protection of his own interests by seeking an adjournment, doing so in such a way which would keep him informed as to the outcome of any such application. I am not persuaded that he has acted responsibly either in his own interests or having regard to the commitment of community resources which was made available in his interests.
  9. For those reasons the application for an adjournment is refused. In consequence, as there was no appearance by or on behalf of the Applicant with a view to a hearing and as no evidence was presented on behalf of the Applicant in support of his application for review, the assessment under review will be affirmed.
