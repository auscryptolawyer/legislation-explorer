Re John William Grant and the Commissioner of Police [1988] AATA 112 (8 April 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JOHN WILLIAM GRANT

And: THE COMMISSIONER OF POLICE

No. A87/170

AAT No. 4265

Gun Licensing

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.K. Todd (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Gun Licensing - objection to grant of gun licence - request for reasons for decision - decision to refuse request - whether "a decision the terms of which were recorded in writing and set out in a document was furnished to the applicant" - consideration of whether applicant entitled to statement of reasons for decision - necessity that decision maker notifies applicant and that notice be sufficiently particular.

Gun Licensing - objectiin to grant of gun licence - consideration of extension of time to lodge application for review.

Gun Licensing - law reform

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, ss.28(1), (1AA), (1AB), (1AC), (1A), (1B)

Gun Licence Ordinance 1937, ss.6(1), (2), 7P

Re Hollands and Commissioner of Police (1981) 3 AL:N N78 (Note 46)

Re Sewter and Commissioner of Police (Decision No. 1247, 26 September 1983)

Re P. and Commissioner of Police (Decision No.4019, 18 December 1987)

Hunter Valley Developments Pty Ltd and Others v Minister for Home Affairs and Environment (1984) 58 ALR 305

Tredex Australia Pty Ltd v Button (1986) 68 ALR 635

HEARING

CANBERRA

8:4:1988

ORDER

Declares under s.28(1B) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act") that the request of the applicant for a statement in writing under s.28(1) of that Act was made within a reasonable time after the decision of the delegate of the Commissioner of Police of 27 August 1987;

Decides that the applicant is entitled to be furnished with such a statement within 28 days as provided for by s.29(1AB) of the AAT Act.

DECISION

The questions before me arise from

(i) an application for review of a decision to refuse

to furnish the applicant with a statement of

reasons pursuant to s.28(1A)(a) of the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT

Act"), and of a decision to certify an objection

to the grant of a gun licence to the applicant,

and

(ii) an application foe an extension of time in which

to lodge such application for review.

2. The applicant applied for a gun licence pursuant to the

provisions of the Gun Licence Ordinance 1937 of the Australian Capital Territory ( "the Ordinance").

3. Section 6(1) and (2) of the Ordinance provides:

"6.(1) Subject to this section, the Registrar may, as he

thinks fit, in pursuance of applications made in accordance with Form
A in the Schedule -

(a) grant a gun licence to any person who

satisifies him that he is not less than

sixteen years of age; and

(b) grant a pistol licence to any person who

satisfies him that he is not less than

eighteen years of age,

and renew any such licence.

(2) The Registrar shall submit to the

Commissioner of Police all applications for

licences under this section, and, if the

Commissioner or a member of the Police Force

thereto authorized in writing by him certifies that

he objects to the grant of a licence to any

applicant, the Registrar shall not grant a licence

to that applicant but shall forward to that

applicant a notice in writing informing him of the

objection."

Section 7P of the Ordinance provides:

"7P.(1) Application may be made to the Tribunal

for review of a decision of the Registrar -

(a) refusing to grant or renew a gun licence, a

pistol licence or a gun dealer's licence;

(b) refusing to approve a pistol club for the

purposes of this Ordinance;

(c) cancelling the approval of a body corporate

or pistol club for the purposes of this

Ordinances;

(d) refusing to approve a person for the

purposes of this Ordinance;

or

(e) cancelling the approval of a person for the

purposes of this Ordinance.

(2) Application may be made to the Tribunal for

a review of a decision of the Commissioner of

Police or a member of the Police Force -

(a) certifying that he objects to the grant of a

licence; or

(b)recommending that a licence should not be

renewed."

4. Also involved is s.28 of the AAT Act, which so far as

relevant provides:

"28.(1) Where a person makes a decision in

respect of which an application may be made to the

Tribunal for a review, any person (in this section

referred to as the (applicant') who is entitled to

apply to the Tribunal for a review of the decision

may, by notice in writing given to the person who

made the decision, request that person to furnish

to the applicant a statement in writing setting out

the findings on material questions of fact,

referring to the evidence or other material on

which those findings were based and giving the

reasons for the decision, and the person who made

the decision shall, as soon as practicable but in

any case within 28 days after receiving the

request, prepare, and furnish to the applicant,

such a statement.

(1AA) Where a person to whom a request for a

statement in relation to a decision is made by an

applicant under sub-section (I) is of the opinion

that the applicant is not entitled to be furnished

with the statement, that person shall, as soon as

practicable but in any case within 28 days after

receiving the request, give to the applicant notice

in writing of his opinion.

(1AB) A person who gives a notice under

sub-section (IRA) with respect to a request for a

statement in relation to a decision is not required

to comply with the request unless the Tribunal, on

application under sub-section (1AC), decides that

the applicant was entitled to be furnished with the

statement, and, if the Tribunal so decides, the

first-mentioned person shall prepare the statement

and furnish it to the applicant within 28 days

after the decision of the Tribunal is given.

(1AC) The Tribunal shall- on an application

being made, as prescribed, by an applicant who has

received a notice under sub-section (1AA) with

respect to a request for a statement in relation to

a decisions decide whether the applicant was, or

was not, entitled to be furnished with the

statement.

1 1A) A person to whom a request for a statement

in relation to a decision is made under sub-section

(1) may refuse to prepare and furnish the Statement

if -

(a)in the case of a decision the terms of

which were recorded in writing and set out

in a document that was furnished to the

applicant - the request was not made on or

before the twenty-eighth day after the day

on which that document was furnished to the

applicant; or

(b)in any other case - the request was not

made within a reasonable time after the

decision was made,

and in any such case the person to whom the request

was made shall give to the applicant, as soon as

practicable but in any case within 28 days after

receiving the request, notice in writing stating

that the statement will not be furnished to him and

giving the reason why the statement will not be so

furnished.

(lB) For the purposes of paragraph (lA)(b), a

request for a statement in relation to a decision

shall be deemed to have been made within a

reasonable time after the decision was made if the

Tribunal, on application by the person who made the

request, declares that (the request was made within

a reasonable time after the decision was made."

5. The Tribunal has had occasion in the past to refer, amongst other problems with the Ordinance, to the uneasy relationship between the power of the Registrar of Gun Licences ("the Registrar") to grant gun and pistol licences (s.6(l)), and the power of the Commissioner of Police ("the Commissioner") or a person authorized by him (to whom I shall refer for convenience as his "delegate") to certify that he objects to the grant of a licence (s.6(2) ) See Re Hollands and Commissioner of Police (1981) 3 ALN N78 (Note 46); Re Sewter and Commissioner of Police (Decision No. 1247, 26 September 1983); Re P and Commissioner of Police (Decision No. 4019, 18 December 1987).

6. The fact is, and it is critical for present purposes, that if the Commissioner or his delegate so certifies, it is the decision to so certify that needs to be the subject of an application for review. Were this not so, there would have been no point in enacting s.7P(2).

The relevant facts are as follows:

(i) On 27 August 1987 a First Constable of Police- as

the delegate of the Commissioner, certified in

writing, in a minute directed not to the Registrar

but to the Officer-in-Charge, Special Crime

Section, that he objected to the grant of the

licence. The objection was on the footing that

the applicant was "not a fit and proper person to

be the holder of a licence under the Ordinance in

the Australian Capital Territory". Such an

objection is not based on any criterion to be found

in the Ordinance, but on this point see Re

Hollands, in the passage cited in Re P the

delegate remarked in the minute that a copy of his

report could "perhaps" be forwarded to the

Registrar of Gun Licences for his information.

This document was not furnished to the applicant

until a directions hearing held by the Tribunal on

26 February 1988 for the purpose of resolving the

problems here under discussion.

(ii) Also on 27 August 1987, the same delegate had a

conversation with the applicant in which he

informed him that he would be objecting to the

grant of a gun licence and in which he told him why

he considered that he was not a fit and proper

person to be the holder of such a licence. He

informed the applicant that he had a right of

appeal to this Tribunal, and said that the

applicant had told him that he intended to use that

right.

(iii) On 28 August 1987 the Acting Executive Officer,

Deputy Registrar of Gun Licences, wrote to the

applicant setting out s.6(2) of the Ordinance and

then going on to state as follows:

"An objection pursuant to s.6(2) has been

raised which prevents the Registrar granting a

gun licence to you. You can, if you wish,

appeal against the decision."

Section 7P of the Ordinance was then set out in the

letter.

(iv) On 29 October 1987 the applicant consulted his solicitor

about the matter and was told that he could request

reasons for the decision of the delegate.

(v) By letter dated 2 November 1987 the applicant's

solicitors sought from the Commissioner reasons,

pursuant to s.28(1) of the RAT Act, for the decision to

certify the delegate's objection.

(vi) This letter was responded to not by the Commissioner or

his delegate but by the Deputy Registrar of Gun

Licences, who on I December 1987 declined to give

reasons for the decision on the footing that the request

was not made on or before the twenty-eighth day after

the applicant was "formally notified of the decision".

7. It will be recalled that the minute of 27 August (see para 7(i) above) was not communicated to the applicant until 26 February 1988. The first question is therefore whether the letter to the applicant of 28 August 1987 was "a decision the terms of which were recorded in writing and set out in a document that was furnished to the applicant". It was submitted for the respondent that the letter of 28 August was enough to convey to the applicant what the decision was, and that for this purpose the precise formulation of a carefully drawn court document was not requisite. This being so, it was said, the effect of the decision having been conveyed well enough to the applicant, it was up to him to seek reasons timeously if he wished further adumbration of it.

8. I am not disposed to accept (this submission The applicant was not furnished with a copy of the writing constituting the record of the decision, namely the minute of 27 August, until the directions hearing on 26 February 1988. The letter of 28 August did not record in writing the terms of the decision. It recited that an objection "had been raised" (see paragraph 7(iii) above). It was not sent to the applicant by the decision-maker In all the circumstances I find that the letter of 28 August 1987 was not a decision the terms of which were recorded in writing. Nor was it a response by the person who made the decision. The minute of 27 August was such a decision, but it was not furnished to the applicant. Section 28(1A)(a) of the AAT Act therefore does not apply, so that it is s.28(1B) that is applicable.

9. In all the circumstances I consider that the only proper manner in which to resolve the impasse is,

(i) to declare, pursuant to s.28(1B) of the AAT Act,

that the request of the applicant for a statement

(n writing under s.28(1) of the AAT Act in

relation to the decision of the delegate of the

Commissioner of Police to certify his objection to

the grant of a gun licence to the applicant was

made within a reasonable time after the decision,

and

(ii) to decide that the applicant is entitled to be

furnished with such a statement.

10. This statement of reasons is to be provided within 28 days of this Decision: See s.28(1AB) of the AAT Act. It is true that there was no application specifically made as such by the applicant under s.28 ( 1AC) of the AAT Act. For that matter however the letter of I December 1987 which indicated refusal by the Deputy Registrar of Gun LicenceS to furnish a statement was stated to be pursuant to s.6(2) of the Ordinance. No mention was made of s.28(1AA) of the AAT Act. The reality of the matter, and the need to resolve the impasse, dictates to me that I should regard the procedures of s.28(1AA) and s.28(1AC) respectively as having been effectively invoked.

11. It also follows inexorably from these reasons, and from the declaration under s.28(lB), that the time for the making by the applicant of his application for review should be extended to 14 December 1987, being its actual date of lodgment.

12. I should add that I have not failed to take into account the decisions of the Federal Court to which I was referred by the representative of the respondent. It is perfectly correct that those decisions emphasise the need for prompt action in relation to time limits: See Hunter Valley Developments Pty Ltd and Others v Minister for Home Affairs and Environment (1984) 58 ALR 305 I have also taken into account the distinction between notification of a decision and the provision of reasons for a decision: See Tredex Australia Pty Ltd v Button (1986) 68 ALR 635. But the circumstances of this case are quite special, and are influenced greatly by the structure of the Ordinance. An applicant is entitled to expect from an arm of government that the right person will notify a decision, and that the decision will specify with reasonable particularity what that decision was. It is noteworthy that not only did this not occur here, but that the document that did purport to be the decision was not even in the first instance directed to the Registrar.

13. Having said all this, I would wish to emphasise that I intend no criticism personally of the officers who, in the event, have been found to have failed to carry out the correct procedures. The fault is in the clumsy and awkward provisions of the Ordinance, which have been criticised by the Tribunal for some years. The persons who administer the Ordinance should not have to have a lawyer by their side to get it right, but that is about what it comes to as the Ordinance stands. It is to be trusted that if there is to be a new or substantially amended Ordinance its provisions will accommodate not only the need for revision of substantive requirements in relation to gun licensing, but also the need for revision of the unsatisfactory procedural requirements that have led to problems in this and other cases. Amendment of the Ordinance in this regard alone is not only warranted but necessary.
