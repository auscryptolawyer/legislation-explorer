Hardwick and Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales [1979] AATA 62 (18 May 1979)

Last Updated: 12 March 2007

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - Tax Agents' Board - Cancellation of registration of tax agent on proof of bankruptcy - No discretion in Board - Review by Administrative Appeals Tribunal - No discretion in Tribunal. Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 as amended s.251(K)(3).

DECISION AND REASONS

Re: D.H. HARDWICK

And: TAX AGENTS' BOARD, NEW SOUTH WALES

AAT Decision No 196

No. 79/10017

John A. Nimmo J.

Date: 18 May 1979.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

No. 79/10017

Re : D.H. HARDWICK

And : TAX AGENTS' BOARD,

NEW SOUTH WALES

DECISION

The Tribunal affirms the decision of the Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales, that the registration of the applicant as a tax agent be cancelled on the ground of the applicant's bankruptcy.

DATED this 18th day of May 1979.

JOHN A. NIMMO, J.

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. 79/10017

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re : D.H. HARDWICK

And : TAX AGENTS' BOARD,

NEW SOUTH WALES

REASONS FOR DECISION

This is an application to review a decision of the Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales, made on 22 February 1979 that the registration of the applicant as a tax agent be cancelled on the ground of the applicant's bankruptcy.

The application is brought pursuant to s.s.(5) of s.251(K) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 19 36 which reads -

"(5) An application may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of the cancellation under this section of the registration of a tax agent, being a cancellation made after the commencement of this sub-section."

The sub-section was deemed to have commenced on 1 July 1976 (sections 2 and 16 of the Income Tax Assessment Amendment Act No. 57 of 1977).

In a letter dated 22 January 1979 the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Sydney, advised the New South Wales Tax Agents' Board as follows:

"You are advised that on 10 August 1977 a sequestration order was made against the abovenamed taxpayer upon the petition presented on 7 December 1976 of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, following the taxpayer's failure to comply with the provisions of a bankruptcy notice served on him on 25 May 1976 and issued in respect of a District Court judgment for $6,736.89 obtained on 4 December 1975.

By order of the Court dated the 8th day of June 1977, Mercredits Finance Ltd was substituted as a petitioning creditor and the taxpayer was subsequently made bankrupt.

As this is the case it is recommended that the Board cancel Mr Hardwick's registration as a tax agent under section 251K Sub-section (3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act."

At its meeting held on 22 February 1979 the Board, on the strength of that letter and purporting to give effect to the provisions of s.s.(3) of s.251(K), cancelled the applicant's registration as a tax agent. Sub-section (3) reads -

"(3) A Board shall cancel the registration of an individual as a tax agent upon his death or bankruptcy, or his permanently ceasing to carry on business as a tax agent."

The grounds relied on by the applicant for a review of the Board's decision are those set out in paragraph 6 of his Application for Review. They read -

"(a) Since my bankruptcy arose as the result of financial affairs unconnected with my practice either as an accountant or a tax agent I challenge the validity of the Tax Agents' Board decision on the following legal basis. Section 251(K)(3) of the Income Tax Assessment Act is, by applying the maxim expressio unius, intended to deal with bankruptcy arising in connection with the business of tax agency as suggested by the third criterion of the sub-section's operation, namely ceasing business as a tax agent. Section 251(k)(3) is accordingly inapplicable to my case and the Board's decision is invalid being based on a mistake of law.

(b) My livelihood has been severely jeopardized by this decision since I cannot effectively carry on my profession as a public accountant without the ability to practice as a tax agent."

It was common ground before this Tribunal that a sequestration order was made against the applicant on 10 August 1977. Evidence given by the applicant before the Tribunal establishes that his bankruptcy had no connection with his practice as an accountant and tax agent which he had conducted from the year 1962 until the cancellation of his registration on 22 February 1979. It arose out of his having guaranteed payment of moneys payable under a hire purchase agreement which a mining company of which he was a shareholder and director entered into with a finance company. The mining company, which ultimately went into liquidation, made default in its payments and a judgment under the guarantee was obtained by the finance company against the applicant. His failure to meet the judgment resulted in his bankruptcy. The applicant's evidence also establishes that although he has been able to carry on the accountancy side of his practice since his bankruptcy, his income earning capacity as a private practitioner has suffered by reason of his inability to practise as a tax agent. Since the date of the sequestration order he has derived additional income as an employee of a small printing company. As the company's accountant he receives a weekly salary of $104.00.

Mr Boultbee, who appeared for the applicant, contended that the scheme of s.251(K), the third limb of ss.(3) and the provisions of s.s.(5) of the section indicate an intention by the legislature to give the Board a discretion whether or not to cancel the registration of a tax agent on his becoming a bankrupt and that the Board should have exercised that discretion in favour of the applicant.

After construing the section as a whole and the two provisions referred to by Mr Boultbee, I am satisfied that his contention must be rejected.

In my view, nothing emerges from the exercise which in any way would support even an implication that the Board has a discretion under s.s.(3).

Once it has been established to the satisfaction of the Board that a tax agent has been made bankrupt, the Board has no discretion in the matter. It is bound under s.s.(3) to cancel the agent's registration.

Referring to s.s.(3), the President of the Tribunal, Brennan J., in Re Adams and The Tax Agents' Board (1976) 12 ALR 239, said at p. 240:

"The sub-section, unlike sub-section (2), gives the Board no discretion once it has been proved that the tax agent has been adjudicated bankrupt. ... The Board was bound to cancel the registration once it was proved that a sequestration order had been made. (In Re Burton and Blinkhorn (1903) 2 K.B. 300)."

The President went on to say:

"The Board decided that that sub-section 'did not contain any discretionary provisions and there was no alternative but to cancel Mr Adams' registration because of his bankruptcy'. That decision was clearly right."

In the unreported case of Re Tomkins and The Tax Agents' Board (77/12001), the President repeated the conclusion he reached in the Adams' case.

In the unreported case of Re Rodgers and The Tax Agents' Board of Western Australia (78/18006), Smithers J. reached a similar conclusion, for at p. 4 he said:

"On reference to the precise terms of s. 251(K)(3) one is immediately struck by the feature that registration must be cancelled on death or bankruptcy"

and at p. 5 -

"The mandatory nature of the statutory provision is apparent from the decision of the President of the Tribunal, Brennan J., in Re Adams and The Tax Agents' Board (1976) 12 ALR 239."

On a review of a decision of the Board cancelling a tax agent's registration because of his bankruptcy, the Tribunal is in no better position than the Board was in when it made the cancellation. It, too, has no discretion in the matter and is therefore bound to affirm the Board's decision regardless of the circumstances under which the applicant's bankruptcy came about and of the adverse consequences the Board's decision may have had and may continue to have on the livelihood of the applicant. For these reasons the Board's decision is affirmed.
