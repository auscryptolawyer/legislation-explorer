Kingston and Minister for the Capital Territory [1980] AATA 2 (24 January 1980)

Last Updated: 13 March 2007



Rates - Re-determination of unimproved value - land in 1949 used for dumping of rubbish - area in intervening years developed to become prestige area as shown by prices paid around relevant date for land in vicinity - valuation on basis of comparable sales.

R. and A. E. KINGSTON

THE MINISTER FOR THE CAPITAL TERRITORY

No. 79/5010

AAT Decision No 354

The Honourable Mr J. B. K. Williams (Senior Member)

24th January 1980

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No. 79/5010

Re: R. and A.E. KINGSTON

And: THE MINISTER FOR THE CAPITAL

TERRITORY

DECISION

The Tribunal affirms the decision of the Minister of State for the Capital Territory confirming, under Section 29 of the Rates Ordinance 1926, the re-determination of the unimproved value of Block 8 Section 1A, Forrest, Australian Capital Territory, in the sum of $41,000.00.

DATED this Twenty-fourth day of January 1980.

(Sgnd. J.B. K. Williams )

(Senior Member)

REASONS FOR DECISION

The applicants in this matter, Mr & Mrs Kingston are the lessees from the Crown of residential land at 1 Baudin Street, Forrest, ACT, described as Block 8, Section 1A. The land was valued for rating purposes as at 1 January 1976 in the sum of $41,000.00. The applicants objected to this valuation pursuant to Section 29 of the Rabes Ordinance of the Australian Capital Territory but the original valuation was confirmed. They now seek a review of this decision.

The applicants purchased a lease of the subject block in 1949 and subsequently erected a residence upon it. The block is described in the valuation report, in evidence before the Tribunal, as being an irregular shaped inside block situated in a prestige area of Canberra. It is further described as being located on the lower side of the road with the block falling sharply towards the rear boundary. The block has an easterly aspect and no views.

Two main areas of contention are raised by the applicants. The first is that when they purchased the block it had been used as a rubbish dump for the dumping of building materials, bottles, cans and other rubbish. They claim that this necessitated extra foundations when erecting their residence upon the land and the removal of large stones when they obstructed the laying down of foundations. In constructing the lawn and gardens, bottles and tins had to be removed.

The second objection is that bad drainage caused water to gather upon the land following heavy rain and that this situation had only been remedied when they constructed a drain.

The respondent relies on five sales designated in the valuation report in evidence before the Tribunal as sales "A", "B", "C", "D" and "E". Each of these was a sale in the Forrest area reasonably adjacent to the subject property. At the hearing before the Tribunal evidence for the respondent was given by Mr. F.G. Clarke, a qualified valuer employed in the ACT Valuation Section of the Australian Taxation Office. He said that the original valuation had been done by a departmental valuer no longer in the employ of the department. Mr. Clarke said that he became involved in this matter at the objection stage. It appears from Mr. Clarke's evidence that the original valuation was based upon sales "B", "D" and "E" only and that, for some unexplained reason, sales "A" and "C" were not known or were not available to the original valuer. However, Mr Clarke when considering the objection had regard to all the sales "A", "B", "C", "D" and "E". Each of these was a sale of improved land, and in each case it was necessary to analyse the total sale price and deduce the land component in the sale. He said that he was satisfied that each analysis was correct.

Sale "A" was of a property at 10 Dampier Crescent, Forrest, described as Block 7 of Section 38. It is opposite the subject block as both the subject block and the land in sale "A" are located near the intersection of Baudin Street and Dampier Crescent. A contract for the sale of the property comprised in sale "A" was entered into, in January 1976 and the transaction completed in June 1976. The land has an area of 1600 m2. The total sale price was $82,000.00. Upon analysis, a land component of $48,000.00 was deduced. Mr. Clarke stated that he considered this block to be superior to the subject block, mainly, it appears, for the reason that it is situated on the high side of the road.

Sale "B" was of a property at 31 Tasmania Circle, Forrest and the land is described as Block 4 of Section 42. It has an area of 2195m2. The sale took place on 2 June

1975. The total sale price was $85,000.00 and following an analysis of the sale a land component of $40,000.00 was deduced. It was valued for rating purposes in the sum of $37,000.00. Mr. Clarke stated that he considered this block to be slightly inferior to the subject block.

Sale "C" was of a property at 16 Tennyson Crescent, Forrest and the land is described as Block 6 of Section 1. A contract for the sale was entered into in October 1975 and the transaction completed in November 1975. The land has an area of 2479m2. The total sale price was $87,000.00 and following an analysis of the sale, a land component of $49,000.00 was deduced. It was valued for rating purposes in the sum of $42,500.00. Mr. Clarke described the land as being located in a very prestigious area with a northerly aspect but no real views to the front but with a restricted outlook towards Red Hill from the rear. He considered it superior to the subject block.

Sale "D" was of a property at 41 Melbourne Avenue, Forrest and the land is described as Block 2 Section 2. The sale took place on 2 October 1975. The land has an area of 2251 m2. The total sale price was $90,000.00. An analysis of this sale produced a deduced land component of $55,000.00. It was valued for rating purposes at $44,000.00. Mr Clarke described this property as being located in a first class residential area and he considered it to be superior to the subject block.

Sale "E" was of a property at 48 Dominion Circuit, Forrest and the land is described as Block 27 of Section 3. The sale took place in September of 1975. The area of land contained in the block is 1397m. The total sale price was $71,000.00 and following an analysis of this sale, a deduced land component of $39,000.00 was obtained. It was valued for rating purposes in the sum of $33,000.00. Mr. Clarke described this property as having a much smaller area than the subject block, much lower in elevation and considered it to be overall inferior to the subject block. He did not consider it to be really comparable with the subject property but had included the sale in the valuation report to complete the sales evidence available in the area to indicate the kind of price that people were prepared to pay for property in Forrest.

It will be observed from the foregoing that there is a substantial difference between the unimproved values applied to each of the properties in sales "A", "B", "C", "D" and "E" and the deduced land components arrived at following an analysis of each sale. It may be illustrated as follows:-Deduced Land Component Applied Unimproved Capital

Sale "A" $ 48,000 $ 38,000

Sale "B" $ 40,000 $ 37,000

Sale "C" $ 49,000 $ 42,500

Sale "D" $ 55,000 $ 44,000

Sale "E" $ 39,000 $ 33,000

No real explanation of this difference emerged at the hearing. As has been said, when the original valuation was made, no regard was had to sales "A" and "C" as the evidence is that the valuer had no knowledge of them. It is however apparent that the valuer having knowledge of and having analysed sales "B", "D" and "E" was disposed to assign to each of them an unimproved value substantially less than the deduced land component particularly in the case of sale "B" where the difference was $11,000.00. This suggests to me that the valuer had some reservations in mind, the nature of which does not appear in the evidence. Mr. Clarke was unable to explain this other than to say that the original valuer had been "conservative". Further difficulties appear when consideration is given to the eventual analyses of sales "A" and "C" by Mr Clarke which, if each be accepted as accurate, show a deduced land component, substantially in excess of that considered by the original valuer, presumably based upon the evidence of sales "B", "D" and "E" to be the unimproved values of blocks "A" and "C". An anomaly is to be seen in the fact that the land in sale "A" considered by Mr. Clarke to be superior to the subject block has been valued at a lower sum. This state of the evidence does not make the task of the Tribunal an easy one; further it must be productive of confusion in the minds of Forrest ratepayers in general and the applicants in particular.

However this may be, it is necessary to keep in mind the provisions of Section 5 of the Rates Ordinance of the ACT which defines the unimproved capital value of a parcel of land as the capital sum that might be expected to have been paid for it on the relevant date, that is 1 January 1976, upon certain assumptions, the only one of which is relevant for present purposes, is the assumption that the only improvements on the parcel of land were Commonwealth improvements by way of clearing, draining, levelling etc.

Upon established principles of valuation, followed by the Tribunal on numerous occasions, the best way to arrive at the unimproved capital value as defined, is to ascertain what comparable land in the vicinity is fetching on dates as close as possible to the relevant date. It was thus open for the respondent to have regard to sales "A" to "E" as being sales in the neighbourhood of the subject land at times reasonably proximate to the relevant date. It is, I think, clear that, whilst in the original valuation no regard was had to sales "A" and "C", the respondent was entitled to have regard to these sales when they came to notice whilst considering objections.

Putting aside for the moment the matters which the applicants claim to be special considerations affecting their block, it is plain that the sales evidence indicates that very substantial prices were, at about the relevant date, being paid for land in Forrest.

In the cross examination of Mr. Clarke at the hearing before the Tribunal, Mr. Kingston suggested that of all the sales, the land in sales "A" and "C" were blocks most comparable with his own. Mr. Clarke agreed with this. The deduced land component in sale "A" was $48,000.00 and in sale "C" was $49,000.00. No attack was made on the analyses of these sales so that upon the evidence, there is nothing to suggest that they were erroneous. Whatever conservatism or caution may have been felt by the original valuer in determining values in Forrest following his analyses of sales "B", "D" and "E" alone, may well I think, have been dissipated had he become acquainted with sales "A" and "C".

As has been said, the land in sale "A" is opposite the subject land and, in the opinion of Mr. Clarke, superior to it. It is substantially smaller than the subject block. From my observation the superiority largely rests upon it being on the high side of the road, and is by no means a marked superiority. As for sale "C", the land is approximately the same area as the subject block. It has restricted views of Red Hill from the rear. Again I think its degree of superiority is small.

The case for the applicants placed emphasis upon the condition of the land at the time of their acquisition of the lease of it in 1949. It was said that the land had been used as a dump for unused building materials and other rubbish. This created difficulty when sinking building foundations. Also, rubbish had to be removed over a period of time to enable lawns and gardens to be laid down.

I accept in substance this account of the condition of the land at the time of leasing but the question for determination is the impact this condition would have upon a potential purchaser of the land on the 1 January 1976. At the time of the applicants' acquisition of the lease, the area was a newly developing one. In the years intervening up to 1976, the area developed considerably into a high class residential suburb. Assuming the subject land to be in its original state on 1 January 1976, many developments had occurred in the area extrinsic to the subject land itself and which, upon the sales evidence, have made the area a very desirable one in which very substantial prices were paid around the relevant date. Assuming the subject land to be in its 1949 condition on the relevant date it may be that a potential purchaser would be seeking some discount by reason of the condition of the land. The difficulty is to ascertain the amount of the discount he might seek. I do not think the answer to this lies in trying to quantify the amount of labour and effort and money expended by the applicants in bringing the land to its 1976 condition.

Having regard to the sales evidence in general and in particular to sales "A", "C" and "D", it appears to me that the deduced land values show an appreciable difference between them and the value attributed to the applicants' land. Allowing for some superiority in these blocks over the subject block, by reason of siting and position, it is my opinion that there is sufficient difference to absorb any discount a potential purchaser of the subject land may have sought by reason of its condition. As has been said, the land was on 1 January 1976 located in a prestigious and sought after area, circumstances which might well cause potential buyers to treat less seriously the condition of the land than might otherwise be the case.

Another matter raised by the applicants was that of water accumulation upon their land following heavy rain. It appears from the evidence that an old creek bed runs across Sections 1 and 1A, Forrest and across the bottom of the subject land. It further appears that the Commonwealth in 1953 constructed a drain to alleviate the situation and the applicants connected their land to it. The applicants state that the Commonwealth's drain was blocked by tree roots in the late 50's causing some back-up of water on their property until the roots were cleared. I understood the applicants' difficulty in this respect to be concerned with past events rather than events current in January 1976. Upon the evidence it does' not seem to me that any water accummulation which might have been experienced in the past would have had any appreciable impact upon a potential purchaser in January 1976 even assuming that he was aware of it.

Upon a consideration of the whole of the material before the Tribunal I am not persuaded that the value attributed to the subject land is "too high" to employ the term used in Section 29(2) of the Rates Ordinance when regard is had to the sales evidence in the area. Accordingly I consider that the respondent's decision should be affirmed although I think that this conclusion has unsatisfactory aspects, particularly arising from a comparison of the applicants' valuation with that of the land in sale "A". I conceive the task of the Tribunal is to determine the unimproved value of the applicants' land upon the sales evidence available and not upon a basis of uniformity with neighbouring blocks which may well, upon the evidence before the Tribunal, be incorrectly valued.
