ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - tax avoidance - income splitting - geologist delivering services as agent of family trust - arrangement to distribute income earned by his personal exertion through trust to family members - whether applicant derived income whether arrangement a sham - whether arrangement has purpose or effect within s 260

REASONS FOR DECISION

26 August 1987 Deputy President RD Nicholson

While this decision was being prepared the Tribunal learned of the death of the Applicant. It expresses its sympathy to his family and friends.

The Tribunal adopts the same course as did the Tribunal constituted by Mrs.Dwyer, Senior Member, in the matter of Re Davis and Director-General of Social Security (1984) 6 ALN N332 (not reported on this point) and the Tribunal presided over by Mrs Balmford, Senior Member in the matter of Re Wiseman and Department of Transport (No 2) (Decision No 2621, 11 April 1986) and considers it appropriate to proceed to a decision in this proceeding although the Applicant is now deceased. In doing so it notes the paragraph from the judgment of the High Court, constituted by Knox CJ, Higgins and Starke JJ, in Ryan v Davies Brothers Limited [1921] HCA 53; (1921) 29 CLR 527 at 532-3, cited by the Tribunal in Davis and Wiseman (No 2).

Objection Decisions Under Review

The Applicant seeks review of the following objection decisions:

(1) In respect of the income year ended 30 June 1977: the inclusion in the Applicant's income of $7924 as "net income from geological consultancy fees".

(2) In respect of the income year ended 30 June 1978:

the inclusion in the Applicant's taxable income of the sum of $15,129 as "net income from geological consultancy fees".

(3) In respect of the income year ended 30 June 1979:

the inclusion of the sum of $5,752 in the Applicant's taxable income as "net income from geological consultancy fees".

In respect of each of these amounts it is contended on behalf of the Applicant that they were incorrectly included in his assessment and should correctly be included in the return of the trustee of the Applicant's family trust pursuant to Division 6 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").

Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction arises pursuant to sub-section 189(2) of the Act applied in accordance with section 222 of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986. The time limits normally applicable to applications to this Tribunal pursuant to section 29 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 do not apply in relation to an objection decision - sub-section 14ZD(2) of the Taxation Administration Act 1953.

Burden of proof

Paragraph (b) of section 190 of the Act provides that the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive lies upon the Applicant: cf. Gauci v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 75 ATC 4257 at 4261; McCormack v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 79 ATC 4111; MacMine v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 79 ATC 4133.

Facts

The Applicant was a geologist. In October 1976 he learned that some geologists were to be cut from the staff of company A with which he was then employed. Upon being requested to do so he resigned from that company on 1 November 1976. He then set about contacting a number of people in the mining industry to find opportunities for work.

In early December he was offered a contract with company B. During November, while his enquiries for employment continued, he had undertaken field work for company C in his own name. On 15 December 1976, the enquiries not having yielded him employment, he telephoned company B "and agreed to start work for them on January 24th" (to quote the words in the Applicant's diary maintained at that time).

He applied for and obtained unemployment benefits from 10 December 1976. These continued to be paid until 4 February 1977.

On 24 January 1977 the Applicant reported to the office of company B and "arranged to be paid daily at $80 a day as a contractor". For the rest of that week he attended that office making preparation for forthcoming field work.

On 27 January 1977 the Applicant recorded in his diary that he arranged to see his accountant re a trust. This entry was followed by some three or so words heavily inked over so as to be illegible. At the same time the Applicant was in touch with his public liability and health insurer. On 1 February he recorded he had called on his accountant who advised setting up a family trust with a company established by them as trustee. The cost would be approximately $250 with associated costs. The accountant's company had been incorporated on 3 December 1976.

The Applicant's evidence was that he understood he was thereafter working for his family trust although "the paperwork" was not complete. He said no advice was given to him of tax advantages in establishing the trust and that all he wanted was a company giving him limited liability. By accepting his accountant's advice to use the trustee company incorporated by them and used for multiple trusts, he was saving money in comparison with incorporating a company himself.

On 11 February 1977 the Applicant recorded in his diary:

"Talk with ... on taxation etc. on my contract. Apparently ... administrator in the Melbourne office is making the fuss. Pointed out that I was not directly employed but through company and Trust."

On or about this day he wrote to company B as follows:

"As discussed briefly with you, I would like to point out that I understand I am working on contract to [company B] at a rate of $80 a day or $40 a half day, plus all expenses. When I started in contract and consulting geology, I organised the above Trust and in fact, [company B] are employing ..., as Trustee for the ... Family Trust. The effective portion of this, is [the trustee], a Company, and as such is not liable to taxation of income at source.

Perhaps I should add that I am covered both by Public Liability Insurance to $250,000 and accident and sickness insurance. This, I believe, absolves [company B] from holding Workers Compensation" and Public Liability cover for me.

My accountant, ..., has advised me, that even though [company B] supply me with equipment, it is the Company and not me personally who is employed to carry out the work."

Although in his letter he added "... in the past few months, I have been employed by two other groups in the above capacity", he said in sworn evidence that was not the case and the statement was made in an attempt to support the contents of the letter. On 14 February 1977 the Applicant recorded the Melbourne office of company B thought the employment arrangements were "o.k." but considered he may have to have worker's compensation through the Trust.

By a letter dated 17 February 1977 the Applicant's accountants instructed their solicitors to establish a family trust.

On 25 February 1977 the Applicant, at company B's office, "rang up accountant and checked how things were going". He also "presented bill to [company B] and got $2,200 for work to date" (emphasis added). His solicitors forwarded the deed of trust for execution.

On 1 March 1977 the Applicant's accountants forwarded the trust deeds for execution and advised:

"Once this document has been signed and returned, you should start operating the business under the name of the Trust. This entails the following adjustments:-

  1. A new bank account should be opened, if not already done so, and all business dealings from this time should go through this account. The account should be named "... Trustee for the ... Trust".
  2. All stationery, including orders, correspondence, invoices, etc. to be headed "... Trustee for the ... Family Trust".
  1. When contracting for Bank Loans, Hire Purchase Agreements, Leases or any other form of contract, the description should be "... Trustee for the ... Trust".
  1. All creditors should be notified of the change, to enable them to send accounts in the new name. This notification takes place by the change of name on letterheads, change of name on order forms and changes to the cheques for payment."

On the same date the proposed trustee (a company of which some of the accountants were partners) resolved to act as trustee of the Applicant's family trust.

The Applicant recorded in his diary on 14 March 1977:

"Went to bank and organised account for Trust Fund. Also called in on accountant ... and got him to send necessary papers through to ANZ bank when they were ready."

An account was opened in the name of the Trustee of the Applicant's family trust ("the family trust account") and the Applicant credited it with $2200 from company B (an invoice headed with the name of the trustee and signed by the Applicant having been issued for that amount on 12 March 1977, including a request that the cheque be payable to the trustee). The settled sum of $25 was credited to this account at the same time.

The next day the Applicant's accountants forwarded the executed trust deed and payment of costs to their solicitors. The trust deed named the primary beneficiaries of the trust as the children of the Applicant and his wife (and their children and spouses) and also named the Applicant and his wife as additional members of the class of general beneficiaries.

On 21 March 1977 the Applicant credited the family trust account with $2160 from company B (an invoice in the form previously described having been issued on 29 March 1977). The next day the Trustee resolved to give the Applicant authority to operate solely on the family trust account. The following day the Trustee resolved to give the Applicant authority to run the daily operations of the trust's business "but all major decisions would be made by the Trustee".

On 14 April 1977 the Applicant credited the family trust account with $1400 from company C (an invoice in the form previously described having been issued on 7 April 1977). On 3 May 1977 the Applicant credited the family trust account with $1600 from company B (an invoice in the form previously described having been issued on 29 April 1977). On 7 June 1977 the Applicant credited the family trust account with $2000 from company B (an invoice in the form previously described having been issued on 30 May 1977).

A cheque was drawn on the family trust account on 30 May 1977 and the butt notated "Transfer of [settlor named] $25 to set up Trust Fund to a savings account". The next day the sum of $25 for the Applicant's family trust was paid into an account at the ANZ Bank Perth after legal advice to the effect it was undesirable for it to be banked in the family trust trading account.

On 30 May the trustee resolved to sell a Ford Falcon Motor Vehicle and purchase a new Datsun 120Y, the Applicant being instructed to carry out and complete the purchase. The Falcon had not been transferred to the trust and the Datsun was registered and insured in the Applicant's name although there was an understanding that it was trust property.

On 27 June 1977 it also resolved that trust income be applied for the benefit of beneficiaries comprising four children of the Applicant.

In the 1977/78 year of income, on 7 March Company D issued a service order to the trustee (marked for the attention of the Applicant) for geological and related services from this date for six months, following the expiration of which he became an employee of that company in his own name. On 25 March 1978 an invoice issued from the family trust to company D for $1530, followed by further such invoices usually at monthly intervals. On 30 June 1978 the trustee again resolved concerning distribution but added the Applicant and his wife to the children as beneficiaries in specified percentages.

At the commencement of the 1978/79 year of income the trustee resolved to charge the children's beneficiaries loan accounts for maintenance and education at the rate of $30 per week and to pay that charge to the Applicant and his wife. On 27 June 1979 it resolved to distribute trust income to the children and the Applicant (principally) in specified amounts.

There are also facts pertaining to the operation of the Applicant's family trust and the role of the trustee which apply generally throughout the dates covered above. The Applicant headed correspondence and invoices with the name of his trustee as trustee for his named family trust. The address given on the correspondence and invoices was his home address, which was the address of the trust but not the registered office of the trustee. The business address of the family trust on its income tax returns was the Applicant's home address. Cheques arising from services rendered by the Applicant (on his account as an employee of the trust) were payable to the trustee. The trustee did not itself advertise nor did the directors of the trustee company solicit for geological business. Such solicitation or any approaches were made to the Applicant. He did not receive written instructions from the trustee on how to carry out his work, nor did he receive any salary or enter into a written contract of employment. Neither the trustee nor the trust had any income earning asset nor expertise in geological matters apart from that possessed by the Applicant as a general beneficiary of the trust. When funds were received by the trust as a consequence of work carried out by the Applicant and after they had been paid into the trust bank account, not infrequently a cheque for the same amount was drawn on that account payable to the Applicant's joint account. The telephone at the Applicant's home was in his name but the account for it was paid by the trustee.

The Applicant was a member of the Mining Club, an organisation of exploration geologists active in Western Australia. His evidence was that many members of that club mentioned to him that they had restricted their mode of operation so that they carried on provision of their services through a trust so that he regarded that as a common practice in relation to geological field work.

Existence of trust

The Tribunal finds that the Applicant's family trust was constituted on 14 March 1977. Although the Applicant may have considered that the trust was in operation and that he was employed by it prior to that date, it was not in existence until that date.

At the hearing attention was directed to the cheque drawn on 30 May 1977 and the possibility raised that the sum of $25 there withdrawn was repaid to the settlor thus negativing the trust. Having considered that the next day the amount of the settled sum was credited to the separate account at the ANZ Bank, the Tribunal considers the greater likelihood is that the cheque drawn on 30 May was the cheque deposited in credit on 31 May. It also reaches this view after consideration of the words on the cheque butt dated 30 May 1977 in which the reference to the settlor's name may be read, not only as transferee of the amount, but as an adjectival description of the sum of money being transferred out to the separate account. The Tribunal does not therefore accede to the argument of the Respondent that the Applicant has failed to establish the creation of the trust. In any event, in view of the conclusion reached on other matters, determination of this matter is not critical to resolution of the proceeding.

Relationship between Applicant and family trust

The Applicant received no remuneration from the family trust by way of wages for the work which he did in the 1977 income year, nor did he receive such in the 1978 or 1979 years of income although in these latter two years he benefited by way of distribution from the trust. There is a lack of contractual relationship between the Applicant and the family trust. The Applicant certainly held himself out as being associated with the trust, signing correspondence and invoices in the manner indicated. That is not, of itself, evidence of the legal character of a relationship by way of employment or sub-contract between him and the family trust. The Tribunal therefore follows case P45 (1982) 82 ATC 213 at 216 where it was said in relation to the operation of the same trustee company in relation to another trust, that if there had been some agreement to employ the taxpayer it would surely have been possible to indicate on what terms he was to be employed and that the inference to be drawn from the absence of evidence was obvious.

Unlike case P45, however, there is here evidence of the existence of an agency relationship. From 23 March 1977 the Applicant had the authority of the directors of the trust to run the trust's daily operations and from the previous day he had the sole authority to operate on the family trust account. He conducted trust business on letterhead identifying the trustee and the trust. The Tribunal considers the Applicant was the agent of the trustee, arrangements having been made whereby he was to carry out duties on behalf of it.

Derivation of income by Applicant

It was next argued on behalf of the Respondent that the trustee did not derive any of the income of the geological consulting business and that it should be deemed to have been derived by the Applicant in accordance with sub-section 25(1) of the Act.

It is apparent that any income from the geological consulting business derived prior to 14 March 1977 could not have been derived by the trustee which was not until that date constituted as trustee of the Applicant's family trust. The Applicant cannot succeed therefore in his objection relating to the 1977 year of income in respect of income derived prior to that date (the payment after that date not preventing the occurrence of derivation prior to that date - see section 19 of the Act).

Turning to the application of the Respondent's arguments to income earned during the balance of the 1977 year and the 1978 and 1979 years, the question to be decided is whether the Trust truly derived the income from the geological consulting services delivered by the Applicant in these years of income or whether the Applicant in providing those services truly derived the income himself. If the latter is the case the income will have been received by the Trust to which it was paid as a consequence of a direction from the Applicant as the person deriving it: cf Case G38 75 ATC 239. The existence of the agency relationship between the trust and the Applicant does not preclude the Applicant being found to have derived the income in question: Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Clarke [1927] HCA 49; (1927) 40 CLR 246 at 260-61 per Isaacs ACJ.

There is evidence which the Respondent argued pointed to the Applicant having derived the income personally: the Trustee had no capacity to provide geological services other than through the Applicant; the trustee could only offer skills appropriate to those of a trustee; there is no evidence the trustee was actively involved in geological business or advertising geological services; the trustee did not employ any staff (on one occasion when field assistants were required by the Applicant they were engaged by the contracting company); there was no telephone in the name of the trustee to enable it to carry on business; the directors of the trust were not signatories on the family trust account; the bank statement for that account went to the family trust at the Applicant's address which was also the address of the trust; the trust had no registered interest in the vehicle used by the Applicant which had been bought and insured in the Applicant's own name; the services that were sought by customers both before and after the establishment of the trust were those of the Applicant.

There is some similarity of the facts in this matter with those in Case R116 84 ATC 761 (see 763-5) namely: there was not at any relevant time a contractual arrangement between the trustee or the trust and the Applicant by which the Applicant was required to perform duties for the trustee or the trust as an employee or as a sub-contractor; the trustee had no employees; there was no minute in the trustee's minute book or other evidence which indicated a basis upon which the Applicant might be remunerated by it or that remuneration was in fact paid to him; there was no evidence that the consultancy business carried on by the Applicant prior to the establishment of the trust was at any time sold to or transferred to the trust; there was no real evidence (putting aside mere assertions by the taxpayer) that the trustee carried on business in the relevant period; there was no evidence that the trustee made decisions concerning the manner in which work under the various contracts would be carried out nor was there evidence to dispel a conclusion that the Applicant alone controlled all the activities and finances of the consultancy business, which produced the assessable income in issue, including the disposition of its receipts and authorisation for payment of expenses incurred subject to the exception that he sought approval for the purchase of the vehicle; the evidence overall indicates clearly that any contracts were entered into on the basis that the Applicant would be wholly and personally responsible for the proper and due performance of the work required to be undertaken; the Applicant's activities continued in the years in issue (as in past years) to be the only source from which the assessable income in issue for those years was derived; transfers of funds for use for private purposes were shown to have been made.

There is here, however, evidence not present in Case R116 to the following effect namely: the Applicant was constituted as agent for the trust; he signed correspondence and invoices on behalf of and in the name of the trust and trustee; company B accepted that it was engaging the services of the Applicant through an engagement concluded with the trustee of his family trust; remuneration from company B and other companies referred to was made payable to the trustee of the family trust and paid into the family trust account; there was evidence that protective clothes used by the Applicant were paid for by the trust and that vehicle expenses were claimed as deductions by it.

One factor present in Case R116 (supra) is not present here namely: there is evidence to support the proposition that company B and the other companies referred to were aware that they were in fact dealing with the trustee company. Case P45 is also to be distinguished because there is here evidence that there was no lack of understanding of the plan devised for him by this Applicant and he did not bypass the trust or ignore it in the conduct of his operations after the date of the establishment of the trust. He did not conduct the business of geological consultancy just as he had done before the trust was created but instead insisted on dealings with the trust in terms of contracts concluded and payments made.

The reasons in Case R116 (supra) in paragraphs 13 and 14 make apparent that the question is whether this Tribunal is satisfied on the evidence that the trust was used for the purposes of carrying on the business in the years in issue for which the assessable income was derived. In the opinion of the Tribunal the essential distinction between the facts in this application and the facts in Case R116 and P45 is that the trust was used for the purpose of carrying on the business in those years despite the existence of a number of factors supporting the view that the Applicant did not derive the income. The Tribunal is satisfied the trust was being used for the purpose of carrying on the business from 14 March 1977 and that consequently the Applicant has discharged the burden upon him. The result is the Tribunal cannot accept the argument that the Applicant himself derived the income from that business from that date.

Doctrine of sham

It was next argued by the Respondent that the establishment of the trust was a sham viewing it in all the circumstances of the matter. The term "sham" has been considered in a number of cases: Snook v London and West Riding Investments Ltd (1967) 2 QB 786 at p 802 per Diplock LJ; Albion Hotel Pty Limited v The Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1965] HCA 4; (1965) 115 CLR 78 per Windeyer J at pp. 91-92; Arbuckle v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1964) 13 ATD 378 per Windeyer J at p 388; Coppleson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 81 ATC 4019 at pp 4021-4022; Jaques v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1923] HCA 70; (1924) 34 CLR 328 at p 358 per Isaacs J; Hancock v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1961] HCA 90; (1961) 108 CLR 258 per Windeyer J; Boydell v James (1936) 36 SR (NSW) 620 at 627; Perpetual Trustee Co (Ltd) v Bligh (1941) 41 SR (NSW) 33 at 39; Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland, Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1985] HCA 83; (1985) 62 ALR 545 at 580 per Dawson J. The question is whether the establishment of the trust was a sham in the sense of a transaction having apparent legal consequences but not intended by the parties to operate according to its tenor - something set up merely to dissemble (Arbuckle (supra, at p. 388). In Snook (supra, at p 802) Diplock LJ. said that for acts or documents to be a sham, with whatever legal consequences follow from this, all the parties thereto must have a common intention that the acts or documents are not to create the legal rights and obligations which they give the appearance of creating.

There is no evidence here that all the parties to the establishment of the trust shared a common intention of the requisite type or that all the parties did not intend it to operate according to its tenor. The evidence is that the Applicant indeed intended it to operate according to its tenor and took steps over a period of time to ensure that it did by concluding contracts in the name of the trust, receiving payments to the trust and issuing correspondence and invoices in the name of the trust. In the opinion of the Tribunal the Applicant succeeds in establishing that the trust was established with an intention to create the legal rights and obligations which it appeared to create. The consequence is that the doctrine of sham does not, in the view of the Tribunal, have application to these facts.

Application of section 260

It is contended on behalf of the Commissioner that the well known provisions of section 260 of the Act apply. In the opinion of the Tribunal this is correct and the application must be considered in accordance with the principles laid down by the Privy Council in Newton v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1958] UKPCHCA 1; (1958) 98 CLR 1 as recently affirmed and explained by the decisions of the High Court of Australia in Gulland, Watson and Pincus (supra).

It is first necessary to particularise the nature of the arrangement made or entered into by the Applicant. In the view of the Tribunal it was:

(i) the formation of the family trust;

(ii) the agreement or arrangement by the Applicant to become the agent of the trust and to deliver his professional services only on its behalf.

It is necessary to consider whether this arrangement has any of the stated purposes or effects referred to in sub-section 260(1).

The Applicant's evidence was that he was motivated generally to enter into the arrangement to provide himself protection against liability and specifically into use of his accountant's trustee company because it saved him money. The Applicant's diary having only become available during the hearing, he was not questioned in relation to the illegible words in the entry of 27 January 1977. In any event, section 260 makes no reference to the motives of the taxpayer (Gulland and Watson, supra, at p 551 per Gibbs CJ) and its application is not to be determined entirely by reference to those motives.

Furthermore, it is not necessary that any of the purposes or effects stated in sub-section 260(1) be the dominant or sole purpose or effect. The arrangement may have several purposes and effects. The question is whether the purpose or effect of the arrangement, ascertained from the terms of the arrangement itself and from the overt acts by which it was carried into effect, has the requisite purpose or effect.

In the view of the Tribunal the arrangement made by the Applicant, like those in Gulland, Watson and Pincus (supra) bears on its face an indication of a purpose to avoid tax even though the arrangement reveals other purposes. In the words of Kitto J in Peate v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1964) 111 CLR 443 at p. 469 cited in Gulland and Watson at p 553-4, the arrangement bears ex facie the stamp of tax-avoidance. It comes within the principle enunciated by Brennan J in Gulland and Watson (supra, at p 564) as follows:

"When a taxpayer, prior to making an arrangement caught by s 260, has been in receipt of assessable income from a particular source and he would have continued to be in receipt of assessable income from that source but for the effect of the arrangement, the taxpayer is assessed to tax after the arrangement takes effect on the footing that he has continued in receipt of assessable income from that source."

In this instance the purpose is the effect and no distinction is to be drawn between them. The arrangement clearly altered the incidence of tax and relieved the Applicant from liability.

In deciding whether the arrangement can be taken beyond the operation of section 260 the Tribunal must consider if it was fairly referable to ordinary business or family dealing; if the avoidance of tax was merely incidental; or if the Applicant was taking advantage of a specific and particular provision of the Act. In the view of the Tribunal none of these possibilities is applicable here. The arrangement is not fairly referable to ordinary business or family dealing; the proper inference in the arrangement is that it was made for the purpose of avoiding tax so that the objective is not merely incidental; and none of the arrangements attract a specific provision of the Act (the fact that the company might be taxed under Division 7 and the trust under Division 6 not entitling the Applicant to rely on this principle of choice - Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4851). In particular we do not consider the Applicant's evidence of what he was told in the Mining Club establishes that arrangements such as the one here were "normal and regular" (Gulland, Watson and Pincus, supra, at 555 per Gibbs CJ).

The distinction in the authorities between an arrangement which modifies an antecedent transaction or situation and one which merely orders a taxpayer's affairs in relation to income from a new source or in relation to a deduction from which the previous transaction or situation did not preclude him, is of no assistance in this proceeding. Here if there was no antecedent transaction, there was an antecedent situation consisting of a taxpayer's practise of his profession in a particular manner. The arrangement changed that situation and did so in order to alter the incidence of income tax (cf Gulland and Watson, supra, per Dawson J at p 585). It is a case where the only relevant antecedent circumstance is that the taxpayer is in receipt of income (Gulland and Watson (supra, per Gibbs CJ at p 557).

The arrangement having the purpose or effect required by sub-section 260(1), that sub-section annihilates the arrangement so that the Applicant is liable to tax if the actual facts thus left exposed are such as to render him liable. The family trust being annihilated it is apparent the taxpayer is left in the situation in which he is in receipt of the income from the application of his professional skills. The result is that the income so received is his income.

Conclusion

For the reasons given the Tribunal will affirm the objection decisions under review.
