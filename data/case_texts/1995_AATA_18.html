DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION



INCOME TAX - Deductibility of expenses relating to subdivision - applicants unable to substantiate expenses.



Tax Administration Act 1953 Section 14 ZZK

Tribunal : Bruce Barbour, Senior Member

WHEREAS:



  1. The Tribunal made a decision in this matter dated 3 February 1995, pursuant to section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975;

  2. It has come to the Tribunal's attention that there were errors in the identification of the trustee company on the second line of paragraph 5, page 2 of the written reasons, in that the company is incorrectly identified as “M & P Leasing Co”, and not “M & P Co”;

  1. The Tribunal desires to amend the decision so as to rectify the error and wishing to do so with the least cost and inconvenience to the parties, applies the slip rule referred to in the case of Milson -v- Carter (1893) AC 638 and applied in the Federal Court of Australia pursuant to Order 35 Rule 7 of the Federal Court Rules;

NOW THE TRIBUNAL THEREFORE orders:



(i) That the first sentence of paragraph 5 of page 2 be amended to read: " For the tax years under review, the respondent distributed the income received by M & P Co as trustee, from the sale of the subdivided properties, to MB and PB as the beneficiaries of the trust.”.

) NT94/6 & 7

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:

Applicants



And: Commissioner of Taxation

Respondent



Tribunal: Mr B A Barbour, Senior Member



Date: 3 February 1995

Place: Sydney

Reasons for Decision



  1. These are applications for review of objection decisions of the respondent relating to Mr B (MB) and Mrs B (PB), for the tax years ending 30 June 1982, 1983, 1984, 1986, and 1987. The matter was heard before me on 12 January 1995. In order to preserve the confidentiality of the applicants, their names and the names of their associated companies have been made anonymous.
  2. The Tribunal had before it documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (T documents). MB attended the hearing on behalf of himself and his wife, PB, who advised the Tribunal by telephone that her husband was authorised to act on her behalf. MB tendered a folder of documents prepared by his accountant, Mr Lynch. The respondent, represented by Mr Gibb of counsel, tendered tax returns of M & P Co for the years 1981 to 1987. MB gave oral evidence to the Tribunal.
  3. I was concerned at the outset of the hearing that MB was unrepresented, that he did not appear to understand fully the nature of the transactions on which he was giving evidence, and that he clearly wanted to put forward submissions which suggested a casual relationship between M & P Co and the other two companies (Blackacre Investments Co and M & P Leasing Co) such that funds expended by them should be properly attributable to M & P Co. Because of the restricted nature of the matters before me I encouraged MB to enter into confidential negotiations with the Commissioner with a view to allowing him to discuss a settlement which might be able to take into account matters not before the Tribunal but which relate to the other companies of which MB was concerned. However, despite time being given for that purpose, no solution or resolution was able to be reached between the parties.
  4. The facts of the application are set out briefly in the respondent's Statement of Facts and Contentions:
    • Prior to 2 July 1980, Blackacre was owned by the applicant MB. The applicants MB and PB resided at Blackacre.
    • On or about 2 July 1980 MB agreed to transfer Blackacre to M & P Co, that company subsequently being registered as proprietor of Blackacre.
    • On or about 2 July 1980 the applicants, as directors of M & P Co, met and resolved that the company hold Blackacre in trust for the applicants.
    • In the period from 1 July 1981 to 30 June 1987 (‘the relevant period’) Blackacre was subdivided and the subdivided blocks of land (‘the blocks’) were developed by persons including Blackacre Investments Co, M & P Leasing Co and MB.
    • The applicants resided at Blackacre during the relevant period.
    • Blocks were sold progressively over the period by M & P Co.
  5. For the tax years under review, the respondent distributed the income received by M & P Leasing Co as trustee, from the sale of the subdivided properties, to MB and PB as the beneficiaries of the trust. The applicants had conceded through their representatives (letter to respondent dated 18 May 1994) that amounts received from the sales are assessable in the years of income. The trustee received $51 500 in 1982, $71 200 in 1983, $6000 in 1984, $83 950 in 1986 and $45 000 in 1987. Further amounts of $200 in 1982, $220 in 1983 and $633 in 1987 were assessed as income, and none of these amounts were placed in issue before me.
  6. The applicants argued that all costs associated with the subdivision should be allowed as against that income, and principally in the following areas: the cost of the land; the cost of subdividing the land; the cost of building a ‘spec’ house on the land.
  7. Prior to considering the evidence on each of these matters, I would indicate that I find MB to be a generally honest man, who appears to be involved in a number of complex negotiations relating to the subdivision of his land. I found that his memory is not good in relation to events that happened some time ago and that he does not clearly understand the complexities and interrelationships of the dealings relating to the subdivision of Blackacre.

Transfer of land

  1. The respondent allowed, as against the proceeds of sale, the cost of the land at Blackacre of $36 000, the primary support for this being the Australian Valuation Office (AVO) report of May 1989. That report values the land (3.713 hectares) at $36 000, and the improvements at $100 000, as at 1 July 1980. The respondent did not allow the house improvement, as the applicants still live in that house, and it has not been sold. The value of the land was divided amongst each of the subdivided blocks (amounting to $1200 a block).
  2. Before me, MB contended that the value of Blackacre, when transferred to M & P Co, was $220 000. He referred me to a photostat copy Valuation record of the Tamworth City Council, used for rating purposes, which included a sticker that indicated the value of Blackacre as $220 000. MB also noted that he had purchased the land in 1970 for $20 000, and had constructed a house and other improvements upon it.
  3. I am unable to accept the claims of MB. Section 14ZZK places upon him the onus of proving the respondent's assessment excessive, or, on this point, that a greater deduction should be allowed. MB was not able to provide convincing supporting evidence (such as an independent valuation) to confirm the alleged sale or transfer of the land for $220 000.
  4. He could point me to no agreement to show that M & P Co had paid him $220 000 for the property, or that he had provided to that company the funds for such a purpose by way of a loan. In fact, no records of any loan transaction could be produced. There is no mention in the transfer document, or the minutes of the meeting of M & P Co that occurred on the same day as the transfer, and relate to the Blackacre purchase, as regards the sum concerned. Nor is their any independent recollection by MB of that company meeting or of the formalities of the transfer.
  5. In short, there is no documentary or other evidence which supports any amount as being paid or recorded in the financial dealings of any of the companies for the transfer of the property. The reference to the sticker on the council records is insufficient for me to prefer it to the valuation report, and the date of that information does not accord with the date of the transfer.
  6. A report of B C Sharrock values the house and land (1600 sq m) at $95 000 in 1980, and this is not substantially different to, although less generous than, the AVO’s valuation of the improvements alone at $100 000. There is no valuation in the Sharrock report for the remainder of the land that was subdivided.
  7. In relation to the transfer of the land, MB has included in his valuation the value of the residence on that land, which he and his wife have retained. Notwithstanding that the land was transferred to M & P Co, there has never been any sale of the house, and MB and PB have remained in residence there. Given the method of calculation of the respondent, that is, to offset from each sale the value of the block of land as assessed by the AVO, and given that this house was never sold, it cannot form part of the amount determined as value for the transfer of the land, for the purposes of the subdivision. Hence it is not necessary for me to prefer either the AVO or the Sharrock valuation.

Subdivision Costs

  1. The respondent initially allowed the applicant the sum of $13 967, or $466 a block, for the costs of development. Further amounts of $2020 in 1982, $3030 in 1983, $506 in 1984, $3215 in 1986 and $4060 in 1987 were allowed after the initial objection of the applicants. MB submitted that the development costs were significantly more than this.
  2. A careful examination of all of the material provided, most specifically exhibit A, does not show any direct link between any of the monies expended in relation to the subdivision and M & P Co. All items which were listed in what MB said were ledgers appear to relate to amounts owing and/or paid by Blackacre Investments and/or M & P Leasing Co, the only entities which appear to have direct day to day responsibilities in the development of the subdivision. MB was unable to direct me to any specific entry where the expense incurred was attributable to M & P Co.
  3. The material in exhibit A does not establish a link between the payments made to persons for the purpose of the subdivision, and M & P Co, and hence there can be no deduction to the company in respect of those payments.

‘Spec House’

  1. The respondent did not allow any amounts in respect of a house that was built on one of the subdivided blocks, in the name of PB. No costs were allowed in respect of this house as the respondent stated that there were no records produced to substantiate the outlay. The applicant claimed $35 000 as a deduction.
  2. However, there is no evidence that MB was able to provide during the course of the hearing which showed any relationship between M & P Co and the spec house, built for the purposes of advertising and selling the subdivision. The only expenses nominated by MB in oral evidence as relating to that house are shown as being paid by M & P Leasing Co through its own cheque accounts.

Conclusion

  1. One of the difficulties for the applicant in this matter was that corporate entities other than M & P Co were involved in the subdivision, and from the material before me, it appears that they primarily bore the costs involved. The complex corporate structure, and the inadequacy of records, have meant that deductions that may have been properly allowed to M & P Co cannot be claimed, as there is no proof of deductable expenses being incurred.
  2. I therefore affirm the objection decisions under review.
