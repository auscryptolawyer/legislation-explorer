REASONS FOR DECISION



1. These are applications for review of objection decisions of 7 September 1993, wherein the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (the Commissioner) disallowed the applicants' objections in full.



2. The evidence before the Tribunal comprised the documents filed pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, (the T documents) together with exhibits. The applicant, who gave oral evidence, was represented by Mr M Martin of Counsel. Mr PE Hack of Counsel represented the respondent.



3. On 13 January 1993 Notices of Amended Assessment of income tax was issued to the applicants. An Amended Assessment increased the male applicant's taxable income for the year ended 30 June 1989 by $93,000 to $114,668 and imposed additional tax of $33,618.42 for incorrect return. The other Amended Assessment increased the female applicant's taxable income for the same year by $57,000 to $73,126 and imposed additional tax of $19,619.61 for incorrect return.



4. By Notices of Objection dated 10 March 1993 the applicants lodged written objections against the Amended Assessment and claimed that the Amended Assessment should be reduced by:



(a) the excision of the sum of $93,000 and $57,000 respectively (or some part thereof) from their respective assessable incomes;



(b) the remission of the sum of $33,618.42 and $19,619.61 (or some part thereof) respectively termed additional tax for incorrect returns in the notices of amended assessments.



5. The Notices of Objection were made pursuant to the provisions of s14ZU of the Taxation Administration Act 1953, (hereinafter referred to as "the Act"). By written Notices of Decision dated 7 September 1993, the Commissioner decided to disallow the applicants' objections in full. Each decision of the Commissioner is a "reviewable objection decision" and an "appealable objection decision" as those terms are respectively defined in s14ZQ of the Act. The applicants applied to this Tribunal for review of the decisions pursuant to s14ZZ (a) (i) of the Act, but for the sake of convenience we will refer in these reasons to the male applicant as "the applicant".



6. The applicant will succeed in this application if he satisfies s14ZZK of the Act, which as far as is relevant for the purposes of this review provides:



" 14ZZK. On an application for review of a reviewable objection decision:



(a) the applicant is, unless the AAT orders otherwise, limited to the grounds stated in the taxation objection to which the decision relates; and



(b) the applicant has the burden of proving that:



(i) if the taxation decision concerned is an assessment .......

- the assessment is excessive; or .. ; or

(ii) ...



(iii) in any other case - the taxation decision concerned should not have been made or should have been made differently.



7. The grounds stated in the taxation objection to which the decision relates, and repeated as grounds of appeal supporting the Application for Review, are as follows:



"GENERAL GROUNDS



A. The amended assessment is invalid in that it was made arbitrarily and in an improper exercise of the power to amend an assessment vested in the Commissioner of Taxation under s. 170 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("ITAA").



Particulars



The amended assessment was issued for a collateral purpose namely to assist the Commonwealth of Australia in litigation in the Federal Court of Australia between the Commonwealth and A Company (in which the taxpayer and the taxpayer's spouse are sole shareholders and directors) in relation to amounts said to be recoverable by the Commonwealth pursuant to purported assignments allegedly granted to it by students to whom the Overseas Students (Refunds) Act allegedly applies.



B. The provisions of the ITAA, in particular Part IIIA thereof, which, when incorporated and read as one with the Income Tax Act 1986, would have the effect of imposing a tax upon capital gains, as opposed to income, are of no effect in that when so incorporated and read they constitute laws imposing taxation which deal with more than one subject of taxation, contrary to s. 55 of the Commonwealth Constitution.



PARTICULAR GROUNDS



Further or alternatively;



(i) Neither the amount of $93,000 (nor any part thereof) by which the taxpayer's taxable income was increased in the Amended Assessment constitutes:

(a) income under s. 25(1), ITAA;

(b) a royalty under s. 26(f), ITAA;

(c) income under any other provision of the ITAA.



(ii) Properly characterised, the credits totalling $97,000 in entries in the loan account of the taxpayer in the Annual General Ledger of the business known as B for the year ended 30 June, 1989 are, in the hands of the taxpayer, accruals of capital not income and represent the value of intellectual property rights and/or know how disposed of by the taxpayer to B.



(iii) A designation of amounts totalling $93,000 as goodwill or estimated goodwill in the Annual General Ledger of B for the 1989 income year is not determinative of the character of those amounts in relation to the taxpayer for the purposes of the ITAA.



(iv) No part of the sum of $93,000 constitutes a net capital gain under Part IIIA, ITAA in that such intellectual property rights and/or know how as were disposed of by the taxpayer to B constitute assets acquired by the taxpayer prior to 20 September, 1985.



(v) Alternatively, insofar as some or all of the sum of $93,000 constitutes an accrual of capital in respect of the disposal of know how to B by the taxpayer, know how does not constitute an "asset" for the purposes of Part IIIA, ITAA and no income tax is payable upon its disposal.



(vi) Further or alternatively, the net capital gain which accrued to the taxpayer was NIL or some lesser sum than $93,000 having regard to the indexed cost base of such intellectual property and/or know how calculated in accordance with s. 160ZH, ITAA.



(vii) Further or alternatively, insofar as any portion of the intellectual property rights and/or know how has been improved after its acquisition by the taxpayer and such improvement occurred on or after 20 September, 1985 (none of which is admitted), that improvement does not constitute a separate asset for the purposes of Part IIIA under s. 160P, ITAA as its indexed cost base would not exceed $50,000 (indexed in accordance with s. 160Q, ITAA).



(viii) Further or alternatively, insofar as the sum of $93,000 or any part thereof represents a disposal of goodwill:



(a) that sum represents a disposal of an interest in a business having a worth less than $1,000,000 and the Commissioner should have reduced the amount of any capital gain by one-fifth pursuant to s. 160 ZZR, llAA; and/or



(b) the net capital gain which accrued to the taxpayer was NIL or some lesser sum than $93,000 having regard to the assets indexed cost base calculated in accordance with s160ZH, ITAA.



(ix) Further or alternatively, no part of the said sum of $93,000 constitutes assessable income under s. 124P, ITAA.



(x) Further or alternatively, no part of the said sum of $93,000 constitutes assessable income under any other provision of ITAA.



(xi) As a penal provision, s. 223, ITAA requires the presence of mens rea before it operates to impose additional tax.



(xii) The taxpayer did not omit any assessable income from the taxpayer's return or, insofar as any assessable income was omitted (which is not admitted), such omission was not made knowingly by the taxpayer.



(xiii) Further, the taxpayer has neither made any statement to a taxation officer that was false or misleading nor omitted from any statement to a taxation officer any matter or thing without which the statement is misleading or if any such statement or omission was made (which is not admitted);



(a) it was not made knowingly; and/or



(b) it was not false or misleading in any material particular (having regard to other information otherwise in the Commissioner's possession concerning B at the time of the issue of the taxpayer's 1989 assessment).



(xiv) Further, no part of the amount of $93,000 relates to the entering into or carrying out of a tax avoidance scheme within the meaning of Part IVA, ITAA either for the sole or dominant purpose of enabling a person to pay no tax or less tax or at all.



(xv) Further, the amount of additional tax imposed under the ITAA should be remitted in full or alternatively, to a sum less than $33,618.42."



8. The applicant says that he and his wife compiled extensive material intended for use in teaching courses in early childhood education, and child care. They began work in preparation for, and in respect of this material, in the year 1980. He says that they completed the work towards the end of 1984. The applicant says further that the material was written down initially but at a very early stage he and his wife realised "the massive task it was going to be" and placed the material on to computer discs. The hard copy of the material being 28 volumes of written material were admitted into evidence as Exhibit 4. The applicant gave evidence that he and his wife worked almost exclusively together. "We'd attend the libraries together, we'd search all the indexes, we'd look at material together, almost look - obviously there were times when I'd go and collect and photocopy articles and various materials, but predominantly we worked together whether it was at the library or at home." He says that they each worked on average, 30 hours per week in the preparation of the materials.



9. The applicant says that he was an officer of the State Public Service from 1980 to 1987, employed mostly if not wholly on a full time basis. He was a clerk initially, and by 1987 when he resigned from the Public Service, he was a research officer. He holds a Bachelor of Arts from the University of Queensland, majoring in psychology and anthropology. There was no teaching component in his Degree.



10. He says that his wife has a Bachelor of Educational Studies, a Diploma of Teaching, a certificate of Special Education, and a graduate Diploma of Special Education.



11. The teaching material was completed by the end of 1984, and the applicant and his wife decided to establish a business, offering initially a nanny course and a governess course; and subsequently a child care assistant course. The applicant said in evidence that they had the intention also to provide remedial courses for children with learning difficulties as his wife had the remedial and special education qualifications. The business was called C. It was owned and operated by the applicant and his wife and courses commenced in January 1986.



12. On 7 January 1988 the company A was incorporated. The applicant and his wife were the only directors and shareholders; and the company replaced the applicant as the trustee of his family trust and took over the operation of the business formerly run by the applicant and the wife under the business name of the business C. A began to use the syllabus or written materials which had been previously used by the applicant and his wife in the running of their business C.



13. The applicant contends that he and his wife subsequently charged the company a fee for what he regarded as an exclusive licence to use the syllabus. The fee charged was $150,000. When questioned in examination-in-chief as to how he arrived at that figure, the applicant replied:



"We took a fairly simple approach to it. We figured, we calculated the time that we commenced it and the time we completed it which was in approximately five years. We were spending each about 30 hours a week on it and calculated from that, and simply charged ourselves a figure of $10 an hour was reasonable. We actually - I mean, we didn't consider that to be the true value, we always considered it to be a lot higher than that, but it was a nominal value that we placed on it." [Transcript p.13]



He said in evidence also that he believed that he discussed the valuation with his accountant; but the evidence discloses that his accountant did not become the company's accountant until June 1989 and the applicant's recollection is not correct.



14. The applicant says that this licence arrangement with A was formalised on 1 May 1989 by a written document entitled Meeting and Record of Resolution by the applicant and his wife. [T 14/84-85] wherein it was resolved to offer the licence to A for a sale price of $150,000. A second document bearing that same date, and entitled Meeting of Directors of A is, on the applicant's evidence a true record of the Meeting which took place on 1 May 1989 accepting the applicant and his wife's offer to sell the licence. We shall describe these documents as "the Offer/Acceptance 1 May 1989". The applicant gave evidence that the originals of these documents were created and typed up on 1 May 1989 and that he had no professional advice as to the drafting and drawing up of the documents. He said also that at no time did he show these documents to his accountant.



15. In June 1989 the applicant engaged a new firm of accountants. An accountant of this firm gave oral evidence that shortly before 6 June 1989 he commenced to act as accountant for A, and other companies in which the applicant and his wife were involved. In July 1990 he wrote up the Journal of the applicant's family trust, of which A was trustee, in relation to the preparation of the 1989 accounts. A copy of the relevant page of the journal appears at T6/26. We accept the accountant's evidence that this page was written up in July 1990 following meetings between himself and the applicant.



The relevant entries are as follows:



"..........................................................................................................................................

............................................................................................................................................

Goodwill (NEW ACCT) 853 85000

Loan - applicant 87001 53,000

- applicant's wife 87002 32,000



(Being applicant's estimated value of Goodwill brought into the business by each individual)

[stamp 'Posted' 11.7.90]



Goodwill 853 6500.00



Loan - applicant 87001 40,000

- applicant's wife 87002 25,000



(Being adjusted to goodwill figure of $150,000 per applicant's advice)

[stamp 'Posted' 17.7.90]"



16. The accountant said in evidence that at these meetings he and the applicant discussed amounts which had been withdrawn from the bank accounts of the trust "and it was a matter of trying to decide what was the nature of these amounts that had been withdrawn."



"We discussed a number of alternatives as to whether they could be - as to what the nature of these drawings could be, whether they are in the nature of salaries or loans or expense payments or for things that (the applicant said his wife) may well have contributed to that business - to the trust - by themselves prior to the formation of the trust." [Transcript p114]



17. The accountant said also that he recalled the applicant saying that the drawings (which totalled $150,000) certainly were not in the nature of loans to himself and his wife, they did not appear to be in the nature of salaries, and there was no evidence of expense payments "...and it really came back to a conversation about what they'd contributed to the business by themselves before the business was put into the trust." [Transcript p115] The accountant said in evidence that the applicant told him that there was a lot of effort put in by himself and his wife into developing systems, processes, procedures over a period of time and, in the applicant's opinion this had some value to the trust, which should be recognised.



18. The distribution of the $150,000 between the applicant and his wife as shown in the journal entries was $93,000 to the applicant and $57,000 to his wife. The applicant when asked by his Counsel to account for this apportionment stated that it came about simply because he had done more work. The accountant said that he recalled that the apportionment bore some relation to the drawings that had been apportioned between them, or taken between them. The accountant is here referring to the size of the respective loan accounts of the applicant and the wife in the trust in 1989 when the sums of $93,000 and $57,000 were respectively credited against those loan accounts.



19. The accountant gave evidence that he did not have any discussions with the applicant regarding the journal book entries until June or July 1990; that he had discussed with the applicant the manner in which the work he had put into preparation of material was to be valued, and had told the applicant that he might be required at some time in the future to prove that the values were correct and could be substantiated. The accountant continued to act for A until December 1992, and said in evidence that at no time whilst he was accountant for the company, did he see documents or copies of the documents allegedly created by the applicant on 1 May 1989, and being the offer and acceptance of the licence.



20. Counsel for the applicant declined to cross-examine the accountant at the Hearing. His evidence is not controverted. We accept the accountant as a witness of truth.



21. The applicant's 1989 income tax return did not include the said sum of $93,000 as income for that financial year, and nor did the applicant's wife's 1989 return include the said sum of $57,000 as income for that year.



22. The solicitor who has acted for the applicant and his wife, and various companies which they had formed including A, since June 1989, was called by the respondent. A is now in liquidation and we were informed that the liquidator of the company had waived solicitor/client privilege.



23. The solicitor gave evidence that he did not see the Offer/Acceptance 1 May 1989 document until the public examination into A's affairs conducted in the Magistrates Court in August 1993; none of these documents were shown to him while he was acting for the company. The solicitor was questioned as to documentation.



"Do you recall ever discussing with the directors of the company whether there were documents which supported the goodwill payment of $150,000? - - - I recall discussions of the payment of $150,000, and what it was for. I don't recall any documentation relating to the payment of $150,000.



Was there reference to documentation - there being documentation? - - Not of which I'm aware." [Transcript p106]



24. The solicitor said in evidence that as at June 1992 he had adopted a particular style in the recording and presentation of Company Minutes. An example of this style appears in the T documents at T8/29-30, being a copy of the minutes of a meeting of the directors of A (the applicant and his wife) on 15 June 1992, as prepared by the solicitor. The style is distinctive, and resembles closely the style of the Offer/Acceptance 1 May 1989 purportedly prepared by the applicant more than one month before the solicitor commenced to act for the applicant, his wife, and their companies.



25. Counsel for the applicant declined to cross-examine the Solicitor. His evidence is uncontested and we perceive no valid reasons not to rely upon his testimony. We accept his evidence.



26. The documentary evidence discloses that the company A went into voluntary liquidation in March 1993 following a resolution by the directors that the Company was unable to pay its debts. The applicant completed and signed a "Questionnaire for Directors and Officers" dated 6 April 1993. [T12/79-82] At page 80 the following questions and answers appear:



”How often were directors' meetings held in the three years prior to liquidation ?



REGULAR MEETINGS.



Where were these meetings held ? Who attended ? VARIOUS PLACES. wife (named) & husband (named).



Were minutes of these meetings kept? If so, by whom?



NOT KEPT GENERALLY BUT ANY KEPT WHERE [sic] WITH COMPANY RECORDS.



Where is the minute book now located ?



UNKNOWN"



and at page 81 the following appears:



"By whom were the books and records kept? (Show names and periods and indicate whether the persons were employees.)



MYSELF AND ACCOUNTANTS.



What steps did you take to ensure that the books and records were properly kept?



EVERY STEP TO KEEP PROPERLY.



Do you at present have any of the books and records in your possession ?



NO. "



27. The applicant agreed in evidence that the minutes of 1 May 1989 were located by him some time between the signing of the questionnaire on 6 April 1993 and their production to the Australian Taxation Office (the ATO) on 11 May 1993. The following appears in the Transcript of the applicant's cross-examination at page 45:



"Where were those minutes when you completed this questionnaire? - - Well, I didn't know where they were. We'd shift locations, you know, of the colleges and so on and I subsequently found those. They had been put in with the original certificates or registration of the C I didn't have a minute book as such. We never, sort of, generally kept minutes.



So - - -?---It didn't like - you know, a little minute book.



Somewhere between 6 April 1993 and 11 May 1993, when they were produced at the Tax Office, these two minutes just were found?---it wasn't only those two minutes. There was a whole lot of other original documents, and original certificates that came - -



All right. Well, these two minutes and all the other original documents. What other original documents were there?- - -I can't remember now. There were some other minutes of another company, I think. I can't remember now. There were some other original certificates. They had been filed under the - in another place with the original certificates of the C "



28. The applicant said in evidence, that as at April - May 1993, he was somewhat concerned about the issue of goodwill "being the term used in the journal book of the company". He was aware in April 1993, that the ATO was very interested to find out what the $150,000 was all about; and had discussed those concerns with the accountant. The following appears in the Transcript of the applicant's cross-examination at pages 46-47.:



"At the time that you filled out this questionnaire you must surely have had in the forefront of your mind the fact that there are other documents, minutes of the company, which evidenced this transaction which you know the Tax Office is interested in and yet you appear not to have referred to it. Is there any reason for that?- -Yes, there is a reason. Through that whole period I was under a tremendous amount of stress for various reasons, which you're aware of, and---"



...



"All right you say you were under stress?--- A lot of stress there, a tremendous amount of stress there, and I answered as truthfully as I could and I gave everything that I had that I was aware of. I went up personally on my own to see the liquidator and told them - he said anything - if you come across anything else please give it to them and that's what I did.



Right, thank you. But the fact remains, though, that these documents were not produced to the Tax Office until a month or so later?--- We first gave it to them on - at that meeting in C's office whenever it was - May '93. Neither the solicitor or the accountant ever asked me for any minutes, or anything like that."



29. We found the applicant's evidence on this topic unconvincing.



30. The company A ceased to offer courses after 31 December 1992. Another company of which the applicant and his wife were the only shareholders and directors, C, then used the material to offer the courses. Later when company C was wound up also, the company D of which the applicant's son and father-in-law are director and shareholders began to offer the courses and use the material. This remains the position. The applicant is an employee of this latter company.



31. It is a condition of the purported grant of lease, as recorded in the minutes of 1 May 1989, that the licence is revoked automatically if the "licensee becomes insolvent, and/or the courses are anyway subject to adverse reputation". The applicant said in evidence that he had no thoughts at that time that the company might become insolvent and when questioned as to why such conditions were included, he said:



"Why? Well, you know, I inquired about other licence agreements. I looked through a whole series of different licence agreements and particular things - there was software, computer software, which we had on hand, just - lots of different clauses and that and there were that seemed to be - the essence of it seemed to be that you give a licence and not ownership so much as - for a certain time." [Transcript p55]



...



"Now, you say that the idea of a licence revocable in the event of insolvency or adverse reputation came to you from looking at similar licence agreements for software?---ln part. I can 't remember.



In part, could you nominate one software licence which terminates on insolvency?---No, I can't.



I mean, Word Perfect, those sorts of things?---No, I can't - I can't. I don't know -



What was it that you felt was beneficial to you and your wife to have a licence which determined in the event that the recipient- - - ?--Okay, okay--



- - - become insolvent - - ?---1'11 tell you. Our lease agreement that we had on the property there. I - I remember looking at - at the lease agreement. I thought 'Well, this is sort of - sort of similar in those terms.' I thought well - and somewhere there, I think, in the lease agreement they say that if the company becomes insolvent the lease is cancelled or whatever, and I wanted to use the same sort of model here. I think I - that's - I think the lease - our lease agreements say it or did at that time, anyway.



So, you equated the licence to use intellectual property with the lease of real property. Is that what you are saying?---No, well - no I didn't. No, I didn't say that. I simply looked at that licence to use property, which is a lease, that's how I - correctly or incorrectly - and they had that sort of facility in there." [Transcript pages 56-57]



32. We found the applicant's evidence on this topic unconvincing.



33. It is significant in our view that nowhere in the extensive and carefully and comprehensively drawn Notice of Objection, dated 10 March 1993, is there any reference to the Minutes of 1 May 1989 to the existence of any informal arrangement having existed prior to 1 May 1989, in relation to the use of any written material by the company. There is no reference to the material having been used by the company under licence. The Minutes of 1 May 1989 were produced to the ATO on 11 May 1993. The ATO auditor said in evidence that prior to 11 May 1993 he had requested documentation relating to the $150,000 transaction. We accept this evidence.



34. We found the applicant a most unimpressive witness. His evidence was contrived and evasive; the inconsistencies were legion and we were forced to the conclusion that he prevaricated as he gave his oral evidence.



35. We did not hear evidence from the applicant's wife, who according to the certificate from the doctor, has significant health problems which render her unable to attend the Hearing.



36. The applicant submits that the payment of $150,000 was a licence fee, that there was an agreement of an informal nature in 1987 when the company was incorporated and that this agreement was formalised on 1 May 1989. The work created by the applicant and his wife is industrial property in accordance with s124K of the Act because it contains rights which are possessed by the taxpayers which entitle them to ownership of copyright. The syllabus was created before 1985; the licensing of the industrial property constitutes of disposal of part of the asset. The licence was exclusive, the payment was reasonable in the circumstances, and falls squarely within s124V of the Act as a capital payment which does not attract capital gains tax.



37. The Commissioner argues that the payment had nothing to do with a licence fee. It was to compensate the applicant and his wife for their efforts in the preparation of material. It was paid in to the applicant and his wife's respective loan accounts; the payments of $93,000 and $57,000 are income from personal exertion representing income under ordinary concepts. The purported licence is not an exclusive licence, and if the document, (the Offer/Acceptance 1 May 1989) were to be accepted as genuine, it records a payment made in consideration of a grant of a non-exclusive licence; such a payment is to be assessed as a revenue receipt. The Commissioner, in any event, submits the document did not come into existence at the time the applicant has sworn that it did. The applicant has not established a factual foundation upon which the Tribunal could rely, and he has not satisfied the onus of proof set out in s14ZZK of the Act. The Commissioner submits also that s124V of the Act is not relevant for the purposes of this review, and the issue to be determined is whether the payment received is assessable income in accordance with s25 of the Act.



38. We have set out the parties submissions in a brief and general way. The summary is by no means a complete account of their respective submissions, all aspects of which we have taken into account.



39. We have examined the whole of the evidence carefully and in detail, and we are satisfied on the evidence that there was no formal or informal licence agreement in existence between the applicant and his wife and the Company at any relevant time. We are satisfied also that the document (Offer/Acceptance 1 May 1989) came into existence some time subsequent to 6 April 1993.



40. We accept the Commissioner's submission that s124V of the Act is not relevant to this matter. We consider that the applicant has not provided a proper factual foundation upon which we can act safely in relation to the nature of the transactions between the applicant and his wife, on the one hand, and the company on the other, in relation to the company's use of the written material. In all the circumstances the applicant has not discharged the onus of proof as provided in s.14ZZK of the Act.



41. For these reasons the Tribunal affirms the decisions under review.
