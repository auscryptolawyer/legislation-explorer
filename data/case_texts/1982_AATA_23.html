Re James Hamilton and Commissioner of Superannuation [1982] AATA 23 (19 February 1982)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JAMES HAMILTON

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. V81/226

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mr J.O. Ballard (Senior Member)

Sir Ernest Coates (Member)

Dr M. Glick (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - invalidity retirement benefits determined under sub-s. 66(2) - application for review - whether obesity substantially contributed to incapacity

Superannuation Act 1976 ss. 66, 154 and 184.

HEARING

CANBERRA

19:2:1982

ORDER

(a) to set aside the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation made upon reconsideration pursuant to s. 154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") on 15 April 1981 (whereby he varied a decision taken on his behalf by his delegate to issue under s. 184(2) of the Act a benefit classification certificate and to determine that the benefit entitlement of the applicant should be determined under sub-s. 66(2) of the Act) and the same is so set aside; and

(b) in substitution therefor to decide that the applicant's entitlement should be determined under sub-s. 66(1) of the Act and the same is so decided.

DECISION

1. This is an application for review of a decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the respondent") under paragraph 66(2)(c) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act"). Sub-section 66(2) of the Act reads:

"(2) Subject to sub-sections (3) and (4) of this section and section 79, where -

(a) a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on

the ground of invalidity before attaining his maximum retiring age;

(b) there was in force in respect of the person, immediately before the person's retirement, a benefit classification certificate; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the certificate or by a physical or mental condition connected with such a condition or such conditions."

(The section then sets out limited invalidity benefits).

2. The applicant in this matter, James Hamilton, was formerly a postman with Australia Post. He joined the former Postmaster General's Department in 1974 and was accepted into the Provident Account. The reasons for acceptance to the Provident Account, as opposed to superannuation, were -

"(1) His blood pressure does not meet Superannuation standard but is

satisfactory for his age

(2) Overweight

(3) Vision (see Eye Report)."

3. The applicant was retired from Australia Post on the advice of a

Commonwealth Medical Officer, Dr. Hewat, on 25 July 1978 on the following grounds:

Cervical and lumbar spondylitis )

) 75%

Obesity 97 kg )

Alcoholism 5%

Persecution complex 20%.

4. It is not in issue that the applicant was involved in a motor vehicle

accident in 1976 as a result of which he suffered a whip-lash injury, that he hurt his back while bending at work on 8 May 1978 or that he has not worked since.

5. On 3 June 1980 a benefit classification certificate was issued under sub-s. 184(2) of the Act, as contained in the Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978, specifying the following relevant conditions:

Raised Blood Pressure Injury to Right Elbow

Overweight Anxiety State

Vision Defective

Cervical & Lumbar Spondylitis.

However, a reconsideration by the Commissioner for Superannuation under section 154 resulted in the deletion of the cervical and lumbar spondylitis, injury to the right elbow and anxiety state and on 15 April 1981 the Commissioner informed the applicant's solicitors accordingly stating:

"Having regard to the available medical evidence, I am of the opinion that the conditions "Cervical and lumbar spondylitis" and "Obesity", that conjointly contributed 75% toward the incapacity which was the ground for Mr. Hamilton's retirement, were connected with the condition "Overweight" a condition that was relevant for the purposes of the superseded Act and specified in the benefit classification certificate issued in respect of Mr. Hamilton, the issue of which, subject to the variation referred to paragraph 1 above, is herein confirmed. Mr. Hamilton's benefit entitlement therefore falls to be determined under sub-section 66(2) of the Act."

The applicant then sought a review of the Commissioner's decision under sub-s. 154(6).

6. It was argued before this Tribunal, for the applicant, that overweight, as a ground for retirement, did not cause or substantially contribute to the applicant's retirement or to a condition connected with such a condition; for that reason the applicant did not fall under sub-s. 66(2) of the Act. In the alternative the applicant argued that the certificate of Dr. Hewat, the Commonwealth Medical Officer, was wrong as a matter of fact in that the percentage incapacity due to a depressive anxiety state was a larger factor than he allowed for; plainly, on any view of the evidence the applicant's obesity was not relevant to the anxiety state.

7. This alternative argument may be dealt with shortly. The Tribunal heard the evidence of Dr. Cole, a psychiatrist called by the respondent, Dr. Syme and Dr. Kornan, psychiatrists called by the applicant, and intimated at the hearing that the facts would be found in accordance with the evidence of Dr. Kornan, who had seen the applicant in 1977 and again in 1981. On that evidence the Tribunal did not propose to disturb the finding of the Commonwealth Medical Officer that 20% of the applicant's incapacity at the time of his retirement was due to mental disease although we would prefer to accept Dr. Kornan's description of the mental disease as post traumatic anxiety state.

8. It is not argued for the respondent that the incapacity, which was the ground of the applicant's retirement, was caused by obesity nor is it argued that the incapacity was substantially contributed to by obesity. What is contended, as set out in the Commissioner's reconsideration, is that the Commonwealth Medical Officer was correct in linking the obesity and the spondylitis, that this was correctly assessed at 75% of the incapacity of the applicant and that, as such, obesity was a physical condition connected with the incapacity, which was the ground for the retirement for the purposes of paragraph 66(2)(c) of the Act.

9. The applicant gave evidence and called his general practitioner, Dr. Thomas, his treating orthopaedic surgeon, Mr. Beetham, and two psychiatrists, Dr. Syme and Dr. Kornan. The respondent called an orthopaedic surgeon, Mr. Gayton, Dr. McInnes a Senior Medical Officer, Dr. Hewat a general practitioner, who is a Commonwealth Medical Officer at Ballarat, and who recommended the applicant's retirement, and Dr. Cole a psychiatrist. The Tribunal called Mr. Sweet, Personnel Officer with Australia Post, who said that the applicant was in receipt of compensation under the Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees) Act 1971 ("the Compensation Act") for total incapacity for a spinal injury. Other medical reports were before the Tribunal pursuant to s. 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

10. Both Dr. Cole and Mr. Gayton saw the applicant for the purpose of a Medical Board, set up under section 58 of the Compensation Act. Dr. Cole was very emphatic as to the inappropriateness of such a board for the purpose of an adequate psychiatric examination. That was indeed a significant reason for this Tribunal deciding to prefer Dr. Kornan's evidence as to the applicant's psychiatric condition. In our view this also applies, with less force in relation to an orthopaedic examination than a psychiatric one, to the evidence of Mr. Gayton who was examining the applicant in the context of an entitlement to compensation; indeed it was not apparent until after Mr. Gayton had departed that his examination had been as a Member of such a Board and not in his rooms.

11. We were impressed with the evidence of the applicant's treating general practitioner, Dr. Thomas, and by that of the treating orthopaedic surgeon, Mr. Beetham. Dr. Thomas did not consider that overweight caused spondylitis nor did he think it contributed to it. In his view the applicant's overweight was minimally relevant. He did not feel that the applicant was grossly overweight. Mr. Beetham said:

"Did you consider that he received a soft tissue injury to the lumbar and cervical spine due to the accident or did you consider that there was an aggravation or acceleration of the cervical and lumbar spondylitis, or a combination of both? What did you find? - - - I considered in the car accident he suffered a typical soft tissue injury to his neck and lower back. This had been further aggravated in the course of his work, as far as the lower back was concerned, by a lifting incident in 1979. This soft tissue injury had resulted in him suffering from continuing low back

pain.

If he had not been involved in this motor car accident in February of 1976, would he have been able to continue working as a postal officer? . . . Yes.

MR BALLARD: Which injury was that?

MR RUDDLE: The motor car accident? . . . The motor car accident in 1976.

Did the motor car accident weaken his back so that when he was bending over in 1978 - - -

MR BALLARD: That is what is known as a leading question.

MR RUDDLE: When you saw him what was your view in relation to his weight. Did you consider he was overweight or obese or what? - - - No. I describe him as of big physique.

He was invalided out by a Dr. Hewat in early 1978 on the basis of cervical and lumbar spondylitis, alcoholism, and persecution complex. He put the cervical and lumbar spondylitis at 75 per cent and he conjoined that with obesity. In your view as an orthopaedic surgeon, do you consider that his weight caused cervical or lumbar spondylitis? . . . I do not consider that his weight caused his present conditions of cervical or lumbar spondylitis.

Did his weight substantially contribute to his lumbar and cervical spondylitis? . . . Having sustained the soft tissue injury which he did, the fact that he was a heavier build aggravates that condition, yes.

But if he did not have the motor car accident would his weight have substantially contributed to cervical and lumbar spondylitis? . . . No.

You have said that in your view he suffered soft tissue injury to the cervical and lumbar spine in the accident. Would his weight have any effect on that? . . . Not in causing it, no.

And not in contributing to it, substantially contributing to it? . . . That is correct."

12. The question is then whether the obesity is a physical condition

connected with the spondylitis which is, on any view of the evidence, a substantial contributing factor to the incapacity. The lumping of the spondylitis with the obesity flows from Dr. Hewat's report. He explains his reasons for this:

"Did you at the time of making a report include the percentages or the estimated percentage which contributed to each ground of incapacity? . . . I tended to lump them together and stated that the cervical spondylitis and lumbar spondylosis, with his obesity, would comprise about 75 per cent to 80 per cent, and that his nervous state would comprise about 20 per cent."

13. Dr. McInnes, a full-time Commonwealth Medical Officer, comments:

"Do you have any comments to make on that statement in your experience as a senior medical officer? Is that a normal practice? How would this situation arise? . . . As I said I would have preferred to have listed the conditions separately and listed percentages separately. I agree with what he said, that the conditions are interrelated, in that there is a causative relationship between obesity and lumbar spondylosis. There is no causative relationship the other way. I think it would be preferable to list them as separate conditions rather than two things that tend to go together in a two-way relationship."

We would agree with Dr. McInnes. Dr. McInnes went on to say that he did not consider the obesity was a contributing factor to the cervical spondylitis, which is, of course, an unspecified portion of the 75%.

14. As to the 5% incapacity due to alcoholism Dr. Hewat said that the basis of this diagnosis was "on discussion with the patient and on information available to me by another practitioner". Dr. McInnes said there was no other evidence he had seen that would bear out a diagnosis of alcoholism. Dr. Thomas, the general practitioner, was very surprised to hear of the suggestion of alcoholism. We comment on the undesirability of a member of the medical profession exercising functions as a Commonwealth Medical Officer in respect of somebody treated in the same practice except when this cannot be avoided in very remote areas. Dr. McInnes would not take alcoholism into account as a Commonwealth Medical Officer if it did not interfere with a man's work; that, we think, is the right approach and there is no evidence that it did.

15. We prefer the evidence of Dr. Thomas and Mr. Beetham, specifically that set out in paragraph 11 above, to that of Dr. Hewat and find the facts accordingly.

16. On these facts the applicant was accepted for employment as a mail officer despite his obesity and he was able to do that work until he was injured in a motor vehicle accident in 1976. It was the development of the spondylitis and a mental disease both resulting from this injury which changed him from a man who was able to work to a man who was incapacitated for work and this we find as a fact. Even if the applicant were not obese he would remain incapacitated for work because of his back and mental conditions, neither of which are relevant factors for the purpose of sub-s. 184(2) or sub-s. 66(2).

17. On these facts overweight was one of the conditions as a result of which Hamilton was on the Provident Account. It was therefore correctly on the Benefit Classification Certificate. However, the overweight did not affect his ability to work as a mail officer before the accident and the degree of the obesity was not significantly worse when he was retired than when he was able to work as a mail officer. The effect of the obesity was to make the incapacity resulting from the spondylitis, which was a sequel to the accident, worse but that spondylitis would still be present without the obesity. Clearly the incapacity was not caused by the obesity. On these facts of the matter we do not think it can be said to have been substantially contributed to by the obesity.

18. We have been referred to Vaughan and the Commissioner for Superannuation (2 A.L.N. 366) and Re Burr and the Commissioner for Superannuation (3 A.L.N. 1) which, of course, deal with the facts of those particular cases. Here the factual situation is different and, having found that the obesity was not a substantial contributory factor to the incapacity we also find that it was not a condition connected with a condition specified in the certificate.

19. In view of these findings the decision of the Commissioner will be set aside and the applicant's benefit entitlement determined in accordance with sub-s. 66(1) of the Act.
