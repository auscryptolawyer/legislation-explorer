CATCHWORDS

TAXATION : Income Tax- deduction for losses from prior years – continuity of beneficial ownership in company, failure to disclose change in ownership, reasonable exercise of discretion to remit part of additional tax



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 80A, 80A(1), 80AA, 80E, 170, 223, 227



Income Tax Ruling 2517



Re Douglas Blair Panton and Commissioner of Taxation

Saunders v Vautier [1841] EngR 629; (1841) 49 E.R. 282



REASONS FOR DECISION



9 November 1999 Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) Mr J D Horrigan (Member) Mr I R Way (Member)



1. The applicant company seeks review of decisions on objections made by it in respect of assessments of income tax for the years of income ended 30 June 1988, 1989, 1990 and 1991. In each year of income the issue is whether the company is entitled to a deduction for losses of previous years and consequential issues as to whether amended assessments were authorised and whether additional tax assessed is appropriate.

2. Section 80AA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 provides for an allowable deduction for losses incurred by a taxpayer in years of income preceding the subject year of income. The section applies to losses incurred by a taxpayer engaging in primary production in the year of income that commenced on 1 July 1957 and subsequent years.

3. However losses incurred by a taxpayer which is a company will not be allowable unless the continuity of beneficial ownership of the company test in s 80A is satisfied or the continuity of business test in s 80E is satisfied.

4. As will become clear the applicant has disposed of its primary production business and cannot satisfy the tests in s 80E.

5. Section 80A(1) relevantly read as follows in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1988 to 30 June 1991;

"80A(1) Notwithstanding sections 80, 80AAA and 80AA but subject to this section and sections 80B, 80DA and 80E, a loss incurred by a company in a year before the year of income shall not be taken into account for the purposes of section 80, 80AAA or 80AA unless –

(a) the company satisfies the Commissioner; or

(b) . . .

that, at all times during the year of income, shares in the company carrying between them –

(c) the right to exercise more than one-half of the voting power in the company;

(d) the right to receive more than one-half of any dividends that may be paid by the company; and

(e) the right to receive more than one-half of any distribution of capital of the company,

were beneficially owned by persons who, at all times during the year in which the loss was incurred, beneficially owned shares in the company carrying between them rights of those kinds."

(Amendment by Act No 57 of 1990 not relevant for present purposes.)



6. At the hearing, which was held in conjunction with the hearing in Re Douglas Blair Panton and Commissioner of Taxation, the company was represented by Mr Panton. Mr Hack of counsel appeared for the respondent. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged pursuant to s 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and the documents lodged in the Re Douglas Blair Panton case. Further documents were tendered and marked as exhibits. Oral evidence was given by a number of witnesses. Further material has been submitted to the Tribunal since the hearing and has been included in the evidence.

7. We make the following findings of fact:

(a) The applicant was incorporated in 1958 for the purpose of carrying on business as a dealer in motor vehicles.

(b) Douglas Blair Panton married Dorothy Margaret Roorke on 21 December 1957 and the marriage was dissolved on 9 September 1985 and the decree made absolute from 7 October 1985 – there was one child of the marriage, Susan Alexa Panton born on 15 January 1961.

(c) By deed dated 16 May 1961 the Susan Alexa Panton Trust was created which appointed Douglas Blair Panton as trustee with Susan Alexa Panton the primary beneficiary.

(d) By the terms of the deed the property of the trust was to vest in Susan Alexa Panton upon her attaining 25 years of age which she did on 15 January 1986.

(e) The share structure of the company is unnecessarily complex because of past attempts at tax minimisation. We have set out some details of the share structure in our decision in Re Douglas Blair Panton and Commissioner of Taxation and need not repeat those details here. Suffice it to say that the company was controlled by Douglas Blair Panton as governing director at all relevant times but the net wealth of the company vested in five ordinary shares and those shares were held by Douglas Blair Panton as trustee of the Susan Alexa Panton Trust at all relevant times. The remaining shares in the company had rights to participate in return of capital on winding up limited to the face value of the shares.

(f) 5000 deferred ordinary shares with limited rights as to return of capital but rights to participate in distribution of profits were held at all relevant times by an associated company. The shareholders in this company were Douglas Blair Panton and Dorothy Margaret Panton with each owning five ordinary shares plus preference shares which had the effect of giving Mr Panton control of the company. The rights to dividends are illustrated at T37/411.

(g) The company purchased a grazing and farming property known as "Talgai" in 1968. It is clear from the material before us that the property became the focus of the company's business and was the company's principal asset. Activities included grazing and crop farming and the respondent does not dispute that the business was one of primary production. However the primary production business was not profitable whereas limited profits were made from motor vehicle trading (T37/415 and 416).

(h) Consequent upon a property settlement evidenced by a "Deed of Compromise" dated 29 April 1987 (T44) Dorothy Margaret Panton transferred her shares in the company and the associated company to Douglas Blair Panton and resigned as a director of the company. The company sold its cattle and the Talgai property and effectively ceased carrying on business as a primary producer. The contract for sale of the property for a consideration of $1.7 million is dated 2 March 1988 (T45).

(i) As already noted we are satisfied, and find, that upon sale of "Talgai" there was a significant change in the business of the company so that it cannot be said to have been carrying on the same business as it carried on previously.

(j) On 29 January 1986 Susan Alexa Panton executed a deed assigning all her right title and interest in the five ordinary shares of the company to Dorothy Margaret Panton on trust for the said transferee and Douglas Blair Panton. The effect of the assignment was considered by Elliott J of the Family Court. On 20 March 1986 his Honour delivered judgment on an application for discharge of restraining orders previously made by the Court. In the course of his reasons his Honour said:

"My understanding of what has occurred is that the daughter of the parties has assigned her beneficial interest in the trust. That beneficial interest was comprised of a handful of shares held by a trustee of the trust for her benefit.

The value of the shares is reflected solely in the fact that upon a winding up of the company she, as the holder of those shares, would be entitled to the entirety of the corpus of the company's property. The shares, however, do not give her any control over the operation of the company, or of its property, or of any disposition of its income.

I cannot see that a transfer of shares is needed in the event. The daughter's assignment appears to me, on all the material, to have been perfected. The situation thus is that the trustee merely holds the shares or trusts different from those upon which the shares were held prior to the assignment of the daughter's interest."



With respect, we agree. In so far as his Honour suggests that the shares were entitled to the whole of the distribution on a winding up, we understand his Honour to have allowed for the rights to return of paid up capital in relation to other shares.

(k) Independent tax accountants were consulted in 1990/91 regarding the ramifications for the company of the change in beneficial ownership of the company.

Consideration

8. Having made those findings of fact we have come to a clear view that we are bound by the facts to find that there was not the necessary continuity of ownership.

9. There have been two relevant changes in ownership. Firstly the assignment by Susan Alexa Panton on 29 January 1986 and secondly the transfers of shares by Dorothy Margaret Panton in consequence of the Deed of Compromise. It should be noted that Dorothy Margaret Panton has not assigned her interest in the five ordinary shares which are the subject of the trust so that she and Douglas Blair Panton now have the right in equity to receive one half each of any distribution of capital of the company on a winding up (in lieu of Susan Alexa Panton). We note that Susan Alexa Panton did not purport to terminate the trust in accordance with the rule in Saunders v Vautier [1841] EngR 629; (1841) 49 E.R. 282.

10. It may be that is not the intended result of the Deed of Compromise but that is the result that must follow the assignment for the reasons explained by Elliott J.

11. We are not satisfied that the company satisfied paragraph 80A(1)(e) of the Act and therefore s 80AA does not apply to allow a deduction for losses of previous years.

12. The transfers by Dorothy Margaret Panton need not be considered further so that application of the tracing provisions because of her holding of shares in the associated company need not be pursued.

13. The returns (Exhibit 5) lodged in 1993 by the company for the years subject to review did not disclose the changes in ownership.

14. It follows, in our view, that the applicant, having failed to make a full and true disclosure to the respondent, cannot support a contention that the amended assessment are not authorised by s 170 of the Act. The change in beneficial ownership of the five ordinary shares was not disclosed to the respondent. There was therefore a failure to disclose relevant information which the respondent needed to make the correct decision in relation to the previous years losses. There was no mystery about the assignment of the beneficial interest. It was the cornerstone for the subsequent orders of the Family Court which had the effect of requiring the company's governing director to partially liquidate the company and distribute to the shareholders for the benefit of Mrs Panton.

15. The only issue was that the respondent was not told about the assignment and was not put on his enquiry to find out about it. That is notwithstanding that the company sought advice from independent accountants in 1990/1991 about the effects of the assignment. In this regard we note that the company's relevant returns were lodged with the respondent on 27 August 1992 ie, well after receiving the independent advice.

16. The returns were false and misleading in a material particular so that s 223 of the Act operated to impose additional tax at 200% of the tax which became payable on the amended assessments.

17. The respondent has exercised the discretion under s 227 of the Act by remitting part of the additional tax on a basis which is said to comply with the respondent's guidelines in Income Tax Ruling 2517.

18. Whether the remissions are in accordance with those guidelines is not a matter for this Tribunal to decide. We must decide whether the decisions to remit portion of the additional tax imposed by s 223 were the preferable decisions.

19. We think they were! The company lodged incorrect returns after it had received professional advice that there was a real issue about change of ownership to be resolved. The returns did not address the issue because they failed to disclose the change in beneficiaries of the Susan Alexa Panton Trust. In the context of the proceedings in the Family Court and the consultations with the accountants we can only conclude that the company's returns were deliberately false for the purpose of defrauding the Commonwealth.

20. We do not see any basis for interfering in the respondent's exercise of the discretion which, in the circumstances, might be thought to be on the lenient side but not so much as to cause us to make new decisions.

21. The objection decisions under review will be affirmed.
