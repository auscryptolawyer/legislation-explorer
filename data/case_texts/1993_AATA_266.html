11. At about this time, a development occurred which, I find, influenced the eminence grise behind this scheme. On 2 July 1979, Board of Review No 2 decided in Case L40 79 ATC 220 that the application of sec 52 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") was limited to cases where, had there been a profit rather than a loss, the profit would have been assessable pursuant to the (former) sec 26(a) rather than sec 26AAA. In the events that occurred, this decision was reversed on appeal by a single judge of the Supreme Court of Victoria (sub nom Werchon v FC of T 81 ATC 4670). Quoting from the headnote, the judge held that the history of secs 26(a) and 52 did not justify the conclusion that the property referred to in sec 52 is limited to property acquired for profit-making by sale. To conclude that "property" in sec 52 is not so limited is not to deny that sec 52 is a companion to sec 26(a): it means that they are not necessarily exclusively each other's companions. The judge held that the words used in sec 52 were general enough to mean that any loss upon a sale of property was deductible if it occurred in circumstances where, had there been a profit on the sale, it would have been assessable irrespective of the purpose for which the property was acquired. That decision was published in December 1981.

It is appropriate at this point to make certain findings of fact in relation to exh "B", the document which purports to be the minutes of a meeting of the unitholders of the D Unit Trust, signed and dated 30 June 1981 by N. Whilst I am unable to determine whether any meeting occurred as stated in that document, I am satisfied beyond doubt that - whatever else may have occurred, even assuming that there was such a meeting on that day - the said resolution was not made at such a meeting, but was invented sometime after the issue of the notice of amended assessment on 22 July 1983 referred to in the previous paragraph. My reason for reaching that conclusion is that it was not until the issue of that assessment that the figure of $1,634,250 bore any significance in relation to the income of the D Unit Trust. For similar reasons, I make like findings of fact in relation to the other purported minute tendered as exhibit "C". I should add that N was involved in a serious motor vehicle accident in 1986, resulting, inter alia, in him suffering head injuries which in turn resulted in serious impairment of memory. I am satisfied that this memory loss is genuine; indeed it was not challenged by the Crown. In these circumstances, whilst N's inability to recall whether the meetings referred to in the abovementioned minutes took place and whether the minutes were in fact signed on the date on which they purport to have been so signed is "neutral" ("I just can't - I honestly don't remember signing them"). It is trite to point out that, sympathetic as I am to N's predicament, his genuine and excusable lack of recall cannot lessen the onus of proof which he bears in these proceedings.

27. N objected on 16 December 1988 ("T7") and the respondent advised of the disallowance of that objection on 4 December 1990. It is this decision which is the subject of N's application to this Tribunal.

28. The threshold question in relation to N's application (but not in relation to Mrs N) is whether subsection 170(2) is satisfied, thus enabling the respondent to issue the amended assessment on 15 November 1988 in respect of N's 1981 year of income. At the relevant time, that subsection provided:

Where a taxpayer has not made to the Commissioner a full and

true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his

assessment, and there has been an avoidance of tax, the

Commissioner may -

(a) where he is of opinion that the avoidance of tax is due to

fraud or evasion - at any time; and

(b) in any other case - within 6 years from the date upon which

the tax became due and payable under the assessment,

amend the assessment by making such alterations therein or

additions thereto as he thinks necessary to correct an error in

calculation or a mistake of fact or to prevent avoidance of tax

as the case may be.

29. As will be seen from that provision, before the section can come into operation, the respondent must be able to point to "an avoidance of tax". As a result, it is necessary for me to come to a conclusion on the substantive issues in this case before I can arrive at a decision in relation to the "sec 170" issue.

30. I must therefore ask myself whether any amount "flowed" - in a tax sense - from the D Unit Trust to K, as unitholder, in respect of the 1981 year of income of the D Unit Trust.

31. As far as relevant, sec 97(1) provides:

Where a beneficiary of a trust estate who is not under any

legal disability is presently entitled to a share of the income

of the trust estate -

(a) the assessable income of the beneficiary shall include -

(i) so much of that share of the net income of the trust estate

as is attributable to a period when the beneficiary was a

resident; and

...

32. The first question in this context is whether there was any income of the D trust estate of which K was presently entitled to a share.

33. Sec 97 deals with the case where a beneficiary is presently entitled to a share of the income of a trust estate - not to a share of the net income of a trust estate: see Union Fidelity Trustee Co. of Australia Ltd. v FC of T [1969] HCA 36; (1969) 119 CLR 177, at 188; and FC of T v Totledge Pty Ltd 82 ATC 4168, at 4174. It appears that the words "income of the trust estate" in the section refer to income for trust law purposes (unless defined otherwise by the trust deed, which is not the case here): Davis & Anor v FC of T 89 ATC 4377, at 4403.

34. Whether the D Unit Trust had any income according to trust law for the 1981 year of income can be ascertained by examining its operating statement for that period ("T9" p 31) which states:

35. Notwithstanding that the applicants concede - as indeed they must - that the transactions involved in the "Black and Baer" scheme by the A Partnership were not effective in creating a deduction for the purposes of calculating D's individual interest in the partnership loss, it seems to me, in circumstances where the respondent did not argue before me that:

(1) the transactions involved in the instant case constituted a

sham; and/or

(2) the A "partnership", which on one view could be said to

have been engaged in a "one-off" transaction the sole purpose

of which was to create an artificial tax loss, was not an

association of persons carrying on business in common with a

view to a profit;

D's share of that partnership's loss will, nevertheless, reduce the amount
of income according to trust law of the D Unit Trust for the 1981 year of income. In all the circumstances, I do not believe the Tribunal should seek to argue a case for the respondent that he himself deliberately avoided.

36. If the D Unit Trust did not have any trust law income for the 1981 year of income to which any beneficiary could have been presently entitled - as I believe it did not - it follows that the applicants must succeed. It may well be, as Kitto J noted in Union Fidelity (supra) at 187, that "this is a sufficiently odd conclusion to make one suspicious of it", nevertheless, that conclusion follows from my finding. Had the amount of D's interest in the A partnership scheme loss not been sufficient to produce a loss of trust law income for the year, then, adopting the "proportionate" view of sec 97 (which appears to be the correct approach: cf Davis), and assuming for present purposes that K is presently entitled to a share of the income remaining after deducting the scheme loss, it would follow that the entire net income of the D Unit Trust (which would not be reduced by its share of the scheme loss) would be subject to tax.

37. In view of this "sufficiently odd conclusion", it is likely the matter will go on appeal. In the circumstances, I propose to deal with the other issues which arise, in the event that my findings (i) that the D Unit Trust did not have any trust law income for the 1981 year of income to which any beneficiary could have been presently entitled, and (ii) that that resulted in no amount being included in the assessable income of the respective unitholders in the D Unit Trust, are found to be wrong.

38. The remainder of my decision is therefore predicated on the assumption - contrary to the above findings - that the D Unit Trust did have an amount of such income which was not reduced by the amount of the A Partnership scheme loss.

39. Was K 'presently entitled' to a share of such income? Clauses 40 and 41 of the D Unit trust deed ("T14") provide:

40. Having made in paragraph 20 various findings in relation to the "minutes" dated 30 June 1981, I am satisfied that the procedures necessary to bring clause 41(b) into operation in respect of the 1981 year of income were not validly carried out. Whilst it would appear at first glance that clause 41(a) is "self-operative" in that it states that "the Trustees shall in each Accounting Period ... pay, apply or set aside the net income of the Trust Fund (the net income being the amount remaining after the operation of clause 40(b)) of that Accounting Period to or for the benefit of the Unit Holders in proportion to the number of Units of which they are respectively registered as Holders at the end of the Accounting Period" (my emphasis), nevertheless, I have concluded that, having regard to paragraphs (d) and (f) of clause 41, a determination by the Trustee is, in fact, required in order to activate paragraph (a) of that clause.

41. If a determination by the Trustee is required in order to activate clause 41(a), and there being no evidence that such a determination was made, it follows that clause 41(f) comes into play. In those circumstances, the effect of that provision is that the Trustee holds the net income of the Trust Fund on a separate fixed trust for the beneficiary unitholders - including K - in proportion to their respective unit holdings in the unit trust. On that view, it follows that, from the establishment of that separate trust, the beneficiaries of that trust became presently entitled to their respective shares of the net income of the (unit) Trust Fund because (i) those amounts can be said to have been applied for the benefit of each beneficiary; cf Sacks & Ors v Gridiger & Ors 90 ATC 4299; Commr of IR (NZ) v Ward 69 ATC 6050; and Case E47 73 ATC 385; or, (ii), the rule in Saunders v Vautier [1841] EngR 629; (1841) 4 Beav 115; 49 ER 282 enables the beneficiaries to call for the trust funds and cause the winding up of the trust.

42. If I am wrong, and clause 41(a) comes into operation without the need for a determination by the Trustee, it would follow that the beneficiary unitholders - including K - would be presently entitled to their respective proportions of that income, as the term `presently entitled' has come to be understood as a result of such decisions as FC of T v Whiting [1943] HCA 45; (1943) 68 CLR 199, and Taylor & Anor v FC of T [1970] HCA 10; (1970) 119 CLR 444.

43. Mr Brandis, of learned counsel for the respondent, submitted in the alternative that K and the other beneficiaries of the D Unit Trust would be deemed to be presently entitled to a share of the income of the Trust Fund pursuant to sec 95A(2). Having found `present entitlement' without recourse to the various deeming provisions in Division 6 of Part III of the Act, it is not strictly necessary for me to make a finding in relation to that submission, although I have some difficulty in seeing how it assists the respondent's case since, at the relevant time, the wording and effect of subsecs 97(2) & 98(2) was substantially different from their present import, such that, at the relevant time, the trustee would be assessed in respect of K's share of the net income of that trust in the event that present entitlement was "deemed" pursuant to sec 95A(2). Thus, if K were found to be presently entitled to a share of the income of the D Unit Trust, consideration would need to be given to the applicant's submission that K held its interest in the D Unit Trust in its own right, and not as trustee of the N Family Trust.

44. Whilst the absence of a mention of the holding of the units in the D Trust in the trust tax return which K lodged in respect of the N Trust for the 1981 year of income could be said to support a submission that K did not hold those units as trustee, nevertheless, when viewed together with:

(i) lodgement of K's company return for that period (exhibit 2)

which also did not disclose that K held units in the D Trust;

and stated that all assets in the balance sheet were held in

the capacity of trustee of the N Family Trust and that it had a

right of indemnity against those assets in order to meet its

liabilities; and

(ii) N's evidence that he could not recall any time when K

acted otherwise than as trustee of the N Family Trust,

I am satisfied on the evidence that K did indeed act in its

capacity as trustee when it held the units in the D Unit Trust.

45. The next question is, therefore, whether N and Mrs N were presently entitled to a share of the income which "flowed" from the share of the D Trust's income to which K was presently entitled as trustee.

46. Firstly, I am satisfied that the unsigned minute (exh 3) which is set out in paragraph 14 above, can be relied upon as evidencing the noted resolution validly made on 30 June 1981. As noted in that paragraph, that resolution (which was effectively made by N and Mrs N as the directors of K) was attached to K's notice of objection to the sec 99A assessment which it received on or about 14 July 1982. As trustee of the N Family Trust, no one was in a better position than K to state whether such a resolution and determination was made in accordance with that resolution.

47. In the result, having regard to the terms of the N Family Trust ("T22"), it follows as a consequence of that determination that N and Mrs N became presently entitled (either pursuant to sec 101, or the meaning given to the term "present entitlement" by the authorities) to shares of the income of the N Family Trust which, in turn, would result in the inclusion in their respective assessable incomes of the amounts to which the respondent has assessed them in the amended assessments the subject of these applications.

48. The final - and threshold question - in respect of N's application, assuming I am wrong in my finding that there has been no avoidance of tax - cf paras 35 and 36 above - is whether the amended assessment, the subject of the objection decision being reviewed in relation to his application, is authorised by sec 170. On that assumption it becomes necessary to make a finding based on sec 170 in relation to N, I am satisfied that (i) the failure in the return of the N Family Trust to disclose its interest in the D Unit Trust, and (ii) the failure of N in his return to disclose the fact that the Trust was a unit holder in the D Unit Trust (N was a director of D as well as K, the corporate trustee), constitutes "fraud or evasion" within the meaning of subsec 170(2)(a). In the circumstances, the Commissioner's amendment would have been authorised by the Act.

49. For the above reasons the objection decisions under review are set aside and the applicants' objections allowed in full.
