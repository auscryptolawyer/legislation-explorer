TAXATION - Inclusion of eligible termination benefit in taxable income.

Re:



Applicant

REASONS FOR DECISION



20 May 1994 Mr T E Barnett (Senior Member)



1. The applicant has sought review of an "assessment dated 3 August 1993 for the income year ended 30 June 1993". That assessment was based on taxable income of $8,679.00 which included an eligible termination benefit of $1,046.00 ("ETP") released to the applicant from Westpac Approved Deposit Fund (the "ADF"), upon which tax instalments of $222.19 had already been deducted by ADF. After crediting that sum of $222.19 and taking account of the tax threshold and rebate provisions the respondent assessed tax payable (the amount due) at $105.61.

2. On 29 September 1993 the applicant filed notice of objection and the matter was reconsidered by the respondent which disallowed the objection on 5 January 1994 and the applicant sought review of that decision by the Tribunal on 1 February 1994.



3. The respondent then reconsidered the matter further and, after taking account of allowable rebates, and reducing the rate from 20 per cent to 15 per cent by amended assessment dated 24 February 1994 reassessed the amount of tax payable as $50.31.

4. The main basis of the applicant's objection is that she feels it was wrong and unfair to include the ETP of $1,046.00 as part of her taxable income as full tax had already been deducted in respect of this sum. Had this not been included in her taxable income it would have been only a few dollars over the tax threshold. She also claims that the method of calculating the rebate was unfair and harsh considering her financial circumstances.

5. In support of her case she tendered various financial documents, including her group certificates, and correspondence with the Insurance and Superannuation Commission which have been listed, included as exhibits and considered.

6. Ms Misztal for the respondent made a detailed and exhaustive survey of the facts and made very detailed analysis of the legislative provisions applicable in simple language. Before doing so she handed up an outline of her submissions reproducing the relevant provisions (Exhibit R1). She argued however, that the correct assessment is as set out in the amended assessment of 24 February 1994 which re-affirmed that the taxable income was $8,679.00 but altered the amount of tax payable based upon that amount.

7. Having considered the legislation and the documents and having heard submissions, the Tribunal finds that the sum of $1,046.00 ETP was correctly included in the applicant's taxable income for the year ending 30 June 1993.

8. The legislation is complex but when carefully traced through that is the clear result.

9. The applicant's fear that she has somehow been "double taxed" is unfounded. The fact that she had already been taxed on that income does not stop it from being income - and it took her over the threshold. (She was of course credited with the tax instalments deducted.)

10. On the question of calculating the allowable rebate the applicant's feeling that this was done unfairly in the August 1993 assessment has, in effect, been confirmed by the amended assessment which was made after she filed her application with the Tribunal. She has not sought to challenge the calculation in that amended assessment and, after hearing Ms Misztal's explanations the Tribunal accepts them as correct.

11. The applicant challenged and sought review of the assessment of 3 August 1993. In that she has succeeded (in part) because, although she failed to satisfy the Tribunal (or the Deputy Commissioner) that the ETP should not have been included in her taxable income, she has persuaded the Deputy Commissioner and this Tribunal that the amount of the assessment was wrong.



Decision

12. The decision under review being the assessment of the 3 August 1993 is set aside and it is substituted with a decision in the terms of the assessment of 24 February 1994 showing taxable income of $8,679.00 with tax payable of $50.31.
