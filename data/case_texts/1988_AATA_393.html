Steele and Commissioner for Superannuation [1988] AATA 393 (9 February 1988)

Steele and Commissioner for Superannuation [1988] AATA 393 (9 February 1988)

Last Updated: 20 February 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Superannuation - issue by the respondent of a benefit classification certificate (BCC) - whether retirement caused by condition specified on the BCC - whether condition which caused the applicant's application to join the Superannuation Fund to be' initially rejected could be the basis of the BCC - test to be applied when deciding the reasons of the decision by the Superannuation Board to reject the applicant's application when these reasons were not stated

Words and Phrases

"incapacity ... ground for ... retirement"

Superannuation Act 1976 ss.66, 154, 184

Superannuation Act 1922 ss.4, 5

Re Gunzburg and Commissioner for Superannuation (No. 3466; decided on 7 May 1987)

The Tribunal sets aside the decisions under review and remits the matter to the respondent for reconsideration in accordance with the following directions:

  1. the conditions by reason of which the Superannuation Board was not satisfied under sub-section 5(1) of the Superannuation Act 1922 that the applicant's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Fund under Part III of that Act were: chronic bronchitis, osteoarthritis of the left knee and a history of lumbo sacral pain.
  2. the incapacity which was the ground for Mr Steele's retirement was not caused or substantially contributed to by those conditions or one or more of those conditions or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with those conditions or one or more of those conditions.

Introduction

1 This is an application under Section 154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act"), lodged on 24 July 1986, for review of two decisions of a delegate of the respondent made on 27 June 1986:

(a) varying a decision taken by another delegate on 19 November 1982 to issue, in respect of the applicant, pursuant to sub-section 184(2) of the Act, a benefit classification certificate specifying the conditions "Chronic Bronchitis", "Osteoarthritis of (L) Knee" and "Lumbo Sacral Pain" by deleting the last of those conditions and substituting therefor the condition "History of musculo-ligamentous strain at the lumbar-sacral level"; and

(b) confirming the decision also taken by that other delegate on 19 November 1982, to be of the opinion under paragraph 66(2)(c) of the Act that the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a condition or conditions specified in the benefit classification certificate or by a condition or conditions connected with such conditions.
  1. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to Section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act") and numbered T1 to T30, together with other material lodged at the hearing, to some of which it will be necessary to refer. Evidence was given at the hearing by the applicant, who called no other witnesses; and by Dr Downie, a general surgeon, Dr Gayton, an orthopaedic surgeon, and Dr Twomey, a general practitioner practising in Albury, all of whom were called by the respondent.
  2. Mr Steele, who was born on 3 November 1925, commenced temporary employment with the Commonwealth Department of Works as a mechanical fitter in 1951. In December 1971 he applied to become a contributor under the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the 1922 Act").
  3. Sub-section 4(5) of the 1922 Act provided, in effect, that a temporary employee could, in certain defined circumstances, be deemed to be an employee within the meaning of that section (i.e., in effect a permanent employee). Section 5 of the 1922 Act provided, in effect, that if a permanent employee underwent a medical examination as prescribed, the Superannuation Board ("the Board") after considering that report, would decide whether or not "the health and physical fitness of the person are such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the [Superannuation] Fund or to the Provident Account, as the case may be".
  4. Thus the 1922 Act provided for three different possible results, based on the medical examination of a person who is, or becomes, a permanent employee, of an application to become a contributor under that Act: acceptance as a member of the Superannuation Fund; acceptance as a member of the Provident Account, which provided significantly lower benefits; or outright rejection.

6. On 13 April 1972 Mr Steele was examined by Dr Grant, a Commonwealth Medical Officer practising in Wodonga. On 6 June 1972 a delegate of the Board made a decision that Mr Steele was unacceptable as a contributor to either the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account.

7. Later in 1972 the medical standards for acceptance of contributors to the Provident Account were relaxed. On 16 November 1973 Mr Steele was accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account without further medical examination.

  1. The superannuation scheme established by the 1976 Act ("the scheme") was organised upon a different basis. Every permanent employee was accepted into the scheme, but benefits were adjusted, on the basis of the medical examination, by the issuing of a benefit classification certificate ("BCC") which related to the risk: of the employee's being unable, by reason of a particular condition, to remain an employee until the retiring age. The inclusion in the scheme of employees like Mr Steele who were contributors to the previously existing Superannuation Fund or Provident Account, required the enactment of transitional provisions in the 1976 Act. The present matter arises under those provisions.
  2. The relevant provisions of the 1922 Act, as in force at the relevant time are sub-section 4(5) and Section 5 which read as follows:

4(5) Where -

(a) a person employed by the Commonwealth otherwise than in a permanent capacity is by the terms of his employment required to give the whole of his time to the duties of his employment;

(b) that person has been so employed for a continuous period of not less than three years; and

(c) the Public Service Board, or, on appeal from the Public Service Board, the Treasurer, certifies that that person's employment is likely to be continued for a period of at least seven years,

the Treasurer may direct that that person be deemed to be an employee within the meaning of this section, and that person shall be deemed to be such an employee as from the date of the direction.

...

5(1) Except as otherwise provided by this Act, and notwithstanding anything contained in any other Act, a person who becomes an employee after the commencement of the Superannuation Act 1965 shall not contribute to the Fund under Part III of this Act or to the Provident Account unless -

(a) he has, before becoming an employee, or within such period after becoming an employee as the Board allows, undergone a medical examination by a legally qualified medical practitioner approved by the Board';

(b) a report of the result of the examination has been furnished by the medical practitioner to the Board; and

(c) the Board is satisfied, after considering the report of the medical practitioner, that the health and physical fitness of the person are such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Fund under Part III or to the Provident Account, as the case may be.

(2) Where -

(a) a person who becomes an employee after the commencement of the Superannuation Act 1965 has, whether before or after becoming an employee, undergone a medical examination by a legally qualified medical practitioner approved by the Public Service Board;

(b) a report of the result of the examination has been furnished by the medical practitioner to the Public Service Board; and

(c) the Public Service Board certifies, after considering the report of the medical practitioner, that the health and physical fitness of the person are such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Fund under Part III of this Act or to the Provident Account,

the Superannuation Board may, in its discretion, accept him as a contributor to the Fund under Part III of this Act or to the Provident Account.

The definition of "employee" in sub-section 4(1) reads, so far as relevant: "a person employed in a permanent capacity by the Commonwealth, who is by the terms of his employment required to give his whole time to the duties of his employment ..."

  1. The relevant provisions of the 1976 Act are sub-sections 66(2) and 184(2) and (7), which at the relevant time read as follows so far as relevant:

other than -

  1. Section 184 of the 1976 Act was amended by Section 76 of the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act (No. 2) 1986 with effect from 18 December 1986; i.e., after the making of the decision under review and lodging of this application for review. Mr Whithear, for the respondent, submitted, and the Tribunal accepts, that the amendments were not relevant to the issue before us. Accordingly, it is not necessary for us to pursue the question as to whether our decision is to be made under the legislation as amended, or under the legislation as it stood prior to that date, and we will proceed to consider the matter on the basis of the text as it stood at all relevant times.
  2. On 21 June 1982, with effect from 9 October 1982, Mr Steele was retired from the Australian Public Service on the ground of invalidity. On 19 November 1982 a delegate of the respondent issued a BCC specifying chronic bronchitis, osteoarthritis of left knee, and lumbo-sacral pain. The basis of issue of the certificate was that the delegate formed the opinion, in terms of sub-paragraph 184(7)(c)(i) of the 1976 Act, that those were the conditions by reason of which the Superannuation Board had not been satisfied that Mr Steele's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund.
  3. The incapacity which was the ground for Mr Steele's retirement was described by Dr Twomey, Commonwealth Medical Officer, in his report of 3 April 1982 upon which the delegate relied, as being caused by a prolapsed intervertebral disc at the L5/S1 level. The delegate formed the opinion, in terms of sub-paragraph 184(c)(i), that that incapacity was caused or substantially contributed to by a condition specified in the BCC. That being so, he decided on 19 November 1982 to apply sub-section 66(2) of the 1976 Act, with the result that Mr Steele received a reduced pension benefit under the scheme.

14. Mr Steele requested a reconsideration of those two decisions of the delegate, and the result of that reconsideration was the making, on 27 June 1986, of the decisions under review, which are set out in paragraph 1 supra.

The Issues

15. In effect, the matters which the Tribunal must determine are:

(a) what, if any condition or conditions should appear on the BCC: i.e., subject to (d) below, what was the physical or mental condition or conditions by reason of which the Superannuation Board, on 6 June 1972, was not satisfied that Mr Steele's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund under Part III of the 1922 Act;

(b) what was the incapacity which was the ground for Mr Steele's retirement on 21 June 1982;

(c) did (i) the condition or conditions described in (a) or any of them; or

(ii) a condition or conditions connected with them or any of them

cause or substantially contribute to that incapacity; and

(d) if yes to (c), did that condition or those conditions exist at the time when Mr Steele became a contributor to the Provident Account on 16 November 1973.

16. Mr Steele, who was unrepresented, submitted that Dr Twomey's report of 3 April 1982 was based on incomplete information about conditions from which he suffered, and was therefore incorrect; and that doctors who had examined him subsequently had been misled by that report. The pain in his lower back at the time when he was examined by Dr Twomey was not connected with the prolapsed disc, but was caused by a duodenal ulcer which was not diagnosed until February 1983. In his submission, the condition which caused the incapacity which was the ground of his retirement was the duodenal ulcer, which was neither caused nor substantially contributed to by any condition from which he suffered in June 1972.

Question (a)(i): 6 June 1972

  1. The Superannuation Board, in 1972, gave no reason for its decision. As the Tribunal said in Re Gunzburg and Commissioner for Superannuation (No. 3466, decided on 7 May 1987) "This, however, is not a novel situation. It has been faced by this Tribunal in other decisions". It must be remembered that the role of the Tribunal in this context is not to review the decision of the Superannuation Board; but merely to ascertain what was the physical or mental condition (or conditions) by reason of which the decision was made.
  2. Dr Grant, as has been said, examined Mr Steele on 13 April 1972. In completing the Part B "Personal Medical History" section of the form, Mr Steele disclosed that he had had diptheria as a child, that he had suffered all his life from asthma and shortness of breath, that he had had an eye injury with continuing effects for which he had received workers' compensation, he had had a cartilage removed from his left knee and his knee gave him a lot of trouble, and that he had in 1970 "strained my back lifting at work, now I must be very careful how I lift and how much". Dr Grant noted that the asthma and shortness of breath had been much less since coming to Australia and did not need treatment; that Mr Steele smoked 20 cigarettes a day and coughed up sputum; that he had 60% loss of vision [in the injured eye] which was painful in bright light; that the knee history suggested osteoarthritis; and that Mr Steele had had "sudden sharp pain in back when lifting tool box. Not X rayed. Cannot do heavy lifting. Has been given back exercises to do". He concluded that Mr Steele was fit to contribute to the Provident Account, and added under the heading "Reasons for Recommendation and Remarks":

"This man can cope with his job and I think can do so for further seven years. However, in view of his chronic bronchitis and ? osteoarthritis of knee I doubt if he can reach retiring age."

  1. It will be noted that Dr Grant appears there to have directed his mind to each of the two separate issues which arose. The first was, whether Mr Steele's employment was likely to continue for a period of at least seven years, so that the Treasurer might direct, under sub-section 4(5) of the 1922 Act, that he be deemed to be an employee of the Commonwealth; the second was, whether under Section 5 of that Act, he should be accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund or to the Provident Account. It would appear that Dr Grant was recommending that Mr Steele be deemed to be an employee, but that, because of his bronchitis and the condition of his left knee, he should be accepted into the Provident Account rather than the Superannuation Fund.
  2. It is not clear from the material before the Tribunal whether Dr Grant forwarded his report to the Superannuation Board under sub-section 5(1) or to the Public Service Board under sub-section 5(2). The space on the form for the return address has not been completed. However, it appears to have been the latter, as the next document, chronologically, before the Tribunal is a memorandum from the Commonwealth Public Service Inspector ("CPSI") to the Director, Health, dated 18 April 1972 headed "John Steele T/S", which reads: "The attached papers are referred for review". In a space provided for reply on the same page, there appears a handwritten memorandum addressed to the CPSI which reads "Reject - lumbo sacral pain. D.J. O'Donnell, Director of Health 5/5/72". A note endorsed on that memorandum signed with indecipherable initials reads "Reject 10/5/72". It may be that that note was in fact a decision by a delegate of the Public Service Board under sub-section 5(2)(c), but there is no evidence before the Tribunal from which it can reach any conclusion on the subject.

A letter dated 12 May 1972 from the CPSI to the Superannuation Board, under the reference "TVS" reads:

"John Steele - Applicant for Benefits Under Section 4(5) of the Superannuation Act

The attached papers are referred in accordance with Paragraph 21 of your Circular 1968/14.

[sgd

CPSI]"

No information as to the content of or relevance of Circular 1968/14 was before the Tribunal.

  1. At the foot of that letter is a handwritten note which reads: "Discussed with Dr Thomson on 6.6.72. Reject. J.G. Downes 6.6.72". And the decision of the Superannuation Board was formally recorded by the inclusion on page 1 of the Medical Examination form, under the heading "Superannuation Board Decision" of the note "Unacceptable for Superannuation or Provident. Delegate of the Board J.G. Downes 6.6.72".
  2. It must be remembered that there were two matters for decision: whether Mr Steele was eligible to be deemed to be an employee under sub-section 4(5); and whether if so deemed, he should be accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account under Section 5. The note on the Medical Examination form clearly relates only to the latter issue. It appears from a letter dated 14 June 1972 from the "Manager Operations Branch" (presumably of the Superannuation Board) to the CPSI that no decision had been made under sub-section 4(5); that the decision on 6 June was intended as a decision under Section 5 only and that it was a decision that the Superannuation Board was not satisfied that Mr Steele's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to either the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account. A rejection under Section 5 was no doubt seen ' as obviating the need for a decision under sub-section 4(5). The letter of 14 June 1972 read, so far as relevant:
    1. If the abovenamed applicant had been deemed to be an "employee" within the meaning of Section 4(5) of the Superannuation Act, the determination under Section 5(1) of the Act, on the basis of the medical evidence supporting the application, would be that the applicant's health and physical fitness are not such as to justify that person's acceptance as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund under Part III of the Act or to the Provident Account.
    2. The application is unsuccessful, therefore, on medical grounds and advice to this effect may now be conveyed through the appropriate Department to the applicant.
    3. If the applicant desires to question the matter of non-acceptance the relevant correspondence should be addressed to the Secretary, Superannuation Board, Canberra, when the Board could consider the case under the dispute provisions of Section 141 of the Superannuation Act.

23. Mr Steele accepted the invitation to question the decision, and his attention was drawn to a circular dated 19 October 1972 stating that the medical standard for acceptance to the Provident Account was now "a three year period of likely efficient service". The circular continued:

This change in minimum medical standards for the Provident Account does not change the medical standards for acceptance to the Pension Scheme nor does it have any effect on those provisions of the Superannuation Act whereby the certification of the availability of seven (7) years' future likely employment is required as a pre-requisite of being deemed an employee for the purposes of the Act. Therefore, if the employment aspect is clear, a person may be accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account if the delegate of the Superannuation Board, having regard to the person's state of health, is satisfied that a further period of three (3) years' efficient service could reasonably be expected.

24. The letter to Mr Steele which enclosed a copy of the circular concluded:

Should you decide to lodge a fresh application ... arrangements would be made for you to be deemed an employee for the purposes of Section 4(5) and accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account without further medical examination.

Mr Steele lodged his fresh application on 24 October 1973 and on 30 October 1973 Mr Downes completed Part D of the application form by expressly deciding, as a delegate of the Superannuation Board, that Mr Steele's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account.

25. However, there is no record on the file of the Treasurer's ever having directed that Mr Steele be deemed to be a permanent employee under sub-section 4(5). The application form referred to in the preceding paragraph includes both a statement by the Chief Officer of the Department of Works, the Department which employed Mr Steele, that his employment was likely to continue for a period of at least seven years (the prerequisite for permanence); and a certificate to that effect signed for one R.A. Moffatt, apparently of the Public Service Board. But Part C of the form, providing for the recording of a direction by the Treasurer in terms of sub-section 4(5), has not been completed. However, in the material before the Tribunal is a memorandum addressed to the Secretary to the Treasury, and dated 9 November 1973, which reads:

Mr Steele has lodged a fresh application under Section 4(5) of the Superannuation Act.

The medical evidence has been examined and it is considered that Mr Steele's health and physical fitness are such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account.

Part D of the Form SB20 has been completed in this Office and the Form (in triplicate) is attached for deeming action. Two copies of this memorandum are also attached to enable a copy to accompany the deeming advices issued to the Department and this Office.

Mr Steele has apparently been treated as a permanent employee since his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account, and accordingly we assume that the Treasurer's direction was duly given and is recorded in a document which is not before us.

26. It will be remembered that the question before the Tribunal at this stage is the reason for the decision of 6 June 1972. It is no longer possible to ascertain with certainty what information was before Mr Downes when he made that decision. It seems probable, however, that he had Dr Grant's report. Also on the file is a copy of Mr Steele's sick leave record for the previous three years, extracted on a standard form on 24 March 1972, in the Department of Works a Departmental officer having completed on 21 March Part B of Mr Steele's application to become a contributor. It appears from the form that the sick leave record was prepared for the Commonwealth Medical Officer and was thus, presumably, before Dr Grant when he examined Mr Steele and completed his report on 13 April; and it thus may well have been before Mr Downes. Mr Downes must be assumed also to have had before him the memorandum of the Director of Health referred to in paragraph 20 supra which read "Reject - lumbo sacral pain".

27. The sick leave record covers the period from 27 June 1969 to 14 March 1972 and may be summarised as follows:

Bronchitis/Tonsilitis/Laryngityis/Severe Cold

5 periods totalling 10 days

Influenza

4 periods totalling 5 days

Fluid on left knee/Pain left knee/Strained knee ligaments

6 periods totalling 11 days

Upset stomach/dysentery

2 periods totalling 1 1/2 days

Back injury/pain in back

2 periods totaling 6 days: one period of 4 days occurred in July 1970 and is no doubt the occasion referred to by Mr Steele in his information to Dr Grant; the other period of 2 days occurred in October 1971.

28. In Re Bailey and Commissioner for Superannuation No 352: decided on 24 December 1979: noted at 2 ALN 666 the Tribunal, presided over by Mr Hall Senior Member (as he then was) said:

"In a case such as the present, where there is no direct evidence to establish the physical or mental condition or conditions by reason of which the Superannuation Board did not accept the applicant as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund, it appears to us that the proper test to apply when considering the material before the Board when it reached its decision is - what condition or conditions must have influenced the Board to accept the person into the Provident Account rather than the Fund. Unless, on a fair consideration of that material, the Tribunal can feel satisfied that a particular physical or mental condition was, in fact, a reason which influenced the Board's decision, the Tribunal would not, in our view, be justified in finding as a fact that the condition should be taken as being specified in the benefit classification certificate which is deemed, by s 184(2) of the Act, to be in force in respect of the applicant."

In that case the Superannuation Board had accepted the recommendation of the Commonwealth Medical Officer who had examined the applicant, and the Tribunal said (not reported on this point):

"Where in the medical report relating to a person who has applied for permanent appointment to the Public Service, the section at the end headed "Reasons for Recommendation and Remarks" clearly identifies a mental or physical condition or conditions which may lead to that person's early retirement and where the Superannuation Board or its delegate has accepted that recommendation without comment, it is proper, in our view, to accept that the Board has adopted the reasons given by the Commonwealth Medical Officer in support of his recommendation. This was the view which the Tribunal adopted implicitly, if not explicitly, in Harrison's Case (supra)."

However, in the present case, the recommendation of Dr Grant that Mr Steele be accepted to the Provident Account (set out in paragraph 18 supra) was not accepted, although his recommendation that he be rejected for the Superannuation Fund was accepted.

29. In Re Richardson and Commissioner for Superannuation (No 478: decided on 5 June 1981;) the Tribunal, presided over by Mr Todd, Senior Member (as he then was) said of sub-paragraph 184(7)(c)(i):

"This definition thus requires a reference not to a condition of the person that is merely proved to have existed and to have been a condition to which reference might properly have been made, but rather to a condition that has actually been taken into account by the Superannuation Board for the purposes of coming to a conclusion as to the health and physical fitness of the person."

30. We note that in Re Harrison and Commissioner for Superannuation (No. 137: decided on 1 February 1979; noted at (1979) 2 ALN 353) the Tribunal, also presided over by Mr Todd, found that, for reasons related to the purposes of the provisions of the 1922 Act relating to medical examinations,

"a medical history of a condition, physical or mental, may be described as a "condition", for the purposes of the statutory provision, even though the condition itself be not apparent at the time of the examination."

31. The respondent referred, in the context of this issue, to certain medical reports prepared subsequently to the decision of 6 June 1972, as being descriptive of the applicant's medical condition on that date. However, it is clear from the authorities cited above (and indeed from the terms of sub-paragraph 184(7)(c)(i)) that what must be ascertained for present purposes is not what was the applicant's medical condition at that time, but what was, rightly or wrongly, in the mind of the delegate of the Superannuation Board. In Re Hegedues and Commissioner for Superannuation (1982) 4 ALN N139 the Tribunal, presided over by Mr Ewart Smith, Senior Member, said:

"What has to be examined, therefore, is not what physical or mental conditions the applicant was in fact suffering from at the time he underwent the medical examination for the purposes of s 5 of the superseded Act, but (in the absence of any withholding of any information or furnishing of false information, which was not alleged in this case) what were the conditions that caused the Superannuation Board (through its delegate) not to be satisfied that his health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the superannuation fund. For this purpose, it seems to us clear that what must be examined is the evidence that was available to the Board or its delegate at the time when the decision was made to exclude him from that fund, and not evidence that became available only subsequently and which could not have affected the decision that was taken at that time. In other words, what has to be considered is what was before the Board or its delegate when the decision was taken in 1975, not what has happened or been learnt since which, if it had been available to the Board or its delegate, would have been taken into account."

32. On that basis we turn to consider the material which we have found must have been before the delegate of the Superannuation Board, namely Dr Grant's report, the sick leave record, and the recommendation of the Director of Health. The conditions which were the basis of Dr Grant's conclusion that the applicant would not reach retiring age and (impliedly) should not be accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund, were chronic bronchitis and osteoarthritis of the left knee. The condition which appears to have been the basis of the recommendation of the Director of Health was described in that recommendation as "lumbo sacral pain". One occurrence of back strain appears from Dr Grant's report, and two periods of absence on that ground appear from the sick leave report. In none of those places is the expression "lumbo sacral" employed; nor is any other description localising the pain to a particular area of the back. We note also that the absences attributed to back pain totalled only six days in almost three years, despite the fact that Mr Steele was engaged in heavy physical labour. (As to this see the report of Dr Hembrow cited in paragraph 43 infra.) However, as we have said, it is not for this Tribunal to review the decision of the Superannuation Board; our task is merely to determine what was the physical or mental condition (or conditions) by reason of which the Superannuation Board, on 6 June 1972, was not satisfied that Mr Steele's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund under Part III of the 1922 Act.

33. We find that those conditions were: chronic bronchitis, osteoarthritis of the left knee, and (adopting the view of the Tribunal in Harrison supra) a history of lumbo sacral pain. Accordingly, those conditions, by virtue of sub-paragraph 184(7)(c)(i) of the 1976 Act are, subject to sub-paragraph (iii)(A) of paragraph 184(7)(c), to be specified on the BCC to issue in respect of Mr Steele in accordance with paragraph 184(2)(c) of that Act should the Tribunal, standing as it does in the shoes of the Commissioner by virtue of Section 43 of the AAT Act, be of the opinion defined in that paragraph.

Question (b): 21 June 1982

  1. The next question to determine is, what was "the incapacity which was the ground for the retirement of" Mr Steele on 21 June 1982 (effective from 9 October 1982). That retirement followed a medical examination on 3 April 1982, by Dr Twomey, a Commonwealth Medical Officer, who was asked to assess Mr Steele's fitness for continued duty. At that stage Mr Steele had been on "compensation leave" since 23 April 1981.
  2. Dr Twomey said in his report, under "brief history of the condition(s) including origins and the time first noted by contributor":

"Main problem consists of prolapsed IV disc L5/S1 related to original back injury 5/5/80. Also injury to (L) knee 1973 symptoms now minimal [complained of] lumbar backache worse on sitting and lying down. Examination reveals pain on extension lumbar spine flexion limited to finger tips 30 cm from floor. Tremor of legs present during examination and stiffness of patient's legs during examination makes assessment difficult".

He ticked the box opposite the statement "I consider that the examinee is unfit for continued employment in the nature of the duties presently occupied [sic] and should be re-deployed on duties of the following nature" and continued:

"should have sedentary type work not involving walking or lifting: suggest he spends his time performing checking duties if available".

He said that the condition would "affect his ... ability to undertake the duties outlined in the departmental statement" because "Chronic pain and limitation of movement lumbar spine of flexion and extension will restrict his ability to perform stated tasks"; that a "continuance of duties would tend to aggravate previous symptoms of pain"; and that Mr Steele was precluded by his condition from undertaking work involving "bending and lifting, crawling or lying on ground".

  1. It appears that no duties suitable to be undertaken by Mr Steele were available, and he was retired. No medical report other than that of Dr Twomey appears to have been prepared at that time. The space on the medical report form for completion as a Recommendation of the Department of Health has been completed and date stamped 19 April 1982, but the text in that space is not legible on the copy before the Tribunal. The conclusion is inescapable that the retirement was based on Dr Twomey's report.
  2. The Tribunal, presided over by Mr Todd, Senior Member, as he then was said in Re Burr and Commissioner for Superannuation (No 411: decided on 30 September 1980: note only at (1980) 3 ALN N1 at paragraph 22:

"The phrase "incapacity which was the ground for ... retirement" is not we consider necessarily to be seen as itself connoting a physical or mental condition. It would seem to relate more properly to the outward result that leads to a person being unable to perform, that is to say to lack the capacity to perform, his or her duties. The distinction between "incapacity" and a "condition" is clearly made in the legislative text, where the question is raised as to whether the incapacity was caused or substantially contributed to by a condition or conditions. We understand Dr Kay's difficulty with the phrase "coronary insufficiency" as having been that he did not regard it as a disease process. In Blakiston's Gould Medical Dictionary, 4th ed, coronary insufficiency is defined as "Prolonged precordial pain or discomfort without conventional evidence of myocardial infarction; subendocardial ischemia due to a disparity between coronary blood flow and myocardial needs". It is thus a result, a symptom, which is precisely what Dr Kay meant when he declined to regard coronary insufficiency as a disease. But it is we think appropriate to regard it as having constituted the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement. Likewise, coronary sclerosis may on the evidence be aptly regarded as having been the physical condition which caused or substantially contributed to the coronary insufficiency".

  1. In the present case, the incapacity apparent from Dr Twomey's report was chronic pain in, and limitation of movement of, the lumbar spine. That was, to adopt the words of the Tribunal in Burr, "the outward result" that led to Mr Steele's "being unable to perform, that is to say to lack the capacity to perform his ... duties". As we have said, the decision to retire Mr Steele was based on Dr Twomey's report, coupled with the inability of the Commonwealth to provide suitable duties. Thus we find that the incapacity which was the ground of Mr Steele's retirement, in terms of paragraph 184(2)(c) of the 1976 Act, was chronic pain in, and limitation of movement of, the lumbar spine.

Question (c): the relationship between (a) and (b)

  1. We now turn to consider whether any of the conditions, by reason of whether the Superannuation Board, in June 1972, was not satisfied that Mr Steele's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund, or any condition connected with any of those conditions, caused or substantially contributed to the chronic pain in, and limitation of movement of, Mr Steele's lumbar spine, which we have found to be the incapacity which was the ground of his retirement in June 1982.
  2. Those three conditions, which are set out in paragraph 33 supra, are chronic bronchitis, osteoarthritis of the left knee and a history of lumbo sacral pain. It is clear that the bronchitis and osteoarthritis are not related in any way to the retirement and can be disregarded. This leaves us to consider, in this context, the history, such as it was, of lumbo sacral pain, which existed when the decision of the Superannuation Board was made on 6 June 1972.
  3. That "history of lumbo sacral pain", consisted, on the evidence set out above, of two episodes:

- a sudden sharp pain in the back felt by Mr Steele when lifting a tool box in 1970, which resulted in his taking 4 days sick leave following which he had, in April 1972, to be careful what he lifted and how much (paragraph 18 supra)

- two days sick leave in October 1971 (paragraph 27 supra).

42. Dr Grant, in April 1972, did not see that back condition as significant for either of the two purposes of his report (as to which see paragraph 19 supra).

43. The only other relevant medical report from that period is the report of Dr Hembrow, an orthopaedic surgeon of considerable experience, who is now deceased. Dr Hembrow examined Mr Steele's left knee and back in April 1973 for purposes related to workers compensation. As to Mr Steele's back, he reported:

"On examination of his back he has a full range of movement. He complains of slight pain at the extreme of range. In my opinion the disability is very slight".

He stated that Mr Steele "had a mild sprain of the right lower back" which was due to the tool box lifting incident, that "the effects were permanent but the disability is negligible". No further treatment was required, and "the employee's back condition places no restrictions on his employment".

  1. Mr Steele said that in January 1983, after having considerable back pain for several years, he was diagnosed as suffering from a duodenal ulcer, which has since been treated, and while he continues taking the medication for the ulcer he suffers no back pain. He produced a number of recent medical reports, from different doctors, to the effect that he now has a full range of movement in his back.
  2. A number of medical reports before the Tribunal had been prepared in relation to Mr Steele's compensation claim arising out of an injury involving a gas cylinder in May 1980, and at the request of the respondent in those proceedings. Mr Steele expressed the opinion that the authors of those reports were influenced by Dr Twomey's report (see paragraph 35 supra) and did not go behind that report. He said that on the day when he was examined by Dr Twomey he had arrived an hour late, due to a time difference between New South Wales and Victoria (Mr Steele lives in Wodonga and Dr Twomey practises in Albury). The examination thus began badly, and was in other ways not a particularly happy or satisfactory occasion. Mr Steele had refused to make available certain reports of his general practitioner, Dr Fitzgerald, and this, in his view, meant that there was information not available to Dr Twomey which would have made a difference to his conclusion.
  3. At the hearing Dr Twomey, who gave his evidence by telephone, said that he had not himself made the diagnosis of a prolapsed intervertebral disc, but had "picked it up from sort of other information". When he was informed of the recent medical reports referred to in paragraph 44 supra, he suggested that the restriction in movement which had been noted on his examination might have been associated with muscle spasm.
  4. Mr Critchley who examined Mr Steele in May 1982 (i.e. after Dr Twomey) said in his report

X-rays of the lumbar spine show considerable narrowing of the intervertebral disc between the fifth lumbar and first sacral vertebrae with some osteophytic formation and minor degenerative changes are seen in the cervical spine.

and concluded that Mr Steele was suffering from spondylosis and degenerative lumbar disc disease.

  1. Mr McDonald examined Mr Steele in 1983, 1984 and 1985. He concluded, as to Mr Steele's back "It is probable in the fall that was described that he suffered an aggravation of a pre-existing degenerative spine disease". He showed some cynicism about the genuineness of Mr Steele's back pain. Mr Hooper also examined Mr Steele in each of those years and expressed similar views.
  2. Mr Downie, who had examined Mr Steele in April 1987 for the purpose of these proceedings, said in his report that the range of movement of Mr Steele's lumbar spine was not consistent with any significant disability at all. He had radiological changes of some severity in the lower lumbar region, although there were no X rays later than 1983. In answer to the question

Is there any relationship between the condition from which Mr Steele was suffering at the time of his entry to the Scheme namely musculo ligamentous strain in the lumbo-sacral level and his retirement incapacity (prolapsed IV Disc)?

he wrote

I think it most probable that this man's pre-existing diagnosis of musculo-ligamentous strain in the lumbo-sacral level is the earlier problem which led to his degenerate lumbo-sacral disc and the subsequent severe reaction.

  1. At the hearing he said that the radiological appearance of Mr Steele's lumbar spine would have taken a minimum of five years to develop and probably longer. He had assumed that the injury in 1970 was a disc injury not diagnosed at the time: people who have back injuries which are diagnosed as soft tissue injuries may develop radiological signs later.
  2. Mr Gayton examined Mr Steele in October 1985 in the context of his superannuation. He said in his report that he considered that a correct description of the condition for which Mr Steele suffered in April 1972 was "a musculo-ligamentous strain at the lumbo-sacral level"; and he did not consider that that condition, or any condition connected therewith, caused or substantially contributed to "the incapacity which was the ground for Mr Steele's retirement, namely 'Prolapsed I/V disc L5/S1'" (to use the words in which the question was put to him).
  3. At the hearing Mr Gayton pointed out that Mr Steele was doing heavy manual labour from 1972 to 1980. That, to Mr Gayton, indicated that Mr Steele had recovered from the minor incidents which had affected his back before 1972. He agreed that those incidents were a possible cause of Mr Steele's lumbo-sacral disc degeneration, but not that they were a probable cause. They were minor injuries to his back: the severe injury occurred in 1980, and he did not really think there was much connection between them. Asked why he saw those injuries as minor, he said that Mr Steele returned to work in one or two weeks on those occasions; the average worker who has hurt his back is off work for three to twelve months.
  4. Having considered the evidence, the Tribunal finds, on the balance of probabilities, that the "history of lumbo sacral pain" which was present in 1972 did not cause or substantially contribute to the chronic pain in and limitation of movement of Mr Steele's lumbar spine which was the incapacity which we have found to be the ground of his retirement in June 1982. We note Dr Hembrow's report (see paragraph 43 supra) as to the condition of his back in April 1973. We also note Dr Twomey's diagnosis in January 1986, in the context of his application for an invalid pension, that he had had degenerative lumbar disc disease for 6 years. Mr Gayton's evidence was specifically directed to the question in issue. Further, he considered Mr Steele's back in the context of his working life, rather than in isolation. We find his conclusions of more assistance than those of any of the other surgeons whose reports were before us.
  5. The majority of the Federal Court in Commissioner of Superannuation v Miller [1985] FCA 445; (1985) 63 ALR 237, the leading case on the meaning of "connected with" in Section 66 of the 1976 Act, found that it related to a causative connection. Davies J, with whom Pincus J expressed himself as agreeing, said

In the context, I would read the words "connected with" in ss 16(10)(b) and 66(2)(c) as dealing with the circumstance where the immediate or proximate cause of the incapacity leading to the retirement was a medical condition other than the condition existing at the time the employee became an eligible employee, but where, nevertheless, there is a chain of causation such that the medical condition existing at the time when the employee became an eligible employee can be said, through a chain of events, to have caused or substantially contributed to the incapacity causing the retirement.

  1. On that basis, we are satisfied, on the balance of probabilities, that the incapacity which was the ground for Mr Steele's retirement was not caused or substantially contributed to by any physical or mental condition which was connected with the history of lumbo sacral pain which was present in 1972.
  2. Accordingly, the answer to question (c) in paragraph 15 is "No", and it is not necessary for us to consider question (d).
  3. Mr Steele, as we have said, considered that the pain in his back from which he was suffering in 1982 was caused by the chronic duodenal ulcer which was diagnosed on 1 February 1983. It is not necessary for us to reach any conclusion as to the cause of that pain and we do not do so.
  4. For the reasons given the decisions under review will be set aside and the matter remitted to the respondent for reconsideration in accordance with appropriate directions.
