Edward Van Oort-Pieck and Tax Agents Board New South Wales [1995] AATA 149; (1995) 95 ATC 2107 (2 June 1995)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

EDWARD VAN OORT-PIECK v. TAX AGENTS BOARD NEW SOUTH WALES

No. N94/1315

AAT No. 10218

Number of pages - 4

Taxation

(1995) 95 ATC 2107

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

G.L. McDONALD (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - registration of tax agent - exemption from pre-requisites sought - discretion of Tax Agents Board - whether inability to obtain requisite experience on basis of age discrimination a reason for exemption - found not to be relevant within the limited basis for exemption

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.251BC(1)(ii), 251JA, 251L(1), (2)(a), (b)

Income Taxation Regulations reg.156(1)(a), (2)(a), (b), (c)

HEARING

SYDNEY, 16 May 1995

2:6:1995

The Applicant appeared in person.

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

G.L. McDONALD The applicant is applying for a review of the respondent's decision dated 8 December 1994, in which the respondent declined to exercise a discretion exempting the applicant from the operation of the requirements for registration as a tax agent in New South Wales.

2. At the hearing, Mr van Oort-Pieck represented himself and gave oral evidence in support of his application. Mr Godwin, a solicitor with the Australian Government Solicitor, represented the respondent. Mr Eastman, the secretary to the New South Wales Tax Agents Board, gave oral evidence. The Tribunal had before it the documents filed for purposes of section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

3. The provisions of section 251JA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") require the Tax Agents Board ("the Board") to register a person as a tax agent if inter alia he or she satisfies the Board that he or she is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters. Section 251BC of the Act disqualifies a person from registration if, inter alia -

"251BC (1) . . .

(a) . . .

(b) . . .

(ii) the person does not hold such qualifications (whether

academic, by way of experience or otherwise) as are prescribed."

The prescription is set out in regulation 156 of the Income Tax Regulations, the relevant provisions of which are in the following terms-

"156 (1) For the purposes of subparagraph 251BC(1)(b)(ii) of the

Act, the following qualifications are prescribed:

(a) the person:

(i) shall have completed the academic requirements for the award

of a degree, diploma or other qualification from an Australian

university, college of advanced education or other tertiary

institution of an equivalent standard, and have passed

examinations in such subjects, under whatever name, which an

appropriate authority of the university, college of advanced

education or other tertiary institution certifies to the Board to

represent a course of study in accountancy of not less than 3

years' duration and in commercial law of not less than 18 months'

duration or shall possess such other qualifications as the Board

regards as equivalent to those qualifications;

(ii) shall have:

(A) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for

not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding 5 years;

(B) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent

that the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in

sub-subparagraph (a); or

(C) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as

the Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant

employment as referred to in sub-subparagraph (a); and

(iii) shall have, by written examination, successfully completed a

course of study in Australian income tax law acceptable to the

Board;

. . .

(2) In this regulation 'relevant employment' means employment by a

person or a partnership or as a member of a partnership in the

course of which there has been substantial involvement in income tax

matters including:

(a) the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax

returns;

(b) the preparation or examination of objections to assessments

issued in respect of such returns; and

(c) the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns,

assessments or objections."

4. There is no dispute that the applicant qualifies under the provisions of regulation 156(1)(a)(i) as he holds the equivalent of a Bachelor of Economics (with Honours) from Leiden University in the Netherlands, a graduate Diploma of Accounting from Macquarie University and is a certified practising accountant. Additionally, he has completed six tax subjects in the ATAX Bachelor of Taxation programme at The University of New South Wales. However, the applicant concedes that he does not qualify under the provisions of regulation 156(1)(a)(ii). Indeed, it is the applicant's evidence that he has tried unsuccessfully to obtain employment by applying to between 100 and 150 public accountancy firms so that he might acquire the requisite experience. He believes that he is precluded from obtaining employment due to age discrimination.

5. Since the applicant has been unsuccessful in obtaining the necessary experience, he has sought exemption under the provisions of section 251L of the Act. The relevant sub-sections, 251L(1) and (2), are in the following terms:

"251L (1) A person, other than a person exempted under this section,

shall not demand or receive any fee for or in relation to the

preparation of any income tax return or objection, or for or in

relation to the transaction of any business on behalf of a taxpayer

in income tax matters, unless he is a registered tax agent.

Penalty $2,000

251L (2) A Board may, in its discretion, exempt any person from the

operation of this section upon being satisfied -

(a) that the total income derived by that person as a tax agent

during the period of 12 months immediately preceding the date of his

application for such exemption did not exceed $40;

(b) (Omitted by Act No 28 of 1944)."

It is because the applicant has been unable to become registered as a tax agent under section 251JA(1) of the Act that he has made application for the exemption.

6. To assist in determining how the legislation should be interpreted, Mr Godwin referred to its legislative history. The registration of tax agents was introduced into the Federal legislation by Act No 10 of 1943. The legislation was designed to replace the then existing system of tax agents being registered under State legislation in Queensland, South Australia, Tasmania and New South Wales. There was no such legislation covering Western Australia and Victoria. The purpose was to introduce a uniform system following the passage of uniform income tax legislation introduced in 1942. In his second reading speech, the Treasurer, Mr B. Chifley, said, inter alia (House of Representatives, Hansard, 11 February 1943, p.559-560):

"Two most important features of the proposals are - first, that

subject to minor exemptions, no person other than a registered tax

agent may charge a fee for preparing income tax returns . . . ."

7. In the form in which it was originally introduced, section 251L(2) of the Act had a further sub-clause (b), which was compendious upon sub- clause (a). It was in the following terms:

"(b) that there is no registered tax agent within a reasonable

distance of the place where the applicant for such exemption resides

or carries on business."

Sub-clause (b) gives some context to what was sought to be achieved in section 251L(2) of the Act. However, sub-clause (b) was repealed by Act No 28 of 1944.

8. Mr Godwin submitted that section 251L(2)(a) of the Act may have been retained in order to permit tax agents, who had been registered under the State system, to continue in operation without the need for registration under the Federal system in cases where they only had a small workload. Mr van Oort-Pieck submitted that whatever the history relating to the section, the fact that it remained in place meant that it was open to be used and that if, as here, the Board insisted on the requirements set out in regulation 156 to be fulfilled, then that would render section 251L(2)(a) of the Act otiose.

9. Part VIIA of the Act and Part 9 of the Income Tax Regulations provide a comprehensive requirement for the registration of tax agents. The provisions relating to both academic and experience pre-requisites provide for the exercise of a discretion to allow the Board flexibility when considering applications for registration. Section 251L(1) of the Act provides for a penalty in circumstances when a person, who is not so registered, acts as a tax agent. Section 251L(2) of the Act requires a person, who wants exemption, to be both a tax agent and to have earned less than $40 per annum, i.e. the sub-section allows a person, who has been registered as a tax agent, to claim exemption if he or she does not earn an income as a tax agent in excess of $40 per annum. The figure expressed in the original legislation was pounds 20, and Parliament has not sought to change it, except by converting it to a dollar equivalent. This interpretation is consistent with the "minor exemptions" mentioned by the then Treasurer when the legislation was introduced in 1943.

10. The applicant, being unable to qualify under the provisions of the Act, is not entitled to be registered as a taxation agent. The Board's decision refusing to exercise its discretion in his favour is, in the view of the Tribunal, the correct decision. For the reasons given, the Tribunal is of the view that the provisions of section 251L(2) of the Act are not applicable to his circumstances. It follows, that the claim of discrimination on the basis of age with respect to obtaining employment of the type which would lead to the the applicant gaining sufficient experience do not arise in the context of section 251L(2) of the Act and is not an issue within the discretion of the Board.

11. The decision under review should, for the reasons given, be affirmed.
