Re Ronald Paul Rees and Commissioner of Superannuation [1983] AATA 279 (19 August 1983)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: RONALD PAUL REES

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. A83/13

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Deputy President A.N. Hall

Dr A.H. Marsh (Member)

Mr I. Prowse (Member)

CATCHWORDS

superannuation _ retirement on medical grounds at age 45 years _ psychiatric disorder _ mental health improved after retirement _ determination that health so restored as to be able to resume full-time duties in Public Service _ likelihood of recurrence of psychiatric symptoms on re-employment _ decision of Commissioner for Superannuation set aside

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s.43

Superannuation Act 1976, s.75(1)

HEARING

CANBERRA

19:8:1983

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the decision under review and in substitution therefor decides that it is not satisfied that the health of the applicant has become so restored as to enable him to perform any duties of a kind that are, in the opinion of the Tribunal, suitable to be performed by him.

DECISION

1. At the conclusion of the hearing of this matter, the Tribunal delivered orally the following ex tempore Reasons for Decision which are now reduced to writing pursuant to s.43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

2. The facts in this case fall within a relatively small compass. The applicant, Mr Ronald Paul Rees, who is now aged 55, was retired from the Public Service on the grounds of invalidity on 4 February 1974. He was then aged 45 years. The medical reasons for that retirement were 'personality disorder of the paranoid hypochondriac depressive type'. The applicant's substantive position in the Service at the time was a Clerk Class 5 in the Statistics Branch of the Department of Treasury.

3. Following his retirement his medical condition was reviewed biannually and there are medical reports in evidence before us of examinations conducted by Commonwealth Medical Officers in 1975, 1977 and 1979 (see Exhibits T4, pp15-16, 19-20 and 28-29). These reports and the clinical notes taken at the time of each examination show, as one might have hoped, a progressive improvement in Mr Rees' psychiatric condition. So much so that Dr McGregor, the Commonwealth Medical Officer who examined Mr Rees on 23 May 1979, considered that whilst he was not fit to resume his former duties, he could satisfactorily perform clerical duties, especially on a part-time basis.

4. It was following this review by Dr McGregor (as to which the applicant was somewhat critical regarding the attention given to the review by the doctor) that steps were initiated by the Commissioner for Superannuation, pursuant to s.75(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976, which provides as follows:

'75.(1) Where the Commissioner is satisfied, after receiving the report or reports of a medical practitioner or medical practitioners with respect to the health of a person to whom invalidity pension is payable, that the health of the person has become so restored as to enable him to perform duties of a kind that are, in the opinion of the Commissioner, suitable to be performed by him (having regard to the duties performed by him immediately before his retirement on the ground of invalidity and to such other matters as the Commissioner considers relevant), the Commissioner shall so inform such person or authority as the Commissioner considers appropriate with a view to that person or authority finding suitable employment for the pensioner.'

5. In the first instance, a delegate of the Commissioner wrote to Mr Rees by letter dated 28 May 1980, advising him that the Commissioner was satisfied that his health had become so restored as to require the Commissioner to take action in terms of s.75(1) of the Act. On the same date a letter was sent to the Regional Director of the Public Service Board advising of the Commonwealth Medical Officer's recommendation and asking him to arrange suitable employment on either a permanent or a temporary basis.

6. There then followed further correspondence including the provision by the applicant of a medical report dated 19 June 1980 from his treating doctor, Dr Geoffrey Holt (Exhibit T4, p38). In that report Dr Holt expressed the opinion that it was unlikely that Mr Rees would be able to cope any better (with Public Service duties) in 1980 than he could in 1973 _ even more because he was by then 7 years out of touch.

7. On 30 June 1980, advice was received by the Commissioner from the Regional Director of the Public Service Board that the prospect of finding part-time clerical duties for a person such as Mr Rees were fairly remote. It was some few months later that a decision was taken to have Mr Rees examined by Dr Merrifield _ a psychiatrist practising in Canberra.

8. This examination took place on 18 November 1980 and we have Dr Merrifield's report dated 2 December 1980 in evidence before us. Dr Merrifield reviewed the whole history of the matter including the psychiatric evidence, namely a report dated 10 August 1973 from Dr J.B. Truman, that had been available at the time when Mr Rees retired from the Public Service. Dr Merrifield said that having reviewed that history he had no doubt that it was appropriate that Mr Rees had been retired when he was. As the result of his examination, however, Dr Merrifield considered that Mr Rees' mental health had improved and that there was no doubt that he would be capable of fulfilling the work as set out in his duty statement, a copy of which had been sent to Dr Merrifield with the other papers.

9. Dr Merrifield was called by the Commissioner to give evidence at the hearing. It appeared from his evidence that what he was contemplating in saying this was that Mr Rees would be able to undertake clerical duties. Dr Merrifield said that he was not directing his mind specifically to any advances in technology that might have occurred in the intervening years.

10. Dr Merrifield had gone on to say in his report that there were 3 issues that had to be looked at in this case. The first of them was that, at the time when he saw Mr Rees, he had already been retired for 6 years and had never really had the expectation that he would go back to work. Dr Merrifield commented that, in those circumstances, if he were returned to work he would go back with resentment and without any devotion. Secondly, he posed the question as to what sort of a mental reaction the applicant would have if put back into a Public Service work environment. He felt that this was totally unpredictable, but added that he could imagine that it would be a very stressful situation. Thirdly, he said that there was a problem in relation to people who retire from the Public Service for reasons either physical or mental, and who, after retirement, lose their symptoms. One has to predict whether by going back into the former work situation _ which at least in part caused the presentation of those symptoms _ there is going to be a reactivation of the earlier condition.

11. Dr Merrifield commented that, in the present case, Mr Rees was a man who had had a less than satisfactory and a less than satisfying career in the Public Service. In 1973, he had presented with severe symptoms which had certainly justified his retirement. Dr Merrifield went on to say that, with the alleviation of the pressures of the Public Service system, Mr Rees had really lost all that symptomatology and in that sense was fit to go back to work. Dr Merrifield then commented:

'The question which I guess is almost unanswerable is that entering that situation with no motivation and with hostility; with his views that the Service is against him; one has then to try and project whether that is going to produce a return of all his symptoms.'

After canvassing that issue further, he said, 'I cannot answer the question but in terms of probability I suspect that that is what may happen.'

12. Dr Merrifield's report was sent to the Commissioner under cover of a letter dated 16 December 1980 from Dr H.S. Turner, a Senior Medical Officer of the Department of Health (see Exhibit T4, p50). Dr Turner's letter, so far as relevant, was as follows:

'My opinion is that Mr Rees is likely to have an exacerbation of his symptoms if he returns to work in the Public Service particularly as he was retired on the grounds of invalidity about six years ago.

As Dr Merrifield states, there is no way to answer the question of the return of his symptoms other than to allow him to return to work.

However his attitude from the beginning would be such that he is unlikely to perform his work (anywhere in the Public Service) competently.'

13. In the light of these reports, the Commissioner advised the Public Service Board by letter dated 19 January 1983 (Exhibit T6, p55) that he was of the opinion that the health of Mr Rees had become so restored as to enable him to perform duties of a kind that were suitable to be performed by him, namely, duties of a clerical nature. It was after being advised of this decision that Mr Rees applied to this Tribunal for review. Mr Rees appeared on his own behalf and made an oral statement to the Tribunal. Mr R. Wilde appeared on behalf of the Commissioner and, as earlier noted, called Dr Merrifield to give evidence.

14. In the intervening years since his retirement, Mr Rees has been engaged in some work activities which he said have mainly been designed to help restore him to a satisfactory state of physical fitness and to help him recover from the experiences of his Public Service career. The work that he has done included a period of fairly heavy physical work assisting 2 days per week on a property at Collector. In latter years, he has been involved in a small carpet cleaning business which he undertakes on a part-time basis, doing contract carpet cleaning work in Canberra, Braidwood and surrounding districts. Mr Rees has not, since his retirement, been engaged in any clerical duties of any consequence and has not kept up-to-date with the rapid advances in computer technology that would be necessary if he were to return to work such as he had been doing prior to the date of his retirement.

15. The information before the Tribunal about Mr Rees' income from his part-time business activities is not comprehensive but, so far as it goes, the evidence is that in the combined financial years ended 30 June 1978 and 30 June 1979, his total earnings were less than $5,500. In the financial year ended 30 June 1982 his net profit from his business was $1,463. This level of income tends to confirm that the work activities in which Mr Rees has been engaged have been of a truly part-time nature. It certainly does not suggest that Mr Rees has at any stage been engaged in full-time employment since his retirement.

16. Thus the evidence establishes that Mr Rees, who is now 55 years old (an age at which many Public Servants are thinking about retirement) has not held down a full-time job for the past 10 years and, in particular, has not worked in clerical or related duties in a Public Service type environment for that time. It is also important to note that, particularly in latter years, he has been, in effect, his own boss. He has been able to work on a part-time basis, at his own pace and answerable to no one but himself and his customer.

17. The evidence given by Dr Merrifield in amplification of his report establishes that whilst Mr Rees is presently free of the psychiatric symptoms that had been so much in evidence during the latter part of his Public Service career, he nevertheless has continuing underlying personality traits and, in some respects, entrenched _ perhaps paranoid _ attitudes towards the Public Service. Dr Merrifield considered that at the applicant's age, there was no real prospect of that position changing and that the attitudes which manifested themselves in psychiatric symptoms prior to his retirement, could well be exacerbated or recur if Mr Rees were to be restored to a position within the Public Service. Dr Merrifield said that in his opinion the attitudes which were likely to produce such a reaction were now beyond Mr Rees' conscious control and had become an entrenched part of his personality. He did not consider that there was any likelihood of those entrenched attitudes disappearing.

18. In the statement of reasons before us, it was said, as we earlier noted, that it was the Commissioner's opinion that Mr Rees' health has become so restored as to enable him to perform duties, namely those of a clerk, which in the opinion of the Commissioner were suitable to be performed by Mr Rees. The Commissioner had before him, of course, the report of Dr Merrifield. In paragraph 7 of the statement of reasons, the Commissioner, after noting Dr Merrifield's opinion that Mr Rees' mental health was such that he would be capable of fulfilling his former duties, commented as follows:

'There is, however, a possibility, perhaps a probability, that because of his antipathy towards the Public Service, Mr Rees' return to work in the Public Service could be unsuccessful. Thus it seems that whether or not Mr Rees' return to work would be successful would lie very much in his hands, that if Mr Rees wanted to make his return to work a success he could so so.'

19. In relation to these statements we would have to say that having had the benefit of hearing the evidence of Dr Merrifield, we could not agree with the Commissioner's conclusion. In our view, the matter has got beyond any question of the applicant's conscious control.

20. The Commissioner went on to say, in paragraph 8:

'The possibility that Mr Rees may adopt an attitude towards returning to work in the Public Service that might militate against a successful return is not a matter that I consider relevant to my consideration under section 75. The issue is whether Mr Rees' health has become so restored as to enable him to perform duties of a kind that are, in my opinion, suitable to be performed by him (having regard to the duties performed by him immediately before his invalidity retirement and as such other matters as I consider relevant). I am satisfied that his health has become so restored.'

21. It is unnecessary for us to deal with this point directly because we take a different view of the psychiatric background to Mr Rees' attitudes. We would certainly consider that the psychiatric history that Mr Rees has is a relevant consideration when one comes to decide, in terms of s.75, whether there is work that is suitable to be performed by him.

22. In summation, therefore, whilst we consider that the applicant's mental health has undoubtedly improved since the date of his retirement, we are persuaded by the evidence of Dr Merrifield (supported as he is by the report of Dr Turner) that, having regard to his psychiatric history, to his previous employment record in the Public Service, and to his fairly limited work experience in the past 10 years, Mr Rees' health is not so restored as to enable him to perform any duties in the Public Service. In our view, that extends either to duties of a clerical nature, or duties that may be more compatible with the type of non-clerical work that he has done on a part-time basis in recent years.

23. In our opinion, the probabilities are, that if Mr Rees were returned to the Public Service he would quickly suffer a recurrence of his former symptoms, and we believe that, in substance, that is the opinion of Dr Merrifield and Dr Turner. That probability is, as we have said, a proper matter for consideration on the question of whether there is suitable employment available in the Public Service for Mr Rees.

24. Mr Wilde put to us, and properly so, that the Commissioner must have regard, in his administration of the Act, to the protection of the Superannuation Fund against claims upon it that are not properly sustainable in accordance with the Act. We would not, of course, seek to deny that proposition, and it is a matter that we ourselves have taken into consideration in coming to our decision in this case. Nevertheless, it is only one of the factors to be considered. One must also have regard to the nature and purpose of the Superannuation Act as being intended to provide, in appropriate cases, for retirement and invalidity benefits to people who come within the terms of that legislation.

25. In reaching the conclusion that we have in this case, we have had regard to the whole of the evidence and documentary material before us. Taking all that evidence and material into account we would propose to set aside the decision under review and to substitute the opinion that we have expressed for that of the Commissioner.
