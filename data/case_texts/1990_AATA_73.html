Re Taxation Appeals No: St87/182 [1990] AATA 73; 21 ATR 3348 (3 April 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No: ST87/182

AAT No. 5809

Income Tax

[1990] AATA 73; 21 ATR 3348

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

D.J. Trowse(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Allowable Deductions - whether amounts expended in completion of training programme deductible under ss.51(1)

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss.51(1)

Ronpibon Tin NL and Tongkah Compound NL v F.C. of T. [1949] HCA 15; (1948-49) 78 CLR 47

F.C. of T. v Maddalena 71 ATC 4161

F.C. of T. v Klan 85 ATC 4060

HEARING

ADELAIDE

3:4:1990

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

This reference relates to expenditure incurred in the completion of a training programme undertaken at the London School of Tropical Medicine and raises the question as to whether those outgoings qualify for deduction in terms of sub-section 51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ('the Act'). That course had been approved by the Royal Australasian College of Physicians and the successful completion of same was regarded as a final compliance with the advanced training requirements for admission as a Fellow of that professional body. The amount in issue is $7113 and the year of income is 1982.

2. It should be noted that the applicant has resided out of Australia for several years and that, at his request, the hearing of his application proceeded on the basis of a statement of agreed facts and written submissions.

3. The factual background, which has been extracted from the statement of agreed facts and other correspondence tendered by agreement, is as follows:

. the applicant has been a licensed medical practitioner in

South Australia since graduation in 1974

. the applicant was employed as a medical registrar at a public

hospital in South Australia from January 1975 until March

1979

. that service qualified as basic physician training for the

purposes of the Royal Australasian College of Physicians

('the R.A.C.P.')

. after passing an examination set by the R.A.C.P. in March

1979, the applicant became an advanced trainee physician with

that body

. the applicant then elected to train further in the

specialisation of infectious diseases, the effect of which

was an obligation to undertake three years of approved

advanced training in order to satisfy the entry criteria

required of a Fellow

. the position of registrar was extended to end in January 1980

. in February 1980, the applicant was successful in obtaining a

position as registrar at a public hospital in Victoria

. both the positions as registrar were approved by the R.A.C.P.

as part of the training programme for specialisation in

infectious diseases

. the applicant, having decided to study overseas, resigned

from the Victorian posting in January 1982

. before leaving Australia, the applicant had -

(1) unsuccessfully applied for grants and travelling

scholarships from the R.A.C.P. and the University of

Adelaide

(2) attended interviews with senior officials of the

Victorian and South Australian State Health

Departments and given verbal assurances that, on

completion of requirements for the Fellowship of the

R.A.C.P. and training in the field of public health

overseas, he would be well-qualified to apply for a

permanent position in their departments

. on the 17 February 1982, the applicant left Australia for a

six month period of full-time postgraduate study at the

London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine

. that study was approved by the R.A.C.P. as completing the

advanced training requirements for admission as a Fellow of

the R.A.C.P.

. at the time of departure from Australia there was no

agreement for re-employment between the applicant and any

employer

. during the period February to June 1982, the following

amounts were expended and claimed by the applicant in his

1982 return of income -

Passport 30

Fee for course 2500

Qantas Air Fare 948

Departure tax and ticket allowance 30

Examination fees 205

Accommodation in London 1800

Meals, local fares and general

living expenses 1600

$7113

. upon completion of the course, the applicant travelled to the

United States, and from September 1982 to June 1983 he

completed a Masters of Public Health course at Harvard

Medical School

. the applicant returned to Australia in June 1983 and was

awarded the status of Fellow of the R.A.C.P.

. shortly after his return, the applicant accepted a position

at the Center for Disease Control in Atlanta, Georgia in the

United States of America

. from 17 February 1982 to 30 June 1982, the applicant did not

derive any income from personal exertion

. to date, the applicant has not been engaged in general

practice, or carried on a business at any time

. although the applicant has indicated an intention to seek a

position in Australia, he still remains employed at the

Center for Disease Control in Georgia

4. Subsection 51(1) of the Act provides:

'All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they

are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable

income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a

business for the purpose of gaining or producing such

income, shall be allowable deductions except to the

extent to which they are losses or outgoings of

capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature,

or are incurred in relation to the gaining or

production of exempt income.'

It was not disputed that the applicant was not at any stage carrying on a business for the purpose of producing assessable income and thus the applicant is limited to the first of the two limbs.

5. The first limb was comprehensively considered by the High Court in the case of Ronpibon Tin NL and Tongkah Compound NL v F.C. of T. (1948-49) 78 CLR 347, and the following excerpts taken from the joint judgment demonstrate the nature of the relationship which must exist between the derivation of assessable income and the incurring of the outgoing, if such expenditure is to qualify as an allowable deduction. At pages 56-57 the court made the following observations:

'For expenditure to form an allowable deduction as an

outgoing incurred in gaining or producing the

assessable income it must be incidental and relevant

to that end. The words "incurred in gaining or

producing the assessable income" mean in the course of

gaining or producing such income ...

In brief substance, to come within the initial part of

the sub-section it is both sufficient and necessary

that the occasion of the loss or outgoing should be

found in whatever is productive of the assessable

income or, if none be produced, would be expected to

produce assessable income.'

and further at page 58:

'It is therefore necessary to return to the opening

words of s.51(1) and inquire to what extent the

expenditure of the respective companies was incurred

in gaining or producing the assessable income.'

From the foregoing, it is clear that the extent or degree of the relationship between the outgoing and the assessable income is of prime importance.

6. It is of some significance that the Commissioner in disallowing the claim at the assessment stage cited his reasons as 'College of Physicians Training Programme disallowed as employment had ceased before programme commenced'. Not only is that statement incorrect, but the unfortunate effect is that the applicant has expended most of his efforts in endeavouring to overcome that apparent deficiency. To a lesser extent, he contended that there existed a connection between the outgoings in question and his earnings derived from the hospital in Victoria during the period July 1981 and January 1982, and also with income likely to be derived in the future as a result of his additional qualification.

7. The submission that the expenditure was in some way related to his income-earning activities whilst occupying the position as registrar to the hospital in Victoria seems to be based on the premise that some of the outgoings under review were in fact paid during that same period. Such an interpretation is a misconstruction of the sub-section. The test is the extent or degree of the relationship between the outgoings and the gaining of assessable income. The Tribunal is not able to perceive any connection whatsoever between the outgoings of $7113 which relate to study commenced after January 1982, and the performance of duties prior to that date. In short, they were not incurred in the course of gaining or producing that income.

8. The deductibility of outgoings directed to the improvement of prospects in the future was addressed by Ormiston J. in the case of F.C. of T. v Klan 85 ATC 4060. Mr Klan, a history teacher, resigned his teaching post in Australia and travelled to the United Kingdom where he obtained a teaching position. He contemplated that the experience gained from teaching overseas would further his career prospects as a teacher in Australia. Upon his return that expectation was realised. In holding that the outgoings associated with the travel were not deductible under sub-section 51(1) the learned judge had the following to say at page 4067:

'In truth what the expenditure related to was the

obtaining of a new position either as headmaster, or,

as events have so far turned out, as head of a

department. Regrettable as it may seem, "the cost to

an employee of obtaining his employment does not form

an outgoing incurred in the course of earning the

wages payable in the employment": per Barwick C.J. in

F.C. of T. v Maddalena 71 ATC 4161; (1971) 45 ALJR 426.'

and further at page 4068:

'Although the deduction claimed in Maddalena's case

was for rather mundane expenditure, the principles

there stated are directly relevant to the present case

and they solve the temporal dilemma posed by the

requirement that outgoings should be incurred "in"

i.e. in the course of, gaining or producing assessable

income. It is one thing to assert that it is

unnecessary that the outgoing need produce, as a

matter of causation, assessable income in the year in

which the outgoing was incurred. It is quite another

to say that expenditure incurred which is entirely

unrelated to existing activities which produce

assessable income can come within the section because

its object and long-term effect is better to equip the

taxpayer to earn income in the future. In my opinion,

the taxpayer's removal expenses to Colwyn Bay are

properly characterised as outgoings incurred to fit

him to obtain employment in new and more remunerative

positions as a headmaster or head of department on his

return to Australia. It follows that, as those

payments were of "moneys spent to obtain a new

employment" they were not allowable deductions as

outgoing incurred in gaining or producing assessable

income: cf per Menzies J. in Maddalena's case.'

9. I find that the object of undertaking the course of study at the London School of Tropical Medicine was to gain the qualification of Fellow of the R.A.C.P., in the expectation that such advancement would equip the applicant to obtain employment in new, and more remunerative, positions. On the authority of Maddalena and Klan, it follows that the outgoings subject to this review were not incurred in the production of assessable income. In similar vein, the expenditure now in question was not incurred in the production of the salaried income from the Center of Disease Control in the United States of America. In any event that income is exempt income in terms of section 23(q), as it then was, and thus the preclusion clause contained in sub-section 51(1) has application.

10. For the reasons stated above, the Tribunal affirms the Commissioner's decision on the objection.
