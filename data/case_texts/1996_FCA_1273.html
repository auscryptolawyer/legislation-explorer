Radilo Enterprises Pty Limited v Commissioner of Taxation [1996] FCA 1273 (29 February 1996)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

RADILO ENTERPRISES PTY LIMITED v. COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. G99 of 1995

FED No. 86/96

Number of pages - 7

Income Tax Assessment Act

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEW SOUTH WALES DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

DAVIES J

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax Assessment Act - review of assessment - entitlement to a rebate under s.46(1)(b) - whether rebate excluded by s.46D - whether issue of non-redeemable, non-cumulative convertible preference shares by bank to a non-private company "may reasonably be regarded as equivalent to the payment of interest on a loan" - what is a loan - what is equivalence.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) - ss.46(1)(b), 46D

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v The Myer Emporium Ltd [1987] HCA 18; (1987) 163 CLR 199

Minister for Industry and Commerce v Western Mining Corporation Ltd (1985) 7
FCR 67

HEARING

SYDNEY, 7 February 1996

29:2:1996

Counsel for the applicant: Mr D.H. Bloom QC

Mr K.J. Burgess

Solicitors for the applicant: Rosenblum and Partners

Counsel for the respondent: Mr A.H. Slater QC

Mr R. Hamilton

Solicitor for the respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

THE COURT ORDERS THAT:

1. The objection decision be set aside and the matter be remitted to

the Commissioner of Taxation to amend the assessment so as to allow

Radilo Enterprises Pty Limited a rebate in accordance with s.46 of

the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth).

2. The respondent pay the costs of the proceedings.

NOTE: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

DAVIES J This is an appeal from a disallowance by the Commissioner of Taxation of the applicant's objection to an income tax assessment issued in respect of the period 15 December 1994 to 11 January 1995. Radilo was incorporated on 15 December 1994 and is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Bain Securities Ltd ("BSL"), which itself is controlled by Deutsche Bank AG, a listed company in Germany. Radilo is therefore not a private company for the purposes of s.46 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) ("the Act").

2. As a test case and by arrangement between BSL and the Commissioner, Radilo was incorporated and acquired from BSL 100 of the non-redeemable, non-cumulative, converting, preference shares ("the CP shares") in the capital issued by the Australian and New Zealand Banking Group Ltd ("ANZ") which were held by BSL. The CP shares carried an entitlement to fixed non-cumulative preference dividends of $6.625 per CP share payable on 11 January and 11 July of each year.

3. The issue is whether Radilo, not being a private company, was entitled to a rebate on the dividend of $663 which it received, that is to the ordinary rebate allowable under s.46(2)(b) of the Act, or whether that rebate was excluded by the provisions of s.46D of the Act. Section 46D provides, inter alia:-

"46D(1) In this section, unless the contrary intention appears:

...

'commencing time' means 1 o'clock in the afternoon, by legal time in

the Australian Capital Territory, on 10 December 1986;

'finance' includes money raised by the issue of shares;

'finance arrangement', in relation to a company, means an arrangement

entered into or carried out by any of the parties to the arrangement

for the purpose, or for purposes that included the purpose:

(a) of enabling the company or an associate of the company to obtain

finance (whether by way of renewal or otherwise); or

(b) of enabling the company or an associate of the company to obtain

an extension of the period for which finance was obtained under an

earlier arrangement;

'loan' includes the provision of credit or any other form of financial

accommodation;

...

46D(2) A dividend is a debt dividend for the purposes of this section

if, and only if:

(a) the dividend is paid to a shareholder after the commencing time in

respect of a share in a company (whether the company is a resident or

a non-resident);

(b) the dividend is paid:

(i) in respect of a share issued after the commencing time; or

(ii) under a finance arrangement entered into after the commencing

time; and

(c) having regard to:

(i) the manner in which the amount of dividends in respect of the

share was to be calculated;

(ii) the conditions applicable to the payment of dividends in respect

of the share; and

(iii) any other relevant matters;

the payment of the dividend may reasonably be regarded as equivalent

to the payment of interest on a loan.

46D(3) A shareholder is not entitled to, and shall not be allowed, a

rebate under section 46 or 46A in respect of:

(a) a debt dividend paid before 1 July 1987; or

(b) the unfranked part of a debt dividend paid on or after 1 July

1987."

4. The Commissioner of Taxation took the view that the dividend received by Radilo was a "debt dividend" for the purposes of s.46D. Section 46D(2) uses the expression "may reasonably be regarded". These words do not confer a discretion or decision-making power upon the Commissioner of Taxation but are an integral part of the description of a "debt dividend". It is for the Court to determine whether the dividend "may reasonably be regarded as equivalent to the payment of interest on a loan."

5. In late 1990 and early 1991, the management of ANZ wished to raise additional capital so as to build up the ANZ's capital adequacy ratio. ANZ's management desired to raise Tier 1 capital, that is to say, capital which the Reserve Bank would regard as core capital comprised of the highest quality capital elements.

6. In mid-1991, ANZ offered to the public and subsequently issued 6,000,000 non-redeemable CP shares of $1 each at a premium of $99 each. The shares carried a right to a non-cumulative preference dividend (unfranked) of $6.625 per CP share payable on 11 January and 11 July, being 13.25% per annum. On 11 July 1995, the shares were to convert into fully paid ANZ ordinary shares. I need not set out the formula for conversion. Its effect was that the conversion would be made by reference to a 10% discount to the prevailing weighted average sale price of fully paid ordinary shares in ANZ sold on the Australian stock exchange during the 5 trading days preceding the conversion date, 11 July 1995. The formula had the effect that each share would convert into a number of ordinary shares that would have a value of $111.11 by reference to that weighted average sale price.

7. The shares were non-redeemable and they were convertible, that is to say, the terms of the issue bound ANZ and the shareholders to the conversion of the CP shares into ordinary shares of ANZ on 11 July 1995. The directors had power to determine a later optional date for conversion, 11 July 1996, 11 July 1997, 11 July 1998, of which shareholders might take advantage.

8. The CP shares were non-cumulative. Dividends were payable out of available distributable profits. Terms of the issue provided inter alia:-

"2.1 The following provisions of Article 4B(3)(b) shall apply to the

Preference Shares:

(a) Article 4B(3)(b)(i), namely, that dividends are to be declared and

paid in full if, in the opinion of the Directors, the distributable

profits of the Company are sufficient to cover the payment in full of

dividends on the Preference Shares on a Dividend Payment Date, and

also the payment in full of all other dividends stated to be payable

on such date on any other preference shares expressed to rank pari

passu with them for participation in profits;

(b) Article 4B(3)(b)(ii), namely, that if, in the opinion of the

Directors, the distributable profits of the Company are insufficient

to cover the payment in full on the Preference Shares on any Dividend

Payment Date, and also the payment in full of other dividends stated

to be payable on such date on any other preference shares expressed to

rank pari passu with them for participation in profits, then dividends

shall be declared by the Directors pro-rata between those shares to

the extent of the available distributable profits, as provided by

Article 4B(3)(b)(ii);

(c) Article 4B(3)(b)(iii), namely, that if, in the opinion of the

Directors, the payment of any dividend would breach or cause a breach

of the capital adequacy requirements applicable to the Company and/or

its subsidiaries under the rules applicable to Australian banks then

no dividend shall be declared or paid;

(d) Article 4B(b)(iv), namely, that the Preference Shares shall carry

no further right to participate in the profits of the Company and if

and to the extent that all or any part of any dividend is not paid for

the reasons described in paragraphs (b) or (c) above, the holders

shall have no claim in respect of such non-payment;

..."

9. CP shareholders had the right to receive notices, reports and balance sheets and to attend general meetings but did not have the right to vote save at a meeting convened for the purpose of reducing capital, for a winding up or for sanctioning the sale of the undertaking or where the proposition to be submitted to the meeting directly affected their rights and privileges or the dividend on the shares was in arrears more than six months.

10. The CP shares were listed on the Stock Exchange and traded thereon. In mid-1994, BSL invested $25m in the purchase of CP shares on the market, paying $118.50 per share. Later, 100 of these shares were transferred to Radilo for the purpose of setting up this test case. Counsel agreed that the case should be considered on the facts as they would have been had BSL retained the shares.

11. A director of BSL, Mr P J Costa, gave evidence that the Bain and Co group regarded the CP shares as equity instruments and that the shares were therefore held by BSL. Had they been regarded as debt instruments, they would have been owned and traded by another subsidiary, BCML. However, other evidence given to the Court shows that the CP shares attracted many fixed interest investors who would otherwise have invested in debt instruments of one form or another.

12. Because many of the holders of the CP shares were investors who preferred to invest in term securities at a fixed rate of interest, ANZ came to perceive that the terms on which the shares had been issued were likely to give rise to a problem. The problem was that, if those shareholders who preferred to invest their moneys at interest for a term sold their shares shortly before or on the conversion date of 11 July 1995, or shortly thereafter, there would be likely to be a fall in the value of the shares which would disadvantage the holders of the CP shares, who might not recover their capital, and it would disadvantage also the other holders of ordinary shares in ANZ. To overcome this problem, ANZ made an offer on 22 September 1994 to buy back the ordinary shares into which the CP shares would convert. Counsel agreed that, for the purposes of this test case, the offer to buy back was irrelevant and is to be ignored save insofar as it supports the point on which the Commissioner relies, namely that the CP shares attracted many investors who otherwise would have invested in fixed interest term securities.

13. It had been anticipated in the planning stage that the CP shares would attract such investors. Thus, on 19 March 1991, McIntosh Corporate Ltd wrote to the Chief Strategist, ANZ:-

"... we feel the likely market for such an instrument would be the

fixed interest divisions of the various institutions."

On 27 March 1991, Potter Warburg wrote to the Chief Strategist, ANZ:-

"... it is felt that the instrument is likely to have more appeal to

fixed interest rather than equity investors because of its essentially

fixed interest character."

It was apparently considered that the 10% discount on conversion would be sufficient to encourage investors to retain their shares for conversion and to hold them thereafter rather than selling them.

14. The issue between the parties is a brief one and turns upon the interpretation of s.46D of the Act rather than upon any question of fact. The view taken by the Commissioner was that a dividend paid on the CP shares would be "equivalent to the payment of interest on a loan" because it would be a payment of a fixed rate of interest and, on conversion date, the shareholder would receive its capital either in the form of the shares to which the CP shares were converted or in the form of cash if it chose to sell the shares. Mr A H Slater QC, with whom Mr R Hamilton appeared for the Commissioner, submitted that the matter should be looked at from the point of view of the investors, who obtained a fixed rate of interest and a means of recovering back the capital either by retaining the shares including the ordinary shares after conversion or by selling them. Mr Slater submitted:-

"In the present context, a dividend will 'reasonably be regarded as

equivalent to ... interest on a loan' if it performs the same relative

function (produces the same effective result) in relation to an

investment by the recipient as would interest in relation to a loan of

the funds invested.

...

It is not to the point that ANZ would not be required to repay the

investors; the relevant perspective is not that of ANZ. The investors

were offered a practical opportunity to recoup their investment."

15. Mr D H Bloom QC, with whom Mr K J Burgess appeared for Radilo, submitted however that it was necessary to look to the nature of the arrangement between ANZ and its shareholders. Mr Bloom submitted that, as between ANZ and the shareholders, there was no element of loan.

16. I agree with the construction which Mr Bloom puts upon s.46D. In my opinion, the words "debt dividend", "loan", "the provision of credit", and "any other form of financial accommodation" all indicate that what the section anticipates is a transaction having characteristics equivalent to those of a loan. Although the term "finance" is defined as including "money raised by the issue of shares", we are concerned in these proceedings, not with whether or not there was a "finance arrangement", but with whether or not the payment of the dividend "may reasonably be regarded as equivalent to the payment of interest on a loan."

17. The essence of a loan is that there be a debt, that is, moneys outstanding to be repaid at a fixed or determinable time or on demand. See Chitty on Contracts, 27th ed., 1994, para 36-202. As Mason ACJ, Wilson, Brennan, Deane and Dawson JJ said in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v The Myer Emporium Ltd [1987] HCA 18; (1987) 163 CLR 199 at 218:-

"Interest is regarded as flowing from the principal sum (Federal

Wharf Co Ltd v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (1930) 44 CLR 24 at

p.28) and to be compensation to the lender for being kept out of the

use and enjoyment of the principal sum: Riches v Westminster Bank Ltd

((1947) AC 390, at p.400)."

18. There was no such element and no equivalent element in the terms on which the shares were issued. ANZ issued the shares to obtain long-term Tier 1 capital. There was no provision for repayment, redemption or return of capital and no collateral arrangement promising a return to the shareholder. Each shareholder who invested in the shares, either in the original float or on the market, purchased intangible property, namely, shares in the capital of ANZ. The issue of shares on the basis of their being non-cumulative, preference shares and the issue of preference shares on the basis that they be convertible into ordinary shares have been standard arrangements for a very long time. Forms 212 and 273 of Palmer's Company Precedents, 12th ed. 1922, provide precedents for appropriate articles of association. In past years, fixed interest preference shares have been common.

19. The approach taken by the Commissioner has treated the word "equivalent" as if equivalence in an economic sense is sufficient. By way of example, Pt XVA of the Customs Act 1901 (Cth) deals with tariff concession orders and turns upon the issue of "substitutable goods". Earlier forms of this legislation dealt with goods that were either identical, or served "similar functions" or in respect of which there was cross-elasticity of demand. See Reprint No 7, s.269B(3) and (4) and s.269C. At an earlier time, the words "a suitable equivalent" were used. See Minister for Industry and Commerce v Western Mining Corporation Ltd [1985] FCA 214; (1985) 7 FCR 67 at 69. In the present case, there was a cross-elasticity of demand as between ANZ's CP shares and the many forms of debt instruments which were on the market, and there was in that sense substitutability between the securities. Section 46D does not, however, look to equivalence in the sense of cross-elasticity of demand, though that may be a relevant factor.

20. Mr Slater, without referring to any supporting authority, submitted that the matter must be looked at from the point of view of the shareholder. The section does not, however, imply that that should be done. Indeed, s.46D(2) twice uses the word "paid" and refers to "the payment of the dividend" and "the payment of interest" rather than the receipt thereof.

21. Section 46D is designed to overcome tax avoidance or tax minimisation arrangements. In my opinion, it does not apply to the payment of a dividend on shares issued by a public company which is quoted on the Stock Exchange, there being no arrangement for the repayment of the capital by a redemption, buy-back, distribution of capital, collateral payback or otherwise. In relation to the CP shares, there was no such arrangement, at least not until 22 September 1994 when ANZ offered to buy back the ordinary shares into which the CP shares were to be converted, a fact which I have been asked to disregard. In my opinion, a dividend on the CP shares could not reasonably be regarded as equivalent to interest on a loan. There was no debt element, disguised or otherwise.

22. For these reasons, I shall order that the objection decision be set aside and that the matter be remitted to the Commissioner of Taxation to amend the assessment so as to allow Radilo a rebate in accordance with s.46 of the Act. The Commissioner should pay the costs of the proceedings.
