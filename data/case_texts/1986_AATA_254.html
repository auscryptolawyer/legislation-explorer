B439/1984 and Commissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 254; 86 ATC 1023 (29 August 1986)

Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax Assessment Act - assessable income - compensation for termination of management agreement paid to land developer - whether capital or income receipt.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 – ss 25(1).

C of T (NSW) v. Meeks [1915] HCA 34; (1915) 19 CLR 568

McLaurin v. F.C of T. [1961] HCA 9; (1961) 104 CLR 381

Van Den Berghs Ltd v. Clark (1935) AC 431

Case G38 (1956) 7 TBRD 217

Californian Copper Syndicate Ltd v. Harris (1904) 5 TC 159

F.C. of T. v. Cooke and Sherden (1980) 80 ATC 4140

DECISION AND REASONS

Re: B439/1984

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

AAT Decision No 2884

Date August 29, 1986

Dr P Gerber, Senior Member

Mr K L Beddoe, Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. B.

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And:

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Tribunal: Dr P Gerber (Senior Member)

Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 29 August 1986

Place: Brisbane

1. This matter was heard before Taxation Board of Review No 3 constituted by Mr K L Beddoe, Dr P Gerber and Dr G W Beck. In accordance with section 223 of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 the Tribunal has been constituted by Dr Gerber and Mr Beddoe for the purpose of making this decision.

2. The issue in this application is the assessability in 1973 of $14,286 received by the applicant (hereinafter referred to as "A") as beneficiary under a trust. The amount is one seventh of the sum of $100,000 paid to the trustee, Sands Pty Ltd, and described in the trust tax return for 1973 as "compensation for termination of management agreement". A's wife and each of their four children - aged between two and 11 years at 30 June 1973 – also received one seventh and the remaining one seventh was income to which no beneficiary was presently entitled and remained for accumulation within the trust. The objection lodged by A also covered a sum of $113 being his one seventh share of certain motor vehicle expenses claimed by the trust and disallowed by the Commissioner. This part of the objection was subsequently abandoned and no evidence or argument about car expenses was offered a the hearing.

3. A note accompanying the 1973 return of Sands Pty Ltd as trustee for the Sands Trust reads as under;

"COMPENSATION FOR TERMINATION OF MANAGEMENT AGREEMENT - $100,000.00

On 14th June, 1972, Developments Pty Ltd which had acquired a subdivisional property known as Waters Estate, advised Sands Pty Ltd of the management arrangements to be made between the two companies. This provided that Sands Pty Ltd would receive a Management Fee of $100,000.00 payable at the rate of $5,000 per quarter during the currency of the project plus 10% of the net profits of its project (see attached letter dated 14th June). The feasibility showed the Management Fee as commencing i n October 1972 (see copy attached). Subsequently, instead of proceeding with the development, Developments Pty Ltd sold the property on 21st June, 1973 and paid Sands Pty Ltd $100,000 in full settlement of all Management Fees.

It is claimed that the amount of $100,000 received represents compensation for the cancellation of the Management Agreement under which the company would have earned more than this amount and is, therefore of a capital nature and not subject to income tax.

The date of sale of the property was 21st June, 1973 and it had been agreed by letter dated 26th March, 1973 that on settlement, the company would be entitled to $100,000 in lieu of management and other fees due to it. Although the receipt for the sum of $100,000 was dated 9th July, 1973, it is considered that it was earned on the date of settlement, 21st June, 1973 and was a capital receipt in the year ended 30th June, 1973. The amount was distributed as a capital sum as per schedule below.

(Sgd) A

Director

DISTRIBUTION SCHEDULE

A 1/7th

Mrs A 1/7th

Child 1 1/7th

Child 2 1/7th

Child 3 1l/7th

Child 4 1/7th

Accumulation within the Trust 1/7th"

There was no dispute as to the bona fides of the payment of the $100,000 and the Commissioner accepted that the transaction fell for consideration within the 1973 tax year.

3. The subdivisional property certainly seemed to have been at least financially blessed. It was bought by Sands Pty Ltd on 24 September 1971 for $320,695.95. A is a director and shareholder of Sands as well as a beneficiary in the Sands Trust. According to his evidence, Sands bought the land as nominee for another company which was financially unable to proceed. A third company provided the purchase money, but it also could not proceed with development and the land had to be sold. By contract dated 15 June 1972 Sands sold the land to Developments Pty Ltd, a company controlled, according to A by a man prominent on the land development scene in Australia and hereafter called G. The price was $650,000. Sands Pty Ltd received "$48,000 something" commission as a result of that sale. G did not appear at the hearing and we did not have the benefit of evidence from him. However, documentary evidence established that Developments sold the property by contract dated 8 June 1973 to a company (later in this decision called "Shelter") in which Sands was a shareholder. The sale price was $1,325,000.

4. A is himself an experienced developer and it is against a background of his belief that Waters Estate was an appropriate property to which to apply his expertise that he explained some events interspersed among these sales. It seems that after Sands bought the land as nominee (September 1971) and two other companies could not find the money to proceed, A searched for other parties that could proceed. He knew G and he said "I approached G to see if he was interested in the proposition of developing Waters".

5. After negotiations "sometime early in June 1972" the following letter dated 14 June 1972 was addressed to Sands Pty Ltd by Developments and signed by G as managing director:

"The Secretary

Sands Pty Ltd

QUEENSLAND

Attention: Mr A

Dear Sirs

re: Developments Pty. Limited

re: Waters Estate

I refer to my discussion with you and I now seek to confirm in principle the Management arrangements to be made between this company and your company viz;

1. Your company will manage the Waters Estate project on behalf of this company, including the planning and development and sales of the finished product.

2. Your company will at all times retain during the currency of the Management contract the services of Mr A who shall devote to the project such of his time and attention as is necessary or expedient for the purpose of efficiently carrying out the management tasks of your company.

3. The management services to be rendered by your company will commence from the date the Estate is acquired by this company and end when its sales are completed unless previously determined as hereinafter provided.

4. The remuneration to be paid by this company to your company shall be –

(a) A management fee of $100,000 payable at the rate of $5,000 a quarter during the currency of the project, it being agreed however that the management fee shall not exceed the sum of $100,000 and that if the project is completed before the end of the period budgeted the balance of the fee shall be paid in a lump sum at the end thereof.

(b) A sum equivalent to 10% of the net profits earned by the company for the project payable at the end thereof provided always that at the discretion of the company it may make advances to your company on account of the profit due to it from time to time- Profit for the purpose of this clause shall be determined by deducting from gross receipts from sale, cost of the purchase of the land including legal costs and stamp duty thereof, all development costs, management fees payable to your company, rates, taxes and other holding charges, costs incurred in respect of sales including advertising and commission payable together with legal and other charges and an amount equivalent to 12% per annum calculated on a monthly basis on all capital utilised by the company in relation to the project.

(c) If the company at its discretion decides at any stage to sell the project or its residue for a lump sum, then the management fee payable to your company will be discounted back at the rate of 7% per annum on the assumption that it has been budgeted that the project will continue until July 1977. A resultant cash payment will be made to your company. Your company's right to 10% of the net profits will remain unimpaired.

5. This agreement may be determined by the company –

(a) If your company fails to carry out its duties and obligations as herein provided.

(b) If the said A dies or becomes incapacited or ceases to be available to your company and no substitute acceptable to this company at its absolute discretion is available.

(c) If your company shall be guilty of any misconduct or any breach of duty in relation to the obligations which it owes to this company.

If you agree in principle with the above, I will arrange for our solicitors to draw a formal contract.

Yours faithfully

G.

Managing Director"

A said that in addition to the consideration for management services referred to in this letter he subsequently negotiated a right for Sands Pty Ltd to acquire at cost the estate land designated for a caravan park and for a small shopping centre. His counsel put questions to A and elicited responses as follows:

What was the intention for the development of this area when you first spoke to G? --- To develop it - to gain all the approvals and develop it into a residential subdivision, and a canal subdivision, and caravan park, and a small shopping centre.

Now, do you recall when the subject of the ownership of the caravan park and shopping centre first arose in discussions between yourself and G? --- Shortly after the 14 June 1972, when we were having discussions of a management agreement.

And can you recall now the terms of that discussion, and who first raised the subject, what was said - that type of thing? --- I had been offered one proposition on 14 June which I felt I should get more out of it for the amount of work I was putting into the development, and I asked for - that I be able to buy the caravan park and the shopping centre which was agreed to by G.

And it is right, is it not, that as a result of that agreement, your entitlement was to receive 10 per cent of the profits, the right to buy the shopping centre and caravan park at cost, and management fees of $5000 per quarter? --- Yes."

Following these questions counsel for A hastened to point out that it was Sands Pty Ltd, i.e. the trustee of A's family trust, that was the contracting party with the stated entitlements.

5. According to A's evidence he received the following letter from Developments dated 20 June 1972:

"Mr A

Sands Pty Ltd

Dear Mr A

I refer to previous correspondence with reference to the Management Agreement I have discussed with you, and in particular my letter of the 14th June last and subsequent discussions.

As I verbally indicated to you I am agreeable to your additional condition, namely that your company should have the right to purchase the Caravan Park and Shopping Centre site at cost, subject however to some revision of the other conditions in the earlier offer I made to you. If you agree I will arrange a draft agreement to incorporate this change.

However there have been indications that we may be able to sell the whole project 'as is' at a reasonable profit, so that until this is resolved I do not think you should proceed with the development.

If it is decided to sell 'en globo' the company would be prepared to offer you $85,000 in full settlement of all your rights i.e. your Management Fee, your share of the profit and your right to acquire the Caravan Park and Shopping Centre at cost. I would consider this to be more than reasonable compensation to you as the development has not proceeded to the stage where a significant Management Fee would be justified, so that what you will primarily be giving up is your right to share in the profits and your rights to acquire at cost the properties you want to develop and retain for your own company. It is difficult to put a value on this but $85,000 cash now against potential future benefits should be attractive to you.

Please let me know your views, as your attitude would be one of the factors I would take into consideration in deciding whether the company should sell the whole project.

Yours sincerely

DEVELOPMENTS PTY LIMITED

(SGD) G

Director"

A said he was not satisfied with a full settlement of $85,000 and made representations to G. Also tendered was a letter signed by the secretary of Developments and dated 26 March 1973. When responding to questions about it, A referred to yet another sales contract dated 22 March 1973 involving a company previously unmentioned which we have called "Torrens River". The letter reads:

"A Esq. ,

Sands Pty Limited

QUEENSLAND

Dear Sir

Re: Waters Estate

I confirm that on settlement of the sale of the above, your company will be entitled to $100,000 in full settlement of all Management and other fees due to it."

The questions and answers relating to this letter were as follows:

"Counsel: Do you recall receiving that letter? --- Yes.

Are you able to say how much before you received that letter, that you reached agreement with G as to payment of $100,000? ---Probably some months before - at the time of Torrens River signing a contract to purchase.

Tribunal Member: Torrens River signing a contract? --- Yes, Torrens River signed a contract to purchase from Developments, and then that contract was - they gave their rights over to Shelter to purchase, because the finance company wanted a company that had not had trading before.

Counsel: So that the agreement that you made about the $100,000 was made at the time the Torrens River contract was signed? --- Yes.

And when, subsequently, the property was sold by Developments to Shelter, did Torrens River Pty Limited execute or release Developments from its obligations under the contract between Developments and Torrens River? --- Yes.

Would you have a look at this document, please. Is that document a copy of the release document that was executed? --- Yes.

Do you see there that it gives the date of the contract of sale between Development's Pty Limited, and Torrens River? --- 22 March, 1973.

1973. Are you able to say from that about when you negotiated the figure of $100,000 with G? --- At that time, I would say.

March 1973? --- Yes."

6. The picture that we see emerging from this evidence can be summarised:

  • There was confirmation "in principle" by Developments on 14 June 1972 that Sands Pty Ltd would be paid $100,000 in quarterly instalments of $5000 and would further receive 10% of the net profit on the Waters development project in return for total management;
  • no formal contract was ever signed to this effect and there was no evidence as to agreement by Sands to undertake management, but there is no doubt that A held out his company to G as development managers and A said his intention when he first spoke to G was "to develop it - to gain all the approvals and develop it";
  • Developments indicated by letter dated 20 June 1972 agreement to the granting to Sands of a right to purchase at cost the caravan park and shopping centre land provided there was "revision of the other conditions in the earlier offer";
  • in the same letter Developments offered $85,000 "in full settlement of all rights" in the event Developments sold the land "en globo";
  • there was also no evidence that these conditions became subject to a formal contract;
  • on 26 March 1973 - i.e. shortly after Developments sold the land firstly to Torrens River and then, following release, to Shelter, a company owned by Sands and three other corporations – Developments confirmed that on settlement of the sale Sands would be entitled to $100,000 "in full settlement of all management and other fees due to it";
  • the $100,000 was paid and details were shown in the note enclosed with Sands' 1973 tax return and reproduced earlier in paragraph 3.

7. It can hardly be necessary to consider the assessability of management fees and a share in business profits received by a taxpayer holding himself out and functioning as a manager of developmental projects. Such fees and profits obviously are income under s.25(1) even on the narrowest interpretation of that section. If such a taxpayer receives fees for services which are, in fact, never rendered, the fees are attributable to the business or proposed income earning activity of the taxpayer and hence are assessable (vide C. of T. (N.S.W.) v. Meeks [1915] HCA 34; (1915) 19 CLR 568.) It follows that if Developments and Sands had proceeded according to Developments' letter of 14 June 1972, all the receipts by Sands would have been assessable howsoever paid and regardless of whether Sands actually performed any management services. By the letter dated 20 June 1972 Developments indicated to Sands that Developments offered a lump sum in "settlement of all your rights", and it must be noted that these rights arose only because Sands was rendering, and had previously expected to further render management services to Developments. The rights were to management fees, a share of profits and the right to acquire at cost the caravan park and shopping centre land. Counsel for the applicant submitted that the inclusion of the right to acquire some land at cost caused such a lump sum to be not assessable.

8. Before proceeding it is worth pointing out that we do not think it matters that there was at no stage written agreement between the parties covering those rights. The actions of both G (as evidenced in documents) and of A lead to the conclusion that G intended that Sands (via its executive A) totally plan and manage the development and that A expected to do this in return for benefits of various kinds. Only the extent of the benefits remained subject to protracted negotiation; the intentions and expectations of G and A were otherwise clear - especially to each other - from early June 1972.

9. On the authority of McLaurin v. F.C. of T. [1961] HCA 9; (1961) 104 CLR 381 it is accepted that a single undissected sum received in settlement of a claim for unliquidated damages cannot be dissected into a part that falls for assessment under the Income Tax Assessment Act and a part that does not. If, therefore, the $100,000 received by Sands includes a part that is not assessable the entire sum avoids assessment. The only part that might be not assessable is the undetermined - and, it seems, undeterminable - amount paid as compensation for the loss of the right to purchase at cost the caravan park, and shopping centre land.

10. Generally speaking, unless one deals in rights, a right to acquire property is an item of capital. (Van Den Berghs Limited v. CIark (1935) AC 431 and in particular the judgment of Lord MacMillan at 432.) An interesting case where this principle was applied was Case G38 (1956) 7 TBRD 217 heard by Taxation Board of Review No 2. The taxpayer, who received remuneration for managing his father's estate, as well as his brother had a right to purchase land forming part of the estate. The taxpayer abandoned the right to purchase an area of 1365 acres in consideration of being sold 256 acres at a price well below market value. The Commissioner sought to assess the difference between the market value and the price paid for the land on the basis that it was tantamount to payment of remuneration. The Board refused to accept this contention and found that the reduction in price was in consideration of the surrender of his rights under the trust so that the transaction was one of capital. But it cannot be said that consideration for the surrendering of rights is always an item of capital and whether or not such consideration is capital must depend on the circumstances in which the rights are acquired. Where a taxpayer outlays money to purchase a right (e.g. an option) or becomes entitled to a right under the terms of a will, the situation seems to us quite different from that prevailing in the case under review here. Sands was a company actively engaged in seeking out development properties to manage. Having found such a property - and that property having fallen eventually into the hands of Developments - A proceeded to negotiate management terms with G, the chief executive of Developments. A managed to secure an offer which contained three elements - a quarterly payment of money, a share of the estate net profit and a right to acquire some land at cost. Faced with these circumstances one must question whether, from the point of view of the income earning activities of Sands, there was any difference in character between the three elements. All three were offered because G expected Sands to provide an agreed range of management services. We have concluded that the value of the right to purchase the caravan park and the shopping centre land at cost was simply part and parcel of the remuneration package which would have been derived by Sands if Developments had proceeded. The entire remuneration package would have been assessable in the hands of Sands had it not been a trustee passing on the trust income to beneficiaries.

11. What we have said in the preceding paragraph is, we believe, an application to the facts of this case of the principle laid down so long ago in Californian Copper Syndicate Limited v. Harris (1904) 5 TC 159 and stated in the words of the Lord Justice Clerk at 165-166:

"It is quite a well settled principle in dealing with questions of assessment of Income Tax, that where the owner of an ordinary investment chooses to realise it, and obtains a greater price for it than he originally acquired it at, the enhanced price is not profit in the sense of Schedule D of the Income Tax Act of 1842 assessable to Income Tax. But it is equally well established that enhanced values obtained from realisation or conversion of securities may be so assessable, where what is done is not merely a realisation or change of investment, but an act done in what is truly the carrying on, or carrying out, of a business."

There is here not an owner entering upon "an ordinary investment"; e.g. an investor securing an option, but "an act done in what is truly the carrying on of a business". It follows that every element in the composite sum of $100,000 is assessable in accordance with s.25(1). Having made this finding, there is no need to consider the question of assessability under s.26(e) which was also argued on behalf of the Commissioner. It perhaps should be said that the right to acquire the land at cost was capable of being converted to money or money's worth - indeed, as part of the $100,000 related to it, the right was converted to money by that payment. This being so, the decision of the Full Federal Court in F.C. of T. v. Cooke & Sherden 80 ATC 4140 excluding from s.25(1) the value of free holidays given by manufacturers to soft drink retailers does not apply.

12. There was no disagreement between the parties that A was presently entitled to one seventh of the 1973 income of the Sands Trust and was not under a legal disability. It follows from the inclusion of $100,000 in the assessable income of the trust and in the absence of related deductions, that a further $14,286 should be included in A's assessable income in accordance with s.97(1).

13. The decision under review is affirmed.
