Re Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia v Alan Edward Jackson [1990] FCA 425; 96 ALR 586/21 Atr 1012 27 FCR 1 (31 October 1990)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

And: ALAN EDWARD JACKSON

No. G189 of 1990

FED No. 604

Income Tax

[1990] FCA 425; 96 ALR 586/21 ATR 1012

27 FCR 1

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEW SOUTH WALES DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Burchett (1), von Doussa (2) and Hill(3) JJ.

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - whether Commissioner may make determination under s.177F Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 after disallowance of objection to assessment and after institution of appeal to the Court by taxpayer - whether Commissioner, having made assessment and later making s.177F determination, must amend prior assessment - structure of Part IVA, assessment, objection and appeal and amended assessment provisions considered.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.166, 167, 177F, 177G, 185, 186, 190, 226.

HEARING

SYDNEY

31:10:1990

Counsel and Solicitors for Mr A.H. Slater instructed by the

Appellant: Australian Government Solicitor

Counsel and Solicitors Mr P.J. Lanigan instructed by

for the Respondent Messrs Emil Ford and Company

ORDER

1. The appeal be dismissed.

2. The appellant pay the respondent's costs of the appeal.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

In my opinion, the appeal should be dismissed with costs for the reasons to be given by Hill J.

I agree that the appeal should be dismissed for the reasons given by Hill J.

The appellant, the Commissioner of Taxation, by leave, appeals against so much of the judgment and orders of a judge of the Court, (Gummow J) as answered one of a number of preliminary questions ordered by his Honour to be decided separately from other issues arising in the proceedings brought by the respondent, Mr Jackson, against the Commissioner concerning Mr Jackson's liability for income tax for the years of income ended 30 June 1982, 30 June 1983, 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985. This appeal is concerned only with the 1982 and 1983 years of income.

2. The facts are not, so far as they concern this preliminary question, in dispute. On 30 July 1984 the Commissioner issued notices of amended assessment increasing Mr Jackson's taxable income by $3138 for the 1982 income year and by $7892 for the 1983 income year and the tax payable thereon accordingly. These amendments were explained in the adjustment sheets which accompanied the assessments. By way of example, the adjustment sheet for the 1982 year is in the following terms:

amended for the following reason(s).

------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------

Net income returned by Waugh Family

Trust No. 1 from accountancy practice 1857

Add - Non allowance expenditure:

Salary to your 18914

amended assessment 21926

------------------------------------------------------------

3. Mr Jackson duly objected to the amended assessment, his objection was disallowed, and being dissatisfied with the Commissioner's decision on his objection, Mr Jackson requested the Commissioner to treat the objection as an appeal and forward it to the Supreme Court of New South Wales. As a result of the vesting in this court of original jurisdiction in income tax matters by Act No. 23 of 1987, the appeals in respect of the 1982 and 1983 income years were referred to this Court for hearing on 14 January 1988 and 18 January 1988 respectively.

4. From the Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions filed by the Commissioner in the proceedings and dated 11 August 1988 it appears that until 30 June 1981 Mr Jackson carried on practice as an accountant and tax agent in his own name and derived income by way of fees from his clients. On 1 July 1981, a trust deed was entered into establishing the Waugh family trust No. 1 of which Waugh Taxation Consultants Pty Ltd was trustee, and from some date in or about October 1981, when Waugh Taxation Consultants Pty Ltd was registered as a tax agent, the practice formerly conducted by the respondent was conducted by the trustee.

5. It was the Commissioner's first contention that the income comprising fees paid by the clients of the practice was income derived by Mr Jackson and dealt with at his direction or on his behalf. The Commissioner also indicated that he proposed to rely upon the provisions of Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) ("the Act") and s.260 of the Act. The document referred to determinations having been made pursuant to s.177F of the Act on 15 May 1985 and 26 May 1986.

6. It may be inferred that the requirement to formulate its case for the Court alerted those advising the Commissioner either that no determinations were in fact made on the dates referred to above, or alternatively that any such determination was ineffective. Nothing turns on this. However on 11 August 1988, the date of the Commissioner's Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions, a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation purported to make determinations under ss.177F(1) and (2) in respect of each of the years of income the subject of this appeal. For present purposes it can be said that each of the determinations was in substantially similar form. That for the 1982 income year read as follows:

"I, Janice Mary Brady, in the exercise of the

powers and functions conferred upon me as Deputy

Commissioner of Taxation by delegation from the

Commissioner of Taxation under the provisions of

the Taxation Administration Act 1953, hereby

determine for the purposes of sub-section 177F(1)

of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 that, in

respect of the taxpayer named in Column 1 below

who in the year of income specified in Column 2

has obtained, or would but for the operation of

section 177F obtain, a tax benefit in connection

with a scheme to which Part IVA of that Act

applies, the whole of the amount otherwise not

included in the assessable income of that taxpayer

as specified in Column 3, being the amount that

would have been included, or might reasonably be

expected to have been included in the assessable

income of that taxpayer of that year of income if

the scheme in connection with which the Waugh

Family Trust No. 1 had not been entered into,

shall be included in the assessable income of the

taxpayer of that year of income.

Column 1 Column 2 Column 3

Year Ended

Alan E Jackson 30 June 1982 $36,706

10 Waugh Crescent

is deemed to be included in the assessable income of

income specified in Column 2 by virtue of sections 17,

7. The effect of the determinations was that the sum of $36,706 and the sum of $31,997 should be included in the assessable income of the respondent for the 1982 and 1983 years of income respectively. Notice of the making of the determinations was presumably given to Mr Jackson shortly thereafter. No attempt was made by the Commissioner further to amend the assessments.

8. The question formulated for decision by Gummow J, as amended, was in the following terms:

"2. In an appeal against the disallowance by

the Respondent (i.e. the Commissioner) of an

objection against an assessment of the taxable

income of a taxpayer for a year of income made on

the ground that a particular amount is included in

the assessable income of the taxpayer by reason of

the provisions of the Act other than Part IVA, is

the Commissioner entitled to place reliance upon a

determination, made pursuant to s.177F (1) and

made after the disallowance of the objection and

after the institution of the appeal, that a like

amount should be included in the assessable income

of the taxpayer pursuant to s. 177F (1) (a)?"

9. His Honour answered this question in the negative. He expressed his conclusions in the following passage:

"Part IVA played no part in the processes of

assessment for the two years in question. To

intrude now into the matters of which the Court is

seized, the effect of determinations expressed to

have been made after the institution of

proceedings would be to change the nature of those

matters from challenges to the decisions of the

Respondent some years ago to disallow the taxpayer's

objections to particular amended assessments.

Put another way, upon a proper construction of

the Act, the making of a determination under s.

177F (1) (a) to cancel the whole or part of the

amount referable to a tax benefit is then given

effect (i) by a determination (under sub-s. 177F

(2)) of the provision of the Act by which the

amount is deemed included in the assessable income

of the taxpayer, and (ii) by assessment or amended

assessment as earlier described in these reasons."

10. Part IVA was inserted into the Act by Act No 110 of 1981, s.7, as a general anti-avoidance provision to replace s. 260, which was at the same time amended, so as to not to apply to any contract, agreement or arrangement made or entered into after 27 May 1981. Unlike s.260, however, Part IVA does not operate of its own force. It is predicated upon the making of a determination by the Commissioner pursuant to s.177F(1).

11. The prerequisites for the making of a determination under that subsection are that a taxpayer has obtained a tax benefit, or would but for the operation of s.177F(1) have obtained a tax benefit, and that the obtaining of that tax benefit was in connection with a scheme to which Part IVA applies. The concept of "tax benefit" is defined in s.177C as a reference either to:

"(a) an amount not being included in the

assessable income of the taxpayer of a year of

income where that amount would have been included,

or might reasonably be expected to have been

included, in the assessable income of the taxpayer

of that year of income if the scheme had not been

entered into or carried out; or

(b) a deduction being allowable to the taxpayer

in relation to a year of income where the whole or

a part of that deduction would not have been

allowable, or might reasonably be expected not to

have been allowable, to the taxpayer in relation

to that year of income if the scheme had not been

entered into or carried out,..."

12. There will be a scheme to which the Part applies where there is a scheme (defined in s.177A(1) in broad terms) which has been entered into or carried out and where having regard to the various matters set out in s.177D(b):

"it would be concluded that the person, or one

of the persons, who entered into or carried out

the scheme or any part of the scheme did so for

the purpose of enabling the relevant taxpayer to

obtain a tax benefit in connection with the scheme

or of enabling the relevant taxpayer and another

taxpayer or other taxpayers each to obtain a tax

benefit in connection with the scheme (whether or

not that person who entered into or carried out

the scheme or any part of the scheme is the

relevant taxpayer or is the other taxpayer or one

of the other taxpayers)."

13. The purpose, to which s.177D refers includes a reference to dominant purpose: s.177A(5).

14. Ss.177F and 177G are in the following terms:

"CANCELLATION OF TAX BENEFITS, ETC.

177F.(1) Where a tax benefit has been obtained, or

would but for this section be obtained, by a taxpayer

in connection with a scheme to which this Part applies,

the Commissioner may -

(a) in the case of a tax benefit that is referable

to an amount not being included in the assessable

income of the taxpayer of a year of income - determine

that the whole or a part of that amount shall be

included in the assessable income of the taxpayer of

that year of income; or

(b) in the case of a tax benefit that is referable

to a deduction or a part of a deduction being allowable

to the taxpayer in relation to a year of income -

determine that the whole or a part of the deduction or

of the part of the deduction, as the case may be, shall

not be allowable to the taxpayer in relation to that

year of income,

and, where the Commissioner makes such a determination,

he shall take such action as he considers necessary to

give effect to that determination.

(2) Where the Commissioner determines under paragraph

(1)(a) that an amount is to be included in the

assessable income of a taxpayer of a year of income,

that amount shall be deemed to be included in that

assessable income by virtue of such provision of this

Act as the Commissioner determines.

(3) Where the Commissioner has made a determination

under sub-section (1) in respect of a taxpayer in

relation to a scheme to which this Part applies, the

Commissioner may, in relation to any taxpayer (in this

sub-section referred to as the 'relevant taxpayer') -

(a) if, in the opinion of the Commissioner -

(i) there has been included, or would but for this

sub-section be included, in the assessable income of

the relevant taxpayer of a year of income an amount

that would not have been included or would not be

included, as the case may be, in the assessable income

of the relevant taxpayer of that year of income if the

scheme had not been entered into or carried out; and

(ii) it is fair and reasonable that that amount or a

part of that amount should not be included in the

assessable income of the relevant taxpayer of that year

of income,

determine that that amount or that part of that amount,

as the case may be, should not have been included or

shall not be included, as the case may be, in the

assessable income of the relevant taxpayer of that year

of income; or

(b) if, in the opinion of the Commissioner -

(i) an amount would have been allowed or would be

allowable to the relevant taxpayer as a deduction in

relation to a year of income if the scheme had not been

entered into or carried out, being an amount that was

not allowed or would not, but for this sub-section, be

allowable, as the case may be, as a deduction to the

relevant taxpayer in relation to that year of income; and

(ii) it is fair and reasonable that that amount or a

part of that amount should be allowable as a deduction

to the relevant taxpayer in relation to that year of income,

determine that that amount or that part, as the case

may be, should have been allowed or shall be allowable,

as the case may be, as a deduction to the relevant

taxpayer in relation to that year of income,

and the Commissioner shall take such action as he

considers necessary to give effect to any such determination.

(4) Where the Commissioner makes a determination under

sub-section (3) by virtue of which an amount is allowed

as a deduction to a taxpayer in relation to a year of

income, that amount shall be deemed to be so allowed as

a deduction by virtue of such provision of this Act as

the Commissioner determines.

(5) Where, at any time, a taxpayer considers that the

Commissioner ought to make a determination under sub-section

(3) in relation to the taxpayer in relation to a year of

income, the taxpayer may post to or lodge with the

Commissioner a request in writing for the making by the

Commissioner of a determination under that sub-section.

(6) The Commissioner shall consider the request and

serve on the taxpayer, by post or otherwise, a written

notice of his decision on the request.

(7) If the taxpayer is dissatisfied with the

Commissioner's decision on the request, the taxpayer

may, within 60 days after service on the taxpayer of

notice of the decision of the Commissioner, lodge with

the Commissioner an objection in writing against the

decision stating fully and in detail the grounds on

which the taxpayer relies.

(8) The provisions of Division 2 of Part V (other than

section 185) apply in relation to an objection made

under sub-section (7) in like manner as those provisions

apply in relation to an objection against an assessment.

AMENDMENT OF ASSESSMENTS

177G.(1) Nothing in section 170 prevents the amendment

of an assessment at any time before the expiration of 6

years after the date on which tax became due and

payable under the assessment if the amendment is for

the purposes of giving effect to sub-section 177F(1).

(2) Nothing in section 170 prevents the amendment of

an assessment at any time if the amendment is for the

purpose of giving effect to sub-section 177F(3)."

15. For the appellant, it was submitted that the Commissioner could at any time make determinations under ss.177F(1) and (2). Having done so, in the case of a tax benefit that related to the non inclusion of an amount in assessable income, that amount or part of that amount as the case might be was, by force of s.177F(2) thereupon included in assessable income. It was further submitted that, at least in a case such as the present where the amount included in assessable income by force of the determination required no net increase or decrease of taxable income or tax payable, the Commissioner was not thereby amending the assessment, and in consequence was not required to issue a notice of amended assessment. Thus, the reference in s.177F(2), to the Commissioner taking such action as he considered necessary to give effect to the s.177F(1) determination involved, in a case such as the present, the Commissioner doing no more than relying upon the consequence of the determination to support the assessment before the Court.

16. In support of this submission, counsel for the Commissioner argued that the following propositions were established by the cases and led inexorably to the conclusion contended for:

*In an appeal under Part V of the Act, a taxpayer must show

that the assessment is for an amount exceeding his actual

liability for tax. If he fails to show this, his appeal

will fail: Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Dalco (1990)

[1990] HCA 3; 64 ALJR 166.

*The Commissioner may point to any relevant facts or

statutory provisions as supporting the taxable income and

tax payable as assessed by him, whether or not he adverted

to them at the time of making the assessment: Federal

Commissioner of Taxation v Wade [1951] HCA 66; (1951) 84 CLR 105, Federal

Commissioner of Taxation v Reynolds (1981) 81 ATC 4131.

*An assessment may be upheld in reliance upon a

determination under s.177F made after the assessment and it

is not necessary for there to be an amendment of the

assessment: Fletcher v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (No.

1) (1988) 19 FCR 442.

17. Not all of these propositions can be accepted without qualification.

18. First, it must be noted that the issue in Dalco, as stated by Brennan J, with whose reasons Mason C.J., Deane, Dawson, Gaudron and McHugh JJ. agreed, was:

"In proceedings on appeal to a court pursuant to

Div 2 of Part V of the Act against an assessment

made under s 167(b), does the taxpayer discharge

the burden of proving that the assessment is

excessive where (a) he does not prove that the

amount assessed as his taxable income in fact

exceeds his taxable income, but (b) he shows that

the Commissioner formed a judgment as to the

amount of his taxable income on a wrong basis?"

19. S.167 permits the Commissioner to make what is commonly referred to as a default assessment. It is an assessment "of the amount upon which in his judgment income tax ought to be levied." The consequence of this exercise of judgment is that that amount becomes the taxable income of the taxpayer for the purposes of s.166 of the Act, the normal assessing provision.

20. As the judgment of Brennan J points out, s.190(b) of the Act places the burden of proof "that the assessment is excessive" upon the taxpayer. The word "excessive" refers to the "substantive liability" of the taxpayer: McAndrew v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1956] HCA 62; (1956) 98 CLR 263 at p 271. But, as that case points out, a taxpayer may, in a case where his substantive liability depends upon the fulfilment of some condition precedent (there the prerequisites to the making of an amended assessment under s.170(2) of the Act) show that the assessment is excessive by showing that the prerequisites to the making of the assessment are not present. Thus in McAndrew (supra), Taylor J, in a passage accepted as correct by Mason and Wilson JJ. (with whom Stephen and Aickin JJ. agreed) in F.J. Bloemen Pty Ltd v The Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1981] HCA 27; (1980-81) 147 CLR 360 at 375, said at pp 282-3:

"...there is no reason for thinking that an

assessment, made in purported but not justifiable

exercise of a statutory power, may not properly be

described as excessive; it purports to impose a

specified liability and, upon appeal, the claim of

the appellant is that he is not liable to pay any

part of it. Whether the particular ground upon

which he seeks to escape or reduce the liability

merely touches the accuracy of the assessment or

assails its validity as an assessment, he is, in

the words of s.185, 'dissatisfied with' the

assessment because it purports to impose upon him

a liability in excess of that to which he may

lawfully be subjected and I can see no reason why,

in either case, his complaint may not be

accurately described as a complaint that his

assessment is excessive."

21. In Dalco, the power to make the assessment was not in dispute. Further, it was established in George v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1952] HCA 21; (1952) 86 CLR 183 that a taxpayer could not challenge the existence of the matters in s.167(b), as Brennan J points out in Dalco, presumably either because these matters were not conditions precedent to the power to make an assessment, or because, having regard to the provisions of s. 177(1) the fulfilment of these conditions is part of the due making of the assessment and thus conclusively established by the tender of the assessment or a copy of it, under that subsection. Thus the distinction between a case such as Dalco and a case such as McAndrew is readily apparent.

22. In proceedings under Part V of the Act, where a determination under s.177F has purported to be made and is relied upon by the Commissioner, it will be open to a taxpayer to challenge the existence of the conditions precedent to the making of the determination, for example, the existence of a scheme, whether there was a tax benefit, and whether the necessary conclusion as to purpose is to be arrived at. It will also be open to a taxpayer to show that no determination was made, or that an invalid determination was made, with the consequence that no amount was to be included in assessable income as a result of the section or alternatively, that a deduction otherwise allowable to him will continue to be allowable. This is not to say that in a case where the Commissioner has made a determination, which the taxpayer succeeds in demonstrating is invalid, the taxpayer will necessarily have shown that the assessment is excessive, for the Commissioner may seek to argue that without the operation of Part IVA, the assessment is correct, and in such a case, the taxpayer will be required to show that the taxable income as assessed was excessive in amount.

23. The Commissioner's second proposition may be accepted, but subject to the possible qualification, not relevant to the present appeal, that where the Commissioner has more than one power to assess, and in making an assessment exercises his power under one section and thereafter seeks to justify the exercise of that power under another assessing power contained in another section, he may be limited to supporting the assessment upon the original ground: Danmark Pty Limited and Forestwood Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1944) 7 ATD 333.

24. It is the third proposition which requires close attention.

25. First, it must be said that the context in which the issue arose in Fletcher (supra) was quite different from that arising in the present case. In Fletcher, the taxpayers had been assessed to tax by the Commissioner disallowing to them deductions for interest said to have been an allowable deduction under s.51(1) of the Act. They elected to refer the Commissioner's decision on their objection to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal. The Tribunal was constituted by Mr Bannon QC, a Deputy President and Mr McMahon a Senior member. In the proceedings before the Tribunal the Commissioner did not seek to rely upon Part IVA and no argument was addressed to the Tribunal on the operation of that Part.

26. The Tribunal determined to affirm the Commissioner's decision on the objection. Mr Bannon QC, inferentially, seems to have been of the view that but for the provisions of Part IVA, the taxpayers were entitled to a deduction for the outgoings of interest which he found to have been incurred by them. He rejected a submission made by the Commissioner that Subdivision D of Division III of the Act operated to disallow the deductions otherwise allowable under s.51. He then proceeded to find against the taxpayers on the basis that Part IVA operated to disallow to them the deductions. Mr McMahon agreed with Mr Bannon's "conclusions" and "decision" but added some brief observations, the effect of which was to say that the outgoings for interest were not deductible under s.51(1). He did not say specifically that he agreed with the learned Deputy President's application of Part IVA. Of course, on Mr McMahon's view, if the outgoings were not deductible under s.51(1), Part IVA would not touch the matter, since there was no tax benefit on which it could operate. The taxpayers appealed on a question of law to the Full Court of this Court.

27. Before this Court the principal issue was whether the Tribunal, upon which is conferred by s.43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth) for the purposes of review the power to exercise "all the powers and discretions that are conferred by any relevant enactment on the person who made the decision", was thereby empowered to exercise the discretion conferred upon the Commissioner under s.177F(1) and presumably itself to make a determination under that subsection, notwithstanding that the exercise of that power would be at a point of time later than the making of the original assessment.

28. For the taxpayers it was argued that the only point of time at which the Commissioner could make a s.177F(1) determination was the time of making the original assessment. This argument was rejected by the Court. After noting that there was no doubt that the Commissioner could make a s.177F determination at the time of making the assessment, the Court, in a unanimous judgment, continued: (453)

"Thereafter, by virtue of s.186, the

Commissioner incurs a duty to consider the

objection, to disallow it or to allow it either

wholly or in part, and to notify the taxpayer of

his decision. In considering the objection, the

question for the Commissioner is the correctness

of the original decision, that question being

considered in the light of the terms of the

objection but taking account of all the

information then available to the Commissioner

regarding the amount of the taxable income of the

taxpayer and the amount of the tax payable

thereon. It may well happen, for example, that,

between the date of the original assessment and

the date of determination of an objection, new

information comes to the Commissioner or that

there is some change in the relevant law.

Subject to the limitations imposed by s.170 of the

Act, these are matters properly to be taken into

consideration by the Commissioner, in any case, in

determining whether to issue an amended

assessment. As the issue of an amended assessment

is a possible result of the consideration by the

Commissioner of an objection to an assessment, it

must be appropriate for the Commissioner to take

account of such matters in determining an

objection to an assessment.

It follows that, in determining an objection to

an assessment, the Commissioner is entitled to

make a determination under s.177F of the Act; and

thereafter to give effect to that determination by

an appropriate decision under s.186...

Once it is understood that, in exercising his

powers under s.186, the Commissioner would have

been free to exercise a discretion under s.177F of

the Income Tax Assessment Act, it follows that, in

reviewing the Commissioner's decision under s 186,

the Tribunal is free to exercise that same

discretion if, upon the material then before it,

it seems proper to take that course.

29. After commenting on a question of the power of the Tribunal to make the compensatory adjustments referred to in s.177F(3) to (8) the Court then said at p 454:

"Upon the view just expressed, the current

position is similar to that which applied during

the period in which the jurisdiction to review

taxation determinations was vested in Boards of

Review constituted under the provisions of Div 1

of Part V of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

Those provisions were repealed in 1986, at the

time when jurisdiction in taxation matters was

conferred upon the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal. Section 193(1), which was also

repealed at that time, formerly provided that, for

the purposes of reviewing decisions of the

Commissioner under the Act:

'the Board shall ... have all the powers and

functions of the Commissioner in making

assessments, determinations and decisions under

this Act, and such assessments, determinations and

decisions of the Board, and its decision upon

review, shall for all purposes (except for the

purpose of objections thereto and review thereof

and appeals therefrom) be deemed to be assessments,

determinations or decisions of the Commissioner."

30. While the Court accordingly determined that the Tribunal did not err in holding that it had jurisdiction to determine under s.177F "that the deductions claimed by the applicants should not be allowable in the relevant year of income", it then held that in making the determination the Tribunal had denied to the taxpayers procedural fairness. Accordingly the matter was remitted to the Tribunal for further hearing, where a differently constituted Tribunal held the deductions to be unavailable to the taxpayers under s.51(1), with the result that it was unnecessary for the Tribunal to make a determination under s.177F(1). An appeal from this later decision of the Tribunal was subsequently dismissed by the Full Court of this Court: Fletcher and Ors v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation (No 2) (1990) 90 ATC 4559.

31. The Court did not consider, nor was it required to consider in Fletcher (No.1), the question how the Tribunal should give effect to the determination which it was empowered to make, since in any event the matter had to be remitted to the Tribunal for rehearing. The passages from the judgment, which I have set out above, rather suggest that the Court was of the view that the Commissioner, in making a determination at the time of considering an objection was required to amend the assessment. The reference to s.193 of the Act and the equating of the powers there conferred upon Boards of Review with the powers conferred upon the Tribunal by s.43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act suggests that the Court may have taken the view that the Tribunal, in conducting its review of the Commissioner's objection decision had conferred upon it by s.43 the power to make an assessment.

32. It is not appropriate in the present case to consider whether the Administrative Appeals Tribunal does have power to make an assessment, consequent upon the making of a s.177F determination. If it does, then presumably the provisions in the Act as to giving notice of that assessment, and objecting and appealing that assessment would need to be construed as inapplicable to the Tribunal. What is of significance here is that the question whether the Commissioner, having made an assessment, and then at a later time (including, albeit not limited to, the time when he considers the taxpayer's objection) having made a s.177F determination, is thereby to be taken to have made an amendment to the original assessment, was not decided in Fletcher.

33. To decide this question, it is necessary to analyse the structure of the Act. Relevantly for present purposes, the Commissioner is authorised to make an assessment by s.166 of the Act of a taxpayer's taxable income and the tax payable thereon. The determination of taxable income requires the Commissioner to calculate the assessable income of the taxpayer in accordance with the provisions of the Act and subtract therefrom the allowable deductions to which he is entitled, except in a case to which s.167, not presently relevant, applies: s.17. As soon as conveniently may be after making the assessment, the Commissioner is required to serve notice of the assessment upon the taxpayer: s.174. It is the service of the notice that subjects the taxpayer to the liability to pay the tax assessed: s.204.

34. Assessment is thus, as Kitto J observed in Batagol v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1963] HCA 51; (1963) 109 CLR 243 at 252 a:

"...process by which the provisions of the Act

relating to liability to tax are given concrete

application in a particular case with the

consequence that a specified amount of money will

become due and payable as the proper tax in that case."

35. Earlier in his Honour's judgment, in a passage referred to with approval by Mason and Wilson JJ. in F.J. Bloemen Pty. Ltd. v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (supra) at 371-372, his Honour commented: (251-252)

"Assessment in the sense of mere calculation

produces no legal effect. No step that the

Commissioner may take, even to the point of

satisfying himself of the amount of the taxable

income and of the tax thereon, has under the Act

any legal significance. But if the Commissioner,

having gone through the process of calculation,

serves on the taxpayer a notice that he has

assessed the taxable income and the tax at

specified amounts, the tax becomes by force of the

Act due and payable on the date specified in the

notice or (if no date is specified) on the

thirtieth day after the service of the notice:

s.204. Thus, and thus only, there is brought

about an `ascertainment' of the taxable income and

of the tax, in the sense that thereafter it is

possible to say what could not have been said

before: that amounts have been fixed so that they

are to be taken for all purposes (except those of

appeal: see s.177) to be the result flowing from

the application of the Act in the particular case.

The respective amounts of the taxable income and

the tax have been rendered certain."

36. A taxpayer who is dissatisfied with the assessment, notice of which has been served upon him may, within 60 days of such service lodge with the Commissioner an objection in writing against this assessment: s.185(1). In that objection he must state fully and in detail the grounds on which he relies. That objection must be considered by the Commissioner who is empowered to disallow it, or allow it wholly or in part: s.186. He is required, by the same section, to serve the taxpayer with written notice of his decision.

37. Thereafter the taxpayer is given a choice, if dissatisfied with the Commissioner's objection decision, either to request the Commissioner to refer the decision for review by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal or to request him to refer it to this Court: s.187. That choice may, in a particular case, be of the utmost significance, for while the Administrative Appeals Tribunal is empowered to make a full administrative review of the decision, and in so doing may exercise afresh the powers and the discretions of the Commissioner, this Court has no such power. Thus, in a case where the exercise of discretion by the Commissioner may have been involved, this Court cannot stand in the shoes of the Commissioner and do again that which he has done, but is limited to the ordinary grounds of judicial review, namely to ensuring that the Commissioner has addressed himself to the right issue, that his decision is not affected by an error of law, that he has not taken some extraneous factor into consideration nor failed to take some relevant factor into consideration: Avon Downs Pty Ltd v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation, [1949] HCA 26; (1949) 78 CLR 353 at 360. Thus, by way of example, it could not be doubted that, if a case involving the exercise of the discretion to make a determination under s.177F were to come before this Court, the Court's power to review the discretion would be limited as set out above. In particular, the Court could not itself exercise the discretion.

38. Subject to the limitations set out in s.170 of the Act and elsewhere (eg. s.177G discussed post), the Commissioner is empowered by s.170(1) of the Act to amend an assessment:

"by making such alterations therein or additions

thereto as he thinks necessary".

39. Where the taxpayer has, before an assessment has been made, made a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment, an amendment cannot be made after the expiration of 3 years from the date upon which the tax became due and payable under the assessment: s.170(3). Where no such full and true disclosure has been made, and there has been an avoidance of tax, the Commissioner may amend the assessment by "making such alterations therein or additions thereto as he thinks necessary to correct the assessment" within six years from the date upon which tax became due and payable under the assessment, unless he is of the opinion that the avoidance of tax has been due to fraud or evasion, when no time limit is imposed: s.170(2).

40. The provisions requiring notice to be given by the Commissioner of assessments made by him apply equally to amended assessments. The taxpayer is given a limited right to object to an amended assessment by s.185(2), which provides:

"Where an assessment has been amended in any

particular, the right of a taxpayer to object

against the amended assessment is limited to a

right to object against alterations or additions

in respect of, or matters relating to, that particular.

41. The procedures thereafter for the consideration of objections and the making of a decision by the Commissioner thereon, and the referral by a taxpayer dissatisfied with the amended assessment of the objection decision to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal or to this Court, apply equally to an amended assessment as they apply to an original assessment.

42. It is implicit in the submissions of the Commissioner that no amendment of an assessment in the statutory sense is required, or for that matter could be effected, if the consequence of a determination being made under s.177F(1) is that a taxpayer's taxable income and tax payable remain the same. That implication is of considerable significance in the resolution of the present appeal.

43. It will have been observed that s.170 refers to the amendment of an assessment, inter alia by making "additions" to the assessment, as well as by making "alterations therein". No doubt, an alteration would be made in each case where the taxable income was increased or reduced, and consequentially the tax payable was increased or reduced. It is not clear what the legislature contemplated by the making of "alterations", but the juxtaposition of "alterations therein" and "additions to" the assessment suggests that the process of amendment includes the disallowance of deductions allowed in the previous assessment or the exclusion of items of income previously taken into account, but in addition, the making of amendments to the process of calculation by the addition of new items of income or the allowance of deductions not previously allowed.

44. S.185(2) of the Act refers to the alterations or additions made by an amended assessment as well as to the "particular" in respect of which the amendment has been made. The section was amended in 1986, by Act No. 48 of 1986, as a result of the decision of this Court in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Offshore Oil N.L., (1980) 80 ATC 4457. Previously the limitation on the right to object to an amended assessment was found in a proviso to s.185, in the following terms:

"Provided that, where the assessment is an

amended assessment, the taxpayer shall have no

further right of objection than he would have had

if the amendment had not been made, except to the

extent to which by reason of the amendment a fresh

liability in respect of any particular is imposed

on him or an existing liability in respect of any

particular is increased."

45. The factual context of Offshore Oil was somewhat complicated. Suffice it to say that the Commissioner had amended two assessments by including in assessable income certain interest accrued due in the preceding year but received by the taxpayer in the year the subject of assessment and deducting from the assessable income as originally assessed interest which had accrued due during the year of income but which was not in fact received in that year. The result of the calculation was that there was an increase in assessable income, and a corresponding increase in taxable income and tax payable. The taxpayer sought in its objection to the amended assessment to contend that it was entitled to have allowed as a deduction certain items which had not been taken into account in the original assessment, albeit that it had been open for the taxpayer to object to the original assessments on the ground that these deductions should have been allowed to it. It was held by the Court that the then proviso fixed a monetary limit within which a full right to object against the amended assessment remained, with the consequence that the taxpayer was entitled, the assessment imposing upon it a fresh liability, within the monetary limit to raise in its objection its claim to the deductions sought.

46. Deane J, with whose judgment Franki J agreed, said at 4462:

"The process of assessing tax will ordinarily

involve a number of distinct stages namely, the

ascertainment of the components of assessable

income, the determination of the amount of

assessable income, the ascertainment of any

deductions to be made from assessable income for

determining taxable income, the determination of

the amount of taxable income and the calculation

of tax. The components of assessable income can

be regarded as being on 'the credit side of the

account the credit balance of which represents the

taxable income of the taxpayer' (per Latham C.J.,

Trautwein's case ((1936) [1936] HCA 77; 56 CLR 63) ... at p 94).

The components of the various deductions can be

regarded as being on the debit side of that

account. Any increase in an item on the credit

side or decrease in, or deletion of, an item on

the debit side will constitute, for the purposes

of sec. 185, an increase in an existing liability

in respect of a particular. Any introduction of

an item on the credit side will constitute a fresh

liability in respect of a particular. So

understood, the references to 'existing liability'

and to 'fresh liability' embrace inchoate

'liabilities' at any stage of the process of

assessment be they 'in respect of' the inclusion

of, or the increase in, an item in the

ascertainment of assessable income when the

relevant liability is the inchoate liability

represented by the derivation of assessable income

or the exclusion of, or the decrease in, a

deduction in the assessment of taxable income

where the relevant liability is the inchoate

liability represented by attributability of

taxable income.

47. Later in the judgment, his Honour referred to the phrase "in respect of any particular" as more appropriately on the facts of that case referring to the inclusion of the particular amount of interest and the exclusion of the other amount of interest rather than to the net increase in assessable income which the process produced. This in his Honour's view produced, what with respect was a sensible result, in that a taxpayer, while not objecting to the exclusion of the interest excluded from assessable income by the amendment, could object to the inclusion of the new amount of interest in assessable income.

48. Lockhart J, with whose judgment Franki J also expressed agreement, said at p 4466 that the words "any particular" refer to "the constituent elements in the assessment of taxable income, treating them as separate sources of liability." In a passage of some significance, his Honour said at 4466-7:

"An amended assessment may not increase the

amount of taxable income; but, by the process of

amendment, change the constituent elements going

to make up the reassessed taxable income. New

sources of income may be introduced, new

deductions allowed, old deductions previously

allowed now disallowed or vice versa. The

possibilities are numerous. In the result, the

taxable income may be more or less than it was

under the original assessment or remain the same."

49. While the legislature saw fit to amend s.185 to overcome the actual result in Offshore, it did so by confining the right to object to the "particular" the subject of the amendment. Thus, in the example referred to by Deane J, a taxpayer could accept the exclusion of interest excluded, but object to the inclusion of the new interest amount, notwithstanding that the effect of the objection was that no alteration was made to the taxable income. However, a taxpayer in the position of Offshore Oil NL could not object on the ground that some deduction should have been afforded him, because that was not a particular in respect of which the assessment was altered.

50. In a case such as might be suggested by the facts in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Reynolds (1981) 81 ATC 4131, where the assessment was originally made by the Commissioner upon the basis that a particular profit was included in assessable income by s.26AAA of the Act, by virtue of property having been acquired and sold within twelve months, and it was subsequently discovered, perhaps as a result of the taxpayer's objection, that the sale was outside the twelve months' period, the Commissioner, if of the view that the amount was in any event included in assessable income under say s.25(1) of the Act as income within ordinary concepts, need make no amendment to the assessment. For in seeking to rely upon another section of the Act, he makes no alteration to any constituent element of the assessment. There has been no alteration to a particular of the assessment. However, the application of Part IVA will invariably involve such a step. The present case provides a good example.

51. It seems from the adjustment sheet, as elaborated upon by the Commissioner's Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions, that the amended assessments the subject of objection were made by treating the taxpayer as having derived the whole of the income claimed by him to have been derived by the trustee of the trust. Some of the deductions claimed to have been incurred by the trustee were accepted as having been incurred by Mr Jackson, presumably treating the trustee as his agent in incurring the relevant expenditure. Thus, although the adjustment spoke in terms of additions of "net income" and subtraction of certain other items, the reference to "net income" must presuppose taking into account, in determining the assessable income, a new item of income and granting to the taxpayer deductions not previously allowed to him, as well as removing from the account certain items of expenditure that had apparently been included as income in the original assessment.

52. When the Commissioner came to consider Part IVA, he must be presumed to have taken the view that the relevant tax benefit obtained by Mr Jackson was referable to an amount not being included in Mr Jackson's assessable income, which but for the scheme would have been so included or might reasonably be expected to have been so included. At least prima facie, this would seem to be the gross amount of the income derived by the trustee. Thus, if the prerequisites for the operation of Part IVA are satisfied, the Commissioner is empowered by s.177F(1) to make a determination that the whole or a part of that amount is to be included in assessable income.

53. A prerequisite of the Commissioner making the determination, however, will be his conclusion that but for the operation of Part IVA the whole of the amount would not have been included in the assessable income. Notionally, therefore, the Commissioner will commence the process of making a determination under Part IVA, in a case such as the present, by excluding the amount included in assessable income in the previous assessment (in this case that assessment being itself a default assessment), presumably because he has taken the view that that amount was not assessable income of the taxpayer. Having done so, he then proceeds to make his determination under s.177F(1). In the present case, this involved the Commissioner making determinations that an amount (which happened to equate to the net figures added to taxable income in the previous amended assessments) was to be included in the assessable income of the taxpayer in each of the years in question.

54. The result of this process can be seen, therefore, to be that the Commissioner must be taken to have removed from the assessable income theretofore calculated the amount of gross income derived by the trustee, and to have added to that assessable income the amount referred to in his determination. These are, of course, alterations to the constituent elements of the credit side of the account which constituted the particulars of the assessment then on foot. Prima facie, if that were all that were done, the deductions for amounts claimed to have been incurred by the trustee, which had been allowed as debits to the constituent items in the previous assessments would continue to be allowable. Thus, the effect of the determination under s.177F would be a reduction in Mr Jackson's taxable income.

55. For Mr Jackson's taxable income and tax payable to remain the same after the determination, it would therefore be necessary for the Commissioner to form the view that the deductions which he had previously allowed were not properly allowable, perhaps because they were not in fact in respect of amounts incurred by Mr Jackson. It would only be by further amending the amended assessments in the manner I have described that the Commissioner could produce the result that the taxable income and tax payable after making the s.177F determination would remain the same, with the consequence that the Commissioner could at the hearing before the Court rely upon the s.177F determination.

56. It may be said that the amended assessment made by the Commissioner on 30 July 1984 did not proceed on the basis which I have described, having regard to the way the adjustment sheet which accompanied those determinations is worded. But it is clear that what is said in the adjustment sheet involved a short cut in much the same way as the proposition, in Offshore, that the Commissioner had merely added a net amount of interest, rather than adding one amount of interest and subtracting another, was thought by Deane J to involve a short cut. The process of calculation of taxable income involves commencing with assessable income and subtracting therefrom allowable deductions, not treating net amounts as assessable income.

57. The same result would ordinarily seem to follow in the disallowing of a tax benefit referable to a deduction. If the Commissioner has previously taken the view (in making an assessment) that the taxpayer was not entitled to a deduction, his making of a determination that an amount not be allowable as a deduction to that taxpayer necessarily involves him in concluding that the deduction previously not allowable was in fact allowable. Thus, to give effect to his determination, he will be required to make two alterations, at least, to the original assessment. The first to allow the deduction claimed; the second, as a result of making the s.177F(1) determination, to disallow an amount (it may, depending on the determination, be the whole or part of the deduction) which he has determined should not be allowable.

58. It is, perhaps, interesting to note that while s.177 F(2) specifically provides that the amount of a determination relating to income is to be included in assessable income, there is no corresponding provision relating to deductions. It is, in any event, implicit. But the only way that the Commissioner can, in such a case, give legal effect to his determination will be to issue, subject to the limitations imposed by s.170 and 177G, an amended assessment. Where there has not been a prior assessment, it is of course obvious, that the only way a determination could be given effect to would be by way of an assessment including an amount in assessable income or disallowing a deduction as the case may be, with or without compensatory adjustments under s.177F(3).

59. Against this view of s.177F, it was submitted by counsel for the Commissioner that if the legislature had intended that the words "give effect" to the determination meant no more than make an assessment, it could have said so quite simply. But this criticism implies that no more is involved in giving effect to a determination than making an assessment of taxable income and tax payable thereon. This is not so.

60. First, once a s.177F(1) determination is made, the making of that determination leads to the making of a determination, if appropriate, under s.177F(2). It also requires the Commissioner to consider the question of the making of compensatory adjustments under s.177F(3), by way of further determinations. It is true that if the Commissioner does not act under s.177F to make compensatory adjustments, a taxpayer not being the taxpayer whose tax benefit was the subject of the s.177F(1) determination, and perhaps the taxpayer himself, may at any time request the Commissioner to make a s.177F(3) determination, and is given rights if dissatisfied with the Commissioner's decision in respect of that request to object and appeal. But this does not exclude the Commissioner's obligation to consider the issue of the making of such adjustments once he has made the s.177F(1) determination.

61. Further, another consequence of the making of a determination under s.177F(1), and the inclusion of income or the disallowance of a deduction by reason of the making of that determination, is that the taxpayer becomes by force of s.226 of the present Act (s.226(2A) of the Act in the years of income in question) liable to pay by way of penalty additional tax equal, in effect, to double the tax which would have been exigible had the tax benefit overcome by the determination never existed. However, the Commissioner is empowered by s.227(3), formerly s.226(3), to remit the whole or any part of the additional tax payable. Under the present law, the Commissioner is required to assess additional tax payable: s.227(1).

62. The view that a determination under s.177F(1) is to be carried into effect by an assessment is given added weight by a number of other considerations.

63. First, at the time when Part IVA was inserted into the Act, a taxpayer was bound by his notice of objection. If, in a case such as the present, the Commissioner could make a determination after making an assessment, the taxpayer would have had no right at all to object in respect of the making of the determination and in proceedings under Part V of the Act would have been bound by his original notice of objection and confined to the grounds stated therein. As it happens, Mr Jackson's objection contained no ground relating to Part IVA.

64. It is true that there now exists power in the Administrative Appeals Tribunal, or this Court, to order that the taxpayer not be limited to the grounds stated in his objection (s.190(a)), but the taxpayer has no automatic right to such an order. While there could be little doubt that an order would be made if required in such a circumstance, the point should be made that the legislature in amending s.190(a) in 1986 cannot be taken to have thereby conferred a power upon the Commissioner to make a s.177F(1) determination without proceeding to an assessment, where such power did not exist before. Further, it does not seem to me to be an answer to say that prior to 1986, the Court or Tribunal in the interests of fairness could have either permitted the taxpayer to argue the Part IVA issue, or have prevented the Commissioner from relying upon it.

65. Second, the view that an assessment is required to be issued, of which notice is given to the taxpayer, ensures to a taxpayer, who desires to appeal, the right to elect in accordance with the Act, whether to refer the objection decision to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal or the Court, each having, as indicated above, different functions and powers. According to the Commissioner's contention, the taxpayer could be bound by an election to appeal to the Court, made at a time when no issue arose under Part IVA; he could thus lose irrevocably the right of administrative review on the merits which the Act plainly intended to provide at his election.

66. Third, the requirement that an assessment be issued ensures that the limitation period in s.177G(1) applies alike to persons who have been assessed on the basis that the scheme has produced a tax benefit, and who have paid the tax assessed, and to persons who have been assessed originally on the wrong basis that the scheme has been ineffective, quite apart from the operation of Part IVA. In no case may the Commissioner amend an assessment (other than to give effect to a compensatory adjustment under s.177F(3)) later than six years from the date tax became due and payable on the prior assessment. While s.177G(1) does not compel the answer that, in all cases, the making of a determination under s.177F(1) is given effect to by an assessment, it is clearly consistent with such an approach.

67. Fourth, it is difficult to conceive, as a matter of principle, that where what is referred to the Court under s.187 is the objection decision, with which the taxpayer is dissatisfied, an event occurring thereafter (other than perhaps a retrospective change of law) can alter the nature of the proceedings. That the court can take no notice of events that occur between the date of the objection decision and the time of hearing seems to flow from the decision of the High Court in Kolotex Hosiery (Australia) Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1975] HCA 5; (1974-5) 132 CLR 535, where the Court regarded as irrelevant the formation of a state of mind by the Commissioner after the date of assessment. Kolotex was a case involving the exercise by the Commissioner of a discretion. Barwick C.J. at 541, after stating that the Commissioner's satisfaction as at the time of the assessment was the relevant matter, said:

"In emphasizing that the Commissioner's state of

mind at the time of assessment is the relevantly

critical fact, I include in that connexion the

time up to the date of the issue of the

assessment, a period during which the Commissioner

may reconsider his assessment. Once the

assessment is issued, however, it is the

Commissioner's state of mind which then exists as

to the relevant matters which is the critical

element in the process of assessment:..."

68. In the same case Stephen J at p 576 also expressed the view that the requisite state of mind of the Commissioner must be, to have statutory significance, that existing before the issue of the notice of assessment.

69. While it is true that the issue in Kolotex, depending, as it did, upon judicial review of an administrative discretion, is quite remote from the present problem, I know of no decision, and Counsel for the Commissioner was unable to refer us to one, where evidence of events occurring after the date of the objection decision has been either admitted or regarded as being of relevance in the determination of a tax appeal.

70. Counsel for the Commissioner sought to emphasise what had been said by the High Court in Dalco (supra) by Toohey J at 173, where his Honour expressed agreement with Wilcox J in this Court:

"the task for the taxpayer, upon an appeal or a

review under Pt V of the Act, is to show that the

amount of money for which tax is levied by a

particular notice of assessment exceeds the actual

substantive liability of the taxpayer."

71. We were referred also to a passage in the judgment of Brennan J in the same case where his Honour said at 168:

"...the purpose of the procedure of assessment,

objection and appeal or review is to ascertain the

true tax liability of the taxpayer under the

substantive provisions of the Act. Oftentimes,

the grounds of an objection and the Commissioner's

notice of decision thereon will define the issues

for determination by a court entertaining an

appeal against the assessment; but not necessarily

so. It is not the grounds of the objection

against an assessment but the objection itself

which is treated as an appeal and forwarded to a

Supreme Court for hearing and determination: ss

187 (1)(b), 197, 199. It would be inappropriate

for a court determining an appeal to make an order

altering the tax liability assessed (s199) unless

the court were satisfied that the amount to which

it proposed to alter the assessment represented

the true tax liability of the taxpayer. Although

the grounds of objection limit the grounds of

appeal, the ultimate question for the court

hearing the appeal is not whether the grounds have

been made out but whether the amount assessed as

taxable income is wrong."

72. The passages cited point out with some force that it is for the taxpayer to show that the assessment objected to is excessive. They do not, however, support the view that the issue before the Court or a Tribunal is other than the review of the objection decision. To succeed in that review the taxpayer must, conformably with Dalco and with s.190(b) of the Act, show that the assessment does not represent his true tax liability, but the review or appeal, as the case may be, is not, as other proceedings in the law are not, wholly unconfined; it must be limited to events affecting that substantial liability no later than the date upon which the objection decision is made.

73. Finally, acceptance of the Commissioner's submissions would have produced somewhat anomalous results in a case where, after the taxpayer's substantive liability under an assessment had been litigated in the Board of Review (that is, prior to the tax jurisdiction being vested in the Administrative Appeals Tribunal) the Commissioner had lost because the scheme of the taxpayer was held to be successful, and the Commissioner had appealed to the Supreme Court: cf. Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Mantle Traders Pty Limited (1980) 80 ATC 4588. In such a case, it could not have been said that any error of law had been involved in the Board's decision on the Part IVA matter. Indeed, if Part IVA did apply, the Board's decision would presumably have been correct. How would the Commissioner's grounds of appeal have been worded to take into account a determination after the appeal had been commenced?

74. Under the present law, an appeal to this court from the Administrative Appeals Tribunal is an appeal on a question of law:s 44 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, Brown v Repatriation Commission [1985] FCA 194; (1985) 60 ALR 289 at p 291, Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Brixius (1987) 16 FCR 359. The Commissioner, in an appeal from the Tribunal where he had been unsuccessful, would be unable to raise at all the question of the operation of Part IVA, as that would have been a matter not before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal. Yet, if the Commissioner's submission be accepted, the making of the s.177F determination does not involve the amendment of the assessment, so the Commissioner would be unable at a later time to issue an amended assessment, notwithstanding that the limitation period had not yet run. The interpretation which I believe to be correct would bring about the result that the Commissioner, having made the s.177F determination would be able to amend the assessment, and if it were objected against and the objection were disallowed, the question of the operation and effect of Part IVA could be litigated, at the option of the taxpayer, either in the Administrative Appeals Tribunal or this Court.

75. For these reasons I am of the view that the appeal should be dismissed with costs.
