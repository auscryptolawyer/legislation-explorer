Re Taxation Appeals [1989] AATA 45 (3 March 1989)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

Nos. NT87/163, NT88/2070 and NT88/2071

AAT No. 4942

Income Tax

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Dr P. Gerber(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Family trust - Assessable income - Introduction of additional trust to family trust - Profit of the family trust distributed to the newly introduced trust - Equivalent amount (less promotor's fee) returned on same day to family trust as "premium on issue of unit" - Subsequent capital distribution to appointor - Whether introduction of additional trust ineffective and/or a fraud on the power - Whether arrangement void as against the Commissioner - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936-1980, sec. 97, 98, 260.

Newton v. FC of T (1959) AC 450

Jaques v. FC of T [1923] HCA 70; (1924) 34 CLR 328

Hancock v. FC of T [1961] HCA 90; (1962-1963) 108 CLR 258

Mullins v. FC of T 75 ATC 4288

Slutzkin v. FC of T 77 ATC 4076

Stamp v. F.C. of T. 88 ATC 4803

F.C. of T. v. Whiting [1943] HCA 45; (1943) 68 CLR 199

Taylor v. F.C. of T. [1970] HCA 10; (1970) 119 CLR 444

HEARING

CANBERRA

3:3:1989

ORDER

Pursuant to subsection 43(1)(a) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, the Tribunal affirms the objection decisions in each case, save as to the issue of penalties in the case of NT87/163 as to which the Tribunal gives the parties liberty to apply if they are unable to reach agreement on that issue.

DECISION

These cases stem from objections to the Commissioner's assessments by three taxpayers (henceforth referred to as Maud, Dick and Dora, the latter two being at the relevant time under a legal disability). All were beneficiaries of the Allen Family Trust ("The Trust") during the year ended 30 June 1980.

2. In the 1980 tax year, the Commissioner, like Caesar in Gaul, divided the net income of the trust ($156,583) into three parts, including one-third (approximately) in the assessable income of Maud, Dick and Dora respectively which he assessed pursuant to sections 97 and 98, treating all three beneficiaries as being "presently entitled".

3. The taxpayers, on the other hand, point to the following purported distributions of trust funds from the Allen Family Trust to :

The Boston Trust $156,583

Dick 1,040

Dora 1,040

made in the exercise of the discretion of Parsnip Productions Pty Ltd, which was the Trustee.

4. The Allen Family Trust was created by deed dated 27 June 1979 and so far as relevant provided as follows:

(i)The Appointor was Tobias Allen;

(ii)The Trustee was Parsnip Productions

Pty Ltd;

(iii)The Primary Beneficiaries were -

(a) Maud, Dick and Dora (wife and

children of Tobias respectively);

(b) Any further children of Tobias

Allen born after 27th June 1979

and before the vesting date;

5. Provision for distribution was made in Clause 2 of the Deed:

"Until the vesting day the Trustee shall stand

possessed of the Trust Fund UPON TRUST as to the

income thereof before the end of each financial

year to divide and pay the same such portion

thereof as the Trustee may in his absolute

discretion deem meet to or among the Specified

Class ... either solely to one of them or to such

one or more of them in such shares and proportions

as the Trustee may in his absolute discretion deem

meet AND in respect of any portion of such income

as shall not have been divided and paid as

aforesaid at or prior to the end of each financial

year to divide or pay the same to or among the

members of the Specified Class in equal shares, and

it is hereby declared that for the purposes of this

Deed and subject to this Clause the members of the

Specified Class are presently and absolutely

entitled to the said income in equal

shares."(italics added)

6. "Specified Class" referred to above is defined in Schedule 1 as follows:

"The Specified Class" means those persons

referred to in the Second Schedule to this

Deed together with such other persons if

any, appointed in accordance with Clause 17

at any time during the continuance of this

Trust ...

7. Clause 17 provided generally for the addition of beneficiaries, cl. 17(c) providing specifically that any person thus appointed may, inter alia, include

"a trust under which any one or more of the members

of the Specified Class are beneficiaries whether

absolutely, contingently presently or prospectively

and whether such Trust be a Discretionary Trust or

otherwise."

8. On 1st May 1980 the Boston Trust was created by deed, the provisions of which were identical with those of the Allen Family Trust save that:

(i) The Appointor was Parsnip Productions Pty Ltd

(ii) The Primary Beneficiaries were:

(a) Maud

(b) Bluechip Pty Ltd

9. On the same day (May 1, 1980) the Boston Trust was added as a beneficiary of the Allen Family Trust pursuant to clause 17 of the Trust Deed. Evidence of this addition was tendered as an exhibit which contained a minute of a resolution of Parsnip Productions Pty Ltd. Again on that fateful day, the Allen Family Trust distributed income in the sum of $156,583 to the Boston Trust, that sum along with another distribution of $156,584, which came from another trust, going to the credit of Parsnip Productions Pty Ltd which was the Trustee of both the Allen Family Trust and the Boston Trust. At the end of the day, the sum of $156,583, after "day-touring" through the now well recognized series of exotic companies with funny names and, after deducting $30,000 and $7,500 as the reward for outfits calling themselves Farmston Pty Ltd and MD Finance Pty Ltd for their promotional assistance, the balance finding its way back unerringly into the hands of a brother of a partner of Tobias, and ultimately into the hands of Tobias himself.

10. It is the distribution from the Allen Family Trust to the Boston Trust that the Commissioner attacks on the basis that, as a matter of law, the variation of the Trust Deed which introduced Boston as a beneficiary was ineffective. Heavy reliance is placed upon the failure to comply strictly with the terms of Clause 17 of the Trust Deed set out above. In the event of this argument failing, and as a sub-argument, the Commissioner submits that the purported exercise of the power by Tobias Allen was "tantamount to a fraud on the power and would be bad in any event". If the variation is ineffective and/or if there is a fraud on the power - as to which I make no comment - this may afford some comfort to the defeated beneficiaries in equity; it can only assist the Commissioner to the extent that it underlines the extreme artificiality of the whole scheme or arrangement and emphasizes its fiscal purpose. It was faintly argued for the Crown that the Commissioner is entitled to rely on equities which exist for the benefit of other individuals. In the view I have formed of the evidence, I do not need to pursue this argument further.

11. Alternatively, the Commissioner relies on sec. 260 of the Tax Act to render the purported distribution to Boston void as against him because the appointment of Boston and the dealing with the Trust's profit was an arrangement which had as its main purpose and effect the reduction of the liability to income tax. This is amply demonstrated by the mass of evidence relied upon to show the commercial unreality of what took place on 1 May 1980. As already noted in paragraph 9 above, the effect of the arrangement was that on that day the entire profit left the Allen Family Trust in the form of a distribution to Boston, only to return at a later date (albeit reduced by the promotor's commission) to the the Allen Family Trust in a form described in its return as "premium on issue of unit" which in turn made a "capital distribution " to Tobias. On this evidence, I was invited by Mr Emmett of learned counsel for the Commissioner to infer:

"...that the only possible explanation for this

elaborate transaction is to strip the Allen Family

Trust of money which would otherwise be income of

those beneficiaries; send it on a journey to Perth

and back again, where it is transmogrified into a

capital sum which is slightly reduced, and received

back into the same trust and then distributed to Mr

Allen as capital distribution."

12. I now turn to the argument involving sec. 260. Mr Cohen, who ably argued the applicant's case, sought to counter the reliance based on this notorious anti-avoidance provision by pointing to the statement in Newton v. F.C. of T. (1959) AC 450 at 467 where, in considering the effect of sec. 260 upon an impugned arrangement, Lord Denning, delivering the judgment of their Lordships, stated:

"In the words of the courts of Australia, (sec. 260)

is an 'annihilating' provision - the Commissioner

can use the section so as to ignore the

transactions which are caught by it. But the

ignoring of the transactions - or the annihilation

of them - does not itself create a liability to

tax. In order to make the taxpayers liable, the

Commissioner must show that moneys have come into

the hands of the taxpayers which the Commissioner

is entitled to treat as income derived by them.

Their Lordships agree with the way in which

Fullagar J. put it in his judgement: 'Section 260

alters nothing that was done as between the

parties. But, for the purposes of income tax, it

entitles the Commissioner to look at the end result

and to ignore all the steps which were taken in

pursuance of the avoided arrangement...' (my italics).

13. Thus the applicant contends that the portion which appears in italics above (with slight modifications as a result of statements in Hancock v. F.C. of T. [1961] HCA 90; (1962-1963) 108 CLR 258) is authority for the proposition that as "nothing has come into the hands of or for the benefit of the taxpayer", sec. 260 can have no application. It was indeed common ground that none of the monies were paid to Maud, Dick or Dora (apart from the customary $1040 distributed to each of the (then) two infants), but that is no answer to the applicant's contention.

14. Division 6 of Part III of the Act provides for the assessment of Trust income and seeks to tax any beneficiary who is "presently entitled" to the income of a trust. In F.C.T. v. Whiting [1943] HCA 45; (1943) 68 CLR 199, the High Court held that a beneficiary is "presently entitled" if he has a present right to have the income paid to him by the trustee. Subsequently, in Taylor v. FCT 70 ATC 4026 it was recognized that a beneficiary may be "presently entitled" even though under a legal disability (such as infancy). It follows that a beneficiary may be taxed though not in actual receipt of his/her share of the income of the trust.

15. With great respect to Mr Geoffrey Cohen, I have not been persuaded that the quotation, relied on from Newton's case, has the effect contended for. As has already been pointed out, Division 6 of the Act specifically provides for a tax liability on income which a beneficiary does not yet have "in his hands", so that Lord Denning's statement that a liability to tax only arises if it can be shown that monies have come into the hands of a taxpayer which the Commissioner is entitled to treat as income must be read against the peculiar facts of Newton's case.

16. Although Jaques v. F.C. of T. [1923] HCA 70; (1924) 34 CLR 328 was not cited by either party, it seems to me that the decision in that case wholly governs the outcome of this application. The principle to emerge from Jaques may well be the only aspect of sec. 260 to remain intact after the subsequent analyses of the section by the various judges of the High Court.

17. The facts in Jaques were set out by Barwick CJ in Mullens v. F.C. of T. 76 ATC 4288 where, at pp 4295-4297, the learned Chief Justice noted that the case involved "three companies (which) had reached a concluded agreement for the reconstruction of one of them which was to be placed in liquidation." As originally contemplated, this was to involve a transfer of assets in exchange for shares with no concomitant tax advantage. It was subsequently realized by the parties that by carrying out the reconstruction by a different method, substantial tax deductions could be claimed. In the result, the earlier documents and resolutions were revoked and new arrangements were entered into. In dealing with the application of sec. 260 in that case, Barwick C.J. prefaced his remarks with the observation:

"(T)hough the section speaks of the purpose in

entering into the transaction, it can have no

relevance if, being effective, the transaction does

not alter the incidence of tax, as that expression

has come to be understood. As I have already

pointed out, there will be no relevant alteration

of the incidence of tax if the transaction, being

the actual transaction between the parties,

conforms to and satisfies a provision of the Act

even if it has taken the form in which it was

entered into by the parties in order to obtain the

benefit of that provision of the Act. It would be

otherwise if there had been some antecedent

transaction between the parties, for which the

transaction under attack was substituted in order

to obtain the benefit of the particular provision

of the Act. ... Various reasons were given by the

participating Justices (in Jaques) but in all the

reasons the existence of the first transaction, and

the fact that the second was but a form of carrying

out the agreed reconstruction, are essential to the

conclusion that the new arrangements were

ineffective because of sec. 53 of the 1915 Act (the

forerunner of sec. 260) to obtain for the

shareholders the benefit of the deduction for calls

for which sec. 18(1)(i) of the 1915 Act provided.

... It would follow, in my opinion, from the

reasoning of all these Justices, that had there

been no antecedent agreement and the companies had

from the outset agreed in terms of the new or

substituted agreement, the shareholders would have

been entitled to a deduction of the amount paid as

calls on the mining shares. The choice of the

second form of reconstruction, if originally taken,

though chosen so as to obtain the benefit of the

statutory deduction, could not have been struck

down by s.53 of the 1915 Act...". (my italics).

18. The above view was adopted with apparent approval by Aickin J in Slutzkin 77 ATC 4076 at 4083 and by Mason J in Cridland 77 ATC 4538 at 4541.

19. What emerges from Jaques' case and its gloss in subsequent decisions is that the section will not permit a "reconstruction", for the purpose of altering the incidence of tax, of a pre-existing state of affairs or antecedent transaction. For fiscal purposes, it is thus even beyond the powers of the gods to alter the past.

20. If one commences with the assumption that sec. 260 is no more than an annihilating section and does not itself impose tax, nor permits a reconstruction of any transaction, so that the mere avoidance of tax is irrelevant unless, "if the transaction were swept aside, a factual situation involving a payment of tax is exposed ..." (per Barwick C.J.; Slutzkin v. F.C. of T. infra at p 4080), it follows that the alteration to the trust in this case is void as against the Commissioner, and this is so notwithstanding a clause in the deed of settlement in standard form providing for the addition of, inter alia, a trust of which any one or more of the members of the specified class are beneficiaries. If such an addition is subsequently undertaken for a purpose which defies explanation by reference to ordinary family dealing and which, ex facie, is obviously designed to bring about a result that less tax will be paid, it is annihilated for tax purposes.

21. It will thus be seen that by annihilating the exercise of the trustee's discretion to pay the income of the trust to the Boston Trust, the very tax liability imposed by the respondent is, without reconstruction, clearly exposed by clause 2 of the Trust Deed, which obliges the trustee to pay the undistributed income "to or among the members of the Specified Class in equal shares".

22. For the sake of completeness, reference should be made to Stamp v. F.C. of T. 88 ATC 4803 which was relied on by the applicant. It is sufficient for me to say that I have not been persuaded that the decision in that case has any application to this case. In Stamp, the taxpayer asserted (and was believed) that she was unaware that she was a named beneficiary in the deed. In the present case, no such evidence was adduced. Again, to the extent that Stamp is relied upon for the proposition that the beneficiaries in the instant case did not participate in the scheme or arrangement by which the profit went on its merry way, ending up with Boston and thereafter (semble) in the hands of Tobias Allen in the form of capital, it is apposite to point out that none of the applicants were called as witnesses and no explanation was offered for such omission. It is trite to point out that the onus of proving lack of participation is on him/her who seeks to rely upon it.

23. The Commissioner imposed sec. 226(2) penalties for (i) failing to lodge Maud's return in the time required by the Act and (ii) for a purported expenditure claimed as a deduction in excess of the amount actually incurred. I have no evidence in the exhibits which give any indication as to the nature of the claimed expenditure or, indeed, what penalty has been imposed. I am therefore unable to deal with that aspect of Maud's application.

24. For the above reasons I direct that the objection decisions be affirmed in each case, save as to the issue of penalties in the case of Maud, as to which I give the parties liberty to apply if they are unable to reach agreement on that issue.
