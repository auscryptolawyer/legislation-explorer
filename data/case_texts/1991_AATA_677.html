ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

INCOME TAX - assessable income - resignation benefit on termination of employment - whether eligible termination payment - whether "excessive benefit" - whether assessable - discussion of retirement

Income Tax Assessment Act ss 23F(1)(2)(h)(i)(ii)(iii)(iv), 26(d), 26AFA(1)(a)(aa)(b)(i)(ii)(c)(i)(ii)(iii)(iv), (2)(a)(b)(i)(ii)(iii), (4), 27A

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re

Applicant

And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

23 August 1991 Mr BJ McMahon (Deputy President)

1. The applicant was born in 1935. He was employed by a family company prior to being employed by Company One from 1 July 1983. Prior to and after that date he was a member of a superannuation fund established on 2 March 1978, which was set up to provide benefits for staff of the group of companies. He continued to be a member after taking up employment with Company One, following its incorporation on 8 April 1983. For the year ended 30 June 1983, he was not paid any salary but was paid director's fees of $1,000. In 1984 he was paid a salary of $30 000 and director's fees of $2 000. This was repeated in 1985. in 1986 he was paid a salary of $30,000 but was not paid any director's fees. A further sum of $30 000, less tax, was paid to him on 13 June 1986 bringing the total amount of his salary for income tax purposes for that year to $60,000.

  1. He claims to have resigned from the service of Company One on 17 June 1986. At that time he had a service period of 18 years. His normal retiring age would not occur until year 2000. He received a resignation benefit from the Fund of $202,642. As will be seen later, this appears to have been calculated on a "final salary" of $60,000.
  2. The Commissioner did not regard the benefit as an eligible termination payment and assessed the amount in full. This application is brought to review an objection decision which affirmed that assessment.
  3. Company One carried on a large business as a motor vehicle dealer. It held a franchise for a well known mass produced car. The company had a turnover of some 70 new vehicles per month and about 60 used vehicles per month. Most of its business was carried out through sales for private use rather than through fleet sales. Its turnover was between $25million and $27million per year. In 1986 the applicant was engaged for 6 days per week for some 6 or 7 hours per day, carrying out his duties for the company.
  4. Although Company One was ultimately owned for the benefit of the applicant's former wife and his first family, he readily agreed that it was a one-man company. He made all the day to day decisions in the management of the company. Although it was not recorded, he was clearly its general manager and chairman. Board meetings were not held frequently The only other director of Company One was the applicant's former wife. He said in evidence that he would discuss matters with her from time to time, but mainly the material presented to the board was left for her inspection and was available to her only if she asked for it.
  5. Early in 1986, the applicant personally controlled all aspects of the affairs of Company One. There were 7 managers dealing with such branches of the business as new cars, used cars, customers' service and spare parts. The 60 employees of the company and all its activities were located in Premises One. The applicant gave evidence that his managers earned between $30,000 and $40,000 per year, made up of salary, commission and bonuses.
  6. Towards the end of March or the beginning of April 1986, the applicant was approached by the owner of another motor vehicle dealer business in the same town who wished to retire. Following some discussions, it was agreed that this business should be purchased by Company Two, a company substantially owned by the applicant's second wife and his second family, but, as with Company One, in fact controlled by the applicant. Company Two bought the new business and for a time continued to trade in Premises Two.
  7. A property company in the group had acquired a large area of land, approximately 1 kilometer away from Premises One and Two. The applicant was responsible for developing this area. It was intended that the first tenant would be Company One and its business. The arrangement of the move occupied a good deal of the applicant's time. Not only was it necessary to ensure that the accommodation requirements of Company One were met, it was also necessary to negotiate with the various authorities concerning permits and planning authorisations in the large scheme of the developing area in question.
  8. The applicant said in evidence that he "turned over" his sales functions to an employee who previously had been one of his managers. He then added that this made that employee a general sales manager. Later in his evidence he said that the employee took over all of his functions and that he became a general manager. The applicant said that those of the applicant's functions relating to administration and finance were also put under the responsibility of an administration manager. He said that he intended to leave Company One and to spend his time running the business of Company Two. That business dealt in quality cars, which meant that there were fewer sales and there was more time to spend with customers.
  9. At the beginning of 1986 he had an office in the main selling area of Premises One. After Premises Two were acquired, he travelled in between them although he still retained his original office. The general sales manager, or the general manager, did not move into the applicant's office. By June 1986 the applicant said that he was spending 25 per cent of his time in Business Two, 10 per cent of his time in Business One and the balance of his time dealing with the design and construction of Premises Three. The transfer of Company One and Business One to Premises Three did not take place until the first weekend in August 1986. He retained his office in Premises One until that transfer was concluded.
  10. Although he spent time in Business Two he continued to receive weekly reports and stock reports of Company One. He continued, of course, to serve on the board of directors of Company One and Company Two. The evidence suggests that he was, for all practical purposes and at all relevant times, in control of all the material affairs of both companies.
  11. During the first half of 1986, he held some discussions concerning his entitlement to superannuation. The fund was managed by the AMP Society. Between March and June 1986 several requests were made to AMP to ascertain the maximum tax withdrawal benefit available to the applicant based on various salaries, ranging from $35,000 to $60,000 per annum. The applicant said that during these discussions, he pointed out that he had always paid himself much less than he warranted. Others in comparable positions were paid more by their companies. He agreed that there had been discussions about the levels of benefits which he could draw from the plan and that he was in favour of obtaining the highest level of those benefits.

13. The alleged retirement date of 17 June 1986 appears as a result of discussions leading to the respondent's auditors report. It is not, however, recorded specifically in any evidence that was put before me. Indeed the evidence was quite vague as to when any retirement (if there was such a retirement) took place. Whatever the date, the applicant denied that he had any intentions of returning to the service of Company One. He agreed, however, that the person whom he variously described as a sales manager, general sales manager and general manager, was unsatisfactory and that he had left the company towards the end of 1986. Although he received reports of the affairs of Company One, he denied that any of the managers reported to him and alleged that the information he obtained was in his capacity as a continuing director of Company One. Nevertheless at the beginning of 1990, it was clear that the affairs of Company One were in an unsatisfactory state. At the request of a finance company, he went back to managing the company on a day to day basis. This continued until approximately June 1991. During that interval, he agreed that his role was identical to that prior to June 1986, except that the organisation had grown considerably in the interval and consequently he had less contact with customers in the second management period than he did during the original management period.

  1. There was no evidence put before me of any board meetings at which the retirement of the applicant or the appointments of substitute general managers had been discussed or endorsed. The applicant agreed that there would be no such minute. The applicant agreed that after 1 July 1986 he would have spoken regularly to his "successor" about the running of the business. The successor was not a director of the company at any time. He complained to the applicant of the difficulties he was experiencing in obtaining cooperation from other staff members.
  2. Some evidence was given by the secretary of Company One. In early 1986, he was the accountant for all the companies in the group and secretary of the majority of them. In early 1986 he was located at Premises One. From August 1986 he was located in Premises Three. He agreed that during 1986 there were changes and responsibilities in running the business but that the personnel were the same. He said that in June 1986 the applicant took a "lesser involvement in the running of Company One" but that he did not resign as a director. The company secretary agreed that he had reported to the applicant on a daily basis even after he acquired more financial responsibility. He agreed that he did not report to anyone else, including the person who is described as the general manager. In the witness' view the applicant was the owner of the business and any major decision had to go back to him for approval.

16. An eligible termination payment is defined in s 27A of the Income Tax Assessment Act in the following terms -

"27A (Interpretation)

"eligible termination payment", in relation to a taxpayer, means -

(a) any payment made in respect of the taxpayer in consequence of the termination of any employment of the taxpayer, other than payment -

(i) to which paragraph (b) applies;

(ii) of an annuity, or supplement, to which section 27H applies;

(iii) from a fund in relation to which section 121 DA applies, or has applied, in relation to the year of income commencing on 1 July 1984 or any subsequent year of income;

(iv) of an amount to which section 26AC or 26AD applies; or

(v) of an amount that, under any provision of this Act, is deemed to be a dividend paid to the taxpayer;

(aa) any payment made to the taxpayer in consequence of the termination of any employment of another person, where:

(i) the payment is made after the death of the other person;

(ii) the taxpayer was not a dependant of the other person at the time of the death of the other person and is not a dependant of the other person at the time of payment;

(iii) the payment is made to the taxpayer otherwise than as trustee of the estate of the other person; and

(iv) he payment is not a payment:

(A) to which paragraph (ba) applies;

(B) of an annuity, or supplement, to which section 27H applies; or

(C) from a fund in relation to which section 121 DA applies, or has applied, in relation to the year of income commencing on 1 July 1984 or any subsequent year of income;

(b) any payment made from a superannuation fund in respect of the taxpayer by reason that the taxpayer is or was a member of the fund, not being a payment -

(i) that is income of the taxpayer;

(ii) to which paragraph (d), (da), (e) or (ga) applies; or

(iii) that is a benefit to which subsection 26AF(1), 26AFA(1) or 26AFB(2) or (3) applies,

reduced by any amount that has been or will be included in the assessable income of the taxpayer under subsection 26AF(2), 26AFA(3) or 26AFB(5) in respect of the transfer by the taxpayer of a right to receive the payment or any part of the payment;

........."

17. The first question to be determined therefore is whether there has been a termination of the applicant's employment. In Reseck v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 75 ATC 4213 at 4216, Gibbs J observed that it was a question of fact whether employment had indeed been terminated -

"....In most cases in which a workman ceased his employment on a Friday and commenced employment again with the same employer on the following Monday it would be impossible to say that his employment had ever been terminated. If there were a contract agreement or arrangement whereby the employment of the workman was terminated and recommenced it would no doubt be possible to invoke the provisions of sec. 260 of the Act, but even without the aid of that section in many cases when all the facts had been regarded the proper conclusion to be drawn would be that there had been no termination of the workman's employment at all...."

18. It is sometimes difficult to establish termination of employment when an applicant moves from one company to another within the same group and continues to carry out overlapping functions in relation to both companies. It is possible to gain some assistance from formal records. This was illustrated in Case K76 78 ATC 703. In the present circumstances no such assistance is available. The only evidence of termination of employment is that is from the applicant himself, corroborated to a limited degree by the company secretary.

  1. In Case C103(1953) 3 TBRD 602 at 605 and 606 the Board of Review said that "In order to establish, for the purposes of s 26(d) that there has been retirement of a taxpayer from an office or employment it is necessary to prove : (a) that the taxpayer has in fact relinquished his office or employment, and (b) that at the time he relinquished it, he had no intention of ever resuming it". In my view that test is equally appropriate for determining whether there has been any termination of employment. It is a test that has been referred to on a number of occasions, most notably in Case B19 1970 ATC 88.
  2. Not only must the applicant prove a termination of employment, he must also show that the payment is made "in consequence of" the termination of any employment. It follows that the termination should precede the payment. In Reseck Gibbs J said at 4216 4217 : "Within the ordinary meaning of the words a sum is paid in consequence of the termination of employment when the payment follows as an effect or result of the termination". It is true that His Honour was discussing causation as the relevant nexus between the termination and the payment. Nevertheless, a nexus must be established and a temporal sequence established before that can be considered. If the applicant had not retired, he would not have acquired an entitlement to a benefit. This was discussed in Mcintosh v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 79 ATC 4325 at 4328.
  3. It is clear from the applicant's description of his duties with Company One that he was totally in control of every activity that was carried on within that company. In cross-examination he agreed that Company One was "a one-man show". Whatever interpretation is put upon the evidence, it is clear that the applicant certainly had not retired from the service of (or terminated his employment by) Company One by 17 June 1986. He continued to occupy the same physical office and to carry out the same functions of total supervision and control at least until the August bank holiday when Company One transferred its business to Premises Three. Even after he devolved some of his day to day functions and authority on other managers, he continued to concern himself with what was by then the most important activity of Company One namely, the finalisation of the new premises and the transfer of the business to them. On any view, he could not be said to have retired prior to that transfer.
  4. In my view, however, he has continued to maintain the essential functions which he had as general manager (or managing director) although he has devolved responsibility to a large degree on others for the less important day to day tasks in the running of the company. The business of Company Two was moved to Premises Three in 1988. The business of both companies has continued to be run under the total control and direction of the applicant at all times, and particularly since then. Even the devolution of responsibilities was revoked by him in Company One in January 1990 at the request of the finance company.
  5. In my view, the applicant has not shown that his employment with Company One in the capacity in which he served it has been terminated. It certainly has not been shown that it was terminated prior to the payment of the superannuation benefit on 23 June 1986. In my view, the applicant-has also not demonstrated that even if he had retired, he had no intention of ever resuming his former managerial functions. Accordingly the benefit he received could not have been in consequence of the termination of his employment and could not therefore have been an eligible termination payment.
  6. In case I should be found to be wrong in this assessment, I will deal with the remainder of the arguments advanced by the applicant and the respondent.
  7. In order to exclude the value of a benefit from the applicant's assessable income he must show that the provisions of s 26AFA(1) have been complied with. At the relevant time, this was in the following terms -

"26AFA(1) Where -

(a) in a year of income and on or after 7 December 1983, a taxpayer receives or obtains a benefit of any kind out of, or attributable to assets of, a section 23F fund;

(aa) if the fund is an exempt fund within the meaning of section 26AFB - the benefit was received or obtained by the taxpayer before the proclaimed superannuation standards day;

(b) the benefit -

(i) is not a benefit that the taxpayer has a right to receive from the fund; or

(ii) is an excessive benefit; and

(c) the Commissioner is satisfied that the taxpayer received or obtained the benefit -

(i) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, a member of the fund;

(ii) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, a dependant of a person who was, or had been, a member of the fund;

(iii) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, associated with a person who was, or had been, a member of the fund; or

(iv) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, associated with a person who had made contributions to the fund, being contributions to which Subdivision AA of Division 3 applied,

the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income shall include the amount or value of that benefit.

26AFA(2) [Excessive benefit] Where -

(a) sub-section (1) would, but for this sub-section, apply to the amount or value of an excessive benefit received or obtained by a taxpayer out of, or attributable to assets of, a section 23F fund; and

(b) the Commissioner, having regard to -

(i) the nature of the fund;

(ii) the circumstances by reason of which the benefit is an excessive benefit; and

(iii) such other matters relating to the receiving or obtaining of the benefit by the taxpayer as the Commissioner considers relevant,

is staisfied that it would be unreasonable for sub-section (1) to apply to the whole or part of the benefit,

that sub-section does not apply to the benefit, or to that part of the benefit, as the case may be."

26. The Commissioner contended that the applicant did not have a right to receive the benefit from the fund, or alternatively that the benefit was an excessive benefit. The provisions of s 26AFA(1)(b) therefore applied.

27. "Excessive benefit" is defined in s 26AFA(4) in the following terms -

"26AFA(4) In this section –

.............

"excessive benefit" means a benefit of any kind that is excessive in amount or value having regard to the matters mentioned in subparagraphs 23F(2)(h)(i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) as in force at any time before the commencement of section 1 of the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No 4) 1987:

.............

28. The relevant paragraph of s 23F(2) is (h) which is in the following terms -

"23F

23F (1)

(2) Subject to the succeeding provisions of this section, this section applies, in relation to a year of income, to a superannuation fund, not being a fund of a kind referred to in paragraph 23(jaa), if –

...............

(h) the benefits that any employee has, or the dependants of any employee have, the right to receive from the fund are not excessive in amount having regard to -

(i) the remuneration paid to the employee for services rendered by him to his employer;

(ii) the period of the service rendered by the employee to his employer;

(iii) the benefits, pensions and allowances that have been, are being or may be provided for the employee or his dependants from any other fund to which this section or section 23FA applies in relation to the year of income or has applied in relation to a previous year of income;

(iiia) the benefits that have been, are being or may be provided for the employee or his dependants from roll-over annuities within the meaning of Subdivision AA of Division 2; and

(iv) any other matters that the Commissioner considers relevant; and

29. To some extent the tests to be applied in considering compliance with s 26AFA(1)(b) overlap. The terms of the trust deed are such that if the benefit is excessive, the member has no right to receive it. This is found in Clause 4 of the deed and in Rule 13 -

"Clause4 Notwithstanding any other provisions of this Deed and the Rules the Trustee shall limit the benefits and contributions under the Plan in respect of each Member to such extent as will ensure that -

(a) the benefits being provided under the Plan in respect of the Member; and

(b) those benefits together with the benefits which have been or are being provided in respect of the Member under any other superannuation or similar plan or fund and which are for the purposes of this Clause to be taken into account

are not in the opinion of the Trustee greater than the benefits which are approved from time to time or in any particular case for the purposes of section 23F of the Act.

...............

Rule 13(1) If a Member classified as an executive director ceases prior to his normal retirement date (other than by reason of his death or the circumstances set out in Rule 6 or 10) to be in the service of the Employer the benefit payable to the Member shall be an amount equal to the Member's Credit at the date he ceases to be in the Employer's service.

................

  1. There is a discretion provided in s 26AFA(2) empowering the Commissioner to exclude the whole or part of the benefit as excessive, having regard to the considerations set out in the section. That discretion however does not apply in determining whether or not the taxpayer has a right to receive the benefit from the fund.
  2. I will deal with that question first. Even if the $60,000 paid in 1986 can properly be regarded as a salary, it appears that the extent of the benefit was wrongly calculated. The amount standing to the member's credit, in accordance with Rule 13, was $113,140-37. If payment had been made in accordance with the rules, it should not have exceeded that amount. The fund manager, however, applied a formula involving a multiple of final average salary, but in doing so confused the final salary with the final average salary. If the applicant's true salary was $30,000 for 1984,1985 and 1986, the average would have been $30,000. A multiple of that figure calculated in accordance with Income Taxation Ruling, IT 2201 would have been $101,321-21. If the $60,000 is regarded as salary this would have lifted the average for the 3 years to $40,000. A calculation on a similar basis would have resulted in a figure of $135,904.95. The fund manager however took the final salary of $60,000 instead of an average salary, and arrived at the sum of $202,642 by applying the guideline formula. The formula, however, was also incorrectly applied in another respect. Maximum benefit is payable to an employee only where he has completed 20 years service with the employer. The applicant had completed only 18 years service at the date of his retirement. No attempt was made to pro rata the result.
  3. More importantly, however, both Clause 4 and Rule 13 and the definition of Member's credit put an absolute limit on the amount of any benefit at such amount as is approved for the purposes of s 23F of the Act. If the fund manager had calculated correctly in accordance with the deed, the final result would have had to be reduced or otherwise adjusted to ensure that it did not exceed the approved amount. For this reason the various Income Taxation Rulings dealing with the calculation of benefits have not only a policy effect, but also a legal effect. Relevant rulings are IT 294 at paragraphs 5, 17 and 31, IT 2026 at paragraphs 7 and 8, the formulas at the end of IT 2027, IT 2067 at paragraphs 2,3, and 7, IT 2201 at paragraph 2, 6 and 12, and the policy statement contained in IT 2274. All of these would be imported by reference into the terms of the Deed to set a limit to the benefit, no matter how it was calculated. Their application would have resulted in a sum much lower than that paid to the applicant.
  4. To counter this argument, the applicant submitted that the benefit was in fact not excessive because of the value transferred to the fund when the applicant left the family company to join Company One (the evidence about this value was practically nil) director's fees of $2,000 per annum from Company One, and fees from associated companies, of varying amounts, over 3 years. The applicant also contended that a sum of $8,400 per annum should also be added as the value to him of the employer's contributions to the Fund. Taken with the additional sum of $30,000 the applicant contended that his total remuneration would base a benefit calculation well within the excessive benefit guidelines.
  5. For this argument the applicant relied upon the definitions of member's credit, review date and salary in the Rules -

................

"Member's Credit" at any date in relation to a Member means the amount to which the transfer value (if any) in respect of the Member together with the contributions in respect of the Member have accumulated after making such allowance as the Trustee shall decide from time to time (after considering the advice of the A.M.P.) for interest to that date and for the Member's share of the charge for expenses made in accordance with the Policy and for the cost of providing the amount by which the benefit payable on the Member's death or disablement exceeds the Member's Credit PROVIDED THAT for the purpose of determining a Member's Credit the contributions in respect of the Member shall be such amount as is required in order to fund a benefit at the Member's normal retirement date equal to seven times the Member's final average salary or such lesser mount as is approved from time to time for each Member for the purposes of Section 23F of the Act.

"normal retirement date" means the Member's sixty-fifth birthday in the case of a male or the sixtieth birthday in the case of a female.

.................

"salary" means either

(a) the remuneration (whether described as salary or wages or otherwise) at which the Member is employed by the Employer and includes such fees as (in the case of an executive director) are payable to the Member in respect of his directorship but does not include any overtime or special grant or allowance for residence travelling or otherwise; or

(b) in any special case such amount as is for the purposes of the Plan agreed upon between the Member and the Employer.

........"

35. It was submitted that the definition of salary, in particular, was wide enough to encompass these other items. However, on no reading of the definition, in my view, can one include director's fees from other companies than the employing company. Furthermore as the review date as a mechanism for calculating average salary did not occur until September, a considerable time after the alleged retirement, the payment of the benefit and the calculation of the final average salary must be based on the figures at the preceding September. The overriding terms of the deed however must govern the rules. The basis of the Commissioner's approval is imported into the contractual arrangement as a legal condition. The Income Taxation Rulings clearly exclude from calculation one off benefits, like the $30,000 and also other associated benefits to which the applicant's counsel referred. Whether or not they are envisaged by the Deed, these supplementary emoluments can not be taken into account if they will result in a benefit larger than that permitted by the Rulings. The end result is that if the benefit does not fall within the excessive benefit guidelines the applicant is not entitled to receive it. He did receive it, and accordingly the requirements of s 26AFA(1) have not been met. Because he received an amount to which he was not entitled, the discretion contained in sub-section (2) may not be exercised.

  1. An incorrect return penalty of $91,830-76 was imposed by the respondent. This was calculated on an interest component of 14.026 per cent for the period from the due date of the original assessment to the date of completion of the audit and a culpability component of 50 per cent. I see no reason why this imposition of additional taxation should be disturbed. The wrongful payment was not the result of carelessness. It arose as a result of negotiations with the fund manager to maximise the applicant's benefit (which was immediately loaned back to Company One). The calculation was made with no reference to the respondent before hand. It was made to arrive at what the manager mistakenly perceived to be the maximum allowable withdrawal benefit on a basis that could not subsequently be justified.
  2. The objection decision under review is therefore affirmed.
