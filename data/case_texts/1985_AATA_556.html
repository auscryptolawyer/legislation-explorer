Farrelley and Commissioner for Superannuation [1985] AATA 556 (23 December 1985)

Last Updated: 19 February 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

SUPERANNUATION - Benefit Classification Certificate - certificate issued specifying "history low back pain" - whether applicant not likely to continue to be eligible employee until maximum retiring age - Linden's case and Bewley's case considered as to appropriate test.

Superannuation Act 1976 S.16(4)

Bewley and the Commissioner unreported as yet A84/186

Linden and the Commissioner for Superannuation 2 ALN N659

CHRISTOPHER J. FARRELLEY

Applicant

COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

No. A85/53

AAT Decision No 2489

Mr J.O. Ballard (Senior Member) Mr D.R.S. Craik (Member)

Dr D. Travers (Member)

23rd December 1985 Canberra

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No: A85/53 GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: CHRISTOPHER J. FARRELLEY

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

Tribunal : Mr J.O. Ballard (Senior Member)

Mr. D.R.S. Craik (Member)

Dr D. Travers (Member)

Date : 23rd December 1985

Place : Canberra

Decision : 1. The decision under review is set aside.

2. In substitution the applicant should not be issued with a benefit classification certificate in relation to any low back problem.

J.O. BALLARD

Presiding Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No: A85/53 GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: CHRISTOPHER J. FARRELLEY

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

This is an application for review of a benefit classification certificate issued by a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the Commissioner") under sub-section 16(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act").

  1. The applicant in this matter, Christopher John Farrelley, is now a Computer Programmer with the Health Insurance Commission. He was permanently appointed to the Public Service on the 4th January 1982 in the Department of Veteran Affairs. He then sought to become an eligible employee for the purposes of the Act. For the purposes of that application the applicant was medically examined. As a result of those medical examinations a delegate issued a benefit classification certificate under sub-section 16(4) of the Act. That sub-section reads:-

"The Commissioner shall consider the report or reports, and such other matters (if any) as the Commissioner considers relevant, and, if he is of the opinion that the person is not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with a physical or mental condition or conditions referred to in the report or reports, to continue to be an eligible employee until the person attains his maximum retiring age, the Commissioner shall issue a benefit classification certificate to that effect, being a certificate in which the relevant condition or conditions is or are specified."

The benefit classification listed "injured right knee" and "history low back pain". The applicant sought reconsideration of that decision. As a result of that reconsideration the benefit classification certificate was amended by deleting the "injured right knee".

  1. The certificate was confirmed in relation to "history low back pain". It should be recorded that at the hearing of the matter the Commissioner's representative conceded that this description was not a correct statement of a condition for the purposes of a certificate. He asked that if the decision be otherwise affirmed it be varied to describe the condition appropriately. In view of our findings this does not become an issue.
  2. The applicant sought review by this Tribunal giving the following reasons:-

    1. Injuries of a temporary nature - not recurring.
    2. No thorough examination before issuing certificate.
  3. Conflicting medical opinion.
  4. No evidence found by specialist - only guesswork."

  5. The applicant provided a report from Dr Coyle, an orthopaedic surgeon. He said that he was unable to call Dr Coyle because he could not afford this. The representative of the respondent wished to question Dr Coyle. On the request of the parties Dr Coyle was summoned as a witness called by the Tribunal; his fees are payable by the Commonwealth pursuant to section 67 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act"). Evidence was also given by Mr McDowall, the applicant's treating chiropractor. Called by the respondent was Dr Robson, a neurosurgeon, and Dr Bromhead, a general practitioner who is a Commonwealth Medical Officer. A report was also available from the applicant's general practitioner Dr Caine.
  6. In evidence the applicant said that he told the medical officer that he was suffering from lumbago, it was true that the chiropractor had been treating him for backache, but it was not low backache; he believed this pain was of a temporary nature and it had since gone. The applicant last had back pain when he had been injured at work in May 1985. This occurred when he was lifting electronic equipment; he had received compensation for this incident. The back had healed up and it was normal again, he did not think that this constituted a history of low back pain. He had seen Dr Caine because of pain in the kidney area and he went to see him to ensure that there was nothing wrong with the kidneys. Dr Caine reported that he had treated the applicant for a back complaint in 1976; the pain was usually sited over the right loin and an x-ray on the 30th November 1976 showed no abnormality; in his opinion there was nothing seriously wrong with the applicant in the spine or in the knee; the backache Dr Caine thought to be muscular pain, probably a chill or strain, the pain responded well to simple analgesics.

7. Mr McDowall, the chiropractor, had first seen the applicant in May 1981. He had seen him approximately 60 times. The applicant had seen Mr McDowall with pain between the shoulder blades but it resolved itself in three weeks. The applicant presented with neck discomfort, aches and pains in the joints some in the mid low back. X-rays showed narrowing of the spine at L5 S1 in May 1981. It was possible that low back pain related to the narrowing but X-ray evidence did not necessarily mean that patients had pain. The only visits on which the applicant complained of low back pain were 29th October 1984 and 1st May 1985. Mr McDowall had a note in 1985 that there was a work related injury and he had treated the applicant each day during that week. On about 45 visits the applicant had no complaints. Seventy percent of Mr McDowall's practice is preventative. With the present rate of chiropractor care Mr McDowall did not think Mr Farrelly would pack up in his working life. The 1981 X-rays shows disc narrowing; these were misalignments in the neck and thorassic spine as well as the disc narrowing in the L5S1. The applicant would get by without care in the absence of trauma. The applicant was examined by another person at the practice on the 4th December 1981 and Mr McDowall had no details of that.

  1. In his report of an examination of the 10th November 1985 Dr Coyle said that he thought the risk of low back pain causing early retirement was exceedingly unlikely and probably no greater than the risk for any clerical or sedentary worker. In evidence he agreed that he had not been aware of the 1984 and 1985 consultations with Mr McDowall. It was important to know how much back pain the applicant had had. Dr Coyle saw many people with back pain but it was not his experience that it was a common cause of early retirement on its own. He did not think there was any risk of the applicant not completing his job. If the applicant had come to him complaining he would discuss weight control but not limit his leisure activities, Dr Coyle could not say the damaged disc was the cause of pain but he would think it predisposed him.
  2. Dr Robson had seen the applicant in May 1984. He found no abnormality in the applicant's back or legs which would come from the back. He arranged X-rays and these showed narrowing of the lumbo-sacral disc space and slight posterior displacement of L5 S1. He thought that the passage of time, without any other factor, would add to wear and tear and he thought that this X-ray would look worse in time. Whether this would lead to early retirement was not possible for him to answer. If he had to make the sort of guess which this situation calls for he would say that the applicant in the future would possibly have more trouble.
  3. In evidence Dr Robson said that if the 1976 X-rays were normal by comparison with the 1984 findings he must think there had been a change over the years. It was possible that the changes would continue but the applicant did not complain when he saw him. Dr Robson could not say whether it would interfere with the applicant's performance as a computer programmer; he did not think there was a real risk. The applicant would probably have no problems until he retired; he would probably get through without much more trouble as the community has lots of people with back pain and relatively few who are forced out of work because of it. There was a possibility that the applicant's back would get worse quite suddenly but to say more probably than not Dr Robson would need a history of recurrence of severe back ache. If treating the applicant Dr Robson would not ask him to limit his life style.
  4. Dr Bromhead did not conduct a medical examination himself but considered the papers including another Commonwealth Medical Officer's report. He found that the applicant had lumbo-sacral back pain but formed no opinion on the degree of the severity or from the reports. He could not say whether it was likely to recur or regress. The "work" injury was recent and a prognosis could not have been given at that time. Dr Bromhead had concluded that there was a significant chance of the condition preventing the applicant continuing to be an eligible employee; it was of the order of 25 to 30%.
  5. Mr Anforth for the respondent sought leave to put further questions to Dr Robson and Dr Coyle after they had left. This was not objected to by the applicant. The Tribunal indicated that it would be acceptable for the parties to put these questions to the doctors and give the answers from the bar table. However the parties requested that questions framed by them be put to the doctors by the member's clerk. This was done.
  6. The matter was then reconvened under sub-section 40(5) of the AAT Act for the presiding member to receive the questions and answers in the presence of parties. The effect of these answers is that both doctors thought that there was a 5% chance of premature retirement due to the applicant's back condition. Neither doctor thought that that chance was of the order of 10% or 25%. Dr Robson however was reluctant to put a percentage figure on the likelihood of the applicant retiring early as he felt it was unrealistic to give an accurate estimate and that it was not possible to assess something which did not have a percentage basis.
  7. It should be recorded that before the hearing the respondent had applied that the matter stand over generally until an appeal had been heard in the matter of Bewley and the Commissioner A84/186 unreported as yet ("Bewley's case"); this application had been rejected by the Tribunal. At the conclusion of the hearing it was submitted for the respondent that if the Tribunal applied the report of a Dr Grahame set out in the decision Bewley's case and the reasoning in Linden and the Commissioner for Superannuation 2 ALN N659 ("Linden's case") then a 5% chance was enough to justify the benefit classification certificate. In Linden's case the Tribunal interpreted Parliament's intention in using the word "likely" to mean reference to "conditions which give rise to a 'real risk' and not merely to conditions which create a 'probability' or 'better than even chance' that the person will not continue until attaining maximum retiring age". In Bewley's case the Tribunal had gone beyond that and had adopted a more probable test; it was said that that had caused the appeal as the Commissioner did not consider that was the proper interpretation of sub-section 16(4) of the Act. It was argued that the applicant had a deemed physical condition and this was likely to recur; this could be present without being symptomatic. If the test in Linden's case was applied in this matter the Commissioner should succeed.
  8. We accept the considered opinion of the two specialists that the degree of risk arising from the applicant's back condition is of the order of 5% that he will not continue to be an eligible employee until he attains his retiring age. Not only is this the view of two specialists but it is also their view after physical examination. We do not accept the view of a Commonwealth Medical Officer who has not examined the applicant in preference to two specialists who have.
  9. We accept that there is some difference of opinion of the interpretation of the words "that the person is unlikely" where it appears in sub-section 16(4) of the Act as between the decision in Linden's case and Bewley's case. This will no doubt be clarified when Bewley's case is considered in the Federal Court.
  10. We do not have to discuss the issues that will then arise. We reject the argument that if Linden's case applies then a 5% risk is sufficient to warrant the issue of a benefit classification certificate. We do not attach a percentage figure to the words "real risk". We are clearly of the view that a 5% risk is only minimal and not a real risk. We accept the applicant's evidence as to his type of work. We construe the evidence of the specialists as indicating that there is no real risk of the applicant being prevented from continuing to be an eligible employee until he reaches his retirement age by a low back problem. It might be otherwise if the applicant's work required constant heavy physical labour; if that were the case we would expect that to be reflected in the evidence of the medical specialists.
  11. We add that we deprecate the attempts to require the specialists to set percentages on medical risks. We agree with Dr Robson that this is quite unrealistic. We place reliance on the general evidence/both specialists that there was, in fact, no real risk of the applicant retiring early due to back problems. We so find.
  12. In these circumstances the decision to issue the benefit classification certificate must be set aside.
