Re Taxation Appeals [1991] AATA 95; 22 ATR 3201 (23 April 1991)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. VT88/521

AAT No. 6861

Income Tax

[1991] AATA 95; 22 ATR 3201

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

P.J.B. Burns(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - whether income returned by a family discretionary trust is assessable income of the trust, the taxpayer who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned or the net income of a partnership of the taxpayer and his wife - whether sec. 260 or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 is applicable - rate of additional tax imposed - deductability of expenditure items.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 17, 19, 25, 51(1), 223, 227, 260, Part IVA

Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4851

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland; Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation; Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4765

Jolley v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4197

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting 89 ATC 4358

Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4830

Case W 58 89 ATC 524

Income Tax Laws Amendment Bill (No. 2) 1981 - Second Reading Speech

Income Tax Laws Amendment Bill (No. 2) 1981 - Explanatory Memorandum IT 2121

HEARING

MELBOURNE

23:4:1991

ORDER

The Tribunal:

(i) sets aside the decisions under review and remits the matter to the Commissioner to allow deductions from the Applicant's assessable incomes as agreed between the parties and set out in paragraph 19 of the statement of the Tribunal's reasons for this decision.

(ii) the culpability component in the additional tax imposed is to be calculated at the flat rate of 25%.

DECISION

The primary question at issue in these applications is as to whether income returned by a family discretionary trust assesed by the Commissioner of Taxation (the Commissioner) to the Applicant for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986 inclusive is assessable income of the trust, the Applicant who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned or the net income of a partnership of the Applicant and his wife.

2. Other matters in dispute which arise from the assessment of the income to the Applicant are-

1. The deductibility of certain items of expenditure

incurred during the relevant years and,

2. The imposition of additional tax for incorrect returns.

3. At the hearing the Applicant was represented by Mr T. Murphy of Counsel and the Commissioner by Mr G. Davies of Counsel. Three witnesses were called, namely the Applicant, his wife and their tax adviser.

4. On issuing amended assessments to the Applicant for each of the abovementioned years of income the Commissioner advised him that sec 260 and/or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Act) applies to include the gross income disclosed in the returns of income of the trust in his assessable income. Further in the event that Part IVA of the Act applies the amounts are includible in his assessable income by virtue of the operation of secs 17, 19 and 25 of the Act.

5. The Applicant lodged objections against the amended assessments and on their disallowance by the Commissioner requested reference of the decisions to the Tribunal.

6. The Applicant is a mechanical engineer and until 1980 was employed by Ansell International in that capacity. In that year he resigned from his employment to pursue a career in private practice. After considering various options as to the way in which the business should be structured he decided to form a partnership with his wife. Profits and losses were to be shared equally. Returns of income for the partnership were lodged with the Australian Taxation Office (the ATO) and returns of income lodged by the Applicant and his wife disclosing their respective shares of the partnership income were accepted and assessments issued to them.

7. The business was conducted from the Applicant's home in a room set aside for the purpose. The room was equipped with the essentials required to perform engineering design work, such as drafting equipment, drawing files, calculators and storage facilities. This equipment was owned by the partnership. The Applicant performed the contract engineering services for a number of clients including a three year contract with Wilson Electric Transformers. His wife was involved in book keeping and the collection and delivery of parts. She also assisted in the tracing of designs, copying and some drafting work.

8. The business was conducted by the partnership until 31 December 1981. On that date it was sold to a company (C Pty Ltd) as trustee for the LJM Family Trust. The sale price of the business was $12,035 of which $10,000 was good will. C Pty Ltd was incorporated on 15 December 1981. Its paid up capital comprises 2 shares each of $1, one of which is held by the Applicant and the other by his wife. The trust deed was settled on 29 December 1981.

9. Copies of the trust deed and the certificate of incorporation of the company were tendered in evidence together with books of account, miscellaneous invoices and company minute books.

10. The trust deed was prepared by a firm of solicitors and gives the Applicant wide powers. He was appointed both the guardian and appointor and as a result of being appointor had the power to remove and appoint any trustee. Beneficiaries were the Applicant, his wife and their children. The trustee has the discretion to distribute the income as it sees fit and also has the power to remove a person from the class of beneficiaries, to declare trusts in substitution for the trusts set out in the document and to amend, revoke or add to the provisions of the deed. However the trustee was not permitted to exercise any of those powers without giving the guardian or appointor seven clear days notice of its intention to exercise the powers.

11. In his evidence the Applicant said that the trust arrangements were entered into following discussions between himself, his wife and their accountant. He said that the trust would provide a more professional approach as in talking to clients a company was more substantial and a more recognizable entity than a partnership. He also thought it would provide a broader base of income support for their children and in particular for their son Wayne who is an invalid suffering from severe brain damage. Another benefit available through the trust arrangement, he said, was the provision of superannuation and also as an avenue for the future admission of their children into the business. He said that the tax advantages of the arrangement were discussed but the main reasons for entering into it were to provide facilities for Wayne and the flexibility of the trust itself. There was also he said the added advantage of limited liability.

12. Under cross-examination he agreed that clients would not have been aware of the trust arrangements and those arrangements would have had no commercial significance. Clients would have been dealing only with an incorporated body.

13. The question of the significance of the trust and the provision of security for Wayne was also raised by Counsel for the Commissioner. Examination of the company/trust records disclosed that distributions made to Wayne each year were transferred to the Applicant and used for Wayne's maintenance and at the end of the period there was no money standing to Wayne's credit as a result of the trust arrangements. The money for his maintenance still came from the Applicant's work as a contractor but instead of coming directly from his pocket was channelled through the books of account of the company. Distributions made to the other children equalled the threshold amount above which tax would have become payable but this provision did not operate in Wayne's case because he is an invalid.

14. Contracts entered into by the company for mechanical engineering work provided that the services were to be performed by the Applicant and generally rates were fixed solely with reference to the hours he worked and the type of work performed. Occasionally work was performed for an agreed payment. Also during the year of income ended 30 June 1986 several subcontractors were employed. Their rates of payment were fixed allowing a small margin of profit to the trust company. Their employment during that period was minimal as only $1,850 in fees was paid to them.

15. Wages payable to the Applicant were fixed at the end of each year after discussions between the Applicant, his wife and their accountant and allowed for a profit margin to the trust. There was no directors' resolution passed fixing the Applicant's salary. All domestic and other private expenditures of the Applicant and his wife incurred each year were debited to a loan account and at the end of the year this account was credited with wages payable to the Applicant. Distributions made to the child beneficiaries were also credited to the loan account in recognition of the maintenance expenses paid for them during the year.

16. The Applicant's wife said in her evidence that she was present at meetings when her husband's wages were discussed. She said that she believed they were fixed at a market rate but she would always accept the accountant's advice on this aspect. She was also present at meetings when the distributions to be made to beneficiaries were discussed and she said that she would accept the accountant's advice as to the appropriate distributions to be made.

17. The accountant in his evidence said that he advised the Applicant and his wife on the different structures available to them on going into business. The options which he mentioned were that the Applicant could conduct the business as an individual on his own account, form a partnership with his wife or use a family company/discretionary trust arrangement. As the Applicant and his wife wanted to go into business and because there was some uncertainty as to the future he advised them that a partnership would be appropriate because costs were less than a corporate structure and much easier to dismantle. As a result the partnership was formed and it continued for about 2 1/2 years. Some time prior to December 1981 he had further discussions with the Applicant and his wife about future business opportunities and family problems associated with their invalid son Wayne. They felt that there were opportunities for expansion of the business and there was a desire to employ subcontractors to work on projects and also to provide for superannuation for the Applicant as protection for the family in the event of his death. This latter aspect was said to be of particular importance because of the invalidity of their son Wayne. As a result he advised them to enter into the company/trust arrangments as he considered that this was an easier way to provide income for Wayne than using a company structure.

18. The accountant was questioned closely by Counsel for the Commissioner with regard to the formation of the partnership and as to whether there was a written partnership agreement. He believed there was an agreement prepared but he was unable to find a copy on his files.

19. At the hearing it was agreed between the parties that in the event that sec 260 or part IVA applied the following amounts should be allowed as deductions.

Year ended 30 June 1984, 1985, 1986

$4,177 $6,216 $9,177

20. Mr Murphy's submissions on behalf of the Applicant were based upon the following claims-

. The creation of the trust had not resulted in a

reduction of the Applicant's assessable income on the

basis of figures submitted by the Applicant's accountant.

. If the arrangements were set up to obtain a tax

benefit it is necessary to identify and quantify the

benefit and if the arrangement provided increased

superannuation this was not a tax benefit.

. He referred the Tribunal to the explanatory

memorandum circulated by the Treasurer on the

introduction of Part IVA where it stated that the

function of the section is to provide "an effective

general measure against those tax avoidance

arrangements that - inexact as the words be in legal

terms - are blatant, artificial and contrived". He

submitted that the arrangement was not a blatant or

paper scheme and the change in the tax position in

the Applicant was commensurate with the change in the

fiscal position.

. The choice by a taxpayer of a structure which

provides the lesser tax liability will not necessarily

attract Part IVA and it is clear he may carry on a

business as a trust as a matter of law and practice.

. The trust provided flexibility not available under

other structures eg. a company, because it gives an

ability to make capital distributions to

beneficiaries and it was ideal for the provision of

benefits to the Applicant's invalid son.

21. It was contended by Mr Davies for the Commissioner that the taxpayer gained a tax benefit under para (a) sec 177C(1) of the Act because a benefit is gained by a taxpayer if by reason of a scheme an amount is not included in his assessable income which should have been or might reasonably have been expected to have been included. The benefit, Mr Davies said, arose from the splitting of income where the source of the income was the Applicant and that it was the activities of the Applicant as an engineer which gave rise to the derivation of the income. This was achieved, Mr Davies asserted, firstly by the partnership between the Applicant and his wife and secondly by the use of a trust. He then referred the Tribunal to the decisions in Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4851, Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland, Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4765, Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting 89 ATC 4358 and Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4830 in support of his case.

22. He said that the vice is not that the Applicant changed from a partnership to a trust but it is that the person who was earning the income split that income by some other mechanism. He claimed that to attempt to analyse what the position would have been under the partnership gives rise only to speculation but it does highlight that the Applicant utilised firstly a partnership and later a trust to split his income. He referred the Tribunal to the decision of the late Hartigan J., then President of this Tribunal, in case W58 89 ATC 524 where it was decided that Part IVA applied to the setting up of a trust in circumstances similar to those in the instant case where it was clear it was not just an artificial contrived scheme related to paper benefits.

23. In respect of Mr Murphy's claims that the choice by a tax payer of a structure which provided a lesser tax liability does not necessarily attract Part IVA and he may carry on a business as a trust as a matter of law and practice, Mr Davies referred the Tribunal to case W58 (supra) where the late Hartigan J. said at p 537 -

"The mere fact that the Act recognises that there are such

things as trusts, partnerships and so forth and then

provides how those trusts and partnerships should be taxed

does not mean that the mechanism is one expressly provided

for by the Act for the purposes of sec 177C(2)(a)(i)."

24. The claim that the trust arrangement provides additional security for Wayne is not supported by the facts. There were no monies standing to his credit in the books of the trust at the end of the period under review and as pointed out by Mr Davies the money for Wayne's maintenance still came from the Applicant, but instead of being directly from his pocket was channelled through the books of account of the company.

25. Mr Davies also submitted that although the partnership had been accepted by the Commissioner there had not been a partnerhip because there was lack of evidence as to a partnership agreement and there had been no discussion between the Applicant and his wife as to their respective entitlements to the capital of the partnership. Further, he said, income receipts were paid into a joint bank account used for private as well as business purposes and decisions in respect of contracts entered into by the partnership were made solely by the Applicant.

26. Mr Murphy cited the decision in Jolley v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4197 in support of his claim there was a partnership between the Applicant and his wife. He also submitted that if the vice is not the change from the partnership to the trust, but the diversion of income firstly to the partnership and then to the trust as submitted by Mr Davies, as the partnership was formed prior to 27 May 1981 Part IVA cannot apply to the arrangements.

27. The validity of the partnership was not at issue until it was raised by Mr Davies at the hearing. Although the evidence is not conclusive I accept that a partnership subsisted between the Applicant and his wife during the relevant years.

28. The evidence does not support the proposition that the formation of the partnership was an interim step in and part of the arrangement which later led to the company/trust structure. The partnership had been operating for some 2 1/2 years prior to the implementation of the trust arrangements.

29. The company/trust arrangement which was entered into after 27 May 1981 enabled the Applicant to divert his personal income to other family members. Irrespective of what the Applicant's tax position might have been if the business had continued to be conducted as a partnership, the trust by facilitating the diversion of his personal income provided him with a tax benefit in the terms of para (a) of sec 177C(1) of the Act.

30. For the abovementioned reasons it is the finding of the Tribunal that Part IVA applies to the trust arrangements and the income is properly assessable to the Applicant. Taxable incomes should be recalculated allowing deductions from assessable income of $4,177, $6,216, and $9,177 in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1984, 1985 and 1986 respectively as agreed between the parties.

31. Finally there is the matter of the additional tax imposed under sec 223(1) of the Act. The Applicant entered into the arrangements which were apparently in general use in the profession in which he operated on the advice of his accountant and tax adviser. In the circumstances the culpability component included in the additional tax imposed should be reduced to a rate of 25% flat for each of the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986 inclusive.
