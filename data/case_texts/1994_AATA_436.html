QT93/105 and Commissioner of Taxation [1994] AATA 436; (1994) 27 ATR 1242; 94 ATC 179 (11 February 1994)

Last Updated: 14 May 2009

Sales Tax : In situ pool tax payment - refund declaration by pool purchaser - declaration not allowed where no payment received by Commissioner



Swimming Pools Tax Refund Act 1992 : Ss.3, 4(3), 4(4), 4(5), 4(7)



AAT Decision No 9301



DECISION AND REASONS



Re:

APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT



No : QT93/105

Date : 11 February 1994

Place : Brisbane

Tribunal : Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)





DECISION



The Tribunal decides:

(a) that it has jurisdiction to review the respondent's decision; and

(b) that the decision under review be affirmed.



(Sgd) K L Beddoe

(SENIOR MEMBER)



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) NO. QT93/105

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:

APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT



REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 11 February 1994

Place: Brisbane



The applicant seeks review of a decision by the respondent dated 6 May 1993 to disallow a declaration made by the applicant under sub-section 4(5) of the Swimming Pools Tax Refund Act 1992 ("the Act").



2. In making the declaration the applicant was seeking refund under the Act of in situ pool tax amounting to $906 purportedly paid by the applicant to the pool builder as part of the contract price for construction of a pool by the pool builder.



3. The Act has the effect of allowing the refund of in situ pool tax payments paid to the respondent in purported compliance with the requirements of the sales tax law. Section 4 provides the exclusive factual situations in which the Commonwealth is liable to make such refunds.



4. Sub-section 4(3) allows for a joint declaration by the pool builder and the pool purchaser that a specified amount of an in situ pool tax, passed on to the pool purchaser, has not been refunded to the pool purchaser.



5. Sub-section 4(5) of the Act provides for the making of the relevant declaration by the pool purchaser alone where a declaration cannot reasonably be made under sub-section 4(3).



6. Sub-section 4(4) then provides that:



"If a declaration is made under sub-section (3); the Commonwealth is liable to make so much of the in situ pool tax refund payment as equals the amount of the tax that was passed on to the pool purchaser and not refunded, but is liable to make the payment to the pool purchaser instead of the pool builder."



7. Section 3 provides that:



"'in situ pool tax payment' means an amount paid to the Commissioner in purported compliance with the requirements of the law relating to sales tax, or under agreement, to pay in situ pool tax."



8. At the hearing the applicant represented himself and Mr Hack of counsel appeared for the respondent.



9. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. The following documentary evidence was tendered:



. prescribed payment documents in relation to the construction of the swimming pool by the pool builder (Exhibit A);



. contract between the applicant and the pool builder for the construction of the swimming pool (Exhibit B);



. bundle of receipts and letters to applicant from pool builder (Exhibit C);



. preliminary profit and loss report for the pool builder 1990/91 (Exhibit 1);



. Australian Tax Office Sales Tax Returns for the pool builder June 1990 to May 1991 (Exhibit 2);



. Sales Tax Summaries for July 1990 to September 1991 (Exhibit 3);



. Tax Returned/Paid Reconciliation and Summary Sheet (Exhibit 4).



10. The Tribunal also had the benefit of oral evidence from the applicant; a former director of Holdings Pty Ltd trading as the pool builder, a representative of the liquidator of Holdings Pty Ltd; an officer of the Australian Taxation Office; and a former director of Holdings Pty Ltd who was not cross-examined.



11. The applicant into entered a contract with the pool builder on 1 August 1990 for the construction of a pool for a consideration of $12,000. This contract price was subsequently reduced to $11,750 as evidenced by Exhibit C. The pool was built during the period 1 September 1990 to 5 October 1990.



12. On 15 December 1992 the applicant lodged a declaration under sub-section 4(5) of the Act seeking a refund of sales tax in respect of the swimming pool which was constructed in situ.



13. Oral evidence was given, and the Tribunal so finds, that the pool builder is now in liquidation.



14. The contract between the applicant and the pool builder makes no mention of a sales tax component in the price. It appears that the applicant was unaware of any part of the contract price being referable to sales tax payable by the respondent.



15. The company documents and the sales tax returns tendered as evidence include a $906 sales tax component for the applicant's in situ pool construction. The Tribunal is satisfied and finds that part of the ultimate contract price paid by the applicant must have included a sales tax component.



16. The Tribunal is satisfied and finds that no in situ pool tax payment was made to the respondent by the pool builder in respect of the applicant's pool constructed under the contract dated 1 August 1990. The Sales Tax Summary, the Tax Returned/Paid Reconciliations and the Preliminary Profit and Loss Reports of D Pools all evidence that no sales tax at all was paid by the pool builder after 1989.



17. In the hearing before the Tribunal the respondent submitted that there was no reviewable decision in that no in situ pool tax payment within section 3 of the Act was ever made to the Commissioner. However, sub-section 4(7) confers on the Tribunal jurisdiction to review a decision of the Commissioner to allow, or not allow, the pool purchaser to make a declaration.



18. The Tribunal is satisfied that in refusing to refund the sales tax, the respondent was actually refusing to accept the applicant's declaration under sub-section 4(5) of the Act. If the declaration had been allowed by the respondent the Commonwealth would be liable to refund the payment by virtue of sub-section 4(8) and 4(4) of the Act without more.



19. As no payment was ever received by the respondent in respect of the applicant's in situ pool construction, a relevant declaration could not properly be made by the applicant under sub-section 4(5) of the Act. It is unfortunate that part of the contract price did include this sales tax component, however, the wording of the Act is clear. There has not been an "in situ pool payment" as defined (s.3); it follows that there cannot be an "in situ pool tax refund payment" as defined (s.3) under section 4 and the respondent was not therefore authorised under the Act to allow the applicant to make a declaration.



20. For these reasons the decision under review will be affirmed.



I hereby certify that this and the preceding four (4) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT93/105 and COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION.



Signed: (L HOPE) (Associate)



Date: 11 February 1994
