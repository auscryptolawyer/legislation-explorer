Negative Instruments Pty Ltd (Formerly Xl Petroleum Pty Ltd) v the Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1994] FCA 1438; (1994) 94 Atc 4809 (1994) 29 Atr 423 (31 October 1994)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEGATIVE INSTRUMENTS PTY LTD (formerly XL Petroleum Pty Ltd) v. THE

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

No. VG157 of 1993

FED No. 807/94

Number of pages - 6

Income Tax

(1994) 94 ATC 4809

(1994) 29 ATR 423

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

HEEREY J

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax- deductibility for petroleum exploration - related company holder of authority to prospect - related company party to joint venture - monies outlaid by applicant - whether monies belonging to or expended by joint venture

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) ss.124 AH(1), 124 AJ(3)

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 17 October 1994

31:10:1994

Counsel for the applicant: C M Caleo

Solicitor for the applicant: Mallesons Stephen Jaques

Counsel for the respondent: T Murphy

Solicitor for the respondent: Australian Government

Solicitor

ORDER

1. Appeal allowed.

2. Order the decision of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal given on 2
April 1993 insofar as it affirms the decision of the respondent to disallow the applicant's objection dated 12 February 1990 under s.185 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Act) against the assessment of income tax dated 22 October 1986 in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1981 be set aside and in lieu thereof the applicant's said objection be allowed.

3. Order that the matter be remitted to the respondent to amend the applicant's assessment of income tax for the said year of income in accordance with law.

4. Order that the respondent pay the applicant's costs of the appeal, including reserved costs.

NOTE: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules

DECISION

HEEREY J This appeal from the Taxation Appeals Division of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal constituted by Dr P Gerber, Deputy President, raises the question of deductibility under s.124AH(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) (the Act) of monies outlaid in relation to petroleum exploration.

2. The applicant (formerly called XL Petroleum Pty Ltd and to which I shall refer as XL Petroleum) failed before the Tribunal on the ground that the payments in question were not "incurred" within the meaning of s.124AH(1) of the Act, which provides:

"Subject to this section, expenditure incurred by the

taxpayer during the year of income on exploration or

prospecting in Australia for the purpose of discovering

petroleum is an allowable deduction."

3. All the shares in XL Petroleum were owned by Mr Ian Sykes. XL Petroleum and Mr Sykes owned all the shares in another company XLX NL (XLX). XLX was the registered proprietor of an authority to prospect ATP 270P under the relevant Queensland petroleum legislation.

4. On 14 May 1980 XL Petroleum and XLX entered into an agreement in writing in the following terms:

"XLX NL hereby agrees that XL Petroleum Pty Ltd will earn in

XLX's ATP 270 a 50% working interest in graticular blocks

numbers 1361 and 1289 on the Queensland Government Map

series B rear and east of Windorah Queensland in return for

drilling one hole to about 8,000 metres or less at about

shot point 230 on line AB of XLX during 1980-81 at XLX's

sole cost, risk and expense."

5. The two blocks mentioned were part of the land covered by ATP 270P.

6. On 27 June 1980 XL Petroleum entered into a drilling contract with O D and E Pty Limited (OD and E) under which OD and E agreed to drill a well, the location of which was described as "`XL Flank No 1' east of Windorah within ATP 270, Queensland" commencing on or about 14 December 1980 to a contract depth of 8,000 feet. Payment was to be on a day work basis together with payment for positioning contractor's rig, trucks, camp, crew and rigging up. OD and E acknowledged that XL Petroleum had prepaid the mobilisation and the rig down fees of $102,945 and $22,370 respectively with interest until the money was due to be paid by XL Petroleum for the first well.

7. In July 1980 XLX and Abrolhos Oil and Investments Limited (Abrolhos) entered into an agreement. This agreement was apparently executed under seal on 6 November 1990. The agreement recited that XLX was the registered owner of ATP 270P, that Abrolhos had agreed to pay moneys to XLX "in connection with the exploration of the tenement", that XL proposed that "a well to be named `XL Flank East of Windorah Number One Well'" should be drilled "by XL Petroleum Pty Ltd for and on behalf of XLX" that XL Petroleum had entered into a contract for the drilling of the well with OD and E and that it had been agreed between XLX and Abrolhos "that XLX or its nominee shall be the Operator for the purposes of drilling the well". The agreement then provided

"1. Abrolhos hereby agrees to pay to XLX a sum equal to

all costs incurred by XLX or by XL Petroleum in

drilling the Well in accordance with the drilling

contract ..."

8. Clause 2 provided that "XLX is hereby appointed the Operator of the joint venture constituted by this agreement". Under cl.3, not more than 30 days prior to 14 October 1980 XLX was to submit to Abrolhos a budget of expenditure to be incurred in connection with the drilling of the well and Abrolhos should within 14 days of the receipt of the budget or by 14 October 1980 or such other date as should be agreed, whichever should be the later,

"pay to XLX the sum shown on such budget as being the

estimated cost of drilling the Well for and upon payment of

which sum ... Abrolhos shall subject to the consent of the

Minister of Mines of the State of Queensland be deemed to be

and become the owner of a 10 per cent undivided working

interest in the tenement."

9. On 5 November 1980 Mr Sykes submitted a budget to the directors of Abrolhos. It was in these terms:

"Dear Sirs,

We submit this budget by hand to your directors via Mr

Mullens and Mr Ferguson on their visit to XL Trough Barcoo

Junction No 1 Well on Thursday 6 November 1980 re XL Flank

Windorah East No 1 ATP 270. (There follows a list of items

totalling $1,108,815)

As you are aware you are taking over a similar contract XL

Petroleum Pty Ltd has with XLX NL to buy a 10 per cent

interest for the cost of one hole whether a dry one or a

producer which must be drilled (and completed if necessary)

at your sole risk, cost and expense. Please pay the funds

to the account of XL Petroleum Pty Ltd account No 124-123,

Commonwealth Trading Bank Cabramatta, NSW no later than by 1

December 1980.

Yours faithfully,

Ian G Sykes

For XLX NL and for XL Petroleum Pty Ltd"

10. On 5 November 1980 Mr Sykes wrote as Chairman of XL Petroleum to the Manager of the Commonwealth Trading Bank at Cabramatta advising that on 1 December an amount of $1,108,815 would be paid by Abrolhos into XL Petroleum's account 123-124 at the branch. The letter requested that amounts of $200,000, $400,000 and $500,000 be placed on deposit for 30, 45 and 60 days respectively and the balance paid into the account.

11. At a meeting of directors of XLX held on 18 December 1980 it was resolved

"that the amount due to the company from Abrolhos Oil and

Investments Limited in respect of ATP 270P be advanced to XL

Petroleum Pty Ltd at call by the company and subject to a

deferred payment of interest at a rate to be determined by

the directors ..... but not in excess of 3 percentage points

above trading bank overdraft rates.

12. On the same day a meeting of directors of XL Petroleum resolved

"that the Company accept a loan from XLX NL at call and

subject to a deferred payment of interest at a rate to be

determined by the Directors upon call of loan monies but

being at a rate up to and not in excess of three percentage

points above that of trading bank overdraft rates on the

$1,108,815."

13. Abrolhos paid the sum of $1,108,815 in two amounts, $910,000 on 2 January 1981 and the balance of $198,815 on 12 January. Both payments were paid into the account of XL Petroleum at the Commonwealth Trading Bank, Cabramatta.

14. OD and E did the drilling and, according to a statement of agreed facts put before the Tribunal

"... an amount of $1,231,216 was outlaid by XL Petroleum in

the year of income ended 30 June 1981 in relation to

petroleum exploration expenditure in the East Windorah hole

located in the area covered by ATP 270P as shown in the

amended accounts of XL Petroleum Pty Ltd furnished to the

Commissioner of Taxation in respect of the 1981 year."

The Tribunal's Decision

15. The primary basis of the Tribunal's decision was that s.124AH(1) is limited to the holder of a petroleum prospecting or mining right. Counsel for the Commissioner did not seek to uphold the decision on that ground. The concession in my view was correct.

16. The alternative reasoning of the Tribunal, which counsel for the Commissioner did support, is contained in the following passages:

"45. I have little doubt that without the interposition of

Abrolhos, (and leaving aside the failure to obtain

ministerial approval and/or the stamping of the XLX/XLP

contract of 14 May 1980), XLP could have legitimately

claimed the deduction for the cost of exploration of the

East Windorah Well. However, once Abrolhos acquired a 10%

interest in the venture, the whole undertaking took on a

different legal complexion. Thus the agreement between

Abrolhos and XLS undoubtedly had the effect of bringing

about a joint venture between the parties; cf clauses 1 and

2 of the Agreement (para 6 above). It follows that,

contrary to Mr Sykes' assertion, XLX could not deal with the

Abrolhos monies 'as it liked'. In my view these monies -

earmarked for the exploration of the XL Flank East Windorah

No. 1 Well - were held by XLX in trust for the joint

venturers. It follows that XLX could not lend these funds

to XLP without the consent of its co-venturer Abrolhos. In

the absence of such consent, it would appear that XLP must

be deemed to have held these `loan' funds on a constructive

trust; the fact that both companies were owned by Mr Sykes

is irrelevant.

46. If I am correct in the above, it follows that whilst

XLX was authorised to carry out the exploration `by itself

or through its servants and agents and such independent

contractors as it may engage', and notwithstanding that XLP

drilled the hole `for and on behalf of XLX', XLX remained

the `operator' for purposes of the Act - one does not become

an `operator' merely pursuant to a cosy `loan' arrangement

which had, as it sole purpose, the `transfer' of whatever

available tax deduction enures for the benefit of the

`operator'. Nor does one become an `operator' simply by

paying for the drilling operation. Once it is established

that the $1,108,815 which XLP paid OD and E for the drilling

the well (sic) had as their origin the `loan' from the joint

venture, it is disqualified from claiming the deduction

pursuant to sec 124AJ(1), if only because it fails to pass

the requirements of the preamble of the section, viz

For the purposes of this Division, where the holder of

a petroleum prospecting or mining right has, for a

consideration provided or to be provided by him, not

being a payment of a share of income derived by him

from the sale of petroleum ... not being a share of

income derived by him from the sale of petroleum ...

or a consideration by way of assignment or sub-lease

of a petroleum prospecting or mining right, procured

the performance of the work ..."

Conclusion

17. In my opinion it was not open to the Tribunal on the evidence to find that monies paid by Abrolhos to XL Petroleum or monies paid by the latter to OD and E were trust monies of a joint venture between XLX and Abrolhos. Neither before the Tribunal nor in this Court did the Commissioner raise any contention that the transactions were a sham or liable to avoidance under s.260 of the Act. The use of expressions such as "cosy `loan'" is inappropriate. Either there was a loan from XLX to XL Petroleum or there was not. The evidence was only consistent with XLX lending to XL Petroleum the money which it (XLX) was entitled to received from Abrolhos upon presentation of the budget. That money belonged to XLX to do with as it wished. It was the price received by XLX for sale of the ten per cent interest in the tenement to Abrolhos.

18. If the payment from Abrolhos could be said to be earmarked for any purpose, it was for a purpose of XLX, not of any joint venture. In any case, it is hard to see how there was not consent on the part of Abrolhos to the application of the monies. The XLX/Abrolhos agreement recorded that XL Petroleum was to drill the well and had engaged OD and E for that purpose. At XLX's direction (see letter of 5 November 1980) Abrolhos paid the money direct to XL Petroleum. Objectively considered, it must have been apparent to Abrolhos that as between XLX and XL Petroleum the former was lending the money to the latter.

19. Prior to the XLX/Abrolhos agreement, XL Petroleum had by the agreement of 14 May 1980 undertaken a contractual obligation to XLX to drill the well at "its (XL Petroleum's) sole cost, risk and expense". Also prior to the XLX/Abrolhos agreement XL Petroleum had entered into the drilling contract with OD and E. Thus before any joint venture between Abrolhos and XLX came into existence, XL Petroleum had contracted as principal for the drilling of the well and had made its own arrangements for the drilling.

20. Moreover, the arrangements XL Petroleum made with its bank for the application of the Abrolhos funds are not consistent with the latter being "earmarked " for the drilling of the well. At the time, XL Petroleum's account was active. There were substantial credit balances throughout January 1981 ranging from about $140,000 to over $900,000. There were as many as ten debits and credits on the one day. It appears no attempt was made before the Tribunal to trace payments in from Abrolhos to payments out to OD and E.

21. The Commissioner argued that once it is found the money was the money of the joint venture, s.124AJ(1) applies and the deduction is claimable by XLX/Abrolhos and not by XL Petroleum. But that assumption was not open on the evidence. As far as XLX is concerned, s.124AJ(3) applies since consideration by the holder of the right (XLX) for the performance of the work was an assignment of an interest in the right.

22. The Commissioner also sought to argue that the payment to OD and E was not "incurred" by XL Petroleum within the meaning of s.124AH(1) because the payment was incurred for the purpose of acquiring the 50 per cent interest under the agreement of 14 May 1980. However this argument is inconsistent with the facts agreed upon during the course of the Tribunal hearing which accepted that "an amount of $1,231,216 was outlaid by XL Petroleum Pty Ltd in the year of income ended 30 June 1981 in relation to petroleum exploration expenditure on the East Windorah Hole".

23. I make the following orders:

1. Appeal allowed.

2. Order the decision of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal given

on 2 April 1993 insofar as it affirms the decision of the respondent

to disallow the applicant's objection dated 12 February 1990 under

s.185 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Act) against the

assessment of income tax dated 22 October 1986 in respect of the

year of income ended 30 June 1981 be set aside and in lieu thereof

the applicant's said objection be allowed

3. Order that the matter be remitted to the respondent to amend the

applicant's assessment of income tax for the said year of income

in accordance with law.

4. Order that the respondent pay the applicant's costs of the

appeal, including reserved costs.
