Re Hadfield Finance Pty Ltd v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1988] FCA 252 (21 July 1988)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: HADFIELD FINANCE PTY. LTD

And: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. VG34 of 1988

Income Tax

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Sweeney J(1).

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s.105AA - judicial review of administrative decision, being refusal by Commissioner to grant further period in which to pay dividends for the purpose of making sufficient distribution under Part VII - whether error of law.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.) s.105AA

Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 (Cth.) s.13

Minister for Aboriginal Affairs and Anor v. Peko-Wallsend Limited and Ors. [1986] HCA 40; (1985-6) 162 CLR 24

HEARING

MELBOURNE

21:7:1988

Counsel for the applicant: Mrs. A. Moshinsky

Solicitor for the applicant: Mowbray

Counsel for the respondent: Mr. G. Nettle

Solicitor for the respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The application be dismissed with costs.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order

36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

This is an application pursuant to the provisions of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 ("the AD(JR) Act") to review the decision of the respondent made or purported to be made pursuant to the provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").

2. The decision in respect of which the application is made is the refusal to grant a further period in which the applicant may pay dividends for the purpose of making sufficient distribution, in relation to the year of income ended 31 July, 1981, pursuant to s.105AA of the Act.

3. The material questions of fact are set out in the statement pursuant to s.13 of the AD(JR) Act issued by the respondent dated 4 January, 1986 as follows:

"1. Hadfield Finance Pty. Ltd. was a private company

for income tax purposes having a substituted

accounting period ending 31 July.

2. The company lodged its return of income for the

income year ended 31 July 1981 on 21 December 1981.

3. The return for the income year ended 31 July 1981

was assessed without any adjustment being made to

the income returned by the company.

4. Notification issued from this office, dated 16

March 1982, advising the company that no primary

tax was payable on the income shown on the return

lodged for the income year ended 31 July 1981.

5. Notification issued from this office, dated 16

March 1982, advising the company that it had to

distribute $122,459 in dividends before 31 May 1982

if it was to avoid additional tax under Division 7.

6. The abovementioned notification also advised the

company that any liability to pay additional tax

under Division 7 for the income year ended 31 July

1981 would be determined upon the assessment of the

company's return of income for the income year

ended 31 July 1982 and the company was to show

details in respect of any dividends paid or

credited during the period 1 June 1981 to 31 May

1982 in its return for the income year ended 31

July 1982.

7. The company lodged its return of income for the

income year ended 31 July 1982 on 28 January 1983.

There were no details shown of any dividends having

been paid during the period 1 June 1981 to 31 May

1982.

8. A notice of assessment of additional tax under

Division 7 for the income year ended 31 July 1981

issued on 22 February 1983 showing the tax assessed

as $61,229.50.

9. The company lodged an objection against the

assessment of additional tax under Division 7 for

the income year ended 31 July 1981 and enclosed a

copy of a directors meeting minute showing a

dividend of $125,325 having been paid on 29 June

1981.

10. Based upon the abovementioned information, the

objection was allowed in full and a notice of

amended assessment of additional tax under Division

7 for the income year ended 31 July 1981 issued on

14 November 1983 to reduce the tax assessed to nil

11. On 20 September 1984 the company's tax agent

produced a copy of a minute of a directors meeting

held on 29 May 1981 which showed that the

abovementioned dividend of $125,325 was in fact

paid on 29 May 1981 and not 29 June 1981.

12. An inspection of the minute book supported the fact

that the dividend was paid on 29 May 1981 and not

29 June 1981, in fact, the minute book contained no

record of any directors meeting having been held on

29 June 1981.

13. A notice of amended assessment of additional tax

under Division 7 for the income year ended 31 July

1981 issued on 19 September 1985 restoring the tax

assessed to $61,229.50.".

4. An affidavit by John Allan Newton ("Mr Newton"), a director of the applicant was filed in support of the application, in which he deposed that the applicant did not receive the notification referred to in paragraph 5.

5. In relation to the matters referred to in paragraphs 9, 11 and 12 of the respondent's statement Mr. Newton said, inter alia:

"15. In searching the company's taxation records after

the receipt of its assessment to additional tax, I

located a document which purported to be a Minute

of a Meeting of Directors of the company held on

the 29th June, 1981, which purported to declare a

dividend of $125,325.00. I had no recollection of

that meeting but assumed that it recorded dividends

declared in respect of Hadfield's taxable income

for the year ended 31st July, 1981 and, as it had

been made within the prescribed period and exceeded

the amount necessary to avoid additional tax, I

submitted a copy of it together with an objection

to the Taxation Office on or about 23rd March, 1983

...".

"17. On or about the 14th day of November, 1983 the

Respondent issued to Hadfield an assessment levying

additional tax pursuant to Division 7 in respect of

the year of income ended 30th June, 1980 ...

18. As a consequence I perused Hadfield's records,

including its Minute Book, as I do from time to

time of every company of which I am a director, and

I located in the Minute Book a Minute recording a

meeting of directors held on the 29th May, 1981

which resolved to declare a dividend in the sum of

$125,325.00. Again I had no recollection of this

meeting, which, if held on 29th May, 1981, would

have been too soon to satisfy the requirements of

Division 7 in relation to the year of income ended

31st July, 1981 but when I made further

investigations and in particular when I compared

the amount of the purported dividend with that

required to be declared so as to avoid additional

tax pursuant to Division 7 in respect of the year

of income ended 31st July, 1980, I came to the

conclusion that the meeting had indeed been held on

the 29th May, 1981 and not on 29th June, 1981. It

therefore followed, in the absence of any other

minute recording any other meeting of directors

between the 1st June, 1981 and the 31st May, 1982

wherein the company resolved to declare a dividend,

that the company had failed to satisfy the

requirements of Division 7 in respect of the year

of income ended 31st July, 1981.

19. As the company had erroneously, although

innocently, advised the Respondent that a dividend

in the sum of $125,325.00 had been declared on the

29th June, 1981, I telephoned the Respondent

immediately and advised Mr. P. Maxwell of the

mistake. He advised me that it was the

Commissioner's practice to send out notices

advising companies which might be liable to

Division 7 tax of the amount of the dividend which

it must declare and of the period in which it must

declare it in order to avoid additional tax in

respect of any given year of income and that such a

notice had been sent to Hadfield. I advised him

that the company had not received any such

notification and, as set out above, I made

enquiries of the company's present officers and tax

agents, together with enquiries of its former

officers, shareholders and tax agents, and was

advised by them that no such notice had ever been

received. ...

20. On or about the 8th December, 1983, as it was

apparent that the company had satisfied the

requirements of Division 7 in respect of the year

of income ended 31st July, 1980, but not in respect

of the year of income ended 31st July, 1981, it

requested the Respondent to issue an amended

assessment reducing the additional tax to nil in

respect of the year of income ended 31st July, 1980

and to grant, pursuant to section 105AA of the

Income Tax Assessment Act, a further period in

which the company might declare a dividend so as to

satisfy the requirements of Division 7 for the year

of income ended 30th June, 1981. ...".

6. That request for the further period of time, and a similar request made on 19 October, 1984 by the applicant's tax agents, were refused by the respondent by letter dated 3 September, 1985.

7. Reasons for the decision were requested and were received by the applicant on 5 February, 1986.

8. The reasons for the decision not to grant an additional period for making sufficient distribution under s.105AA are stated by the respondent as follows:

"Having regard to the matters abovementioned (which

were the findings on material questions of fact,

which have been earlier set out) and in particular

the fact that no adjustments were made to the

taxable income returned by the company for the

income year ended 31 July 1981, the company was not

entitled to any additional period in which to pay

dividends for the purpose of avoiding additional

tax under Division 7.

The exact date the dividend of $125,325 was paid

was a fact known to the company and any mistake

about the date is not a sufficient reason to grant

an additional period for making sufficient

distribution.".

Section 105AA of the Act provides:

"105AA. (1) Where-

(a) a notice of assessment in respect of the income of

a company of a year of income has not been served

on the company before the prescribed time;

(b) before the prescribed time there was served on a

company a notice of assessment in respect of the

income of the company of a year of income on the

basis of which the company would not have had a

distributable income in respect of that year of

income and after the prescribed time there has been

served on the company a notice of amended

assessment in respect of that income on the basis

of which the company has a distributable income in

respect of that year of income; or

(c) after the prescribed time there has been served on

a company a notice of amended assessment in respect

of the income of the company of a year of income on

the basis of which the distributable income of the

company in respect of that year of income is

greater than it would have been on the basis of the

assessment before it was amended,

the company may, by writing signed by the public officer

of the company, request the Commissioner to determine a

further period in which the company may pay dividends

for the purpose of making a sufficient distribution in

relation to that year of income.

(2) On receipt of the request the Commissioner may grant

the request and determine such a further period or he

may refuse the request.

(3) The Commissioner shall serve, by post, on the

company that made the request, a notice in writing of

his decision on the request and, where the request is

granted, the notice shall be so served before the

commencement of the further period determined by the

Commissioner.

(4) In deciding whether to grant or refuse the request,

the Commissioner shall have regard to-

(a) the date on which the company lodged a return of

its income of the year of income to which the

request relates;

(b) if the company did not make a full and true

disclosure in its return of all the material facts

necessary for an assessment of the taxable income

of the company of that year of income-the date on

which such a full and true disclosure was made; and

(c) any other matters that he thinks relevant.

(5) If the Commissioner determines a further period in

relation to a company in relation to a year of income

and dividends (other than special fund dividends) are

paid by the company during the further period, so much

(if any) of the amount of those dividends as-

(a) is specified in a notice in writing signed by the

public officer of the company and lodged with the

Commissioner not later than 30 days after the end

of the further period; and

(b) does not exceed-

(i) in a case to which paragraph (1)(c)

applies-the excess of the amount that is the

undistributed amount in relation to the

company in relation to that year of income

over the amount that would have been that

undistributed amount on the basis of the

assessment before it was amended; or

(ii) in any other case-the undistributed amount in

relation to the company in relation to that

year of income,

shall, for the purposes of this Division, be deemed to

have been paid during the prescribed period in relation

to that year of income.

6. In this section, 'the prescribed time', in relation

to a company in relation to a year of income,

means-

(a) in the case of a year of income preceding the

year of income that ended on 30 June 1963-the

end of that last-mentioned year of income; or

(b) in the case of any other year of income-the

time of expiration of the fifteenth day before

the end of the prescribed period in relation

to that year of income.".

The grounds relied upon by the applicant were:

"(i) that the making of the decison was an

improper exercise of the power conferred by

the Act;

(ii) the decision involved an error of law;

(iii) the Respondent took irrelevant

considerations into account in the exercise

of the power;

(iv) the Respondent failed to take relevant

considerations into account in the exercise

of the power;

(v) there was no or no sufficient evidence or

other material to justify the making of the

decision; and

(vi) the decision was otherwise contrary to

law.".

9. In support of its contention that the decision was an improper exercise of the power conferred by the Act, the applicant gave further particulars as follows:

"The Respondent has sought to penalize the

Applicant notwithstanding the clear scheme of the

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ('the Act') relating

to the taxation of dividends paid by subsidiary

companies to holding companies.".

10. In my opinion, no basis has been established upon which one could conclude that the respondent "sought to penalize the Applicant notwithstanding the clear scheme" of the Act.

11. Further particulars of the contention that the decision involved an error of law were given as follows:

"(a) The Respondent has failed to consider or to

adequately consider the following:

(i) that the Applicant had made a full and

true disclosure of its income and of

the sources of its income and of the

dividends paid by it for the years of

income ended 31st July 1980 and 31st

July 1981;

(ii) that the Applicant lodged its Income

Tax Return for the year ended 31st July

1981 on the 18th December 1981 which

income tax return showed the amount and

nature of the Applicant's income;

(iii) that the Applicant had voluntarily

disclosed its failure to declare a

dividend within the prescribed period

to the Respondent;

(iv) that the scheme of the Act applying to

the taxation of dividends paid by

subsidiaries to holding companies in

circumstances such as in the present

case is such that if a dividend is

declared within the prescribed period

there is neither tax pursuant to

Division 7 borne by the subsidiary nor

tax pursuant to section 44 of the Act

borne by the holding company;

(v) that the scheme of Division 7 of the

Act is that tax pursuant to that

division is only to be imposed in

circumstances where profits are

deliberately retained by the company

and not distributed as dividends solely

or primarily to avoid the payment of

tax by the shareholders on the

dividends they would otherwise receive;

(vi) that the failure to declare a dividend

was an oversight of the Applicant and

was not a contrivance to avoid tax

which might otherwise be payable by the

holding company;

(vii) the failure of the Applicant to receive

a notice from the Respondent advising

of the amount of the sufficient

distribution which it must make and the

date by which it must make it in order

to avoid tax pursuant to Division 7 of

the Act.

(b) The consideration of or the giving of undue

weight to the following:

(i) the maximisation of the tax payable by

the Applicant;

(ii) the knowledge of the Applicant as to

the date of payment of the dividend.".

12. These particulars were also given in respect of the contention that the respondent took into account irrelevant considerations.

13. Particulars (i) to (iv) were not, in my opinion, substantiated by the applicant, as there is no reason to think that the respondent failed to take into account the history of the applicant's returns and its various accounts of the declaration of dividends.

14. Paragraph (v), in my opinion, seeks to put a gloss upon the Act which is not justified. Division 7 speaks for itself and is not properly to be characterised in the language of this paragraph.

15. So far as paragraph (vi) is concerned, the respondent was not obliged to conclude that "the failure to declare a dividend was an oversight of the applicant and was not a contrivance to avoid tax which might otherwise be payable by the holding company". The respondent was entitled to consider generally the circumstances in relation to the declarations of dividends, and there is no reason to conclude that he did not do so.

16. Paragraph (vii) is based upon an assertion that the respondent should have concluded that the applicant failed to receive a notice from him "advising of the amount of the sufficient distribution which it must make and the date by which it must make it in order to avoid tax pursuant to Division 7 of the Act".

17. The respondent found, as a material fact, that notification issued from his office "dated 16 March 1982 to avoid tax being paid by its holding company in respect of the dividend which it would have otherwise received.".

18. Included in "the evidence or other material" on which his findings were based, the respondent stated:

"Advice to company, dated 16 March 1982, showing

potential additional tax liability under Division 7

for the income year ended 31 July 1981.".

19. Mr. Newton, in his affidavit, set out to show that it did not receive the notification dated 16 March 1982 advising it that it had to distribute $122,459 in dividends before 31 May 1982 if it was to avoid additional tax under Division 7. An answering affidavit was filed by the respondent seeking to show that the notification was duly posted to the applicant. This evidence as to whether the notification was received was inconclusive, but in any event, the respondent's decison is to be reviewed in the light of the material available to him at the time (see the Peko-Wallsend case[1986] HCA 40; , 162 C.L.R. 24 at p.45). In the course of correspondence between the parties, the applicant had contended that this notification was not in fact received by it. It was, in my opinion, open to the respondent, without error of law, to conclude that it was so received, and its Section 13 statement, properly understood, shows that he reached this conclusion. It was the receipt of the notice, not merely its sending, that became a material question of fact.

20. The final paragraph of the respondents Section 13 statement reads as follows:

"The exact date the dividend of $125,325 was paid

was a fact known to the company and any mistake

about the date is not a sufficient reason to grant

an additional period for making sufficient

distribution.".

21. In my opinion, this knowledge of the company, which was no stranger to Division 7 questions, afforded a basis upon which the respondent was entitled, without error of law, to base the exercise of his administrative discretion.

22. There is, in my opinion, no reason for concluding that the respondent took any irrelevant consideration into account.

23. The weight to be given to the considerations which he took into account was a matter for the respondent, there being no contention that his decision was manifestly unreasonable (see Peko-Wallsend at p.41).

24. The application is dismissed, with costs.
