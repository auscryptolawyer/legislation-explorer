ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - whether applicant's share in family partnership income was more or less than an equal share in various income tax years - effect of deed variations - whether profit includes in its meaning loss - whether statement on tax return can vary partnership agreement by itself - whether amendment of agreement can have retrospective effect

Income Tax - whether an amended assessment authorised by section 170 - full and true disclosure - insufficient information provided to work out apportionment in terms of deed

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s. 92(2)

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION ) VT86/5616-5617

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The questions arising for our decision in this case are as follows:-

(a) Whether the applicant's individual interest in the net loss of a business carried on in partnership with his wife (referred to herein as the "accommodation" partnership) is, pursuant to sub-section 92(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"), more than $6,524 in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1981.

(b) Whether the amended assessment in issue in respect of that year was authorised by section 170 of the said Act.

(c) Whether the applicant's individual interest in the net loss of the "accommodation" partnership is, pursuant to the same sub-section, more than $6,486 in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1982.

(d) Whether the applicant's individual interest in the net loss of a further business carried on in partnership with his wife (referred to herein as the "Motor Lodge" partnership) is, pursuant to sub-section 92(2), more than $4,102 in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1982.

(e) Whether the applicant's individual interest in a loss sustained in relation to properties jointly owned with his wife is, pursuant to sub-section 92(2), more than $3,767 in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1984.

2. The applicant, an accountant, appeared in person and presented his own case. The Commissioner of Taxation (the respondent) was represented by one of his officers.

  1. It appears that from about 1974 the Motor Lodge partnership carried on the business of letting holiday units essentially in mountain resorts which it owned. The business was carried on at that stage by a partnership which comprised the applicant, his wife, his father and his mother, who shared the profits and losses equally. It also appears that, initially, the marketing of the units through advertisements, etc., was handled by outside agents but that, by about 1978, the applicant and his mother undertook most, if not all, of the marketing work. By about that time the marketing of holiday units had extended beyond those owned by the partnership to units owned by third parties.
  2. It seems that, in or about 1978, the applicant's father and mother ceased to be partners and the business was divided into two parts. The part concerned with the letting of units (Motor Lodge) was carried on in partnership by the applicant and his wife, and the other part, concerned with the marketing side of the business (accommodation), was also carried on in a separate partnership by the applicant and his wife. Unlike Motor Lodge, the accommodation partnership owned no assets apart from small items of office furniture. No written partnership agreements were entered into, and during the two years of income preceding those in issue the profits and losses in both partnerships were shared equally. No agreement between the partners was reached concerning the payment of salaries, and it seems that none were paid.
  3. During those two years, and during the years of income in issue ended 30 June 1981, 1982 and 1984, the applicant worked full time in outside employment (unrelated to either of the partnerships) between the hours of 8.30 a.m. and 4.30 p.m. per five day week. During the 1979 and 1980 income years and until 25 January 1981, the applicant's wife also worked in unrelated outside employment between the hours of 9.00 a.m. and 5.30 p.m. per five day week. On 13 November 1980, the applicant's wife applied to her employer for the grant of 39 weeks' unpaid maternity leave with effect from 26 January 1981. In a letter dated 9 December 1980, her employer advised her that her request for leave had been granted and that, if she intended to resume her employment, a notice of that intention was required to be given to her employer not less than four weeks prior to the time of expiration of her maternity leave. In a letter dated 16 September 1981, her employer advised her that the request made in her letter dated 8 September 1981, that she be granted a further 13 weeks' unpaid maternity leave had been granted and that the condition referred to earlier concerning re-employment still applied. It seems that the applicant's wife was on maternity leave for the periods indicated and that she resumed outside employment at the end of that time. For present purposes, however, it is of some significance that the applicant's wife did not receive income from salary during her maternity leave and that the losses in issue, as claimed by her husband, would have provided her with few tax benefits, if at all.
  4. Generally, it seems that both partners were involved in the day-to-day activities of the partnerships and, in that connection, were obliged to work in the evenings and on some weekends. It seems also that the applicant's activities were more concerned with the bookkeeping and banking side of the businesses and with the compilation of information to be used in bulletins which were issued to interested parties from time to time. His wife's activities were apparently directed to answering telephone calls (some pre-recorded after January 1982), discussing vacancies and tariffs to be charged, assisting with the mail and assembling information bulletins and despatching them as required.

7. We were given to understand that the nature of the partnerships' activities was much the same during all of the years 1979 to 1984 inclusive. However, the level of the activities in each year varied throughout each year, with the consequence that the hours devoted to those activities by the applicant and his wife also varied throughout each year. However, the evidence of the partners in relation to the hours devoted to those activities was far from satisfactory, and in some material respects was in conflict. It was conceded in evidence that no records of hours worked by each in any of the years in issue were maintained. No records of hours worked were tendered in evidence before us. However, it seems that, on the most favourable view of the evidence, for several weeks before and during the busy periods of Christmas, Easter and the winter months (commencing some time in April and ending in about September) in each year, the partners, between them, would have worked some 40 hours per seven day week on average on those duties, although, on occasions, some 60 hours might have been worked. In the non-busy periods of each year, the partners between them would have worked some 20 hours per seven day week on average. Having regard to the evidence overall, we do not accept the assertions of the applicant's wife that, apart from short absences concerned with visiting a health clinic and recreational walks, she was always at home and therefore engaged on partnership business, both during the day and at night, throughout the relevant periods. We would observe that, during that time, she was responsible for maintaining the home and no doubt devoted much time and attention to caring for her baby.

  1. It seems that, with the birth of the baby on 24 February 1981, the applicant's wife necessarily did not work at all on partnership affairs during the six days that she spent in hospital following the birth, and that those duties absorbed something less of her time than 10 hours per week during January 1981 and in February 1981 during the period before the birth of her child. It seems probable that her partnership duties were also limited for a time after her discharge from hospital, which, in any event, was during a quiet period for business purposes.
  2. In a photocopy of a document which purported to be a "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement", and which was said to affect the division of profits and losses of the accommodation partnership for the year ended 30 June 1981, it was stated:-

"Profits of the partnership will be divided equally between the partners subsequent to the provision of partners salaries as agreed by the partners, except in the case where during any particular year one partner is employed inside the business and the other partner is employed outside the business, then the partner employed inside the business will be entitled to a minimum 75% share of any profits before partners salaries are taken into account and will not share in any net loss for that particular year, or as decided by the partners from time to time.

For the purposes of this agreement, a partner shall be considered to be employed outside the partnership, if for a period of at least 10 months during the financial year that partner was engaged in permanent employment with a firm not related to the partnership for a minimum of 20 hours per normal week.

A partner shall be considered to be employed inside the partnership, if for a period of at least five months during the financial year that partner was not engaged in regular employment outside the partnership and was engaged in partnership business a minimum of 20 hours per normal week.

This variation to the partnership agreement is to take effect with the financial year commencing July 1, 1980."

10. This document was signed by both partners and bore the date, 20 July 1980, although, in cross-examination, the applicant admitted to the possibility that the document might have been signed at a later point in time and ante-dated. In his evidence, the applicant stated that he had drawn up the document and that it accurately reflected the terms of an oral agreement that he had reached with his wife some time before the end of June 1981. The applicant could not recall whether the typed document in evidence had been preceded by a hand-written document prepared at or about the time that the alleged agreement had been entered into. If a hand-written document had existed, it had been, it was said, either lost or destroyed once the typed document had been prepared. It was also said that it had not been practicable to prepare the typed document until July 1980, because of the absence overseas of both partners. Two stamps to the value of $7 were affixed to the document and bore on their face the notation "21/7/80". In cross-examination, the applicant admitted that he was uncertain as to whether he or someone else had made the notation. He was also unable to say whether the document had been submitted to the Stamp Duties Office for examination and payment of duty. However, the document in evidence does not bear any cash register imprints or other notations which might be identified with the Stamp Duties Office. The evidence of the applicant's wife concerning this deed, and the two further deeds to which we make reference later in these reasons, was vague and indicates, in our view, that she was not familiar with their terms and that, even if she were, she had little idea as to the practical effects of their application. We have strong reservations as to whether there was a genuine coming together of minds for the purposes of the alleged agreements.

11. In the accommodation partnership return of income for the year ended 30 June 1981, a loss of $13,047 was disclosed. It seems that, in conformity with his understanding of the effect of the "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement" referred to earlier, the applicant regarded the whole of that amount as having been sustained by him as a partner in that year, and therefore claimed it as being deductible from amounts returned as assessable income in his personal return of income. In the original assessment that was issued to the applicant in respect of that year, the respondent allowed the applicant's claim. However, in an amended assessment that was issued subsequently and which is the assessment in issue, the respondent disallowed the claim to the extent of 50% and treated the amount of $6,523 as a loss which was, for tax purposes, available to the applicant's wife as a partner.

12. Having regard to the ground of objection concerning the power of the respondent in terms of section 170 of the Act to issue the amended assessment in issue, it is necessary to provide details surrounding the issue of that assessment. The original assessment based upon a taxable income of $6,928 was issued on 9 November 1981. An amended assessment, based on a taxable income of $8,488, was issued to the applicant on 13 May 1983, and brought to account as additional income an adjustment which concerned the affairs of the Motor Lodge partnership. The objection directed against this assessment was withdrawn in a letter dated 15 February 1984, subject to various allowances which need not concern us here. The further amended assessment, based on a taxable income of $14,722, was issued to the applicant on 7 June 1984. In arriving at the taxable income for this assessment, a further small adjustment in favour of the applicant was made to the distribution included from the Motor Lodge partnership. The amount in dispute of $6,523, however, arose out of an adjustment to the distribution from the accommodation partnership which, in turn, gave rise to the objection dated 21 July 1984, the decision in respect of which the present review is concerned.

13. It is relevant to observe here that it is not known whether the copy of the "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement", referred to earlier, was included with the attachments lodged with the partnership return of income or with the applicant's return of income and, if not, the date on which it was submitted to the respondent. In the circumstances, it is not possible to say whether the copy of the deed was in the possession of the respondent before the issue of the original assessment on 9 November 1981, or the issue of the first amended assessment on 13 May 1983. However, putting to one side the uncertainty surrounding the date of receipt of the copy of the deed, it is apparent that, on the assumption that its contents were known to the respondent before the issue of the original and first amended assessments, it would not have been possible for the respondent, on the basis of the deed itself, to have determined the quantum of the profits or losses that should have been distributed in terms of the deed to the applicant and his wife. Neither the accommodation partnership return of income nor the applicant's personal return nor the attachments that accompanied those returns were, of themselves (or when considered in conjunction with the deed), sufficient to enable the respondent to arrive at what might be regarded as the correct basis of apportioning profits and losses or to calculate the correct amounts of the distributions that were required to be made in terms of the deed. It is our view that the particulars already adverted to in these reasons, concerning the times worked by the applicant and his wife, both on partnership business and in outside employment, were a necessary pre-requisite to a correct calculation of the distributions. Having regard to the documents in evidence, it is apparent that these particulars were not provided to the respondent before either of the assessment dates mentioned, and it would seem therefore that the respondent could not have become aware of them until the hearing of the matters in issue with which we are presently concerned. In the circumstances, it is clear, in our opinion, that the applicant did not make a full and true disclosure of all material facts so as to preclude the respondent in terms of section 170 of the Act from issuing the further amended assessment dated 7 June 1984. (See generally Australian Jam Co. Pty. Ltd. v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1953] HCA 52; (1953) 88 CLR 23; W. Thomas & Co. Pty. Ltd. v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1965] HCA 54; (1965) 14 ATD 78; McAndrew v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1956] HCA 62; (1956) 98 CLR 263.)

  1. In the accommodation partnership return of income for the year ended 30 June 1982, a loss of $12,971 was disclosed. Again, apparently in conformity with his understanding of the effect of the "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement", the applicant regarded the whole of that amount as having been sustained by him as a partner in that year and claimed it as being deductible from amounts returned as assessable income in his personal return of income for that year. As was the case with the 1981 year, the respondent treated the 1982 loss as being equally divisible between the applicant and his wife and disallowed the amount of $6,485 as a loss deductible in the hands of the applicant.
  2. In the 1982 Motor Lodge partnership return of income, a loss of $11,684 was disclosed. Apparently, to give effect to a purported "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement" dated 14 July 1981, concerning this partnership, the applicant claimed the whole of that amount as a deduction in his personal return of income. The deed reads as follows:-

"Profit of the partnership will he divided equally between the partners subsequent to the provision of partners salaries at the amount agreed by the partners, except where in any one financial year the total amount of capital and loans subscribed by the partners exceeds the amount of profit, in such circumstances profits will be divided in proportion to total capital and loans subscribed by each partner during the year or as decided by the partners from time to time.

This variation to the partnership agreement is to take effect with the financial year commencina July 1, 1981."

  1. In the original assessment dated 13 May 1983, which was issued to the applicant in respect of the 1982 year, the respondent treated the loss of $11,684 as being equally divisible between the applicant and his wife, which action, together with adjustments concerning repairs which need not concern us here, had the effect of reducing the returned loss by an amount of $8,831. However, in an amended assessment dated 7 June 1984, which had the effect of allowing the partnership repair claims in part, the disallowed amount of $8,831 was reduced, with the consequence that the partnership loss was increased and assessed as being the amount of $8,203. It was agreed between the parties that the amount of the loss in issue in relation to the Motor Lodge partnership was therefore $4,101, being the one-half (to the nearest dollar) of the loss treated by the respondent as being available as a deduction to the applicant's wife and not to the applicant.
  2. No separate partnership returns of income were in evidence in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1984. However, it appears from the applicant's personal return of income for that year that particulars of income and outgoings applicable to the Motor Lodge partnership, which, in earlier years, were disclosed in a separate return, were, in 1984, included in his personal return. In the 1984 return of income, the applicant described the income and outgoings as being applicable to two jointly owned properties, presumably by the applicant and his wife. It seems, however, that the Motor Lodge partnership continued in existence during the 1984 year and that it was the owner of the properties in respect of which the income was said to have been jointly derived. It also seems that, in calculating his share of the loss sustained by the partnership in 1984, the applicant had regard to the previously mentioned "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement" dated 14 July 1981, and a further "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement" dated 5 June 1984, which was signed by both partners. This latter deed reads as follows:-

"It is hereby stated by the partners that in the variation to the partnership agreement dated July 14, 1981, the division of profits was intended to encompass both positive and negative profits that is, to include profits and losses.

The following restates the intent of the agreement as from July 1, 1981 and as it is desired that the agreement will apply currently and until otherwise specified by the partners.

Profit or loss of the partnership will be divided equally between the partners subsequent to the provision of partners salaries at the amount agreed by the partners, except where in any one financial year the total amount of capital and loans subscribed by the partners exceeds the amount of net income, in such circumstances profits or losses will be divided in proportion to total capital and loans subscribed by each partner during the year or as decided by the partners from time to time."

  1. It appears that, in applying the terms of the deeds for the 1984 income year, the applicant returned one-half of the gross receipts as assessable income and claimed as a deduction the whole of the outgoings less a small amount which, apparently, represented one-half of outgoings paid out of partnership funds to which hoth partners had subscribed. We were given to understand that the applicant's wife returned one-half of the gross receipts as assessable income and claimed a deduction in respect of the one-half of partnership funds utilised in the payment of expenses.
  2. The respondent disallowed the loss as calculated by the applicant and claimed by him as a deduction. The respondent assessed him on the basis that he was entitled only to a deduction of $3,767, being one-half of the net loss of $7,534 which was calculated by the respondent as being available for distribution to both the applicant and his wife.
  3. We turn now to examine the "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement" dated 20 July 1980, in relation to the evidence concerning the accommodation partnership adduced in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1981. In that year, a loss was sustained by the partnership. From the evidence it is apparent that the applicant was, in terms of the deed, employed "outside the partnership" because in that income year he was engaged for 12 months, i.e. two months in excess of the requisite period, "in permanent employment with a firm not related to the partnership for a minimum of 20 hours per normal week". In fact, the evidence indicates that he worked some 40 hours per five day week in that employment.
  4. However, the question of whether the applicant was employed "inside the partnership", in terms of the deed, has also to be considered. As indicated, the applicant worked for the whole of the 1981 income year in unrelated outside employment. Therefore, he does not fall within the ambit of the first condition that must be satisfied for the purposes of that phrase in that he was not a partner who was not engaged in regular employment for five months or more outside the partnership. The facts of the case also support the proposition that he was not engaged on partnership business for a minimum of 20 hours per normal week as required by the second condition for the purposes of determining whether he was employed "inside the partnership". It will be recalled that during the non-busy periods of the year, which in total aggregated several months, the partners between them only worked in the business for some 20 hours per seven day week on average. With both conditions satisfied as is required to be done, the applicant was clearly not employed "inside the partnership" and it follows, therefore, that he was employed "outside the partnership" in terms of the deed.
  5. Under the first paragraph of the deed, it also follows that the applicant was entitled to share in the net loss sustained by the partnership in the 1981 income year. However, the extent of his entitlement to that loss must be determined in the light of his wife's entitlement, if any, to share in that loss, a matter to which we now turn.
  6. In the 1981 income year, the applicant's wife worked for approximately seven months in unrelated outside employment which was some three months short of the requisite period of 10 months required to be worked to qualify her as being "outside the partnership" in terms of the deed. However, the position in relation to her being employed "inside the partnership" raises a more complex question. Whilst it is true that she did not work for at least five months during the income year in outside employment as is required under the first condition concerning employment "inside the partnership", she did not, as is required by the second condition pertaining to that phrase, engage in partnership business for a minimum period of 20 hours per normal week in that period or throughout the whole of the year if the condition should properly be construed as applying to the full year. It will be recalled that she spent six days in hospital in the latter part of February 1981, following the birth of her child, and that for some time before and after her hospitalisation she worked in the business for less than 10 hours per week. It follows therefore that the applicant's wife did not qualify as being employed "inside the partnership" because of her failure, as required, to satisfy both conditions.
  7. It will be observed that the first paragraph of the deed speaks of the equal division of profits between the partners and then proceeds to spell out, by way of a variation to the continuing oral agreement between the partners that was entered into in about 1978, how the profits and losses would be divided in the event that one partner was employed inside the partnership and the other was employed outside the partnership in terms of the deed. However, in the event that the partners were not so separately employed, as in the instant case the above analysis indicates, it is not possible to give effect to the exception concerning the division of profits and losses or to the variation provided by the deed to the oral agreement under which profits and losses were to be shared equally. In our view, the respondent was therefore correct in treating the loss of $13,047 as being equally divisible between the partners and in disallowing the loss of $6,523 as a deduction to the applicant.
  8. For similar reasons, we are also of the view that the respondent was correct in treating the loss of $12,971 sustained by the accommodation partnership in the 1982 income year as being equally divisible between the partners and in disallowing the loss of $6,485 as a deduction to the applicant. However, it will be observed, in relation to this year, that, although the applicant's wife was not employed "outside the partnership" in terms of the deed on the basis that she did not resume outside employment until about January 1982, she nevertheless did not comply with the same condition required to be satisfied by the phrase "inside the partnership" because of her failure to engage in the partnership business for a minimum period of 20 hours per normal week either in the period of seven months in which she was not employed outside the partnership or throughout the year.
  9. In the 1982 income year it is also necessary to consider the continuing Motor Lodge oral partnership agreement between the partners and the effect on that agreement of the variation deeds, dated 14 July 1981, and 5 June 1984 (referred to earlier), for the purposes of ascertaining how the loss in that year should he distributed.
  10. As already indicated, the Motor Lodge oral partnership agreement provided for the equal sharing between the partners of profits or losses in the 1982 income year. On the basis of the information disclosed to us the whole of the loss was claimed as a deduction by the applicant. However, the deed of variation, dated 14 July 1981, was not concerned at all with the division of the 1982 loss, concerning as it did a basis of distributing a possible profit of the partnership in that year which did not eventuate. In these circumstances, it is quite clear, in our opinion, that that deed could have no application to the distribution of the loss which, in terms of the oral agreement, was required to be divided equally between the partners.
  11. That the deed, dated 14 July 1981, in the light of hindsight, was ineffective for the purposes for which it might be thought to have been intended is evidenced, we consider, by the later deed, dated 5 June 1984, in which a basis was provided for distributing both profits and losses. However, the question remains as to whether the 1984 deed might properly be regarded as having had retroactive application to the 1982 income year.
  12. In a letter to the Deputy Commissioner dated 30 June 1982, concerning a change of trading name for the Motor Lodge partnership, the applicant advised, inter alia, as follows:-

"The change involved a change in trading name only. There was no change to the partnership agreement or the relationship between the partners at this date.

The assets, income sources and business nature of the partnership did not change. There was no change to the partnership hank account save to change the name under which the account operates."

30. It would appear from this letter that as at 30 June 1982, the oral agreement between the partners for the sharing of profits and losses equally was still in force, subject, however, to the possible operation of the deed dated 14 July 1981, which, as pointed out earlier, concerned only the distribution of profits in the circumstances described in the deed. However, in a statement made within seven days of that letter and included in an attachment to the 1982 return of income of the partnership and signed by the applicant on 7 July 1982, it was said that:-

"The loss and increase in fixed assets were completely funded by one partner this year. As per the partnership agreement in these circumstances the total net income/loss was distributed to that partner."

31. Clearly, in our opinion, the reference to the partnership agreement in the above statement was to the oral agreement or, on the most favourable interpretation as to its import, to that agreement as varied by the deed dated 14 July 1981. The statement which was made after the end of the 1982 income year could not, in our opinion, be regarded of itself as being a further variation with retroactive effect on those two agreements, nor could it be seen to be, in our view, as at the time of signing on 7 July 1982, as a variation of the earlier agreement which de facto existed from that time until the last deed referred to in these reasons was executed on 5 June 1984. See generally Waddington v O'Callaghan (H. M. Inspector of Taxes) (1929-1932) 16 TC 187; Trollope & Colls etc. v Atomic Power etc. (1962) 3 All ER 1035; Rowe (B. & H. G.) v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 82 ATC 4243.) For these reasons, we are of the opinion that the respondent was correct in treating the loss of $8,203 sustained by the Motor Lodge partnership in the 1982 income year as being equally divisible between the partners and in disallowing the loss of $4,101 in issue as a deduction to the applicant.

  1. We turn now to consider the applicant's claim in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1984. As indicated previously, this claim concerns an amount of $3,767, being one-half of the net loss of $7,534 which was calculated by the respondent in respect of the Motor Lodge partnership as being available for distribution to both the applicant and his wife. However, before proceeding to an examination of this matter, we would observe that, despite the changed basis of supplying particulars of income and outgoings to the respondent, Motor Lodge, previously referred to as a partnership, continued as a partnership (albeit as co-owners in a joint venture) in the 1984 income year within the meaning of that word as defined in section 6 of the Act and that, for present purposes, nothing turns upon that change. In substance, the applicant's case is based on acceptance of that proposition and the operation of the "Deed of Variation of Partnership Agreement" dated 5 June 1984.
  2. Apart from the statement referred to in paragraph 30 above, concerning a possible variation of the oral agreement and the deed of variation dated 14 July 1981, the documents before us do not evidence any change in the rights of both partners to share in the losses equally. In the circumstances, and having regard to our earlier comments in relation to the statement of 7 July 1982, we are of the opinion that the rights of the partners to share the losses equally remained unchanged until, at the earliest, 1 July 1983. This latter date has been selected with the possibility in mind that the deed of 5 June 1984, might be considered to have retroactive effect as from 1 July 1983.
  3. Our task now is to determine whether that deed had retroactive effect as from 1 July 1983, or whether it was effective only as from 5 June 1984, and, in either event, whether the conditions precedent to the operation of the deed were satisfied (see paragraph 3 of deed).
  4. From our previous comments it will be recalled that, apart from the statement of 7 July 1982, and the deed of variation dated 5 June 1984, no evidence is before us which would support the proposition that the oral agreement for the sharing of profits and losses equally was varied. Therefore, it seems to us that, if that deed had any effect at all, it could only have been effective as from 5 June 1984. The mere fact that the deed was dated at that time, which was in the last month of the year of income, does not of itself, in our opinion, mean that its effectiveness necessarily had retrospective application as from the first day of that year of income. Whilst the deed itself speaks of an intention that the earlier deed, dated 14 July 1981, should have applied as from 1 July 1981, and should have applied to both losses and profits as from that time, it remains, in our opinion, simply as a belated expression of intent which did not, on the facts, find expression in reality.

36. In considering the application of the deed dated 5 June 1984, to the year of income ended 30 June 1984, we were reminded by the applicant of the decision of the Board of Review (No. 2) in Case 035, 83 ATC 154, and the reference to the reasons for that decision in the reasons of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for its decision in Case T81, 86 ATC 1109, presently under appeal to the Federal Court. Both cases concerned the deductibility of losses sustained by a husband and wife from the letting of jointly owned properties. In Case 035, there was no formal agreement between the parties and there was no evidence adduced to indicate any basis for dividing profits and losses. In this connection, the only evidence adduced was that the parties shared the view that if losses were sustained, the effect of those losses would be minimised by having the husband claim them in full as a tax deduction. In the circumstances, the Board concluded that, in the absence of any agreement concerned with the sharing of profits and losses as distinct from claims for tax purposes, losses could only be divided equally because of the fact of joint ownership of the income producing properties. Because of the pending appeal in Case T81, we make no further reference to it other than to indicate that, in our opinion, Case 035 had little, if any, relevance to it. However, we would add that, in our opinion, both cases are distinguishable on their facts from those in the instant case and are not relevant for present purposes.

37. We have indicated earlier that the deed dated 5 June 1984 could have application to the period commencing on that date to 30 June 1984 inclusive, providing the criteria set out in the deed for the sharing of losses were satisfied. Balance sheets as at 5 June 1984 and 30 June 1984, were not tendered in evidence, with the consequence that we do not know what the position of the capital and loan accounts of each partner was at those dates, nor do we know what movements took place in those accounts during that period. Whilst various documents in evidence indicate that various expenses of a revenue and capital nature were paid by the applicant during the year, we do not know on the evidence whether some part of those outgoings should properly be regarded as subscriptions of capital and loans in terms of the deed. On the other hand, assuming in the applicant's favour that some part of the outgoings represented subscriptions to capital and loans, we are not able on the evidence to determine the amounts so subscribed. In this connection, it will also be recalled that the applicant and his wife each returned 50% of the gross proceeds of Motor Lodge as income for tax purposes and that, although the applicant appeared to pay most of the partnership outgoings from private funds, his wife as partner also contributed to payment of some expenses apparently out of partnership funds. Furthermore, in this connection, it is observed that the applicant's wife, in her evidence, clearly indicated that she was personally unaware of the thrust of the deed dated 5 June 1984 (or of the earlier deeds for that matter) or of the complexities surrounding an interpretation of it. In our opinion, a finding therefore is open to us, on the balance of probabilities, that she was not in reality a bona fide party to the deed which should in consequence he regarded as being of negatory effect. From the wife's viewpoint, the partnership arrangement, in our understanding of her evidence, was a simple one involving the sharing of profits and losses equally. However, it would appear that, being disenchanted with the partnership operations at the relevant time, she had determined not to contribute further to its capital or outgoings out of her private funds, although by inference, it seems, she was content to let her husband make whatever tax claims, in respect of the expenses paid by him, which he considered appropriate.

  1. Having regard to the above comments, it will be seen that, in our opinion, the deed dated 5 June 1984, possibly had no legal effect. However, in any event, the applicant has failed to discharge the onus of proving, by establishing appropriate facts concerning the position in relation to capital and loan subscriptions, that the assessment in respect of the 1984 income year was excessive. In the circumstances, we consider that the respondent correctly treated one-half only of the 1984 net income of $7,534, viz. $3,767, as being available to the respondent as a deduction.
  2. For the above reasons, we would affirm the objection decisions in relation to the assessments in issue in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1981, 1982 and 1984.
