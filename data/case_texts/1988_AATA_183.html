Re Divers Applicants and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 183 (17 June 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: DIVERS APPLICANTS

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Nos. NT85/3879; 4917; 4934; 4970; 5058; 5071; 5080; 5084; 5106; 5107; 5255;

5847; 6011; 6016; NT86/1--3; 1288; 1302; 2465; 2805; 3080; 11626; 12355;

NT87/7720-1; 7771-4

AAT No. 4428

Practice and Procedure

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Practice and Procedure - taxation Appeals Division - Applicants not appearing at Preliminary Conference - whether Tribunal should dismiss - special considerations applicable to Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act assessments.

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 - ss.5(1)(f), 5(2)(f).

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax-Vendors) Act 1982.

Companies Code - s.456(4).

Marsh & Anor. v. F.C.T. 85 ATC 4345.

Case U78 87 ATC 464.

HEARING

SYDNEY

17:6:1988

ORDER

The Applicant having failed to appear at a Preliminary Conference, the Tribunal dismisses the application for review without proceeding to review the decision in respect of which the application was made.

DECISION

These reasons for decision relate to applications brought before the Tribunal by 24 different companies. Although each Applicant was a distinct legal entity, there were many common features to the applications. In each instance the Tribunal, according to its own records, has forwarded notices to the Applicants at the address for service provided by the Commissioner to the Tribunal at the time of submitting formal documents. The notices called on the Applicants to attend before the Tribunal in Preliminary Conference. In each instance the Applicant has failed to be represented at the appointed time and place. In similar circumstances I have previously dismissed many such matters but, having reserved a decision on an application to dismiss for non-attendance at the appointed time and place for hearing, and having become aware of the occurrence of many incidents as referred to in the course of the reasons for decision on that application (cf. reasons for decision in NT86/7928 - handed down this day) I reserved these applications also.

2. In each of these cases, just as with any situation in which an Applicant fails to appear at either a Preliminary Conference or a hearing, I acknowledge the possibility that that absence may be occasioned by any of the factors I referred to in those reasons for decision. But for factors yet to be mentioned, a "de-listing" direction might be appropriate (cf. pa.5 - post).

3. The common circumstances which touch the present applications are:-

1. all of the assessments arise out of the provisions

of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act

1982 ("the T(UCT)A Act");

2. all of the objections are the objections of the

target company;

3. all of the objections are in common form;

4. all of the objections claim that the Commissioner

erred in failing to allow to the Applicant a

claimed deduction for moneys paid to the

"Children's Assistance Fund" and no other claim;

5. all objections and requests for reference were

lodged from the same office; and

6. all Applicants provided the same address for

service.

4. Drawing upon my own experience in dealing with applications flowing out of the T(UCT)A Act, I take into account the circumstance that, as yet, despite all the Tribunal's efforts to provide opportunities for the hearing and determination of such issues as have been raised in the name of other target companies, no-one has yet availed themselves of the opportunity to proceed. Indeed, my belief is that no target company claiming a deduction in relation to moneys paid to Children's Assistance Fund has ever been represented before the Tribunal. In addition, to date no representative vendor who had objected to the non-allowance of the claimed deduction by the related target company, and who has been provided with an opportunity to proceed to hearing, has availed himself of that opportunity.

5. There is yet another matter of particular significance. It would not be appropriate to only remove the applications from the list of matters awaiting hearing, as if thereby the Tribunal had discharged its responsibilities in the matter. There is a factor which distinguishes matters arising under the T(UCT)A Act in this way from the vast mass of applications to the Taxation Appeals Division. The difference lies in this: that, by force of ss.5(1)(f) and 5(2)(f) of the T(UCT)A Act, the Commissioner is not able to assess any taxpayer as liable to pay vendor recoupment tax imposed under the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax-Vendors) Act 1982 until such time as "the period for objecting against the assessment (of the target company) has expired and any objection (by that company or by any other person) against the assessment has been finalised".

6. In relation to 20 in number of the Applicants, I also take into account the circumstance that the Commissioner's representative has presented to the Tribunal a record obtained from the Corporate Affairs Office, New South Wales, or given evidence of such a record, indicating that in each of the cases mentioned, a company identical in name to the Applicant company has been struck off the register (s.456(4) Companies Code 1982). Accepting as I do that that information is correct, then at least in the absence of reinstatement to the register pursuant to the provisions of ss. 456(5) or (6) of the Code, there is no longer in existence an Applicant competent to proceed with the application. (Marsh & Anor. v. F.C.T. 85 ATC 4345; and Case U78 87 ATC 464).

7. That being so, each objection came to be "finalised" with the striking-off order taking effect (Marsh - ante). But whether that be so or not, in order that there may be no doubts but that the applications have been "finalised", I will direct that in each instance the application be dismissed.

8. In relation to the applications of two other Applicants (NT85/5255 and NT86/3080), the circumstances are identical to those attributed to the last-mentioned group, save that there is no evidence before the Tribunal indicating that those companies have been struck off the register. It may be that they remain on the register in New South Wales or it may be that they were incorporated on a different register and have been struck off on that register. In two other cases (NT85/5080 and NT86/2465) the evidence was that the companies' names remain on the register in New South Wales. Nonetheless, I think the difference to be of insufficient significance to cause me to come to any other conclusion than that reached in relation to the former group. Accordingly, I will direct that these four applications be dismissed also.
