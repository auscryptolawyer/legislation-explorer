Carlyle and Commissioner for Superannuation [1989] AATA 324 (7 March 1989)

Last Updated: 26 March 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

SUPERANNUATION - injury to left ankle - overweight – whether real risk of injury due to such conditions

Superannuation Act, 1976 (Cth.) s.16(4)

CHARLES SELWYN CARLYLE

Applicant

COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

No N87/1126

AAT Decision No 5031

Deputy President C.J. Bannon, Q.C.

Mr R.N. Watterson (Senior Member)

Dr M.E.C. Thorpe (Member)

Sydney

7 March 1989

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) No N87/1126

) GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: CHARLES SELWYN CARLYLE

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Deputy President C.J. Bannon, Q.C.

Date : 7 March 1989

Place : Sydney

Decision : THAT, for reasons given orally at the hearing of this application, the decision under review:

(1) is affirmed with respect to the issuing of a benefit classification certificate pursuant to sub-s.16 of the Superannuation Act. 1976, (Cth.) ("the Act") specifying the condition, "Injury Left Ankle";

(2) is set aside with respect to the issuing of a benefit classification certificate pursuant to sub-s.16 of the Act specifying the condition,

"Obesity".

C. J. BANNON

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) No N87/1126

) GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: CHARLES SELWYN CARLYLE

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

CORRIGENDUM

Tribunal : Deputy President C.J. Bannon, Q.C.

Date : 17 April 1989

Place : Sydney

THAT the last word "Obesity" in paragraph (2) of the Decision dated 7 March 1989 be omitted and replaced with the word "Overweight".

C.J.BANNON

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) No. N87/1126

) GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: CHARLES SELWYN CARLYLE

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

7 March 1989 Deputy President C.J.Bannon. O.C.

Mr R.N. Watterson (Senior Member)

Dr M.E.C. Thorpe (Member)

At the conclusion of the hearing of this application for review, the Tribunal stated orally the terms of the decision intended to be made, and the reasons therefor. Those reasons are set out below.

Mr Charles Selwyn Carlyle ("the applicant") was born on 14 June 1936 (T4, p.19). He joined the Commonwealth Public Service on 19 January 1985 as a temporary employee and became a permanent employee in July 1985. On 18 September 1985 a medical examination was carried out for the purposes of sub-s.16(2) of the Superannuation Act. 1976 (Cth.) ("the Act") and a Benefit Classification Certificate was issued.

That certificate notes two physical conditions as affecting the applicant, one being an injury to the left ankle and the other that he is overweight (T3, p. 18). The applicant sought a review of that decision and the matter has come before this Tribunal.

The applicant suffered a motorcycle accident when he was living in New Zealand, which resulted in a fractured left ankle. The injury occurred in about 1957, when Mr Carlyle was about 20 years of age. Although the applicant was taken to a hospital, he was not admitted as a patient. Eighteen months later an X-ray showed a fracture in the left ankle, an arthrodesis was performed and a screw inserted.

Dr M. Benanzio, an orthopaedic surgeon, very clearly explained that the arthrodesis was not perfect. One of the bones was properly screwed to another, but the other bones left some jagged edges and seemed to be held together with some fibrous tissue. However, the ankle has held well and the applicant has led an active life. He has been engaged in playing squash and bush walking and has suffered no practical injuries or disabilities as a result of that injury over the years, although he has given up snow skiing and ice skating.

The other condition claimed to be affecting the applicant was that he was "overweight". At one stage he had reached the weight of 97 kilograms, however, when he was medically examined for the Public Service at the age of forty nine, his weight was down to 87 kilograms and it is now down to 84 kilograms. The medical evidence before us suggests that it would not do any harm if he lost a few more kilograms. We have carried out some mathematical calculations which suggest 87 kilograms is close enough to 13 and a half stone in the English system of weights. It does not appear to us that that is an excessive weight for the applicant to be carrying. Many a man who has been fat and jolly has weighed a great deal more than that, and apart from Shakespeare's Julius Caesar's dislike of Cassius, it would be a sad thing if the Commonwealth Public Service was peopled only with people of a lean and hungry look.

The respondent has very fairly, in my view, not pressed that ground, and having listened to the medical evidence, we are of the opinion that the overweight condition listed on the Benefit Classification Certificate should be removed.

As to the injured left ankle, having listened to Dr Benanzio, we were inclined to the view that it should not be removed from the Benefit Classification Certificate. Dr R. Pillemer (F.R.C.S.), in his medical report, Exhibit C, took the view that there is "a small but identifiable risk" with respect to the applicant's ankle. During evidence he spoke further about the condition of osteoarthritis in the ankle being somewhat advanced. However, we prefer the view expressed by Dr H.G. Marsh and associates that the arthritis was in a benign condition. He consider that although there is not a great risk, there is a real risk that something might happen to the applicant's ankle, so that he might suffer another injury which could affect him and prevent him from working.

In dealing with this matter it has to be borne in mind that the Tribunal has to be fair both to the employer and to the employee. We do not consider it a great risk, but nevertheless we believe there is some real risk that that ankle could deteriorate.

The respondent has explained that a Benefit Classification Certificate which has such a condition noted will not prevent the applicant receiving his full superannuation if he attains the age of 65 and is still employed in the Public Service. The Benefit Classification Certificate would only act against the applicant if he is retired from the Public Service because of an injury to his ankle. In these circumstances we are satisfied that the words of s.16(4) of the Act do not require any elaboration from us, and we shall not elaborate any further upon the interesting discussions by Deputy Presidents Layton and Thompson as to the meaning of the section.

We note that the Commonwealth Commissioner for Superannuation is prepared to pay the costs of Dr Pillemer's attendance today. We do not propose to make any other order or recommendation as to costs.

For all the resons we have expounded, we conclude that the decision under review is affirmed with respect to the issuing of a Benefit Classification Certificate pursuant to sub-s.16 of the Act specifying the condition "Injury Left Ankle" but is set aside with respect to the issuing of a Benefit Classification Certificate specifying the condition "Overweight".
