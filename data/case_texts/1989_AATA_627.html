Last Updated: 17 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

INCOME TAX - Exempt Income - "scholarship" provided by employer Duty to study - No obligation to render services post-scholarship.

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) s. 23(2)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) Nos. NT87/6994-6995

)

Applicant

Respondent

AAT decision 5453

Date : 26 October 1989

Place : Sydney

Decision : The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

(Sgd) Peter M.

Roach Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. NT87/6994-6995

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

26 October 1989 Mr Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

In 1953, when the first major expansion of university education and training was under way, the Parliament amended the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") to provide that certain scholarship income of full-time students would be exempt from income tax.

2. During the academic year of 1978 (sic) the Applicant, who was a medical technologist, applied himself successfully full-time to the final year of a course of study with a tertiary institution. He graduated with the degree of Bachelor of Science. At the time he was a mature age student in his 32nd year. He was able to undertake the course because he had been successful when he applied for, and was granted, what the "grantor" described as a "scholarship". His eligibility arose because he was a member of the staff of a public hospital in New South Wales. One result of his successful application was that the authorities at the horoital at which he served released him from the duties of his employment at that hospital leaving him free to commit himself on a full-time basis to his studies. Furthermore, the Applicant was not under any obligation, express or implied, legal or moral, to return to ordinary duty in the service of that hospital upon the completion of the academic year.

3. The value to him of the "scholarship" was that he was provided with an income equal to the income he would have derived had he continued about his ordinary duties in the service of his hospital during the period of the scholarship, although without becoming entitled to any special allowances by way of overtime or any penalty rates. Monies were paid to him in the same periodic v cycle as salary had previously been paid to him by his employer institution. Furthermore, he received the monies directly from his employer, and he received them after the deduction of tax instalment deductions which the employer considered itself obliged to deduct. Whether the employer was correct in so doing lies close to the heart of the matter. But that circumstance cannot of itself be a determinant of the present application. It follows that nothing was received directly by the Applicant from the "grantor" of the scholarship. Why that was so should become clear upon considering the relationship between the two institutions recognised by the Applicant as being both legally and practically independent of each other:- his employer (the public hospital) and his benefactor ("Health Commission of New South Wales"). hospital was a distinct legal entity from the Health Commission. They were probably correct for, at the material times, it appears from the provisions of the Health Commission Act (N.S.W. ) that the Commission had a general mandate to superintend and organise the provision of hospitals and health services in New South Wales, doing so by organising the provision of those facilities by others. I propose to resolve the question by presuming the assumption to be correct. That being so, when the Health Commission promoted the "scholarships" it was doing so in the discharge of its general duties. Its aim, by the provision of scholarships, was to contribute to an improvement in the level of qualifications of those engaged in the provision of health services. So it was that, when the Applicant made application for the scholarship, he applied to the "Staff Development Division" of the Health Commission. His application was successful. He then had to seek, and upon seeking was granted, leave of absence by the hospital.

  1. But he did not receive anything from the Health Commission. What he claims to have been "scholarship" income came to him by way of payments directly from his employer. No doubt the hospital had had financial provision made for it by the Health Commission to compensate it for the additional expenses it would incur by reason of the absence of the Applicant from his ordinary duties.
  2. It follows that, if it is appropriate to characterise the monies received during the period of study as "scholarship" income, it must be accepted that it was the employer who provided that income. That does not mean of itself that an employer could not be the grantor of a scholarship to an employee or that such a scholarship could not generate exempt income. But, in the circumstances of the present case I am not persuaded that it is appropriate to so characterise the payments received.

7. My conclusion, upon the entirety of the evidence, is that it is to be recognised that the benefits enjoyed by the Applicant during that academic year were made possible by the initiatives of the Health Commission. It promoted the idea of providing financial assistance for employees to study; it secured the co-operation of the hospital by ensuring that it would grant the Applicant leave of absence from his ordinary hospital duties; and it did so by making financial provision in its grants to the hospital to enable it to fund the income to be paid to the Applicant when not rendering service directly to the hospital at the hospital. But, as between the Applicant and the hospital, what happened was that the hospital granted the Applicant relief from his ordinary duties of work to enable him to study, doing so on the basis that while he was studying, the hospital would continue to pay him his base salary. Meanwhile, he continued to be recognised as, and to be, a member of the staff of the hospital and continued to accrue entitlements proper to employees of the hospital. But, although he was without obligation to perform his ordinary duties at the hospital, I am not persuaded that he was without obligation to the hospital.' I am satisfied that he had an on-going obligation during the academic year to the hospital to apply himself to his studies. I am satisfied that had he not so applied himself - had he been so foolish as to treat the benefaction as an opportunity to take a "paid holiday" - the hospital would have been under no obligation to continue its financial support.

8. I am satisfied that, although the use of the term "scholarship" may have been quite appropriate to describe the study opportunities provided to the Applicant, that use of the term cannot be determinative of the question to be resolved before me. The Applicant will only be entitled to the relief he seeks if he satisfies me that the income in question falls within the scope with Section 23 of the Act insofar as it provides:

"The following income shall be exempt from income tax:

...

(z) Income derived by way of a scholarship... bursary or other educational allowance by a student receiving full time education at a school, college or university, but not including

(i) an amount received by the student from a person or authority upon condition that the student will (or will, if required) render, or continue to render, services to that person or authority;

..."

9.. In my view the Applicant must fail. For in my view the money was not to be described 'income derived by way of scholarship', but rather simply as remuneration paid by an enployer to an employee as employee to enable the employee to undertake studies in the interests, not only of the employee, but the employer. That conclusion accords with the view taken in case R31 (84 ATC 295). In that decision the Board found that the taxpayer was an employee of the Health Commission, rather than of the hospital. But, in my view, that finding makes no difference. In both instances the employer was the provider of the funds and the employment relationship continued and provided the foundation for the payments in question.

10. The decision of the Commissioner under review will be affirmed.
