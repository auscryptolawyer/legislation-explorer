Re Taxation Appeals [1991] AATA 5; 22 ATR 3028 (14 January 1991)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. VT88/464

AAT No. 6584

Income Tax

[1991] AATA 5; 22 ATR 3028

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

P.J.B. Burns(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - whether income returned by a family discretionary trust is assessable income of the trust, the taxpayer who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned or the net income of a partnership of the taxpayer and his wife - whether sec. 260 or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 is applicable - rate of additional tax imposed - deductibility of expenditure items.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 17, 19, 25, 51(1), 223, 227, 260, Part IVA

Foster v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 151 82 CLR 606

Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4851

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland; Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation; Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4765

Cyclone Scaffolding Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 87 ATC 4021

John v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4101

Jolley v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4197

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting 89 ATC 4358

Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4830

Case W 46 89 ATC 442, Case W 58 89 ATC 524

Income Tax Laws Amendment Bill (No. 2) 1981 - Second Reading Speech

Income Tax Laws Amendment Bill (No. 2) 1981 - Explanatory Memorandum IT 2121

HEARING

MELBOURNE

14:1:1991

ORDER

The Tribunal:

(i) sets aside the decisions under review and remits the matter to the Commissioner to allow deductions from the applicant's assessable income as agreed between the parties and set out in paragraph 23 of the statement of the Tribunal's reasons for this decision.

(ii) the culpability component in the additional tax imposed is to be calculated at the flat rate of 25%.

DECISION

The primary question at issue in these applications is as to whether income returned by a family discretionary trust assessed by the Commissioner of Taxation ("the Commissioner") to the applicant for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986 inclusive is assessable income of the trust, the applicant who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned or the net income of a partnership of the applicant and his wife.

2. Other matters in dispute which arise from the assessment of the income to the applicant are:

1. The deductibility of certain items of expenditure incurred during

the relevant years.

2. The powers of the Commissioner to amend the applicant's assessments

for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985.

3. The imposition of additional tax for incorrect returns.

3. At the hearing the applicant was represented by Mr T. Murphy of Counsel and the Commissioner by Mr G. Davies of Counsel. Three witnesses were called, namely the applicant, his accountant and tax adviser and an officer of the Australian Taxation Office ("the ATO").

4. On issuing amended assessments to the applicant for each of the abovementioned years of income the Commissioner advised him that sec. 260 and/or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") applies to include the gross income disclosed in the return of income of the trust in his assessable income. Further in the event that Part IVA of the Act applies the amounts are includible in his assessable income by virtue of the operation of secs. 17, 19 and 25 of the Act.

5. The applicant lodged valid objections against the amended assessments and on their disallowance by the Commissioner requested reference of the decision to the Tribunal.

6. The applicant and his wife were previously residents of New Zealand. They migrated to Australia arriving here on 23 October 1980. For some twelve and a half years before his arrival in Australia the applicant had been engaged in work associated with computers. For a period of approximately 3 months immediately prior to coming here this business had been conducted in partnership with his wife. After arriving in this country and as stated by him wishing to do things correctly and acting on advice from a friend he visited Miss P. a chartered accountant. As a result of her advice the applicant and his wife formed a partnership in what was stated to be a interim measure.

7. Miss P. said in evidence that she had gained some expertise in the computer field and that when approached by the applicant and his wife had advised them initially to commence business in partnership whilst they considered their future activities. These, she said, included the possible expansion of the business from consultancy work to the sale of computer software and hardware and the opening of a restaurant. She said that it was usual in the computer field for consultants to operate through a company as larger clients did not like dealing with individuals. Apart from that fact she enumerated other advantages of using a company as limited liability, and that better superannuation arrangements could be made. The trust she stated also provided a more convenient avenue for the introduction of other family members into the business.

8. A copy of the partnership return for the year of income ended 30 June 1981 lodged with the ATO was tendered in evidence. This return disclosed income from computer consultancy for the period 13 November to 11 May 1981 and it was stated that the net income was distributed equally between the partners. A note in the return stated that:

"The net assets of the partnership were sold to the A family

trust on the 11th May 1981 and the Partnership was dissolved at

that date."

9. In an enclosure forwarded with the return the following additional information was provided:

"30 June 1981

Additional information

(a) All contracts are entered into in the Partnership name. No

formal partnership agreement has been prepared or business name

registered.

(b) Partnership commenced on the 13 November 1980 and was dissolved

on the 11 May 1981.

(c) Name of bank account (applicant and wife). Name of bank -

Commonwealth Trading Bank of Australia. Date account opened - 17

November 1980. Both partners have authority to operate account.

(d) Capital for partnership provided from private sources.

(e) All contracts are entered into in the name of (applicant and

wife).

(f) The relationship of the partners is that of husband and wife.

Mr ............. services to the partnership involve computer

consultancy. Mrs ............ services to the partnership involve

the accounting and secretarial functions."

10. The ATO advised the partnership that it had been allocated a file number and a card containing that advice was tendered in evidence. Also tendered were the applicant's return of income for the year ended 30 June 1981 together with his assessment notice for that year which disclosed that his share of the partnership income had been returned by him and assessed accordingly.

11. The applicant's wife from whom he is now separated is presently residing in New Zealand and no evidence was given in respect of her return of income for that year.

12. In evidence the applicant stated that whilst the partnership was operating he performed all of the computer consultancy work and negotiated contracts with clients on its behalf including the fixing of hourly rates in respect of services to be performed by him. His wife, he said, took no part in these negotiations and merely kept the partnership accounts. Under cross examination he agreed with a suggestion by Mr Davies for the Commissioner that as they had three children at the time his wife's main duties would have entailed looking after them. Partnership assets which were later sold to the family trust consisted of a small computer, stationery and filing cabinets.

13. The trust referred to in the abovementioned return of income (A) family trust was settled on 11 May 1981 and the trustee company of the trust (C) was incorporated on 9 May 1981. Copies of the trust deed and the certificate of incorporation of the company "C" were tendered in evidence.

14. The trust deed was prepared by a firm of solicitors and gives the applicant wide powers. He was appointed both the guardian and appointor and as a result of being appointor had the power to remove and appoint any trustee. Beneficiaries were the applicant, his wife and their 3 children. The trustee has the discretion to distribute the income as it sees fit and also has the power to remove a person from the class of beneficiaries, to declare trusts in substitution for the trust set out in the document and to amend, revoke or add to the provisions of the deed. However under Clause 26/3 the trustee was not permitted to exercise any of those powers without giving the guardian or appointor seven clear days notice of its intention to exercise the powers.

15. Issued capital of "C" comprises two shares each of $1.00 paid up to that amount and now held by the applicant and one of his children. The share now held by the child was originally owned by the applicant's wife and transferred to their son in 1988. Both the applicant and his wife were directors of "C" and the applicant was also appointed secretary. He was also employed as a computer expert; in fact he performed all of the consultancy work undertaken and his wife performed secretarial duties. No formal contracts of employment between them and the company were tendered and examination of the minute book did not disclose any record of minutes of meetings in respect of their employment. In evidence the applicant thought that employment contracts had been prepared but his recollections were hazy. The applicant's wife ceased employment with "C" during the year of income ended 30 June 1985 and did not receive a salary from it in subsequent years.

16. A bundle of documents was tendered in evidence. These documents comprise invoices, cheque butts, bank statements and pro forma documents for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986 inclusive.

17. The documents disclosed that business dealings with clients were transacted in "C's" name and clients invoiced by it. In addition amongst the documents was a form of agreement dated 1 September 1985 used by "C" in contracting its services with a client. The applicant stated in evidence that this agreement was slightly more complex than the normal because it also included hours worked and the name of other consultants out in the field. Otherwise it was a typical example of the type of contract used by "C" with companies with whom it entered into contractual arrangements for the supply of computer consultancy services.

18. The reference to other consultants refers to the fact that in addition to the performance of computer consultancy work, "C" kept a file of other contractors and arrangements were made from time to time for their employment by clients on payment of a fee. These contractors were not employed by "C".

19. Contracts for the supply of services by the company were normally obtained through an agent. These services were performed on a project basis for large well known companies and involved programming as well as advice on the type of computer equipment which they should purchase. Contracts were generally long term and ranged in duration from several months up to almost two years. As was the case whilst the partnership was operating his wife's duties were confined to preparing letters and keeping accounts.

20. Further the only source of income earned by the company during the years under review was attributable to the activities of the applicant in the computer world.

21. It was also stated that it was intended that "C" should develop what was described as a "relational data base" which it is said makes programs simple and also to import calculators and other equipment but nothing came of those proposals. The applicant also stated that he established a data base of companies providing information such as the type of hardware, software they had and contact points and names of about 1,000 computer contractors.

22. An audit officer employed by the ATO was called to give evidence in respect of income tax ruling IT2121 dated 12 December 1984. This ruling relates to the diversion of personal exertion income to a family trust or other entity and followed the decision in Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4851. Because of difficulties encountered in administering the ruling in connection with the reopening of assessments the audit chief at the ATO sent a directive to the various audit areas. That directive stated that auditors were permitted to amend returns for three years prior to the issue of the ruling but for consistency of treatment as a general rule returns for the years prior to the year of income ended 30 June 1984 should not be amended.

23. At the hearing it was agreed between the parties that in the event that either sec. 260 or Part IVA applies that the following amounts should be allowed as deductions:

Year ended 30 June 1984 1985 1986

$13,326 $12,221 $9,007

24. In opening the case for the applicant Mr Murphy submitted that Part IVA of the Act does not apply and referred the Tribunal to the opening words of sec. 177D which reads as follows:

"This part applies to any scheme that has been or is entered

into after 27 May 1981."

25. He then submitted that if there was any scheme it was entered into prior to 27 May 1981 and specifically if at all on 11 May 1981, that being the date upon which the trust commenced to carry on business.

26. Mr Davies' submission for the Commissioner was based on the primary contention that sec. 260 applies but also in the alternative that Part IVA applies. He submitted that sec. 260 only applies to a contract agreement or arrangement entered into up until 27 May 1981 and that Part IVA is expressed to operate to schemes after that date. He also expressed the view that there can be an overlap between the two and on a given set of facts there may be an arrangement which was entered into prior to 27 May 1981 in respect of which sec. 260 may apply and also a scheme which has taken place after that date. The reason for that, he submitted, was because scheme under Part IVA is more widely defined than contract, agreement or arrangement and that it is defined specifically to include action, course of action or conduct.

27. Under sec. 260 he stated, the Commissioner seeks to attack the establishment of the trust and the employment contract that took place and the incorporation of the trustee company. In relation to the scheme he submitted it is more the course of action which takes place whereby the applicant each time that he negotiates a contract chooses to do so as an employee of the company. That he said is an action or a course of conduct that takes place after 27 May 1981 and therefore within Part IVA.

28. For reasons which are discussed later it is the finding of the Tribunal that sec. 260 of the Act applies and therefore further consideration of Counsels' submissions in respect of Part IVA is unnecessary.

29. Sec. 260 reads as follows:

"260.(1) Every contract, agreement, or arrangement made or

entered into, orally or in writing, whether before or after the

commencement of this Act, shall so far as it has or purports to

have the purpose or effect of in any way, directly or indirectly-

(a) altering the incidence of any income tax;

(b) relieving any person from liability to pay any income tax or

make any return;

(c) defeating, evading, or avoiding any duty or liability imposed

on any person by this Act; or

(d) preventing the operation of this Act in any respect,

be absolutely void, as against the Commissioner, or in regard

to any proceeding under this Act, but without prejudice to such

validity as it may have in any other respect or for any other

purpose.

(2) This section does not apply to any contract, agreement

or arrangement made or entered into after 27 May 1981."

30. In connection with the operation of sec. 260 Mr Davies referred the Tribunal to a number of cases namely, Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland, Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4765, Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4846, Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting 89 ATC 4358, Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4830.

31. It is unnecessary to set out in detail here the careful analysis of those cases which Mr Davies provided to the Tribunal. Suffice it to say that they clearly establish save in perhaps exceptional circumstances that an arrangement whereby personal exertion income of a taxpayer is diverted to a family trust established for the benefit of his family is caught by sec. 260.

32. Mr Murphy's first submission against the application of the section in this case was based upon his contention that it did not apply to every arrangement where some sort of tax benefit is obtained through ordinary family and business dealings and that it only applies where the transaction is entered into for the dominant purpose of obtaining a tax advantage. With respect to that contention Mr Davies referred the Tribunal to the comments of Gibbs C.J. in Gulland supra at page 4772 where he said:

"Secondly, the avoidance of tax (by which I mean to include any

of the purposes mentioned in sec. 260) need not be the sole

purpose of the arrangement."

And later on the same page where he says:

"If tax avoidance is one of the main purposes of the

arrangement in the sense that it is not inessential or merely

incidental, that is enough."

33. Secondly, Mr Murphy submitted that sec. 260 would not apply because if it is applied there must be something upon which tax may be levied. He referred to the case of John v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4101 at pages 4108 to 4109:

"Secondly, sec. 260 effects a fictitious annihilation of

contracts, agreements and arrangements. It does not proceed to

substitute an alternative basis on which tax should be

calculated. Of course, in some cases the annihilation of a

legal form will itself reveal the basis for the calculation of

tax. F.C. of T. v Gulland 85 ATC 4765; [1985] HCA 83; (1985) 160 CLR 55 was

such a case. There the annihilation of the arrangements in

question revealed the source of income as the personal

exertions of the taypayer respondents in the same form as had

existed prior to the arrangements which were held to offend

sec. 260."

34. He then submitted that if sec. 260 is applied and the arrangement is annihilated what is left is a partnership and on that basis on the evidence the applicant would have had a higher taxable income. There is therefore he concluded no avoidance of tax.

35. Before considering that aspect it is necessary to decide whether a partnership within the terms of sec. 6 of the Act subsisted prior to its purported dissolution on 11 May 1981 and if so whether the partnership was part of an agreement or arrangement caught by sec. 260 and struck down by that section as submitted by Mr Davies for the Commissioner.

36. Mr Murphy claimed that there was a partnership between the applicant and his wife and cited the decision of Jolley v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4197 as supporting that view. He referred the Tribunal to page 4203 where the Court said:

"It was not in dispute that a business was carried on with a

view to profit. Whether the business was carried on in

partnership was the remaining question to be answered in

determining whether there was a partnership in law. ...If that

finding could not be made by the Tribunal, it would be

necessary for it to determine whether there was an association

between the applicant and his wife pursuant to which they were

jointly entitled to the profits of the business, such a

circumstance being sufficient to satisfy the definition of

partnership for the purposes of the Act."

And at page 4205 where the Court said:

"Mrs Jolley's lack of participation in the management of the

business was in itself of no consequence. A partnership may

exist notwithstanding that a partner may have no interest in

anything but profits and may be denied a right of participation

in management (See Robert Coldstream Partnership v F.C. of T.

[1943] HCA 33; (1943) 68 CLR 391). The right to share in the profits of the

business is prima facie evidence of partnership although not

conclusive."

37. He then submitted that in this case there is the verbal agreement reached in Miss P.'s office between the applicant and his wife to carry on business in partnership.

38. The reality of the initial partnership as a legal entity was not seriously challenged by the Commissioner. The evidence disclosed that the taxpayer and his wife had agreed to operate as a partnership, they operated a joint bank account, all accounts and orders were sent in joint names and the parties had concluded, as between themselves, that whatever profits there were at the end of the trading year would be shared equally.

39. On those facts and in accordance with the principles set out in Jolley's Case (supra) I find that there was a partnership within the definition of sec. 6 of the Act up until its dissolution on 11 May 1981.

40. Mr Davies then submitted that in the event that the Tribunal accepted that there was a partnership in existence as at 10 May 1981 (the day immediately preceding its purported dissolution) the Commissioner relied on sec. 260 to strike down that arrangement.

41. Although Mr Davies could not provide the Tribunal with a precedent where a partnership had been struck down by sec. 260 he submitted that there was no reason why it could not be done and that the partnership arrangement in the instant case is precisely like the trust. There is a man earning income through a consultancy business which results solely from his efforts and the main purpose of entering into the partnership was to divert income from the husband to the husband and his wife.

42. On those facts there is little doubt that the purpose of establishing the partnership was to split the applicant's income so enabling the reduction in income tax otherwise payable if the income had been assessable solely to him.

43. I find that there are no particular circumstances that remove this case from the principles set out in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland, Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting, Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, supra and sec. 260 applies to void as against the Commissioner the trust arrangements.

44. I also find that for the reasons already set out that sec. 260 applies to void the partnership as against the Commissioner.

45. In the event that I am wrong in coming to the latter conclusion it is necessary to consider the manner in which sec. 260 would operate in relation to the partnership and its subsequent dissolution after the section is applied to void the trust arrangment.

46. As Gibbs C.J. said in Pincus's Case (supra) at pp 4776-4777,

"Section 260 renders an arrangement void as against the

Commissioner only in so far as it purports to have one of the

purposes or effects specified in the section. The dissolution

of the partnership, in itself, had no such purpose or effect,

but it formed an integral part of the arrangement to which sec.

260 applied... However what was left after the annihilation of

the arrangement was not the original partnership. The four

doctors were in fact no longer practising in partnership and

the section does not require or permit fiction to be

substituted for fact."

47. The Tribunal must therefore be satisfied that the partnership continued and that it has not come to an end by the operation of law. This is what the applicant has failed to do in this case. On the evidence, I am satisfied that what remains after the arrangement is annihilated does not satisfy the legal requirements of a partnership, viz an association of persons who, in relation to the outside world, have come together in order to carry on business for profit, or are "in receipt of income jointly". The joint bank account was closed and orders and accounts were channelled through the trust account. For good measure all of the income was derived from the applicant's personal exertion with minimal contribution by his wife. On either view the income is properly assessable to the applicant. Taxable incomes for the years under review should be recalculated allowing deductions from assessable income of $13,326, $12,221 and $9,007 in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1984, 1985 and 1986 respectively as agreed between the parties.

48. The next matter for consideration is as to whether the Commissioner's power to amend the applicant's returns of income for the years ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985 was precluded by sec. 170(3) of the Act as it applied during that year. At the time 170(3) read as follows:

"170(3) Where a taxpayer has made to the Commissioner a full

and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his

assessment, and an assessment is made after that disclosure, no

amendment of the assessment increasing the liability of the

taxpayer in any particular shall be made except to correct an

error in calculation or a mistake of fact; and no such

amendment shall be made after the expiration of 3 years from

the date upon which the tax became due and payable under that

assessment."

49. In support of his contention that there had been a true and full disclosure Mr Murphy cited Foster v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1951) 82 CLR 606, Cyclone Scaffolding Pty. Ltd. v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 87 ATC 4021, and Case W46 ATC 442.

50. As in Daniel's case supra, an examination of the applicant's personal return and the company and trust returns would not have disclosed to the Commissioner the details of the arrangement and the manner in which the income was earned. I find that there had not been a full and true disclosure of all material facts necessary for his assessment and that sec. 170(3) does not preclude the Commissioner from amending the assessments for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 1985.

51. Finally there is the matter of the additional tax imposed under sec. 223(1) of the Act. On his arrival in Australia the applicant sought professional advice from Miss P. as to the most appropriate way to conduct his business in Australia and as a result formed the partnership with his wife and later the company/trust arrangement. In those circumstances the degree of culpability is less than might otherwise be the case and the culpability component included in the additional tax imposed under sec. 223(1) should be reduced to a rate of 25% flat for each of the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986 inclusive.
