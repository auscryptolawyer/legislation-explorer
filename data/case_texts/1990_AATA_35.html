Re Taxation Appeals [1990] AATA 35; 21 ATR 3331 (19 February 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. QT85/2236

AAT No. 5790

Income Tax

[1990] AATA 35; 21 ATR 3331

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Hon Justice R.N.J. Purvis(1) (Presidential Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Determination pursuant to s.177F Income Tax Assessment Act - deduction not to be allowable - procedural defects alleged in assessment process - validity of assessment - notice of assessment - delegation by Commissioner of powers and functions - administration of the Act - authorization of officers not sub-delegation

Income Tax Assessment Act - ss.170(2), 175, 177, Parts IVA, V and VI

Taxation Administration Act - s.8

F.J. Bloemen Pty Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1981] HCA 27; 147 CLR 360

Commissioner of Taxation v Dalco - 90 ATC 4088

McAndrew v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 98 CLR 281

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Clarke [1927] HCA 49; 40 CLR 246

O'Reilly and Others v The State Bank of Victoria and Commissioners [1983] HCA 47; 153 CLR 1

Commissioners of Customs and Excise v Cure and Deeley Limited (1962) 1QB 340

Carltona Limited v Commissioners of Works (1943) 2 A11 ER 560

HEARING

BRISBANE

19:2:1990

ORDER

The application to set aside the assessment and the determination is dismissed.

DECISION

At the conclusion of the hearing of this application for review, the Hon. Mr Justice R.N.J. Purvis stated orally the terms of the decision intended to be made and the reasons therefore. Those reasons are set out below.

2. The applicant in these proceedings has raised as a preliminary issue the validity of the assessment the subject of the objection and of this application for review, and the validity of the determination purportedly made pursuant to s.177F of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

3. The said assessment issued under the hand of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Brisbane, on 2 April 1984. Together with the assessment there issued an income tax adjustment sheet which contained within it the following statement:

"It has been determined for the purpose of s.177F that

the whole of the deduction otherwise allowable to you

for your individual interest in the net loss of Film

Production Limited Partnership No. 170 shall not be

allowable as a deduction."

4. The statutory provision referred to in the adjustment sheet and reflected in the Notice of Assessment, s.177F, by ss.(1), provides that where a tax benefit - that is a tax benefit within the meaning of Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act - has been obtained, or would but for this section be obtained by a taxpayer in connection with a scheme to which this part applies, the Commissioner may, so far as is relevant to these proceedings, in the case of a tax benefit that is referable to a deduction or part of a deduction being allowable to the taxpayer in relation to the year of income, determine that the whole or a part of the deduction, or of the part of the deduction, as the case may be, shall not be allowable to the taxpayer in relation to that year of income. Where the Commissioner makes such a determination, he shall take such action as he considers necessary to give effect to that determination.

5. It is in respect of the making of that determination that the applicant in these proceedings raises the preliminary issue.

6. On behalf of the respondent, the Commissioner, it is submitted as a preliminary argument that it is not open to the applicant to challenge the making of the assessment in that the matters challenged relate to alleged procedural defects in the assessment process.

7. The provisions of ss.175 and 177 of the Assessment Act preclude the applicant from challenging the assessment. It is only questions affecting the substantive liability to tax and the quantum thereof that may be the subject of appeal.

8. S.175 of the Act states that the validity of an assessment shall not be affected by reason that any of the provision of the Act - that is the Assessment Act - have not been complied with. S.177, so far as it is relevant to these reasons, provides that the production of a Notice of Assessment or of a document under the hand of the Commissioner, a second Commissioner or a Deputy Commissioner, purporting to be a copy of a Notice of Assessment, shall be conclusive evidence of the due making of the assessment, and except in proceedings under Part V on a review or appeal relating to the assessment, that the amount and all the particulars of the assessment are correct.

9. The Notice of Assessment has been tendered in these proceedings as an exhibit and has thus been "produced" within the statutory meaning of that word.

10. The sections of the Act to which I have just referred have been considered in two relatively recent decisions of the High Court, namely, F.J. Bloemen Pty Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1981] HCA 27; 147 CLR 360, and more recently in The Commissioner of Taxation v Dalco, the decision and reasons therefore being handed down on 9 February 1990.

11. In Bloemen (supra), Mason and Wilson JJ, in their joint reasons for decision at p 375, made reference to the provisions of s.177 and Part V of the Act, and to what had been said by Taylor J. in McAndrew 98 CLR at 281, namely, that for the reasons therein expressed, s.177(1) was intended to make it impossible for a taxpayer, in proceedings other than by way of appeal, to challenge an assessment on any ground.

Their Honours continued by saying:

"This interpretation gives expression to the policy

which underlies, and is manifest in, the statutory

provisions. The effect of this policy is that, once

the Commissioner takes advantage of s.177(1) by

producing an appropriate document, the taxpayer is

precluded from contesting that the Commissioner has

made an assessment . . . . . . . . (he) is, of course,

entitled to dispute his substantive liability to tax in

appeal proceedings.

. . . . . . . . .

Although s.190(b) places the onus on a taxpayer upon a

reference or appeal of proving that the assessment is

excessive, it enables him to contest his substantive

liability to tax. It is then for the board upon a

reference or the court on an appeal, within the

framework of the taxpayer's objection, to ascertain

whether he is liable to tax and, if so, in what amount

the Part V procedures accordingly protect the taxpayer

and enable him to have his liability to tax determined.

. . . . . . . . .

It is true that Part VI contains large powers to enable

the recovery of tax; powers the exercise of which may

make life uncomfortable both for the taxpayer and

perhaps others who owe money to the taxpayer. So much

may be conceded, but the Act does not proceed upon the

hypothesis that the Commissioner will be motivated in

the exercise of his powers by improper or collateral

purposes. As Isaacs ACJ observed in Federal

Commissioner of Taxation v Clarke (41), after stating

that s.39 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1922-1925 (a

(provision analogous to s.177 of the Act) made the

assessment unchallengeable:

"The Act so far trusts the Commissioner and does

not contemplate, in my opinion, a curial diving

into the many official and confidential channels of

information to which the Commissioner may have

recourse to protect the Treasury."

Mason and Wilson JJ continued in Bloemen at 376 and said:

"It does not necessarily follow from what we have said

that the Act excludes the general jurisdiction of the

Supreme Court. Section 177(1) specifically operates by

compelling a court, for example the Supreme Court, in

the exercise of its jurisdiction to treat a notice of

assessment on its production as conclusive evidence

that the assessment has been duly made and thereby

foreclosing that issue. In theory s.177 leaves the

Supreme court with jurisdiction to decide whether an

assessment has been duly made in a case in which an

appropriate document is not produced.

. . . . . . . . .

It is not to be supposed that Parliament intended that

s.177(1) should have no application to the actual

making of the assessment, thereby compelling proof by

oral evidence in recovery proceedings that an

assessment had been made. The consequence is so

radical as to make the argument quite unacceptable."

12. In Dalco (supra) the reasons in Bloemen and other earlier decisions were considered. Toohey J said:

"S.177(1) then provided that the production of a notice

of assessment 'shall be conclusive evidence of the due

making of the assessment and (except in proceedings on

appeal against the assessment) that the amount and all

the particulars of the assessment are correct'. The

effect of sec.177(1) is that, on production of an

assessment, 'the taxpayer is precluded from contesting

that the Commissioner has made an assessment or that in

making the assessment he has complied with the

statutory formalities'."

13. In the course of his reasons, Brennan J, after referring to McAndrews' case, said:

"Thus it was held in McAndrews' case that it was open

to a taxpayer on appeal to challenge the fulfilment of

the conditions mentioned in s.170(2) (ie fraud or

evasion) governing the power of the Commissioner to

impose a tax liability by amendment of an assessment."

14. There is a similarity between the fulfilment of the conditions mentioned in ss.170(2) and the satisfaction of, or the fulfilling of, the conditions referred to in s.177D and F of the Assessment Act.

15. I consider accordingly, and for the reasons set forth above, that there is substance in the submission that has been made on behalf of the respondent in relation to the provisions of s.177 of the Assessment Act, and that the applicant is precluded from challenging the assessment as he has sought so to do in these proceedings. I would dismiss the application to invalidate the assessment consequent upon the determination.

16. But, be it that I might be mistaken in this regard, I will proceed and consider the issues raised as they relate to the making of the determination referred to in the adjustment sheet.

17. Purportedly pursuant to s.81 of the Taxation Administration Act, the Commissioner did, by instrument of delegation dated 13 November 1979, delegate to, and I quote:

" - the persons for the time being occupying, or

performing the duties, the offices of

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Sydney

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Parramatta

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Melbourne

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Brisbane

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Perth

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, Hobart,

such of my powers and functions under the Acts

specified in the Schedule to this Delegation, or under

those Acts as amended from time to time, as are

respectively specified in that schedule."

18. So far as the Income Tax Assessment Act is concerned, the delegation was as to, and I quote:

" - all of my powers and functions except those under -"

Thereafter, there was specified a number of sections and subsections of the Assessment Act to which the delegation did not run.

19. Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act in which is contained s.177F was not part of the Assessment Act in 1979, having been enacted in 1981. However, my attention has not been drawn to any amendment to the delegation to exclude S.177F from the powers and functions so delegated. The authority of the Commissioner to so delegate is conferred by s.8 of the Taxation Administration Act, ss.1 of which provides, and I quote:

"The Commissioner may, either generally or as otherwise

provided by the instrument of delegation, by writing

signed by the Commissioner, delegate to a Deputy

Commissioner or any other person all or any of the

Commissioner's powers or functions under a taxation law

or any other law of the Commonwealth or a Territory,

other than this power of delegation."

20. On 3 November 1983, Mr Levy, a Deputy Commissioner, signed a document purporting to be an authorization under the Income Tax Assessment Act, which document stated, and I quote the document:

"Mr Colin James Shawcross, Acting Senior Advising

Officer Class 9, Appeals Branch, is hereby authorised

to exercise the powers and functions under sub-section

177F(1) involving the making of determinations for the

purposes of Part IVA of the above Act and the taking of

such further action as may be necessary to give effect

to such determination. This authority is to be

exercised only in relation to participants in the UAA

film partnership arrangements."

21. By a determination made under date 5 January 1984, the Deputy Commissioner, Mr Levy, made a determination purportedly for the purposes of ss.177F(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. Such determination is in evidence and appears as an annexure to the affidavit of John Barry Cooper of 19 February 1990. The determination is under the hand of Mr Shawcross, Senior Advising Officer, the latter subscribing that the determination was "made and stamped by me".

22. On 6 March 1989, the Commissioner, pursuant to his power of general administration of the Act, purported to exercise an instrument of ratification, which instrument is in evidence. I need not read the details of the instrument of ratification into these reasons other than by noting that the Commissioner purported to ratify and confirm the determination of 5 January 1984 to which I have just made mention. It was submitted on behalf of the applicant that the delegation by the then Commissioner in 1979 was to the six Deputy Commissioners so identified "jointly", they then being required to exercise the powers and functions so delegated together and not severally.

23. The authorization of Mr Shawcross that I have recently mentioned was, it was submitted, a power or function so delegated and/or the determination under Part IVA was a power or function so delegated, the same having, however, been performed by Mr Levy alone. The Act or Acts were thence not performed pursuant to the delegation and were of no force or effect. It was further submitted that the authorization of Mr Shawcross by Mr Levy was, in fact, a sub-delegation, an act Mr Levy was not empowered to carry out.

24. The delegate of the Commissioner, it was submitted, did not himself have the power under s.8 of the Administration Act to delegate. Thus the determination of Mr Shawcross was a nullity, the determination of Mr Levy was the adoption of that of Mr Shawcross and was hence of no force or effect. It was finally submitted that there was no power in the Commissioner to ratify and confirm the determination.

25. As to the first of these submissions, the delegation of November 1979 is to be read as a whole. It is apparent from its face that there is reference to individual acts being done in pursuance of the delegation. The instrument provides that an act done in pursuance of the delegation may be reviewed and altered by the Commissioner of Taxation in instances where the act done does not affect the amount of tax payable and this within a period of three years commencing on the day on which the act was done. Again, where the act done affects the amount of tax, then it may be reviewed or altered within a period commencing on the day on which the act was done. There is reference to single acts being done and they being done on individual days.

26. The Acts detailed in the Schedule are varied and diverse. The Schedule does not only make reference to the Income Tax Assessment Act but extends over sales tax legislation, payroll tax legislation, estate duty, gift duty, stevedoring industry legislation and produce legislation. It is not conceivable that an authorization in respect of the many acts and functions to be performed under the legislation to which I have just made mention should be intended to require the persons, the Deputy Commissioners the subject of the delegation, to act collectively and jointly.

27. Accordingly, I am of the view that on a proper construction of the instrument of delegation, it is competent for one of the persons holding the office specified to exercise the delegated power or function.

28. I turn to the second submission, namely, that the empowering of Mr Shawcross was an act of sub-delegation and not of authorization. The authorization was to exercise powers and functions under s.177F(1), this entailing the making of a determination for the purpose of Part IVA and giving effect to it, included in such determination being the findings pursuant to s.177C and s.177D referable to "a tax benefit".

29. It is readily conceivable that a Commissioner or Deputy Commissioner would physically be unable to carry out the multitudinous number of functions incidental to the exercise of a power and the raising of assessments. The duties cast upon officers of the respondent was recently referred to in O'Reilly v The State Bank of Victoria and Commissioners [1983] HCA 47; 153 CLR 1. At 31, in the course of delivering his reasons for decision, Wilson J had this to say:

"No permanent head of a department in the Public

Service is expected to discharge personally all the

duties which are performed in his name and for which he

is accountable to the responsible Minister. I share

the view expressed by Sashs J. in Commissioners of

Customs and Excise v Cure and Deeley Ltd (87):

'The commissioners are in a position parallel to

that of the Ministers referred to in the judgment

of Lord Greene in the Carltona Case (88), in that

their functions are so multifarious that they could

never personally attend to them all, and the powers

given to them are normally exercised under their

authority by responsible officials of the

department.'"

His Honour continued at page 32 by saying:

"The question is whether the existence of the power of

delegation requires that the Commissioner or his

delegate must direct his mind personally to the

exercise of every power or function vested in him.

Stated in that way, in my opinion, the question admits

only of one answer. The practical administrative

necessity to allow a Deputy Commissioner to exercise

the powers delegated to him by the actions of officers

authorized by him is evident. The opposing argument

would oblige the Commissioner himself to delegate his

powers, not only to the Deputy Commissioners, but to a

host of departmental officers throughout Australia,

rendering each of them a Commissioner in his own right.

It would be wholly destructive of any semblance of

administrative order and efficiency."

30. At page 12 in his reasons for decision, the Chief Justice said:

" - it seems to me that there exists, as the Parliament

must have known, a practical necessity that the powers

conferred on the Commissioner by the Act should be

exercised by the officers of his Department who were

acting as his authorized agents."

31. For these reasons, I consider that it was competent for Mr Levy to authorize Mr Shawcross to act in accord with the document of 3 November 1983.

32. The third submission made by the applicant related to the act of ratification and the document raised to this end. It was submitted that the Commissioner does not have a power to ratify or confirm; there is not a statutory provision conferring this power upon him. Even if he does have the power, it is one that extends to prospective ratification and to ratification of some act which is valid and permits of such ratification. Thus, it was submitted, Mr Shawcross had to have the statutory power to make the determination before it could be the subject of any ratification.

33. In view of the findings that I have made and as they appear above, it is not necessary for the Tribunal to address this question. It was quite clearly stated by counsel appearing for the respondent Commissioner that the reason for the purported act of ratification was to forestall a finding that the act of Mr Shawcross in the first instance was without authority. I have found that he was so duly authorized. It is my view that the act of Mr Shawcross was with authority and was competent in the circumstances.

34. The application to set aside the assessment and the determination is dismissed.
