REASONS FOR DECISION

24 April 1987 Deputy President R.K. Todd

  1. This is the review of a decision of the respondent Commissioner to include in the assessable income of the applicant for 1983/84 an amount of $6,073 that she received from her employer while engaged in a full-time course of study at a College of Advanced Education ("CAE").
  2. It is contended for the applicant that the payments were exempt income under s.23 (z) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). The Commissioner contends that the amounts either were not derived by way of a scholarship or, alternatively, constituted amounts received upon condition that the student would render, or continue to render, services to the authority.
  3. The relevant parts of section 23(z) are as follows:

"23(z) income derived by way of a scholarship, bursary or other educational allowance or educational assistance (other than assistance provided by the Commonwealth for secondary education or assistance provided by the Commonwealth in connection with the education of isolated children) by a student receiving full-time education at a school, college or university, but not including -

(i) an amount received by the student from a person or authority upon condition that the student will (or will if required) render, or continue to render, services to that person or authority;".

  1. A claim by the applicant in relation to amounts paid in the first half of the 1982 academic year (fiscal 1981-82) was rejected by Taxation Board of Review (No.1) (Case R31, 84 ATC 295). That reference however was decided on the basis that it was a condition of the scholarship that the applicant return to work at the end of the 1982 academic year. As that condition had been fulfilled by the time the applicant commenced the 1983 academic year, payments received in the second half of which fell within fiscal 1983-84, the Tribunal must therefore decide the matter on the basis of a different fact situation. I should add that the 1982-83 year was not before the Tribunal as the objection had been lodged out of time.
  2. To assist the hearing of the matter, both sides agreed to accept that the evidence put before the Board of Review could be adopted as if it had been given to the Tribunal. In this matter, therefore, there is a combination of evidence given at the Board of Review hearing and of evidence given at the Tribunal hearing, the same two witnesses having been called in each case.
  3. In 1981, the applicant was employed as a Nursing Sister Grade 2 by the Health Commission ("the Commission") in a public hospital in a capital city. She applied, and was selected, for what was described as a "scholarship" to undertake a two-year Applied Science (Nursing) Course on a full-time basis at a CAE. This scholarship was provided by the Commission, and was open to the Commission's nurses and those employed at an independently run public hospital.
  4. The evidence was that while on the course the applicant had in accordance with the terms and conditions which applied to the course

been paid at the rate of $14,591 per annum, with \ or salary rate subject to National Wage Increases and to Arbitration decisions affecting the same rate in the Hospital Employees (Nursing Staff - ACT) Rates of Pay Award. This rate was equal to that of a Sister Grade 1

had all statutory College fees paid by the Commission

had superannuation and taxation installments deducted

continued to accrue salary increments, sick leave, recreation leave and long service leave. Under the terms of the guidelines for the course an officer undertaking it would have been entitled to maternity leave if that situation had arisen at all times been considered by the Commission to be an employee, her duties being however different from other employees, namely to attend the particular course at the CAE.

  1. It was also clear from the evidence that, as a two year scholarship recipient, the applicant was required to return to work for the Commission in the break between the two academic years, the factor which decided the previous case against her, and that the Commission hoped that she would return after the conclusion of the course, guaranteeing her employment at that time. There was, however, no condition express or implied that she had to return after the completion of the whole course. The highest terms in which the obligation was ever put by the witness from the Commission was that there was a "moral obligation" to return.
  2. The questions to be decided by the Tribunal are whether the amounts were so received by way of a scholarship etc. and, if so, whether or not they were received on condition that the applicant would, if required, render services to the Commission.
  3. The latter question may be disposed of briefly. In my opinion the condition that services be rendered had been discharged once the applicant returned to the course at the beginning of the academic year 1983. I would resolve this point in the applicant's favour.

11. The logically prior question, namely whether the amounts in question were received by way of a scholarship etc., is however another matter. The words "scholarship" and "bursary" have, in my opinion, a well understood meaning. In the Macquarie Dictionary the meanings given are:

"scholarship"

'3. The sum of money or aid granted to a scholar'

"scholar"

3. A student who because of merit etc. is granted money or other aid to pursue ... studies'

"bursary"

'2. A scholarship'.

On the face of it these meanings confirm the understanding, within the ordinary use of language, that a scholarship is a grant of money or aid for the purpose of study, and that those words do not comprehend a situation in which a person is released from the conventional duties of employment for the purpose of undertaking a course of study while remaining an employee. The words "other educational allowance or educational assistance" are wider, but are not, in my opinion, so wide as to embrace what is no more than the continuance of salary and the retention of other rights of employment during attendance at a course in substitution for the normal duties of employment formerly, and in all likelihood prospectively, performed and likely to be performed respectively. This is essentially the view adopted by Board of Review (No.1) in Case E3, 73 ATC 9 and, differently constituted, in Case J58, 77 ATC 499; and by Board of Review (No.2) in Case N28, 81 ATC 154.

12. In the first of those cases, a bank officer was granted a "full-time bursary" to undertake an honours degree course. The "bursary" carried full-time leave on full pay, payment of fees and a textbook allowance. During the leave the taxpayer continued to receive full salary and increments. The Board (Mr JL Burke, Chairman, Mr RC Smith QC and Mr RE O'Neill, Members) held that the payments were received by the taxpayer in his capacity as an employee rather than as a student. The amounts paid to the taxpayer, putting on one side the amounts paid as textbook allowances and university fees, had "... the character of salary paid to the taxpayer during a period of full-time leave, a period during which the employer-employee relationship subsisted and all employee benefits continued. Although, during the period of his leave, the taxpayer was a student for the purposes of s23(z) the salary payments received by him were so received in his capacity as an employee and not as a student".

  1. The second case involved an officer of the Attorney-General's Department who had attended a six-month legal workshop course at the Australian National University. The same issue arose as in Case E3, and the Board (now including Mr. Fairleigh Q.C. in place of Mr. Smith), came to the same conclusion. Similar reasoning was applied in the case before Board of Review (No 2) (Mr KP Brady, Chairman, Mr LC Voumard and Mr JE Stewart, Members), namely Case N28.
  2. I agree, with respect, with the reasoning and with the conclusions adopted in these three cases, and consider that they should be applied in the present case.
  3. The applicant's representative placed considerable stress on the fact that in her case before the Board of Review in relation to the 1981-82 year Mr BR Pape (Member) had said in paragraph 9 of his Reasons that "the evidence established that the sum of $4,618 was received by her in the capacity as a student ..." [underlining supplied]. In a written submission it was stated that as Mr Pape had said in the same paragraph "that the taxpayer was at all material times employed as an officer of the Commission" it "clearly [followed] that he [rejected] that payment was made to her in the course of her employment". I am afraid that, as I see it, this conclusion does not follow clearly or at all. In the paragraph in question Mr. Page was dealing with the point that at that stage the applicant was, while a student, subject to a condition that she would, if required, render or continue to render services to the Commission. He commenced paragraph 6 of his Reasons by finding that at all times the taxpayer was an officer of the Commission, a statement which he repeated in paragraph 9 itself. He found that "under the terms of the so-called 'scholarship' she was granted leave of absence from her duties as a Sister Grade 2 ... for the 1982 and 1983 academic years to undertake the course at a reduced salary rate ...". Since that case was decided on the point whether the applicant was subject to a condition requiring her to render, or continue to render, services to her employer, the question whether she received the amounts in question as an employee or as a student was not crucial. I have found in favour of the applicant on that point, and thus have clearly to face the problem faced by the Boards of Review in the three cases mentioned above. I find that while the applicant was a student in the academic year 1983 she was continuing to receive salary in her capacity as an employee of the Commission and not as a scholar in receipt of a scholarship.

16. The objection decision under review will be affirmed.
