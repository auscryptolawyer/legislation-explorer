Gladys May Mclean and Commissioner of Superannuation No: A92/13 Aat [1993] AATA 343 (15 November 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GLADYS MAY MCLEAN v. COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No: A92/13

AAT No. 9123

Number of pages - 21

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

G. ETTINGER (Senior Member), D. TRAVERS (Member) AND H. JULIAN (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - revocation of Benefit Classification Certificate - issue of new BCC specifying the conditions "anxiety\depression" - whether applicant failed to answer medical examination form properly or gave false or misleading information actual or constructive knowledge.

Public Service Act 1922 s 76W

Superannuation Act 1976 ss 16, 16AC, 66

Re Hocroft and Commissioner for Superannuation (1991) 23 ALD 369

Joel v Law Union and Crown Insurance Company (1908) 2 KB 863

Australian Government

Solicitors

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the decisions under review and remits the matter to the respondent for reconsideration in accordance with the following directions:

(i) The Benefit Classification Certificate specifying the condition "hypertension" issued on 10 September 1987 pursuant to section 16(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 is correct;

(ii) The said Benefit Classification Certificate is not to be revoked and no new certificate is to be issued; and

(iii) Pursuant to section 66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976, the Commissioner is not to be of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground of the applicant's retirement was caused, or substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the Benefit Classification Certificate issued pursuant to section 16(4) or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions.

DECISION

G. ETTINGER, D. TRAVERS AND H. JULIAN On 4 February 1992, the applicant lodged with the Tribunal an application for review of the following:

(a) A decision of the delegate of the Commissioner for

Superannuation dated 29 June 1990, to revoke a Benefit

Classification Certificate ("BCC") in force in respect of the

applicant dated 10 September 1987 and to issue in substitution

a new BCC specifying the condition "anxiety state" in addition

to the condition specified in the first-mentioned BCC, namely

"hypertension". That decision was varied by a delegate of the

Commissioner for Superannuation on 7 January 1992, who made a

determination to revoke the BCC and issue a substitute BCC

specifying the conditions "anxiety/depression" and

"hypertension"; and

(b) A decision of the delegate of the Commissioner for

Superannuation, dated 29 June 1990, that was affirmed on 7

January 1992 that the incapacity that was a ground for the

applicant's retirement was caused or was substantially

contributed to by a condition specified in the BCC or a

connected condition.

2. At the hearing, the applicant was represented by Mr D Peters of Counsel and the respondent was represented by Mr P Hanks of Counsel. Oral evidence was given by the applicant, the applicant's husband Mr B McLean, Dr J Foo, Dr H V Veness, Dr C Anantharajah and Dr T Lee. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (T1-T32) and the following exhibits:

ISSUES BEFORE THE TRIBUNAL

3. The relevant legislation in these proceedings is the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act"). The issues before the Tribunal are:

(a) Whether the applicant, pursuant to section 16AC of the Act,

failed to properly answer a question or gave false or

misleading answers in relation to questions asked of her for

the purposes of the issue of the BCC; and if so,

(b) Whether the BCC issued pursuant to section 16(4) of the Act

should have been revoked and a new one substituted to include

in addition to the condition "hypertension", any other

condition(s), namely "anxiety/depression"; and if so,

(c) Whether pursuant to section 66(2) of the Act, the

incapacity

which was the ground for the applicant's retirement was caused

or was substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental

condition(s) or by connected condition(s) specified in the BCC

issued pursuant to section 16AC(4) of the Act.

THE UNDISPUTED FACTS

4. The applicant was born on 6 April 1935. She commenced employment as a school ancillary with the ACT Schools Authority in 1977 and as a result of permanent appointment, she became an "eligible employee" for the purposes of the Act on 4 September 1986.

5. Pursuant to section 16(2) of the Act, upon being appointed a permanent employee, and in connection with the application to become an eligible employee for the issue of a Benefit Classification Certificate ("BCC"), the applicant was required to undergo an examination by an approved medical practitioner. On 29 July 1986, the applicant was examined by Dr M Rajasingham, a Commonwealth Medical Officer ("CMO").

6. On 29 July 1986, the applicant completed a Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History (T 7) in connection with the examination by Dr Rajasingham. In this form, the applicant provided details of her personal medical history.

7. In the Examining Medical Officer's Report Form of 29 July 1986 (T 7), submitted in conjunction with the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History, Dr Rajasingham opined that the applicant did not meet the General Medical Standard for reasons of hypertension. On 4 August 1986, Dr B Bromhead, a Senior Medical Officer ("SMO") concurred with Dr Rajasingham's opinion. Pursuant to section 16(3) of the Act, this report was submitted to the Commissioner for Superannuation and pursuant to section 16(4) of the Act, a BCC specifying the condition of "hypertension" was issued in respect of the applicant (T9) on 10 September 1987.

8. The applicant was reviewed by Dr K Boyapati (CMO) who, in a report dated 29 July 1989 (T13), stated that the applicant was suffering from "anxiety/depression - 100%" and was unfit for continued employment. Dr Boyapati recommended retirement on medical grounds. Dr D L Hanson (SMO) concurred with Dr Boyapati.

9. The applicant was retired pursuant to section 76W(1) of the Public Service Act 1922 on the grounds of invalidity, effective from 8 September 1989.

10. On 29 June 1990, a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation, pursuant to section 16(11) of the Act as it then was, revoked the BCC in force in respect of the applicant and issued a new BCC which specified the condition "anxiety state" in addition to the condition of "hypertension" which was specified in the revoked BCC.

11. On 7 January 1992, a delegate of the Commissioner varied the decision of 29 June 1990 by substituting a decision to issue pursuant to section 16AC(4) of the Act, a new BCC with effect from 10 September 1987 specifying the condition of "anxiety/depression" in addition to the condition of "hypertension".

EVENTS LEADING TO RETIREMENT

12. The events leading to the applicant's retirement are central to the issues in this case. It is necessary therefore, to examine the circumstances of the applicant's employment in relation to the initial issue by the Commissioner for Superannuation of the BCC, the subsequent revocation of that BCC, the substitution of a new BCC and the retirement of the applicant.

13. The applicant gave evidence that she was "happy" in her work at Melba High School during the period 1977 to 1985. However, in 1985 she began experiencing changes in her workplace. She told the Tribunal that at the beginning of the school year two new teachers arrived and her supervisor, Heather Douglas accepted a one year transfer whilst another teacher, Joy Garland, was designated "acting teacher-in-charge" for 1985. There were changes in routines and methods of teaching and the applicant was required to carry out extra functions for which she said she did not have the available time. When questioned as to how she felt about such changes, the applicant replied that she was "...quite angry".

14. On 27 February 1985 the applicant consulted a locum for her general practitioner Dr Foo, at the Scullin Health Centre in relation to tensions she was suffering. In clinical notes (exhibit R1), the doctor noted that the applicant was experiencing clashes with new staff at school and was "upset" and in tears. A medical certificate was provided, but in evidence the applicant was unable to specify a period for its issue. The applicant was granted sick leave for the period 11 March to 8 April 1985 for "illness".

15. On 12 March 1985, the applicant consulted her general practitioner, Dr Foo. In oral evidence the applicant agreed that she told Dr Foo that she was very distressed and was experiencing problems at school. On the advice of Dr Foo, whom the applicant said she "talked to as a friend", the applicant attended counselling sessions with Mr J Salt of the Scullin Health Centre on several occasions in March, April and September 1985. The applicant told the Tribunal that she "...just talked to him about general strategies for when (she) returned to work".

16. The applicant consulted Dr Foo on one other occasion in 1985 with complaints relating to stress. The applicant was however, unable to recall having told Dr Foo (as recorded in his clinical notes) in October 1985, that it was "...very stressful at work".

17. The applicant told the Tribunal she consulted the Schools Welfare officer in relation to the changes in her workplace in 1985, "...but nothing came of it".

18. The applicant told the Tribunal that her work "...more or less returned to how it had been previously", in 1986. She said she took one week's sick leave in April 1986 in order to go on holiday with her husband who was not well, but was not feeling "stressed" at the time. Although sick leave records of the applicant record the reason for the leave as "medical condition" (exhibit R6) the applicant maintained that it was for a holiday. She added that she had acted in higher duties during the year and enjoyed the work, and received a silver mug and citation as the best ancillary teacher at Melba High School.

19. During July 1986, the applicant was required to attend an examination by the Commonwealth Medical Officer and complete a Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History (T 7). In reference to this Statement, the applicant said at the time, she did not consider herself to have ever suffered from stress, any mental condition or emotional disturbance. Nor did she believe she had ever been treated for stress by a doctor. The applicant stated that she completed the medical questionnaire truthfully.

20. In 1987, the applicant took one year's leave without pay from her position at Melba High School and requested relief work within the school system which was granted. When questioned as to why she took leave, the applicant stated that she wished to spend more time with her husband, her son's studies for which she was paying, were completed, and she wished to avoid coming under the supervision of the teacher with whom she had experienced problems in 1985. She said although the relief work was harder and involved more responsibility, supervising handicapped children, she coped well and enjoyed it. The applicant's husband, Mr B McLean told the Tribunal in evidence that his wife was "...very interested in the work she was doing, and was happy in what she was doing".

21. The applicant took long service leave at the beginning of 1988. In oral evidence she said she did so to avoid returning to Melba High School. The applicant could not recall telling Dr Foo during consultations on 12 February, 24 March and 21 April 1988, that she was suffering from stress. According to the applicant, it was not until the second half of 1988 that she began to feel "intensely" about her situation. Mr McLean gave evidence that his wife started showing signs of distress towards the end of 1988 and in 1989.

22. On 11 August 1988, the applicant attended a medical examination with Dr Anantharajah, a CMO. The applicant could not recall having told Dr Anantharajah that she "...found it extremely stressful to go to work" or that she was "...very apprehensible" and "...feels she can't cope" (as recorded in Dr Anantharajah's notes (T8)). Although the applicant could not recall what she told Dr Anantharajah, she believed that in relaying her symptoms she may have used the word "stressed" mistakenly, intending instead to mean "distressed". In cross-examination, the applicant pointed to errors in the doctor's notes - in particular relating to dates and details as to her children.

23. In January 1989, the applicant consulted Dr Foo in relation to apprehensions that she held about returning to another school as a special teachers' assistant. In March 1989, Dr Foo referred the applicant to Dr T Lee, a psychiatrist. In evidence she disputed some of the information recorded in Dr Lee's reports, particularly that relating to her family history and immediate family relations.

24. In March 1989, the applicant submitted a claim for compensation. In a Compensation Claim form (exhibit R2) dated 10 March 1993, the applicant specified the injury sustained as "stress" and the date of the injury as 1985. In cross examination, the applicant stated that she nominated "stress" as the injury on the advice of the Schools Authority Welfare Officer. She added that:

"It was the Welfare Officer there that said I think we are

looking at compensation here, and requested that I write a

letter going back to 1985....well I was also in tears saying

'well what do I put here?' 'what do I do here?' and being told

what to do and I did it."

The Tribunal notes that in a handwritten statement of the applicant of 8 March 1989 (exhibit R2) attached to the compensation claim, the applicant refers to her problem as an "illness", the onset of which she attributes to 1985. The applicant told the Tribunal that this was an account of the incidents written in 1989 in hindsight and that her references to stress were again incorrectly used.

25. In conjunction with the compensation claim, the applicant also submitted a handwritten list of 10 March 1989 (exhibit R2) in which she detailed sick leave taken for reasons of stress for the period 1985 to 1989. The applicant nominated two periods in 1985 as stress related sick leave (11 March to 8 April 1985; 24 June to 28 June 1985), and an almost continuous period from 12 February 1988 to 30 April 1989. The Tribunal notes that this document, produced by the applicant does not list her as having taken sick leave for reasons of stress in either 1986 or 1987.

26. The Tribunal had before it a letter of Ms Jeannine Farrell, a co-worker of the applicant dated 30 June 1989 (exhibit R3) which was produced in support of the applicant's compensation claim. Ms Farrell stated:

"... (it is) apparent to me that the situation at Melba High

School during the larger part of 1985 had either created or

contributed to a stress problem which Gladys was unable to

mitigate without extended medical leave. Gladys confirmed my

estimation of the position at a later date."

The applicant told the Tribunal that the statement of Ms Farrell did not accurately reflect how she was feeling in 1985 and 1986, and was merely an expression of the author's opinion written in 1989. She also stated that she contacted Ms Farrell in 1989 and spoke to her about how she was feeling in 1989, not 1985.

MEDICAL EVIDENCE

27. Sick leave records of the applicant (exhibit R5) for the period prior to 1985, detail two instances of leave taken for reasons of "stress", one being "acute stress". The applicant's husband had a severe heart attack in 1978, for which she took nine working days leave. A short time later, on the day her husband was returning home from hospital, the applicant's father died. She took five days leave. In 1982, the applicant had a week of leave due to what was described as "stress", relating to events connected with her daughter. She also attended counselling.

28. The sick leave records show the applicant took sick leave on three occasions in 1985, for "illness" (11 March to 8 April 1985) and "sick(ness)" (24 to 28 June 1985; 5 to 6 December 1985). There were another six days recorded as sick leave during 1985 for keratoid treatment.

29. In 1986 the applicant took a total of 10 working days for "sick(ness)" or "medical condition." She also took two weeks leave on compensation leave for a lacerated finger.

30. In 1987, the applicant was on leave without pay, and doing relief work in a "special" school. There is no record before the Tribunal of sick leave taken.

31. Throughout 1988 and until the applicant's retirement in September 1989, (exhibit R5), there was considerable sick leave taken by the applicant.

32. The applicant first consulted Dr J Foo, her general practitioner, on 12 March 1985, in relation to the problems she was experiencing at school. In his clinical notes (exhibit R1) Dr Foo noted that applicant was "very distressed" about problems at school. A medical certificate was issued for the period 11 March until 8 April 1985. Dr Foo recommended to the applicant that she attend counselling sessions with Mr J Salt of the Scullin Health Centre. On the advice of Dr Foo, the applicant attended counselling sessions with Mr J Salt of the Scullin Health Centre on a number of occasions in March, April and September 1985. In his clinical notes, dated 25 March 1985, 12 April 1985 and 30 September 1985 (exhibit R1), Mr Salt indicated that the applicant was experiencing difficulties in her work, particularly with teachers in her department.

33. In oral evidence, Dr Foo stated that at no time did he indicate to the applicant that she was seeing the counsellor for medical reasons. In a letter dated 19 June 1992 (exhibit A1), Dr Foo advised that the counselling service provided by Mr Salt was intended for patients of the Scullin Health Centre "...who were not in any sense ill". Dr Foo stated that:

"This counselling was provided for patients who required help

to cope with the ordinary vicissitudes of life"

34. Dr Foo was again consulted by the applicant on 29 October 1985 and recorded in his clinical notes (exhibit R1) that the applicant complained of a "...full head all the time" and of it being very stressful at work. In oral evidence, the applicant denied ever using the expression "full head" and could not recall having told Dr Foo that it was very stressful at work at that time. Dr Foo responded in oral evidence that as the term "full head" was noted in quotation marks, it would have been supplied by the applicant. He added that the applicant would have told him it was very stressful at work on his prompting.

35. In a report dated 10 June 1992 (exhibit A1), Dr Foo stated that the applicant had consulted him in 1978, 1982 and 1985 with "stress" problems. However, in Dr Foo's opinion, the applicant was not in these instances "in any way suffering from 'anxiety/depression' as medically defined but was reacting to stressful life events in a manner that most of us would do". He referred to stressful family events. Dr Foo added:

"......she was not given any medication (tranquillisers or

antidepressants) which I would have probably done if I felt

that she was pathologically anxious or depressed".

The Tribunal notes he did not refer the applicant to a psychiatrist until March 1989.

36. In an earlier report dated 27 March 1990 (T20), Dr Foo stated that during the period 29 October 1985 until 12 February 1988, the applicant "...appeared to be coping". There is no record in Dr Foo's clinical notes of this time of the applicant complaining of stress. In oral evidence, Dr Foo opined that there would have been no reason for the applicant to believe that she was suffering from any nervous condition during this period. However the Tribunal notes that a report of Dr Foo dated 25 January 1989 produced for the applicant's compensation claim, referred to work related stress symptoms suffered by the applicant on 12 March 1985.

"She had continued to be affected by the episode with both

physical and mental symptoms. Her inability to work last year

(1988), was related to this"

37. The applicant consulted Dr Foo on numerous occasions in relation to work related problems from 1988 onwards. Clinical notes of 1988 record the applicant as suffering from "stress" or "stresses" from February 1988 onwards.

38. The Tribunal notes that notwithstanding certain contradictions between Dr Foo's written and oral evidence, he was the applicant's treating doctor for many years, and the conclusions the Tribunal draws are as follows:

a) Mr Salt of the Scullin Medical Centre was consulted to

assist with situational problems which had arisen in the

applicant's life.

b) Dr Foo's medical records show no reports of complaints of

stress by the applicant between early 1986 until early 1988.

c) Dr Foo did not diagnose "anxiety depression" as medically

defined in relation to the applicant until early 1989.

d) Dr Foo did not treat the applicant with medication for any

psychiatric illness and did not refer her to a psychiatrist

until March 1989.

39. The applicant attended an examination by Dr C Anantharajah, CMO,Class 1, on 11 August 1988. She concluded that the applicant was suffering from a "history of anxiety, stress" (T8). In a Form 14B dated 12 August 1988 (exhibit R5), Dr Anantharajah recommended that the applicant:

"...is unfit for continued employment in the nature of the

duties of the position presently occupied and should be

re-deployed on duties of the following nature: Fit to work as

school's (sic) assistant in a different school."

40. In 1989 the applicant was referred to Dr T Lee, a psychiatrist, by her general practitioner, Dr Foo. Dr Lee saw the applicant on three occasions in 1989 (15 March, 28 March and 1 May 1989). In a report dated 7 August 1989 (T12), Dr Lee diagnosed the applicant as suffering from "Anxiety Depressive Reaction". Dr Lee concluded that:

"It is my impression that Mrs McLean is no longer capable of

continuing work in her previous position because of extreme

nervousness, agitation, irrational fears and intense loss of

confidence."

In a later report dated 14 July 1990 (exhibit R6), Dr Lee stated that:

"...problems from her work commencing in 1985 generating

emotions of resentment and anger finally triggered off a

decompensation of her coping capacities and inability to

reintegrate back to normal functioning. The probable aetiology

of the condition seems to be the difficult work circumstances

in early 1985 and subsequently and patient's inability to

handle the stress generated by them, in the context of some

background emotional problems."

41. In oral evidence, Dr Lee stated that on the three occasions he saw the applicant she was a "...very unhappy woman who had a lot of difficulties but not perhaps tantamount in the initial period, to a psychiatric illness". He was of the opinion that the applicant did not believe she had a problem until 1988. Dr Lee was however, unable to comment on whether the applicant suffered from a medical condition prior to April 1986 as he said his opinion would only be based on the perceptions of a general practitioner.

42. When asked as to why the applicant may not have mentioned the effects of the events of 1985 during the 1986 medical examination conducted by the CMO, Dr Lee said:

"I believe that this lady actually has no conception of her

own, you know, difficulties at an emotional level and if

anything at all, you know - I do not know that she is a person

who would lie. I believe that she is a person who is honest and

perhaps to a fault...I think in my mind that she would not

outrightly go out and lie, you know in any form that she would

have filled in, you know, about herself."

43. In regard to the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History completed by the applicant (T 7), Dr Lee opined that the applicant's answer of "No" to question 61 - "Are you aware of any circumstances regarding your health which would interfere with the satisfactory discharge of the duties of the position you occupy or have applied for?" - was affected by her own perception of her problems. Similarly her response to question 67 -"Have been absent from work or full time education through illness or injury within the past two years" - "yes, flu ext (sic)", was the result, in Dr Lee's view, of the applicant's acknowledgment of "a trivial medical complaint". It was the opinion of Dr Lee that the applicant would not have perceived herself as weak or suffering from a nervous disorder, and if she had taken time off work, she would relate it to a physical rather than psychological problem. When asked in cross-examination whether there was any reason why the applicant there was any relevant information to offer as to her state of health, Dr Lee replied:

"I think she couldn't, you know, she is not of that mind of

perceiving that there were, you know, these emotional problems

with her. These are not tantamount to a disease condition or a

diagnosis. It is really a personality trait that made her

vulnerable under certain circumstances to pressures and

problems."

44. The applicant consulted Dr H V Veness, a consultant psychiatrist, on several occasions (12 June 1991; 8 August 1991; 23 October 1991 and 24 February 1992). In a report dated 11 May 1992 (Exhibit A2), Dr Veness concluded that:

"...Mrs McLean, back in 1986, did not regard herself as having

ever suffered from a nervous disorder, despite her troubles of

1985. She was not referred for any specific treatment for such

a condition until 1989. It was not until 1988 that the symptoms

became really prominent."

In his opinion, her "symptoms" prior to 1988 "...were those of any normal person under stress". In oral evidence, Dr Veness stated:

"...the '85 incident was a temporary situation. She was

reacting, yes, with symptoms that a doctor would say were

stress related, but there is no evidence that she had anything

in the nature of psychopathology, severe anxiety, crippling

depression - anything like that during those years of '86 and

'87."

He also stated that his distinction of the 1985 incident as a temporary situation was based upon a history of a "gap" in problems after the incidents of 1985 until 1988 and that had there been a continuation of symptoms between 1985 and 1988 his assessment would be that her anxiety state began in 1985, fluctuated and gradually worsened until 1988.

45. In regard to the questions presented to the applicant on the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History (T 7), Dr Veness in his report of 11 May 1992, was doubtful that the applicant was familiar with the terms used in that form and would not have regarded herself as suffering from anxiety state or depression. He also did not regard it as unreasonable for a person to answer "no" to question 3 of the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History regarding depression or difficulty in sleeping, as very few people had not experienced difficulty in sleeping or "depression" in the colloquial sense at some stage in their life. With regard to the applicant's visits to Dr Foo, Dr Veness stated that the applicant "...did what a lot of other people do from time to time - to discuss areas of worry in her life (in this case her work and her husband's health) with her general practitioner".

APPLICATION OF THE LAW

46. The Tribunal in this matter must consider several issues. First amongst these is whether the applicant's original BCC should have been revoked pursuant to section 16AC(4), and a new one substituted specifying the additional mental condition. Secondly, pursuant to section 66(2), whether the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement was caused or was substantially contributed to by a physical or mental condition(s) or connected condition(s) specified in the BCC in force at the time of her retirement.

Failure to answer a question properly or giving false or misleading information

47. Section 16AC of the Act provides:

16AC (1) In this section:

"condition" means a physical or mental condition;

"relevant person" means:

(a) .... ; or

(b) .....; or

(c) a person:

(i) who has ceased, because of death or retirement on the

ground of invalidity, to be an eligible employee before

attaining his or her maximum retiring age; and

(ii) whose period of contributory service is less than 20

years.

(2) Subsection (4) to (8), inclusive, apply where the

Commissioner is satisfied, in respect of a person who is a

relevant person:

(a) that:

(i) at or in connection with a medical examination that the

person was required under subsection 16(2) or (6) or 16AB(3) to

undergo; or

(ii) ...

the person failed to answer properly a question asked of him or

her or gave false or misleading information; and

(b) that, if the person had answered the question properly or

had not given false or misleading information:

(i) where there is in force, or there was in force immediately

before the person's retirement or death, a benefit

classification certificate in respect of the person - a

condition or conditions of the person not specified in the

certificate would be or would have been so specified; or

(ii) ...

(3) ...

(4) If subparagraph (2)(b)(i) or (3)(b)(i) applies, the

Commissioner shall revoke the certificate and issue in

substitution for it a new benefit classification certificate in

which the condition or conditions referred to in that

subparagraph is or are specified either in addition to, or

instead of, the condition, or some or all of the conditions,

specified in the revoked certificate.

(5) ...

(6) Except for the purposes of this section, a certificate

revoked under subsection (4) is taken never to have

been issued.

(7) A certificate issued under subsection (4) in substitution

for a revoked certificate is taken to have been issued on the

day on which, and under the provision under

which, the revoked certificate was issued.

(8) ...

(9) For the purposes of this section, a person answers a

question properly if, and only if, he or she gives in answer to

the question all the information that he or she could

reasonably be expected to give, on the basis of:

(a) his or her knowledge about the relevant matters; and

(b) the knowledge that, having regard to his or her knowledge

about the relevant matters, he or she could reasonably be

expected to have about those matters;

if he or she answered the question fully and truthfully on the

basis of the knowledge referred to in paragraphs (a) and (b).

(10) In subsection (9):

"relevant matters" in relation to a question asked of a person,

means:

(a) in any case - the matters that the person could reasonably

be expected to regard as relevant to answering the question;

and

(b) if the question is asked of the person:

(i) at or in connection with a medical examination that the

person was required under subsection 16(2) or (6) or 16AB(3) to

undergo; or

(ii) ... ; or

(iii) ...;

the person's medical history and past and present state of

physical and mental health.

The parties do not dispute that the applicant is a "relevant person" for the purpose of section 16AC.

48. The applicant's state of health and her perception of her medical history and state of health at the time of completing the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History (T 7) in 1986 are in issue before this Tribunal.

49. There are a number of questions in the applicant's Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History the answers to which are central to this case. The applicant in this statement, answered "no" to the following:

Are you now suffering from, or have you ever suffered from or

received treatment for any of the following conditions,

diseases, injuries or disabilities?

1. Nervous fatigue or feelings of weakness or tiredness

2. Anxiety state

3. Depression or difficulty in sleeping

4. Any other mental or nervous condition

59. Any complaint, injury, ache or pain not mentioned above

and

61. Are you aware of any circumstances regarding your health

which would interfere with the satisfactory discharge of the

duties of the position you occupy or have applied for?

62. Have you ever received treatment or sought advice regarding

your health from a person who is not a registered medical

practitioner?

The respondent contended that with regard to questions 1, 2, 3, 4, 59 and 61, the applicant should have answered yes in light of her experience with anxiety, stress and depression in 1978, 1982, and 1985. With regard to question 3, the respondent submitted that Dr Foo's clinical notes recorded insomnia in 1978 and 1980. The respondent also submitted that the answer to question 62 should also have been "yes", given that the applicant had attended the counsellor (a non-medically qualified practitioner).

50. The applicant answered "yes, flu ext (sic)" to:

Supplement to 67. Have you been absent from work or full-time

education through illness or injury within the past two years?

To questions 67, the respondent said the applicant did not answer adequately; there were absences from August 1984 to July 1986 not accounted for, and full disclosure of absences had not been made.

51. The applicant answered "yes, Dr Shepherd (removal of solar keratosis), Dr Foo (B.P.)" to:

Section 5. During the past 5 years have you ever had any

medical examination, advice or treatment, attended or been

admitted to hospital for treatment, or had any test such as

x-ray or electrocardiogram?

The respondent submitted, section 5 had been inadequately replied to, in regard to the applicant's consultation with medical practitioners from August 1981 to July 1986.

(i) The applicant's medical history and state of health

52. The applicant's representative submitted that at the time of the examination in 1986, the applicant did not suffer and had never suffered from a chronic or recurrent psychotic or psychoneurotic illness. In 1986 she did not suffer and had never suffered from a nervous, emotional or mental illness. Mr Peters submitted that the applicant had not been diagnosed as suffering from any of these conditions, nor had she required medical treatment for any emotional disturbance resulting from stress.

53. The respondent submitted that the applicant then had a mental condition which could be described as "anxiety/depression" or "history of anxiety" or "history of stress reaction".

54. The sick leave records of the applicant for the period prior to 1986, indicate two periods (in September 1978 and October 1982) when the applicant was absent for reasons of "stress". The Tribunal notes the applicant's evidence and that of her husband that in September 1978 Mr McLean suffered a heart attack and that her father died shortly afterwards, and that in 1982 her daughter was experiencing problems. The Tribunal is satisfied these were isolated instances, causing a reaction in the applicant not unexpected in the circumstances and that she was reacting to stressful life events.

55. The Tribunal notes the evidence of Dr Foo, the applicant's treating doctor. Although he records instances of "stress" in 1978, 1982 and 1985, the Tribunal is not satisfied that these amounted to a medical condition. The Tribunal notes in particular her treating doctor's opinion that these instances were merely a reaction to stressful life situations. It is also significant that in the period of early 1986 to early 1988, there is an absence in his records of complaints of stress or like symptoms. Dr Foo gave evidence that he prescribed no medication in relation to the applicant's problems, nor did he refer the applicant to a counsellor for medical reasons. He did not refer her to a psychiatrist until March 1989.

56. The medical evidence before this Tribunal does not indicate that the applicant was suffering in 1986 and the preceding period, from a medical condition of anxiety/depression.

57. The Tribunal notes that Dr Anantharajah, CMO examined the applicant on one occasion only, in August 1988, and for approximately twenty minutes. The Tribunal notes that the applicant pointed out inaccuracies in the details of the patient's history as recorded by Dr Anantharajah.

58. Dr Lee examined the applicant for the first time in 1989, and at that time diagnosed her as suffering from "Anxiety Depressive Reaction". The Tribunal notes he declined in oral evidence to give an opinion as to whether the applicant was suffering from a medical condition prior to 1986 as he said he would be relying on a general practitioners' opinion. The Tribunal accordingly notes Dr Lee's opinion regarding the applicant's condition in 1986 and prior.

59. In the opinion of Dr Veness, who initially examined the applicant in 1991, prior to 1988, the applicant was not suffering from a medical condition of severe anxiety or depression, and the incidents of 1985 were temporary only. The Tribunal notes that Dr Veness based this opinion on a history of an absence of problems after the incidents of early 1985 until early 1988.

(ii) The applicant's actual knowledge of her medical history and health, matters she ought to have regarded as relevant and what matters she could reasonably have been expected to regard as relevant to answering the questions asked

60. It is necessary for the Tribunal to determine the matter of the applicant's actual knowledge of her state of health at the time of completing the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History in 1986; in particular, whether the applicant believed she was suffering from anxiety or depression at that time.

61. The applicant's representative, Mr Peters submitted that the applicant had answered the questions contained in the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History truthfully, according to the knowledge she had at July 1986, and as a reasonable person possessed of that knowledge would have answered.

62. Mr Peters for the applicant submitted that questions 1, 2, 3, 4, and 59, must be read in the light of the heading, "Are you now suffering from, or have you ever suffered from, or received treatment for any of the following conditions, diseases, injuries or disabilities?" He further submitted that the words "suffer from" indicate a state of abnormality. Mr Peters submitted that question 61 is directed to conditions, diseases, injuries or disabilities in a medical sense which are not specifically covered by specific questions, but is not to be interpreted as a "dragnet" question covering "...anything and everything not specified". Similarly, the applicant's representative argued that question 62 can only be construed as referring to a condition, disease, injury or disability in the medical sense.

63. Mr Peters also submitted that Re Hocroft and Commissioner for Superannuation (1991) 23 ALD 369, had adopted the position of the court in Joel v Law Union and Crown Insurance Company (1908) 2 KB 863, that answers to a medical questionnaire should not be construed with hindsight.

64. The respondent submitted that the applicant had not met the standard of disclosure required by section 16AC. It was argued that the standard of disclosure is a high one which has been emphasised by the inclusion of subsections 16AC(9) and (10) in the Act. In the respondent's submission the applicant had failed to answer properly questions asked of her in a Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History (T 7) completed on 29 July 1986, namely questions 1, 2, 3, 4, 59, 61, 62, supplement to 67, and section 5.

65. In particular, it was argued by the respondent that pursuant to section 16AC(9), the applicant could reasonably be expected to have answered "yes" to questions 1, 2, 3, 4, 59, 61 in view of anxiety, stress and depression suffered as result of work-related stress in 1978, 1982 and 1985. It was also submitted by the respondent that the applicant could reasonably have been expected to answer section 5 and question 67 more adequately. According to the respondent, in not answering "yes" to the above questions, the applicant failed to properly answer, or gave false or misleading information in answer to those questions.

66. The Tribunal notes the evidence of the applicant that at the time of completing the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History, she did not consider herself to be suffering or to have suffered from the conditions alleged by the respondent.

67. Dr Foo's evidence has been that although he referred the applicant to Mr J Salt for counselling in 1985, at no time did he indicate to her that she was attending counselling for medical reasons. Neither did he refer her to a psychiatrist (until 1989), nor prescribe any medication to the applicant that might have led her to believe she was suffering from a medical condition such as anxiety or depression. The Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant did not attend counselling in 1985 for medical purposes.

68. In the opinion of Dr Lee, psychiatrist, the applicant is a person who is "...honest perhaps to a fault", who would not lie when completing a medical form about herself. It was also his opinion that the applicant did not believe she had a problem until 1988. Dr Veness, a psychiatrist also opined that the applicant did not regard herself as suffering from a nervous disorder prior to 1988. With regard to the applicant's medical condition and the state of her knowledge of it in 1986. The Tribunal notes that the applicant did not consult Dr Lee until 1989, and Dr Veness until 1991.

69. The Tribunal notes details of the compensation claim submitted by the applicant in 1989. The respondent brought to the Tribunal's attention that in this compensation claim, the applicant indicated that she was suffering from a condition of stress, the onset of which she attributed to 1985. The Tribunal accepts that the applicant is a truthful person, compliant and obedient to authority, and that she was assisted to complete the claim, accepting the advice given with regard to this. The Tribunal is mindful that these documents were produced in 1989, several years after the applicant completed the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History. It is not satisfied that it is a true reflection of the applicant's actual knowledge as to her state of health in 1986.

70. It is the view of the Tribunal that the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History is drafted in such broad terms that any person completing the form would have difficulty doing so, and would literally be required to reply "yes" to at least some of the questions. It is surely not the intention of the makers of this form to have persons answering question 20, for example, answer yes, if they occasionally had swelling of the feet or ankles, after a long car or plane trip.

71. The Tribunal accepts the evidence of the applicant with regard to knowledge of the state of her health.

Whether the incapacity which was the ground of the applicant's retirement was caused or substantially contributed to by a physical or mental condition(s) or connected condition(s) specified in the BCC issued pursuant to section 16(4).

The Tribunal, on the weight of the evidence, accepts that the applicant was not suffering from "anxiety/depression" in July 1986. It also accepts that in answering the questions asked of her in the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History, she conveyed what she could reasonably have known about her health in July 1986. The Tribunal accepts that the applicant's retirement in 1989, was due to the condition "anxiety/depression". The Tribunal finds that there was no connection between any condition, which caused or contributed substantially to the incapacity which resulted in the applicant's retirement and any condition, (namely hypertension), listed in the valid BCC.

CONCLUSIONS

72. In order to determine matters relating to the applicant's superannuation entitlement, the Tribunal must determine whether the applicant failed to disclose relevant matters of which she had actual or constructive knowledge when completing the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History. It is necessary therefore to have regard to the following: firstly, to the applicant's medical history, (her past and present state of health); secondly, the applicant's actual knowledge of her health at the time of the 1986 examination; thirdly, to the matters which the applicant could reasonably be expected to regard as relevant to answering the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History.

73. On the evidence before it, the Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant was not suffering from anxiety or depression, in the medical sense at July 1986 and in fact not until 1988. In reaching this conclusion, the Tribunal notes particularly the evidence of Dr Foo, the applicant's treating physician, who did not believe the applicant had a serious psychiatric problem until early in 1989. The oral evidence of the applicant, her husband, and Doctors Foo, and Veness support the contention that there was no history of anxiety or depression prior to 1988. Dr Lee was unable to comment. The events of 1978, 1982, 1985 appear to have been serious incidents in the applicant's life, but were described by the doctors as situational stresses, and reactions to stressful life events, and were dealt with by counselling. The Tribunal accepts that the above named events were dealt with by the applicant with her medical advisers' assistance, and that medically diagnosed anxiety/depression did not start before 1988.

74. On the evidence before the Tribunal, it accepts, that there was also a period, 1986 to 1988 in which the applicant did not have the medical condition anxiety/depression. Indeed she was recommended for a transfer to another school by Dr Anantharajah in 1988, and continued to work with handicapped children at a job the applicant described as harder than her normal work, throughout 1987. It was not until April 1988, that the applicant began to have considerable sick leave, and early 1989 that the applicant was referred to a psychiatrist in relation to difficulties she was experiencing.

75. The applicant's treating doctor has in his notes used the term "stress" for events in 1985 to explain how she was feeling, but the Tribunal is satisfied that her reference, or her treating doctor's reference to the term at this time was not intended to infer or define a medical condition. Rather it was intended to be interpreted in its everyday sense to mean difficulties relating to a new person in charge and consequent new procedures and a perceived downgrading of the applicant in her job.

76. The Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant did not believe she was suffering from a medical condition of anxiety or stress in July 1986 nor at any time prior to that date. The applicant was not prescribed medication, nor did she receive psychiatric treatment until 1989. Her treating doctor, Dr Foo gave evidence that at no time until 1989, did he give the applicant any reason to believe she was suffering from a medical condition of anxiety or stress. She could not therefore reasonably have been expected to have given any different replies with regard to a medical condition of anxiety or stress. There is also general agreement between Doctors Lee and Veness that the applicant would not have regarded herself as suffering from a nervous disorder until 1988.

77. The Tribunal finds that the applicant properly completed the Statement of Personal Medical, Superannuation and Other History. In the Tribunal's view, the applicant answered the appropriate questions fully and truthfully on the basis of all the information she could reasonably have been expected to give in 1986. Accordingly, the Tribunal finds that the delegate incorrectly revoked the BCC issued in respect of the applicant on 10 September 1987. The reissue of the BCC on 29 June 1990 specifying the condition "anxiety state" as well as "hypertension", and the BCC on 7 January 1992 specifying the condition of "anxiety/depression" in addition to "hypertension" should not have taken place.

78. With regard to section 66 of the Act, the Tribunal finds that the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement, was not a condition listed in the original BCC of 1987. Given the findings of this Tribunal that the applicant did not suffer from the psychiatric condition of "anxiety/depression" in 1986, and indeed not until after 1988, and that she did not know that she suffered from that condition until after that date, the original BCC should not have been revoked and reissued with the qualifications, "hypertension" plus "anxiety/depression".

79. The Tribunal therefore, sets aside the decisions under review and remits the matter to the respondent with the directions as follows:

(i) The Benefit Classification Certificate specifying the

condition "hypertension" issued on 10 September 1987 pursuant

to section 16(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 is correct;

(ii) The said Benefit Classification Certificate is not to be

revoked and no new certificate is to be issued; and

(iii) Pursuant to section 66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976,

the Commissioner is not to be of the opinion that the

incapacity which was the ground of the applicant's retirement

was caused, or substantially contributed to, by a physical or

mental condition or conditions specified in the Benefit

Classification Certificate issued pursuant to section 16(4) or

by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with

such a condition or such conditions.
