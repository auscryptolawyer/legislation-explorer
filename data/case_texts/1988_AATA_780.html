ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - ASSESSABLE INCOME - whether valid Trust created -whether certain transactions including loans and release a sham.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, sub-s51 (1)

Snook v London and West Riding Investments Ltd (1967) 2 QB 786

Applicant

REASONS FOR DECISION

20 December 1988 Deputy President RA Layton

Ms W Purcell (Senior Member)

Mr D Trowse (Member)

  1. This review concerns two references related to the years of income 1984 and 1985. The items disallowed by the Commissioner as deductions pursuant to sub-section 51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Assessment Act") in the year ending 30 June 1984 were an amount of $10,000 interest payable to the P Family Trust ("the Trust") and in the year ending 30 June 1985, a further amount of $5,150 interest payable to the Trust together with other miscellaneous matters. The applicant is not pursuing the latter matters disallowed and therefore the only items at issue are the amounts of interest allegedly due to the Trust.
  2. The Commissioner's assessment also included a penalty for late return in the income year 1984. The applicant does not dispute that a penalty is owing but argues the amount of the penalty would be reduced if the applicant is successful on the primary contention that the interest accrued to the Trust should be allowed as deductions pursuant to sub-s51(1) of the Assessment Act.
  3. At the hearing, the applicant was represented by her husband who is a solicitor. The respondent was represented by counsel. The evidence before the Tribunal comprised supplementary documents lodged with the Tribunal and further documentation was tendered during the course of the hearing. In addition the applicant gave oral evidence and called a solicitor, Mr W.
  4. In essence, the applicant claims that an amount of $200,000 borrowed from the Trust at an interest rate of 10% per annum was used temporarily in the gaining of assessable income and as such the interest accruing thereon during that period qualified for deduction in terms of sub-s51(1) of the Assessment Act. Notwithstanding a provision in the loan agreement that interest be paid on the last day of June in all years of the currency of the deed, it is noteworthy that this did not occur and in fact no amounts of interest have ever been paid. We also observed that the amount purportedly advanced by the Trust to the applicant on 5 January 1984 had as its source a "loan" by the applicant to the Trust of the same day. The "loan" to the Trust was forgiven by a Deed of Release also dated 5 January 1984. It should be recognised from the outset that no movement of funds resulted from these so-called transactions. Throughout this activity the moneys remained invested in the applicant's name with a building society.
  5. The Commissioner questions whether or not a valid trust had been established, and contends that even if such a trust existed the transactions were a sham and were devised for the purpose of avoiding the payment of income tax. In the further alternative, the Commissioner submits that any interest allegedly payable by the applicant to the Trust did not represent expenditure incurred in the years under review.
  6. This review is unusual in the extent to which there is a lack of original documentation. There were no original documents (including true copies of same) available to the Tribunal for the Trust deed, any of the loan agreements, the Deed of Release, nor the Change of Trustee agreement. Bearing in mind that the applicant has the onus pursuant to s190 of the Assessment Act, this lack of original documentation is significant in the circumstances of this case.
  7. The facts on the balance of probabilities are as follows. The applicant is an emigrant from Uganda and came to Australia in October 1972. A number of her relatives left Uganda at the same time and settled in either India or the United Kingdom. The relatives who settled in India needed support from either the applicant's family or from those relatives who emigrated to the United Kingdom.
  8. The applicant acquired home units in Australia which were either purchased or built in her name. These units were gradually sold and the proceeds placed in accounts in her name with a building society. There is a suggestion in the applicant's evidence that some of the proceeds of the unit sales may have been placed in a trading bank, however on the whole, her evidence is that most of the proceeds from the sale of the units were placed in the building society.
  9. Evidence was placed before us as to the accounts held in the name of the applicant with the building society (see Exhibit R1). Account no 815288-05 was opened on 6 August 1979 and as at 4 August 1983, the balance was $913.76 being the highest peak reached and by January 1984 it stood at $42.55. The account was still open as at 2 October 1984.
  10. Account no. 1983261-06, was opened on 1 August 1983 with a deposit of $25,000. A further deposit was made in September 1983 and the whole of the proceeds, namely $25,326.20, was withdrawn on 8 September 1983. We have no evidence as to what that money was used for.
  11. A third account, no. 2040658-05, was opened on 7 December 1983 with a deposit of $190,000. This amount remained in the account with a further deposit of $5,937.50 being made on 7 March 1984. The whole of the amount, namely $195,937.50, was withdrawn on 7 March 1984 and placed in Account No. 1983279-08. Over the period of 7 December 1983 until withdrawal on 7 March 1984, no interest was recorded as having accrued.
  12. Account, no 1983279-08 was opened on 7 March 1984 with the deposit of $195,937.50 from account no 2040658-05. That balance remained in the account (although a small amount was withdrawn in March 1984) and the interest accrued until 30 June 1984, was the sum of $5,589.22.
  13. Therefore so far as accounts held at the building society are concerned, as at December 1983, the applicant held a sum of $190,000 in round figures to her credit. We had no evidence as to the amount held in her name with the trading bank.
  14. The applicant's evidence is that she went to see a solicitor, Mr W, being a person previously known by herself and her husband. The applicant told Mr W that she had a sum of $200,000 which she wished to use to make provision for her family living in India. Mr W suggested that the best arrangement would be to set up a family trust in Cyprus and to appoint her sister and brother-in-law as trustees of the Trust. The applicant wished to invest the said $200,000 in Australia. Mr W suggested that after setting up a family trust in Cyprus she should loan the sum of $200,000 at interest to the Trust, then execute a Deed of Release so that the Trust was no longer required to repay either the capital or interest on the loan, and in turn, the Trust should loan the said amount back to the applicant at interest. The applicant agreed to these arrangements and left the details to Mr W Mr W gave evidence that thereafter he arranged for the Trust deed, two Memoranda of Loan and the Deed of Release to be drawn up. These documents after being signed by the applicant were forwarded for execution to a solicitor in Cyprus known to Mr W, which solicitor was named as the settlor of the Trust.
  15. Turning now to address these documents. A purported copy of the Trust deed was tendered as Exhibit A2. Mr W gave evidence that the back page of the copy was endorsed by him after he sighted the original document on a trip to Cyprus. The Trust deed in the opening line purports to be executed on the 5th day of January 1984 which date is typed in as though it were typed at the same time as its contents and not, for example, inserted after execution by the relevant parties. The copy deed purports to be an agreement between a Cypriot solicitor described as 'the settlor' and a Cypriot trustee company called Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd. The evidence of Mr W is that Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd is a well-known Cypriot trustee company similar to Elders Trustee Company in Australia. The Cypriot solicitor is one of the directors of that Cypriot trust company. Mr W did not know either the number nor the names of any other directors of that company.
  16. The copy Trust deed is in the usual form requiring an initial settlement fund payment of £100. The Trust is named and the numerous family beneficiaries are described. Paragraph 4(11)(g) of the agreement allows the trustee to "by writing waive or vary any right exercisable by it which arises from a failure to comply at or within the proper time with any term of any agreement for sale mortgage lease or other contract".
  17. The final page of the document provides for its execution. Opposite the phrase, "SIGNED SEALED AND DELIVERED by the said [Cypriot solicitor] in the presence of:-", the abbreviation "(Sgd)" appears, but there is no indication of any witness. Alongside the phrase "THE COMMON SEAL of INTER JURA CY (TRUSTS) LTD was hereunto affixed in the presence of:-", appears a ring with "LS" inside it, and next to the headings "Director" and "Secretary", the abbreviation "(Sgd)" appears.
  18. Mr W gave evidence that he would have placed these notations on Exhibit A2 after he had sighted the original, that the ringed letters "LS" stood for the company seal and that the director and secretary would have signed the document. When asked whether the name of the director who signed the document was the Cypriot solicitor, Mr W was unable to say whether that was so or not but thought it unlikely. There is no notation as to whether the agreement was stamped in a manner with which we are familiar in Australia and no evidence before us as to what formalities, if any, may have been required to formalise the document in Cyprus.
  19. The next document to which we will refer is a purported copy of a Memorandum of Loan also dated the 5th day of January 1984 between the applicant and the Trust (Exhibit A3). Again the date of execution is typed as though it was part of the contents. Again there appears on the second page the abbreviation "(Sgd)" opposite the place of execution of the lender, being the applicant. No notation as to any witness is shown. Opposite the signature of the trustee, being Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd appears the abbreviation "(Sgd)". There is no suggestion that a company seal was affixed nor that the document was signed by a director and secretary of the company.
  20. This memorandum purports to evidence terms and conditions of the loan, although para 1 states that the "receipt of which sum the Borrower acknowledges". In para3, there is an agreement for the lender to pay interest at the rate of 10% per annum on the last day of June in each year. The document itself states that the agreement is in accordance with the law of Cyprus.
  21. The third document purports to be a copy of a Deed of Release between the applicant and the Trust in which the applicant agrees to release the Trust from any obligation to repay the capital sum of $200,000 or any interest thereon. This document also bears the typed date of 5th day of January 1984 (Exhibit A4). Opposite the phrase "SIGNED, SEALED AND DELIVERED by the applicant", the abbreviation "(Sgd)" appears. Opposite the phrase "SIGNED SEALED AND DELIVERED by the Trustee", the name Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd, is typed in and under that name the abbreviation "(Sgd)". There is no indication as to who signed that document for the company nor does it purport to be executed under the common seal or signed by a director and secretary of the company.
  22. The final document is a Memorandum of Loan again dated 5 January 1984 (Exhibit A5). The copy document purports that the Trust lend the applicant $200,000 but that "Until payment to the Lender of the principal sum the Borrower will pay to the Lender on the last day of June (10%) per annum". Opposite the place for signature for the Trust company Mr W has endorsed the copy with "Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd (Sgd)". There is no indication of any witness nor by whom it was signed on behalf of that company, nor does it purport to be executed under common seal by a director and secretary. Opposite the name "Borrower", is the signature of the applicant as witnessed by another solicitor, Mr J.
  23. The evidence of the applicant is that she saw Mr W on several occasions and signed three documents being the two Memoranda of Loan and the Deed of Release, on 5 January 1984. There is no evidence which suggests that the documents had previously been executed by Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd before the applicant signed them.
  24. The applicant gave further evidence that as far as she could recollect the Trust deed was not executed until some time later (transcript pp36, 37).
  25. The evidence of Mr W was that all four documents were executed on or about 5 January 1984. He said that the documents were forwarded to the Cypriot lawyer for execution after the applicant had signed them and that the copies before the Tribunal were endorsed by him after he had sighted the originals some months later when he visited Cyprus. He was uncertain of the time of this visit and further, stated that his trip to Cyprus was not undergone for the specific purpose of checking those documents. His recollection was that he probably sighted the originals of the documents in the solicitor's office and then made the copies.
  26. There are some unsatisfactory aspects to Mr W's evidence and this is not to be taken as a reflection on his credibility but as to his recollection. Mr W cannot actually recall seeing the originals of the documents although, in his view, he would not have made "copies" of the exhibits if he had not sighted the originals. He was unable to say whether the "copies" were made in Cyprus or where the originals were located subsequently, but thought it possible that they had been sent to new trustees upon a change of trustees being executed between Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd and the sister and brother-in-law of the applicant in the United Kingdom. No document evidencing this change of trustee was before us, either the original or a copy, and we similarly have no evidence as to when that change of trustees occurred.
  27. Mr W indicated that the procedure he followed in setting up this Trust and the related agreements was similar to other trusts and documents prepared for other persons who sought to set up like overseas trusts. He prepared the documentation without charge because the applicant's husband was a solicitor, and after they were signed by the applicant and after his attendance in Cyprus he had no further role to play. Mr W could, therefore, give no evidence as to whether the trust fund settlement figure of £100 was paid nor whether any moneys were paid into a trust fund.
  28. The applicant gave evidence that she thought that a bank account had been opened in Cyprus, but could give no evidence as to any amounts which may have been paid into that account. There was no documentary evidence of such an account nor any documentary proof of the settlement money having been paid into such an account.
  29. There are therefore a number of deficiencies in the documentation and evidence. The first is the absence of the originals or true copies and the lack of specific and reliable evidence to indicate that the originals were appropriately signed and witnessed and enforceable according to Cypriot law (quite apart from the date of their execution other than the typed date which appears on each of the documents). Included in our concerns about the original documentation is whether or not the trustee company, Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd, executed the documents appropriately such that the original Trust was set up and further that the loan agreements not under seal are also valid documents. The doubts are further reinforced by the fact that the settlor was also a director of the trustee company and it is questionable as to whether the Cypriot solicitor signed the document not only as the settlor but also on behalf of the trustee, so that the parties to that agreement were not at arm's length.
  30. For these reasons, we are not satisfied that a trust was validly constituted and that the settlement fund of £100 was paid. The further complication is that we do not have before us any documentation as to the change in trustees whereby the applicant's sister and her brother-in-law apparently became trustees in lieu of Inter Jura Cy (Trusts) Ltd.
  31. The findings in the above two paragraphs alone would mean that the applicant would not be successful in her claim, as there would therefore be no legal liability for her to pay interest to a Fund which was not validly constituted. However, we will assume for the purposes of further argument that a valid trust was set up as in our view the applicant has further difficulties to overcome.
  32. The applicant's evidence is that she agreed to and understood the combination of documents to set up a family trust to which she loaned $200,000 with interest and then released the Trust from any obligation to pay either the loan or interest and then she took out a loan from the Trust for the same amount with the principal to be paid on demand and interest to be paid at the rate of 10% in June of each year.
  33. The applicant gave evidence that in January 1984, she and her husband were living at an address at B. and that she wished to sell that house. She was unable to sell it but in the meantime found a suitable house at K. which she wished to purchase. The purchase price for K. was $300,000 and in order to complete this acquisition, she required to call upon the $200,000 loan from the Trust and which still remained in the account with the building society. The house was purchased on 3 October 1984 and there is no evidence as to the source of the balance of moneys.
  34. The applicant stated that in about October 1984 she rang her sister and brother-in-law in the United Kingdom and advised them that she was unable to pay the interest to the Trust whereupon she said that the payment of interest was 'waived (transcript pp.53, 56 to 60). The applicant was very uncertain as to what interest was being waived, whether it was interest which accrued up until 30 June 1984, or whether it was the interest from June 1984 to October 1984 and beyond. We note, however, that the only interest payable as at October 1984 would have been the interest due up until June 1984. The only effective 'waiver' as at October 1984, could have been for that interest and not for any accrual from June 1984 to October 1984 which would not have been payable until June 1985.
  35. The applicant gave further evidence of another conversation with her sister and brother-in-law in July 1985 when again she telephoned them to say that she and her husband had difficulty in paying any interest to the Trust. The applicant gave evidence that the interest was "waived" and, in her view, it was a permanent waiver of any interest owed then or may become payable in the future. This evidence was later varied following an assertive and leading re-examination by her husband, but we do not accept the evidence which she gave on such re-examination to the extent that it varied from her previous evidence.
  36. The applicant did not sell the house at B. until October 1985.
  37. In summary, even if a valid trust had been constituted, there were never any moneys placed into a trust fund in consequence of the loan made by the applicant to the Trust (Exhibit A3) and therefore no subsequent receipt of the moneys from the Trust in accordance with the loan agreement from the Trust to the applicant (Exhibit A4). The moneys which were to comprise the substantial portion of the $200,000 trust fund remained in the building society accounts both before and after the setting up of the Trust. In those circumstances, we fail to see how the loan agreements were implemented. Our conclusion is that the loan of $200,000 never came into existence and on that basis the question of interest and its deductability does not arise.
    1. In view of our findings to this stage, the question of sham will be addressed in brief terms.
    2. In the well known decision in Snook v London and West Riding Investments Ltd (1967) 2 QB 786 at 802, Diplock LJ described a sham in the following manner:

"Acts done or documents executed by the parties to the sham which are intended by them to give to third parties or to the court the appearance of creating between the parties legal rights and obligations different from the actual legal rights and obligations (if any) which the parties intend to create ... For acts or documents to be a sham ... all the parties thereto must have a common intention that the acts or documents are not to create the legal rights and obligations which they give the appearance of creating."

  1. On the facts in this case, none of the documents which were allegedly executed between the parties were in fact acted upon. The moneys were not loaned by the applicant to the Trust, the Trust was never formed in that no settlement moneys were paid into any trust fund, and no interest was paid pursuant to any alleged loan between the applicant and the Trust. The parties seemed to completely disregard the documentation and merely proceeded to do what they wished with the $200,000, and with any interest which they received on that amount. Therefore, the documents were clearly, in our view, documents which were not intended to create binding legal rights and obligations between the parties, but merely to give that appearance. Further, the strong inference is that the documents would not have to be complied with if it was not convenient. In the meantime, however, the applicant intended to have the benefit of claiming a deduction for what purported to be a legal liability to pay interest on an alleged loan. We therefore consider that all of the purported transactions as recorded in the documentation were a sham in the circumstances.
  2. Even if the applicant could have overcome the difficulties of the existence of the Trust and related documents as well as the sham, she would still have encountered difficulties with the first limb of s51(1) of the Assessment Act. Under that limb, it is necessary to show a nexus between the incurred expenditure in the gaining or producing of assessable income. The assessable income which the applicant obtained in relation to the said $200,000 was interest of $5,589.22 as at 30 June 1984. This was admittedly on a sum of $190,000 and we do not know what interest may have accrued on any further amount of $10,000 held in an account other than the building society. It would therefore be necessary for the applicant to discharge the onus that the alleged interest of 10% payable to the Trust amounting to $10,000 in the year 1984, was necessarily incurred in the gaining or producing of assessable income, as distinct, for example, from being merely interest incurred to obtain a tax deduction to set off against income received by investment of that money. The applicant may well have foundered in discharging that onus.

42. In all of the circumstances, the objection decisions under review should be affirmed.
