Re S & T Income Tax Aid Specialists Pty Limited and Christopher Forward and Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales [1987] AATA 132 (9 April 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: S. & T. INCOME TAX AID SPECIALISTS PTY. LIMITED and CHRISTOPHER FORWARD

And: TAX AGENTS' BOARD, NEW SOUTH WALES

No. N86/255 and N86/256

AAT No. 3364

Tax Agent - Administrative Appeals Tribunal

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

C.J. Bannon QC (Deputy President)

C.J. Stevens (Member)

G.R. Taylor (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Tax Agent - board cancelled registration of applicant as a tax agent - whether nominees referred to in annual notices were "employed" by applicant - whether applicant exercised adequate "supervision and control" over employees - what is adequate supervision and control - whether applicant guilty of misconduct as a tax agent - whether applicant is a fit and proper person to be registered as a tax agent.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal - jurisdiction - secondnamed applicant nominee of tax agent - Board cancelled registration of secondnamed applicant as a nominee - s. 251J(11)(d) - whether AAT has power to review cancellation of registration of nominee - s. 251K(5) - AAT's jurisdiction relates to decisions affecting tax agents but not nominees.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.) ss. 251J, 251K, 251N.

Re Chapman and Tax Agents' Board of South Australia 84 ATC 4353

Fencott v. Muller (1983) 152 CLR 570

Zuijs v. Wirth Brothers Pty. Ltd. [1955] HCA 73; (1955) 93 CLR 561

Australian Mutual Provident Society v. Allan (1978) 52 ALJR 407

Hatton v. Beaumont (1977) 2 NSWLR 211

Re Su and Tax Agents' Board, South Australia 82 ATC 4284

Re Whatson and Tax Agents Board (1983) 5 ALN No. 48

Re Perusco and Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales 82 ATC 4695

Re Denton and Tax Agents' Board, South Australia 83 ATC 4009

HEARING

SYDNEY

9:4:1987

ORDER

THAT the decision to cancel the registration of S. & T. Income Tax Aid Specialists Pty. Limited be set aside.

DECISION

By consent two applications for review lodged by S. & T. Income Tax Aid Specialists Pty. Limited ("S. & T.") and Christopher Forward were heard together by this Tribunal. It is convenient to deal with both decisions at the same time.

RE: CHRISTOPHER FORWARD

2. Christopher Forward seeks a review of a decision by the respondent, Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales ("the Board") to cancel his registration as a nominee of the firstnamed applicant, S. & T. The Board served upon S. & T. a notice dated 23rd April 1986 (T11 pp. 27-29) stating, amongst other things, that at its meeting on 17th April 1986, the Board was of the opinion that Christopher Forward was no longer a fit and proper person to be a nominee of S. & T. and accordingly removed him as a nominee pursuant to the provisions of section 251J(11)(d) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.) ("the Act").

3. Mr Forward gave evidence before this Tribunal and was cross-examined. There was no suggestion that he was not a person of good fame and character. The chief matter relied upon was a failure to adequately supervise unqualified persons in the preparation of tax returns. Mr Forward may have possible grounds for seeking judicial review of the decision of the Board, but it appears clear to the Tribunal that it has no power under section 251K(5) or under any other section of the Act to review the same. Section 251K(5) provides for review of decisions affecting tax agents but not nominees. (See Re Chapman and Tax Agents' Board of South Australia 84 ATC 4353 at 4356).

4. It was faintly argued that the Tribunal enjoys an accrued jurisdiction similar to that of a court exercising Federal judicial power. However this Tribunal is not a court and the accrued jurisdiction of courts stemming from Chapter 3 of the Constitution has no relevance to a limited administrative body such as this. See Fencott v. Muller (1983) 152 CLR 570 at 603. The Tribunal therefore considers it has no jurisdiction to review the Board's decision removing Mr Forward as a nominee and his application is accordingly dismissed.

RE: S. & T. INCOME TAX AID SPECIALISTS PTY. LIMITED

5. S. & T. is a company of which Sarwat McGuid is the managing director and principal shareholder. Mr McGuid is an accountant who studied accountancy in Canada (obtaining a diploma in accounting from the University of Montreal) after migrating there from Egypt, which appears to be his native country. He came to Australia in 1973. According to his affidavit (Exhibit B) he has worked in the accounts department of the Belgium Bank in Cairo, as an accounts clerk and accounts supervisor for Affiliated Business Factors Corporation, Massey Ferguson, the Steinbergs chain of department stores, and also for H. & R. Block, Tax Agents, all in Canada. In Australia he became the New South Wales manager of H. & R. Block, then worked for ITP (another group of tax agents), commencing business with his own company, S. & T. in 1976. S. & T. now has 16 offices, some of which are franchised. It appears that although Mr McGuid has been doing evening courses at the University of New South Wales, his qualifications as an accountant have not yet been recognised by the Board.

6. In order for a company to operate as a tax agent, it is required in law to have one or more nominees employed by the company and registered by the Board. (Sections 251J(3) and 251K(4) of the Act). The Act does not spell out the qualifications of a nominee beyond the words of section 251J(6) which provides:-

"If the Board is satisfied that any person in respect of

whom a request is made under sub-section (5) is a fit

and proper person to prepare income tax returns and

transact income tax business on behalf of taxpayers, it

may register that person as an additional or

substituted nominee of the tax agent for the purposes

of this Part."

7. Some light on the nominee's duties is thrown by section 251N(1) and (2) which provides:-

"Sec. 251N(1)

A registered tax agent or a person exempted under

section 251L shall not allow any person, not being his

employee, a registered tax agent or, in the case of a

partnership which is registered as a tax agent, a member

of that partnership -

(a) to prepare on his behalf, either directly or

indirectly, his own or any other income tax return

or objection; or

(b) to conduct on his behalf, either directly or

indirectly, any business of himself or any other

person relating to any income tax return or income

tax matter.

Penalty: $1,000

Sec. 251N(2)

A partnership or company which is registered as a tax

agent shall not allow any person to do anything

specified in paragraph (1)(a) or (b) except under the

supervision and control of a nominee of the partnership

or company.

Penalty: $1,000."

8. On 23rd April 1986, the Board wrote to S. & T. a letter in the following terms (T94 p. 208-210):-

"The Board, at a meeting held on 17 April 1986, gave

consideration to the question of the continued

registration of S & T Income Tax Aid Specialists Pty

Ltd (The Company) as a Tax Agent, in the light of the

Board's letter of 22 October 1985.

Having regard to the information supplied by its

solicitors, Gilbert M. Johnstone & Co., in their letter

of 11 November 1985, and, in particular, the submission

in respect of the question relating to the Board

exercising its discretion pursuant to the provisions of

sub-section 251K(2), the Board has found that -

- The Company, by its managing director Mr Sawat

McGuid, in various applications for the

registration of additional or substituted nominees,

proposed persons who were not employees of the

Company in terms of sub-section 251J(5) of the

Income Tax Assessment Act (The Act);

- The Company, by its managing director Mr Sawat

McGuid, in various annual notices furnished

pursuant to Section 251JA of the Act, listed as

nominees a number of persons who were not employed

by the Company;

- The Company, by its managing director Mr Sawat

McGuid, allowed Mr Riad Taoube, a person not being

its employee, to prepare income tax returns on its

behalf in contravention of Section 251N(1) of the

Act;

- The Company, by its managing director Mr Sawat

McGuid, allowed Mr Riad Taoube to prepare income

tax returns whilst not under the control and

supervision of a nominee in contravention of

Section 251N(2) of the Act;

- The Company, by its managing director Mr Sawat

McGuid, generally allowed the preparation of income

tax returns or objections and the conduct of

business of taxpayers in income tax matters to be

carried out without being under the control and

supervision of a nominee in contravention of

Section 251N(2); and

- The Company, by its managing director Mr Sawat

McGuid, in various cases, caused the Tax Agent's

certificate on the front of income tax returns to

be completed in contravention of Section 165(1) of

the Act when in fact no fee had been charged by the

Company for preparing or assisting in the

preparation of those returns.

The Board was therefore satisfied that the Company was

guilty of misconduct as a Tax Agent and, in addition,

was no longer fit and proper to remain registered as a

Tax Agent.

Further, the Board was satisfied that Mr McGuid was, in

terms of Section 251K(2)(c), a person who was not of

good fame, integrity and character.

Further, the Board was satisfied that Mr Christopher

Forward was, in terms of Section 251K(2)(c), not fit

and proper to be a nominee of the Company.

Further, the Board was satisfied that in relation to

the nominees of the Company they were not exercising

control and supervision of the operations of the

Company in terms of Section 251N(2).

Having considered the submissions made on behalf of the

Company the Board nonetheless decided to exercise the

discretion bestowed upon it by Section 251K(2) and

cancel the registration of the Company pursuant to the

provisions of sub-paragraphs 251K(2)(b)(ii) & (iii) and

paragraph 251K(2)(c).

Notwithstanding the above, the Board was of the opinion

that Mr Christopher Forward, being a nominee of the

Company, was no longer a fit and proper person to be a

nominee of the Company and accordingly removed him as a

nominee pursuant to the provisions of Section

251J(11)(d).

Further, notwithstanding any of the above, in respect

of Messrs Albert Ferrazzano & Ray Dominello, the Board

was of the opinion that neither person was, at the time

the Company sought application pursuant to Section

251J(5) to have those persons registered as additional

nominees, employed by the Company and therefore the

subsequent decision to grant registration is of no

effect.

Notwithstanding any decision made in relation to

paragraphs 251K(2)(b)(ii) & (iii), the Board, having

removed Mr Forward as a nominee and having declared the

registration of Messrs Ferrazzano & Dominello to be of

no effect, cancelled the registration of the Company

pursuant to the provisions of paragraph 251K(4)(a),

there being no nominee registered in respect of the

Company.

An appeal against this decision may be lodged with the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal within the time

prescribed by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act."

9. Applications for registration of nominees are made on forms provided by the Board. In each application lodged with respect to S. & T. the proposed nominee has signed the document stating he is employed by that company. 10. With respect to a number of the persons named, the Tribunal is satisfied that they were never employed by S. & T. At most they were prepared to check returns prepared by employees of S. & T. on a fee for service basis but not in a master and servant relationship. In particular Mr Borromeo was never an employee. At least one person nominated sent a letter to S. & T. requiring that his name be removed. Mr McGuid explained that he regarded these persons as employed in the sense of being available to act as nominees if pressure of work required their services. We are not satisfied with his explanation.

11. On behalf of the Board it was submitted that the Tribunal has no jurisdiction to review the de-registration of S. & T. The submission was on the following basis. The Board having decided that Mr Forward was the only stated nominee who was employed, then S. & T.'s registration was required to be cancelled pursuant to section 251K(4)(a) once the Board served a notice on S. & T. pursuant to section 251J(11)(d) of the Act removing Mr Forward as a registered nominee.

12. However the question of who is an employee is not committed by section 251J(11)(b) finally to the opinion of the Board and this is a matter open to this Tribunal for review. In evidence before us we have forms of application by S. & T. for the registration of Albert Ferrazzano (T42 p. 71-72) and Ray Dominello (T44 p. 74-75) as nominees. The applications bear signatures of these persons, indicating in each case an awareness of the application and its contents, one aspect of which is a description as an employee of S. & T. Also, there are two affidavits of Mr Dominello (Exhibits E and 10) stating he considered himself to be employed by S. & T. and the affidavits by Albert Ferrazzano, (Exhibits 5 and 6) which do not really deny the master and servant relationship. The Tribunal considers the evidence sufficient to form the prima facie view that S. & T. had a relationship of employer and employee with these two persons falling within the control tests enunciated in Zuijs v. Wirth Brothers Pty. Ltd [1955] HCA 73; (1955) 93 CLR 561 and Australian Mutual Provident Society v. Allan (1978) 52 ALJR 407. Neither of the said deponents was cross-examined. It is true that Ferrazzano resigned, but that was after the Board made its decision.

13. The Tribunal therefore is of the opinion that its jurisdiction to review the de-registration of S. & T. was not taken away by the Board's decision to remove Forward's name as a nominee. Indeed it would be strange if the Board could defeat the Tribunal's jurisdiction under section 251K(5) of the Act by removing a company's nominees under section 251J(11) of the Act. Such a result is unlikely to have been intended by Parliament and may lead to the conclusion that notwithstanding the use of the word "shall" in section 251K(4), the sub-section is directory and not mandatory. See Hatton v. Beaumont (1977) 2 NSWLR 211. However, in view of the conclusion that S. & T. had two nominees at the time of the Board's decision, it is unnecessary to decide this.

14. Having considered the evidence in these proceedings, the Tribunal is satisfied that the first four findings of the Board as set out in its letter (T94 p. 208-210) have been established. The Board was satisfied for the Tribunal to review its decision on the basis of those four findings and upon the Board's finding that there was a breach of section 251N(2) of the Act, which the Tribunal also considers to be established. Although there were conflicts in evidence between Mr McGuid and Mr Mitchell, the Secretary to the Board, the Tribunal is not prepared to find that Mr McGuid is not a person of good fame and character. It was conceded on behalf of the Board that neither Mr McGuid nor his company had ever knowingly been party to lodging any false or misleading income tax returns.

15. Mr McGuid's sins appear to be in three principal directions. He was either reckless or careless in claiming certain persons to be nominees of the company, who had at most signed a nomination form stating they were employees, when in fact they rendered services, if any, on a part time basis for a short time and then in failing to notify the Board that they had ceased to be nominees. Secondly, he permitted Mr Taoube, who was later convicted, in about March 1985, of an offence involving the lodging of one or more false income tax returns, to prepare tax returns which were lodged in the name of S. & T. as a registered tax agent, when Mr Taoube was not an employee of S. & T. Mr McGuid said he checked Mr Taoube's returns, but we are satisfied that the supervision required by the Act did not exist. Contrary to Mr McGuid's views, the Act does not permit franchising of persons to prepare returns. The Act requires in the case of a company that returns be prepared and supervised by employees of the registered agent. Mr McGuid pointed to the actions of rival chains of tax agents who openly franchise tax shops under well known corporate names. Mr Mitchell said this was known to the Board, but in those cases, the person in charge of each franchised shop was himself a registered tax agent. Whether or not such franchising of names leads to deception of the public is not a matter for this Tribunal. With regard to Taoube we find that S. & T. did not discharge its responsibilities in accordance with the Act. The only thing that can be said in its favour is the concession that there was no knowledge of Taoube's illegal activities.

16. The third principal matter concerning Mr McGuid and S. & T. is the inadequate understanding of the responsibilities imposed by the Act. Section 251N requires much more than that the nominee supervise the preparation of a tax return in the sense of checking a document prepared by an unskilled employee in order to see if the figures appear reasonable. The evidence established that unqualified employees (termed consultants) interviewed the client, examined the client's expenditure receipts and vouchers and entered the particulars in a tax return form, generally a salaried employee's return. The client's receipts and vouchers were then returned to the person concerned. The next step was for the completed document to be perused and checked by another consultant who was provided with a tape check on the figures therein. If these matched and the return appeared reasonable, it was passed to a manager for further checking before being passed to a nominee for his perusal and checking prior to signing or, if any doubt arose as to its correctness, for the taking of appropriate action. Evidence was given that the nominee would take one to three minutes to deal with an "S" return. Section 251N calls for much more than this. It requires supervision and control of the preparation of the returns and of the business transacted by S. & T. relating to any income tax return or income tax matter. This involves, in our opinion, at least making spot checks on the accuracy of the initial material from which the returns are prepared and supervising the office work. S. & T.'s nominees have in many cases done no more than a few hours work in moonlighting jobs over and above their normal employment, making a rough appraisal of the completed work of unqualified employees of S. & T. When the Act speaks of the nominee being an employee and supervising the work it is really calling for substantial supervision and employment while the agent's office is open to transact tax business.

17. It appears to the Tribunal that Mr Mitchell, the Secretary to the Board, has acted conscientiously with regard to this matter, in spite of some criticisms by Mr McGuid.

18. The Tribunal considers there has clearly been misconduct by S. & T. as a tax agent. Learned counsel for S. & T. has undertaken to the Tribunal, on behalf of his client, that should the Tribunal exercise the discretion conferred by section 251K(2) of the Act in favour of allowing the agent to remain registered, his client will:-

1. Apply to the Board to register four named persons

including Mr McGuid as full-time nominees of S. &

T.

2. In the event of the four persons not being

approved, to submit other names so as to achieve

the situation that S. & T. will have four-full time

nominees employed continuously through the tax

return preparation season.

3. That outside the tax season it will employ, in

addition to Mr McGuid, one full time and two part-

time nominees.

4. That it will ensure proper wage records are

maintained in respect to all nominees.

5. That it will implement steps to ensure each nominee

brings to its notice any criminal charges or

convictions he or she may have and that it will

inform the respondent of any such charges and/or

convictions within seven days.

6. That it will ensure all officers and employees are

subject to supervision by its nominees at least

once per week. Such supervision will include spot

checks on returns currently being prepared and

discussions with compilers of returns about methods

of compilation. Further that Mr McGuid will visit

each office at least once per week to carry out

supervision.

7. That it will ensure all returns are signed only by

nominees who are employed by S. & T.

19. The Tribunal has considered the decisions in Re Su and Tax Agents' Board, South Australia 82 ATC 4284, Re Whatson and Tax Agents Board (1983) 5 ALN No 48, Re Perusco and Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales 82 ATC 4695 and Re Denton and Tax Agents' Board, South Australia 83 ATC 4009.

20. Each case has to be considered on its own facts. In our opinion Mr McGuid has not shown a proper understanding of the responsibilities of a tax agent. Suggestions were made to us that the system of supervision and control in other tax agency chains is similar to that of S. & T.; if that were so (and the Tribunal is not in a position to comment further) that would be no excuse for Mr McGuid, although it may be an explanation.

21. We consider Mr McGuid and his company S. & T. are deserving of a severe censure as a mark of the Tribunal's disapprobation of the laxity displayed in the past. However, it is a serious matter to deprive the applicant of its business, especially where there has been no dishonesty on its part in lodging returns. Mr McGuid's company guide to its operatives (Exhibit J) shows some effort to comply with the law. Mr McGuid himself has extensive experience in accountancy and his company performs some service in the lodgment of tax returns by salaried workers. The Tribunal would respectfully draw to the attention of the Board and the Government, the desirability of considering the possibility of spelling out in more detail the responsibilities of nominees. This case has taken five days in hearing and that in itself may be a lesson learned at some expense.

22. In all the circumstances, the Tribunal finds the charge of misconduct proved, but in the exercise of the discretion conferred by section 251K decides that the decision to cancel the registration of S. & T. be set aside.
