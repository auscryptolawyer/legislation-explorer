Wright and Commissioner for Superannuation [1978] AATA 27 (17 April 1978)

Last Updated: 14 March 2007

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No. 77/10022 .

Re: BERYL ELIZABETH WRIGHT

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

AAT Decision No 66

DECISION

The Tribunal affirms the decision of the Acting Commissioner taken on 3 August 1977.

Dated this seventeenth day of April 1978.

K.S. Edmunds

(Senior Member)

Dr M. Glick

(Member)

R.B. Hutchison

(Member)

REASONS FOR DECISION

This is an application to the Tribunal by Mrs .B.E. Wright to reconsider the decisions of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation made on 3 August 1977 that the provisions of sub-section 184(3) and of sub-section 66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 were applicable to her.

Mrs Wright who was born 20 October 1927 received permanent appointment to the Public Service on 9 January 1975. The medical examination which preceeded this appointment took place on 25 November 1974 and the report of the examination contained a recommendation that she be accepted for appointment under Superannuation Fund conditions. The reasons for the recommendation were stated to be that she was "a normal healthy middle aged woman". She was subsequently admitted a contributor to the Fund.

During her medical examination Mrs Wright completed and signed in the presence of the examining doctor a document entitled Medical Examination Form which contains, inter alia, the following:

"PERSONAL:

Have you now or have you previously had any of the following

complaints? Answer yes or no in the space provided.

1.

(a)Nervous fatigue No
(b)Mental or nervous conditions No

(c) Anxiety State No

(d)Neurasthenia No
(e) Fits No
(f)Epilepsy No
(g)Persistent headaches No
(h)Dizziness or turns No
(i)Attack of unconsciousness or weakness No
(j)Migrane No
(k)Disturbance of sensation of muscular activity No
(l)Persistent Breathlessness No

2 (Blank)

3 (Blank )

NOTE; All questions must be answered.

Please give:

11 Details of any complaint, illness or injury not mentioned above.

As a child Mumps, Measles, Chicken Pox

12 Details of absence from school or work due to sickness over

past two years.

Tonsillitis, Flu, Car accident

13 Details of all accidents, surgical treatment or operations.

Car accident

14 Are you in good health now?

Yes

In a letter dated 15 May 1976 addressed to the Personnel Officer, Department of Defence, Dr. E.J. Alam wrote: "Re: Mrs Wright.

This good lady has been under my care in excess of 14 years and is under treatment for Petit Mal, latterly Grand Mal. All aggravated by a recent Motor Vehicle accident. This has resulted in an Anxiety Depressive, highly emotional and nervous state and in the interest of her health it would be adviseable that Mrs Wright retire from work."

On 2 July Mrs Wright submitted herself for Medical Examination and the Medical Officer reported that she was suffering From "Anxiety state and Epilepsy" and under the heading "Brief Sistory of Disability" he stated:-

"Motor Vehicle accident in 1974. This has aggravated an existing problem - Epilepsy and also resulted in an anxiety state of major proportions".

'The Doctor's clinical notes on examination contained the comment "Agitated woman. Full of moans and complaints", and under Prognosis wrote the following:

"Recovery unlikely. N.B: The conditions leading to retirement in this case were present "before her appointment. Mrs Wright withheld information at the time of this medical examination. Public Service Board regulations in this case require that she forfeit her superannuation entitlement."

Mrs Wright retired on the 13 July 1976 but being advised of the Medical Officer' s comments submitted to the Department of Defence a Statutory Declaration dated 28 July 1976 which stated: "I, Beryl Elizabeth Wright, 12/127 Clovelly Road RAEDWICK, Clerical Assistant Grade 2 do solemnly and sincerely declare as follows

1.That as far as I was truthfully aware I was under medical treatment for Petit Mal.
2.At no stage prior to 1974 and prior to a motor vehicle accident in 1974 was I aware that medical treatment was for any reason other than Petit Mal.
3.Under these circumstances I applied for and was accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund.
4.At no stage did 1 knowingly conceal or falsify that application."

The Delegate of the Commissioner on the material set out above being satisfied that the requirements of sub-section 184(3) were met, issued in her name a benefit classification certificate in which the condition Epilepsy was specified. He also formed the opinion that the incapacity the ground for retirement was caused or was substantially contributed to by a physical or mental condition specified on the benefit classification certificate. Mrs Wright's invalidity benefit was accordingly calculated under section 66(2) of the Act.

Mrs Wright objected to the foregoing decisions of the Delegate of the Commissioner and by letter dated 4 January 1977 requested that the decision of the Delegate be reconsidered by the Commissioner.

When reconsidering the decision the Acting Commissioner had received in addition to the foregoing material a letter dated 3 May 1977 from Dr Alam, written comments by Dr Dunnett, Commonwealth Medical Officer, dated 23. May 1977 and observation made on 13 July 1977 by Dr Ryan of the Department of Health in relation to the link, if any, between the disabilities "Epilepsy and Anxiety State", the two complaints from which Mrs Wright was considered to be suffering at the time of her retirement. Each of these is set out hereunder: Letter from Doctor Alam;

"With reference to your communication of the 21 April, 1977, regarding Mrs Wrights' appeal against your decision against her superannuation pension payments, I have to state that Mrs Wright has been a patient of this practice since well before I took over in May 1962.

Perusal of my clinical notes, which date back to May 1962, (and I might add that there were no clinical notes available prior to my take over of this practice) indicates that Mrs Wright was on Tridione, and Dilantin. There is no record of Major Seizure, namely Epilepsy, on my notes up until the mid 1970's, particularly following her Motor Vehicle accident, and the onset of Thyrotoxicosis.

Mrs Wright had "been treated for Migraine, and other general conditions, but it seems that the Thyrotoxicosis really unmasked the Epilepsy, and that this is the case, has been confirmed by the fact that treatment for her Thyrotoxocosis has demolished any subsequent turns. Her husband states that there have been no Major Turns since the Thyrotoxicosis has ceased.

As far as the Dilantin and Tridione were concerned, they were kept on because of previous decisions of the late Dr J.J. Ward, my predeccessor.

So, in SUMMARY it appears that Mrs Wright has been treated with these Neuroleptic drugs since 1962, or prior to that, and Major Seizures did not occur until the onset of Thyrotoxicosis, presumably precipitated by the stress of her Motor Vehicle accident in the mid 1970's.

She has always been an extremely conscientious person, both as to compliance to medical treatment, and truth, and I commend her for reinstatement as to superannuation pension payments."

Comments from Dr Dunnettt

"After examining Mrs Wright's file I have established that Mrs Wright has been on Neuroleptic drugs since 1962, and has admitted in a Statutory Declaration that she was aware she was suffering from Petit Mal.

She was injured in A.M.V.A. in February 1974 and her general practitioner Dr Alam has stated that major seizures did not occur until the onset of Thyrotoxicosis, presumably precipitated by the Motor Vehicle accident. Subsequently, at the time of her medical examination in November 1974, she answered 'No' to the question "Are you suffering or have you suffered from Epilepsy or Fits?"

In my opinion, Mrs Wright was suffering from Petit Mal, and was aware of this at the time of her medical examination in November 1974".

Observation made by Dr Ryan;

"This woman had epilepsy 'Petit Mal' - prior to her medical examination which she did not disclose. Persons with epilepsy are naturally anxious in case attacks occur at work or at social events and particularly in car driving or in proximity to moving machinery."

On 3 August 1977 "the Acting Commissioner confirmed each of the decisions taken by the Delegate of the Commissioner on 5 October 1976 and on 18 September 1977 Mrs Wright made an application for review of the decisions of the Acting Commissioner and submitted the following grounds:

"To the best of my knowledge I do not and never have suffered from Epilepsy.

My personal Physician Dr E.J. Alam of Darlinghurst who has treated me for the past 15 years has furnished information to this effect to the Superannuation Board and has also provided complete details of my medical history to the Superannuation Board.

My condition on retirement from work was aggravated by a Motor Vehicle accident which caused me to suffer an over active Thyroid. Dr Peter Kendall of Macquarie Street is treating me for this condition and associated severe headaches and nervous depression and has stated that this was caused as the result of the Motor Vehicle accident. This condition combined with the nervous after effects of the accident caused me to suffer an attack of Grand Mal.

Up to the present time the medical terms Petit Mal, Grand Mal were not known to me. As far as I am aware I am not being specifically treated for Petit Mal or Grand Mal.

The combination of Thyroid condition and Motor Vehicle accident prompted Dr Alam to recommend that I be retired from work due to my anxiety, nervous and emotional state.

At my medical examination for permanent appointment I was thoroughly examined and accepted as a contributor to the superannuation fund.

At my medical examination for retirement Dr Moses Government Medical Officer tested my blood pressure only and recommended that I be retired with a condition Epilepsy.

It would appear that this decision has resulted in my not receiving superannuation benefits. I fail to see how such a cursory examination could reveal a disability such as Epilepsy.

The Acting Commissioner states that my retiring disabilities were anxiety state and Epilepsy and on the advice of Department of Health goes on to state that the condition anxiety state was substantially contributed to by condition Epilepsy that was in evidence on my becoming a contributor under Superannuation Act 1922."

At the hearing before the Tribunal Mr Wright who represented his wife referred the Tribunal to a definition of "Petit Mal" in the Penguin Medical Encyclopedia by Dr Peter Vingate and exhibited a document signed by Dr EJ Alam reading as, follows:

"Perusing Mrs Wrights file and to clarify what I see in it.

There Was no record in my files, or memory of Mrs Wright over 15 years as patient of her having Epilepsy prior to her accident in 1974 - when Epilepsy was precipitated by Thyrotoxicosis, not aggravated by it. (I had kept her on Tridione and Delantin - as had been instituted by Dr John J. Ward (now deceased) prior to 1962).

By nature, not in view of her worry over previous non existant seizures, Mrs Wright was and is an anxious, worrying conscientious person.

I pen these observations in view of what I see in her file, and feel such clarification should be stated".

Mrs Wright in her declaration dated 28 July 1976 acknowledges that she was aware she was under Medical Treatment for Petit Mal. Her physician Dr Alam stated quite categorically in his letter dated 15 May 1976 to the Personnel Officer that Mrs Wright was and had been, "under his treatment for Petit Mal, latterly Grand Mal. All aggravated by a recent Motor Vehicle accident. This has resulted in an anxiety depressive highly emotional and nervous state."

The Motor Vehicle accident referred to happened in February 1974. In his letter dated 3 May 1977 Dr Alam states that Mrs Wright was being treated with Tridione and Dilantin and had been so treated since 1962 and by her former physician prior to that date.

Dr Alam appears in his letter of 3 May 1977 and the document dated 14 December 1977 to treat a major seizure only, as Epilepsy. He attributed the seizure experienced by Mrs Wright in November 1975 "to the onset of Thyrotoxicosis which he says "really unmasked the Epilepsy".

Apart from the Petit Mal reference to which Mrs Wright referred, the Tribunal has consulted the British Medical Dictionary (MacNalty), Goulds Medical Dictionary, Blacks Medical Dictionary 31st Edition, Dorlands Illustrated Medical Dictionary 24th Edition Butterworths Medical Dictionary, Stedmans Medical Dictionary 4th Edition 1976, the British Encyclopaedia of Medical Practice and the Oxford Dictionary. It is clear from all these authorities that Petit Mal is the name given to a form of manifestation of the complaint Epilepsy.

On the material contained in the foregoing paragraphs the Tribunal considers that Mrs Wright should have furnished information of this complaint when undergoing the medical examination required by virtue of Section 5 of the superseded Act. Moreover in view of the guidelines under which medical officers make their recommendations for the consideration and determination by the Superannuation Board whether a person be accepted for the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account, the Tribunal considers that had Mrs Wright disclosed she was receiving treatment for Petit Mal she would have been accepted only as a contributor for the Provident Account established under the Superannuation Act 1922.

The Tribunal confirms the decision of the Acting Commissioner that sub-section 184(3) applied to Mrs Wright and the benefit classification Certificate correctly stated the physical or mental condition to be epilepsy.

The medical officer who examined Mrs Wright prior to her retirement stated that she was suffering from an anxiety state and epilepsy. The benefit classification certificate issued in the name of Beryl Elizabeth Wright, 12/127 Clovelly Road, Randwick by the Delegate of the Commissioner specified Epilepsy as the condition to which the certificate applied and this certificate is deemed by subsection 184(3) of the Superannuation Act 1976 to have been in force at the time of Mrs Wright's retirement.

Moreover, Dr Ryan has expressed the opinion that persons with "epilepsy are naturally anxious in case attacks occur at work or at social events and particularly in car driving or in proximity to moving machinery". Mrs Wright had had a seizure in November 1975 at work and the effect of this cannot be ignored in relation to her anxiety state at the time of her medical examination for retirement.

The Tribunal considered the document dated 14 December 1977 signed by Dr Alam but, having regard to his obvious separation of Petit Mal from the condition epilepsy, cannot regard it as detracting from the medical opinion of Dr Ryan.

For the foregoing reasons the Tribunal confirms the decision of the Acting Commissioner that sub-section 66(2) is applicable in the case of Mrs BE Wright.

The Tribunal with the whole of the foregoing in mind is of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for Mrs Wrights retirement satisfied the requirements of sub-section 66(2)(c) of the Act.
