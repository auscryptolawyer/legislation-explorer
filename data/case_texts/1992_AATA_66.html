Re Taxation Appeals [1992] AATA 66; (1992) 92 ATC 153, (1992) 23 ATR 1086 (26 February 1992)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. Q T91/33

AAT No. 7769

Taxation

(1992) 92 ATC 153, (1992) 23 ATR 1086

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Justice D.F. O'Connor(1) (President)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - Income Tax Assessment Act - whether takeover defence costs allowable deductions under s51(1) - whether takeover defence costs incurred in gaining or producing assessable income or necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s51, s187

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v The Swan Brewery Co Ltd (1991) 30 FCR 553

HEARING

BRISBANE

26:2:1992

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent to disallow certain expenses incurred by the applicant as tax deductions under s51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (ITAA). The applicant company claimed takeover defence costs as tax deductions in its tax return for the year ending 31 December 1988.

2. At the hearing on 3 February 1992 in Brisbane the applicant was represented by Mr R G Lunney of Thompson Douglass and Co and the respondent by Mr P Hack of Counsel. The Tribunal had before it documents lodged pursuant to s37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (the T-documents) and the following:

Copies of minutes of Meeting of Directors of the applicant

dated 12 November 1987

Copies of minutes of Meeting of Directors of the

applicant dated 17 December 1987

Copy of minutes of Meeting of Directors

of the applicant dated 14 April 1988

"Part A" takeover offer by Company 1

"Part B" response by the applicant to Company 1 offer Part A takeover

offer by Company 2 and Company 3

"Part B" response by the applicant to

Company 2 and Company 3 offer

"Part C" takeover offer by Company 4

"Part D" response by the applicant to Company 4 offer

Annual report of the applicant 1987

Annual Report of the applicant 1988

A bundle of fee accounts to the Chairman of the applicant from the

applicant's legal advisors on the takeover offers

The Tribunal heard evidence from a director of the applicant (hereafter

referred to as "the Director").

3. The applicant company was subject to three takeover offers in 1987 and 1988. The first two were "Part A" offers and the third was a "Part C" offer as defined in the Companies (Aquisition of Shares) (NSW) Code (the Code). The first was from Company 1 on 28 July 1987, the second from Company 2 and Company 3 on 11 November 1987 and the third from Company 4 on 12 April 1988. In the year ended 31 December 1988 the applicant incurred expenditure of $207 557.00 in respect of these takeover offers. In its tax return for that year the applicant requested a ruling on whether this amount was deductible under s51(1) of the ITAA (T3, p33). The respondent issued a notice of assessment on 2 November 1989 (T4, p 89) refusing the requested deduction. The applicant lodged a notice of objection with the respondent on 20 December 1989 in respect of that assessment. An amount of $14 was also claimed in respect of a dividend in the objection. The respondent partly allowed the objection. The $14 claimed in respect of a dividend was allowed but the expenditure in relation to takeover offers was disallowed. A request was made by the applicant pursuant to s187 of the ITAA to have the decision reviewed by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal. It is the decision to disallow the takeover expenditure which is being reviewed by this Tribunal.

4. The Director gave evidence of his history and position with the applicant company. He described the history of the company and outlined the company's shareholding and major shareholders. He also gave evidence of how the company perceived and dealt with the takeover offers. The Director said that the takeover offers were dealt with by obtaining legal advice on preparation of "Part B" and "Part D" statements in response to the offers (as required by sections 22 and 32 of the Code). He stated that the directors of the company perceived all the takeover offers to be inadequate, and that with at least one offer (the Company 2 and Company 3 offer) there was a possibility of a "fire sale" of the company's assets. The directors felt that their actions were necessary to ensure that the shareholders interests were not compromised.

5. Section 51(1) of the ITAA provides:

"All losses or outgoings to the extent which they are incurred in

gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred

in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such

income shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they

are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic

nature,..."

6. This case falls within the principles enunciated in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Swan Brewery Co Ltd ((1991) 30 FCR 553). In that case the full Federal Court (Neaves, Lee and Olney JJ.) determined that the expenditure of the taxpayer in engaging a firm to provide an independent report on a takeover offer for the information of shareholders, and the preparation of a "Part B" statement as required by sections 22 and 23 of the Companies (Aquisition of Shares) (Victoria) Code, were not expenses related or directed to the maintenance of the business activities of the corporation.

7. The applicant contended that the expenditure in question fell within the second limb of s51(1), ie that it was necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing assessable income. It was conceded that the first limb of s51(1) was not satisfied, that is, that the expense was not incurred in gaining or producing assessable income. It was submitted that expenses incurred in fulfilling a legal obligation under the Code to prepare "Part B" and "Part D" statements in response to "Part A" and "Part C" takeover offers were expenses incurred in carrying on the business. It was argued that takeover offers are a common event and that defending them was a part of running a business.

8. Mr Lunney submitted that the Swan case did not bind the Tribunal to come to a decision adverse to the applicant. First he sought to distinguish the case upon the facts, stating that the exact details of the expenditure in the Swan case were not outlined. He stated that in the present case the expenditure was purely that required by law ie the preparation of "Part B" and "Part D" statements. In the Swan case, it was submitted, the expenses incurred may have gone over and above those required by the Code and that this point was not clear in the facts. Mr Lunney submitted that there was in fact an exception to the rule in Swan. He based this upon the following extract from the judgement:

"That is not to say that expenditure could never be so characterized. It

may be that the directors of a trading corporation may perceive a

takeover offer to carry an inherent threat to the continuation of the

corporation's business and impairment of its income earning activities.

In a particular case it may be obvious to a board that the takeover

offer can only lead to a reduction of circulating capital applied in the

business and the curtailment of the ability of the business of the

corporation to gain or produce assessable income. In such a case it may

be argued that the expenditure incurred by directors on behalf of the

corporation in order to better inform shareholders of the worth of their

capital interest and to urge their resistance to the takeover offer was

directed to the maintenance of business activities of the corporation in

the best interests of the shareholders and of the company rather than to

the discharge of a separate duty to shareholders to assess the adequacy

of a bid to acquire proprietorship of the share capital of the

corporation. (See Morgan (Inspector of Taxes) v. Tate and Lyle Ltd

(1955) AC 21.)" (p 562)

9. The respondent submitted that the expenditure was not covered by the first or second limbs of s51(1) and that in any event it was expenditure of a capital nature. It was submitted that the Swan case binds the Tribunal to find that the expenditure in this case is not deductible, and that the exception referred to by the applicant was not a real exception as their Honours in Swan say only that it "could be argued ..." that in the outlined circumstances the expenses described could relate to the carrying on of a business. Further, those circumstances described were not fulfilled in the present case as there was no evidence to support the proposition that the takeovers, if they had gone ahead, would have lead to a reduction of circulating capital of the corporation.

10. The Tribunal considers that the circumstances in the present case do not differ to those in Swan in any relevant way. In the Swan case the expenses in question were the costs of obtaining advice on a takeover offer, preparing a "Part B" statement, preparing an independent report on the offer and printing the "Part B" statement. The Tribunal finds no lack of clarity or detail in the Full Court's treatment of the facts in Swan. The expenses claimed by the applicant are expenses in relation to the preparation of "Part B" and "Part D" statements. Their Honours in the Swan case stated:

"the expenditure upon aids to the consideration of the adequacy of the

valuation of the capital interest of the shareholders contained in the

takeover offer and of the nature of the response to that offer to be

recommended to shareholders owed nothing to the conduct of the business

of the taxpayer.

The nature and profitability of the taxpayer's business and the assets

of the corporation acquired by that business may well have dictated the

worth and value of the interests of shareholders in the share capital of

the taxpayer, but it did not mean that expenditure related to those

interests was necessarily incurred in the carrying on of that business

for the purpose of gaining or producing assessable income and, as the

Tribunal found, it clearly was not." (p 562)

Their Honours were referring to all of the expenditure claimed by the taxpayer in that case which included the preparation of a "Part B" statement. There is no relevant distinction to be made between the preparation of a "Part B" and a "Part D" statement.

11. The Tribunal finds that the expenditure claimed was not necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing assessable income.

12. In light of the above conclusion it is unnecessary to consider the respondent's submission that the expenditure in question was of a capital nature.

13. The expenditure in question is not an allowable deduction under s51(1) of the ITAA, and accordingly, the decision under review is affirmed.
