Last Updated: 14 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - partnership leasing computer equipment - within 12 months of lease use of equipment by persons other than partnership - whether investment allowance deduction allowable.

Income Tax Assessment Act (Cth) - section 82AA(1), 82AB(1), 82AG(3)

W A Hughes Pty Ltd v FC of T 81 ATC 4317 Touraoark Pty Ltd v FC of T 82 ATC 4105

Income Tax - applications to review decisions of Commissioner referred to Tribunal - notices issued to applicant returned unclaimed' - extensive efforts by Commissioner to locate applicant - no change of address advised to Tribunal by applicant and no returns of income lodged for later years with Commissioner whether applications should be dismissed in terms of section 42A(2)(a).

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act (Cth) - section 42A(2 5(a) Administrative Appeals Tribunal Regulations (Cth) - regulation 18(1)

Re: TT86/405-6

Applicant

And :Commissioner of Taxation

Respondent

No: TT86/405-6

AAT Decision No 3242

Date: 20 February 1987

Mr H P Stevens (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No TT86/405-6

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re: TT86/405-6

Applicant

And: Commissioner of Taxation

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

Mr H P Stevens (Senior Member)

The question at issue is whether or not the Tribunal should in terms of section 42A(2)(a) dismiss the applicant's applications without proceeding to review the decisions concerned.

2. Section 42A(2)(a) provides:-

42A(2) [Failure to appear] If a party to a proceeding before the Tribunal in respect of an application for the review of a decision (not being the person who made the decision) fails either to appear in person or to appear by a representative at a preliminary conference held in relation to the application under section 34 or at the hearing of the proceeding, the Tribunal may -

(a) where the only other party to the proceeding is the person who made the decision - dismiss the application without proceeding to review the decision.

3. In his return of income for the year ended 30 June 1980 the applicant claimed a deduction in respect of "Section 80 Loss - 1979 Carried Forward $81,645" and returned a net loss of $33,026 (without allowance for a share of a partnership which was said to be "not available"). For the 1981 year a deduction was claimed for "Section 80 Loss - 1980 carried forward $33,026) and a net loss of $29,871 returned (no reference to the partnership which had ceased on 30 June 1980 due to operations proving "to be a major disaster for ail concerned"). Upon assessment of the 1980 return the carry forward loss was disallowed to the extent of $71,700 and after other adjustments a taxable income of $17,176 resulted. For the 1981 year a taxable income of $4,932 resulted and assessments for both years issued on 22 June 1982.

4. The adjustments made arose out of the operations of the partnership and a claim for an investment allowance of $141,925 in the partnership's return of income for the year ended 30 June 1979. This claim had been disallowed and, as a result, the applicant's share for 1979 had been increased thus reducing the carry forward loss of the year to $10,545. Thus, the common objection to both assessments referred to the partnership claim stating:-

We claim that the assessments should be amended by the allowance, as a deduction from his assessable income the sum of $70,962 or some part thereof, described on the adjustment sheet which accompanies the said notices of assessment as "loss from the partnership of ....., & .....". The adjustment sheet re the partnership of ..... , ..... & ..... described the adjustment as the disallowance of the deduction claimed for investment allowance "as the computer is not a unit of eligible property to which the investment allowance applies". We claim the investment allowance should have been allowed as a deduction from the assessable income for the year ended 30 June 1979 and carried forward as a loss in subsequent years and that the assessments be amended accordingly.

The grounds upon which reliance is placed are as follows:

  1. That the said computer is a unit of eligible property as defined in section 82AQ(1) of the said Act.
  2. That the sum of $141,925, being the investment allowance on the said computer, is an allowable deduction to the partnership of ....., ..... & ..... under the provisions of section 82AA(1) and section 82AB(1).
  3. That pursuant to section 82A3(2) and (3) the prescribed percentage to be used to calculate the investment allowance shall be 40 per centum."

Similar objections were lodged on behalf of the other two members of the partnership.

5. Discussions took place between officers of the Commissioner and members of the partnership on at least two occasions and it was ascertained that the partnership were the lessees of the equipment - declarations by leasing company dated 3 November 1978 in respect of expenditure of $197,548 and $120,123 respectively and one under extension of time dated 29 June 1979 for $37,143 - pursuant to lease agreement of 18 October 1978 for a period of five years. This being so the provisions of section 82AG(3) were relevant. Such provisions stated the entitlement to an investment allowance was lost "if, before the expiration of 12 months after the property was first used, or installed ready for use, by the lessee" and "while the lease was in force the lessee entered into a contract or arrangement with another person for the use of the property by that other person".

6. In this regard it was further ascertained that a company, of which the members of the partnership were directors, by director's minute of 3 May 1979 had resolved to affix the company seal to a contract to provide "the total computing services for" an organisation. It was also resolved to employ the services of the applicant "to provide the software for the" contract and "to purchase the necessary computer time from the partnership" to process the work under the contract. A formal agreement of 11 June 1979 was executed by the company and the organisation. It was also ascertained that the applicant was also using the equipment in his own separate business and paying the partnership for computer time used. Apparently the applicant's wife also had the use of the computer to some degree.

7. As a result of the various arrangements the view was taken that the company, the organisation (VDUs in their premises) and the applicant (also his wife) were all using within 12 months of the lease the equipment leased by the partnership. Accordingly in the light of the Full Federal Court decision in W A Hughes Pty Ltd v FC of T 81 ATC 4317 the objections were disallowed. In that case the taxpayer claiming the investment allowance owned all the shares in two companies which carried on a coal haulage business in partnership hut the Court held it was not entitled to deductions on account of investment allowance because it had granted rights to use to the partnership of a truck it had purchased (s82AA(a)(ii)(c)) and while a lease was in force had entered into an arrangement for the use of a loader by the partnership (s82AG(3)(d)).

8. Overall the "arrangements" turned out to be a dismal failure and as indicated earlier the partnership came to an end. The other partners have accepted the action taken by the Commissioner (objections withdrawn) and the applicant's claim is the only one before the Tribunal. This application was lodged on his behalf with the Commissioner in October 1982 by a firm of chartered accountants but before the matter was transmitted to the Tribunal by the Commissioner the applicant had left the State and his whereabouts are unknown to the chartered accountants (and his former partners). The Commissioner through another Department obtained an address in Queensland and the papers forwarded to the Tribunal gave this as the applicant's address. By notice of 17 October 1986 sent to this address the matter was set down for preliminary conference on 13 November 1986. However this notice was returned unclaimed and at the conference it was orally directed that the matter be removed from the list reserving liberty to apply. By notice of 11 December 1986 the Commissioner requested the matter be relisted at the next sittings of the Tribunal and it was listed by the Deputy Registrar for hearing on 29 January 1987.

9. The notice of hearing (to the applicant) issued to the only address available to the Tribunal and this was also returned unclaimed. Subsequently the Commissioner obtained a fresh address from the other Department and by letter of 14 January 1987 the Acting Deputy Registrar wrote to the applicant as follows:-

This matter was listed for a Preliminary Conference on 13 November 1986. You did not make an appearance and the presiding member, ..... gave a direction that the matter be removed from the list, reserving liberty for either party to apply to have the matter re-listed.

The Commissioner of Taxation has now requested that the matter be listed for Directions as he wishes to have your case struck out for want of appearance as is provided for by s42A(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, which states:

"If a party to a proceeding before the Tribunal in respect of an application for review of a decision (not being the person who made the decision) fails either to appear in person or to appear by a representative at a preliminary conference held in relation to the application under section 34 or at the hearing of the proceeding, the Tribunal may,

(a) where the only other party to the proceedings is the person who made the decision - dismiss the application without proceeding to review the decision."

The matter is listed for directions at 9.30 am on 29 January1987 at Commonwealth Law Courts Building, 39 Davey Street, Hobart. Under the circumstances I would urge you to advise the Tribunal urgently if you cannot attend. You may telephone me, reverse charges, on 002 20 1714 if you wish.

It might be noted this letter referred to a listing for Directions whereas it was in fact listed for hearing. On 15 January 1987 the Commissioner issued three letters to the applicant - one to the new address given the Tribunal, one to the old address and the third to the applicant's previous place of residence in the State - stating:-

Your former tax accountants ..... wrote to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation on 12 October 1983 (sic) asking him on your behalf to refer to a Board of Review an objection which you had made to assessments of your income tax liability for the years of income ended 30 June 1979, 30 June 1980 and 30 June 1981. The Deputy Commissioner did as they requested and the objection was referred to a Board of Review. The proceedings before the Board of Review are now proceeding before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal as a result of reforms which were made to the taxation appeals system by the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act, 1986.

Your objection was the subject of a preliminary conference before, Senior Member of the Tribunal on 13 November 1986. The Deputy Commissioner's representative informed of the fact that your current whereabouts were unknown and asked that your application to the Tribunal be dismissed under section 42A of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 declined to take that step at that time and ordered that your application be removed from the list for hearing, reserving liberty to apply to both parties.

The Deputy Commissioner wished to again apply to the Tribunal for your application to be dismissed under section 42A of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act. The Tribunal has therefore listed the case for mention at 9.30 am on Thursday 29 January 1987 at his request. The hearing will be at the Commonwealth Law Courts Building, 39-41 Davey Street, Hobart.

Please note that your application is liable to be dismissed if you do not appear before the Tribunal on that day. Please ring Mr D Targett of this office on (002) 204474 to acknowledge receipt of this letter and to indicate whether you propose to appear before the Tribunal on 29 January 1987.

Again the nature of the proceeding was misstated - "for mention" and not "for hearing" as set out in the only notice issued to the Commissioner. The opening para. is also defective in that there was no assessment for the year ended 30 June 1979 (a loss apart from partnership claim) and that year could not be (and is not) before the Tribunal.

  1. As at the date of the hearing the Commissioner's letter to the new address given the Tribunal had been returned with quixotic undertones but that of the Tribunal to the same address had not However the Deputy Registrar has now advised that it has also been returned with every indication of having been opened and resealed.
  2. Regulation 18(1) provides:-

A document or statement that is required by these Regulations to be furnished to a person or a notice or other notification that is required to be served on or given to a person may be posted to the person by a pre-paid letter -

(a) where the person has furnished an address at which documents in relation to the proceeding or matter may be served - to that address; or

(b) where no such address has been furnished -
  • (i) in the case of a person not being a company - to the address of his place of residence or business last-known to the person posting the document, statement or notice or other notification; or
  • (ii) in the case of a company - to the address of the registered office of the company,

and, if a document, statement or notice or other notification is so posted, then, for the purposes of these Regulations, the document or statement shall he deemed to be furnished, or the notice or other notification shall be deemed to be served or given, as the case may be, at the time when the document, statement or notice or other notification is so posted.

and there is no doubt that its provisions have been complied with.

12. Turning now to the question at issue the Commissioner is generally concerned about the Tribunal's practice of regarding as "finalised" applications where there has been no final dismissal or otherwise of an application. Such takes place when an order is made withdrawing an application from the Tribunal's list of cases for hearing for reasons such as absence overseas, awaiting conclusion of a "test" case, and the like. It can also take place when a matter is settled and the applicant although agreeing in writing to withdraw his application does not in formal terms do so and no order in terms of section 42A(1) is made. Although the Tribunal recognises such an order does not constitute a final conclusion of the application the Administrative Review Council's Annual Report in terms of section 58 includes such cases under the Applications Finalised heading thereby reducing the number of applications on hand undetermined for 1987 the present wording has been changed and the appropriate item under Applications Finalised will be "delisted with right of reinstatement". In his own Annual Report to Parliament the Commissioner treats as finalised only those where a matter has in fact been finally concluded ie cannot be reactivated and there will be an inability to reconcile the respective figures of the Tribunal and the Commissioner as to applications finalised and undetermined applications. Apart from this aspect which could show considerable divergencies (thousands of test cases are being delisted with right of reinstatement) the Commissioner is concerned that unlike, for example, applicants seeking pensions who have something to gain and who can be expected not to disappear, many taxation applications involve large outstanding amounts where a disappearance is convenient for the applicant. He is also concerned that whilst the practice might not cause inconvenience in the General Division of the Tribunal where the numbers involved are relatively small (undetermined applications as at 30 June 1986 2,031) the situation is quite different in the Taxation Appeals Division where the undetermined applications taken over as at 30 June 1986 were 47,162 - a further 9,960 applications were transmitted to the Tribunal from 1 July 1986 to 7 January 1987 cf. 1,500 approx. for General Division. In addition in respect of many such applications the applicant is the only one who knows the whole of the facts of his case and statutorily the onus of proof lies upon him. It is therefore of some importance to the Commissioner that he obtain complete finality in respect of the applications referred to the Tribunal as soon as possible and not have any more than necessary applications delisted with right of reinstatement. Unless this occurs it is suggested that, apart from those of the Commissioner, "a significant waste of the AAT's resources will ensue".

13. Although these general concerns exist it was the desire of the solicitor who appeared on behalf of the Commissioner to deal specifically only with the position in relation to applicants who have "disappeared". In this regard what he sought was not only a decision upon the present applications but also an expression of view that in such instances - a figure of "some four or five thousand similar cases around the country" was given - the Tribunal would regard it as appropriate to dismiss the applications concerned. Unless such an expression of view could be obtained an unacceptable waste of everyone's scarce resources would result.

14. Whilst the concern is recognised it was made clear by the Tribunal that, since the word "may" in section 42A(2) obviously did not mean "shall", it was necessary to look at each such application individually and it would be both inappropriate and contrary to law to act as desired. The solicitor appreciated this for in response to the Tribunal's statement it "would not be prepared to give a general ruling that would be applicable to all cases irrespective of the facts" he said "I could not expect it; I think it would be very unfair".

15. Insofar as the present matter is concerned it is clear from the terms of regulation 18(1) set out above that actual notice to an applicant is not an essential requirement although the rules of natural justice would, in normal circumstances, require the Tribunal to be satisfied the applicant had received the notice before it would take the step of dismissing an application. However, where it is apparent reasonable steps to ascertain a correct address have been taken without success by the respondent and the applicant has taken no steps to provide an up to date address to the respondent or the Tribunal, there seems no good reason why the Tribunal should require such satisfaction. On the other hand, in the event of a request by the respondent for an order in terms of section 42A(2)(a), it is thought the Tribunal does need to be satisfied that reasonable steps have been taken by the respondent and the applicant has taken no steps.

16. On this aspect the Tribunal accepts that the Commissioner's officers (both here and in another State) have spent considerable time (three days by just one officer in this location) in exploring various avenues; eg searches at transport registries, of electoral rolls, drivers licences and the like in an attempt to locate the applicant who has not contacted the respondent (or Tribunal) nor has lodged any further returns of income. The Tribunal hesitates to accept such attempt as unsuccessful - the view is held the new address is possibly the correct address in which event the applicant has in fact received notice and ignored it - but for the purposes of reaching a decision will treat the attempt as unsuccessful.

  1. Taking into account the whole of the circumstances of the present case it is clear that this is an appropriate case for the Tribunal to exercise its powers under section 42A(2)(a) and it so exercises them.
  2. The above decision has been reached without any consideration of the possible merits of the applicant's claims. However from the known facts and the decision of the Full Federal Court referred to above it would seem the Commissioner's action is correct - see also remarks by Aickin J in Tourapark Pty Ltd v FC of T 82 ATC 4105 at p 4112 re lending and use on "some isolated occasion" as destroying the investment allowance deduction. If this be so then no good purpose would be served by keeping the matter "alive" and it would be another reason (if necessary) for dismissing the applications.
  3. Although it is necessary to look at the facts of each individual case and it is inappropriate to make a general rule applicable to all it might be not inappropriate to say that, where a perusal of the individual facts indicates a prima facie non-accidental disappearance by an applicant who is in debt to the Crown and the applicant has made no attempt to contact either the Commissioner or the Tribunal whilst the Commissioner's officers have taken reasonable steps to locate the applicant, it would be appropriate to dismiss the application. Moreso where it is apparent the claims involve little merit.

20. For the above reasons the applications are dismissed in accordance with section 42A(2)(a) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act.
