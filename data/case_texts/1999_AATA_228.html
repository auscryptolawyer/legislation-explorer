Nguyen and Commissioner of Taxation [1999] AATA 228; (1999) 42 ATR 1030; 99 ATC 2211 (16 April 1999)

Last Updated: 8 October 2009





DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION [1999] AATA 228

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No QT1998/106

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re HUONG THI THU NGUYEN

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



Date 16 April 1999

Place Brisbane

Decision The Tribunal decides that the objection decisions under review are affirmed.



(Sgd) K L Beddoe

Decision No 228/1999 Senior Member

CATCHWORDS

TAXATION : Trusts – beneficiary presently entitled – income diverted from trust – whether presently entitled to diverted income



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 95A(2), 97, 101, 223, 227

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 s 43

Taxation Administration Act 1953 s14ZZK



Jackson v Parrott (1896) 1 Ch 281

Zeta Force Pty Ltd v FC of T 98 ATC 4681

Davis and Anor v FC of T 89 ATC 4377

Harmer v FC of T (1991) CLR 264

Grollo Nominees Pty Ltd v FC of T 97 ATC 4585



REASONS FOR DECISION



16 April 1999 Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



1. The applicant seeks review of an objection decision by the respondent in relation to her amended income tax assessment for the year of income ended 30 June 1992. Two issues arise:

(a) Whether an amount of $19,439.23 was correctly included in the assessable income of the applicant pursuant to s 97 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"); and

(b) Whether additional tax for incorrect return was correctly assessed and, if so, whether there should be a further remission of the additional tax.

  1. Section 97 of the Act includes the following relevant provisions:

"97(1) Where a beneficiary of a trust estate who is not under any legal disability is presently entitled to a share of the income of the trust estate –

(a) the assessable income of the beneficiary shall include –

(i) so much of that share of the net income of the trust estate as is attributable to a period when the beneficiary was a resident; and

(ii) so much of that share of the net income of the trust estate as is attributable to a period when the beneficiary was not a resident and is also attributable to sources in Australia; and

(b) ... .

97(2) A reference in this section to income of a trust estate to which a beneficiary is presently entitled shall be read as not including a reference to income of a trust estate –

(a) to which a beneficiary is deemed to be presently entitled by virtue of the operation of subsection 95A(2) where the beneficiary –

(i) is a natural person;

(ii) is a resident at the end of the year of income;

(iii) is not, in respect of that income, a beneficiary in the capacity of a trustee of another trust estate; and

(iv) is not ... ; or

(b) ... ."



3. Subsection 95A(2) provides, for the purposes of the Act, that where a beneficiary has a vested and indefeasible interest in any of the income of a trust estate but is not presently entitled to that income the beneficiary shall be deemed to be presently entitled to that income of the trust estate.

4. Section 101 of the Act provides that for the purposes of the Act where a trustee has a discretion to pay or apply income of a trust estate to or for the benefit of specified beneficiaries, a beneficiary in whose favour the trustee exercises his discretion shall be deemed to be presently entitled to the amount paid to him or applied for his benefit by the trustee in the exercise of that discretion.

5. Section 223 of the Act provides for the imposition of additional tax for statements made to taxation officers which are false or misleading. Section 227(3) provides that the respondent Commissioner (and this Tribunal by virtue of s 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act")) may remit the whole or any part of the additional tax imposed.

6. At the hearing Mr Robertson of counsel appeared for the applicant and Ms Holmes of counsel appeared for the respondent. The documents lodged in the Tribunal pursuant to s 37 of the AAT Act were put before the Tribunal and a statement of agreed facts was marked as Exhibit A. No oral evidence was led on behalf of either party.

  1. I formally find the following facts as set out in Exhibit A:

"1. The issues in dispute relate to the year of income ended 30 June 1992.

  1. The Applicant was a beneficiary of the Dam and Do Family Trust ("the Trust").
  2. The Trustee of the Trust was Masterhaven Pty Ltd ("the Trustee") (ACN 054026836).
  3. The Trustee carried on business as a clothing manufacturer.
  4. The Applicant was the spouse of DAM Quang Thang, one of the appointers and shareholders of the Trustee.
  5. The Applicant was not a shareholder or director of the Trustee, nor was the Applicant an appointer under the Trust Deed.
  6. The Applicant was appointed secretary of Masterhaven Pty Ltd on 11 December 1991.
  7. Dam Quang Thang was appointed director and principal executive officer of Masterhaven Pty Ltd on 11 December 1991.
  8. Do Van Lam was appointed director of Masterhaven Pty Ltd on 11 December 1991.
  9. Clause 5 of the Trust Deed provides that:

(a) The trustee may in its absolute discretion at any time and from time to time and including the perpetuity date:

(i) Pay or transfer all or part of the income to all the beneficiaries or to one or more of the beneficiaries to the exclusion of the other or others of the beneficiaries and in such shares or proportions as the Trustee may decide.

(ii) Pay or apply or set aside the whole or such part as the Trustee shall think fit of the income to or for the advancement maintenance education support or benefit of all the beneficiaries or to or for the advancement maintenance eduction support or benefit of all the beneficiaries of one or more of the beneficiaries to the exclusion of the other or others of the beneficiaries and in such shares or proportions or manner as the Trustee may decide.

...

(c) Any beneficiary for whom the income is held on trust pursuant to the irrevocable exercise of any discretion by the Trustee under the provisions of this clause 5 shall be deemed to have an immediate and indefeasible vested interest in such income it being the express intention of the Settlor (as the Trustee hereby acknowledges) that such beneficiary shall be presently entitled thereto.

(d) Any beneficiary for whom the income is held on trust pursuant to the revocable exercise of any discretion by the Trustee under the provisions of this clause 5 shall be deemed to have an immediate and indefeasible vested interest in such income on and from the time of exercise thereof it being the express intention of the Settlor (as the Trustee hereby acknowledges) that such beneficiary shall be presently entitled thereto until any subsequent revocation of the relevant exercise payment or transfer.

  1. Clause 6(a) of the Trust Deed provides that:

'In default of and subject to any exercise of any discretion, payment, application, transfer, accumulation or determination pursuant to any other provision of this deed ... the Trustee shall divide the income in each year and stand possessed of such income on the 30th June of such income year for such of the beneficiaries (Dam Quang Thang and Do Lam Van and their spouses) ... in equal shares.'

  1. On 30 June 1992 the Trustee resolved pursuant to clause 5 of the Trust Deed as follows:

'... the net income of the Dam and Do Family Trust for the year ended 30/06/92 be distributed [to] ... Thang Dam, Lam Do, Huong Nguyen and Huong Dam Equally.'

  1. On 30 June 1992 the Trustee further resolved:

'... that the income hereby paid and applied for the benefit of the aforesaid beneficiaries be entered into the books of the DAM AND DO FAMILY TRUST as having been so distributed and that the income be held by the trustee absolutely on behalf of each beneficiary in accordance with the terms of the trust deed.'

and

'... that the company as Trustee of the income so distributed to each of the beneficiaries may in its absolute discretion in accordance with the Trust Deed reinvest the same by way of loan or loans on behalf of each beneficiary to THE DAM AND DO FAMILY TRUST such loan or loans being interest free and repayable on demand.'

  1. Some time after its appointment as trustee the Trustee changed its name to Bluebirds Fashion Pty Ltd with the same ACN.
  2. A trust return was lodged for the Dam and Do Family Trust for the 1992 year of income declaring the net income of the trust as $12,171.00.
  3. No assessment was ever issued by the Commissioner to the Trustee.
  4. The Commissioner did not issue any amended assessment to the Trustee.
  5. In her income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1992 the Applicant showed a distribution of $3,042.00 from the Trust.
  6. On 15 September 1993 the Commissioner issued an assessment to the Applicant in respect of the year ended 30 June 1992.
  7. The Commissioner conducted an audit into the income tax affairs of the Applicant and the Trust.
  8. As a result of the audit, an amended assessment issued to the Applicant on 15 February 1994 increasing her income from the Trust by and amount of $15,666.00.
  9. The Trustee was liquidated on 28 August 1995.
  10. The appointers under the Trust Deed and shareholders of Bluebirds Fashion Pty Ltd, Dam Quang Thang and Do Van Lam, have been declared bankrupt.
  11. The Trust was wound up in 1995.
  12. The Commissioner conducted a further audit into the income tax affairs of the Applicant and the Trust.
  13. As a result of the further audit, an amended assessment issued to the Applicant on 27 August 1997 increasing her income from the Trust by an amount of $731.00.
  14. By notice in writing dated 23 October 1997, the Applicant objected agaisnt the amended assessment dated 27 August 1997.
  15. The Operative Part of a Deed of Renunciation signed by the Applicant and dated 23 October 1997 states:

'Nguyen hereby renounces all her interest in the income and capital of the Trust.'

  1. The Commissioner disallowed the Applicant's objection by letter dated 11 March 1998."

The Applicant's Submissions

8. The applicant contends that she is not presently entitled to any more than the amount of $3,042.00 as disclosed in her return of income. That was her share of the net income for the year of income because:

(a) the resolution of the trustee is only effective in relation to the income of the trust considered by the trustee;

(b) section 99A of the Act applies to tax the trustee on the additional income of the trust at least until the trustee ... discretion or by an Order of the Court;

(c) the applicant is now not able to be considered for any such benefit because of the Deed of Renunciation; and

(d) the net income of the trust estate is only $12,171.00 until the Commissioner assesses the Trustee and the applicant is only entitled to $3,042.00 of that amount.



  1. Mr Robertson submitted that "present entitlement" as a matter of general law is to be determined in accordance with the decision of the High Court in Harmer v FC of T [1991] HCA 51; (1991) 173 CLR 264. In that case the income in dispute was interest derived on moneys paid into Court and deposited at interest with a Building Society by order of the Court. The funds were in the name of solicitors acting for a claimant and who held the moneys on trust pending Orders of the Court for resolution of competing claims. The High Court held that during the year of income there was no beneficiary presently entitled to the interest earned on the moneys deposited with the building society. Section 99A of the Act therefore applied to make the solicitors assessable pursuant to section 99A.
  2. In coming to that conclusion the High Court made it clear at page 271 that whether a beneficiary was either presently entitled or had a vested and indefeasible interest in the income is to be determined as at the time when the interest on the deposit was derived, ie, during the year of income and was not dependent upon later Orders of Court in relation to the moneys.
  3. The High Court quoted with approval the propositions of the parties that:
    • a beneficiary is "presently entitled" to a share of the income of a trust estate if, but only if:

(a) the beneficiary has an interest in the income which is both vested in interest and vested in possession; and

(b) the beneficiary has a present legal right to demand and receive payment of the income

  • such present entitlement does not depend upon the ascertainment of the precise entitlement before the end of the relevant year of income nor does it depend upon whether the trustee has funds available for immediate payment (173 CLR at 271).

  1. Mr Robertson submitted that the words "vested" and "indefeasible" in the context of trust law are technical legal words of limitation ... vested in interest is to be distinguished from vested in possession. Vested in interest is a present right of future enjoyment whereas vested in possession is a present right of present enjoyment. He submits that the additional amount of $65,615 remains at large as trust income because the trustee's discretion to appoint income can only be exercised after the income has been received by the trustee.
  2. The amount in question was "uncovered" by the respondent a number of years later as a result of an audit. It seems to be made up of amounts of income diverted to bank accounts of persons other than the trustee. It is not clear to me that these amounts have ever been received or dealt with by the trustee. Nor is it clear to me what is the amount of income derived by the trustee. Presumably the trustee has not had an opportunity to dispute the respondent's assertion as to quantum because the income has been assessed under s 97 of the Act.
  3. Clause 6(a) of the trust deed for the Dam and Do Family Trust provides for distribution of the income of the trust in default of the trustee exercising the discretion conferred by Clause 5 of the Deed (T29). In essence Clause 6 provides that the trustee shall divide the income in each year and stand possessed of such income on the 30 June of the income year for the appointed beneficiaries as tenants in common in equal shares. The applicant is one of four appointed beneficiaries.
  4. The applicant submits that takers-in-default have no rights to any income that the trustee has not at least considered for distribution under any power of appointment (citing Jackson v Parrott (1896) 1 Ch 281). There was no distribution to the takers-in-default. The applicant's case is that she has two separate rights under the Trust Deed rather than one right divided into two parts. She has a proprietory equitable chose in action as a taker-in-default and also a right to be considered as a discretionary object. When she renounced her interest in the trust that was effective to renounce both rights.
  5. The applicant also submitted that because the trustee had not been assessed on the contended net income of the trust there was an unfair shifting of burden of proof to the applicant. By not assessing the trustee in relation to the net income of the trust the share of the net income attributable to the applicant for taxation purposes cannot be established. Where, as here, no assessment has been made of the trustee then the anterior requirement for the operation of s 97 of the Act is not met and there is no obligation on the applicant to prove that the net income of the trust is excessive. Rather, the applicant's burden is in showing that what is received, if it is received, is not income and also receivibility without receipt is nothing for the purposes of ss 25 and 26(b) of the Act.
  6. For the respondent Ms Holmes relied primarily on the factual material. Particular reference was made to bank vouchers indicating that the applicant was aware of funds being diverted from the trustee (T31). The respondent also submitted that I should draw an adverse inference in this regard because of the failure of the applicant to attend and give evidence.
  7. The respondent also submitted that the resolution of the trustee of 30 June 1999 must be accepted as being applicable to the income knowingly diverted from the trustee by the beneficiaries. In these circumstances the default provision (Clause 6(a)) therefore has no application, the resolution being effective to distribute all of the income of the trust.
  8. In so far as the applicant sought to renounce her interest in the trust in 1997 that was irrelevant to the net income of the trust derived in 1992, she having accepted her interest in the income derived in 1992.
  9. In any event, the respondent submits, the applicant has a deemed present entitlement because of the operation of s 101 of the Act.
  10. In Zeta Force Pty Ltd v FC of T 98 ATC 4681 the Federal Court (Sundberg J) upheld what has been referred to as the "proportionate view" in relation to the operation of s 97 of the Act (so described by Hill J in Davis and Anor v FC of T 89 ATC 4377 at 4403).
  11. At 98 ATC 4686 Sundberg J discussed the operation of s 97 as follows:

"The words 'income of the trust estate' in the opening part of s 97(1) refer to distributable income, that is to say income ascertained by the trustee according to appropriate accounting principles and the trust instrument. That the words have this meaning is confirmed by the use elsewhere in Div 6 of the contrasting expression 'net income of the trust estate'. The beneficiary's 'share' is his share of the distributable income.

Having identified the share of the distributable income to which the beneficiary is presently entitled, s 97(1) requires one to ascertain 'that share of the net income of the trust estate'. That share is included in the beneficiary's assessable income. The expression 'net income of the trust estate' in par (a)(i) has the meaning given it by s 95(1) – taxable income as opposed to distributable income. The words 'that share' in par (a)(i) refer back to the words 'share' in the expression 'a share of the income of the trust estate' and indicate that the same share is to be applied to an income amount calculated according to a different formula (taxable income as opposed to distributable income). Since the income amount may differ according to which formula is applied, the natural meaning to give to 'share' where it appears for the second time is 'proportion' rather than 'part' or 'portion'. When Parliament wanted to convey the latter meaning, as it did in ss 99 and 99A, it used the words 'part".

The contrast between the expressions 'share of the income of the trust estate' and 'that share of the net income of the trust estate' shows that the draftsman has sought to relate the concept of present entitlement to distributable income, and not to taxable income, which is, after all, an artificial tax amount. Once the share of the distributable income to which the beneficiary is presently entitled is worked out, the notion of present entitlement has served its purpose, and the beneficiary is to be taxed on that share (or proportion) of the taxable income of the trust estate."



  1. His Honour then analysed previous relevant cases and concluded that his own analysis was supported by the weight of authority (p 4693) so that the proportionate method is that contemplated by s 97. I respectfully agree as I understand I am bound to do in the light of his Honour's analysis. I therefore uphold the submission for the respondent that I should adopt the proportionate method.
  2. Contrary to the applicant's case I am satisfied that no issue arises as to the quantum of the net income of the trust estate. That amounts of income were diverted from the trust is clear enough but I am satisfied that income was in fact derived by the trustee in that capacity and diverted from the trustee, about which the trustee and the beneficiaries were either aware as a matter of personal knowledge or should be deemed to be aware in the case of the trustee. The income was derived by the trust and was at all relevant times part of the net income of the trust estate although diverted and hidden as to amounts of money. In the light of the analysis of Sundberg J it seems to me to be irrelevant that amounts of income derived by the trust were hidden at the time that the trustee exercised its discretion to distribute a lesser amount to the beneficiaries.
  3. As to the applicant's renouncement of her interest as a beneficiary I accept that she sought to renounce both her right to be considered as a discretionary object of the trust and also her right, in terms of Clause 6(a) of the deed, as a taker in default. I find that the applicant signed the deed renouncing all her interest in the income and capital of the trust more than five years after the end of the year of income and more than four years after lodging an income tax return in which the applicant acknowledged that she was presently entitled to income of the trust. This is not a case of disclaimer upon becoming aware of the interest in the trust. It is an attempt to undo the past – an attempt destined, in my view, to be ineffective in relation to income derived in earlier years. The applicant's argument in that regard relies in part on the prospective operation of Clause 6 of the trust deed. In the light of the analysis by Sundberg J in Zeta Force consideration of Clause 6 is not necessary.
  4. The applicant's burden of proof under s 14ZZK of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 relates to the assessment of her taxable income for the year ended 30 June 1992. That there may also be a burden of proof in relation to the trustee in respect of an assessment of the trustee does not , in my view, alter the effect of s 14ZZK as it applies to the applicant (see Grollo Nominees Pty Ltd v FC of T 97 ATC 4585.
  5. As to penalties the respondent submits that the applicant was not an innocent beneficiary. I accept that is the case. However, the relevant false or misleading statement in this case is that of the trustee when it lodged the trust's return of income for the year ended 30 June 1992. In some circumstances that might be thought to be a basis for a full remission of additional tax under s 227 of the Act. This is, however, not such a case because I am satisfied it is more likely than not that the applicant knew that the net income of the trust and therefore her share of the net income had been understated. I have come to that conclusion by inference from the bank vouchers (T31) which indicate that the applicant was well aware of the diversion of moneys from the trustee. I do not see any reason to interfere with the respondent's decision to remit part but not the whole of the additional tax imposed.
  6. For these reasons the decisions under review are affirmed.

I certify that the 28 preceding paragraphs are a true copy of the reasons for the decision herein of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)
