Re Michael Nassib Said and the Commissioner, Australian Federal Police [1983] AATA 288 (29 August 1983)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: MICHAEL NASSIB SAID

And: THE COMMISSIONER, AUSTRALIAN FEDERAL POLICE

No. W83/20

Freedom of Information Act

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

The Hon. G.D. Clarkson Q.C. (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information Act - exempt document - whether copy may be made with deletions - proceedings in absence of applicant - Secs. 22,37,45 and 63.

HEARING

CANBERRA

29:8:1983

ORDER

The decision of the Tribunal is:

(a) to affirm the decision under review;

(b) to direct that the sealed envelope presently in the safe custody of the Tribunal containing a two page photostat document received by the Tribunal during the hearing of this application from the respondent be delivered to the respondent at the expiration of 28 days after the day on which this decision is furnished to the applicant unless an appeal from this decision to the Federal Court of Australia is commenced.

DECISION

By an application dated 16 December, 1982 the applicant applied to The Commissioner, Australian Federal Police, for access to the documents described as follows:

"All Documents, films, tapes, Reports of any Description, Memorandums, Computer datas, Or any other information which are in your possession and/or Powers. Are urgently requested to be inspected by me personally

prior to making out a list of those which I shall be seeking copies off.

Please Note: That I also require All the Documents on my personal files as abovementioned to be backdated to the date of my arrival in Australia 15th May, 1966 or even beyond if possible."

By a letter dated 3 February 1983, Inspector Bates, the Officer-in-Charge of

the Freedom of Information Branch of that organization, replied saying:

"Notification of Refusal of Request Under Freedom of Information Act

1982.

Findings on Fact

Further to your request under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 (Commonwealth) (the Act), a search of the Registry records of the Australian Federal Police reveal documents that contain information, the nature of which, would, if released, be reasonably expected to disclose or ascertain, information and its source which has been gained in confidence. Thereby destroying the necessary confidential relationships between the Australian Federal Police and the sources of such confidential relationship developed in respect to the enforcement or administration of the law.

In addition the source from which the information was gained by the Australian Federal Police was given in such circumstances that imports that the disclosure of the relevant documents would constitute a breach of the necessary confidence between the information source and the Australian Federal Police."

The letter went on to quote relevant parts of s.37 and s.45 of the Freedom

of Information Act 1982, and to inform the applicant of his right under s.54 of that Act to obtain an internal review of the decision which had been made.

On 16 February 1983 the applicant wrote to The Commissioner of Police requesting an internal review of the decision and this review was carried out at the direction of the Commissioner, by Assistant Commissioner Watt, who on 1 March 1983 wrote to the applicant stating:

"Therefore, having reviewed the decision made by Inspector R. BATES, my

decision is:-

Access to documents in the possession of the A.F.P. is refused.

My reasons for refusing access are:-

(i) The documents are exempt documents under the provisions of the Act as disclosure could reasonably be expected to lead to the identity of a confidential source of information in relation to the enforcement or administration of the law. (Section 37(1)(b) refers).

(ii) Disclosure of the documents would constitute a breach of confidence under Section 45 of the Act."

At the same time the applicant was also informed that he could make an

application to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of the decision "refusing to grant access to the documents covered by your request for access". This the applicant did, by application received on 14 April 1983. The reasons for the application are stated as follows:

"To enable the Applicant to have access to his Police files under the Freedom of Information Act, 1982, to also enable him to correct the suspected false information which the Police Informers have been giving the Police about the Applicant in order to create the many existing problems which the Applicant currently having to endure and last but not least, to allow the Federal Police and the Community generally, the chance to clean up the dirty tricksters among its ranks of informers and therefore reveals the motives behind such falsification of information in order to character assassinate individuals such as myself who stand for truth and Justice."

It will be noted that on several occasions the police officers referred to "documents" and that no notice was given under s.25 of the Act, that the respondent neither confirmed nor denied the existence of any document.

At a directions hearing, counsel for the respondent indicated that according to the respondent, there was in fact only one relevant document, and this was later confirmed in writing. The applicant was not prepared to accept this statement, since his suspicion was that there were many documents in one or more files concerning him.

At the hearing, Inspector Bates, the officer who initiated enquiries following receipt of the applicant's letter of 16 December 1982 gave evidence regarding how the search was initiated and what it disclosed.

He explained the use of the term "documents" as a reference to the two pages or folios of the two-page report which the search revealed.

He gave evidence on oath that no other relevant document had been revealed by the search which had been made.

Whether or not the allegation that other relevant documents might exist was in issue in these proceedings it appeared to me that the applicant was more concerned with that issue than with the grounds on which exemption had been claimed for the report and that there was no possibility of the applicant being satisfied with any possible outcome of these proceedings, unless that question was considered. For that reason I allowed the applicant to cross-examine Inspector Bates at length on this and other aspects.

The document had previously been referred to by Inspector Bates in evidence as a "report . . of two pages and that report named Mr. Said". He said it did not come out of a personal file - which I took to be a personal file relating to Mr. Said - it was not in a file at all and he continued, "in fact the two particular documents were not even headed with his name. His name appeared in the context of the report. There was no file in particular held relative to the person known as Mr. Said."

Whilst I doubt whether the applicant's suspicions have been completely allayed, for myself I accept that a reasonably comprehensive search was undertaken and that the report already referred to is the only relevant document - apart from documents such as those relating to the search which came into existence after the applicant's request - which was thereby revealed.

Having first read the report and proceeding in the absence of the applicant under s.63 of the Freedom of Information Act, I heard further evidence from Inspector Bates regarding the report and further argument by the respondent's counsel.

I then continued the hearing in public and informed the applicant that I had heard further argument regarding the document and its contents, which argument I was unable to detail to him. After further discussion, the applicant decided not to call evidence.

The copy of the relevant document which I had received, I sealed in an envelope and placed in the safe custody of the Tribunal to await this decision.

Bearing in mind the requirements of s.63(2)(a) of the Freedom of Information Act and the content of the report to which I have referred, and of that portion of it referable to the applicant, I concluded firstly that the report of two pages is an exempt document in that its disclosure could reasonably be expected to disclose or enable a person to ascertain the identity of a confidential source of information in relation to the enforcement or administration of the law.

Secondly, I concluded from the information before me, it is a fair inference that the disclosure of the report would constitute a breach of confidence and that the report is on that ground also an exempt document.

Section 22 of the Act provides in effect that when a decision is made not to grant a request for access to a document on the ground that it is an exempt document, but that it is possible for the agency to make a copy of the document with such deletions, that the copy would not be an exempt document and would not by reason of the deletions be misleading, the agency shall make and grant access to such a copy.

I think that within the strictures imposed by s.63 of the Act I am able to say that in this case the content of the report, other than the four lines referred to, is exempt, and that deletions would be necessary from the four lines if that part of the report were to be rendered not exempt.

One might wonder if everything except four lines in which the applicant was named, was deleted from a two page report not about the applicant and any permissible deletions then made from the four lines, whether the remainder could be anything but misleading, but that question may be asked and left unanswered, because in the present case I am satisfied that it is not possible by deletions from the text of the document to produce a resulting document which is exempt but not misleading.

I propose therefore to affirm the decision under review.
