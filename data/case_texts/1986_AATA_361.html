Last Updated: 6 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Allowable Deductions - Hoteliers - Entertainment Expenses - Usefulness of written records - Finding of fact.

Income Tax Assessment Act, s 51, S 190

case Q5 83 ATC 16

No. TT86/367-369

No. B5735-5736/1986

No. B5737-5738/1986

AAT Decision No 3205

Mr. P.M. Roach (Senior Member)

13 November 1986 HOBART

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. B5735-5736/1986

TAXATION APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) No. B5737-5738/1986

Re:

Applicants

And:

Respondent REASONS FOR DECISION

Mr. P.M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. The issues in these two references, heard together by consent, relate to a husband/wife partnership carrying on a business as hotel-keepers in a small country town. The business was carried on throughout the year of income ended 30 June, 1982 and until 8 August 1982 in the following fiscal year.

2. The partnership claimed deductions, described in its returns of income as "Entertaining", in sums of $3,990 and $400 respectively. The claims were intended to describe amounts said to have been expended in the purchase of drinks for patrons of the business - drinks provided in order to promote patronage and thereby increase profitability. It was agreed that the claim in the earlier year also included an amount of $180 spent on the provision of musical entertainment and to that extent entitlement to a deduction was not in dispute. Of the amounts of $3,990 and $400 respectively claimed by the partnership the Commissioner, after objection in one case and before objection in the other, allowed deductions in sums of $1,040 and $100 only thereby increasing the taxable income of each partner by $1,475 and $150 in successive years. The Applicants, being dissatisfied, thereupon became Applicants before the Tribunal. Objections relating to the disallowance of a claim to travelling expenses were abandoned at the hearing.

3. At the hearing evidence was largely directed towards establishing that the business followed a "system" common in the industry. The system involved the partnership drawing funds weekly in cash and then using the funds so drawn as a cash resource in order to pay for drinks provided for customers at the expense of the business. The monies so paid would be included in the "takings" of the bar and found in due course as an indistinguishable part of the figures recorded daily in the cash book for bar-sales. The cash book provided the only documentary record of the drawings. The Commissioner was understandably, and not unreasonably, suspicious at the regularity of entries as to amounts drawn, particularly when drawings of the usual amount were recorded in periods when the proprietors were physically absent from the business. To the extent to which monies drawn were not so expended but rather applied to non-deductible purposes the partnership income would be understated. The case for the Applicants was that the records were accurate or, at least, that no overstatement of drawings applied to deductible purposes was involved.

4. The Applicants sought to establish their case by evidence of the wife as to her husband's practice in drawing funds for the entertainment in question (the monies initially being retained by the husband) and for private housekeeping (paid to her); by evidence of the wife, a regular barmaid and a regular customer, who during a short period also served as a casual barman, as to the practice of providing a daily 6.00 pm "shout" for the entire bar and of other "shouting" from time to time by the husband either as a member of a "school" or otherwise and time to time from both sides of the bar; and by the evidence of three accountants experienced in varying degrees in providing professional services for Hotels, two of whom were not associated with the Applicants, as to the common practice of hoteliers "shouting" and as to the method of recording used by the Applicants being common place and, as a practical matter, sound. I accept all of that evidence so far as it goes but by its terms it falls short of establishing the claim of the Applicants.

5. No evidence-in-chief was elicited from any of the accountants seeking to establish that the amount allegedly expended was reasonable in amount having regard to the nature of the trade or of the particular hotel or its particular trade figures. Nor were any of the Accountants cross-examined to suggest the contrary. Further, the husband-applicant, as the witness potentially best equipped to directly establish the propositions in dispute, did not give evidence due to working commitments interstate. If I accept (as I do) that the amounts in question were drawn to the extent claimed in relation to each week and that "shouts" were paid for from that fund so that the monies so paid were physically returned to the till and later included in gross sales, it does not automatically follow that it is established that the whole of the monies so drawn were so expended.

6. As the evidence stands I am not persuaded that all of the monies so drawn were expended as alleged. In particular I am not persuaded by the evidence of the wife that additional monies were so expended by her husband and, in modest measure, by herself in the interest of the partnership and not recorded at all or claimed as deductions. I am also conscious that the husband was the only person who could have given evidence explaining why drawings were so regularly and consistently recorded when the actual amount expended on "shouts" might reasonably have been expected to vary from week to week.

7. The difficulty is illustrated by Case Q5 (1983 ATC 16), a decision relied on by the Commissioner. There a taxpayer employed by a public company as a shipping superintendent claimed to have bought drinks at Hotels at various ports for sub-contractors, for foremen and for colleagues to secure a greater measure of cooperation then he would have obtained otherwise. The Board of Review said (at page 19):-

"Because we regard the taxpayer as a witness of complete truth, we believe that his situation could well have been one where his outlays were properly deductible. But Boards of Review have stated in very plain terms over many years that something more is required to substantiate a claim for entertainment expenses than a mere estimate of those outlays. For instance, in Case No. 107 (1951) 1 TBRD 453 (which hearing also dealt with a claim for entertainment expenses), this Board as then constituted stated p.456;"

"In our opinion, it is necessary in order to establish an outgoing or outgoings in case of this kind, for the taxpayer to provide details of the expenditure incurred, showing with reasonable precision when, where and upon whom the sum or each of the sums concerned was spent, and the person or persons entertained in the process"

"We agree with that statement in its entirety. In the case before us, it is clear from the testimony of the taxpayer that he kept no records of the monies that he took from his pocket for entertainment expenses and that all three components of his total claim are nothing more than estimates. In the absence of precise, definite and complete evidence as to the nature of the expenditure concerned, we are unable to determine to what extent, if at all, the taxpayer's outlays were incurred for the purpose of producing assessable income".

"Having in mind the nature of the outlays we can appreciate the difficulty of procuring vouchers, but we consider it encumbent on the taxpayer to maintain a contemporaneous written record in a notebook (or similar book possessing a measure of permanence) of the expenditures outlaid by him. In the instant case, the taxpayer had no records to substantiate his claim, not even a diary record. He was not therefore in a position to discharge the statutory onus placed on him".

8. The passage quoted at the one time says too much and too little. I do not accept that a claim such as was presented in that case or in these references cannot be established without producing such a record. Nor do I accept that it is established automatically by producing such a record.

9. Testimony unsubstantiated by written records may be true or may be false. However the presentation of detailed, neat, precise and orderly written records does not of itself establish that they are accurate or even that they were honestly compiled. Such documents are only worthy of credit as a function of the credit worthiness of the persons responsible for their compilation and presentation. I also observe that the factual issue to be determined by the making of an appropriate finding of fact does not differ in nature because it arises in the course of tax litigation rather then in the course of the civil claim (for example, as a claim for loss of profits occasioned by a breach of contract or a tort). In either case a particular question to be determined would be what part of the gross takings of the business were expended in the course of entertaining customers.

10. Making the best judgment I can upon the available evidence and after taking into account the circumstance that the one person who could have given the most direct evidence gave no evidence, I find that in year of income 30 June 1982 the partnership expended $180 on musical entertainment and $2340 on "shouts" and in the year of income ended the 30 June, 1983 the amount of $300 on "shouts".

11. That being so and allowing for $1,040 and $300 already allowed by the Respondent I would reduce the taxable income of each Applicant by the sums of $740 and $100 in the years of income ended 30 June, 1982 and 1983 respectively.
