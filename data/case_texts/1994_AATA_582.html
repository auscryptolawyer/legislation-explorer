DECISIONS AND REASONS FOR DECISION



INCOME TAX - Contract for services frustrated by Taxpayer’s death. Whether sum paid pursuant to contract income derived or only potential income subject to completion of contract.



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - S.25(1)

Frustrated Contracts Act 1978 (NSW) - S.12

REASONS FOR DECISION



5 August 1994 Senior Member M.D. Allen



1. In this matter the executor of the estate of a deceased taxpayer challenges the Commissioner’s assessment as income of the Deceased of an amount paid pursuant to a contract, the performance of the terms of which was frustrated by the taxpayer’s death.

2. The Deceased Taxpayer (D) had skill in design. Because of this skill he was engaged by a syndicate (S) and entered into a contract with the said syndicate. The events were succinctly set out in a statement of agreed facts and issue which was tendered by the parties and became Exhibit A in these proceedings. Those agreed facts (suitably edited) state:



“1. The deceased carried on business as a designer.

2. Pursuant to a written agreement dated 11 September, 1987 between S of the first part and D of the second part [‘the agreement’], D agreed at the request of S to make available his services to S subject to and upon the terms and conditions of the agreement.



3. At the request of S, the deceased attended meetings with representatives of S in Japan on December 7, 8 and 9, 1987.

4. On 4 February, 1988, the sum of US$125,000 [A.$185,847] was paid into D’s bank account. This was the initial fee referred to in Clause 5.2 of the agreement.



5. The deceased died on 1 May, 1988.



3. The relevant clauses of the contract entered into between D and S read:

“1.1 S hereby engages the services of D as from 1st December, 1987 and D shall subject to the provisions of Clause 2.1 hereof make his services available to S from such date for the term of this Agreement. ...



1.2 Should S continue to use D’s services after the expiration of the term of this Agreement, the terms of this Agreement shall extend to the provision of D’s services thereafter, subject to the proviso that the provision of D’s services thereafter may be terminated by either S or D by giving not less than 14 days prior notice in writing to the other.



2.0 TERM



2.1 The term of this Agreement shall consist of two (2) periods of no more than six (6) consecutive weeks each within a twelve (12) month period, the first of such twelve month periods commencing on 1st December, 1987 or earlier if agreed in writing by D.



2.2 S hereby acknowledges and agrees that D shall be entitled to be paid the fee referred to in Clause 5.1(a) hereof in full whether or not S requires the provision of D’s services for any of the six weekly periods referred to in Clause 2.1 hereof.



...



5.0 REMUNERATION



5.1 S shall remunerate D for the services provided hereunder by him as follows:



(a) by a fee of $US125,000.00 for each of the consecutive six (6) weekly periods referred to in Clause 2.1 hereof;



(b) by a fee of $US20,834.00 per week payable pro rata for any services provided by D during the period of two (2) years from 1st December, 1987 but in addition to the two (2) six (6) weekly periods referred to in Clause 2.1 hereof.



...



8.0 TERMINATION



8.1 S shall be entitled to terminate this Agreement by giving D thirty (30) days’ prior written notice and paying D in addition to such notice an amount equal to the fee payable under Clause 5.1(a) hereof in respect of one six (6) weekly period, if D is made bankrupt or if D shall:



(i) commit any serious breach of any provisions hereof; or



(ii) be guilty of any grievous misconduct or gross neglect in performance of any duty herein mentioned.



8.2 If S is placed in liquidation or a receiver is appointed to it or any of its assets or if S breaches this Agreement by failure to make any payments due hereunder within the time stipulated then upon fourteen (14) days notice D shall be relieved of all further obligations on his part.”



4. The issue between the parties was expressed in the following terms:

“Whether the whole or part [and if part, what part] of the sum of $185,847 constitutes assessable income dervied by the deceased in the period 1 July 1987 to 1 May 1988.”

5. It was argued for the Applicant that the case fell squarely within the principles set forth in Arthur Murray (NSW) Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1965] HCA 58; 114 CLR 314, namely, that the amount received by D was not income and that S was entitled to the refund of the said amount pursuant to the Frustrated Contracts Act 1978 (NSW) S.12.

6. Clause 17.1 of the contract between the parties specifically provided:

“The interpretation, construction and performance of this Agreement and the rights and remedies of the parties hereunder shall in all respect be governed by the laws of the State of New South Wales. Each party hereby submits itself to the jurisdiction of the courts of that State and the Commonwealth of Australia having jurisdiction in that State.

7. For his part the Respondent contended that the sum of $US125,000.00 was received by D as income and that, having regard to Clauses 2.1, 5.1 and 8.1 of the contract, the said amount had “come home” to the Taxpayer and thus was income in his hands and taxable.

8. In the course of submissions it was put by the Advocate for the Applicant that D had performed no work under the contract. I reject this submission. There was no direct evidence called as to what transpired at the meetings in Japan of December 7, 8 and 9 but it strains credulity to infer that, having signed a contract for services which was to commence as and from 1 December 1987, D’s travel to Japan to attend meetings with S’s representatives was not undertaken pursuant to the contract. In particular Clause 6.1 of the contract stated that all travel, accommodation and living expenses incurred by D, in providing his services to S, shall be the sole responsibility of S and no evidence was adduced to show any expenditure by D for this trip.

9. To accept the Applicant’s argument, I must find that S would have succeeded in an action pursuant to S12 of the Frustrated Contracts Act (NSW). Although the death of D could be said to have resulted in the contract being frustrated, cf Lobb v Vasey Housing Auxillary (War Widows Guild) [1963] VicRp 38; (1963) VR 239; Cutter v Powell 101 ER 573, this would not be the case if, as argued by the Respondent, D was entitled to the sum of $US125,000.00 in any event upon the signing of the contract.

10. Mr Edmunds of counsel for the Respondent put stress upon Clause 8.1 of the contract which provides for its termination by S. Although, as pointed out by the Applicant’s Advocate, Clause 8.1 deals only with S’s rights to terminate, this clause in conjunction with Clause 2.2 makes it clear that D was entitled to the sum of $US125,000.00 forthwith, upon the signing of the contract. In other words, to use the now familiar term of the High Court in Arthur Murray supra, the amount had “come home” to D.

11. S at no time sought to commence an action seeking the return of the money paid to D. As I interpret the contract, they would have been unsuccessful in that action in that the said amount had properly been paid pursuant to the said contract.

12. The said sum was income in the hands of D, cf Scott v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 3 ATD 142 at p144-5, Hayes v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1956] HCA 21; 96 CLR 47 at p56-57 and Scott v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1966] HCA 48; 117 CLR 514 at p527-528, and as such is subject to income tax.

13. The objection decision under review is affirmed.
