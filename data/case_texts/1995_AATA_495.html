REASONS FOR DECISION



11 April 1995 B H Pascoe Senior Member



1. These proceedings concern an application for review of a decision by the respondent Commissioner of Taxation to disallow an objection lodged by the applicant against an assessment of income tax based on income for the year ended 30 June 1990. In that objection the applicant had claimed that an amount of $70,349 should not have been included in assessable income pursuant to section 26AAC of the Income Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").



2. The facts in this case were not in dispute. The applicant had been an employee of M Ltd which was a wholly owned subsidiary of B Ltd, a public listed company. In April 1988 he was invited to participate in the 1988 B Ltd Executive Share Option Plan. In May 1988 he applied for and was granted options to purchase 10,500 shares in B Ltd at an exercise price of $5.58 per share. The cost of the options was one dollar ($1). In April 1989 the number of options was increased to 21,000 at an exercise price of $2.79 as a result of a one for one bonus issue of shares by B Ltd. The terms of the Share Option Plan relating to the exercise period and cancellation of options were as follows:-

"Commencement of Option Exercise Period



The option exercise period shall commence on the first to occur of the:



(a) Expiration of three years of continuous service by the Participant with B Ltd or one of its subsidiaries from date of issue of options.



(b) Death of the Participant.



(c) Total and permanent disability (as determined by the Board of Directors in their absolute discretion) of the Participant.



(d) Normal retirement (as determined by the Board of Directors in their absolute discretion) of the Participant.



(e) Early retirement of the Participant with consent of B Ltd.



(f) Retrenchment (as determined by the Board of Directors in their absolute discretion) of the Participant.



Option Cancellation



The options issued to the Participant shall be cancelled on the first to occur of the following:



(a) The expiration of five years from the date of their issue if not previously exercised.



(b) Resignation or dismissal (for fraud, defalcation or misconduct) of the Participant from the service of B Ltd or one of its subsidiaries prior to the expiration of three years of continuous service from date of issue of the option.



(c) Bankruptcy of the Participant.



(d) At the expiration of seven days following the resignation or dismissal (for any reason) of the Participant from the service of B Ltd or one of its subsidiaries after the expiration of three years of service.



(e) At the expiration of six months following upon (whenever occurring) the normal retirement, early retirement or retrenchment of the Participant.



(f) At the expiration of one year following upon (whenever occurring) the death or total and permanent disability of the Participant."



3. On 30 April 1989 M Ltd was sold to C Ltd. The applicant, as an employee of M Ltd, continued his same employment but was no longer employed by a subsidiary of B Ltd. At the time of the sale of M Ltd, the Board of Directors of B Ltd decided that it would treat executives of units, divisions or companies within the B Ltd group which were disposed of as having been retrenched, thus giving rise to the discretion to permit the early exercise of options. At a Board meeting on 14 July 1989 it was resolved to permit the applicant to exercise the 1988 Share Option Plan options on the basis that the sale of M Ltd had led to his retrenchment as an executive of the B Ltd group of companies effective from 30 April 1989. By letter of 20 July 1989 the company secretary wrote to the applicant as follows:

"I have been instructed to inform you that following the sale of M Ltd, the directors have decided to exercise their discretion to permit you to convert your 1988 options in B Limited to shares.



This right will lapse if it is not exercised within six months of the date of the termination of your service with the company, which was effective 30 April 1989.



If you wish to take up this opportunity, please fill out and sign the reverse of your option certificate and return it to me together with a bank cheque representing the total exercise price, not later than 30 October 1989."



On 14 September 1989 the applicant acquired 21,000 shares in B Ltd at $2.79 per share for a sum of $58,590. On that date the market value of the shares was $6.14 per share, a total of $128,940.



4. With his income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1990 in which an amount of $70,349 was shown as assessable income, the applicant requested a private ruling pursuant to section 169A of the Act on the taxation treatment of that amount. In the request the applicant submitted that this sum was not assessable under the terms of section 26AAC. The respondent provided a private ruling that the "sum of $70,349 is clearly a benefit obtained via a scheme for the acquisition of shares by an employee and thus remains included in your assessable income". The applicant objected to the subsequent assessment of income tax and it is the respondent's disallowance of that objection which is the subject of these proceedings. The amount of $70,349 was calculated as follows:

Market value of shares acquired $128,940

less Amount paid for options 1

Amount paid on exercise of options 58,590 $ 58,591

$ 70,349



5. The basic argument put by the applicant was that, under the terms of the 1988 Share Option Plan, he had no entitlement to exercise the options after M Ltd had been sold. It was argued that he was not retrenched. He continued to perform his same duties on the day after the date of sale as an employee of M Ltd. He received no payment in consequence of any termination of employment. Whilst his employer was no longer a subsidiary of B Ltd, under no circumstances could he be said to have resigned, been dismissed or retrenched. It was then said that, as he was not an employee of B Ltd and was not retrenched by B Ltd, the Board of Directors did not have a discretion not to cancel the options and to treat him as having been retrenched so as to permit him to exercise the options some five months after ceasing to be an employee of a subsidiary company. This argument, it was said, leads to the only view that there was no legitimate way under the Share Option Plan for the shares to be issued to the applicant. It was then contended that the shares were simply issued as a means of B Ltd raising additional capital without the expense of preparing and lodging a prospectus. Alternatively, it was an unexpected windfall gain to the applicant. Accordingly, it was submitted, the only time the Act can operate is to tax any gain on the ultimate disposal of the shares.



6. The respondent submitted that, notwithstanding that the applicant was not in the employ of a company within the B Ltd group of companies on 14 September 1989, the 21,000 shares were acquired by the applicant in circumstances contemplated by the provisions of section 26AAC(1)(a)(i) and/or (ii) of the Act so that the amount of $70,349 was properly included in assessable income pursuant to section 26AAC(5).



7. The relevant provisions of section 26AAC, insofar as they relate to this matter, are:

"26AAC(1) For the purposes of this section, a taxpayer shall be taken to have acquired a share in a company, or a right to acquire a share in a company, under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees if-



(a) in the case of a share, the share was acquired by the taxpayer-



(i) in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer; or



(ii) as a result of the exercise or operation of a right to acquire the share, being a right that was acquired by the taxpayer in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer; or



(b) in the case of a right, the right was acquired by the taxpayer in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer.



26AAC(2) Where a taxpayer who has acquired a right to acquire a share in a company in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer disposes of, and re-acquires, the right on one or more occasions, each such re-acquisition of the right shall be taken, for the purposes of this section, to be an acquisition of the right in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, that employment of, or those services rendered by, the taxpayer or that relative of the taxpayer, as the case may be.



26AAC(3) A reference in this section to a share in a company, or a right to acquire a share in a company, having been acquired by a taxpayer in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer includes, but is not limited to, a reference to such a share or right having been acquired by a taxpayer-



(a) in pursuance of an agreement, arrangement or understanding under which a company was to issue shares in the company to employees of the company or of another company or to relatives of those employees; or



(b) in pursuance of the terms of a trust deed under which a trustee is required or authorized to sell, or otherwise to transfer, shares in a company to employees of the company or of another company or to relatives of those employees.



26AAC(4) This section applies to and in relation to an acquisition by a taxpayer of a share in a company, or of a right to acquire a share in a company, if, and only if-



(a) in the case of a share, the share was acquired by the taxpayer after 17 September 1974 otherwise than as a result of the exercise or operation of a right that-



(i) being a right that had not previously been acquired and disposed of by the taxpayer - was acquired by the taxpayer on or before that date; or



(ii) being a right that had previously been acquired and disposed of by the taxpayer, was first acquired by the taxpayer on or before that date; or



(b) in the case of a right to acquire a share-



(i) where the right had not previously been acquired and disposed of by the taxpayer - the right was acquired by the taxpayer after 17 September 1974; or



(ii) where the right had previously been acquired and disposed of by the taxpayer - the right was first acquired by the taxpayer after that date,



and a reference in this section to the acquisition by a taxpayer of a share or a right to acquire a share shall be construed accordingly.



26AAC(5) Where a taxpayer has acquired during the year of income a share in a company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees, the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income includes the value of that share at the time when it was acquired by the taxpayer less the sum of-



(a) the amount, if any, paid or payable by the taxpayer as consideration for the share; and



(b) if the taxpayer acquired the share as a result of the exercise or operation of a right (whether that right was unconditional or subject to conditions) to acquire the share - the amount, if any, paid or payable by the taxpayer as consideration for the right."



8. There was no dispute that the original options to purchase shares were acquired by the applicant in respect of, or in relation to, employment of the applicant. What was in dispute was whether the shares acquired on 14 September 1989 were acquired as a result of the exercise of those options. The applicant says that they were not because, under the terms of the Share Option Plan, the option was required to have been cancelled when the applicant ceased to be an employee of B Ltd or one of its subsidiaries. The respondent says that they were, because of the express determination of the directors of B Ltd to permit the applicant to exercise the options to acquire the shares and there was no other way, and no evidence to suggest that there was any other way, by which the applicant could have acquired the shares at the price paid.



9. The submission by the applicant concentrated on the provisions relating to cancellation of the options and the fact that the applicant was not, in fact, retrenched and the directors had no discretion under these provisions to determine that he was. Therefore, it was said the options were required to have been cancelled. Unfortunately, this innovative argument misinterprets the rules. Accepting that the applicant did not resign or retire and was not dismissed or retrenched, then the provisions of the Share Option Plan did not require cancellation. If the directors had not decided that they would treat executives of subsidiaries disposed of as having been retrenched, the applicant would not have been able to exercise the options as, under the rules for the option exercise period, he would not have had three years continuous service with a B Ltd subsidiary from the date of issue of the options and would not have satisfied any of the other criteria set out in (b) to (f) of those rules. The options would have lapsed at the end of five years. The directors did have an absolute discretion to determine that the applicant had been retrenched. By affidavit sworn by the company secretary of B Ltd, he stated that this determination was made as the applicant was considered to have been retrenched because there was no longer a job available to him in the B Ltd group of companies, the company he worked for having been sold. The effect of this determination was to give the applicant an ability to exercise his options prior to the expiration of three years from the date of issue of the options. It may have been possible to argue that the options could not be cancelled at the expiration of six months from the date of the deemed retrenchment, on the basis that this requirement for cancellation only applied to actual not deemed retrenchment. However, the applicant did pay for and was issued shares before the expiration of the six months period. I therefore find that the shares were acquired as a result of the exercise of a right acquired in relation to employment of the applicant.



10. Even if I am wrong in the interpretation of the rules I would still come to the same view. The actual application for the shares was not produced in evidence but there is every reason to assume that the applicant complied with the instructions contained in the letter from the company secretary of 20 July 1989 and completed the reverse of the option certificate as the formal application for the shares which were issued to him. Even if it could be accepted that his rights had lapsed or should have been cancelled, and I do not so accept, I find that the shares were acquired as a result of the exercise of those options acquired by the applicant under the Share Option Plan in May 1988. There was no other way in which he could have acquired or did acquire the shares at the price of $2.79 per share as demonstrated by any evidence provided to me.



11. I find that the provisions of section 26AAC(1)(a)(ii) apply to the relevant shares so as to include the amount of $70,349 in the assessable income of the applicant pursuant to section 26AAC(5) in the year of income ended 30 June 1990. Consequently the decision under review is affirmed.
