Re Vittorio Conte Ex Parte: Vittorio Conte v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1990] FCA 465; 679 Bankruptcy 91 Atc 4043 27 FCR 120 (23 November 1990)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: VITTORIO CONTE Ex parte: VITTORIO CONTE

And: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. QN 589 of 1990

FED No. 679

Bankruptcy

91 ATC 4043

27 FCR 120

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

BANKRUPTCY DISTRICT OF THE STATE OF QUEENSLAND

GENERAL DIVISION

Spender J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Bankruptcy - application to set aside bankruptcy notice or to extend the time for compliance with the bankruptcy notice - no order made extending the time for compliance with the bankruptcy notice within the time fixed for compliance with the notice - whether an act of bankruptcy had been committed - whether court has jurisdiction to extend time for compliance after time for compliance fixed in notice has expired - existence of judgment debt - claim of an agreement between the applicant and the Commission that payments made be apportioned to the applicant's personal liability in priority to the liability of a company of which applicant was a shareholder and director.

Bankruptcy Act 1966 ss. 40, 41(6A)

Thurgood v. National Bank of Australasia Ltd. [1981] FCA 73; (1981) 53 FLR 51

Streimer v. Tamas [1981] FCA 123; (1981) 37 ALR 211

Re Vella; ex parte Seymour [1983] FCA 115; (1982-83) 48 ALR 420

HEARING

BRISBANE

23:11:1990

Counsel for the applicant: Mr T. Somers

Instructed by: N. R. Barbi

Counsel for the respondent: Mr G. Egan

Instructed by: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The application to set aside Bankruptcy Notice No. 589 of 1990 or, alternatively, to extend the time for compliance with Bankruptcy Notice No. 589 of 1990 be dismissed.

The applicant to pay the respondent's costs of the application, to be taxed if not agreed.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with by Bankruptcy Rule 124 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

This is an application by Mr Vittorio Conte to set aside Bankruptcy Notice No. 589 of 1990 or, alternatively, that the time for compliance with that notice be extended pursuant to s. 41(6A)(b) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth).

2. On 18 May 1990 judgment was entered in default of entry of appearance in the District Court at Brisbane by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation against the applicant for "$56,728.08 for debt, $630.50 for costs, and $4,256.45 for interest." The applicant contends that that judgment debt is invalid, there being no debt.

3. The Bankruptcy Act 1966 ss.41(6A), (6B), and (6C) provides:

"41(6A) Where, before the expiration of the time

fixed by the Court or the Registrar for compliance with

the requirement of a bankruptcy notice -

(a) proceedings to set aside the judgment or

order in respect of which the bankruptcy notice was

issued have been instituted by the debtor; or

(b) an application to set aside the bankruptcy

notice has been filed with the Registrar,

the Court may, subject to subsection (6C),

extend the time for compliance with the bankruptcy

notice.

41(6B) Where, before the expiration of the time

fixed by the Registrar for compliance with the

requirements of a bankruptcy notice -

(a) proceedings to set aside the judgment or

order in respect of which the bankruptcy notice was

issued have been instituted by the debtor; or

(b) an application to set aside the bankruptcy

notice has been filed with the Registrar,

the Registrar may, subject to subsection (6C),

extend the time for compliance with the bankruptcy

notice.

41(6C) Where -

(a) a debtor applies to the Court or the

Registrar for an extension of the time for complying with

a bankruptcy notice on the ground that proceedings to set

aside the judgment or order in respect of which the

bankruptcy notice was issued have been instituted by the

debtor; and

(b) the Court or the Registrar, as the case

may be, is of the opinion that the proceedings to set

aside the judgment or order -

(i) have not been instituted bona fide; or

(ii) are not being prosecuted with due

diligence,

the Court or the Registrar, as the case may be,

shall not extend the time for compliance with the

bankruptcy notice."

4. The bankruptcy notice, which was a fourteen day notice, was served on 12 June 1990. The application to set aside the bankruptcy notice was filed on 25 June 1990, and so within the time limited for compliance with the bankruptcy notice.

5. No affidavit under s. 41(7) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 asserting any counter-claim, set-off or cross demand that could not have been set up in the proceedings in which the judgment was obtained was filed.

6. The application to set aside the bankruptcy notice having been filed with the Registrar before the expiration of the time fixed by the Registrar for compliance with the requirements of a bankruptcy notice, by s. 41(6A) "the Court may, subject to sub-section (6C) extend the time for compliance with the bankruptcy notice." A similar power is given in similar circumstances to the Registrar under s. 41(6B). No such extension has in fact been ordered. No proceedings to set aside the judgment or order in respect of which the bankruptcy notice was issued have been instituted by the debtor, and in particular no such proceedings were instituted before the expiration of the time fixed for compliance with the bankruptcy notice. In those circumstances the provisions of s. 41(6C) are not directly relevant. However, where the primary contention is that the judgment is one which should be set aside, because there is in truth no debt, the absence of due diligence in seeking to have it set aside seems to me to be a relevant circumstance in the exercise of the discretion under s. 41(6A)(b).

7. A bankruptcy court has power to go behind a judgment and inquire into the validity of the debt. It is clear that, where a petitioning creditor is seeking a sequestration order, the court has to be satisfied of the existence of the petitioning creditor's debt: see In Re Fraser; Ex parte Central Bank of London (1892) 2 QB 633. The court always has power to go behind the judgment and, if the case is a proper one, should do so. In Wren v. Mahony [1972] HCA 5; (1971-2) 126 CLR 212, Barwick C.J., with whom Windeyer and Owen JJ. agreed, said at 224:

"...where reason is shown for questioning

whether behind the judgment or as it is said, as the

consideration for it, there was in truth and reality a

debt due to the petitioning creditor, a Court of

Bankruptcy can no longer accept the judgment as

satisfactory proof."

and later:

"The Court's discretion in my opinion is a

discretion to accept the judgment as satisfactory proof

of that debt. That discretion is not well exercised

where substantial reasons are given for questioning

whether behind that judgment there was in truth and

reality a debt due to the petitioner."

8. The applicant in the present case seeks to raise those considerations as the basis for setting aside the bankruptcy notice.

9. A number of notices of assessment for the tax years 1982 to 1985 inclusive issued against Mr Conte. Unlike the factual situation in Briggs v. Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation; Ex parte Briggs (1986) ATC 4779, the applicant here concedes that the notices of assessment are conclusive evidence of the making of the assessment and that the amounts and all the particulars of the assessments are correct pursuant to s. 177(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. However, the applicant contends that the full amount of those assessments has already been paid.

10. In Briggs the taxpayer had instituted proceedings to have the assessments quashed. Before those proceedings had been resolved, the taxpayer applied to have the bankruptcy notice set aside or, alternatively, to have a time for complying with the notice extended until such time as the various proceedings in which he was involved had been concluded. Toohey J. adjourned the application to set aside the bankruptcy notice to a date to be fixed and extended the time for complying with the notice until further order.

11. In that case, the time for complying with the notice had been extended pending a decision by the Court. Toohey J. concluded that there was an arguable case to have the assessments quashed and it was therefore appropriate that the application to set aside the bankruptcy notice be adjourned. Toohey J. noted at 4780-1:

"The Bankruptcy Act 1966 does not expressly

confer power on the Court to set aside a bankruptcy

notice. But I am content to adopt the views expressed by

Lockhart J. in Re Sterling; Ex parte Esanda Ltd. (1979)

[1980] FCA 61; 44 FLR 125 in which his Honour held the power to

derive from at least two sources - subsec. 30(1) of the

Bankruptcy Act, which gives the Court power to decide all

questions coming within its cognisance and to make orders

necessary for carrying out or giving effect to the Act,

and the principle that a power conferred by parliament

carries with it the power necessary for its performance

or execution, so that the express power to extend time

for compliance with the requirements of a bankruptcy

notice, when an application to set it aside has been

filed, carries with it the power to set aside the

bankruptcy notice itself. On the matter of necessary

powers, I would refer also to Parsons v. Martin (1984) 58

ALR 395 at p 401.

As to the grounds upon which a bankruptcy

notice may be set aside, there is at least a starting

point in para 40(1)(g) of the Act. That paragraph

identifies as an act of bankruptcy failure to comply with

a notice or satisfy the Court that the debtor 'has a

counter-claim, set-off or cross demand equal to or

exceeding the amount of the judgment debt or sum payable

under the final order, as the case may be, being a

counter-claim, set-off or cross demand that he could not

have set up in the action or proceeding in which the

judgment or order was obtained'. Is that the only

ground? The cases suggest not, though none attempts to

define with any precision other relevant circumstances.

In Re Sterling at p 129 Lockhart J. mentioned some of

the grounds on which notices have been set aside, saying:

'Instances of the exercise of this

power are where the notice is not in accordance with the

terms of the judgment and is calculated to perplex the

debtor; the notice is issued for the whole of a judgment

debt, yet the judgment debtor has paid into court in part

satisfaction of the debt; in truth no debt lies behind

the judgment...and the sum of which payment is required

by the terms of a notice has in fact been paid by the

debtor. '"

12. Toohey J. said later at 4781:

"It seems to me that, however formulated, the

grounds upon which a bankruptcy notice may be set aside

must relate to the form or content of the notice itself,

service of the notice or the existence of the debt upon

which the judgment and in turn the notice is founded."

And later he said:

"In my view a court faced with an application to

set aside a bankruptcy notice is constrained to look only

at the regularity of the notice itself (including

service) and otherwise at the circumstances surrounding

the existence of the judgment debt and any demand which

the debtor may have against the creditor for a comparable

amount."

Here, Mr Conte is asserting that the judgment debt does not exist, the amounts due under the assessments having been paid. If that claim is good, both Re Sterling and Briggs indicate that the bankruptcy notice should be set aside. Alternatively, where that claim is arguable, both Re Sterling and Briggs suggest that the appropriate course would be to adjourn the application to set aside the bankruptcy notice until, as in Re Sterling, the District Court had dealt with the application to set aside the default judgment. In Briggs, the application was adjourned until the Federal Court in Sydney had considered whether the assessments should be quashed.

13. Before considering that aspect of the matter, the question of whether an act of bankruptcy has already been committed has to be considered. If there has already been an act of bankruptcy committed, to grant either of the alternative claims in the application would be futile.

14. An important question in the present case is whether it is competent for the court to extend time for compliance with a bankruptcy notice, at a time when the period for compliance with the bankruptcy notice has well and truly expired.

15. In Re Vella; ex parte Seymour [1983] FCA 115; (1982-83) 48 ALR 420 Morling J. refused to set aside a bankruptcy notice notwithstanding that the applicant had afterwards applied successfully to have the default judgment set aside. In Vella, the applicant did not comply with the requirements of the bankruptcy notice within the time fixed for compliance and she did not during the currency of the notice apply to have the judgment set aside, nor did she, unlike Mr Conte in the present case, apply within that time to set aside the bankruptcy notice.

16. In Re Hanby; ex parte Flemington Central Spares Pty Ltd (1967) 10 FLR 378, Gibbs J. said at 381:

"It is not possible to say that by reason of

subsequent circumstances an act of bankruptcy once

committed ceases to have been committed or must be

treated as though it has never been committed. Of course

this does not mean that a sequestration order may be made

if a judgment has been set aside in circumstances that

show that the debtor was under no liability to the

petitioning creditor, for the court must inquire whether

there is a debt due by the debtor to the petitioning

creditor, and if not, will refuse an order

notwithstanding the commission of an act of bankruptcy."

17. In this case, while an application for extension of time within which to comply with the bankruptcy notice was made within the time for compliance, during that time an extension was not obtained. Was there an act of bankruptcy when that time for compliance expired on 26 June 1990?

18. In Thurgood v. National Bank of Australia Ltd [1981] FCA 73; (1981) 53 FLR 51, Lockhart J. held in such a case that an act of bankruptcy had been committed and there was nothing to be gained from proceeding with the application to set aside the bankruptcy notice.

19. However, in Streimer v. Tamas [1981] FCA 123; (1981) 37 ALR 211, the Full Court of the Federal Court held that an extension of time for compliance with the requirements of a bankruptcy notice could be granted where the time for compliance or other extensions had expired. As Morling J. in Re Vella later acknowledged, the facts in Streimer were "quite special". The time for compliance with a bankruptcy notice had been successively granted until 6 April 1981. On that date, because of the volume of work, that matter was not reached, and the matter was adjourned to the following day. Almost certainly as a result of inadvertence, the trial judge was not requested to, and did not, extend the time for compliance until the following day. On that day, the time for compliance was extended, and a further extension made until further order. The failure to extend the time until the following day might possibly have been remedied by the application of the slip rule: see the English O. 20 r. 11, and the cases in the annotations at para 20/11/3 of the White Practice, including Tak Ming Co. v. Yee Sang Metal Suppliers Co. (1973) 1 WLR 300; and re Inchcape (1942) Ch 394. However, the Full Court did not base its judgment on any such ground.

20. Deane and Ellicott JJ. said at 214:

" It was argued on behalf of the appellant that a

long line of authority makes it apparent that s. 41(6A)

should not be construed as conferring jurisdiction to

make an order extending time for compliance with the

requirements of a bankruptcy notice in circumstances

where, at the time of the proposed order, the time

originally fixed and any previously granted extension or

extensions thereof have expired. The reason for this, it

was said, is that the Act confers no authority upon the

court to annul an act of bankruptcy which has been

committed. Upon the expiry of the time originally fixed

and any extension or extensions thereof without

compliance with the terms of the bankruptcy notice, an

act of bankruptcy is complete. Any subsequent extension

of time would, so the argument proceeds, be futile, since

it could not annul the act of bankruptcy which had

already been committed and which would remain. In

particular, reliance was placed upon King v. Henderson

(1898) AC 720 at 728; Re Grace (1931) 3 ABC 131; Re

McDonald (1934) 8 ABC 184 at 193; Re Edmunds (1936) 9 ABC

1; Re Hanby (1967) 10 FLR 378 at 381;

Re Hayes (1970) 18 FLR 216."

Their Honours then said:

"The above argument plainly possesses

considerable force. We have, however, come to

the conclusion that it should not be accepted."

Their Honours said later at 214-5:

"It would, in our view, be contrary to the plain

import of the words used by the Parliament to construe

s. 41(6A) as requiring not only that one or other of the

alternative express conditions precedent to jurisdiction

be fulfilled within the time originally fixed for

compliance, but as also requiring that both the

application for an order and any initial order be made

within that time."

Their Honours acknowledged that their conclusion might be "productive of a degree of uncertainty and inconvenience in practice".

21. In Re Vella Morling J. said of Streimer's case at 425:

"Whilst the effect of the decision in Streimer's

case was to relieve retrospectively the debtor in that

case from the consequences of his brief non-compliance

with the requirements of the bankruptcy notice served

upon him, the decision cannot be taken as authority for

the proposition that the setting aside of a bankruptcy

notice after the time for compliance with it has expired

has the effect of annulling the act of bankruptcy already

committed."

22. Thurgood's case was not referred to by any member of the Full Court in Streimer v. Tamas (supra) and, with respect, I have some difficulty with the view that Streimer is not authority for the conclusion that what is in truth an act of bankruptcy might be annulled as the effect of a later court order.

23. The act of bankruptcy constituted by s. 40(1)(g) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 directs attention to the time fixed by the Registrar to comply with the requirements of the notice. If, at that time, there has been no release or relief from the obligation to comply with the requirements of the notice by that time, it is difficult to conclude, in the event of noncompliance with the requirements of the notice at that time, that an act of bankruptcy does not then occur.

24. There is much force in what Gibbs J. said in Re Hanby at 381 in his conclusion that has earlier been referred to:

"It is not possible to say that by reason of

subsequent circumstances an act of bankruptcy once

committed ceases to have been committed or must be

treated as though it had never been committed."

25. The judgment of the Full Court in Streimer's Case is binding on me and is clear authority that an extension of time for the compliance with the requirements of a bankruptcy notice can be granted after the time for compliance has expired.

26. I turn now to consider whether the bankruptcy notice ought to be set aside or, alternatively, whether the time for compliance with it ought to be extended.

27. As Lockhart J. noted in Re Sterling, paragraphs (a) and(b) of s. 41(6A) are not mutually exclusive and the court is entitled to have regard to the application to set aside the judgment as a matter relevant to the exercise of its powers both to extend the time for compliance and to set aside the bankruptcy notice.

28. As earlier indicated, the applicant's case for setting aside the bankruptcy notice is that, in short, there is no debt, the payment of the tax owing by Mr Conte having been made. It is clear that payment is a basis on which a bankruptcy notice might be set aside: Re Partridge; ex parte Maidens-Fuller (1945) 13 ABC 185. Here the claim is that, notwithstanding the judgment, in fact no debt is owed to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation.

29. On 1 July 1986, an assessment in the sum of $8,140.14 against Mr Conte in respect of the income year ending 30 June 1982 issued. For the following tax year an assessment in the sum of $19,310.39 issued on 1 July 1986. For the tax year ending 30 June 1984, an assessment in the sum of $30,952.09 issued on 1 July 1986 and in respect of the tax year ending 30 June 1985, an assessment in the sum of $47,180.90 issued. For the tax year ending 30 June 1986, an assessment in the sum of $41,129.12 issued on 15 April 1987. In respect of each of those assessments the amount of $41,129.12 on the notice of assessment issued on 15 April 1987 was the total taxation liability then due by Mr Conte.

30. During 1986 a number of assessments issued in respect of the tax years ending June 1982 through to June 1985 in respect of a company, Decian Pty Ltd, a company in which Mr Conte and Mr Geraldo Bellino were sole shareholders and directors. The amount payable by 22 December 1986 in respect of the final of those notices was in excess of $600,000.00.

31. No objection was made by Mr Conte to any of the assessments against him pursuant to s. 185 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 nor was any application made by him to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of any decision of the Commissioner of Taxation pursuant to s. 187 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. There were, however, discussions between Mr Barbi, who is the solicitor for Mr Conte, Mr Bellino, Decian Pty Ltd and the accountant acting for those parties, and officers of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation.

32. Mr Conte says that in or about December 1986 an arrangement was made with a tax auditor, Mr David Hamilton-Smith, from the Taxation Department, and approved by the Deputy Commissioner that all of the taxpayers, Mr Conte, Mr Bellino and Decian Pty Ltd, would be relieved of penalties and tax provided that a total amount of $400,000.00 was paid. The terms of this agreement, he says, were that there would be an initial payment of $100,000.00, monthly payments of $5,000.00, payment of one-half of equity of sales of any property of the taxpayers, and, importantly, that the payments received were firstly to be applied towards the reduction of the liabilities of Mr Conte and Mr Bellino, and then once they were 'exhausted', towards the assessment of Decian Pty Ltd. He says that between December 1986 and April 1988, a total sum of $234,963.69 was paid. Mr Hamilton-Smith denies that there was any such arrangement as to the application of payments as Mr Conte says.

33. What is clear is that, by a letter dated 7 November 1986, an accountant, Mr Steve Hart, on behalf of Decian Pty Ltd, G. Bellino, and V. Conte wrote to the Taxation Office for the attention of Mr Hamilton-Smith. The letter said in part:

"My Clients would be prepared to make payment of

all the outstanding taxes, in respect to the Company and

the Individuals provided that the total amount does not

exceed Four Hundred Thousand Dollars ($400,000) in the

following manner..."

and there are set out terms which include the payment of $100,000.00 within 21 days of confirmation by the Taxation Department, monthly payments of $5,000.00 and payment to the Taxation Department of an amount representing one-half of any surplus funds from the sale of properties, with full payment to be made no later than two years from the date of the first payment. No mention was made of any agreement, or direction, as to how the payments were to be applied.

34. The letter later said:

"As previously stated our Clients understand

that should the Taxation Commissioner undertake to waive

Penalties and Interest, it would only be conditional upon

our Clients fulfilling all of the conditions on time."

35. In a letter dated 12 December 1986 to Steve Hart and Associates Pty Ltd, headed "INCOME TAX: DECIAN PTY LTD, G. BELLINO AND V. CONTE", the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation wrote:

"It is proposed to accept payment of all

outstanding income tax for the above taxpayers as at 30

June 1985 in accordance with proposals outlined in your

letter of 7 November 1986..."

And the letter concluded:

"If there is not strict compliance with these

conditions, recovery action will be taken immediately."

36. Mr Conte says that the correspondence concerning the arrangement reached in December 1986:

"did not embrace the full terms of the Agreement

between the parties in in so far as the correspondence is

silent as to the express

terms in which the payments were to be applied."

37. The evidence shows that sums of the order of $200,000.00 were paid to the Australian Taxation Office. The evidence suggests that in each case a receipt was issued to Decian Pty Ltd and the file number of that company appeared on each such receipt. Mr Conte says he did not see any of such receipts, and in any event, the significance of the file number appearing on any such receipt would not have occurred to him. I think that the last assertion is likely to be right. It is not in contention that the Taxation Office in fact applied all funds received in reduction of the taxation liability of Decian Pty Ltd. The entitlement so to do is at the core of this application.

38. Mr Conte asserts this appropriation by the Department was a breach of the arrangement reached in December 1986. He says that payments made on 4 August 1987 and 10 February 1988 came from the sales of properties owned by Mr Bellino and himself and the amount of $100,000.00 paid on 24 December 1986 came from joint funds of Mr Bellino, Decian Pty Ltd. and himself. He says that the payments to the Department were made by cheques drawn on Decian Pty Ltd's account, as a matter of accounting convenience. He says that he was not advised during the course of the payments that moneys were being applied in any other way than on his understanding. Mr Barbi, solicitor for Mr Conte, confirms that two payments in August 1987 and February 1988 were paid from the sales of properties owned by Messrs. Bellino and Conte and not Decian Pty Ltd.

39. Mr Barbi says he received instructions on 6 July 1990 to make application to set aside the judgment in the District Court.

40. Mr Conte says that, at the very worst, the moneys should have been applied on a pro rata basis; that is, one third towards the assessment of Decian Pty Ltd, one third towards the assessment of Mr Geraldo Bellino and one third towards his own assessment. He says that, if this had been done, more than $78,000.00 should have been applied towards his assessment and that, as a consequence, the amount referred to in the judgment of 18 May 1990 has been paid.

41. The correspondence evidencing the arrangement in December 1986 is silent as to any agreement as to how the payment made should be applied. However, the terms which were indisputably part of the December 1986 arrangement were not adhered to. On 18 November 1988, a letter sent by the Australian Government Solicitor to Mr Barbi, headed "Decian Pty Ltd v. Commissioner of Taxation" detailed payments made since 24 December 1986 in respect of the 'Compromised Agreement'. Those total $211,538.81 and refers to the last payment having been made on 6 April 1988.

42. Further correspondence ensued, all of it apparently headed "DECIAN PTY LTD V COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION".

43. There is a letter of 9 March 1989 from Mr Barbi to the Australian Government Solicitor which commences:

"I refer to my previous advices of the 21st and

I now enclose Statement of Assets and Liabilities under

the hand of Mr Bellino and Mr Conte."

The statement of assets and liabilities of Mr Conte shows a liability for taxation of $95,000.00 and it appears to have been signed by him. This acknowledgment is inconsistent with Mr Conte's claim that there had been an agreement, and payments made pursuant thereto, the effect of which would be that he then personally owed nothing to the Department.

44. As a result of further negotiations a deed of settlement was executed. The deed is dated 31 May 1989 and, unfortunately, it is equivocal. The first schedule to that deed refers to specified assessments of income tax against Decian Pty Ltd, Mr Bellino and Mr Conte. The recitals state:

"The assessed parties have paid to the

Commissioner the sums of money set out in the second

schedule hereto in part payment of the assessments."

The first schedule shows a liability for primary tax for Mr Conte for the years 1982-85 inclusive of $32,715.01. The second schedule details payments of $209.962.88. The recitals state that Decian Pty Ltd was the registered proprietor of land described in the third schedule, while Mr Bellino and Mr Conte were the registered proprietors of land referred to in the sixth schedule to that deed.

45. The first clause of that deed stated:

"The company, Geraldo Bellino, and Vittorio

Conte hereby acknowledge their joint and several

liability to the Deputy Commissioner in the sum of

$190,036.31."

Clause 5 provides that:

"In full and final satisfaction of the income

tax liability of the assessed parties with respect to the

notices of assessment set out in the First Schedule

hereto, the assessing parties jointly and severally

undertake to pay to the Deputy Commissioner..."

the principal sum of $190,036.31 and interest, by equal monthly payments of $5000.00 of capital and interest.

46. The deed, notwithstanding it was executed on 31 May 1989, speaks of the first repayment being due on 31 March 1989.

47. The terms of the deed appear to me to be inconsistent with Mr Conte's present contention. If the payments of approximately $200,000.00 were on the basis of the agreement he now contends was made with Mr Hamilton-Smith, his liability at that time would have been wholly discharged.

48. By clause 9, the Deputy Commissioner covenanted that, if there was no default by the assessed parties in the observation or performance of the obligations and warranties in the deed, he would take no recovery action against the assessed parties in respect of the notices of assessment listed in the first schedule.

49. By clause 11, if there was default in the payment of principal and interest in accordance with clause 5, the Commissioner was at liberty to issue proceedings against the assessed parties for the amount of the principal sum and interest unpaid.

50. There was default in the performance of the obligations of clause 5 of that deed.

51. On 11 May 1990, a sum of $20,000.00 was paid to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, and Mr Conte says that this was "on the express provision that all action by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation against all the taxpayers would be suspended for a certain period of time". On 8 May 1990, the Australian Government Solicitor wrote to Mr Barbi advising them that it intended to apply for judgment by default against Mr Conte on Thursday 10 May in default of entry of appearance and defence. On 11 May the Australian Government Solicitor advised Mr Barbi that an agreement was reached between an officer of the Taxation Department and Mr Bellino that if the sum of $20,000.00 be paid by 4 p.m. on 11 May, the Deputy Commissioner undertook not to execute any judgment obtained against Bellino and Conte prior to 5 p.m. on Thursday 17 May 1990. It further advised that an application to wind up Decian Pty Ltd would be heard on 12 June 1990.

52. The letter of 11 May from the Australian Government Solicitor to Mr Barbi set out details of the indebtedness of Mr Bellino, Mr Conte and Decian Pty Ltd as at 10 May 1990. On 13 June 1990 a 'without prejudice' offer was made on behalf of Decian Pty Ltd, Mr Conte and Mr Bellino, which was rejected by letter of 15 June 1990.

53. Judgment was obtained in the District Court against Mr Conte on 18 May 1990.

54. By facsimile transmission on 25 June 1990, the Australian Government Solicitor wrote to Mr Barbi denying on behalf of the Deputy Commissioner the assertion that moneys paid as set out in the second schedule of the deed dated 31 May 1989 were credited towards the accounts of Mr Bellino and Mr Conte and points out that payments made were in fact credited to the account of Decian Pty Ltd. By a letter dated 26 June 1990, the Australian Government Solicitor advised Mr Barbi that $10,000.00 was credited to each of Messrs. Bellino and Conte's claimed indebtedness. The writ against Mr Conte had claimed the sum of $66,728.08, but judgment was obtained in respect of debt in the sum of $56,728.08. The letter stated:

"I must confess my surprise at this late

challenge by your clients as regards their indebtedness

to my client. Given the constant attempts by your

clients to settle the indebtedness this year, the failure

to defend (even after specific notice was given to you of

the date on which I would be entering Judgment) and the

acknowledgment of the indebtedness by your clients at a

meeting with my clients on 28 May 1990, I must advise

that any attempt by your clients to now go behind the

Judgments by Default obtained on 18 May 1990 will be

strenuously resisted. "

55. Events since the arrangement in December 1986 are in quite a number of significant respects at variance with what one would have expected if the arrangement had been as Mr Conte now says.

56. In the view I take of the matter, the question of appropriation of payment was left unresolved. It certainly is at large in the correspondence detailing the agreement and, on the evidence before me, I am of the view that no agreement one way or the other was made as to how any payments were to be credited.

57. While the case concerned a debtor who owed two debts to a creditor, Re Walsh; ex parte Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (NSW) [1982] FCA 88; (1982) 42 ALR 727, is of some assistance in respect of the question of appropriation of payment. Lockhart J. said at 728:

"A debtor who owes two debts to a creditor is

entitled to appropriate a payment which he makes to his

creditor to one debt rather than to the other. If he

omits to do so, the creditor may make the appropriation.

If neither makes any appropriation, the law appropriates

the payment to the earlier debt."

He referred to the observations of Green L.J. in Leeson v. Leeson (1936) 2 KB 156 where his Lordship said at 162-3:

"When, however, he does not notify the creditor

of his intention, and when the circumstances are such

that the creditor receives the payment merely in

satisfaction of the debts and the payment is not more

appropriate to the payment of the one debt than to that

of the other the creditor is entitled to make the

appropriation. When it is said that there need not be an

express appropriation of a payment, but that the

appropriation can be inferred, that does not mean that

appropriation of a payment can be inferred from some

undisclosed intention in the mind of the debtor. It is

to be inferred from the circumstances of the case as

known to both parties. Any other view might lead to

injustice, as the creditor's right to appropriate a

payment would be defeated. When the matter is examined

upon principle it will be found that an undisclosed

intention in the mind of the debtor is not sufficient to

support an appropriation."

58. In the circumstances of this case, in my view the payments were made in satisfaction of the debts of the taxpayers without any direction or understanding as to how they might be applied by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation. There seems to me no proper basis on which one should infer that the payments should be applied first to the discharge of the individual taxpayers' liabilities and, if so, in what way or, alternatively, to apply them pro rata as to number of debtors or pro rata as to the quantum of each taxpayer's debt. It seems to me that the Commissioner, in the absence of appropriation by the payer of the payments, was entitled to make the appropriation which he clearly did by appropriating the payments in reduction of the liability of Decian Pty Ltd.

59. In all the circumstances, I am not satisfied that Mr Conte has any real prospects of success in respect of his claim. His present claim appeared very belatedly, and is inconsistent with his signed statement of assets and liabilities in March 1989, the tenor of the deed of 31 May 1989, and with the payment in May 1990, although the last aspect is said to have been a payment made in pursuit of trying to forestall the winding up of Decian Pty Ltd. In respect of the judgment against him, his explanation as to why he did not defend the proceedings I find unconvincing. There has been delay since the entry of the judgment now challenged, which was entered, with notice to Mr Conte, on 18 May 1990. Notwithstanding his intention to set aside the judgment expressed in early July 1990, no such steps in that regard had been made by 2 August 1990.

60. Should I be wrong, and the judgment is, at some later time, set aside, a sequestration order would not, after that circumstance, be properly made on the basis of that judgment.

61. I decline to set aside the bankruptcy notice and I decline to extend the time for compliance with that bankruptcy notice. The applicant should pay the respondent's costs of the application, to be taxed if not agreed.
