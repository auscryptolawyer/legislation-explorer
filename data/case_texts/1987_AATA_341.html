ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - deductions - whether carrying on business of primary production

Income tax - amended assessments – whether necessary connection between failure to make full and true disclosure and avoidance of tax

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s.51(1), 170(2)

Case P14, 82 ATC 62

FC of T v Maurice Estate 77 ATC 4462

REASONS FOR DECISION

25 September 1987 Deputy President R.K. Todd

  1. The principal matter in dispute in this reference involves the question whether the applicant was carrying on a business of primary production: in the years of income ended 30 June 1978 to 1984.
  2. During this period, the applicant was a full time public servant living in Canberra. In 1972 he had purchased the subject property, being an 84 ha property about 25 km from Cooma. The property was in a run-down condition.
  3. Prior to purchasing the property the applicant had investigated the availability of land within a radius of "150 miles", and subsequent to purchasing the property he had obtained a report from the New South Wales Conservation Service. This report, dated 15 November 1973, outlined the classes of land on the property, the potential use of each class, developments that were suggested and measures recommended to avoid soil erosion.
  4. Approximately 30ha of the property was cleared by the applicant, two dams constructed, and a transportable home placed on the property.
  5. The following table shows the purchases, sales, natural increase, deaths and numbers of stock on hand over the period in dispute:

  1. As can be seen from the above, during the seven years in question the applicant purchased fourteen cattle, sold nineteen, six died and there was natural increase of nine. The average number of cattle on the property at the start of each financial year was between six and seven.
  2. As the applicant was working full time in the public service, the time he spent at the property was limited to weekends, holidays and "flex-days". During these periods he repaired fencing, fed and tended to cattle as well as carrying out other projects such as restoring an area that had previously been a gravel quarry.
  3. It was also explained by the applicant in evidence that he had been hindered in his development of the property by two major factors. The first of these was his divorce from his wife which limited the amount of capital he had available. The second was a drought that affected the area for much of the period under review. In addition to these factors, the applicant had medical problems which led to his retirement from the public service on invalidity grounds.
  4. The cumulative effect of the factors mentioned was to inhibit the applicant in carrying out the development of the property that he had intended. It is for the Tribunal to determine whether a business had begun which was then hindered by these factors or, alternatively, whether the activities of the applicant were activities merely in preparation for the establishment of a business.

10. In Case P14, 82 ATC 62 Dr P Gerber (now a Senior Member of this Tribunal) at p63A emphasised again that "whether a man is or is not carrying on a business is, ultimately, a question of fact ...". He went on to set out the factors to be considered as follows:

"9. Doing the best I can with such tried old warhorses as Thomas 72 ATC 4094, Martin [1953] HCA 100; (1952-53) 90 CLR 470 and Ferguson 79 ATC 4261, the following indicia seem to emerge:

(i) 'A man may carry on business although he does so in a small way' (Thomas)

(ii) 'The test is both subjective and objective: it is made by regarding the nature and extent of the activities under review, as well as the purpose of the individual engaging in them ... the determination is eventually based on the large or general impression gained' (Martin).

(iii) 'If the transactions which go to make up the activity or operations of the taxpayer have an element of regularity or repetitiveness, this factor assists in concluding that the taxpayer is carrying on a business rather than indulging in a recreational or hobby activity' (Ferguson)."

11. While the applicant, in his evidence to the Tribunal, satisfied the subjective test of purpose, and while I accept that a business may be carried on in a small way, I am unable to find that the applicant in this case was carrying on a business of primary production. The "large or general impression" is one of an operation which has not yet become a business and is still in a preparatory stage, notwithstanding that it has been in that stage for over 10 years. Additionally, the operation seemed to lack the required "element of regularity or repetitiveness" in either purchases or sales of cattle.

  1. For the above reasons the Tribunal finds that the applicant was not at the material times carrying on a business of primary production.
  2. Amended assessments were issued in this matter on 1 March 1982 for the years ended 30 June 1978-1980. The original assessments had allowed the disputed deductions which were subsequently disallowed in the amended assessment.
  3. Mr J Faulks, appearing for the applicant, submitted that in the applicant's original return there had been full and true disclosure of all material facts necessary for the Commissioner to make his assessment and, therefore, that the provisions of s.170(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("ITAA") prevented the Commissioner from issuing amended assessments.
  4. In his returns for the years 1978-1980 the taxpayer had claimed the full amount of depreciation and interest for a Chevrolet truck. Additionally, he claimed the investment allowance in respect of that vehicle. It was later conceded that there was private usage of the truck and that consequently he was not eligible for the investment allowance and should have apportioned the depreciation and interest.
  5. It was argued for the applicant that there was no connection, as required by Aickin J. in FC of T v Maurice Estate 77 ATC 4462, between the failure to make a full and true disclosure and the avoidance of tax. Mr Faulks submitted that:

"...the failure by the taxpayer to reveal the private usage of the motor vehicle in relation only to the items of interest and depreciation [has] no connection with his status as a primary producer. All relevant facts relating to the business of primary production were known to the Commissioner at the time of the original assessments and it is not suggested by the Commissioner's representative that such disclosure had not occurred or that such knowledge did not exist."

17. In support of the amended assessment the respondent submitted that:

"...the original claim in respect of the motor vehicle expenses arising as it did in connection with the general claim for primary production expenses must have been a factor taken into account by the Commissioner in determining whether to allow the claim for primary production expenses generally..."

  1. As the incorrect claim related directly to primary production, I find that the required connection between the lack of full and true disclosure exists and that the Commissioner was therefore authorised by s170(2) to make the amended assessments.
  2. For the reasons given above the Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.
