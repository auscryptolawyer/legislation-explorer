QT92/102-104 and Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1993] AATA 582; (1993) 27 ATR 1136; 94 ATC 175 (5 August 1993)

Last Updated: 20 May 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION - taxpayer was employed in the Solomon Islands by the Solomon Islands Government - taxpayer was paid a salary supplement by the Australian Government -whether or not the supplement was subject to taxation in Australia - taxpayer was a resident of Australia - the supplement was not exempt income under Section 23AG.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (as amended to 30.6.90) s. 6, 23AG, 25, 169A Superannuation Act 1976 s. 3 Public Service Act 1922 s. 33C

AAT Decision No 8892

DECISION AND REASONS

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No. QT92/102-104

Place: Brisbane

Date: 5 August 1993

Tribunal: Deputy President DP Breen, Presidential Member Senior Member KL Beddoe

Mr JD Horrigan, Member

DECISION

The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

(Sgd) DP BREEN PRESIDENTIAL MEMBER

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. GT92/102-104

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

DECISION AND REASONS

Before: Deputy President DP Breen, Presidential Member

Senior Member KL Beddoe

Mr JD Horrigan, Member

Date: 5 August 1993

  1. On 30 October 1992 the applicant lodged an application for review with this Tribunal. The applicant sought review of objection decisions made by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in relation to assessments made for the years ended 30 June 1988, 1989 and 1990.
  2. Evidence before the Tribunal at the hearing included the Section 37 Statements, or "T" Documents, as Exhibit 1 and the oral evidence given by the applicant. Other evidence, namely Exhibits 5 to 9 comprise largely of various correspondence between the Australian Tax Office, the applicant and a company known as South Pacific Development Associates Limited. It should be noted that copies of the applicant’s contract of service (Exhibit 2) and some notes on his conditions of service (Exhibits 3 and 4) were also before the Tribunal. Lastly, Exhibits 10 to 15 consisted of various pieces of Tax legislation from both Australia and the Solomon Islands in conjunction with income tax rulings relevant to these proceedings.

3. The history of the matter as summarised in the "T" Documents and as confirmed by the evidence given by the applicant can be summarised as follows:

(i) The applicant is an officer with the Australian Customs Service.

(ii) The applicant served with of Customs Department in the Solomon islands from September 1987 to December 1990.

(iii) The applicant, whilst employed in the Solomon Islands, was paid a salary equivalent to the local salary by the Solomon Islands Government. The applicant was also paid a salary supplement by the Australian Government pursuant to the Contract of Service (Exhibit 2) and Memorandum of Understanding (Document T20). It is this salary supplement which is the subject of these proceedings. The salary paid by the Solomon Islands Government has at all times been considered as exempt income under Section 23AG of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936.

(iv) The applicant's employment in the Solomon Islands was in accordance with Contract of Service (Exhibit 2) and a Memorandum of Understanding (Document T20).

(v) While employed in the Solomon Islands the applicant continued to make

contributions to and remained a member of the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme established under the Superannuation Act 1976.

(vi) (a) The applicant queried the taxation treatment of his future Solomon Island Income on 13 May 1987. The Sydney Branch of the Australian Taxation Office on 15 June 1987 advised by letter that inter alia income from the foreign source would be exempt from tax in Australia if it had been subject to tax in the country of its source.

(b) The applicant, when lodging returns for the years ended 30 June 1988, 1989 and 1990 on 27 February 1991 disclosed only Australian-sourced income.

(vii) On 12 August 1991 the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation issued an assessment in respect of the years ended 30 June 1988, 1989 and 1990. Additional tax for late lodgment was imposed in each year. Significantly, on lodging his returns the applicant had included a request for a ruling in accordance with Section 169A. The ruling was issued on 25 July 1991 on the basis that the salary paid and subject to tax in the Solomon Islands was exempt The Commissioner found that the salary supplement had not been subject to tax in the Solomon Islands and was therefore subject to tax in Australia.

(viii) On 30 September 1991 the applicant objected against the above assessments.

(ix) On 1 April 1992 notices of the Commissioner's decision to disallow the applicant's objection was issued.

(x) The applicant again objected and provided further information and arguments to support his assertion (see Documents T24-T26). On 3 September 1992 the applicant was advised that the decision as notified on 1 April 1992 had not been altered. On 2 December 1992 after the lodgement of his application for review with this Tribunal on 30 October 1992, the assessments were amended to remit the additional tax for late lodgement to nil in all years under review.

4. The issue before the Tribunal is twofold -

(a) whether the taxpayer is a resident under Section 6 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936; and

(b) if in fact he is a resident then whether the salary supplement is exempt income under Section 23AG of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936.
  1. The relevant legislation in these proceedings is the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (as amended to 30 June 1990).
  2. The applicant represented himself at the hearing and the respondent was represented by Ms CE Holmes of Counsel instructed by the Australian Government Solicitor.
  3. Ms Holmes, on behalf of the respondent, submitted that the taxpayer was a resident of Australia under subsection 6(1)(a)(iii) as the taxpayer was an "eligible employee" pursuant to Section 3 of the Superannuation Act 1976. Significantly, the respondent did not concede that subsections 6(1)(a)(i) and 6(1)(a)(ii) did not apply to the applicant (see Transcript page 59). Notably, an "eligible employee" under the Superannuation Act is defined simply as meaning "an officer" for the purposes of the Public Service Act 1922. Further, the respondent contended that the applicants income was not exempt income under subsection 23AG(1) as his income was exempt from taxation in the Solomon Islands and therefore did not satisfy the requirements of subsection 23AG(3).

8. The applicant contended that firstly he was a non-resident and, secondly, that if in the instance the Tribunal found him to be a resident of Australia then the salary supplement was exempt income under Section 23AG. The taxpayer contended that he was an "unattached officer" under Section 33C of the Public Service Act 1922. The taxpayer further argued that an "unattached officer" is to be distinguished from "an officer" as required under the Superannuation Act. The applicant therefore submitted that he was not an eligible employee under the Superannuation Act. It was also the applicant's contention that Section 6(1) (a) (i) of the income Tax Assessment Act did not make him a resident as his permanent place of abode was outside Australia. As an alternative argument, the applicant submitted that if the Tribunal found him to be a resident under Section 6 then the salary supplement was exempt under Section 23AG(1). The taxpayer argued that Item 16 in Exhibit 14 did not apply to the supplement. It was therefore the applicant's contention that he did satisfy the conditions under Section 23AG(3) and consequently the supplement was exempt from taxation.

9. The remainder of the respondent's submissions dealt largely with the Deputy Commissioner's reasons for decision. The reasons themselves are not under review and the Tribunal found these submissions largely unhelpful in the determination of this matter.

10. Section 25(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act states:

"The assessable income of a taxpayer shall include -

(a) where the taxpayer is a resident - the gross income derived directly or indirectly from ail sources whether in or out of Australia; and

(b) where the taxpayer is a non-resident - the gross income derived directly or indirectly from all sources in Australia,

which is not exempt income, an amount to which section 26AC or 26AD applies or an eligible termination payment within the meaning of Subdivision AA."

11. In order to determine whether or not the salary supplement is taxable it is therefore necessary to determine whether or not the taxpayer is a resident of Australia. "Non-resident is defined in Section 6 as a person who is not a resident of Australia. "Resident is also defined in Section 6 as -

"(a) a person, other than a company, who resides in Australia and includes a person -

(i) whose domicile is in Australia, unless the Commissioner is satisfied that his permanent place of abode is outside Australia;

(ii) who has actually been in Australia, continuously or intermittently, during more than one-half of the year of income, unless the Commissioner is satisfied that his usual place of abode is outside Australia and that he does not intend to take up residence in Australia; or

(iii) who is an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 or is the spouse or a child under 16 years of age of such a person; and

(b) "

12. The Tribunal finds that the taxpayer is in fact a resident in accordance with subsection 6(1)(a)(i). The taxpayer's domicile has at all times been in Australia. Furthermore, the Tribunal finds that the taxpayer's permanent place of abode was at all times in Australia

13. In the Tribunal's opinion the taxpayer's permanent place of abode was not rendered outside Australia merely by virtue of the fact that he resided in the Solomon Islands for some three and a half years. In finding that the taxpayer's permanent place of abode was in fact inside Australia, the Tribunal considered the following significant factors.

(a) The taxpayer was to remain in the Solomon Islands for a fixed and definite period.

(b) The taxpayer maintained his residential home in Australia.

(c) The taxpayer sought leave without pay from the Australian Public Service so that in the event of his employment with the Solomon Islands being unsuccessful he could return to the Australian Public Service.

14. In the alternative, the Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant remained an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 throughout his period of service in the Solomon Islands. In the Tribunal's view the applicant was correctly required to continue his contributions to the Superannuation Scheme during his service in the Solomon Islands (Document T31). Nothing was said to the Tribunal which would justify a finding that the letter of the Commissioner for Superannuation dated 8 December 1992 (Document T31) was other than correct. The applicant was an unattached officer for the purposes of the Public Service Act 1922. He remained an "officer" for the purposes of that Act; therefore he was a "permanent employee" as defined in subsection 3(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976. It follows that he was an "eligible employee" as defined by the said subsection 3(1) in that he was a permanent employee.

15. On that basis the taxpayer is a resident, the next question to be determined by the Tribunal was whether or not the taxpayer's income was exempt under Section 23AG. Section 23AG(1) provides:

"Where a resident, being a natural person, has been engaged in foreign service for a continuous period of not less than 365 days, any foreign earnings derived by the person from that foreign service is exempt from tax."

16. It should be noted, however, that Section 23AG(1) is subject to the conditions of Section 23AG(3) which states:

"An amount of foreign earnings derived in a foreign country is not exempt from tax under this section unless -

(a) the amount is not exempt from income tax in that country; and

(b) if there is a liability for payment of income tax in that country in respect of that amount - the Commissioner is satisfied that the tax has been or will be paid."
  1. The Tribunal accepts that the taxpayer is within the scope of Section 23AG(1). The issue is therefore whether or not the taxpayer satisfies the conditions stipulated in Section 23AG(3).
  2. The Tribunal finds that the salary supplement is exempt from taxation in the Solomon Islands pursuant to Paragraph 16 of Schedule 1 in the Solomon Islands Income Tax Legislation (Exhibit 14). Paragraph 16 states:

'The emoluments payable out of overseas sources in respect of duties performed in the Protectorate in connection with any technical assistance agreement to which the Government of the Protectorate is a party to any nonresident person or to any person who is resident solely for the purpose of performing such duties, in any case where such agreement provides for (he exemption of such emoluments."

  1. Thus, the Tribunal finds that the taxpayer fails to satisfy the conditions under Section 23AG(3) and therefore finds that the salary supplement is not exempt income under Section 23AG(1).
  2. For these reasons the Tribunal affirms the objection decisions under review.

I hereby certify this and the preceding 9 pages to be a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons for Decision of Deputy President DP Breen, Presidential Member, Senior Member KL Beddoe and Mr JD Horrigan in the matter of QT92/102-104.

Associate
