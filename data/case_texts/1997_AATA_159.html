"Nx95c" and Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner [1997] AATA 159 (9 May 1997)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

"NX95C" v. INSURANCE AND SUPERANNUATION COMMISSIONER

No.

N95/1586

AAT No. 12010

Number of pages - 4

Practice and procedure



COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

D CHAPPELL (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Practice and procedure - direction to dismiss - request for reasons - applicant failed to produce documents after nearly a year - no acceptable explanations for delay - failed to comply with Tribunal's directions within reasonable time - warned three times about dismissal

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 s 42A(5)

HEARING

SYDNEY, 9 May 1997 (hearing and decision)

9:5:1997

Mr John Owens, Manager, appeared for the Applicant

Solicitor for the Respondent: Ms Kimm Wilson, ISC

ORDER

In light of the previous warnings issued by the Tribunal at directions hearings on 26 March and 7 February 1997, and having heard the parties at a directions hearing today, the Tribunal directs that this matter be dismissed under s 42A(5) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 because of the applicant's persistent failure to comply with the directions of the Tribunal, relating especially to the production of documents.

DECISION

D CHAPPELL

BACKGROUND

1. The applicant company requested reasons for the Tribunal's direction dismissing its application for review under s 42A(5) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (AAT Act). The applicant had sought review of a decision of the respondent dated 16 August 1995 which found that it should not be treated as satisfying the superannuation fund conditions in the income years 1986/87, 1987/88 and 1988/89. In the proceedings before the Tribunal, the applicant was represented by its manager, Mr John Owens. The respondent was represented by Ms Kimm Wilson.

2. Section 42A(5) of the AAT Act allows the Tribunal to dismiss a matter in the following circumstances:

(5) If an applicant for a review of a decision fails within a reasonable time: (a) to proceed with the application; (b) to comply with a direction by the Tribunal in relation to the application; a presidential member or senior member, on behalf of the Tribunal, may dismiss the application without proceeding to review the decision

HISTORY

3. The following chronology sets out the history of the application:

- The application for review was dated 20 October 1995. When the respondent did not object to the application being out of time, an extension of time was granted by the Tribunal (direction of Senior Member Barbour, dated 13 November 1995).

- Two preliminary conferences with Conference Registrar Scahill were held in this matter: on 14 February 1996 (after being vacated twice because of the unavailability of the respondent on the first occasion, and the applicant on the second) and 28 May 1996. On 28 June 1996, the matter was listed for hearing on 2 and 3 December 1996.

- On 25 June 1996, the respondent requested that the Tribunal serve a summons to produce documents on Mr Owens, on behalf of the applicant company and two related companies (Viloux Pty Ltd and Savemores Trading Pty Ltd). The summonses were addressed to the respective Proper Officers of each company.

- The matter was listed for a return of summons hearing on 23 July 1996. Mr Owens filed a submission objecting to the breadth of the summons and requesting more time to comply. The hearing on 23 July was vacated, and the matter was listed before myself for a return of summons on 6 August 1996.

- On 2 August 1996, Mr Owens filed another submission reiterating some of his questions and requesting that the 6 August return of summons hearing be vacated. He also lodged his own summons to produce documents.

- The hearing on 6 August 1996 was not vacated. At that hearing no documents were produced by the applicant in reply to the summons and a new return date of 10 September 1996 was set by the Tribunal for the return of documents pursuant to the summonses issued by the respondent and the applicant. Both parties were directed to comply with the new return date.

- At the hearing on 10 September 1996, both parties presented written submissions explaining why they had not produced the requested material. The files requested by the applicant had already been the subject of a freedom of information request. The respondent produced internal memos and electronic mail messages to the effect that both the Sydney and Canberra offices and archives had been searched, but the material could not be found. The Tribunal accepted the respondent's explanations.

- Mr Owens objected to the request for documents from the Viloux and Savemores Trading companies because they were third parties to the proceedings. Mr Owens also objected that too much material was requested, it was difficult to obtain, was of speculative relevance and would entail him sorting through a large number of papers to select the relevant documents. In response to questions from the Tribunal, the applicant admitted he had found some of the requested documents and was given 48 hours to decide if he was claiming privilege. Documents relating to the primary applicant were made available in the Tribunal Registry on 12 September 1996 for inspection by the respondent.

- After the respondent inspected the documents in October, Ms Wilson submitted to the Tribunal that the material produced represented minimal compliance and made it difficult for the respondent to prepare its case in time for December. A directions hearing was requested. In her later submission of 7 February 1997, Ms Wilson explained that more than half of the documents produced in September were either not requested or were outside the time periods specified in the order. As an immediate directions hearing was impossible, the hearing dates of 2 and 3 December were vacated, and the matter relisted for directions. When Member Greenwood proved unavailable at short notice, and the matter could not be relisted because of the unavailability of both parties, the directions hearing was postponed until 1997.

- On 7 February 1997, a directions hearing was held to discuss the availability of material, and clarify the issues in dispute at the substantive hearing. It was established that material from the two related companies was necessary for the respondent to present its case, and potentially relevant. The Tribunal issued a direction allowing the applicant a further 28 days (until 7 March) to comply with the summons. The final paragraph stated: if the applicant does not make every effort to comply with these directions, the Tribunal will consider dismissing the application under s 42A(5) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. - On 11 March 1997, Mr Owens wrote to the Tribunal requesting another month to comply with the direction of 7 February 1997 because of his study commitments. In his submission, he reiterated his arguments (rejected by the Tribunal on 7 February 1997) that documents relating to the other companies were not relevant. He also requested a direction that the respondent produce material pursuant to the summons issued by the applicant (despite the Tribunal accepting the respondent's reasons for non-compliance, substantiated on 10 September 1997). On 12 March 1997, the respondent submitted that the matter should be dismissed, and noted that it was still awaiting documents more than seven months after they were requested.

- At a telephone directions hearing on 26 March 1997, the Tribunal again ruled that the material from the related companies was relevant. The respondent had produced an acceptable explanation for not producing material; the applicant had not, and if material was still not produced within a further 28 days, the matter would be dismissed. The direction issued on 26 March 1997 began: The applicant has failed to satisfy the Tribunal's direction of 7 February 1997 that it and the Proper Officers of Viloux Pty Ltd and Savemores Trading Pty Ltd comply with the respondent's request for material by 7 March 1997. Having heard the parties at a telephone directions hearing today, the Tribunal directs that the applicant have another 28 days (until 23 April 1997) to produce the documents. If the applicant fails again to comply with this direction, the Tribunal will seriously consider dismissing the application under s 42A(5) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. - Mr Owens produced some material in response to the summons on 28 April 1997 and filed a submission on 2 May 1997 requesting a directions hearing to determine whether he had sufficiently complied with the order. At the directions hearing, the respondent complained that the small amount of materials produced represented only notional compliance. When asked why photocopies were produced, and not originals, Mr Owens explained that he had copied extracts of the relevant materials he had at hand. The respondent submitted that this was material that Mr Owens had previously said he did not have access to. His actions also contravened the directions made by the Tribunal at the return of summons hearings in August and September 1996 that Mr Owens not selectively produce material according to his perception of its relevance.

- After a further warning about dismissal, Mr Owens restated his arguments that material about the related companies was not relevant and that the respondent should be asked to produce the material requested in his summons from last year. Satisfied that the applicant had not even made a genuine attempt to comply with the directions of the Tribunal made on 6 August and 10 September 1996, and 7 February, 26 March and 9 May 1997, the Tribunal dismissed the matter under s 42A(5) of the AAT Act (after warnings to this effect on 7 February, 26 March and 9 May 1997).

- The applicant requested written reasons for the direction on 4 June 1997.

DECISION

4. The detailed chronology set out above encapsulates the primary reasons for dismissing this matter; namely, the applicant's failure to produce documents requested as early as June 1996. It was established by the Tribunal on 6 August 1996 that the summonses issued by the respondent appeared relevant to the issues before the Tribunal, and described the material requested with sufficient particularity. Acceptable reasons were given for the respondent's inability to produce the information requested by the applicant, and these reasons were corroborated with records of internal departmental communications detailing completed searches for the materials. The Tribunal never received valid explanations for why Mr Owens did not produce material that, it later transpired, he had access to. When his objections to producing documents were overturned by the Tribunal he continued to resist their production.

5. The Tribunal issued several explicit warnings about dismissing the matter which were effectively ignored. The applicant failed to comply with oral directions issued at the hearings in August and September 1996 and May 1997, and the written directions issued after the hearings of February and March 1997. The applicant's delays suggest a failure to proceed with the application. His actions also obstructed the respondent's preparation of its case. After eleven months, the applicant had not satisfactorily complied with the summons. For three of these months, the threat of dismissal had been made clear to him. The Tribunal has done all in its power to make the applicant aware of the importance of following its rulings. Nevertheless, the applicant has failed within a reasonable time to comply with the Tribunal's directions in relation to the application.

6. For the above reasons, the Tribunal directs that the application be dismissed under s 42A(5) of the AAT Act.
