QT87/1595-1596 and Commissioner of Taxation [1991] AATA 374; (1991) 22 ATR 3066; 91 ATC 186 (13 February 1991)

Last Updated: 27 May 2009

Income tax - Default Assessments - whether excessive - whether applicant had discharged the onus of proving the assessments excessive - remission in part of additional tax.



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 : Ss.161(1), 162., 166. 167, 177(1), 190, 226

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 : S.43



Case X65 90 ATC 492

FCT v. Dalco [1990] HCA 3; (1990) 20 ATR 1370

Gauci v. FCT (1975) 75 ATC 4257

George v. FCT [1952] HCA 21; (1952) 86 CLR 183

Jones v. Dunkel [1959] HCA 8; (1959) 101 CLR 298

McAndrew v. FCT [1956] HCA 62; (1956) 98 CLR 263

McCormack Pty Ltd v. FCT (1979) 79 ATC 4111



DECISION AND REASONS



Re: QT87/1595-1596

APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT



AAT Decision No 6647



No: QT87/1595-1596

Date: 13 February 1991

Place: Brisbane



Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

DECISION



The Tribunal decides that the objection decisions under review be set aside and the objections allowed in part by remitting the additional tax imposed by subsection 226(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 to an amount of $30,000 in respect of each year of income. The objection decisions are otherwise affirmed.



(Sgd) K L Beddoe

(SENIOR MEMBER)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NOS. QT87/1595-1596

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re: QT87/1595-1596



APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION



RESPONDENT



REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



Date: 13 February 1991



Place: Brisbane



The question to be decided is whether default assessments made by the respondent in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1983 and 30 June 1984 are excessive.



2. Subsection 161(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") is the general provision which has the general effect, when implemented by the Commissioner each year, of requiring lodgment of returns of income by the tax paying community of Australia. Section 162 is a more specific provision permitting the respondent Commissioner to require the lodgment of income tax returns by individual taxpayers.



3. Section 166 of the Act provides that the Commissioner shall make an assessment of the amount of the taxable income of any taxpayer and of the tax payable thereon, from the returns and from any other information in the Commissioner's possession. Where, inter alia, the Commissioner has reason to believe that any person who has not furnished a return has derived taxable income, s.167 of the Act authorises the Commissioner to make an assessment of the amount upon which in his judgment income tax ought to be levied. (Although the respondent relies upon paragraph 167(b) in his s.37 statement, that cannot be the relevant provision if returns of income have not been filed.) The amount so determined under that process becomes the taxable income of that person for the purpose of s.166.



4. Section 177 of the Act provides in subsection (1) that production of a notice of assessment or of a copy notice of assessment, shall be conclusive evidence for the due making of the assessment and that the amount and all the particulars of the assessment are correct. This subsection is qualified to the effect that in proceedings under Part 5 of the Act on a review or appeal relating to the assessment, the amount and all the particulars of the assessment are not conclusively proved as correct by the production of the notice or copy notice.



5. However, the Act goes further by providing that in proceedings under Part 5 of the Act on a review before this Tribunal the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive shall lie upon the taxpayer (section 190). Section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 provides, inter alia, that the Tribunal may exercise all the powers and the discretions that are conferred by any relevant enactment on the person who made the decision under review for the purpose of reviewing that decision.



6. At the hearing of this matter Mr Hamwood of counsel appeared for the applicant and Mr Hack of counsel appeared for the respondent.



7. If these matters are notable for anything it is for the lack of evidence before the Tribunal in relation to the applicant's taxation affairs. The applicant did not tender any documents which in any way asserted her taxable income nor did she attend the hearing. I was told from the bar table that the applicant had not lodged relevant income tax returns and I infer she has not otherwise returned or declared her taxable income or her lack of taxable income to the respondent Commissioner. No explanation was given as to her non-attendance at the hearing.



8. In response to a summons directed to be issued by the Tribunal an officer of the Taxation Office produced papers relating to the assessment made against the applicant. Only certain of those papers have been admitted into evidence before the Tribunal.



9. By notices of assessment dated 18 October 1984 the applicant was notified of assessments made in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1983, and 30 June 1984, wherein the taxable income was assessed in each year at $105,025. In the 1983 year tax assessed amounted to $53,721 and additional tax assessed also amounted to $53,721. In respect of the 1984 year tax assessed amounted to $53,506 plus health insurance levy of $291, together with additional tax assessed of $53,506. The applicant lodged valid objections against the assessments asserting that the assessments were excessive, not authorised, that the amount of the taxable income was not the taxable income of the taxpayer and a number of general grounds which I need not set out, but some of which could only have any substance if the applicant had lodged a relevant income tax return.



10. The applicant's case is essentially that the respondent erred in the basis in which he determined upon the taxable income in each of the years of income. Exhibit A which is folder entitled "Report on Investigation" includes a report by an officer of the Taxation Office in which he calculated the asset accretion of the applicant, the applicant's husband and an associated company over the period November 1982 to June 1984. Adopting what could only be described as an arbitrary basis, the officer determined in his report that an amount of $310,050 should be attributed to the applicant as taxable income to be assessed over the two years ended 30 June 1983 and 30 June 1984. The report on its face makes it clear that the basis adopted in making the calculations was arbitrary and, more likely than not, incorrect.



11. However, the applicant's case did not attempt to establish before the Tribunal the amount of the applicant's actual taxable income in each of the relevant years. Counsel for the applicant did concede the amount assessed in part, but there was no basis in fact for that concession other than the information contained within the respondent's file.



12. I do not know the nature of the applicant's income producing activities, if any, because she has not given or led evidence which will allow the Tribunal to make any finding as to her taxable income. I was told from the bar table that she was a wife and mother. It seemed to be implied that such persons do not have a taxable income. That is a proposition which I do not accept. I think I can take official notice that it is a matter of common knowledge that wives and mothers do very often have taxable incomes albeit, it may be claimed, sometimes of inadequate amount.



13. Documents T2 and T7 are copy notices of assessment dated 18 October 1984 in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1983 and 30 June 1984. Although the notices were not formally proved before me the applicant did not seek to produce the original notices nor contest in any way the copy notices in evidence except as to the taxable income, tax assessed and additional tax assessed.



14. The accretions statement prepared by the respondent's officer is as follows (edited to maintain confidentiality):



1. Property at W. $175,000



2. Mt O - own funds $300,000

plus quarterly repayments

November 1982 to June 1984 105,700 405,700



3. (irrelevant) NIL



4. Service Station 39,400

$620,100



In his report the officer recommended that the company be assessed on an income of $875,000 for the year ended 30 June 1984 and that the applicant and her husband be assessed on $310,050 each over the two years ended 30 June 1983 and 30 June 1984. This was done in relation to the applicant. I do not know whether the husband and the company were assessed in accordance with the recommendations. However, it is readily apparent that the accretion statement does not support the recommendations made.



15. Section 190 of the Act indicates that the applicant must establish that the taxable income assessed by the respondent exceeds the applicant's actual taxable income. However, as already noted, the applicant has not given evidence as to her taxable income whether by viva voce evidence or by evidence in writing. The basis of her case is that the methodology adopted by the respondent in forming his judgement as to the amount of the taxable income was flawed and it therefore follows that the taxable income is excessive.



16. Although the methodology of an assessment may be found to be erroneous it does not follow as a matter of simple logic that the assessment is therefore excessive. The actual taxable income may be considerably in excess of the amount assessed by the Commissioner. Clearly the Act does not envisage a game of cat and mouse between taxpayers and the Commissioner. Where the taxpayer is in default within the terms of section 167 the Commissioner must form a bona fide judgment as to the amount of the taxable income. To rectify the position the taxpayer must then establish, on the balance of probabilities, that her taxable income is a certain figure as part of the process of showing that the taxable income as assessed is excessive. To merely argue that the respondent's calculated basis upon which his judgment is based is incorrect is not to establish the excessiveness of the assessment unless the applicant can also establish non-compliance with statutory requirements on the part of the Commissioner in making the assessment (McAndrew v. FCT [1956] HCA 62; (1956) 98 CLR 263).



17. The relationship of section 190 with section 177 was carefully explained by the High Court in George v. FCT [1952] HCA 21; (1952) 86 CLR 183 at 201 as follows:



"Section 190 provides that upon every appeal to the court the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive shall lie upon the taxpayer. With this provision must be read s.177(1), which provides that the production of a notice of assessment, or a document purporting to be a copy under the hand of the Commissioner, the Second Commissioner or a Deputy Commissioner, shall be conclusive evidence of the due making of the assessment and (except in proceedings on appeal against the assessment) that the amount and all particulars of the assessment are correct. The word `assessment' is defined by s.6(1) to mean the ascertainment of the amount of taxable income and of the tax payable thereon. In conformity with this definition s.166 directs the Commissioner to make an assessment of the amount of the taxable income of any taxpayer and of the tax payable thereon. From these provisions both in their present form and in their slightly different earlier form, the law has always been taken to be that, in an appeal from an assessment, the burden lies upon the taxpayer of establishing affirmatively that the amount of taxable income for which he has been assessed exceeds the actual taxable income which he has derived during the year of income: Stone v. FCT [1918] HCA 67; (1918) 25 CLR 389 at 392-3; Moreau v. FCT [1926] HCA 28; (1926) 39 CLR 65; FCT v. Clarke [1927] HCA 49; (1927) 40 CLR 246; Trautwein v. FCT [1936] HCA 77; (1936) 56 CLR 63: `The justice of that burden cannot be disputed. From the nature of the tax, the Commissioner has, as a rule, no means of ascertainment but what is learnt from the taxpayer, and the taxpayer is presumably and generally, in fact, acquainted with his own affairs. The onus may prove to be dischargeable easily or with difficulty according to circumstances': Isaacs ACJ, FCT v. Clarke (1927) 40 CLR at 251."



To the same effect is the dicta of Mason J (dissenting) in Gauci v. FCT (1975) 75 ATC 4257 at 4261 as follows:



"Section 190(b) of the Act imposed on the appellants the burden of proving that the assessments were excessive. The appellants relied on their evidence and that of Graham in order to show that the assessments were excessive. Once that evidence was rejected, the appellants' case necessarily failed.



The Act does not place any onus on the Commissioner to show that the assessments were correctly made. Nor is there any statutory requirement that the assessments should be sustained or supported by evidence. The implication of such a requirement would be inconsistent with sec.190(b) for it is a consequence of that provision that unless the appellant shows by evidence that the assessment is incorrect, it will prevail."



That dicta was approved by Gibbs, Stephen and Murphy JJ in McCormack Pty Ltd v. FCT (1979) 79 ATC 4111 at 4121, 4123 and 4132.



18. It is not the case, as was submitted by Mr Hamwood, that the applicant cannot be required to prove a negative. That is not the effect of paragraph 190(b). The High Court has made it clear, in the decisions cited above, that the applicant will discharge the onus by proving factual material which shows that it is more likely than not that the assessment is excessive.



19. The applicant is presumed to be the person who knows best the facts relevant to the ascertainment of the taxable income. If the applicant chooses not to put that evidence before the Tribunal then the Tribunal may infer that the applicant's evidence would not assist her case. This is more especially the case where, as here, the applicant does not offer any explanation for her absence from the witness stand (Jones v. Dunkel [1959] HCA 8; (1959) 101 CLR 298).



20. The question for decision is therefore whether the applicant has discharged the onus of proof in paragraph 190(b) of the Act by showing that the respondent's basis for forming a judgment as to her taxable income was incorrect and the assessments excessive.



21. In Case X65 90 ATC 492 this Tribunal directed that an assessment based upon a judgment said to have been formed under section 167, be set aside. The essential reason was that the assessment had been made on a basis unknown to the law. The defect in the process, as found by the Tribunal, was that the Commissioner had formed a view as to the amount of tax sought to be collected rather than forming a judgment as to the taxable income of the taxpayer. Counsel for the applicant sought to rely upon that decision. However, it has not been established before me that such an incorrect process was undertaken by the Commissioner in the present case. Certainly the accretions statement can be shown to be erroneous but the amounts assessed have no real relevance to that statement. The accretions statement supports a finding that the applicant derived taxable income during the years of income and I so find. It does not, however, go further and establish the amount of the applicant's taxable income. Nor is there any reason why it must do so. Section 167 only requires the Commissioner to form a view as to the amount upon which income tax ought to be levied having first satisfied himself that there is reason to believe the taxpayer derived taxable income. The section then makes such amount the taxable income of that person for the purpose of section 166.



22. In the present case it has not been established that the respondent Commissioner acted other than correctly in the making of the objection decisions under review and the assessments upon which those decisions are based.



23. I am required to decide whether the applicant has discharged the onus of proving the assessments excessive because she has been able to show that the accretions statement is erroneous and the distribution of alleged income from that statement to the three taxpayers is both erroneous and without any logical foundation.



24. In FCT v. Dalco [1990] HCA 3; (1990) 20 ATR 1370 at 1372 Brennan J posed the questions in these terms:



" . . . does the taxpayer discharge the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive where

(a) he does not prove that the amount assessed as his taxable income in fact exceeds his taxable income, but

(b) he shows that the Commissioner formed a judgment as to the amount of his taxable income on a wrong basis?"



25. The answer appears at page 1375-6 of the judgement of Brennan J (with whom the other judges agreed) where his Honour stated:



"But where, as here, the taxpayer has not proved that his actual taxable income is less than the amount assessed, the court does not know all the material facts and it cannot find that the amount assessed is wrong. A taxpayer who shows on the facts that are known a mere error by the Commissioner in assessing the amount of the taxpayer's taxable income does not show that his objection should have been allowed or that the appeal against the assessment must be allowed. . . . Unless the amount of the assessment is found to be excessive in the sense of being greater than the taxable income on which tax ought to have been levied, the taxpayer fails on his appeal."



26. As in Dalco the applicant has failed to prove, on the balance of probabilities, that her actual taxable income was less than the taxable income assessed in each of the years of income. It follows that I am bound to find that she has failed to discharge the onus resting on her as the applicant and must therefore affirm the objection decisions insofar as they relate to the taxable income assessed and the tax payable thereon.



27. I turn now to the additional tax assessments made pursuant to subsection 226(1) of the Act.



28. Very little was said before me regarding the additional tax assessed. Exhibit A which is the Investigation Report makes it clear that the respondent failed to give proper consideration to the question of remission of the tax when making the assessments. That does not in any sense reflect upon the making of the assessments because it is the Act which imposes the additional tax. Therefore, until the respondent considered whether some part or all of the additional tax should be remitted the Act had the effect of imposing additional tax equal to the tax assessed.



29. The objections include grounds relating to the remission of additional tax so that I am required to consider whether some remission of the additional tax should be made. In the circumstances of this case I cannot see any basis for remitting the additional tax in full. However, I am not satisfied, given the basis of assessment, that the applicant's culpability is such as to justify the imposition of additional tax equal to the tax assessed, and the assessments of additional tax were made accordingly.



30. In the result I have come to the conclusion, on the evidence before me, that the additional tax imposed by subsection 226(1) should be remitted in accordance with subsection 226(3) to an amount of $30,000 in each year of income. I have come to that view taking into account the evidence and the fact that the applicant has, without explanation, failed to give evidence in these proceedings. The reduction in the additional tax, as assessed, reflects the patent excessiveness of the additional tax assessed demonstrated by the documents before the Tribunal. In my view that is sufficient reason to remit portion of the additional tax. It should be a very rare case where the tax and additional tax assessed exceed in total the taxable income. This is not such a case.



31. The objection decisions under review will be set aside and the objections allowed in part by remitting the additional tax imposed by subsection 226(1) of the Act to an amount of $30,000 in respect of each year of income. The objection decisions are otherwise affirmed.



This the preceding nine (9) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matters of QT87/1595-1596.



Signed: (Secretary)



Date: 13 February 1991
