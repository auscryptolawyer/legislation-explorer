Statement of Reasons

THE APPLICATION FOR REINSTATEMENT

Issue for determination

  1. The question the Tribunal needs to determine is whether the application for review should be reinstated under s 102 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) (ART Act). This requires the Tribunal to determine:
    (i) what errors fall within section 102 of the ART Act;

    (ii) the error the Applicant says gave rise to the dismissal;

    (iii) whether that error falls with section 102 of the ART Act;

    (iv) whether there are special circumstances justifying reinstatement of the application ;

    (v) if the error does fall within the scope of section 102 of the ART Act, whether there are any discretionary factors militating against reinstatement;

  2. If the Tribunal is satisfied the application should be reinstated under section 102 of the ART Act, then the Tribunal also needs to determine what orders and directions are appropriate for the Tribunal to make.

The legislative provisions

  1. Section 102 of the ART Act prescribes the conditions under which an application may be reinstated.
  2. The Tribunal may dismiss an application under section 98 of the ART Act if the application fee payable by the Applicant to the Tribunal in respect of the application is not paid by the time prescribed by the rules.
  3. The Tribunal is empowered on an application for reinstatement by a party to reinstate an application that was dismissed in error under section 102(6) of the ART Act. Relevantly, subsections 102 (5) and (6) are in the following terms:
    (5) A party to the proceeding in relation to the application may apply to the Tribunal for reinstatement of the application on the grounds of error within 28 days after the party receives notice that the application is dismissed (or such longer period as the Tribunal, in special circumstances, allows).( emphasis added)

    (6) If a party applies under subsection (5) and the Tribunal considers that the application was dismissed in error, the Tribunal may reinstate the application and make such orders as appear to the Tribunal to be appropriate in the circumstances.

  4. If the Tribunal is satisfied that an error, whether administrative or otherwise was made, the question for determination is whether the Tribunal is satisfied “special circumstances exist” to justify reinstating the application, given the application for reinstatement was made more than 28 days after it was dismissed for non-payment of the application fee.

What errors fall within section 102(6) of the ART Act?

  1. In JTBJ and Secretary, Department of Social Services (JTBJ) [1], the President of the Tribunal recently reviewed the cases[2] which have considered the expression ‘dismissed in error’ and summarised the types of errors that fall within subsection 102(6) of the ART Act.[3] The relevant principles that can be distilled from Kyrou J’s analysis include:
    • Errors are not confined to administrative errors or errors on the part of the Tribunal. There is no textual limitation on what might constitute an error but for the fact the Tribunal has dismissed the application, and the error induced the dismissal;[4] and
    • The provisions only apply to default or summary dismissals and not where there has been prima facie consideration of the merits of the application. (emphasis added)
  2. Justice Kyrou in his judgement, summarises the types of error identified by the Courts as falling within section 42 (a)(10) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth) (AAT Act), the predecessor provision as follows:
    • Where an application for review has been dismissed due to a significant procedural error;
    • Where the application for review is dismissed by consent when the applicant has not actually consented or misunderstood the consequences of consent such that it was not true consent;
    • Where the review is dismissed when an applicant does not appear at a Tribunal event and the applicant did in fact appear, or no notice was given to the applicant or the notice was defective;
  3. In Goldie v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural and Indigenous Affairs[5] ( Goldie), Carr J stated there is ‘a significant degree of desirability of persons being able to rely on the ordinary meaning conveyed by the word “error”.[6] As in Goldie, the dismissal of the application for review in these proceedings occurred without the Tribunal considering the merits of the decision, or even holding a Directions Hearing to assess the merits of the application. The dismissal occurred automatically because the Applicant failed to pay the application fee within the six weeks allowed after the application was lodged with the Tribunal. (emphasis added)
  4. This dismissal is an example of what Carr J described as the “AAT’s default jurisdiction”. The dismissal of the application for a review of the decision of the Respondent did not involve a review of the decision the subject of the application on the merits. Carr J stated:
    “the mechanism is a relatively informal method of bringing a matter back to the Tribunal for consideration with a view to it being considered on the merits. It can be seen as a convenient, prompt and inexpensive means whereby errors which may have occurred in the exercise of the Tribunal’s default jurisdiction can be rectified. That is, any sort of error in dismissing an application summarily, whether the error be administrative error or any other error”. (emphasis added)
  5. Justice Carr went on to state the application for review in Goldie had been ‘summarily dismissed by the Tribunal’.[7] The dismissal decision could “... be properly characterised as in the nature of a default decision’, that is, ‘in default of consideration of whether there were even any prima facie merits in the application [for review][8]

Was there an error in the dismissal?

  1. Section 98 of the ART Act is in the following terms:
    “the Tribunal may dismiss an application if a fee payable by the applicant to the Tribunal in respect of the application is not paid by the time prescribed by the rules” (emphasis added)
  2. That section is not drafted in mandatory terms. On 2 April 2005, the Registrar dismissed the application for non-payment of the application fee. Prior to the Registrar dismissing the application the registry had written to the Applicant on 22, 28, and 30 January 2025 and 14 February 2025 to advise the Applicant that the Tribunal could dismiss an application if the fee was not paid by the end of six weeks after the application is lodged. Clearly there was no error on the part of the registry. The error was the Applicants. In correspondence to the Tribunal on 11 November 2025 the Applicant explained she had made an application for a fee reduction shortly after she filed the application and believed the dismissal letter she received from the Tribunal in April 2025 related to the Applicant’s application to pay a reduced fee rather than the application for the substantive review. During the Directions Hearing held on 2 February 2026, the Applicant told the Tribunal she mistakenly believed the substantive matter would not be listed until she paid the application fee. The Tribunal accepted the Applicant’s explanation.

Special Circumstances Justifying Reinstatement

  1. The Applicant stated, in correspondence with the Tribunal and the Respondent [9]that shortly before lodging the application with the Tribunal in December 2024, the Applicant had given birth to a very premature baby. The baby required an extended stay in the Royal Women’s Hospital Melbourne in the neonatal intensive care and special care units. During the time the baby was in the Royal Women’s Hospital, the Applicant was the primary caregiver. It was understandable in the circumstances that the Applicant was not focused on the application she had lodged with the Tribunal shortly after the birth of her child.
  2. Furthermore, the Applicant advised the Tribunal in the email correspondence and again during the Directions Hearing that during the same period she experienced significant personal and financial disruption that included the loss of her employment. The Applicant explained that while she was unemployed, she did not have “immediate access to funds” to pay the Tribunal’s application fee. The Applicant explained her financial position was the reason the Applicant had made an application for a reduction of the application fee. However, given the other distractions the Applicant faced, the Applicant found that she did not have the time to provide the documentary material required to support that application.
  3. The Applicant stated during the Directions Hearing on 2 February 2026 that, once her financial circumstance had stabilised in the first half of 2025 she applied to the Tribunal for an extension of time until 26 August 2025 to seek review of the Respondent’s decision, not appreciating the application she had filed in December 2024 had been dismissed in April 2025.
  4. It was in fact the Respondent who drew the Tribunal’s attention to the fact the application for an extension of time reference matter 2025/ 5767 was “a substantially similar application for review of decision” that had been dismissed under section 98 of the ART Act because the Applicant had not paid the application fee. The Respondent put to the Tribunal that the more appropriate course might be for “the Applicant to apply in writing for application 2024/10165 to be reinstated under section 102 of the ART Act”.[10]
  5. The Applicant advised the Tribunal and the Respondent in correspondence dated 11 November 2025 that it was the Applicant’s genuine intention to pursue the review of the Commissioner’s decision if the Tribunal were minded to exercise its discretion to reinstate the matter under section 102 of the ART Act.
  6. The Applicant advised the Tribunal during the Directions Hearing that the tax assessed to Applicant as a result of the Respondent’s decision had been paid.
  7. In further correspondence to the Tribunal and the Applicant on 26 November 2025, the Respondent advised it was not opposed to the reinstatement of application 2024/10165 pursuant to section 102 ART Act if the Tribunal had the power to reinstate the application.[11]The Respondent requested the matter be listed for directions to “discuss the Tribunal’s view”. (emphasis added)
  8. In a further email to the Tribunal the Respondent advised the Tribunal it did not wish to make any submission on whether the matter should be reinstated. The Respondent stated in that email “we are in the Tribunal’s hands as to whether it considers that it is empowered to do so.”

Decision of the Tribunal

  1. The Tribunal finds that the matter was dismissed summarily by the Registrar in April 2025. Although the Registrar was not in error, the oversight was the Applicant’s. Given that there is no prejudice to the Respondent, the correct or preferable decision is that the application be reinstated and determined on its merits
  2. the Tribunal is satisfied that special circumstances exist justifying the reinstatement of the application and considers the application was dismissed in error in the sense described by Carr J in Goldie.
  3. Pursuant to section 102 (6) of the ART Act, the Tribunal orders that application 2024/10165-6 be reinstated and directs that the matter be listed for Case Management Directions Hearings on a date convenient to the Tribunal in March 2026.
