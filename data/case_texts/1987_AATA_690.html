QT85/2814 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 690; 87 ATC 974 (24 June 1987)

Last Updated: 23 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - company tax - objection to assessment - sale of "target" company - whether vendor shareholder can challenge his personal liability under the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act (1982) before a recoupment assessment has issued.

DECISION AND REASONS

Re:

APPLICANT

And:

RESPONDENT

NOS: QT85/2814, QT85/2810

AAT Decision No 3748

Date: 24 June 1987

Place: Brisbane

Dr P Gerber, Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And:

RESPONDENT

DECISION

Before: Dr P Gerber, Senior Member

Date: 24 June 1987

Place: Brisbane

Pursuant to subsection 43(1)(a) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the Tribunal affirms the objection decision in relation to the assessments for the years ended 30 June 1976 and 30 June 1977.

（Sgd) P Gerber

SENIOR MEMBER

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And:

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Dr P Gerber, Senior Member

Date: 24 June 1987

Place: Brisbane

The taxpayer in these applications is a company -Leprechaun Pty Ltd ("L"). In the 1976 and 1977 tax years, the respondent Commissioner of Taxation asserts that the taxable income of L was not less than $5156 and $367 respectively.

  1. With respect to the 1976 tax year, an assessment issued on February 24, 1977, claiming that tax was due and payable in the sum of $2191.30. Neither this nor any other amount of tax was paid. On 29 December 1983, a further assessment issued, raising Division 7 tax for 1976 of $595 on the "undistributed amount" of $1186.
  2. In the 1977 tax year, an assessment issued on December 29, 1983, claiming that primary tax was due and payable in the sum of $168.62 together with an amount of $84 raised as additional tax for late return - a total of $252.82. A further assessment issued on the same day raising Division 7 tax for that year of $40 on the "undistributed amount" of $80. In the result the total amount of tax said to be due for the two years now before ne is $3077.12.
  3. All shares in L were sold in February 1977.
  4. On May 14, 1985, the respondent forwarded copies of all of L's aforementioned notices of assessment for the years 1976 and 1977 to the vendor shareholders as a preliminary step towards the raising of a recoupment tax assessment pursuant to the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act (the "TUCT" Act) of 1982. Accompanying these copy notices of assessment was an "invitation" to pay all the outstanding ordinary company tax and undistributed profits tax ($2993.12) by instalments over a period of twelve months. In consideration of such a settlement, the respondent agreed to forego the additional tax for late payment.
  5. On July 3, 1985, some 50 pages of tightly drawn notices of objection against the various assessments of primary tax, undistributed profits tax and sec. 226(3) penalty tax were lodged by one Roberta Coiffeur, being one of the persons upon whom the respondent had served the copy notices of assessment, referred to in paragraph 5 hereof, qua beneficial owner of shares in L.
  6. At the hearing, Mr Wruck, who appeared for L, conceded in his opening that L had earned the income as assessed, and that the primary tax in sum of $2191.30 was the proper amount owing to the respondent. He tendered what purported to be a copy of the sale agreement, setting out the terms and conditions on which the ownership of the shares were transferred from the vendors to the purchaser. The purported copy agreement included L's balance sheet (as at February 3, 1977) showing, inter alia, a tax liability in the sum of $2191.31. Mr Wruck added from the bar table that "as at the sale date, no dividend had been declared by the taxpayer company in respect of the year ended June 1976. It was the understanding of the vendors that the purchaser would effect whatever was necessary in respect of that dividend and the subsequent Division 7 assessment, issued on 29 December 1983, as did the ensuing 1977 prime tax and Division 7 assessments". No evidence was called in support of that assertion (or, indeed, any other assertion). Mr Wruck added with some poignancy: "that as far as we are aware, there is no material available to the Commissioner or to ourselves that deals with the company post the sale date". For good measure, the purported copy agreement and balance sheet which were made an exhibit were unsigned.
  7. I was informed from the bar table that to date the respondent has not raised a recoupment tax assessment. This fact is not in dispute.
  8. I have concluded that in the absence of a recoupment tax assessment, the only "live" issues before me today are the various assessments against L.
  9. In the circumstances, Mr Wruck's interesting and instructive argument "that the vendor shareholders are not liable .... unless the A-L+T test is proven" is premature.
  10. The failure by the applicant to call anything that could be characterized as evidence leaves me no alternative but to confirm the decisions on the various objections. It is, after all, trite to point out that, as in all jurisdictions, an appellant carries the burden of proof, which burden this applicant has monumentally failed to discharge. I am not unmindful of Mr Wruck's plight in that it is claimed that all the relevant witnesses have failed to survive the passing of the recoupment legislation. It is a moving plea in mitigation, but it comes at a point too soon to be relevant to the one and only issue presently before me - whether or not the various assessments issued to the company are excessive.
  11. The assessments under review are affirmed.

This is to certify that this and the preceding 4 pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons for Decision of Dr P. Gerber, Senior Member in the matter No QT85/2814, QT85/2810.

Date: 28 august 1987

Associate
