Re Taxation Appeals [1989] AATA 138 (1 June 1989)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. NT86/2614

AAT No. 5124

Practice and Procedure

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Peter M. Roach(1) (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Practice and Procedure - Taxation Appeals Division - Circuit sittings - Non-appearance for Application - Non-appearance unexplained but unexpected - Appliocation for adjourment - History of non-attendance - Adjournment refused.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 S.42A(2)

Case V103 88 ACT 664

Case V111 88 ACT 712

HEARING

SYDNEY

1:6:1989

ORDER

The Applicant having failed to attend in person or by representative upon the hearing and an application for adjourment having been refused, the Tribunal dismisses the application and confirms the decision of the Respondent under review.

DECISION

On 1 July 1986 this Tribunal assumed responsibility for the determination of tax disputes previously the responsibility of the Taxation Boards of Review. The task facing the Tribunal was a daunting one because it had in prospect the need to overcome years of neglect of the tax litigation system. In the previous decade or more the number of requests for reference for independent review of determinations of the Commissioner upon objections had built up and the rate of build up had been substantially increased by the failure of the system to efficiently and effectively provide for their determination. One of the most serious deficiencies had been manifested through delay: something which is always inimical to justice.

2. From 1 July 1986 the Tribunal set about its responsibilities with vigour: a vigour made possible by the new conditions for the exercise of its jurisdiction. One person could constitute the Tribunal where previously three had been the norm and two the minimum. Unlike practices which had been normative for many years prior to the early 1980's, instead of taxpayers being advised of a hearing date without warning and without benefit of prior consultation and only several years after their request for reference had been made and not acknowledged, the Tribunal began using its Preliminary Conference procedure to ensure that matters were in fact needful of being resolved by a hearing; that the issues to be determined were mutually known; that the issues to be determined were no more extensive than necessary; and that on both sides the parties would be fairly warned as to the evidence to be presented to the hearing so as to by-pass any question of surprise commonly referred to as "trial by ambush".

3. Further, to the great advantage of those concerned, hearings began to take place in regional centres - a practice previously known on a regular basis only in Queensland. The advantages to those genuinely pursuing arguable cases were substantial.

4. But sometimes in its concern to ensure a just determination under fair conditions the Tribunal has been too indulgent. In its concern to achieve justice, by indulgence it has sometimes contributed to injustice. Justice is a quality consistent with firmness and a quality which rarely, if ever, is well served by weakness or indulgence. In less than three years the Tribunal has substantially eliminated the backlog of unresolved disputes. Where hearings had been delayed needlessly for periods of 6, 8 and even 10 years one is now able to say that the norm is nearer 3 years and, if the Tribunal can maintain its effort, it should be possible within the near future to ensure that it will become normative for disputes over assessments to be resolved within 12 months of the dispute arising. Unhappily the present application falls far short of meeting those expectations.

5. The present application relates to the year of income ended 30 June 1985. It relates to the income tax liability of a construction rigger employed by only one employer during the year of income ended 30 June 1985. Whether he was employed at more than one work site or not is not known, but it is known that he was paid $1,167 as a travelling allowance to which he was entitled pursuant to the relevant industrial award. Nonetheless, in his return of income it seems that he claimed a deduction for travelling expenses in the sum of $5,460-00. What information was supplied in support of the claim in the return is not known as the return was not incorporated in the Formal Documents when they were transmitted to the Tribunal. That was notwithstanding the then provisions of the Income Tax Regulations. What happened thereafter can be most clearly seen by reference to a chronological table. In considering it, it should be borne in mind that the application arose in a regional centre. That limited the opportunities for a hearing and occasioned telephone conferences when, but for that circumstance, attendance before the Tribunal might have been more appropriate.

10 December 1985

6. The Commissioner assessed the taxpayer as having derived taxable income of $21,972; disallowed "travelling expenses $4,293" ($5,460 minus $1,167); and paid to the Applicant the credit due to him of $1,672-69.

19 December 1985

7. The Applicant by his then accountant objected to the assessment stating (inter alia):

"Travelling expenses $4,293-00 -

'These expenses were necessarily incurred by the

taxpayer in order to carry out his duties with his

employer and was required to travel from job site to

job site daily as directed by his employer and although

an allowance was received from his employer it was

necessary for the taxpayer to expend the amount

claimed, details of which are as shown in his tax

return.'"

18 February 1986

8. The Commissioner advised the taxpayer that his objection had been considered and disallowed.

26 February 1986

9. The Applicant, by his then accountant, requested reference of his objections for independent review.

23 June 1986

10. The Commissioner referred the matter from his appropriate regional office to the relevent Taxation Board of Review for review.

1 July 1986

11. The Administrative Appeals Tribunal assumed the responsibilities previously borne by the Taxation Boards of Review.

29 September 1986

12. The Tribunal gave notice to the Applicant that his matter would be "called over" on 7 October 1986 at the first regional sittings of the Tax Division of the Tribunal to be held in New South Wales.

7 October 1986

13. There was no appearance by the Applicant and there had been no response from the agent to whose address the notice had been dispatched. The Tribunal directed that the Commissioner should provide an "update" on the taxpayer's address.

24 November 1986

14. The Commissioner advised the Tribunal that he knew of no new address.

6 February 1987

15. The Tribunal again wrote to the Applicant, C/- the original address, seeking information as to the expectations of the Applicant. It was advised of a new address, C/- a new agent. As that agent has at all times from that date had the carriage of the matter, I shall hereafter refer to him as "the agent".

29 May 1987

16. The Tribunal appointed a telephone Preliminary Conference to be held on 29 June 1987.

29 June 1987

17. At the Preliminary Conference the arguments foreshadowed in the Notice of Objection and arguments which were new to the Commissioner were raised and canvassed. The Agent undertook to advise the ATO of relevant particulars in relation to the new argument and he also undertook to make further inquiries in support of the taxpayer's claim. Proceedings concluded that day on the basis that a further Preliminary Conference would be held after mid-August 1987.

22 July 1987

18. A notice of a further telephone Preliminary Conference for "13.08.86" (sic) was issued.

13 August 1987

19. Attempts to conduct the Preliminary Conference by telephone failed. The Member conducting the conference recorded that:

"His answering machine responded and stated he was

engaged on client business."

The Commissioner's representative advised that he had not had any contact since the previous Preliminary Conference. The Member recommended that another Preliminary Conference be held and that the agent be advised that failure to respond might result in dismissal.

31 August 1987

20. Notice of a resumed telephone Preliminary Conference for 10 September 1987 was given coupled with a warning - as to the soundness of which I make no comment -

"Your attention is drawn to the provisions of

Section 42(a)(ii) of the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal Act 1975 under which the Tribunal may dismiss

the application without proceeding to review the

decision where an applicant fails to appear in person

or be represented at the hearing (sic)."

10 September 1987

21. A telephone Preliminary Conference was held. The issues were again canvassed and as it was agreed that there was no possibility of agreement. It was determined that the matter should proceed to hearing in the relevant regional city, but with a request that the hearing should not be listed for at least 6 weeks to enable Union evidence to be obtained.

17 March 1988

22. The Tribunal again gave notice for a telephone Directions Hearing to be held 28 April 1988.

28 April 1988

23. The agent was not available.

2 May 1988

24. A Registry officer spoke with the agent who undertook to advise whether the Applicant was proceeding to hearing and whether a hearing date should be allotted for the week commencing 15 August 1988.

31 May 1988

25. The Registry made further inquiries of the agent without success.

7 July 1988

26. Notice of a resumed telephone Preliminary Conference for 31 August 1988 was given.

31 August 1988

27. A second Member conducted a Preliminary Conference and was asked to allow a further 10 days or so to enable the agent to obtain the further information he needed. The attention of the agent was directed to recent decisions (Case V103 88 ATC 664 and Case V111 88 ATC 712). The matter was adjourned on the basis that there would be a further telephone conference as soon as possible after 16 September 1988.

19 October 1988

28. A notice of a resumed telephone Preliminary Conference was given for 9 December 1988.

9 December 1988

29. When the Member sought to convene the conference the agent was "not available" for the conference. The Member recommended that the matter should be listed for hearing.

3 April 1989

30. A notice was given by the Tribunal listing the matter for hearing in the regional city on 21 April 1989.

7 April 1989

31. A second notice issued from the Tribunal advising that the hearing date had been set for 9 a.m. 20 April 1989.

20 April 1989

32. Upon the matter being called on for hearing there was no appearance for the Applicant but, as the Commissioner's representative advised that as a result of recent communications he was expecting the Applicant to be represented, I caused inquiries to be made as to whether the agent was en route to the hearing. It was then reported that the agent was not on his way to the hearing, but in fact was now seeking an adjournment of the matter.

33. To ensure complete fairness to the Applicant I determined to confer with the agent by telephone from chambers, but doing so only in the presence of the Commissioner's representative. It was then put that problems of having been on notice to attend Court as a witness since the beginning of the week and major negotiations touching the practice of the agent had caused him to overlook the commitment to be in attendance at the hearing. However, it then appeared that he had intended to do no more at the hearing than present a document signed by the Applicant which, according to the Commissioner's representative, had never been seen by the Commissioner. Arrangements were made for the text of that document to be communicated to the Tribunal by facsimile transmission immediately. Three hours passed and further inquiries were made. The Tribunal was then advised that the transmission had been delayed by the need to obtain a copy of the relevant provisions of the industrial award and that the document would be transmitted almost immediately. That undertaking was complied with and, upon the terms of the communication being made known to the Commissioner's representative, he affirmed his determination that the application for adjournment should be opposed.

34. At that point the agent was provided with an opportunity to put forward all arguments as to why the adjournment should be granted. It then appeared that:

(a) the statement of the Applicant first notified to the

Tribunal and to the Commissioner on 20 April 1989

had been in the possession of the agent from

approximately August 1988;

(b) that there was nothing in the terms of the statement

indicating that it was likely that the Applicant was

entitled to have the relief sought;

(c) that the agent, by his observations, indicated that

he was unaware of the relevant principles of law to

be applied and was quite unaware of relevant

decisions of the Tribunal as to travelling expenses

(such as Cases V103 and V111 - ante);

His impression was that if an allowance was provided

for by an industrial award, it automatically made

deductible an amount equivalent to the allowance

received (sed cf. Case V39 88 ATC 335); seemingly

he had not adverted to the circumstance that, even

if there were an automatic entitlement to a

deduction in an amount equal to any allowance

received, the present claim could not be based on

such a principle for, rightly or wrongly, that had

already been conceded: what the Applicant was

seeking was a deduction of further expenses in

relation to which he had not received any allowance.

35. Clearly the primary responsibility of the Tribunal is to to provide for the just determination of matters referred to it under fair conditions. Those conditions of fairness must have regard not only to the interests of the individual taxpayer but also to the interests of the community at large as represented by the Commissioner. In my view, in those circumstances there could have been but one sound decision: the application for adjournment had to be refused. If as a result the Applicant happens to have forfeited an entitlement to a deduction - an improbable, but not impossible, result - the reason lies not with the Tribunal but with his representative. Others should take note, particularly those minded to take too casual a view of the need to attend upon the Tribunal when called on, that the extension of indulgences in the event of non-appearance should not come to be understood as an assurance that such concessions and indulgences may always be had.

36. In consequence the order of the Tribunal was, for the reasons expressed in the foregoing, that the application be dismissed.
