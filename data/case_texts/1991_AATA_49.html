Re Melbourne Western Region Commission Incorporated and Commissioner of Taxation [1991] AATA 49 (28 February 1991)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: MELBOURNE WESTERN REGION COMMISSION INCORPORATED

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Nos. VT87/1701 and VT87/1702

AAT No. 6709

Debits Tax Administration Act

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Mrs R.A. Balmford(1) (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Debits Tax Administration Act 1982 - certificate of exemption - whether applicant a "municipal corporation or other local governing body" or a "public benevolent institution"

Debits Tax Administration Act 1982 ss.3, 11, 22, 25D

Taxation Administration Act 1953 s.14ZF

Grants Commission Act 1973 s.17

Local Government Act 1958 (Vic) s.8

Local Government Act 1989 (Vic) ss.5, 6, 8

Tribe v Salt Lake City Corp., Utah 540 P.2d 499

Commissioner of Pay-roll Tax (Vic) v Cairnmillar Institute (1990) 21 ATR 665

FCT v Launceston Legacy Club (1987) 75 ALR 122

Australian Council of Social Service Inc v Commissioner of Pay-roll Tax (NSW) (1985) 85 ATC 4235

HEARING

MELBOURNE

28:2:1991

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decisions under review.

DECISION

These are two applications for review of decisions of the respondent made on 5 September 1984 disallowing two objections dated 28 May 1984 by the applicant ("the Western Region Commission") to the refusal by the respondent to grant to the applicant in relation to each of two bank accounts held by the applicant with the National Australia Bank a certificate of exemption pursuant to section 11 of the Bank Accounts Debits Tax Administration Act 1982 (now the Debits Tax Administration Act 1982) ("the Act"). The applicant's original applications for certificates of exemption were made on 2 May 1983.

2. On 1 November 1984 the applicant requested that the decisions on the objections be referred to a Board of Review, and the decisions were referred to this Tribunal. By virtue of sub-section 25B(2) of the Act, referral of the decisions on the applicant's objections is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of applications to the Tribunal for review of the decisions.

3. The inclusion of an objection under sub-section 22(1) of the Act in the definition of "objection" in sub-section 3(1) of the Taxation (Interest on Overpayments) Act 1983 brings a decision of the respondent under the Act on an objection under sub-section 22(1) within the definition of "objection decision" in section 14ZB of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 ("the Administration Act"). Accordingly, by virtue of section 14ZC of the Administration Act, the provisions of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act") apply to the objection decisions under review, subject to the modifications referred to in that section.

4. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the AAT Act as modified by section 14ZG of the Administration Act, together with other material lodged at the hearing. Evidence was given by Mr Maddern, executive director of the applicant, and Mr Saville, manager of Western Region Group Training Ltd. The applicant was represented by Mr Maddern and the respondent by Mr Power of counsel. At the request of the applicant the proceeding was heard in public as provided for by section 14ZF of the Administration Act.

5. The Debits Tax Act 1982 (formerly the Bank Accounts Debits Tax Act 1982) imposes a tax on (broadly speaking) debits to bank accounts, and provides that the Act "is incorporated and shall be read as one with this Act". The relevant provisions of the Act are as follows:

" 3.(1) In this Act, unless the contrary intention

appears -

...

`excluded debit' means a debit -

(a) made to an account kept with a financial

institution in the name of -

...

(vi) any of the following:

(A) a public benevolent or a religious institution;

...

being a debit made in relation to a transaction

or transactions carried out by or on behalf of the

institution ... wholly and exclusively in

furtherance of its objects;

...

(vii) any of the following:

...

(C) a municipal corporation or other governing body,

other than such a ... corporation or body the sole or principal

function of which is to carry on an activity in the nature of a

business (whether or not for profit), not being a debit made in

relation to a transaction or transactions entered into by or on

behalf of the ... corporation or body in connection with the

carrying on of an activity (other than an activity that forms a

minor or insignificant part of the functions of the ... corporation

or body) in the nature of a business (whether or not for profit);"

" 11.(1) Where an account holder in respect of an

account kept in Australia applies to the Commissioner in

accordance with this section for the issue of a

certificate of exemption in relation to the account -

(a) if the Commissioner is satisfied that all debits made, or to be

made, to the account are, or are likely to be, either excluded debits
or

exempt debits - he shall issue a certificate of exemption in relation to

the account; or

(b) if the Commissioner is not so satisfied - he shall refuse the

application and shall cause notice in writing of his decision in

relation to the application to be served, by post or otherwise, on
the

person who made the application.

(2) A certificate of exemption comes into force on a day specified in

the certificate as the day of commencement of the certificate (which may

be a day before the day on which the certificate is issued) and remains

in force until the expiration of the day specified in the certificate as

the day of expiry of the certificate or, if no day is specified as the

day of expiry of the certificate, until the certificate ceases to be in

force by virtue of sub-section (5)."

These provisions have not been amended during the relevant period, save that the second passage in brackets in sub-sub-paragraph (a)(vii)(C) of the definition of "excluded debit" in sub-section 3(1) was inserted by the Bank Accounts Debits Tax Administration Amendment Act 1984 with effect from 1 November 1984.

6. Also relevant is section 25D of the Act, which was inserted by the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 and accordingly applies to these proceedings, and which reads as follows, so far as relevant:

" 25D. In proceedings under this Part on a review

before the Tribunal or on appeal to a court -

...

(b) the burden of proving that a prescribed decision

is incorrect, or that an assessment is excessive, lies on

the objector."

"Prescribed decision" is defined in section 20 and includes a decision by the respondent to refuse to issue a certificate of exemption.

7. The submission of the applicant was that it was either a "public benevolent institution" in terms of sub-sub-paragraph (a)(vi)(A) of the definition of "excluded debit" in sub-section 3(1) of the Act, or, in terms of sub-sub-paragraph (a)(vii)(C) of that definition, a "municipal corporation or other local governing body" the sole or principal function of which was not to carry on an activity in the nature of a business. Accordingly all debits made to its bank accounts were "excluded debits" in terms of that definition, and it was therefore entitled to a certificate of exemption pursuant to section 11.

8. I accept the evidence of Mr Maddern and Mr Saville, which was not challenged by the respondent, and on the basis of that evidence and documents before the Tribunal I find the facts set out in this and the following three paragraphs. The Western Region Commission is now incorporated under the Associations Incorporation Act 1981 of Victoria. It was originally established, following a conference held in 1972, by agreement between the councils of a number of municipalities in the Western Region of Melbourne, namely the Cities of Altona, Essendon, Footscray, Keilor, Sunshine and Williamstown, the Shires of Bacchus Marsh and Melton and the Shire (now City) of Werribee.

9. On 18 October 1974 the Minister for Urban and Regional Development advised that in accordance with section 17 of the Commonwealth Grants Commission Act 1973, as it then stood, he had approved the Western Region Commission, representing or acting on behalf of the local governing bodies which had established it, as an "approved regional organisation" for the purposes of that Act. Section 18 of that Act empowers an "approved regional organisation" to apply for the grant, under section 96 of the Constitution, of financial assistance to a State for the purposes of the organisation or of all or any of the local governing bodies which the organisation is representing or on behalf of which the organisation is acting.

10. Rule 4.1 of the Rules of the Western Region Commission provides that "the membership of the Commission shall consist of one councillor appointed by each of the Municipalities within the region". Nevertheless, the expression "member councils" is used in material before the Tribunal and is adopted in these Reasons on the understanding that it refers to the several municipalities listed in paragraph 8 supra, which in fact appoint the individuals who are the members of the Western Region Commission.

11. The purposes of the Western Region Commission as set out in Rule 3 are:

"1. To assist Local Government councils in the region

who are contributors to the Commission to work together and

co-operate on problems of mutual interest.

2. To act as advocates to the Australian and

State Governments on behalf of contributing councils on

matters of regional significance.

3. To submit to the Australian and State

Governments requests for financial assistance, additional

resources and policy changes for the region and

for contributing councils.

4. To strengthen the role of Local Government

in those services provided or co-ordinated by other

authorities and Government Departments especially

the services of planning, social welfare, recreation and

transport.

5. To develop and implement a regional economic

growth strategy directed at the creation of job

opportunities in the western suburbs.

6. To develop and implement a strategy for

regional physical development.

7. To convene and promote regular meetings

between Local Government Officers of the region to provide

a forum for the study of matters of regional

importance and significance.

8. To advance the interests of the region

through various regional objectives determined by public

and private consultation."

12. The Western Region Commission receives funding from member councils and from State and Federal governments. Its activities in the Western Region of Melbourne have included:

- acting as an agency for distribution, to member councils and

voluntary relief agencies, of Commonwealth funds for emergency

relief in the region;

- providing information services for migrant families;

- assisting local government to develop projects for housing

disadvantaged people;

- administering environmental improvement projects

in order to create training opportunities;

- employing people from disadvantaged groups;

- promoting industry;

- assisting with programmes for the alleviation of unemployment;

- establishing a group training scheme to provide apprenticeship and

clerical training opportunities for young people;

- funding development of parks and gardens, creek

and stream improvements, tree planting and general regional

beautification programmes;

- acting as managing body during the establishment of the Living

Museum of the West;

- acting as an advocate, service provider and project manager for

member councils.

13. Mr Maddern submitted that the evidence of the activities of the Western Region Commission established that it was functioning as a public benevolent institution and as a municipal corporation or other local governing body. The distribution of emergency relief was an example of a public benevolent activity; and the purposes set out in paragraph 11 supra were all within the objectives of local government as such.

14. Mr Maddern also referred to other organisations which had been accepted as falling within other definitions contained in provisions in other legislation. However, it did not appear that any of those organisations was sufficiently similar to the Western Region Commission or the legislative provisions sufficiently similar to those with which I am concerned for their situations to be relevant to the matter before me.

15. Mr Power refered me to several definitions of "municipal corporation". Black's Law Dictionary, fifth edition 1979, published by West Publishing Co of St Paul, Minnesota, USA, defines that expression as follows:

"A legal institution formed by charter from sovereign (i.e. state)

power erecting a populous community of prescribed area into a body

politic and corporate with corporate name and continuous succession

and for the purpose and with the authority of subordinate

self-government and improvement and local administration of affairs

of state. A body corporate consisting of the inhabitants of a

designated area created by the legislature with or without the

consent of such inhabitants for governmental purposes, possessing

local legislative and administrative power, also power to exercise

within such area so much of the administrative power of the state as

may be delegated to it and possessing limited capacity to own and

hold property and to act in purveyance of public conveniences.

Municipal corporation is a body politic and corporate, created to

administer the internal concerns of the district embraced with its

corporate limits, in matters peculiar to such place and not common

to the state at large. Tribe v Salt Lake City Corp., Utah, 540

P.2d 499, 502"

and Jowitt's Dictionary of English Law, second edition 1977, published by Sweet and Maxwell Ltd of London, as follows:

"(A) body of persons in a town having the powers of acting as one

person, of holding and transmitting property, and of regulating the

government of the town. Such corporations existed in the chief

towns of England (as of other countries from very early times),

deriving their authority from incorporating charters granted by the

Crown. They were regulated by the Municipal Corporations Acts,

1835 and 1882 and the Local Government, 1933."

Both of those descriptions, despite their obvious inconsistencies, notably as to membership, seem to me to be apt to describe broadly what is, in ordinary usage, meant by the expression "municipal corporation" in the provision with which I am here concerned.

16. Sub-section 8(1) of the Victorian Local Government Act 1958 provided:

"Inhabitants of a shire, etc, a corporation

8.(1) The inhabitants of every shire borough town and city for the

time being subject to the provisions of this Act shall, under the

name of the president councillors and ratepayers of such shire the

mayors (sic) councillors and burgesses of such borough the mayor

councillors and burgesses of such town or the mayor councillors and

citizens of such city as the case may be, be a body corporate with

perpetual succession and a common seal with power to break alter and

change the same from time to time and shall by such name be capable

in law of suing and being sued, of purchasing holding and alienating

land, and of doing and suffering subject to the provisions of this

Act all such other acts and things as bodies corporate may by law do

and suffer."

The by-law making power and other local governing powers of a body corporate so established were set out in later provisions of that Act.

17. The Local Government Act 1958 has now been repealed and replaced by the Local Government Act 1989, and sub-sections 5(1) and (2) and 6(1) of that Act provide:

" 5.(1) A Council consists of its Councillors, who

are the representatives, elected in accordance with this

Act, of persons who are residents in the

Council's municipal district or ratepayers of the Council.

(2) A Council -

(a) is a body corporate with perpetual succession;

and

(b) must have a common seal; and

(c) may sue or be sued in its corporate name; and

(d) is capable of acquiring, holding, dealing with or

disposing or property for the purpose of performing its

functions and exercising its powers; and

(e) is capable of doing and suffering all acts and

things which bodies corporate may by law do and suffer and

which are necessary or expedient

for performing its functions and exercising its powers.

6.(1) The purposes of a Council are -

(a) to provide for the peace, order and good

government of its municipal district; and

(b) to facilitate and encourage appropriate

development of its municipal district in the best interests

of the community; and

(c) to provide equitable and appropriate services and

facilities for the community and to ensure that those

services and facilities are managed

efficiently and effectively; and

(d) to manage, improve and develop the resources of

its district efficiently and effectively."

Sub-section 8(1) provides that a Council has (inter alia) the functions specified in Schedule 1, which reads as follows:

"The functions of Councils include the following: 1. General

public services including - (1) Fire prevention and protection; (2)

Local emergency and safety services; (3) Animal control, protection

and conservation; (4) Animal impounding; (5) Plant control; (6) Tip

establishment and operation; (7) Litter control; (8) Collection and

disposal of refuse; 2. Health, education, welfare and other

community services including - (1) Services for children and

families; (2) Health inspection services; (3) Public conveniences;

(4) Prevention and abatement of nuisances; (5) Child care and

development services and youth services; (6) Aged, disabled and

disadvantaged person services; (7) Migrants (sic) services; (8)

Cemeteries; 3. Planning and land use including - (1) Building

control; (2) Housing and other accommodation; 4. Property services

including - (1) Water, drainage, sewerage, gas and electricity; (2)

Land development schemes; (3) Street maintenance and cleaning; 5.

Recreational and cultural services including - (1) Halls and public

buildings; (2) Sport, recreation, leisure and arts; (3) Parks,

gardens and reserves; (4) Libraries and museums; (5) Historic

buildings and places; (6) Public entertainment; 6. Roads including

- (1) Bridges; (2) Footpaths, bicycle paths and nature strips; (3)

Traffic control and signs; (4) Lighting and drainage of roads; 7.

Any other functions relating to the peace, order and good government

of the municipal district including - (1) Parking; (2) Transport;

(3) Aerodromes; (4) Tourism; (5) Information; (6) Encouragement of

employment opportunities; (7) Encouragement of commerce, industry

and agriculture; (8) Environment control, protection and

conservation; (9) Municipal enterprises (trading or

entrepreneurial); (10) Municipal administration; (11) Weights and

measures."

18. In my view, all of this material, allowing for its inconsistencies (again, notably as to membership) describes broadly the kind of body which is intended to be comprised within the expression "municipal corporation" in sub-paragraph (vii)(c) of the definition of "excluded debt" in the Act. That expression, combined with "or other local governing body" is calculated to subsume any body, wherever it is situate within the Commonwealth, and under whatever legislation it is established, which, in terms of the decision in Tribe v Salt Lake City (paragraph 15 supra) "is a body politic and corporate created to administer the internal concerns of the district embraced with its corporate limits, in matters peculiar to such place and not common to the state at large". The words "municipal" and "local" in sub-paragraph (vii)(c) indicate that that provision is concerned with bodies operating in respect of a discrete district, region or area within a State or Territory.

19. The member councils of the Western Region Commission are, each in respect of its own municipal district, "municipal corporations" by virtue, since 1989, of the Local Government Act 1989, and before that of the various predecessors of that Act since they were severally established. It is possible to conceive of there being two bodies, in respect of a given district, which might fall within that description, each having powers under different heads, in the same way as State (or Territory) and Federal Governments operate on a larger scale. However, on the basis of the evidence set out in paragraphs 8-12 supra, I cannot find that the Western Region Commission is a "municipal corporation or other local governing body" in respect of the whole or any part of the Western Region of Melbourne. It has no powers of government or administration in the region, but operates to supplement, assist and facilitate in a variety of ways the operations of its member Councils, which do have such powers.

20. The authorities on the expression "public benevolent institution" were considered at length by McGarvie J in the Supreme Court of Victoria in Commissioner of Pay-roll Tax (Vic) v Cairnmillar Institute (1990) 21 ATR 665. His Honour emphasised (at page 671) that the question should be examined on the basis of "the ordinary use of the English language in this community today" and found that the Cairnmillar Institute, which provided psychotherapy services for which fees were normally charged, was a "public benevolent institution" for the purposes of the Victorian Pay-roll Tax Act 1971. He said, at page 675:

"In my opinion the authorities establish that a public institution

which provides relief in a direct way to persons suffering from some

unfortunate disability or condition is a public benevolent

institution."

21. In one of the cases discussed by His Honour, FCT v Launceston Legacy Club (1987) 75 ALR 122, Northrop J found the respondent to be a public benevolent institution for the purposes of the Act. He said (at pages 137-138):

"I am satisfied that Launceston Legacy is an institution organised

for the relief of poverty, suffering, distress or misfortune as

discussed in the authorities. It is quite natural to describe it

as a benevolent institution.

...

In my opinion, the essential object of Launceston Legacy is

benevolent. The objects and powers listed in the Objects Section

of its Constitution are incidental and ancillary to that essential

object."

22. The question was considered, in the context of pay- roll tax legislation, by the New South Wales Court of Appeal in Australian Council of Social Service Inc v Commissioner of Pay-roll Tax (NSW) (1985) 85 ATC 4235. The appellant did not give direct relief to poverty or distress: it performed advisory, informative, research and advocacy functions with a view to changing the circumstances which either create or aggravate distress. The appeal was dismissed. Priestley JA, with whom Mahoney J.A. agreed, said (at page 4242):

"(I)t seems to me that `public benevolent institution' includes an

institution which in a public way conducts itself benevolently

towards those who are recognisably in need of benevolence but

excludes an institution which although concerned, in an abstract

sense, with the relief of poverty and distress, manifests that

concern by promotion of social welfare in the community generally."

Street C.J. similarly agreed with Priestley J.A. but expressed the view that the phrase "public benevolent institution" required "the presence of the basic element of direct distribution or dispensation of aid"; a question which Priestley J.A. expressly refrained from deciding.

23. On the evidence before me, those activities of the Western Region Commission related to the provision of relief are comparatively minor in the context of its activities as a whole, and do not involve "direct distribution or dispensation of aid". On the basis of the authorities to which I have referred, I find that it is not a "public benevolent institution" for the purposes of the Act.

24. As the applicant has not satisfied the burden cast upon it by section 25D of the Act of proving that a prescribed decision is incorrect, the decisions under review will be affirmed.
