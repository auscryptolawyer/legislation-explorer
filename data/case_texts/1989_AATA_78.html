REASONS FOR DECISION

  1. The taxpayer, a proprietary company, has appealed against decisions of the Commissioner of Taxation disallowing objections by the applicant to assessments of tax for the years ended 30 June 1976 and succeeding years up to, and including the year ended 30 June 1981. In each year the assessments appealed against were in respect of income tax, Division 7 tax and additional tax under the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936, as amended.
    1. In 1975 X was the beneficial owner of the issued capital of a number of companies which were carrying on in Australia the business of teaching shorthand, typing and other associated skills. These companies (hereafter called the Centres) had schools in Canberra, Sydney and other cities. All of the issued shares in the Centres were in the names of Mr. & Mrs. S who were also the directors of those companies. They were the nominees of X. In the same year, after receiving the written authority, dated 18 November 1975, of the Reserve Bank of Australia under the Banking (Foreign Exchange) Regulations, the taxpayer entered into an agreement dated-9;December, 1975 with D Limited an English company having its registered office in London relating to the grant to the taxpayer of a licence or franchise to use certain systems of teaching shorthand and touch-typing in defined geographic areas within the Commonwealth of Australia. The relevant system for present purposes was the touch-typing system. In a Schedule to the Agreement this system was referred to as: "An improved method of teaching touch-typing contained in six stereo cassette recorded tapes". According to X the reference, but for a typing error, should have been to "An improved system of teaching touch-typing contained in six video tapes and six stereo cassette recorded tapes ".

3. The said agreement provided by clause 2(1) that subject to the written approval of the proprietor, D Limited, being first had and obtained, the operator, being the taxpayer, should have the right to sub-license or sub-franchise all rights accruing to it pursuant to the agreement. It was provided by clause 6 of the agreement that subject to prior determination under clause 10 the term of the agreement should be for a period of 3 years from 1 January 1976 and that the taxpayer should have the option to extend the agreement for a further period of one year and a further option to extend the period for a further year, the first option to be exercised by written notice given not less than 3 months prior to the expiration of the term of 3 years in the case of the first option, and the additional period of one year in the case of the second option.

4 Clause 7 provided: "Subject to Clause 8 hereof the operator shall pay to the proprietor as a royalty in such, sum as shall represent 15% of the aggregate gross receipts by way of fees from students enrolled by the operator or any sub-licensee or sub-franchisee of the operator to attend courses using the teaching system." Clause 8 provided for certain minimum payments to be made by the operator to the proprietor during each year of the term of the agreement, such payments to be by monthly instalments on the first day of each month. Clause 10 provided (inter alia) that if any payment due to the proprietor should fail to be paid within 14 days of the due day the proprietor might terminate the agreement without notice. Subsequent to the execution of the said agreement the taxpayer entered into sub-license agreements with the Centres. These sub-license agreements were similar in their terms to the said agreement except as to geographic area. Each provided for the payment to the taxpayer of royalties of 15.5% of the aggregate gross receipts by way of fees from students enrolled by the Centre or by any sub-licensee or sub-franchisee of the Centre in courses using the teaching systems the subject of the agreement.

  1. The Centres commenced using the said teaching systems in the beginning of 1976. During the year ended 30 June 1976 the taxpayer received from the Centres $61,868.21 in franchise fees. Out of those fees the taxpayer paid $58,675.00 to D Limited. Upon the basis that the said fees should have been accounted for upon an accrual basis rather than upon the basis of actual receipts the Commissioner taxpayer's entitlement to fees from the centres as being $91:997.21. On this basis the entitlement of D Limited under the agreement was $89,029.56. However, the Commissioner disallowed any deduction in respect of fees paid to D Limited and assessed the taxpayer upon a taxable income of $88,600,00 to income tax, Division 7 tax and penalties. In the five subsequent years the Commissioner assessed the taxpayer to income tax, Division 7 tax and penalties upon taxable incomes in each of those years of $125,541, $154,517, $165,199, $180,810 and $127,710 respectively. In so doing the Commissioner disallowed any deduction in respect of franchise fees paid or payable to D Limited.
  2. The issue to be determined is whether the taxpayer is entitled to deduct from its income in each of the years in question the said franchise fees. A question was raised as to whether, if a deduction was allowable, it should be limited to the fees actually paid, but it is unnecessary, in view of our decision, to deal with this question.

  1. For the Commissioner it was submitted that the arrangement between the taxpayer and D Limited was a sham and that there was no liability on the part of taxpayer to pay royalties. To D Limited.
  2. The meaning of the term "sham" was dealt with by Windeyer, J. in Scott v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (No. 2) (1966) 40 ALJR 265 at p.279 where His Honour said:

"The difficult and debatable philosophic questions of the meaning and relationship of reality, substance and form are for the purposes of our law generally resolved by asking did the parties who entered into the ostensible transaction mean it to be in truth their transaction, or did they mean it to be, and in fact use it as, merely a disguise, a facade, a sham, a false front - all these words have been metaphorically used concealing their real transaction"

In Snook v London and West Riding Investments Limited (1967) 2 QB 768 at p.802, Diplock L.J. said:

" for acts or documents to be a "sham", with whatever legal consequences follow from this, all the parties thereto must have a common intention that the acts or documents are not to create the legal rights and obligations which they give the appearance of creating." See also Coppleson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1981) 81 ATC 4019 at pp. 4021-4022; Clyne v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (19B3) 83 ATC 4508 at pp.4515-4156; Sharrment Pty. Ltd. v Federal Trustee in Bankruptcy [1988] FCA 179; (1988) 82 ALR 530 at pp. 536-537.

Limited and then, also by way of legal obligation, to the Anstalt by way of the Danish Company. There was therefore nothing to hide or disguise, and nothing to be gained, by setting up an arrangement which was a sham.

(b) Except for the two letters extending the period of the agreement, and two other letters relating to certificates under clause 8 (3) of the agreement, there was no correspondence between the parties relating to the agreement or the failure on the part of the taxpayer to perform the agreement according to its terms.

17. On the whole of the evidence therefore we are of the opinion that the said agreement of 9 December 1975 was a sham. At the very least, we are not able to conclude, on the balance of probabilities, that the said agreement expressed the real and genuine intention of the parties. It was instead a cloak which was intended to facilitate the transfer of money in the form of franchise fees from this country to the United Kingdom and ultimately to Liechtenstin. The purported deduction of those fees from the income of the taxpayer was, we think, ineffective.

18. It was further argued on behalf of the Commissioner that the franchise fees paid or payable to D Limited were not deductible under section 51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. That sub-section provides:

"(1) All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent that they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income."

  1. The franchise fees were said not to be deductible because they were really incurred for the sole or dominant purpose of obtaining, a tax deduction and, within the meaning of section 51(1), were either not incurred at all or were not incurred in gaining or producing assessable income. The purpose of obtaining a tax deduction was said not to be one which qualified the payments for deductibility under section 51. The overall arrangement was described as being of an artifical and tax avoidance kind. For the taxpayer it was contended that the royalties paid to D Limited were incurred in the course of the sole business being carrying on by the taxpayer. That business was to sub-license the subject matter of the licence. But for the licence there would be no sub-license, no business and. no income received. The two were inextricably connected. The obligation to pay the royalties had to be incurred before the obligations of the Centres under the sub-licences could be created and they had to be created before the income was received.
  2. We are of the opinion that the outgoings in question are not deductible under section 51(1) of the Act. We do not think that they should be regarded as the cost of a step taken in the process of gaining or reducing the taxpayer's assessable income; cf John v Commissioner of Taxation (High Court of Australia: 8 February 1989). They were the subject of a process, the purpose and end of which was the transfer of franchise fees from Australia without paying any tax on them. In the circumstances they should not he regarded as a business expense, or as an expense otherwise incurred in gaining or producing the franchise fees received by, the taxpayer from the Centres.
  3. On behalf of the Commissioner reliance was also placed in the alternative upon Section. 260 of the income Tax Assessment Act and it was argued that the transaction was void against him by virtue of the operation of that section. Section 260 was in operation during the relevant period. As from 27 May 19B1 it has been replaced by Part IV A.

22. Section 260 provided:

"(1) Every contract, agreement or arrangement made or entered into, orally or in writing, whether before or after the commencement of this Act, shall so far as it has or purports to have the purpose or effect of in any way directly or indirectley-

(a) altering the incidence of any income tax;

(b) relieving any person from liability to pay any income tax or make any return;

(c) defeating, evading or avoiding any duty or liability imposed on any person by this Act; or

(d) preventing the operation of this Act in any respect, be absolutely void, as against the Commissioner, or in regard to any proceeding under this Act, hut without prejudice to such validity as it may have in any other respect or for any other purpose.

(2) This section does not apply to any contract, agreement or arrangement made or entered into after 27 May 1981."

In support of the Commissioner's contention it was argued that a dominant or significant purpose of the parties in entering into the agreement for the payment of the royalties was the avoidance of tax that would otherwise have been payable by the taxpayer. It followed that the agreement and the payments thereunder were void against the Commissioner and the taxpayer was liable to be assessed without regard to those payments. For the taxpayer it was argued that the deduction being allowable under section 51(1), could not be denied by reference to section 260.

  1. Since we have reached the conclusion that the franchise payments in question are not deductible under section 51 of the Act it is unnecessary to decide whether section 260(1) applies.
  2. Each of the appeals therefore fails and the decisions of the Commissioner in each of those appeals are affirmed.
