Bhp Australia Coal Limited and Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1993] AATA 156; (1993) 93 ATC 2071 (1993) 26 ATR 1041 (24 May 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

BHP AUSTRALIA COAL LIMITED v. FEDERAL COMMISSIONER OF

TAXATION

Nos. QT92/58 - QT92/60

AAT No. 8737

Number of pages - 7

Fringe Benefits Tax

(1993) 93 ATC 2071

(1993) 26 ATR 1041

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

P Gerber(1) (Deputy President), D.W. Muller(1) (Senior Member) and J.D. Horrigan(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Fringe Benefits Tax - Calculation of taxable values of remote area housing fringe benefits - determination of the market value of recipients current housing right - Fringe Benefits Tax Assessment Act 1986, secs 25, 26, 29, 136.

HEARING

BRISBANE, 29 January 1992

24:5:1993

Counsel for the Applicant: Mrs E.M. O'Reilly

Solicitor for the Applicant: Feez Ruthning

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr P McMurdo

Solicitor for the Respondent: AGS

ORDER

The decisions under review are set aside and the matter is remitted to the respondent for re-assessment in accordance with the Tribunal's findings.

DECISION

P. GERBER, D.W. MULLER AND J.D. HORRIGAN The applicant, seeks a review of the amended assessments of Fringe Benefits Tax (FBT) in respect of the years ending 31 March 1989; 31 March 1990; and 31 March 1991.

2. A hearing of these applications was held in Brisbane on 27, 28 and 29 January 1993, the applicant being represented by Ms O'Reilly of Counsel and the respondent by Mr McMurdo of Counsel. The applicant agreed to full publication of the reasons for decision.

3. The applicant, BHP Australia Coal Limited (previously BHP Utah Coal Limited), is a company incorporated in the State of Queensland.

4. The applicant manages the operations of two joint ventures, namely, Central Queensland Coal Associates and Gregory Joint Venture. The operations included in the joint ventures are:

(a) Central Queensland Coal Associates

Blackwater Mine

Goonyella Mine

Saraji Mine

Norwich Park Mine

Dysart Township

Hay Point Port

Blackwater Township

Gladstone Port and Shipping Office

Peak Downs Mine

Harrow Creek Trial Colliery

Moranbah Township

Mackay Shipping Office

Queensland Coal Development Field Personnel

(b) Gregory Joint Venture

Gregory Mine

Capella Township

Emerald Township

5. In the rule of manager, the applicant lodged a fringe benefits tax return which covers both operations.

6. The applicant provides its employees in may instances with housing accommodation. In this reference we are concerned with the accommodation provided in the towns of Blackwater, Emerald, Capella, Dysart and Moranbah. The type of accommodation provided by the applicant varies, depending on the level of employment and the status of the employee. In general, the accommodation provided is categorised in the following manner:

Mine Manager's residence

Executive Staff dwelling

4/5 Bedroom house - low/high set

3 Bedroom house - low/high set

2 Bedroom unit

Single quarters - good condition

Single quarters - old with share facilities

7. Section 25 of the Fringe Benefits Tax Assessment Act 1986 (the Act) provides that the subsistence of a housing right granted by a person to another person constitutes a "benefit" provided by, in this case, the employer to the employee in respect of the year of tax.

8. The tax is imposed on the "fringe benefits taxable amount" (defined in s.136(1)) which means (relevantly) the sum of the taxable values of all the fringe benefits in relation to the employer in relation to the current year of tax.

9. The taxable values of housing fringe benefits (a fringe benefit that is a housing benefit - s.136(1)) are calculated by reference to formulae contained in s.26 for non-remote housing fringe benefits or in s.29 for remote area housing fringe benefits. The housing fringe benefit in the instant references is provided to houses located in remote areas of Central Queensland which fall within the scope of s.29. Section 29 allows the taxpayer various options for the method of calculation of the taxable values. The option taken by the applicant in this case used a formula which required the determination of "the market value of the recipients current housing right": cf s.26. The Act provides no definition of "market value".

10. On 1 October 1986, a representative of the applicant met with a representative of the respondent and a representative of the Australian Valuation Office (AVO) to discuss an appropriate method of determining the market rental values. Basically, the starting point for most calculations is the market value of the accommodation in the base year. The question for determination is what is "the market value of the recipients current housing right" in respect of each relevant employee of the applicant in the period ended 31 March 1987.

Mr Comben is a registered valuer from Mackay. Mr Comben determined that Mackay was the best base for assessing the market rental for each of the towns stated in paragraph 6 above. 14. The assessments for the years ended 31 March 1989, 1990 and 1991 were amended using Mr Taylor's valuation. The respondent raised and issued Notices of Amended Assessment which increased the applicant's liability for FBT in respect of the periods under review.

15. On 24 December 1991, the applicant lodged with the Commissioner of Taxation objections against the increased liability for FBT and the additional tax imposed for the years 1989, 1990, 1991.

16. On 26 May 1992 the respondent advised the applicant of its decision to disallow the objections in full. The applicant seeks a review of those decisions.

17. At a Preliminary Conference, the Tribunal suggested that the parties agree on the appointment of an independent valuer acceptable to both in the hope that the matter could be resolved satisfactorily without the need of a protracted hearing, involving evidence of competing valuations. In the result, the parties agreed on the appointment of a Mr PJO Turner, a registered valuer based in Emerald. However, the dispute proved to be too intractable to be resolved between the parties, compelling the Tribunal to determine the issue on the evidence.

18. The Tribunal heard evidence from the following witnesses:

Frank Edward Comben - the Mackay valuer mentioned above

Graham Taylor - the AVO valuer mentioned above

Paul James Oliver Turner - a registered valuer mentioned above.

Peter Geoffrey Byrne - a registered valuer residing at

Mooloolaba

Rodney Allen McIntosh - the Taxation Manager of BHP

Trevor Donovan - now retired, had been Town

Development

Manager BHP and Emerald Shire Clerk

19. The Tribunal was referred to Taxation Ruling No. MT 2505 and in particular to paragraph 41 which reads as follows:

"Mining Towns

41. The general approach of ascertaining market rental value by

reference to rents paid for comparable properties in the general

locality should also be adopted where practicable in relation to

housing provided by employers in mining towns, including towns the

general residential infrastructure of which has been provided by a

mining company. While mine employees are often required to pay rent

for their accommodation, those rents are usually concessional and do

not reflect commercial rents. Rather, market rental value would be

reflected in what other town residents who are willing but not

anxious to rent would be prepared to pay to occupy premises

comparable to the accommodation provided to mining company employees.

It will not often be the case that adequate evidence of a commercial

rental market of that kind cannot be found in a mining town.

However, if town housing is occupied exclusively by mine employees,

or the commercial rental market is of such a scale or type that the

accommodation occupied by mining employees is not comparable,

commercial rents charged for comparable accommodation in the nearest

other town of comparable size would be a measure of the market rental

value of mining employees' housing. Where the alternative statutory

amount explained in paragraph 10 is adopted by a mining company,

there is no requirement to ascertain the market rental value of mine

employees' accommodation that is located in a designated remote

locality."

20. The evidence of Messrs Donovan and McIntosh was to the following effect. No other mining company had residential housing or employees residing in the towns of Emerald, Capella and Dysart during the relevant period. It was company policy that it would always have sufficient housing for those workers who required it. The total number of houses available for renting would be equal to the total number of employees, plus some more for maintenance and to allow for employees marrying and requiring more spacious accommodation. In 1986 about 420 homes in Blackwater were occupied by Company employees. About 270 of those were leased by the Company from the Queensland Housing Commission, and about 150 of them were owned by the Company. Around mid-1986, the total number of Company homes in Moranbah was 824, and the number of homes leased from the Queensland Housing Commission was 247. In Dysart it was 730 and 167 homes respectively. From time to time the Company leased a few surplus homes to non-company people on a short-term basis. In 1986 the employees paid a weekly rental of between $8 and $15. Non-company occupants paid about $70 per week.

21. Mr Comben informed the Tribunal that he chose the rental market in Mackay as his base because the figures were to be used for a ten year period and that therefore he wanted a stable rental market with a reasonable supply and demand situation. He stated that Mackay had a stable rental market and that a large number of houses in Mackay were very similar to the three bedroom houses erected in the mining towns. He was very familiar with the rental market in Mackay because his real estate firm had a substantial property management division in that area. He stated that he believed that Emerald evidenced a distorted rental market - some 70 people on the waiting list for every dwelling which became vacant - so that, because of the large local demand for dwellings, that city could not be used as a base. He stated:

"I started with Moranbah because having always been unhappy

with adopting Emerald for the reasons explained, it was obvious that

first of all Moranbah is the biggest town; it's got the most number

of mining dwellings; it's a pure mining town and that's what we are

- what the exercise was all about; it was assessing the rental value

of mining towns, not the rental value of dwellings in the private

sector in towns adjoining mining towns. Now, Moranbah miners and

people there are closely associated with Mackay; they invest; they

shop and visit regularly Mackay. It is closely associated with

Mackay. So, Mackay was obviously the closest town to the

trend-setter, which would have to be Moranbah, because once you assessed
a

rent for mining houses in Moranbah, of necessity those rents would

have to be relative to the other mining towns in the Bowen basin

because the mining companies by forward planning and producing

sufficient houses to meet the demand - and constructing similar

quality houses in all the towns - are not going to slip up by having

different rentals in different towns. You couldn't have a situation

where a miner in Emerald working for BHP was paying substantially

more rent than a miner in Moranbah."

The method by which he arrived at the figures set out in paragraph 11 above is set out in paragraphs 16 and 17 of his affidavit.

"16. I knew from my experience as a valuer of properties in

Mackay that a 3-4 bedroom brick veneer home in Mackay in the newer

and more popular suburbs was renting for approximately $120-$130 per

week. These homes were located in popular residential suburbs and of

similar quality to the executive type dwellings in Moranbah. I

regarded them as having a superior location and superior amenities to

the executive type dwellings in Moranbah. I made appropriate

allowances and adjusted the $120-130 per week rental to $105 per week

for similar type homes in Moranbah. I then added $10 per week on to

this rental figure to determine the value for the mine manager's

residence.

17. In order to ascertain the value for rent for the high and low set

three, four and five bedroom dwellings, I knew from my experience as

a valuer of homes in Mackay that rentals for high and low set fibro

three-four bedroom dwellings was approximately $90 per week. I made

appropriate allowances and adjusted this to $65 per week and $68 per

week for the high and low set three bedroom dwellings and high and

low set four and five bedroom dwellings respectively."

Mr Comben went through a similar exercise in arriving at the rental

figures for the other towns. He did not use Rockhampton as a base

because he thought that it was too remote from Moranbah which he was

using as the leading mining town so far as miners' dwellings were

concerned.

22. Mr Taylor gave evidence on behalf of the respondent, having carried out a rental survey of the cental Queensland area in the towns of Moura, Biloela, Blackwater, Emerald, Capella, Dysart, Moranbah and Clermont together with the coastal cities of Rockhampton, Mackay and Gladstone. The witness concluded that a viable rental market existed in the towns of Emerald, Clermont and Biloela, all being small inland towns adjacent to mining activity and providing some accommodation for the mining companies. This rental evidence was in his view, the best available evidence to apply in those towns as well as in the mining towns of Moura, Blackwater, Capella, Dysart and Moranbah. He found that there was very little evidence of the private rental market in the predominantly mining towns as the majority of the rental accommodation was provided by the mining companies. He also concluded that the rental evidence in the larger coastal towns provided no assistance in determining rental levels in the subject inland mining towns. He thought that they were too distant and the differences in life-style were too great.

23. Based on this survey, Mr Taylor applied the rental levels thus arrived at directly to the various categories of houses provided by BHP in the subject mining towns. His valuations are set out in paragraph 13 above. As stated above these were used by the respondent as the basis for the amended assessments.

24. Mr Byrne was called as a witness to comment on the methodology used by Mr Comben and Mr Taylor. In his affidavit he stated:

"If I had received similar instructions to Mr Frank Comben to

prepare rental valuations for fringe benefit tax purposes in various

mining towns in the Bowen basin in accordance with MT2025, I would

have used similar methodology (i.e. I would have sought a basis in

the coastal towns where a normal supply and demand situation existed

in the house rental market).

The rental market in Emerald at the time Comben prepared his

valuations was "tight" or "lively" due to mining activities in the

region with privately owned rental accommodation in very short

supply. To obtain a basis for rental housing in Emerald I would have

looked to Rockhampton the nearest town where there was a viable

rental market and normal conditions of supply and demand prevailed.

I would have used the same basis for the valuation of mining house

rentals in the town of Blackwater.

Additionally I would have used Mackay as a basis for ascertaining

mining house rentals in the towns of Moranbah, Dysart and Capella.

I would not have used rentals in Emerald as a basis for my valuation

and adjusted them because it had an over-heated market. In my

opinion the private sector in Emerald could not be used in 1986 to

assess mining sector housing because a normal supply and demand

situation did not exist in Emerald at that time.

I refer to Exhibit "B" to the affidavit of Mr Louis Szegedi filed

herein. In my opinion there is no basis for comparing between the

towns of Kingaroy, and the towns under review during the period from

1 July 1986 to 31 March 1987, because they were opposite markets. In

Emerald, rental housing was in tight demand. Whereas in Kingaroy,

(and the nearby town of Nanango) the markets had commenced an acute

phase of over-supply upon completion of construction of the Tarong

Power Station and completion of the Nanango mine, which depressed

market continued for several years.

My chief criticism of Mr Taylor's valuation is that he has used as

his basis for rentals in the towns under review, the rentals in

Emerald, Biloela, and Clermont. Emerald is a mixed-based town with a

mining influence. I have not analysed Clermont and Biloela. I

express my opinion on the assumption that they are mixed-based towns

with mining influence and similar in this regard to Emerald. I have

also proceeded on the assumption that the same supply and demand

function exists in all three towns of Emerald, Clermont and Biloela.

In my opinion, it is not valid to use the rentals prevailing in such

mixed-based mining towns, where a tight rental market exists, for

determining the rent payable for rental accommodation under

conditions of normal supply and demand."

25. Mr Turner, the valuer chosen by the parties as a result of the Tribunal's suggestion that they seek the views of an independent expert, has been in practice as a partner in the firm of Turner Valuers at Emerald since 1982. He has had extensive valuation experience in leasehold and freehold land throughout Central and Western Queensland. Mr Turner prepared an affidavit for the Tribunal and he presented himself for cross-examination by the representatives of both parties at the hearing. In his affidavit he described the method by which he arrived at his valuations as follows:

"4. In December 1985 and January 1986 I conducted a rental survey

on behalf of the B.H.P. Utah Group to assess the private rental

market in the towns of Emerald, Blackwater, Dysart, Moranbah,

Clermont and Capella of wages type housing, 5 bedroom both high set

and low set, 4 bedroom both lowset and high set and 3 bedroom housing

both high set and low set comparable to that type of housing provided

by Utah Development Co; to obtain an overview of the rental market

for similar type housing in the cities of Mackay and Rockhampton. I

was also to assess the probable effect on the rental market in the

towns of Emerald, Blackwater, Dysart and Moranbah should Utah

Development Co. introduce an open rental market with the employee

type housing assuming other mining operations continue.

.....

6. The method of conducting such survey was left entirely to me. The

BHP Utah Group furnished me with lists of houses representing the

various types of wages style housing they provided for their

employees in the various towns concerned and I selected randomly

samples of these to inspect.

7. I contacted the various Shire Councils concerned and obtained in

respect of the various towns concerned, the population, number of

dwellings in the town, amount of vacant land. I contacted banks in

each of these towns and real estate agents in Mackay and Rockhampton

who had extensive rental lists.

8. My purpose in checking rentals for corresponding housing in Mackay

and Rockhampton was to check the strength of the rental market and to

gain a regional perspective. If the inland towns had higher rentals

than the coastal towns, the rental market in the inland towns would

be strong. Conversely, if the inland towns had lower rentals than the

coastal towns, the rental market in the inland towns would be weak.

......

10. I consider that the rental market in Emerald during the period 1

July 1986 to 31 March 1987 was inflated because of temporary

increased demand caused by the electrification of the railway to

Emerald and the construction of the TAFE College at Emerald. This

did not affect the newer type of housing under say seven years old.

11. The quality and presentation of the houses owned by the BHP Utah

Group was generally higher than that of privately owned rental

houses.

12. At the time of my said rental market survey the rents current in

Emerald were from $10 to $20 per week higher than those which

prevailed in Mackay for similar type housing.

13. In the towns covered by my said rental survey and at the time of

such survey, if a three bedroom house commanded a rent of $120 per

week, then a four bedroom house would command a rent of $130 to $140

per week.

14. My said rental survey report sets out what I considered to be the

current private rental market for 5, 4, and 3 bedroom high set and

low set housing in those towns similar to the housing provided at

those locations by B.H.P. Utah Group to its employees. In fixing

those market rentals I had regard to the private market rentals in

the said Central Queensland inland towns concerned solely. In the

towns of Moranbah, Dysart and Blackwater where there was a paucity of

private rental housing, I disregarded private housing rentals which

were high by comparison with those which prevailed throughout the

region. My said rental survey did not cover senior staff or staff

type housing which had a higher standard of quality.

15. I have perused the Tribunal Document no. 23 in these referrals,

Valuation report produced by Mr GA Taylor (AVO) dated 14 November

1988. I agree with the conclusion drawn at the top of page 6 of that

report that at the time to which the report refers 1 July 1986 to 31

March 1987 a viable rental market existed in the towns of Emerald,

Clermont and Biloela and that as those were smallish inland towns

adjacent to mining activity and providing some accommodation for

mining companies and their staff, the rental evidence available in those

towns was the best evidence of rental values to apply in those

towns and in the other predominantly mining towns of Moura, Capella and

Moranbah save that I would substitute the word "strongest" for

the word "best" underlined above.

16. There would have been little if any change in the amount of

rentals commanded in the private market in the towns of Emerald,

Blackwater, Dysart, Capella, Moura, Biloela and Moranbah between

December 1985 to January 1986 the time of my said rental market

survey and the period 1 July 1986 to 31 March 1987, the time to which

the said valuation report of GA Taylor relates."

26. Mr Turner told the Tribunal that, in his opinion, Mr Comben's methodology was not entirely satisfactory. He felt that it was reasonable to start with Mackay as a base, but that the values of the rental markets in other towns in the region should also have been taken into account to provide a regional overview in establishing a base. He could not see any evidence to justify the discount method used by Mr Comben. He also thought that it was not accurate to apply the rental rates set for Moranbah across the board to the other mining towns. It was necessary, he stated, to treat each town individually to "iron out the bumps". Mr Turner agreed in general with the approach taken by Mr Taylor but he disagreed with some of the actual rental values arrived at by Mr Taylor. For example he said:

Q. And what do you understand his methodology to have been?

A. Well, I understand that Mr Taylor looked at the rental markets in

the similar sized towns surrounding where the Utah mine towns are and

obtained the information of what the private rental market was paying

for a type of house similar to the BHP-Utah houses.

That gave Mr Taylor his rental levels for particularly the 4 to 5

bedroom staff houses, and the 3 bedroom staff houses. I feel that the

mine manager dwelling and the executive staff dwellings as Mr Taylor

has got there, I believe that the market wouldn't have paid that type

of value for those houses.

Q. They would not have paid that type of value?

A. It would not have paid that type of value for those houses. In

the absence of seeing evidence of value that that was being paid, I

can't think of any house in Emerald that was getting $240 per week

and at that time, it would have been extremely difficult to get $180

a week for an executive staff dwelling, given that I believe that the

mine manager's dwelling would have rented for about 175.

28. The Tribunal prefers the evidence of Mr Turner as to the weekly market rental values. He has lived and worked in the area for many years, and more particularly, he has been involved in the sale, rental and valuation of properties in the region for over ten years. The Tribunal is satisfied that Mr Turner has a greater familiarity with the subject matter than either Mr Comben or Mr Taylor. Consequently, the Tribunal finds that the appropriate market rental values for the purposes of calculating FBT for the years in dispute are those set out in paragraph 27 above, excepting those categories for which Mr Turner did not specify any figure, or did not provide an exact figure.

29. It therefore remains for the Tribunal to assess the value of the Managers' houses in Blackwater, Dysart and Moranbah, the 2 bedroom units and single quarters. Doing the best we can with the evidence of relative values of the other homes, and taking into account the tenor of Mr Tuner's evidence, the Tribunal determines that the appropriate market rental value of the Managers' houses in Blackwater, Dysart and Moranbah is $90 per week. Using a similar method, the Tribunal determines that the appropriate market rental values for the 2 bedroom units is $65 per week. We have not heard any evidence which persuades us that the weekly rental figures for single quarters as prepared by Mr Taylor are not appropriate.

30. The decisions under review are set aside and the matter is remitted to the respondent for re-assessment in accordance with the above findings.
