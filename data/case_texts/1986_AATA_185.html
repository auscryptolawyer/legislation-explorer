Re AB and Australian Taxation Office [1986] AATA 185 (7 July 1986)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: A.B.

And: AUSTRALIAN TAXATION OFFICE

No. A86/10

AAT No. 2766

Freedom of Information

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

A.N. Hall (Deputy President)

J.D. Horrigan (Member)

N.J. Attwood (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information - request for access to documents containing information upon which taxation investigation based - refusal of access - application for review on ground of denial of natural justice - whether documents exempt under s.37(1)(a) - disclosure of documents at early stage of investigation reasonably expected to prejudice conduct of investigation - ground of exemption made out - Tribunal has no discretion on grounds of natural justice or otherwise to direct disclosure of exempt documents - alternative claim of exemption under s.37(2)(b) not considered

Freedom of Information Act 1982, ss.37(1)(a), 37(2)(b)

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss.161, 264

Attorney-General's Department v Cockcroft (1986) 64 ALR 97

News Corporation Ltd. v National Companies and Securities Commission [1984] FCA 400; (1984) 57 ALR 550

National Companies and Securities Commission v News Corporation Ltd [1984] HCA 29; (1984) 52 ALR 417

Re Waterford and Department of Treasury (1983) 5 ALD 193

HEARING

CANBERRA

7:7:1986

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

This is a review of a decision made by the respondent agency refusing a request by the applicant under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the FOI Act") for access to certain documents of the agency. At the conclusion of the hearing the Tribunal gave a decision affirming the agency's refusal of access and stated orally its reasons for decision. At the request of the parties, those reasons are now reduced to writing. Because the publication of the applicant's name would disclose confidential information with respect to his taxation affairs, the Tribunal orders, pursuant to s.35 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act"), that the publication of the applicant's name be prohibited.

2. The sequence of events leading to the proceedings before the Tribunal started with a letter dated 13 September 1985 from the Commissioner's Office to the applicant which (omitting formal parts) was as follows:-

"From information held by this office it would

appear that income derived from rents has not been

included in your returns of income. Accordingly

you are requested to examine your records and

furnish to this office, within twenty-one (21) days

from the date of this letter, a statement setting

out your total income from rents received during

each of the years ended 30 June 1982 to 1984

inclusive.

Should your examination reveal any omission of

income from your returns you are invited to furnish

an explanation setting out the reasons for any such

omissions."

3. The applicant responded by writing to the Commissioner on 19 September 1985 making a request under the FOI Act for access to the documentation that gave rise to the statement "From information held by this office". The applicant did not reply, at that stage, to the substance of the Commissioner's request for information. Instead he stated that it was his belief that the making of the request under the FOI Act suspended the time within which he was required to reply. The request for access was refused and that decision was affirmed on internal review.

4. In addition to the documents lodged under s.37 of the AAT Act. The Tribunal had before it an affidavit by Mr. J.M. Killaly, who is an Assistant Commissioner in the Enforcement Division of the Compliance Directorate in the Head Office of the Australian Taxation Office. In his affidavit Mr. Killaly has provided the factual background to the matter and in paragraph 7, the substance of which the Tribunal has directed should remain confidential, Mr. Killaly outlined in greater detail the precise respects in which it was claimed that there would be a disclosure of exempt information under the Act if the documents in question were released to the applicant. Having heard the whole of the evidence and argument in the case, the Tribunal is satisfied that that order for confidentiality should be maintained as the disclosure of paragraph 7 would, in the light of the Tribunal's ultimate decision in this matter, disclose matter that is claimed to be exempt.

5. In a Schedule to his affidavit, Mr. Killaly identified the three documents to which the request for access related as:-

(i) An internal request for details/copies of

source documents;

(ii) A document dated 31 March 1984 containing

information supplied as a result of third

party enquiry; and

(iii) A copy of a computer printout.

In a footnote to the Schedule, Mr. Killaly recorded that "other details with regard to those documents have not been provided as to do so would disclose the matters claimed to be exempt". However, a copy of the documents was provided to the Tribunal for its information alone.

6. The agency has claimed exemption for these documents pursuant to s.37(1)(a) and s.37(2)(b) of the FOI Act which provide as follows:-

"37.(1) A document is an exempt document if

its disclosure under this Act would, or could

reasonably be expected to -

(a) prejudice the conduct of an

investigation of a breach, or possible

breach, of the law, or a failure, or

possible failure, to comply with a law

relating to taxation or prejudice the

enforcement or proper administration

of the law in a particular instance;

.....

(2) A document is an exempt document if its

disclosure under this Act, would, or could

reasonably be expected to -

(a) .....

(b) disclose lawful methods or procedures

for preventing, detecting,

investigating, or dealing with matters

arising out of, breaches or evasions

of the law the disclosure of which,

would, or would be reasonably likely

to, prejudice the effectiveness of

those methods or procedures;".

The documents in question also contained information with respect to the affairs of other persons. Originally, therefore, there was also a claim of exemption under ss.38 and 41 of the FOI Act. However, the applicant made it clear that he is not seeking access to any information with respect to the affairs of any person other than himself and that to the extent that any of the documents in question would disclose such information he does not press his request.

7. The applicant, who appeared on his own behalf, presented his case very clearly and forcefully. It is his contention that it would involve a breach of natural justice if the documents in question were not disclosed to him, given that the Commissioner has indicated that he proposes to conduct some enquiries into the applicant's affairs. Miss S. Wade, who appeared for the respondent, submitted that arguments based on natural justice were irrelevant to the issues arising under the FOI Act and that the only question before the Tribunal was whether the claims of exemption were justified.

8. The ground of exemption principally relied upon by the respondent was s.37(1)(a) to which we earlier referred. The Tribunal takes it as settled that the expression "would or could reasonably be expected to" should be construed in accordance with its ordinary meaning (see the decision of the Full Court of the Federal Court in Attorney-General's Department v Cockcroft (1986) 64 ALR 97). It is not necessary to establish a probability of prejudice. What is required is that a judgment must be made as to whether the expectation of prejudice is "reasonable, as distinct from something that is irrational, absurd or ridiculous" (see per Bowen CJ. and Beaumont J. at 106).

9. In support of the claims of exemption, the substance of Mr. Killaly's affidavit evidence (excluding formal parts and paragraph 7) was as follows:-

"4. I say that following the receipt of

information received in the form of document

2, and as a result of that information being

computer-matched with details shown in the

Applicant's income tax returns, it appears to

me that the Applicant failed to disclose

income derived from rents. As a result an

investigation has been commenced and is

continuing in order to ascertain whether the

Applicant has failed to comply with provisions

of the Income Tax Assessment Act, particularly

whether he has failed to comply with section

161 of that Act in respect of income derived

by him in respect of all sources in certain

tax years, especially the years ended 30 June

1982 to 1984. An integral part of that

investigation is the examination of the

Applicant pursuant to section 264 of the

Income Tax Assessment Act, and such

examination is relied upon by the Respondent

to discover matters relating to the financial

affairs of the Applicant which may not be

known to the Respondent prior to the

examination. If the Applicant were granted

access to relevant documents for which

exemption is claimed in this matter, I say

this would enable him to discover the source

of the information referred hereinto above and

the precise nature and extent of the

information presently known to the Respondent.

5. In particular, the disclosure of the documents

sought by the Applicant would frustrate the

powers of the Respondent to make inquiries by

depriving him of the opportunity to exploit

the prospect that the Applicant's answers to

the Respondent's questions will better serve

the attainment of the objects of the

investigation if the answers are given before

disclosure of the documents to him.

6. One of the objects of the investigative

process is to discover matters pertaining to

the Applicant which are not known to the

Respondent. The effectiveness of a section

264 examination as a means for discovering new

information would be effectively destroyed if

the Applicant has access to the documents he

seeks, as the Applicant would be able to avoid

the disclosure of any information which the

Respondent did not already know.

...

8. The release to the Applicant of the documents

sought by him before the conclusion of the

investigation will prejudice the conduct of

the investigation in that it will indicate to

the Applicant

(a) the activities of the Applicant which

are the subject of the investigation;

(b) the activities which are not, at

present, under investigation;

(c) the nature, extent and detail of the

evidence which has been accumulated to

date in relation to the investigation;

(d) the resources available to the

investigators;

(e) the methods of investigation being

used by the Respondent;

(f) the direction of the investigation.

9. The disclosure to the Applicant of the

documents in question could be expected

to disclose the extent to which the

Respondent is basing his enquiries on

proven and substantiated facts on the one

hand, and inferences capable of being

drawn prima facie upon the material

already within the possession of the ATO

on the other hand."

10. In the present case the relevant law relating to taxation with which the applicant is said to have failed to comply is s.161 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITA Act") by virtue of which every person is required to lodge a return, setting forth a full and complete statement of the total income derived by that person during the relevant year of income and of any deductions claimed by him. Furthermore, the Commissioner is empowered under s.264, by notice in writing, to require any person, whether a taxpayer or not, to furnish the Commissioner with such information as he may require and to attend to give evidence and produce books etc..

11. The affidavit evidence of Mr. Killaly satisfies us that, so far as the Commissioner's Office is concerned, there is a question as to a possible failure by the applicant to comply with a law with respect to taxation. The letter of 13 September last was, as it were, the first step in the conduct of an investigation in relation to that possible failure. The question therefore, in the present case, is whether it is reasonable, as distinct from something that is irrational, absurd or ridiculous, to expect that, if the Commissioner is required to disclose, at this stage, the documents for which exemption is claimed, there will be prejudice to the conduct of the proposed investigation.

12. In our view the expectation of prejudice in the conduct of the proposed investigation is reasonable given that it would require the Commissioner to disclose in advance of the conduct of that investigation the precise details of the information available to him with respect to the alleged breach of the Act. For the reasons given by the Full Court of the Federal Court in News Corporation Ltd v National Companies and Securities Commission [1984] FCA 400; (1984) 57 ALR 550, we are satisfied that premature disclosure of these documents would prejudice the conduct of the Commissioner's investigation as it would forewarn the applicant of the direction of the investigation and of the evidence available to the Commissioner and would deprive the Commissioner of the opportunity to put questions to the applicant without fully forewarning him of what those questions might be.

13. Here we have a case of an investigation that has barely begun. It is, in our view, a very clear case where, for the Commissioner to have to disclose his hand prematurely, it would involve alerting the applicant precisely to the nature of the information that has triggered the investigation. There may well come a time when the applicant will need to be confronted with the information presently in the Commissioner's possession. However, we are satisfied that to disclose that information at such an early stage could hardly fail to cause prejudice to the conduct of that investigation (see the High Court decision in National Companies and Securities Commission v News Corporation Ltd reported at [1984] HCA 29; 52 ALR 417 and particularly per Mason, Wilson and Dawson JJ. at pp.437-8).

14. The question whether or not it is a breach of natural justice for the Commissioner to be able to initiate an investigation in such a way is, we would have thought, irrelevant for our present purposes. If there is any case to be made in respect of alleged breach of natural justice, it appears to us that that case has to be made outside the confines of the FOI Act (cf. s.6(1)(a) of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977). In the present case, the only task of this Tribunal is to decide, having regard to the language of the FOI Act, whether the claim of exemption is made out. If so satisfied, the Tribunal has no discretion on grounds of natural justice or otherwise to direct that exempt documents be released (cf. Re Waterford and Department of Treasury (1983) 5 ALD 193 at 203-4).

15. Thus, the Tribunal, being satisfied that all the elements of the claim of exemption under s.37(1)(a) have been made out, proposes to uphold the Commissioner's decision not to release the subject documents under the FOI Act. That conclusion, we think, relieves us, in the circumstances of this case, of the obligation of ruling upon the second ground of exemption under s.37(2)(b). The fact is that, for the Tribunal to deal satisfactorily in its reasons for decision with the alternative claim of exemption, the Tribunal would risk disclosing the very information which has been found, on other grounds, to be exempt. Having regard to s.63 of the FOI Act, the Tribunal would be required to order that that part of its reasons for decision should not be made available to the applicant. Rather than proceed, therefore, to give reasons for decision in respect of that second ground and to require the applicant to be absent while that is done, it appears to the Tribunal that it would be preferable simply to indicate that it finds it unnecessary in the circumstances of this case to rule upon the s.37(2)(b) ground of exemption and to base its decision solely upon s.37(1)(a).

16. For those reasons the Tribunal decides to affirm the decision under review. The formal decision is published forthwith. Whether the reasons for decision are to be published is a matter that will depend upon whether the parties see fit to exercise their right to ask for those reasons to be reduced to writing.
