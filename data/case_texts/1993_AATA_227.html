Robyne Louise Green and Commissioner of Superannuation [1993] AATA 227 (23 July 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

ROBYNE LOUISE GREEN v. COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. A92/117

AAT No. 8855

Number of pages - 6

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

K L BEDDOE (Senior Member) D B TRAVERS (Member) and H G JULIAN (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - deferred benefit - election outside prescribed period - whether desirable to exercise discretion to extend time.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act1975 : Ss 37, 43

Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Act 1973: S.69

Superannuation Act 1976: Ss 135, 137(1), 157(1)

Re Lander and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision 8553)

Re Liddle and Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 191; (1991) 25 ALD 307

HEARING

CANBERRA, 27 April 1993

23:7:1993

ORDER

The Tribunal decides that the decision under review be affirmed.

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr Nash

Solicitors for the Applicant : Porter Pilkington

Solicitor for the Respondent: Ms F Jolly

DECISION

K L BEDDOE, D B TRAVERS AND H G JULIAN The applicant seeks review of a decision of a delegate of the respondent which affirmed a decision to refuse the applicant's request that she be allowed extended time to make an election under sub-section 137(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act").

2. Division 3 of Part IX of the Act contains provisions providing for preservation of rights of contributors to the Superannuation Fund who cease to be eligible employees. It is not necessary to go into the somewhat complex working of the Division except to say that it provides for a "transfer value" as determined in accordance with section 135 of the Act to be transferred to another retirement scheme which is approved and gazetted by the Minister.

3. The Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Scheme is such an eligible scheme and the applicant seeks to have the transfer value of her benefits in the Superannuation Fund transferred to the Defence Force Scheme.

4. Sub-section 137(1) provides that where a person ceases to be an eligible employee and, upon so ceasing, is not entitled to a pension or invalidity benefits, under the Act, that person may, not later than 21 days after ceasing to be an eligible employee make an election in writing that Division 3 is to apply.

5. Sub-section 157(1) of the Act provides that, notwithstanding anything contained in the Act, where an election under the Act is made out of time for making the election and the Commissioner is satisfied that in all the circumstances of the case it is desirable that the late election should be recognised, then the Commissioner may direct that the election be treated as if it had been made within the period allowed for making the election and the election shall have effect accordingly.

6. There was no issue before us that the respondent Commissioner had made a reviewable decision and that the Tribunal had the jurisdiction to review that decision and make a decision in accordance with section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

7. Mr Nash of counsel appeared for the applicant and the respondent was represented by Ms Jolly.

8. The Tribunal heard oral evidence from the applicant and three other witnesses. The section 37 documents ("T documents") and four exhibits were placed before the Tribunal. Following the hearing the Tribunal received further submissions dated 10 May 1993 from the applicant and 28 May 1993 from the respondent.

9. Before going into the facts of the case there is a matter relevant to the evidence called that requires comment. The applicant caused a summons to issue and be served on Mr R S Arnold, an officer of the Retirements Benefits Office which the Tribunal understands to be administered by the respondent. When Mr Arnold was called he did not answer the summons. The explanation, we understand, is that the respondent's officers, having formed the view that Mr Arnold would not be required to give evidence until some later time, had apparently advised him to attend the Tribunal's hearing room at a later time rather than the summonsed time. As was pointed out at the hearing the respondent's attitude that it could in some way determine when the applicant's witnesses would be heard is not accepted by the Tribunal. Although there is no property in witnesses so that it is open to parties to talk to a witness prior to the hearing, it is clearly the responsibility of the party calling the witness to ensure, so far as is possible, that the witness attends in accordance with the summons at the appropriate time. In particular, respondents should not interfere in the conduct of an applicant's case as occurred in this case, albeit that we are satisfied that the interference was an innocent mistake in this case.

10. The factual background to the applicant's case is not in dispute and can be dealt with concisely. At the time of the hearing the applicant was 41 years of age and a serving officer in the Royal Australian Air Force ("the RAAF") with the rank of Flight Lieutenant. The applicant became an employee of the Commonwealth Public Service in 1973 and subsequently became an "eligible employee" upon the introduction of the Act from 1 July 1976.

11. In 1984 the applicant was employed by Australia Post in an administrative position described to us as a Class 6 position. In July 1984 she enlisted in the RAAF, having applied in March 1984. That was not the first occasion on which the applicant had sought to join the RAAF. On enlistment she was appointed as a Flying Officer for a 6 year term of engagement, the remuneration as a Flying Officer being less than her remuneration as a Class 6 Administrative Officer in Australia Post.

12. The evidence before us establishes that it was always the applicant's intention to seek to establish continuity of service between her employment in the Public Service and her enlistment in the RAAF. She was aware that there might be certain advantages and had made up her mind to accept such continuity wherever possible. Furthermore she was aware of the right to preserve entitlements in the Superannuation Fund (Transcript 17 and 24).

13. In this regard she apparently made an enquiry of the RAAF recruitment officers as to her rights in relation to retirement benefits and had been referred to a Mr Ray Carnall by the recruiting officer giving her one of Mr Carnall's business cards. That card is Exhibit A. It refers to a "Ray Carnall Counselling Officer" with the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Authority.

14. The applicant gave evidence, which we accept, that she rang Carnall to obtain information about transfer of her superannuation entitlements. She said that Carnall advised that she should elect to take a refund of her contributions to the Superannuation Fund. The applicant said that the basis of this advice was that Carnall took the view that female members of the services only stayed for three to five years and did not thereby qualify for benefits under the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Scheme. He advised her to pay off the debt on her car and invest the balance. If on the other hand she paid the money into the Defence Force Scheme she would not earn any income on her money for the 20 years service for qualifying for benefit. Nothing was said about the likely credit for service brought about by the transfer value of her interest in the Superannuation Fund.

15. Because of Carnall's advice the applicant changed her original intention and did not seek to preserve her benefits in the Superannuation Fund for the purpose of transferring those benefits to the Defence Force Scheme. She therefore completed a form S2A (T5/27) in which she deleted "elect for preservation of superannuation rights" and adopted "apply for a refund of accumulated contributions". That form is dated 12 July 1984 - the day after ceasing service with Australia Post. We have no reason to doubt and therefore accept that the applicant completed this form after receiving advice from Mr Carnall that we outlined above. We also have no reason to doubt the applicant's evidence that except for the advice from Mr Carnall she would have sought to transfer her interest in the Superannuation Fund to the Defence Force Scheme.

16. In 1988 the applicant married her present husband who also gave evidence in these proceedings. They first met in 1985 i.e. after the applicant had joined the RAAF. The husband was then a serving member of the RAAF and holds the rank of Squadron Leader. His evidence was brief and he was unable to give evidence about what happened in 1984. We do consider it relevant, however, as to events post 1984.

17. In 1989, while working at RAAF Glenbrook, the applicant says that she became aware, for the first time, of the advantages of a transfer value which in effect purchased years of effective service under the Defence Force Scheme. As a consequence of this the applicant wrote a letter to the Defence Force Retirements and Death Benefits Authority. Document T7/31 is a copy of the Authority's response to that letter. That letter outlined the procedure for purchase of effective service and referred the applicant to the respondent Commissioner for the purpose of making an election to preserve benefits under the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme.

18. Consequent upon the advice in T7/31 the applicant wrote to the respondent on 28 March 1989 (T7/29-30) seeking to make a late election to preserve her benefits in the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme and to pay a transfer value to the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Authority. In the letter she summarised facts set out above and indicated that she saw the RAAF as a long term career and she intended to complete 20 years service. On this basis she said that the advice previously given to her (Mr Carnall's advice) was ill-founded and furthermore would not have been given to her if she were a male.

19. Mr Carnall's evidence established that he was employed by the Retirement Benefits Office from 1974 to 1987. At the relevant time he was the counselling officer in charge of Defence Force Superannuation Counselling. A doubt arose as to the evidence in relation to Mr Carnall's duties resulting in the applicant being given leave to further interrogate Mr Carnall in writing. Mr Carnall's letter of 10 May 1993 establishes that he only gave advice in relation to the Defence Force Scheme and not the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme. The written answers are consistent with the Tribunal's view of Mr Carnall's oral evidence. 20. Mr Carnall had no recollection of a telephone conversation with the applicant in 1984. Mr Carnall's evidence is therefore of little assistance to the Tribunal because he could not recall the specific advice given to the applicant. His evidence did establish that it was possible he gave advice to the applicant consistent with the applicant's evidence as to that advice. It is open on Mr Carnall's evidence to conclude that the applicant's conversation with Mr Carnall, which we find did take place, was of fairly short duration and did not raise issues which Mr Carnall thought should be recorded. As Mr Carnall does not remember the telephone call and there is apparently no record of such a conversation, we have come to the view that it is more likely than not that the telephone call did not explore the specific details of the applicant's case. In response to the Tribunal's questions Mr Carnall made it clear that he did not give advice on the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme. There is nothing before us to base a finding that Mr Carnall gave advice regarding preservation of benefits in the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme nor is there anything on which to base a finding that the applicant sought advice about preservation of benefits in that scheme.

21. Mr R S Arnold, an officer of the Retirements Benefits Office, was called by the applicant, as already noted. He gave evidence about factors taken into account by the respondent when deciding whether to extend time to elect for preservation of benefits. These factors include:

(a) whether the interest in the Fund has been rolled over into an

approved deposit fund; and

(b) whether the eligible employee has been given incorrect or

inadequate advice.

Mr Arnold was not asked and did not describe the criteria adopted by the respondent to determine which types of cases justify a finding that it is desirable that the late election be recognised. It may be inferred from his evidence, but we do not need to do so, that the Commissioner only gives favourable consideration to cases falling within the two categories identified by him. As to the criteria applied to these cases we do not know.

22. Both parties sought to support their case on the reasons for decision in Re Liddle and Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 191; (1991) 25 ALD 307 where the Tribunal (D P Forgie dissenting) held that it was desirable to extend time to elect to preserve benefits under section 137 of the Act on the facts of that case. In that case all members of the Tribunal agreed with the exposition of the law by Deputy President Forgie, summarised at paragraph 62 of the learned Deputy President's reasons, as follows:

"Returning to the scope of the discretion in s.157(1) and taking into

account the Commissioner's powers and the fact that the discretion with

which I am concerned appears in the Part entitled, "Miscellaneous", I

consider that a late election should be recognised only if it would

benefit the person making it while, at the same time, not adversely

affecting the payment of pensions and benefits to other beneficiaries

under the Act and not impeding the efficient and proper administration

of the superannuation scheme established by the Act and administered by

the Commissioner. It may be that what would benefit the individual may

not be consistent with the interests of the other beneficiaries and of

good administration. In order to balance what may be competing

interests, it is my view that it can only be desirable to recognise a

late election if there are objectively good reasons which justify the

person's not being required to comply with the time limits otherwise

clearly specified by the Act."

23. Mr Nash submitted that there was no prejudice to the Fund in this case and we agree with that submission. The only prejudice can be to the Consolidated Revenue given that the Defence Force Scheme is funded by the Consolidated Revenue and that would only arise if the applicant succeeded in effecting a transfer value into the Defence Force Scheme. That is not a relevant consideration in this case because we are dealing with a late application to preserve benefits in the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme, not a late election for the purposes of section 69 of the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Act 1973.

24. Much of the applicant's case depends upon the advice given by Mr Carnall. It is submitted that Carnall had a duty to advise the applicant as an employee of the Commonwealth Government and that he breached that duty. We think it is beyond doubt, and so find, that Mr Carnall was held out by the Commonwealth as an expert on retirement benefits matters relating to the Defence Force.

25. It is equally clear, in our view, and we so find, that the applicant sought advice from Mr Carnall for the purpose of deciding whether she should elect to preserve her benefits in the Commonwealth Scheme for the purpose of creating a transfer value in the Defence Force Scheme. Mr Carnall's advice did not accord with the applicant's preferred position but she decided to follow that advice.

26. The applicant's case alleges a breach of duty by Mr Carnall comprising the following matters:

(a) Carnall's advice to the applicant to elect for a refund of the

superannuation contributions;

(b) failure to record the telephone interview;

(c) failure to advise the applicant to seek further advice before

making the election;

(d) failure to pass on the enquiry to the respondent's officers; and

(e) failure to advise the applicant about the effect of a transfer

value in the Defence Force Scheme.

27. These contentions overlook some important aspects of the evidence. The applicant knew of her right to preserve her benefits in the Superannuation Fund. She said so in her evidence to the Tribunal. She also knew of her right to transfer her interest in that fund to the Defence Force Scheme. She did not consult Mr Carnall for the purpose of finding out what she already knew. She consulted him for the purpose of deciding whether she should follow the course of preserving benefits and purchasing effective service with the transfer value in the Defence Fund.

28. This is not a case of a failure to provide information to the applicant as in Liddle and Re Lander and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision 8553). This is a case of the eligible employee being well aware of her rights, seeking advice as to how she should exercise those rights, making a decision in the light of that advice to elect for a refund of contributions and, with the benefit of five years hindsight, seeking the right to change that decision.

29. There has been no failure on the part of the Commonwealth in this case which would justify a conclusion that it is desirable that the late election should be recognised. The applicant acted in full knowledge of her rights but made the mistake, she says, of following Mr Carnall's advice. There is no basis for saying that Mr Carnall's advice was wrong or inadequate although it must have been brief. In those circumstances it was for the applicant to make whatever enquiries and seek such advice as she desired. It was, however, her decision and she says she made the wrong decision.

30. We cannot ignore that the Parliament imposed the 21 days time limit in section 137. No doubt that was put into the legislation for the purpose of certainty of administration. We are not satisfied that there is anything in the facts of this case which justify a departure from the legislative intent.

31. That a decision to elect for a refund of contributions may be thought, with the benefit of 5 years hindsight, to be a wrong decision, does not make it desirable that the late election be recognised and we so find.

32. The decision under review will be affirmed.
