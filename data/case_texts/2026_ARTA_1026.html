Issa and Commissioner of Taxation (Practice and procedure) [2026] ARTA 1026 (10 June 2026)

Issa and Commissioner of Taxation (Practice and procedure) [2026] ARTA 1026 (10 June 2026)

Applicant/s: Elie Issa

Respondent: Commissioner of Taxation

Tribunal Number: 2024/10273

Tribunal: General Member Michael Abood

Place: Sydney

Date: 10 June 2026

Decision: The Tribunal dismisses the application pursuant to section 99 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024.



............[SGD].......................

Michael Abood

General Member

Catchwords

PRACTICE AND PROCEDURE – Applicant given multiple directions to file statement of facts issues and contentions and further evidence over the course of 18 months – Applicant repeatedly failed to meet Tribunal directions within a reasonable time – Applicant failed to appear for a non-compliance directions hearing – Application dismissed.

Legislation

Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) subsections 9, 99 and 100

Cases

Berry v Commissioner of Taxation [2015] FCA 1244

Guse v Comcare [1997] FCA 140

Statement of Reasons

  1. On 27 November 2024 the Applicant lodged with the Tribunal an application seeking review of a reviewable objection decision made by the Commissioner of Taxation (the Commissioner) on 30 September 2024.
  2. The objection decision disallowed an objection made in relation to:
    • An amended assessment for income tax issued by the Commissioner for the income year ended 30 June 2022,
    • An amended assessment of a net amount of goods and services tax (GST) for the tax period covering June 2022; and
    • to administrative shortfall penalties issued in respect of each of these additional liabilities.
  3. The matter had been previously case managed by a registrar of the Tribunal who on 28 February 2025 directed a timetable that required the parties to lodge their Statements of Facts, Issues and Contentions (SFICs) and any evidence over the course of April to June 2025 before the matter was to be brought back for a further case event in late June 2025.
  4. By late April 2025 it became apparent that the Applicant, who was represented by lawyers, was having difficulties complying with the Registrar’s directions and in June 2025 he brought an application that the matter be either placed into abeyance pending the conclusion of another set of legal proceedings he was involved with in the NSW Supreme Court, or alternatively, that he be allowed significant further time to file his material.
  5. According to an affidavit of Mr Issa lodged in support of that application, in addition to prosecuting these proceedings he was also defending proceedings brought by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (the DCT) which sought judgment in an amount of $2,280,147 (the NSWSC Proceedings). Mr Issa deposed that the conduct of concurrent proceedings was placing immeasurable stress upon him which had led to a serious deterioration of his health, both mental and physical, and a depletion of his financial resources. He also referred to impending and serious surgery which would place further pressure upon him.
  6. The Commissioner’s response to Mr Issa’s request was to neither consent nor oppose and when the matter came back before the Registrar on 30 June 2025 it appears that the outcome was that the matter should be placed into temporary abeyance, at least until a further case event in the NSWSC Proceedings had occurred.
  7. The Registrar further listed the application for a case management directions hearing (CMDH) on 26 August 2025 at which the Applicant made a further application to delay the filing of his material. He produced a further affidavit which disclosed that the NSWSC Proceedings had now been listed for hearing in November 2025 and that should he be unsuccessful in those proceedings he would likely withdraw his Tribunal application. In response the Registrar, not unreasonably, stood the matter over until a further CMDH could be conducted in December 2025.
  8. When the matter came back before the Tribunal in December 2025 it became apparent that although the NSWSC Proceedings had concluded in a fashion unfavourable to the Applicant, the proceedings in the Tribunal remained on foot. The Registrar determined that the application should thereafter be managed by a member and the matter was listed before me for a CMDH in February 2026.
  9. At the CMDH on 16 February 2026 Mr Issa was represented by a new representative, being an accountant from Liguory & Associates Pty Ltd, who sought an indulgence of a few further weeks to bring the applicant’s case together. In response and having regard to Mr Issa’s ongoing medical challenges I was inclined to allow a further chance for him to put on his SFIC and evidence and directed a renewed timetable.
  10. As it has turned out, Mr Issa failed to comply with those directions as well as a further timetable made on 23 April 2026. When an explanation was sought from him about his most recent non-compliance he responded in an email relevantly advising:
    At present, I am reassessing my position in this matter due to significant mental health and personal health difficulties which are materially affecting my capacity to manage these proceedings properly.

    My current condition is affecting my concentration, memory, communication, and decision-making. In those circumstances, I am not presently in a position to properly instruct lawyers, provide clear and reliable directions, or work with legal representatives in a way that would allow them to perform their role effectively and provide proper assistance to me. I am also concerned that, if I am required to continue dealing with the matter in my current state, there is a real risk that I may fail to put forward relevant information, miss important matters, or otherwise not defend the proceedings properly.

  11. Upon receipt of this email the Tribunal listed the matter for a non-compliance CMDH yesterday, on 9 June 2026, and advised the parties that any application for extensions of time would need to be supported by evidence served in advance of the listing. Despite my warning the Applicant has taken no steps to bring any applications or furnish any evidence be it by way of medical documents or otherwise to explain his continued non-compliance and why he should be entitled to further opportunities.
  12. The Tribunal must now consider whether it may be appropriate to dismiss the application owing to the Applicant’s non-compliance with a range of directions as well as for his failure to progress the application.
  13. As Justice Jagot, at that time of the Federal Court, explained in considering section 42A(5) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth), which was the direct predecessor to section 100 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (the ART Act), the power to “dismiss an application for review of a decision without proceeding to review that decision if the applicant fails within a reasonable time to proceed with the application or to comply with a direction by the Tribunal in relation to the application” is a power plainly designed to assist the Tribunal in achieving its objectives (see Berry v Commissioner of Taxation [2015] FCA 1244 at [35]- [38]). It is, however, a power that should only be used “very sparingly and only....as a decision of last resort” (See Guse v Comcare [1997] FCA 140).
  14. The Tribunal’s objectives are provided for under section 9 of the ART Act. At a high level it requires the Tribunal to pursue the objective of providing a review mechanism that, amongst other matters, “ensures that applications to the Tribunal are resolved as quickly, and with as little formality and expense, as a proper consideration” of a matter permits, and in a way that “promotes public trust and confidence in the Tribunal”. It is implicit in those expectations that applications before the Tribunal are to be progressed in a timely fashion and that directions made by Tribunal seeking to progress such applications are to be complied with.
  15. The application before me was commenced in November 2024 and, since that time and as I have outlined in the paragraphs above, the Tribunal has made a range of directions and taken a number of steps which have sought to progress the matter. Whilst it may be conceded that the Applicant has provided, from time to time, some legitimate reasons to justify his failure to put on his material, collectively his history of non-compliance with a range of directions can only be described as unsatisfactory.
  16. Further, whilst I am somewhat sympathetic to the health challenges being proffered as an explanation for the case’s non-progress there is insufficient evidence before this Tribunal to explain how such challenges have stifled the Applicant’s ability to comply with timetables, particularly where he has concurrently run proceedings in other forums. It is a fact that almost twenty months since the application was initially brought the applicant is still yet to lodge a SFIC and any evidence that would represent something of a first step towards bringing this matter to a hearing which would allow it to then be determined. In these circumstances it would seem appropriate that the Tribunal should dispose of it for either lack of prosecution or, more simply, for non-compliance.
  17. In all the circumstances I would have dismissed the application yesterday under section 100 of the ART Act for the Applicant’s failure to proceed with the application and for his failure to comply with a range of directions that have been made, in particular, the direction made 23 April 2026 which required the Applicant to file his SFIC and evidence by 21 May 2026.
  18. As it turns out however, the Applicant failed to appear before the Tribunal at yesterday’s non-compliance CMDH. Section 99 of the ART Act provides a distinct basis for disposing of an application in circumstances where an applicant who is properly on notice of a case event fails to appear before the Tribunal.
  19. I am satisfied that the applicant was properly served with a listing notice attaching a MS Teams link well ahead of yesterday’s listing which would have provided him with a fair opportunity to appear if he had chosen to do so. I am also satisfied that in the context of the procedural history outlined above it is appropriate to dismiss this application following the non-appearance of the applicant at the non-compliance CMDH.
  20. For these reasons, although I would have been equally inclined to dismiss the application under section 100, I will instead dismiss the application under section 99 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024.

Date(s) of hearing:
9 June 2026
Applicant Representative:
No Appearance
Respondent Representative:
Self-Represented (Mr Michael Qin of the Australian Taxation Office)
