ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - Allowable Deductions – contributions to superannuation fund - whether a "superannuation scheme" whether "special circumstances" exist - relevance of -employees being "associated persons".

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - ss. 23F, 121C, 82AAA(1), 82AAA(2), 82AAD, 82AAE.

Peate v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1966] UKPCHCA 1; (1966) 116 C.L.R. 38;

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Gulland, Watson and Pincus [1985] HCA 83; (1985) 160 C.L.R. 55;

Scott y. Commissioner of Taxation (1966) 40 A.L.J.R. 265.

REASONS FOR DECISION

25 March 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. The Applicant is a company (Servco). It is a fiction of the law. It exists only in the eyes of the law. But the concept of Servco, and of a vast number of other companies, large and small, complicated and simple, has a significant place in the ordering of the affairs of society. Servco existed throughout the relevant period under consideration; the years of income ended 30 June 1982 to 1984 (inclusive). It existed to serve the interests of three families: the families of Mr. and Mrs. Thomas; Mr. and Mrs. Richard; Mr. and Mrs. Harold. Throughout that period the three husbands were chartered accountants carrying on business in partnership in a country town. Their wives assisted them in that undertaking in the manner to be later described.
  2. Prior to 30 June 1980 Thomas and Richard had been in partnership with others. In that earlier period Servco had been established to carry out a function of providing services to the then partnership by way of making available premises; furniture, equipment, stationery and supplies needed in carrying on the business; the services of non-professional staff whose services were needed by the partnership; and, during at least some periods, to provide the funds needed to enable the professional partnership to operate. Servco is also said to have utilized the services of the then partners to carry out the supervisory duties and organizational undertakings needed to enable Servco to efficiently and economically provide the services and facilities needed by the partnership. As early as 1978 Servco established a superannuation fund, with itself as trustee, with the expressed object of providing superannuation benefits for such employees of Servco as should be admitted to the fund. The trust deed was approved by the Commissioner for the purposes of s.23F of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). In that early period, the only persons admitted to membership of the fund were partners in the firm.
  3. By 1 July 1981 that partnership had been dissolved. One consequence was that it was left to Thomas and Richard to carry on the practice. To do so, they faced the burden of paying out the interests of the retiring partners and of those associated with them from all the commercial interests in which they had been commonly concerned. It was not explained how it came to pass, but without the assets of the superannuation fund changing between 30 June 1979 and 30 June 1980, the identity of the members entitled to those assets did. Liabilities to the newly-retired partners disappeared from the Balance Sheet of the Fund and liability to Thomas and Richard was increased to the same extent.

4, Following the retirement from the professional practice of the former partners, Harold was introduced to the continuing partnership. From that point forward the beneficial ownership of shares in Servco lay with Mesdames Thomas, Richard and Harold and the directors of Servco came to be those three ladies and their husbands. I find that, following the restructuring of the partnership, the practice found itself in somewhat straitened conditions, particularly occasioned by the need to "pay-out" the interests of the former partners. No evidence was presented to enable me to make more precise findings in that regard. In i particular, I was not presented with any evidence as to either the Balance Sheet or Profit and Loss accounts of the professional partnership at any time; or as to the state of accounts between the continuing partners and the retiring partners on the one hand and Harold on the other. However, I accept that their circumstances were such that, in an endeavour to increase the overall profitability of the ventures in which they were engaged, they needed to have the active and quite substantial assistance of all three wives.

5. I find that at all material times Servco provided all the services previously mentioned; that the wives served the company as Directors and as part-time employees working for some twenty hours per week and that those services were provided at a cost to Servco of paying wages and making some provision for them by way of contributions to the superannuation fund; and that Messrs. Thomas, Richard and Harold served the company in the manner previously described while holding office as Directors and, in their view, acting as "employees" at a cost to Servco of making contributions to superannuation funds.

6. When the initial fund was approved by the Commissioner for the purposes of S.23F of the Act, its income was to be exempt from income tax according to the law as it then stood. In that initial period, the entire enterprise faced liquidity problems. As a result, there was a need to preserve within the organization as much money as possible. In consequence, a practice was continued whereby Servco (as trustee of the superannuation fund) lent moneys back to itself to use in its own right. All available funds were used in that way. The loans were made at interest but they were unsecured. The terms of the loan were not formally documented. At that time the circumstance of the loan-back and the structure of the investment portfolio of the trustee were not of any tax significance as to the exempt status of the superannuation fund, or as to the entitlement of Servco to secure deductions for contributions made by it to such a fund.

7. As the date of 30 June 1982 loomed near, Servco proposed to effect further superannuation contributions for its six Directors but, in its judgment, it did not have the capacity to redistribute its existing portfolio (supplemented by the proposed contributions) so as to satisfy the "30/20" ratio then required by the Commissioner pursuant to S.121C of the Act. Recognizing that circumstance, on 1 June 1982 Servco executed a new deed establishing a second superannuation fund ("the No. 2 Fund") and submitted the deed to the Commissioner for approval for the purposes of S.23F of the Act. That submission was not made until 29 July 1982. It is common ground that subsequently the Commissioner provided the approval sought.

  1. No income was derived by the No. 2 Fund in the 1982 year. Servco claimed a deduction for the contributions which it made to that Fund and the claim was allowed. 14. There can be no doubt that the three families had as their objective the maximization of the financial benefits to be had from their efforts. Because of the obligations as to payment of tax created by the enactments of the Parliament, achievement of that objective required that regard be had to the criteria established by the Act as to the levying of income tax. Achievement of their objective also indicated the need to minimize loss to the enterprise which would be occasioned should there be a need to borrow from external sources. In consequence:-

(a) as between each husband and wife, and to the extent allowed by the Act, it was preferable to increase the taxable income of the wife at the expense of her husband so long as her maximum marginal rate of tax was less than his;

(b) it was advantageous to defer the derivation of income by drawing superannuation benefits in later years which had been provided at the cost (inter alia) of diminishing taxable income in earlier years; and

(c) it was advantageous to so organize any superannuation funds and their investment portfolio so as to ensure that they were exempt from income tax.

15. In the circumstances, application of those criteria led to:-

(a) the establishment and operation of Servco as a company to carry out functions which could have more easily, mechanically and conveniently been carried out by a partnership of husbands and wives or the partnership of husbands with the partnership employing the wives;

(b) the wives served Servco as Directors and employees rendering services which they might as easily have rendered to the partnership as employees. For those services, whether considered to be services rendered as employees or as Directors, they drew remuneration by way of salary and also received superannuation benefits;

(c) the husbands also served the company but only as Directors. They rendered services to that company which were only made necessary by the existence of the company. But for Servco, the same substantial functions would have been carried out by the partners in the interests of the partnership. The only benefit they received from Servco was the provision of superannuation benefits;

(d) so long as they could do so and retain tax exempt status in relation to fund income and deductibility for Servco in relation to its contributions, the assets of the fund were wholly lent back: to Servco; and

(e) when the criteria for deductibility in respect of contributions changed and the tax exempt status of the fund was imperiled, the response of all concerned to those new criteria caused Servco to establish a new fund with the intention that it would be managed and conducted so as to comply with the requirements of the Act and so as to ensure that contributors would be eligible for income tax deductions in relation to contributions made.

16. I note that the families might have sought to achieve parity of earnings between spouses by forming a partnership to work in association with the company in which enterprise their interests and benefits might have been equal. That was not done. It is unnecessary to consider why not. It is also unnecessary to consider whether or not such an endeavour would have been effective for any particular taxation purposes. But, be that as it may, the arrangements entered into were quite complicated, but no more so than was necessary to respond to the stipulations of the law. At the time of Peate's Case [(1966) [1966] UKPCHCA 1; 116 C.L.R. 38] the thought of medical practice companies was probably unheard of. By the time of the decisions in Gulland, Watson and Pincus [(1985) [1985] HCA 83; 160 C.L.R. 55), medical practice companies established for no better reason than to allow medical practitioners the advantages of superannuation to a degree only available if they assumed the status of employees, was recognized not only as a widespread practice but as a legitimate and effective one.

  1. The Commissioner attacks the arrangements as not being "bona fide" because of the policy of lending back; because of the establishment of a second fund with a view to only that fund complying with the Commissioner's requirements under the 30/20 rule; and because of the manner in which a trustee acted in the discharge of its trusteeship. In particular, the Commissioner looks to the authority of the decision of Windeyer, J. in the High Court of Australia in Scott v. Commissioner of Taxation (1966) 40 A.L.J.R. 265.
  2. In that instance the Court was concerned with questions as to the deductibility of contributions to a fund which was said to be "a superannuation fund" and thereby to be exempt from tax. The trustee was a company controlled by an individual (Scott). The other appellants (who claimed deductions for contributions made) were the person who exercised that control (Scott) and another company controlled by him. The trustee had engaged in

the business of dealing in land with all of its undertakings being very much under the direction of Scott. As his Honour said of Mr. Scott:-

"He had a lawyer's appreciation of his companies as separate entities in law. He was not always ready to recognize that they were nevertheless puppets whose actions he manipulated as he wished." (p.274)

The beneficiaries of the fund were Mr. Scott; his wife, who rendered services which "were more or less nominal"? and Mrs. Scott's parents, of whom his Honour said:-

"...one of the oddities of the matter is that when they became employees of (the company) they were each well past the retiring age as mentioned in the deed. Moreover, it seems questionable whether when they applied for membership of the fund they were 'engaged in the business of the founder' within the meaning of the definition of 'employee' in the deed", (p.277)

19. In considering whether.., in the fullness of the circumstances of which the foregoing is but an indication, there existed any "superannuation fund established for the benefit of employees", his Honour said:-

"There is no definition in the Act of a superannuation fund. The meaning of the term must therefore depend upon ordinary usage, the attributes of a thing thus denominated being those which things ordinarily so described have. To say that all that one need do to decide whether there was here a superannuation fund of the required kind is to study the deed is a mistake, because the deed must be read with a preconception of what such a fund is, otherwise reading it can provide no answer."

His Honour then went on to say:-

"A superannuation fund for employees may, I take it, be said to be 'established' when property composing the fund is set aside and subjected to appropriate trusts. It is as at the date when this is said to have been done that the effect of what was done must be considered. And it is the legal consequence of what was then done, not the motives of those doing it that must be considered.

I shall assume that a fund set up in the manner and for the purpose that the deed in this case apparently contemplates could be a superannuation fund for the benefit of employees. I make that assumption, notwithstanding that the extent to which an employee would benefit could, it seems, depend very much on the wishes, perhaps on the whims, of the employer. But it is not enough to say that a fund governed by the provisions of a deed such as that we have here could be a superannuation fund within the meaning of the Act. For it to be so in fact the parties concerned must have intended that the deed should take effect and operate according to its tenor; that a fund should be set up subjected to the trusts of the deed; and that Associated Provident Funds should as trustee be bound to carry out those trusts. On the other hand, if the scheme, including the deed, was intended to be a mere facade behind which activities might be carried on which were not to be really directed to the stated purposes but to other ends, then the words of the deed should be disregarded. It was urged for the appellant Associated Provident Funds that it is a real company and that the deed was really executed by it; and that, it was said, is the end of the question. But it is not. A disguise is a real thing: it may be an elaborate and carefully prepared thing; but it is nevertheless a disguise. The difficult and debatable philosophic questions of the meaning and relationship of reality, substance and form are., for the purposes of our law generally resolved by asking did the parties who entered into the ostensible transaction mean it to be in truth their transaction, or did they mean it to be, and in fact use it as, merely a disguise, a facade, a sham, a false front - all these words have been metaphorically used - concealing their real transaction: see the cases referred to by Jordan C.J. in Perpetual Trustee Co. v. Bligh (1940), 41 S.R. (N.S.W.) 33, at p. 39, and Hawke v. Edwards (1947), 48 S.R. (N.S.W.) 21, at p. 23, and Collis v. Magroarty and 0'Sullivan, [1913] S.R.Qd. 25, affirmed [1913] HCA 3; 15 C.L.R. 692.

The onus is on the taxpayer to satisfy me that the assessments appealed against are excessive. It has failed to do so. The inference I draw from the evidence as a whole is that there never was in truth a superannuation fund established for the benefit of employees. I should add that if there were such a fund established it was not in my view being applied at any time for the purposes for which it was established, that is to say for the benefit of employees according to its terms: Mahoney v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1965), 39 A.L.J.R. 62; 13 A.T.D. 519."

Having directed that the appeals of all applicants be dismissed, his Honour concluded:-

"I should add that Mr. Scott believed, and may have been advised by accountants, that by doing what he did he could somehow make appearance and pretence into reality. In this he was not dishonest or fraudulent, merely mistaken."

  1. The passage last quoted is relevant to the circumstances of these families. I am well satisfied that they were neither dishonest nor fraudulent in what they were about. What has to be determined is whether they were mistaken as to the effect of their undertakings.
  2. I am not persuaded that they were. I find nothing in the decision in Scott indicating that if the fund there in question had been administered by the same standards as those applied to the funds which have been considered in the evidence presented before me that his Honour would have reached the conclusion as he did. What persuaded his Honour to the conclusion he reached were the wholly remarkable circumstances and practices which attended the operation of the alleged "superannuation fund". Accordingly, I am satisfied that Servco satisfied the requirements of S.82AAC of the Act in that the payments were made for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits. It was not suggested that the right to receive the benefits was other than "fully secured". What remains to be determined is whether the amounts which have been allowed are too limited.

22. The quantum of allowable deductions in circumstances such as these is to be determined by an application of the provisions of S.82AAE of the Act. Sub-section (a) of that Section provides that the amount allowable

"shall not exceed whichever is the greater of the following amounts:

(i) $400; and

(ii) 5% of the total remuneration paid to the employee...during the year of income..."

THE WIVES

23. Five per cent of the remuneration payable to each wife only exceeded $400 in the 1984 year. As a result, the Commissioner has allowed a deduction of $400 in each of the years 1982 and 1983 (the years in which 5% of remuneration was less than $400) and $410 (equal to 5% of remuneration in the 1984 year). However, sub-paragraph (b) of the same Section provides that:-

"if the Commissioner is of the opinion that there are special circumstances that justify the allowance of a greater deduction, ...(then the allowable deduction) shall be such amount, ... as he considers reasonable."

24. So far as the evidence presented before me shows, the only "special circumstances" pointed to were the circumstance that the Commissioner had allowed a deduction of $1,725 for each wife in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1981. However, that had no bearing on the decision of Servco as to the amount to be contributed in the year ended 30 June 1982 because notice of that decision on the part of the Commissioner did not reach the taxpayer until after the company had made its decision. However, that information as to the 1981 decision was available well before decisions were made in relation to contributions for the years of income ended 30 June 1983 ($1,200 each) and 1984 ($2,100 each). However, before the 1983 decision had to be made, the Commissioner had given notice of only allowing $400 each in relation to the 1982 year and, by the time of a decision for 1984, Servco had had notice that the Commissioner had allowed only $400 each in relation to each of the two preceding years. I find nothing in that as constituting a special circumstance.

25. I have also speculated as to whether it might be appropriate to find "special circumstances" in the circumstance that, during the period in question, it was a common practice of the Commissioner to allow greater deductions than 5% of remuneration. Such considerations might have been established by pointing to the published rulings of the Commissioner and by demonstrating that, having regard to factors such as sex and age; years to retirement; years of service; projections as to future rates of inflation; and so on, that the Commissioner would ordinarily have allowed more than 5% of remuneration as a deduction. However, the simple fact in that regard is that I have not been presented with any evidence which would enable me to form any such view.

26. That being so, I am not persuaded that there are "special circumstances" such as would justify the Tribunal in directing that a greater sum be allowed as a deduction than has already been allowed by the Commissioner.

THE HUSBANDS

27. As paragraph, (a) of s.82AAA(2) provides that:-

"a director of a company shall be deemed to be employed by the company;", I am satisfied that the partners were so "employed" by Servco. Further, I am also satisfied that the partners were persons who were so employed by the taxpayer and who were "engaged in producing assessable income of the taxpayer" - a circumstance which brings them within the definition of "employee" in s.82AAA(1). That being so, and having regard to considerations already expressed in relation to the wives, I am satisfied that there was an eligibility in Servco to have superannuation deductions in relation to the male Directors (S.82AAC). However, as with the claims in relation to the wives as employees, it is next necessary to consider S.82AAE and, as the matter was argued by the Commissioner, S.82AAD.

28. The first question to be considered is whether the non-payment of any current remuneration destroys any entitlement there might be in the employer to deduct contributions made to a superannuation scheme. I have concluded that the Act is not to be read in that way. I do not read S.82AAE as if it stated:-

"Provided that some amount is paid by way of salary, wages or other remuneration, a deduction in respect of contributions may be allowable..."

29. The second question is whether, independently of S.82AAD, $400 or some lesser sum should be allowed. I do not consider that any amount up to $400 is automatically allowable. The Section plainly says that the deduction "shall not exceed" the greater of the two specified amounts. However, as appears from the treatment of the claims in relation to the ladies, the Commissioner did allow their employer $400 as a deduction even though that sum exceeded 5% of their remuneration. In all the circumstances it seems reasonable to me to allow the company a $400 deduction in relation to each of the three husbands, particularly as that amount constituted the entire cost to the company of procuring the services of the husbands.

30. There remains the question under S.82AAD. That section provides that where the payment is in relation to an "associated employee" the amount to be allowed is not to exceed such sum as

"in the opinion of the Commissioner, ... would have been so set apart or paid if the employee had not been associated with the taxpayer".

Clearly, the husbands were associated with the taxpayer in that sense. That is not disputed. However, it is also clear that, despite the artificiality of the entire structure, the husbands did render service as Directors to the company and that the services so rendered were of value to Servco. In all the circumstances, I am satisfied that it is not unreasonable that the company should have incurred an expense of $400 to procure the services of each employee and, accordingly, I am satisfied that it is reasonable that the amount should be allowed.

31. In short, I am satisfied that, if by some strange circumstance the services rendered to Servco by the husbands as Directors had been rendered by other persons bringing the same skills and effort to the exercise, without those persons being associated with the taxpayer, the cost to Servco of procuring those services would have been not less than $400 per person. Accordingly, the claims will be upheld as to $1,200 in each of the two years in dispute. That result accords with the result determined on by the Commissioner for the 1982 year, although that alone cannot determine the result for the later years.
