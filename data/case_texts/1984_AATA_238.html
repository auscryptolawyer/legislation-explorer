Re Joseph Both and the Commissioner of Employees' Compensation [1984] AATA 238 (29 June 1984)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JOSEPH BOTH

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR EMPLOYEES' COMPENSATION

No. N.82/530

Compensation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.C. Jennings (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Compensation - hammer toe injury - operation ten months after work-related foot injury - hammer toe condition activated by trauma - medical treatment and resulting incapacity compensable.

Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees') Act 1971 s.37(1), s.45(1).

HEARING

CANBERRA

29:6:1984

ORDER

The decision of the respondent of 30 June 1983 will be set aside and the matter remitted to him with a direction that the medical treatment and incapacity of the applicant during September and October 1981 were in relation to and resulted from personal injury sustained by him on 5 November 1980. There will be liberty to apply as to the amount payable if the parties are unable to reach agreement.

DECISION

The applicant seeks to establish liability of the respondent for employees' compensation in respect of medical treatment and a period of 4 weeks incapacity for work some ten months after he suffered a fall causing some injuries which were admitted to have arisen out of and in the course of his employment.

I find that the applicant injured his right foot at work on the 5th November 1980 when it was caught in a fire door which he was attempting to open and which was blown back in his face causing him to fall on his back.

I am satisfied that the problems with his foot which subsequently developed as described in the report of Dr. Mahoney and as explained in the evidence of Dr. Deveridge, were occasioned by that injury.

I accept the applicant as a truthful witness. I noted him as saying that prior to the accident he had no trouble with the toe not "the foot" as counsel suggested to him. He was referring to the toe upon which Dr. Mahoney performed an operation in September 1981.

In any event, the trouble which he experienced in his right foot in 1973 does not appear to have any significance for the purpose of the present case, if I accept the opinion of Dr. Deveridge in this regard, which I do without reservation.

Dr. Deveridge saw nothing in the left foot which detracted from his opinion that if the applicant was to be believed as to how his foot was caught when he fell, then on the balance of probabilities that hammer toe condition developed as a result of trauma and not spontaneously as sometimes occurs in later life.

Dr. Mahoney's report of 29 July 1983, provides some corroboration of injury to the foot at the time of the fall, and in any event as I have said, I have no reason to doubt that the applicant was a truthful witness. He said the safety boot he was wearing with a steel cap inside was itself damaged when his foot was jammed.

Dr. Ehrlich who was called as a witness for the respondent, made a report on 18 November 1983 after examining the applicant in which he suggested that Dr. Mahoney be invited to explain the causal connection which he considered may exist between the incident in November 1980 and the damage to the applicant's toe flexor mechanisms.

In evidence, Dr. Ehrlich said that he could not accept the real possibility of any causal connection between the trauma and the need for an operation.

I accept Dr. Ehrlich's surprise at the suggestion that there was a causal connection between the condition of hammer toe and the injury, but he was prepared to concede the possibility that the injury brought the quiescent condition of hammer toe to light and accordingly that the need for an operation arose out of the injury. He just could not accept and I think in this regard he was right, that trauma could cause a hammer toe condition because of the very nature of that condition.

Dr. Mahoney's report makes it clear that he was not too precipitate to resort to surgery. The hardline hypothesis which Dr. Ehrlich preferred, namely that there was contusion which cleared up and that the operation was not related to the injury, does not appeal to me.

The significant temporal link in the chain of events is strengthened by Dr. Mahoney's report that in February a callous was developing on the second toe of the right foot which had been contused in November.

Dr. Mahoney continued to treat his patient conservatively at first, but in August 1981, a Dr. McKenzie who examined the applicant on behalf of the respondent, noted that the hammer toe was getting worse.

I am satisfied that the medical treatment which the applicant obtained was in relation to the injury he had suffered and that the incapacity necessitated by the operation resulted from the work-related injury in the sense that a pre-disposition to develop a hammer toe was activated by trauma. Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees) Act 1971 s.37(1) and s.45(1).
