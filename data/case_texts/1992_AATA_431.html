Correia-Zaknich Unit Trust and Odete Fisheries Unit Trust and Australian Trade Commission [1992] AATA 431 (14 February 1992)

Last Updated: 28 July 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

EXPORT MARKET DEVELOPMENT GRANT - expenditure incurred in payment of consultancy fees for providing advice on export market for prawns - characterisation of expenditure - whether eligible expenditure under s.4(1)(a), (b) - whether expenditure under s.4(2)(a) - whether portion of expenditure ineligible as being incurred by way of payment for export market information shared with persons other than claimant - whether portion of expenditure ineligible as being incurred in relation to export sales of eligible goods produced by persons other than claimant - whether effect of an act was to distribute or transfer expenditure thereby resulting in larger grant entitlement - whether s.38 applicable and should be applied.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. W90/255

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: CORREIA-ZAKNICH UNIT TRUST AND ODETE FISHERIES UNIT TRUST

Applicants

And: AUSTRALIAN TRADE COMMISSION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

  1. This is an application for review of a decision of the Australian Trade Commission ("the respondent"), dated 17 October 1990. In that decision the respondent, pursuant to s.40A(4) of the Export Market Development Grants Act 1974 (Cth) ("the Act"), confirmed, on reconsideration, its earlier decision, dated 27 September 1989, to disallow, in part, a claim by Correia-Zaknich Unit Trust and Odete Fisheries Unit Trust ("the applicants") for a grant under the Act.
  2. It is convenient, at the outset, to summarise the scheme of the Act and to set out its relevant provisions. The Act is described, in its long title, as "An Act relating to Grants for the purpose of providing Incentives for the Development of Export Markets". Under s.13, claims for grants must be submitted to the respondent which is, in turn, required by s.12 to determine whether the claimant has a "grant entitlement" and, if so, to pay to the claimant a grant equal to the amount of the grant entitlement so determined. By s16(1), "the grant entitlement of a claimant, in relation to a grant year, is whichever is the lesser of -
    • (a) 70% of the amount by which the eligible expenditure incurred by the claimant during that grant year exceeds $5,000; and
    • (b) an amount equal to -

(i) in the case of a claimant to whom sub-section (2) docs not apply - $200,000; or

(ii) in any other case - the maximum grant entitlement in respect of that grant year and that claimant as determined in accordance with sub-section (2)." Sub-section (2) is not relevant to the present case.

3. The key expression "eligible expenditure" is defined in s.4 of the Act. That section; so far as is relevant to the present case, provides as follows:-

"4.(1) Subject to the succeeding provisions of this section, a reference in this Act to eligible expenditure is a reference to expenditure that, in the opinion of the Commission, has been incurred by a person primarily and principally for the purpose of creating or seeking opportunities, or creating or increasing demand, for -

(a) the sale by that person for export, or the export by that person and sale by him, of eligible goods manufactured, produced, assembled or processed in Australia;

(b) in the case of a person who manufactured, produced, assembled or processed eligible goods at the time when the expenditure was incurred, the sale for export, or export and sale, of eligible goods manufactured, produced, assembled or processed in Australia by that person; ...

(2) For the purposes of this section, "expenditure" means expenditure to the extent to which it is incurred by a claimant... by way of -

(a) expenses of, contributions towards expenses of, or payments made to an agent for the purpose of –

(i) the carrying out of market research or the obtaining of market information; or

(ii) advertising or other means of securing publicity or soliciting business, not being amounts paid or payable to - ...".

The expression "eligible goods" is defined by s.5 of the Act to include, inter alia, "goods manufactured, produced, assembled or processed, in Australia, where ... not more than 50 per centum of the value of the goods is attributable to materials not of Australian origin; ...".

  1. in the present case there is no dispute that, for the purposes of the Act. the applicants arc "a claimant", the "grant year" is the year 1 July 1987 to 30 June 1988, the relevant goods (as described below) are "eligible goods", and the applicants have a "grant entitlement". The only matter in dispute is the amount of that "grant entitlement".
  2. The applicants' major assets consist of three fishing trawlers, known as "Angelina C", "Amelia C", and "OdeteC". These assets are divided between the applicants as follows:- the major assets of Correia-Zaknich Unit Trust are the "Amelia C" and a 50% interest in the "Angelina C", whereas the major assets of Odete Fisheries Unit Trust arc the "Odete C" and a 50% interest in the "Angelina C". The "Angelina C" is, therefore, the joint property of the applicants. Legal ownership of the property of each of the applicants is vested in the relevant trustee. In the case of Correia-Zaknich Unit Trust the trustee is Correia-Zaknich Holdings Pty. Ltd., whereas in the case of Odete Fisheries Unit Trust, the trustee is Swan Bay Pty. Ltd. Beneficial ownership of the property of the applicants, including the three trawlers, is ultimately vested, by means of a complex arrangement of interlocking family companies and discretionary trusts (the details of which it is not necessary to state), in the three Corrcia brothers (John. Felix and James) and Peter Zaknich ("the beneficiaries"). Because the "Angelina C" is the joint property of the applicants, the beneficiaries commonly describe the relationship of the applicants in respect of the "Angelina C" as the "Angelina C partnership". For the sake of convenience, this description will be adopted by the Tribunal and referred to later in these reasons.
  3. The beneficiaries are engaged in the business of fishing for prawns and exporting their catch, chiefly to Japan. They operate the three abovementioned trawlers for this purpose. The "Angelina C" and the "Amelia C" are operated in the Gulf of Carpentaria, while the "OdeteC" is operated in Exmouth Gulf in Western Australia. During fishing operations, the normal arrangement amongst the beneficiaries is that three of them each operates ("skippers") one of the three trawlers, while the fourth remains based onshore in Penh or Fremantle acting as a point of contact with each of the trawlers.
  4. Since the early 1980s, the beneficiaries have engaged the services of N.C. Read Pty. Ltd., a seafood export and consultancy company, to act as their agent in carrying out overseas market research, providing advice on overseas markets, developing export markets for prawns and assisting in the export sale of their prawns. Mrs. Nancy Read, the owner of N.C. Read Pty. Ltd., gave evidence in which she elaborated on the export market information her company provided to the beneficiaries. She explained that, prior 10 the 1986/37 financial year, it had been the practice for her company to enter into a separate agreement in respect of each of the three abovementioned trawlers but that, in the 1986/87 financial year and the 1987/88 financial year (the latter being the "grant year" for the purposes of the present case), there was only the one agreement, namely, an agreement between "Angelina C and her company. She further explained that this change was made to simplify the paperwork for all those involved. Mr. Felix Corrcia in his evidence, confirmed. this and added that the reason "Angelina C" was singled out to be a party to the agreement was that it was the largest and most efficient of the three trawlers and that a bank account, in the name of "Angelina C", was the only account to which all the beneficiaries were signatories. The money in this bank account was, like the "Angelina C" itself, a joint asset of the applicants.
  5. A copy of a document, dated 1 July 1987, containing the terms of the agreement between "Angelina C" and N.C. Read Pty. Ltd., in respect of the year 1 July 1987 to June 1988, was received into evidence as part of the documents lodged pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth). According to clause (9) of this document, the sum of $36,000 was payable by "Angelina C" to N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. in four quarterly instalments of $9,000 each, due on 30 September 1987, December 1987, 31 March 1988 and 30 June 1988. The document was signed by the beneficiaries and Mrs. Nancy Read. In his evidence, Mr. Felix Correia stated that the export market information and services provided by N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. pursuant to this agreement were used for the benefit of the beneficiaries' entire fishing operations - that is, the operations of all three trawlers, not merely the operations of the "Angelina C".

9. It was clearly established by the evidence that the sum of $36,000 was paid to N.C. Read Pty. Ltd., pursuant to the agreement dated 1 July 1987, in four instalments of $9,000 each. The first three instalments were paid directly from the "Angelina C" bank account on 26 October 1987, 15 February 1988 and 12 April 1988. The fourth and final instalment was not paid directly from the "Angelina C" bank account, but rather was deducted by N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. from the proceeds of sale of a quantity of prawns made by it on behalf of the "Angelina C partnership". There was some uncertainty, arising from the documentary evidence tendered to the Tribunal, regarding the date on which this final instalment of $9,000 was paid. On one document - an invoice from N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. to "Angelina C", dated 8 June 1988 - received into evidence, there appeared a written annotation by Mrs. Nancy Read's secretary that the sum of $9,000 had been received on 29 June 1988. On another document - 3 letter dated 25 September 1990 from Bird Cameron, Chartered Accountants, who act for the applicants - also received into evidence, it was indicated that the final instalment of $9,000 was made by way of deduction from the proceeds of a sale, as mentioned above, on 19 July 1988. Though there appears to be an inconsistency between the two documents, the respondent did not put in dispute the former document. Indeed the respondent, when it made the decision under review and at all other material times, has proceeded on the basis that the full amount of $36,000 was paid by the applicants to N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. within the "grant year" 1 July 1987 to 30 June 1988. The Tribunal is, therefore, prepared to accept that payment of the final instalment of $9,000 was made by 30 June 1988 - that is, within the "grant year" in this matter.

  1. In their claim for a grant for the 1987/88 grant year, dated 18 November 1988, the applicants claimed the sum of $36,000 as eligible expenditure by way of payments for agents/consultants fees in Australia. They, accordingly, calculated their grant entitlement under s.l6(1)(a) of the Act, to be $21,700 - that is, 70% of ($36,000 less $5,000). On 27 September 1989, the respondent determined that the applicants had a grant entitlement in the amount of $4,725. This figure was arrived at in the following way. From the total sum of $36,000 claimed by the applicants as eligible expenditure, the respondent deducted $750 as "non-promotional" (and, therefore, ineligible) expenditure in the way of "document charges". The balance of $35,250 was divided by three on the basis that the total sum of eligible expenditure was to be apportioned equally among the three trawlers, "Angelina C", "Amelia C" and "Odete C" - that is, eligible expenditure in the amount of $11,750 was allowed by the respondent in the case of each trawler. The respondent treated the applicants' claim, dated 18 November 1988, as relating only to the trawler, "Angelina C", and, accordingly, determined a grant entitlement, under S.16(1)(a) of the Act, of $4,725 - that is, 70% of ($11,750 less $5,000).
  2. By letter dated 31 October 1989, the applicants requested the respondent to review its decision, as they were entitled to do under s.40A(2) of the Act. In their letter, the applicants requested the respondent to allow a further $23,500 as eligible expenditure, thereby increasing their grant entitlement by $16,450 to a total of $21,175. It is to be noted that the applicants, by implication, had accepted the respondent's decision that the sum of $750 was not eligible expenditure because it related to "non-promotional" activities - specifically, the preparation of sales documentation. This was confirmed at the hearing by Mr. D. Davies who appeared for the applicants.
  3. By letter dated 17 October 1990 addressed to Mrs. N.C. Read, the respondent notified that, upon reconsideration, it had decided to affirm its earlier decision "that the expenditure in question is not eligible expenditure in terms of Section 4(1) of the Act, in that it was not incurred in the promotion of sales in which the claimant was the principal". It is this decision that is the subject of the present application for review by this Tribunal.
  4. As stated in paragraph 4 above, the only matter in dispute in these proceedings is the amount of the applicants' grant entitlement. Under s.16(1)(a) of the Act, the crucial matter for determination is the amount of eligible expenditure incurred by the claimant that is, the applicants in this matter.
  5. It will be recalled (sec paragraph 3 above) that the definition of the expression "eligible expenditure" contained in s.4 of the Act comprises two limbs. One limb relates to the definition of the term "expenditure" which, by s.4(2), means:

"expenditure to the extent to which it is incurred by a claimant... by way of –

(a) expenses of, contributions towards expenses of, or payments made to an agent for the purpose of -

(i) the carrying out of market research or the obtaining of market information; ...".

The other limb relates to the definition of the composite expression "eligible expenditure" in s.4(1) of the Act. Section 4(1) relevantly provides that a reference to "eligible expenditure" is a reference to "expenditure" (as defined in s.4(2)):

"that, in the opinion of the Commission, has been incurred by a person primarily and principally for the purpose of creating or seeking opportunities, or creating or increasing demand, for -

(a) the sale by that person for export, or the export by that person and sale by him, of eligible goods manufactured, produced, assembled or processed in Australia;

(b) in the case of a person who manufactured, produced, assembled or processed eligible goods at the time when the expenditure was incurred, the sale for export, or export and sale, of eligible goods manufactured, produced, assembled or processed in Australia by that person;...".
  1. In relation to s.4(2) of the Act the evidence establishes to the Tribunal's satisfaction that expenditure was incurred by the applicants for the purposes specified in s.4(2)(a)(i). Such expenditure consisted, of course, of the fees paid by the applicants (constituting the "Angelina C partnership") to N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. pursuant to the agreement of 1 July 1987 (see paragraph 9 above). The respondent submitted, however, that this expenditure was incurred by way of payments made to an agent (N.C. Read Pty. Ltd.) for the purpose of carrying out market research or obtaining market information for the benefit of all three trawlers, "Angelina C", "Amelia C" and "Odete C", and not merely for the benefit of the "Angelina C". Accordingly, the respondent's submission continued, the total amount of expenditure must be apportioned amongst the three trawlers so that only one-third of it is attributable to the "Angelina C" and. therefore, claimable by the applicants constituting the "Angelina C partnership".
  2. The fallacy involved in this submission is that it overlooks the plain words of s.4(2)(a) of the Act. In Export Development Grants Board v. Miller Pohang Coal Co. Pty. Ltd. (1985) 61 ALR 125 the Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia said (at 131-132):

It is desirable to consider separately the questions whether expenses incurred ... are "expenditure" within s.4(2) and, if so, whether they may be "eligible expenditure" within s.4(1). In relation to s.4(2) it is interesting to note that the sub-section does not use the word "purpose" except in the special context of payment to an agent for a specified purpose. Neither does it limit the objects of expenditure by reference to the items specified in sub-s(1). Rather it refers to "expenditure to the extent to which it is incurred by a claimant... by way of expenses of" a particular kind. These words require characterization of the expenses. To the extent that particular expenses are of a specified kind they are "expenditure" within the meaning of the sub-section. The test is an objective one.

See also Sea King Pty. Ltd v. Australian Trade Commission (1986) 69 ALR 277, emphasising that, in applying s.4(2),regard must be had to the expenditure incurred by the claimant, not to what happens thereafter. There can be no doubt that the expenditure incurred by the applicants (constituting the "Angelina C partnership") by way of payment of fees to N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. objectively falls within the kind of expenses specified in s.4(2)(a)(i) of the Act. Consideration of the ultimate purpose to which the market research or market information was to be put, or the intended use of that information, is irrelevant and, accordingly, any question of the apportionment of the expenditure amongst intended users or beneficiaries of the information or research is misconceived.

  1. The respondent also submitted that the sum of $36,000 paid by the applicants to N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. - or, at least two-thirds of it - did not satisfy the test of "eligible expenditure" prescribed by s.4(1) of the Act. According to the submission, s.4(1) requires that the expenditure be incurred by a person primarily and principally for the purpose of creating or seeking opportunities, or creating or increasing demand, for the sale for export or the export and sale by that person of eligible goods, In the present case, the submission continued, the expenditure was incurred by the applicants primarily and principally for this purpose, not only in relation to export sales of prawns caught by the "Angelina C", but also in relation to export sales of prawns caught by the "Amelia C" and the "Odetc C". At the hearing, however, Mr. Godwin, for the respondent, submitted that an apportionment approach should also be adopted in relation to s.4(1) and that, on this approach, one-third of the total expenditure should be treated as "eligible expenditure" - being incurred by the applicants primarily and principally for the required purpose in relation to export sales of prawns caught by the "Angelina C" - whereas the remaining two-thirds, relating to the promotion of export sales of prawns caught by the "Amelia C" and the "Odete C", should not.
  2. In Parker Pen (Australia) Pty. Ltd. v. Export Development Grants Board [1983] FCA 77; (1983) 67 FLR 234 Lockhart J. said (at 242):

The word "purpose" is, of course, susceptible of a variety of meanings depending on its context. In the context of s.4(1) the inquiry must be to ascertain whether the expenditure was incurred by the person primarily and principally for the purpose of creating or seeking opportunities or creating or increasing demand for the stipulated objects, including the sale by that person for export of eligible goods manufactured in Australia. This involves a subjective element. The purpose must be someone's purpose. It is the purpose of the person mentioned in the sub-section. To ignore subjective elements is wrong. There is, of course, a difference between the essential elements in the notion of purpose and the means whereby purpose is ascertained. Purpose may be gleaned either from subjective or objective elements or, more usually, both. A person may say what his purpose is, but the objective facts may cast doubt upon the credibility or reliability of his statement. It is for the Tribunal of fact to consider all the circumstances and conclude whether the requisite purpose has been established. Objective facts arc usually more reliable than mere protestations of purpose, intent or state of mind, which, although susceptible of testing in cross-examination, arc intrinsically impenetrable and inscrutable.

This passage was quoted with approval by the Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia in Miller Pohang Coal (supra, at 132). In that case it was also held that, under s.4(1)(a) of the Act, in order to satisfy the test of "eligible expenditure", the claimant must have been both the person who incurred the expenditure and the person who was engaged in export sales (at 134). Applying these principles to the present facts, the Tribunal has no doubt that the expenditure of $36,000 by the applicants in the grant year 1987/88 by way of payment of consultancy fees to N.C. Read Pty. Ltd. was eligible expenditure within the meaning of s.4(1)(a) of the Act. On the basis of the oral evidence given by both Mrs. Nancy Read and Mr. Felix Correia, which the Tribunal accepts, and the agreement, dated 1 July 1987, between "Angelina C" and N.C. Read Pty. Ltd., a copy of which was received into evidence, this expenditure was incurred by the applicants (constituting the "Angelina C partnership") "primarily and principally" - in the sense of "chiefly"; "mainly": Parker Pen (Australia) Pty. Ltd. v. Export Development Grants Board (supra, at 241) - for the purpose of creating or seeking opportunities, or creating or increasing demand, for the sale by them (constituting the "Angelina C partnership") for export, or the export and sale by them (constituting the "Angelina C partnership"), of eligible goods (prawns) produced or processed in Australia. In this sense, the export sales of prawns caught by the other two trawlers, "Amelia C" and "Odete C", are irrelevant and no question of apportionment of eligible expenditure amongst the three trawlers arises.

19. In any event, there would appear to be another reason for treating the expenditure in question as "eligible expenditure" within s.4 of the Act, namely that the present matter falls within s.4(1)(b) rather than s.4(1)(a) of the Act. Although this was not argued before us, it seems open to the Tribunal to find that s.4(1)(b) applies because the applicants (constituting the "Angelina C partnership") were also the producers or processors of the eligible goods (prawns) at the time when-they incurred the expenditure. If s.4(1)(b) applies, as we believe it does, there is no requirement that the sale for export, or export and sale, of eligible goods be effected by the person who incurred the relevant expenditure. It is sufficient that that person (here, the applicants constituting the "Angelina C partnership") is, inter alia, the producer or processor of eligible goods produced or processed in Australia. That would appear to be this case.

20. There is one further matter to be dealt with. The respondent's final submission was that even if the applicants' expenditure is wholly "eligible expenditure" within the meaning of s.4 of the Act, this is an appropriate case for the application of s.38(1) of the Act. That sub-section provides:

"38.(1) Where the Commission is of the opinion that -

(a) an effect of an act done on or after 20 May 1985 is to distribute or transfer expenditure or income among some or all of the persons (in this sub-section referred to as the "participants") affected by the doing of the act; and

(b) as a result, the Commission would, but for this section, be liable to pay to such of the participants as are claimants, in respect of the grant year commencing on 20 May 1985 or a subsequent grant year, a total amount by way of grant that exceeds the total amount (if any) by way of grant that would have been payable to the participants if -

(i) the act had not been done; and

(ii) such of the participants (if any) who ceased to exist had not ceased to exist,

the Commission may, for the purposes of this Act and to the extent it thinks necessary to prevent or limit that result, treat the whole or any part of any expenditure or income that has been so distributed or transferred as if it were re-allocated among some or all of the participants in such manner as the Commission determines."

21. According to the evidence, in the grant years immediately prior to the 1986/87 grant year, a separate claim for a grant was made in the name of each of the three trawlers, "Angelina C", "Amelia C" and "Odete C". However, in the 1986/87 grant year, the applicants decided to consolidate the claims into one claim in the name of "Angelina C", the largest and most efficient of the three trawlers. This was also done in the 1987/88 grant year, which is the subject of the present proceedings. Because s.16(1)(a) of the Act incorporates an expenditure threshold of $5,000 in the formula for calculating individual grant entitlements, it is clear that the consolidation of three individual claims into one would result in a larger prima facie grant entitlement than the total of the three individual grant entitlements. The question is, however, whether s.38(1) is applicable to the circumstances of the present case and, if it is, whether it is appropriate that the discretion conferred by s.38(1) be exercised in such a way as to reduce the applicants' grant entitlement.

  1. The Tribunal was not referred to, nor is it aware of, any decisions, whether of the Federal Court of Australia or of the Tribunal, which deal with the interpretation of the present s.38 of the Act which was enacted in 1985. Authorities on the former s.38 of the Act, or the similarly-worded s.16 of the Export Expansion Grants Act 1978 (Cth), - such as Re R.J. Gilbertson Pty. Ltd. and Export Development Grants Board (1984) 5 ALN N453 (AAT); Michell Carbonised Wool Exports Pty. Ltd. v. Export Development Grants Board (1984) 2 FCR 93 (FCA); Export Development Grants Board v. Michell Carbonised Wool Exports Pty. Ltd. [1985] HCA 42; (1985) 59 ALR 729 (HCA) - are of limited usefulness in this matter because of the substantial change in the wording of the new provision. One general feature which is, however, common to both the former and present s.38 is that the section is concerned with the objective effects of an act of the "participants", not with their subjective intentions or purposes: see R.J. Gilbertson (supra, at N455); Michell Carbonised Wool Exports (supra, 2 FCR at 96-7, 108-9).
  2. Section 38(1) of the Act is only applicable where the requirements of paras.(a) and (b) of that sub-section are satisfied. It is difficult to see how the requirements of para.(a) were satisfied here. Paragraph (a) requires that an effect of an act done on or after 20 May 1985 be "to distribute or transfer expenditure or income among some or all of the persons ... affected by the doing of the act;". The phrase "among some or all of the persons" presupposes that the relevant expenditure or income was distributed or transferred among more than one person or entity. In the present case, however, the effect of the 1986 consolidation, referred to in paragraph 21 above, was to transfer expenditure to one person or entity, namely the "Angelina C partnership". It is to be noted here that the word "person" is defined (non-exhaustively) in s.3(1) of the Act to include, inter alia, "a partnership". Alternatively, if the "Angelina C partnership" is not itself regarded as one legal person, but rather as the two partners who constitute it (that is, the applicants), then the consolidation did not involve any "transfer" of expenditure at all since the same expenditure would be incurred by the applicants whether the consolidation took place or not.
  3. Even if the requirements of s.38(1)(a) and (b) were satisfied in this matter, the Tribunal is of the view that it would not be appropriate to apply s.38(1) to the present claim for a grant. The object of s.38 is to prevent claimants abusing the export market development grants scheme or re-arranging their affairs to obtain a larger grant, in a way that is not in accordance with the spirit of the Act: see Minister's Second Reading Speech, 21 August 1985, Parliamentary Debates 1985, Vol. H. of R. 143, P.148. The action of the applicants in making a single claim for a grant cannot reasonably be described in these terms. Indeed, having regard to the nature and scale of the prawn-fishing business carried on in this case, a single claim for a grant, in each grant year, covering the entire business seems to the Tribunal to be more appropriate than three separate claims, each in respect of one of the three trawlers -"Angelina C", "Amelia C" and "Odete C" - operated in the business. In the Tribunal's opinion, an appropriate case for applying s.38(1) is where a distribution or transfer of expenditure or income would otherwise result in separate grant entitlements to the participants which in total exceed the maximum single grant entitlement of $200,000 prescribed by s.16 of the Act. That is not this case.

25. For the reasons given above the Tribunal sets aside the decision under review and, in substitution therefor, decides that the grant entitlement of the applicants for the grant year 1 July 1987 to 30 June 1988 is $21,175.
