VT86/2275 and Commissioner of Taxation [1990] AATA 87; (1990) 21 ATR 3383; 90 ATC 359 (19 April 1990)

Last Updated: 29 May 2009



D E C I S I O N A N D R E A S O N S



Taxation - unpaid company tax - copy of notice of assessment

served on vendor shareholders - objection by vendore shareholders

to assessment disallowed - application for review of objection

decision - evidence presented that no proper basis existed for

service of copy of notice - whether Tribunal has power to review

objection decision



Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ss. 18(5) &

(8)



Case W53 89 ATC 489

Duggan and Ryall v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 72 ATC 4239



AAT No 5855



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. VT86/2275

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:



Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal : Deputy President I. R. Thompson



Date : 19 April 1990



Place : Melbourne



Decision : The Administrative Appeals Tribunal has no power to

review the objection decision against the

assessment of income tax payable by N Pty. Ltd. in

respect of the income year which ended on 30 June

1972.



(Sgd I. R. Thompson)

....................

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. VT86/2275

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



REASONS FOR DECISION



19 April 1990 Deputy President I. R. Thompson



1. In June 1975 the respondent served on a company, N Pty.



Ltd. ("the company"), notice of an assessment, made pursuant to



section 166 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITA



Act"), of the amount of its taxable income during the taxation



year which had ended on 30 June 1972 and of the tax payable



thereon. The company was at that time being wound up and,



apparently on 20 August 1975, the liquidator lodged with the



respondent an objection in writing against the assessment.



Subsequently that objection was withdrawn. The company was then



dissolved.



2. In July 1983 the respondent served on three persons, of



whom the applicant was one, a copy of the notice of assessment.



That was done pursuant to section 18(5) of the Taxation (Unpaid



Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("the T(UCT)A Act"). The other



two persons then nominated the applicant as the nominated party



to sign objections on behalf of the company. However, in the



event, an objection was signed by all three of the persons,



including the applicant. The objection was disallowed by the



respondent. The applicant then requested him to refer the



objection to a Board of Review pursuant to section 187 of the ITA



Act, as then in force. In February 1986 it was referred to



Taxation Board of Review No. 2. It had not been reviewed by that



Board by 30 June 1986, when the Taxation Boards of Review were



abolished and the Administrative Appeals Tribunal was given power



and required to hear and determine objections which had been



referred to the Boards but not reviewed (Taxation Boards of



Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986, section 223).



3. Because of the enormous number of objection decisions



which had been referred to the Taxation Boards of Review but not



reviewed by 30 June 1986, and because also of the enormous number



of additional objection decisions subsequently referred by the



respondent to the Tribunal, the review of the objection decision



in these proceedings was not commenced by the Tribunal until



December 1989. At the hearing of the matter the applicant was



represented by Mr B. Saunders, an accountant, and the respondent



by Mr G. A. A. Nettle, of Counsel. Because of an unusual turn



which events had taken in October 1989 it was agreed that the



effect of that turn of events should be considered as a



preliminary issue. Arguments in relation to it were addressed to



the Tribunal by Mr Saunders and Mr Nettle. The Tribunal had



before it the documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the



Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (as modified by section



14ZG of the Taxation Administration Act 1953) and an affidavit



sworn on 18 December 1989 by C, the Manager, Recoupment Tax



Section, Australian Taxation Office, Melbourne; a number of



documents were exhibited to that affidavit.



4. The unusual turn of events to which I have referred was



the dispatch to the applicant of a letter dated 10 October 1989



signed by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation which was in the



following terms:-



"TAXATION (UNPAID COMPANY TAX) ASSESSMENT ACT 1982



[N] PTY. LTD. (NOW DEFUNCT)



Reference is made to the notice issued to you on 15

July 1983, and to the copy of a copy notice of

assessment, served on you as a preliminary step

forward to the raising of a recoupment tax

assessment.



It is advised the facts and evidence surrounding the

issue of the notice on 15 July 1983, have recently

been reviewed. As a result of this review, it has

been determined the opinion formed, that yourself and

Messrs [X] and [Y] were likely to be liable for

recoupment tax in relation to the unpaid company tax

of [N] Pty. Ltd., was erroneous.



Accordingly, the notices of 15 July 1983, to yourself

and Messrs [X] and [Y], are deemed to have been

incorrectly issued and are not supported by the

relevant provisions of the abovementioned Act.



The 'Notices' issued on 15 July 1983, are hereby

withdrawn and it is confirmed your liability as to

recoupment tax in [N] Pty. Ltd., is nil."



5. Subsequently, on 18 December 1989 letters in similar



terms were sent by the Deputy Commissioner to the other two



persons to whom a copy of the notice of assessment of the



company's income and tax liability had been sent pursuant to



section 18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act.



6. In the affidavit sworn by C he stated that he held an



authorisation from the respondent to form opinions for the



purposes of section 18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act and was duly



authorised to make the affidavit on the respondent's behalf.



Paragraph 6 and 7 of the affidavit are as follows:-



"6. I have since that time reviewed the information

in the possession of the Commissioner and I have

formed the opinion that at the time of service

of the copy notices of assessment on upon the

Applicant and Messrs [Y] and [X] recoupment tax

was not likely to become payable by any of the

Applicant or Messrs [Y] and [X] in relation to

ordinary company tax or undistributed profits

tax that was payable or might become payable by

[N] Pty Ltd for the years of income ended 30

June 1971 and 1972.



7. I am also of the opinion that there is not now

any basis for service of copies of the notices

of assessment which were served on the Applicant

and Messrs [Y] and [X] and that none of those

persons is liable under TUCT for recoupment tax

in respect of [N] Pty Ltd for the years of

income ended 30 June 1971 and 1972."



7. The preliminary issue which the Tribunal was required to



address was whether or not it had power to review the assessment



of the company's income and income tax liability for the taxation



year which ended on 30 June 1972. Mr Nettle submitted, in



effect, that as a result of the action taken by the respondent in



October 1989 the applicant no longer had any interest in the



assessment of the company's income tax liability and could not



proceed with his application for review of the objection decision



in these proceedings. Mr Saunders, on the other hand, contended



that, once a person had been served with a copy of a notice of



assessment pursuant to section 18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act, section



18(8) gave him a right, which no subsequent act of the respondent



could take away, to object to the assessment and to pursue his



objection by way of proceedings in the Tribunal for review of the



respondent's decision in respect of it.



8. After hearing Mr Nettle and Mr Saunders and considering



C's affidavit I adjourned the hearing, taking the view that it



would at that stage be premature to decide whether or not the



Tribunal had power to hear and determine the deemed application



for review in these proceedings. I also gave directions intended



to ensure that, when the hearing was resumed, all relevant facts



would be before the Tribunal. In particular I directed that, if



the applicant did not accept that the opinions expressed by C in



paragraphs 6 and 7 of his affidavit were soundly based, he was to



lodge with the Tribunal and serve on the respondent at least 21



days before the date of the resumed hearing an affidavit or



affidavits setting out evidence showing that he and the other



persons named in those paragraphs were persons by whom vendors



recoupment tax was likely to become payable. I directed also



that, if the applicant lodged and served such an affidavit or



affidavits and the respondent still intended to maintain that the



copy of the notice of assessment should not have been served on



the applicant and the other two persons, not less than 14 days



before the date of the resumed hearing the respondent should



lodge with the Tribunal and serve on the applicant an affidavit



or affidavits stating which of the evidence contained in the



applicant's affidavit or affidavits was incorrect and setting out



such evidence as he relied on to establish that it was incorrect.



I directed further that, except with leave of the Tribunal, no



witness should give evidence at the resumed hearing unless an



affidavit sworn by him or her had been lodged and served in



accordance with one or other of the two previous directions.



9. Subsequently, directions hearings were held by telephone



in order to ascertain when the parties might be ready for the



hearing to proceed. I was informed by Mr Saunders that the



applicant and the other two persons on whom the copy of the



assessment had been served had been unable to locate any



documents relating to the company other than those which were in



the possession of the respondent. Mr Saunders and the person



representing the respondent also informed me that the respondent



had made available to Mr Saunders copies of all the documents



which were in his possession relating to the affairs of the



company relevant to its liability to income tax for the income



year which ended on 30 June 1972. Mr Saunders informed me



further that the material was not sufficient for him to be able



to use it as a foundation for an affidavit in the terms required



by the direction and that no affidavit would be lodged or served.



A date was then set for resumption of the hearing.



10. At the resumed hearing the applicant was again



represented by Mr Saunders and the respondent by Mr Nettle. They



confirmed that the information which had been given to me by Mr



Saunders and by the person representing the respondent in the



course of the telephone directions hearings was correct. Mr



Nettle submitted that the application for review should be



dismissed and the objection decision affirmed; Mr Saunders



submitted that the Tribunal should hear and determine the deemed



application for review of the objection decision. In addition to



relying on arguments presented at the initial hearing, he



submitted that it had not been reasonable for the Tribunal to



give a direction that the applicant was to lodge an affidavit



setting out evidence showing that he and the other two persons



were persons by whom vendors recoupment tax was likely to become



payable. However, he did not suggest that any material now



available to either party supported or had ever supported a case



that such likelihood existed.



11. In the statement of the reasons for the ruling and



directions which I gave after the initial hearing I wrote:-



"8. Section 18(5) and (8) are, so far as is relevant

in these proceedings, as follows:-



'18. (5) Where -



. . . . .



(b) the Commissioner is of the

opinion that vendors recoupment

tax is likely to become payable

by a person or persons in

relation to ordinary company tax

or undistributed profits tax, as

the case may be, that is payable

or may become payable by the

company in relation to that year

of income,



the following provisions have effect:



(c) the Commissioner shall serve a

copy of the notice of assessment

on -(i) where, in the opinion of

the Commissioner, the

vendors recoupment tax is

likely to become payable by

only one person - that

person; and



(ii) in any other case - any of

the persons referred to in

paragraph (b);



. . . . .



(8) Where a copy of a notice of

assessment is served under sub-section (5)

in relation to the liability of a company

to pay company tax in relation to a year of

income -



. . . . .



(b) if the copy was served under

sub-paragraph (5)(c)(ii) - one

person who -



(i) is included in the

representative class in

relation to the recoupment

tax referred to in

paragraph (5)(b); and



(ii) is nominated, by notice

signed by more than

one-half of the persons

included in that class,



has the same rights under Division 2 of

Part V of the Assessment Act in relation to

that liability as the company would have

had if the notice of assessment had been

served on the company on the date on which

the copy of the notice was served under

sub-section (5) and, for the purpose of the

exercise of those rights by the person, the

provisions of that Division apply in like

manner as those provisions would apply in

relation to the exercise of those rights by

the company.'



9. The Assessment Act referred to in section 18(8)

is the ITA Act. Division 2 of Part V of that Act

contains the provisions for lodging an objection

against an assessment (section 185), for requesting

reference of an objection decision to the Tribunal

(section 187) and for the referral of an objection

decision to the Tribunal to be deemed to constitute

the making by the taxpayer of an application to the

Tribunal to review the objection decision (section

189(2)).



10. In section 3(1) of the T(UCT)A Act 'vendors

recoupment tax' is defined as meaning tax assessed

under that Act and imposed by the Taxation (Unpaid

Company Tax - Vendors) Act 1982. The circumstances

in which vendors recoupment tax is to be assessed, so

far as the primary taxable amount is concerned, are

set out in section 5 of the T(UCT)A Act. A person in

respect of whom vendors recoupment tax may be

assessed pursuant to section 5 may also in certain

circumstances be liable to pay a secondary taxable

amount by virtue of section 6.



11. As has frequently been emphasized by the

Tribunal, the respondent not only has a power to

assess any tax payable by a person but he also has a

duty to do so if the facts as known to him, or as he

believes them to be, are such that by virtue of the

provisions of the ITA Act, or of any other Act such

as the T(UCT)A Act, tax is assessable. However, he

has no power to assess tax as payable when he knows

that it is not payable. It appears from C's

affidavit, although not expressly stated, that the

respondent has now been able to ascertain facts which

have caused him to conclude that vendors recoupment

tax is not likely to become payable by the applicant

and the other two persons on whom a copy of the

notice of the company's assessment was served. If he

has indeed ascertained such facts, he cannot now

properly assess any amount of vendors recoupment tax

as payable by any of those persons.



12. Mr Nettle submitted that the proper formation of

an opinion that vendors recoupment tax was likely to

become payable by those persons was a condition

precedent to serving on them a copy of the notice of

assessment of the company's tax liability. He based

that submission on the decision of the Tribunal in

Case W53 89 ATC 489 where it held that, in hearing

and determining a deemed application by a person

served with a copy of a notice of assessment under

section 18(5)(c), it was not restricted to deciding

only whether the Commissioner had formed such an

opinion as was referred to in section 18(5)(b). If

it found that such an opinion could not properly have

been formed on the facts as established before the

Tribunal, it should decide that the statutory

pre-condition for the service of the notice of

assessment had not been met, so that there had been

no effective service, and it should therefore set

aside the objection decision. Mr Nettle submitted

further that, if after serving a copy of a notice of

assessment pursuant to section 18(5)(c) the

respondent formed a new opinion that the previous

opinion was wrong, the service of the notice was to

be regarded as having been without effect.



13. In Duggan and Ryall v Federal Commissioner of

Taxation 72 ATC 4239 Stephen J. set aside assessments

made under section 99A of the ITA Act on the ground

that the Commissioner's opinion that it would be

unreasonable that the section apply in relation to a

trust estate in relation to a year of income was

vitiated by error. His Honour's view was that that

was the equivalent of failure to reach such an

opinion."



12. In that statement I noted that the Tribunal did not have



before it any evidence of the facts on which the respondent



formed either his original decision or his new opinion or indeed



evidence that he had in fact formed an original opinion before



serving a copy of the notice of assessment on the applicant and



the other two persons. The possibility existed that the facts



might support either one of two different opinions and that the



respondent had initially come to one of those opinions and had



subsequently changed his mind. It is now clear, however, that



the facts did not at any time constitute a proper foundation for



his forming an opinion that the applicant and the other two



persons were persons by whom vendors recoupment tax was likely to



become payable. That being so, the situation is analogous to



that which existed in Duggan and Ryall, that is to say that the



service of a copy of the notice of assessment was vitiated by the



absence of any proper basis for such an opinion having been



formed. In that regard it is on all fours with the decision of



the Tribunal in Case W53.



13. Because there never was any proper basis for service of



a copy of a notice of assessment on the applicant and the other



two persons, I am unable to accede to the submission by Mr Nettle



that the Tribunal should affirm the objection decision under



review. There was never any proper basis for the objection and



the objection decision itself was therefore a nullity. That



means that the assessment of the income and income tax liability



of the company in respect of the 1972 income year continues to



stand but has not been the subject of an objection by the



applicant or either of the other two persons and that no valid



objection decision has been made in respect of it. I have



decided, therefore, that the proper decision to make in these



proceedings is simply that the Tribunal does not have power to



review the objection decision in respect of the assessment of



that company's income tax liability for that year.
