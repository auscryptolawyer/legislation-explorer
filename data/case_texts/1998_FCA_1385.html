Anthony Henry May v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation for the Commonwealth of Australia [1998] FCA 1385 (2 October 1998)

Last Updated: 4 November 1998

CATEGORY: NO QUESTION OF PRINCIPLE

THE COURT ORDERS THAT:

1. Leave is granted to the applicant to amend the application by adding in paragraph 1 after sub-paragraph 1.2 thereof the following sub-paragraph:

"1.3 The categories of information described in items 5 and 6 in the Schedule to the Notice may only permissibly be sought pursuant to section 264(1)(b) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ."

2. A declaration is made that the notice dated 14 May 1998 served on the applicant by the respondent is valid save for items 1 and 8 in the schedule to the notice.

3. Until 4.00 pm on 30 October 1998 the respondent is restrained from taking any step to bring about the prosecution of the applicant for an offence under the Taxation Administration Act 1953 (Cth) for the reason that the applicant has failed to comply with any aspect of the said notice.

4. The applicant pay 90% of the respondent's costs of the application including reserved costs.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in O 36 of the Federal Court Rules

SUPPLEMENTARY REASONS FOR JUDGMENT

In my reasons for judgment handed down on 1 October 1998 I held that the notice dated 14 May 1998 served on the applicant by the respondent was valid save for items 1 and 8 in the schedule to the notice and that subject to hearing counsel on the appropriate form of order the applicant should have until 4.00 pm on 30 October 1998 to comply otherwise with the notice. I held that the applicant should not be required to provide the information sought in item 1 of the notice. In the course of hearing submissions on the form of orders which should be made, counsel for the respondent drew my attention to the fact that I had made my decision on item 1 on the basis of matters in respect of which the respondent had not been given the opportunity to address submissions. I think that is correct and consistently with principle and the observations in Autodesk Inc v Dyason (No 2) [1993] HCA 6; (1993) 176 CLR 300, I gave the respondent an opportunity to be heard on item 1 and to make any further submissions the respondent wished to make in relation to item 1.

Counsel for the respondent informed me that the respondent did not wish to make any further submissions in relation to item 1. Counsel for the applicant made no submissions in relation to the matter and it seems to me in those circumstances that my reasons for judgment in relation to item 1 should stand.

I expressed the tentative view in the reasons, subject to hearing from counsel, that the applicant should pay 90% of the respondent's costs. Mr Solomon, who appears for the applicant, submitted that the applicant should be paid his costs as he had succeeded in part in his challenge to the decision-making power of the respondent. Mr Solomon submitted that the respondent had not succeeded in showing that he lawfully exercised his power because of my findings that items 1 and 8 should not be required to be answered and should be severed from the notice.

It was said by Mr Solomon that the respondent sought to enforce matters which were beyond his power and that the applicant had shown that the power exercised by the respondent was exercised unlawfully as to part. Mr Solomon emphasised that it is the decision to exercise the power to serve the notice that was challenged. He submitted that as the applicant has, to that extent, successfully challenged the respondent's decision-making process, the process as to part has been vitiated.

It is true that I have found that items 1 and 8 need not be answered but that is because it cannot be said that they are couched in terms such that it can be said that the information sought is to enable the respondent to perform his functions under the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) . It is clear from Fieldhouse v Commissioner for Taxation [1989] FCA 397; (1989) 25 FCR 187 that in an appropriate case parts of a s 264 notice which are shown to be beyond power may be severed so that the remainder of the notice remains valid. The case before me was conducted on the basis that the notice need not be complied with and was invalid because the decision to serve it was vitiated by an improper purpose, an irrelevant consideration or an error of law.

It was argued in the alternative that various items in the notice were themselves invalid even though the decision to serve the notice may not have been vitiated. The major part of the hearing was taken up with submissions and argument in relation to the matters in respect of which I have found against the applicant. The matters in respect of which I found in favour of the applicant took up a relatively small part of the hearing, and in all the circumstances I consider it appropriate that the respondent not obtain the whole of his costs but obtain the major portion of them.

Having regard to the manner in which the proceeding was conducted, the issues which were raised and the substance of the reasons in my earlier reasons for judgment, I consider an appropriate order to make in the exercise of my discretion as to costs is that the applicant pay 90% of the respondent's costs of the application including reserved costs.
