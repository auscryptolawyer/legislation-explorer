ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

INCOME TAX - Full Court of the Federal Court - matter remitted to Administrative Appeals Tribunal for determination according to law - husband and wife - whether partnership existed - whether applicant a sole trader facts not in dispute - conclusion to be drawn from evidence in dispute - recognition of existence of a partnership profits distributed by husband in accordance with wishes or agreement of wife.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 S6(1)

Secondhand Dealers Act 1906 (WA)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION ) W86/3651-5

WT86/3655

Applicant

- and -

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION Respondent

DECISION

AAT Decision No 5705

Tribunal : Deputy President G L McDonald

Date : 7 February 1990

Place : Perth

DECISION

The Tribunal decides that the decision under review should be set aside and the matter be remitted to the respondent with a Direction that during the years of income ended 30 June 1978 to 30 June 1982 (inclusive) the taxpayer derived his assessable income in a partnership between the taxpayer and his wife within the meaning of that term in sub section 6(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 and his liability for income tax should be assessed accordingly.

(sgd) G L McDonald

DEPUTY PRESIDENT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION ) W86/3651-5

WT86/3655

Applicant

- and -

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

7 February 1990 Deputy President G L McDonald

This matter has been remitted to the Tribunal for determination following an appeal to the Federal Court (Jo1ley v Commissioner of Taxation (Full Federal Court unreported decision of 13. March 1989)).

The parties elected not to call any further evidence but both made further submissions to the Tribunal. The Tribunal had before it a transcript of the original proceedings and all of the exhibits tendered at that hearing.

The short point to be determined is whether the applicant carried on a business as a sole trader or in partnership with his wife for the tax years ending 30 June 1978-82 (inclusive).

S6(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") defines partnership as follows:

"'partnership' means an association of persons carrying on business as partners or in receipt of income jointly, but does not include a company;"

It was not in dispute that the business was being carried on with a view to making a profit. The question was whether the business was being carried on by the applicant or by the applicant and his wife in partnership.

There is no dispute about the evidence and whilst I have not had the advantage of hearing the parties I have read the transcript and seen the exhibits of the original proceedings before this Tribunal and references in this decision to the transcript and exhibits are references to the transcript and exhibits of the original proceedings. I have also had the benefit of the further submissions made by Counsel for both parties. I find the facts to be as follows.

After the applicant retired in early 1976 as a production supervisor with Shell Oil Company of Australia Ltd he commenced his own business of buying used oil drums and reselling them to a firm which reconditioned them. Subsequently it was agreed with the reconditioning firm that that work would be carried out on behalf of the applicant's business and the reconditioned drums resold by him to various distributors.

Soon after he commenced business and prior to June 1977 the applicant sought the advice of his accountant, Mr Mansell, as to the form in which he ought conduct his business and he was advised to establish a partnership with his wife. Following receipt of that advice he discussed it with his wife who "agreed to go along with it" (transcript of February 1988 page 6). His wife was to participate in the business, which was operated from their residence, by staying home in the mornings in order to take telephone calls from customers and "spotters" (ie those reporting the whereabouts of used oil drums). His wife was also involved with the mailing of accounts and the banking (transcript of February 1988 page 27). The applicant estimated his wife spent approximately 20 hours per week engaged in the business activities outlined above.

The business was operated by a joint bank account in the names of the applicant and his wife at least from 12 July 1976. Prior to that date the applicant had operated a business bank account in his own name.

Some time in 1980 the applicant's wife lent the business $5,000 on an unsecured basis and interest free "because I considered I was part of the set up" (transcript of 24 February 1988 page 9).

The parties did not register a business name but the accounts were prepared in the joint names of the applicant and his wife.

In June 1981 the applicant applied for and obtained in his own name a licence under the Second-Hand Dealers Act 1906 (WA). Applications for a Second-Hand Dealers licence are personal and are issued only if a magistrate is satisfied as to the applicant's character (S4 Second-Hand Dealers Act 1906}, A rubber stamp was thereafter prepared and titled "R.W. ..... Drum Dealer" which was applied to correspondence and invoices issued by the business. In fact, most receipts and purchase orders addressed to the business were in the name of the applicant's name of "R.W. ....." or "R. .....". However there were 4 receipts from Esso Australia Ltd issued in the names of "R.W. and J.C. ....." (exhibit 10 invoices 74791, 74790, 74355 and 74096). On appeal in relation to those receipts the majority of the Federal Court commented (at P5)

"The reason why the issue of receipts in names suggesting a partnership may be more significant than the existence of other trading records in one name only is plain. Apart from the fact that, so far as partnership law is concerned, invoicing in one name is not inconsistent with the existence of a partnership, a failure to use both names may be a mere matter of neglect. But receipts made out in two names by a person trading with the business strongly suggest that this party has been informed that the business was conducted as a partnerhsip".

It was the evidence of both the applicant and his wife that Mrs ..... either shared in or was entitled to receive half of the profits arising from the business. It appears however that the applicant invested nearly all of those profits in his own name but on trust in the interests of a family trust. Mrs ..... told the Tribunal that she agreed with the way in which the applicant was using the money for investment purposes (transcript 24 February 19B8 page 38).

The business was operated from the applicant's residential address and the phone was in the applicant's name alone (transcript 24 February 1988 page 37).

The relevant information with respect to the establishment of the partnership is set out in the majority of the Federal Court's decision at pages 6-9 (inclusive) consisting of the evidence in chief and cross examination of both Mr and Mrs ...... It is unnecessary for me to requote those passages in their entirety but I have read and considered them carefully. From them I conclude that there was discussion between Mr and Mrs ..... and agreement between them that they would enter into a partnership. Indeed the respondent did not seek to show that such a discussion and agreement had not taken place but rather asserted that an examination of the way in which the parties subsequently conducted their business did not reflect they were acting as partners. As the majority of the Federal Court noted on the appeal of this matter (page 13),

"It is a fine line of distinction that the respondent has argued".

The majority of the Federal Court proceeded to say,

"The establishment of mutual assent and intention is an essential element in demonstrating the existence of a partnership although it does not stand alone and must be assessed with all relevant circumstances including the conduct of the parties" (page 19 ) .

However the subsequent conduct of the parties must be viewed from the perspective that there was an expressed mutual intention to act in partnership. It is on that basis that I have proceeded to reach a decision.

The fact that the wife did not participate in the management of the partnership, the fact that only the husband obtained a Second-Hand Dealer's licence (rather than both the husband and wife obtaining such a licence), the fact that invoices and receipts were in the main issued in the husband's name, the fact that the wife made no capital contribution at the time she joined the partnership (as distinct from the subsequent loan that she made to the partnership), are all in my view equivocal in terms of determining whether the parties were acting in partnership. Indeed as the majority of the Federal Court pointed out in the appeal a partnership may exist notwithstanding that a partner may have no interest in anything other than the profits.

On the other hand, the fact that, apart from the parties agreeing to enter into a partnership, there was recognition of the existence of the partnership contained in the 4 Esso receipts, the fact that there was a joint bank account, the fact that the profits were distributed in accordance with the wishes or agreement of the wife (but by the husband) are matters which evidence the existence of a partnership.

In my view, the latter matters weigh more heavily in favour of finding that a partnership exists than do the former matters in suggesting that no partnership exists. I am therefore satisfied a partnership between the applicant and his wife existed throughout the years under consideration.

For those reasons, the application succeeds and the matter is to be remitted to the respondent with a direction that during the years of income ended 30 June 1978 to 30 June 19B2 (inclusive) the taxpayer derived his assessable income in a partnership between the taxpayer and his wife within the meaning of that term in sub-section 6(1) of the Act and his liability for income tax should be assessed accordingly.
