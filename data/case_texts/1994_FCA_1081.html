Joseph Gutwenger v the Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1994] FCA 1081; (1994) 94 Atc 4294 (12 May 1994)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

JOSEPH GUTWENGER v. THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF

AUSTRALIA

Nos. VG397 of 1991 and VG398 of 1991

FED No. 420/94

Number of pages - 23

Taxation

(1994) 94 ATC 4294

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

OLNEY J

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - referral by commissioner pursuant to s 187 of its decision to disallow applicant's objection against assessment of income tax - applicant's wife bought land - land subdivided and transferred to applicant - majority of lots sold within year of transfer - liability of applicant for income tax on sale proceeds - whether amounts assessed were taxable according to ordinary concepts and usages.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss 25, 25A, 26AAA, 160L(3)(b), 160ZA, 160ZH(9), 168, 187

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Whitfords Beach Pty Ltd [1982] HCA 8; (1981-2) 150 CLR 355.

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 10, 11, 14 and 15 February 1994

12:5:1994

Mr J. de Wijn (Instructed by Gledhill Burridge and Cathro) appeared for the applicant.

Mr G. Davies (instructed by the Australian Government Solicitor) appeared for the respondent.

DECISION

THE PROCEEDINGS

OLNEY J In proceeding VG 397 of 1991 the respondent has referred to the Court pursuant to s 187 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Act) therespondent's decision to disallow the applicant's objection against an assessment of income tax in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1987.

2. In proceeding VG 398 of 1991 the respondent has referred to the Court a similar decision in respect of an assessment of income tax for the year ended 30 June 1988.

3. The two proceedings involve identical issues and were heard together.

4. The hearing occupied four days, namely 10, 11, 14 and 15 February 1994.

5. On behalf of the applicant, affidavit evidence was adduced from the applicant, his wife (Mrs Gutwenger) and Mrs Gutwenger's brother David Barry Holloway (Holloway). Each of the deponents was cross-examined by counsel for the respondent.

6. The respondent relied upon an affidavit of Richard Neil Head who was not cross-examined.

7. A number of documents were tendered by consent without formal proof. BACKGROUND

8. The applicant is a German national and at all relevant times has resided in Germany. Mrs Gutwenger is Australian by birth but is resident in Germany. In 1971 whilst Mrs Gutwenger was working with the Australian Tourist Commission in Frankfurt, she met the applicant. They married in Australia in May 1973. The applicant had not previously visited Australia. Since their marriage the couple have resided in Germany but have visited Australia frequently on holiday.

9. At the time of his visit to Australia in May 1973, the applicant met, for the first time, Mrs Gutwenger's father (Mr Holloway Snr), her brother (Holloway) and her brother's wife, Karen Holloway.

10. For about two years after their marriage the applicant and Mrs Gutwenger resided in a rented apartment in Munich. They then moved to Frankfurt. In about 1978 they purchased their own apartment in Frankfurt which is owned jointly.

11. The applicant is, and at all relevant times has been, employed by Ferrero OHG mbH (Ferrero) a large confectionery company based in Frankfurt. During 1980 and 1981 he became dissatisfied with his job and had some private discussions with the Australian manager of Ferrero who was then wanting the transfer to a position in the United States. The applicant was interested in taking over as Australian manager if the position became vacant. As it happened the position did not become vacant. However, early in 1983 the applicant became aware that his career with Ferrero was about to improve dramatically. In September 1983 he was appointed the sole sales director for Germany and from 1 January 1985 has been a member of the board of directors of the company.

12. Mrs Gutwenger and her brother enjoy a very close relationship, which is shared by their respective spouses. Until his death in August 1983, Mr Holloway Snr was also part of the close family relationship. In 1974 when Mrs Gutwenger was ill, her father stayed with her in Italy for about five months. In October 1978 Mr Holloway Snr suffered a stroke and moved out of his house at Brighton and into a nursing home at Frankston. The Holloways resided in the Frankston area and the nursing home was in close proximity to their place of residence.

13. In the period from their marriage in 1973 until about 1983 it was the practice of the applicant and Mrs Gutwenger to visit Australia every year or so, usually at about Christmas time. Normally, Mrs Gutwenger would precede her husband. The applicant would normally return to Germany before his wife. Initially, the applicant and Mrs Gutwenger would stay with Mr Holloway Snr, but after he moved into the nursing home, they would stay with the Holloways. However, with the passage of time and as their two children grew up, they gave thought to purchasing a house of their own in the Frankston area. Apart from the fact that the Holloways lived in the area, and its proximity to the nursing home where Mr Holloway Snr lived after October 1978, the Frankston area had an added attraction for Mrs Gutwenger in that a close personal friend, Mrs Streader, whom she visited frequently when in Australia, also lived in Frankston. The Gutwengers had in mind a house where Mrs Gutwenger's father could stay with them during their visits to Australia.

14. Early in 1982 Mrs Gutwenger purchased about 20 acres of land in Overport Road Frankston. The land was later subdivided into 27 lots and transferred to the applicant. Within a year of the transfer, 20 of the lots were sold by the applicant. The remaining lots were sold within a relatively short time thereafter. These proceedings have to do with the question of whether, and to what extent, the applicant is liable for income tax on the sale proceeds.

THE PURCHASE OF THE LAND

15. In early January 1982, whilst on vacation at Frankston, the Gutwengers became aware of a property in Overport Road Frankston which was to be sold by auction late in January 1982. The property (Walsh's land) comprised about eight acres on which was erected a large house. From what they were able to ascertain from available information it appeared that the house could be adapted to accommodate both families, that is the Holloways and the Gutwengers. Alternatively, it was thought that a second house could possibly be built on the land to provide separate accommodation for each family.

16. As it happened, Walsh's land was withdrawn from sale before the auction and nothing more was done about trying to acquire it.

17. The applicant returned to Germany on 20 January 1982. Mrs Gutwenger remained for a further three weeks or so.

18. Shortly after returning to Germany, the applicant received a telephone call from Mrs Gutwenger who advised that the land adjacent to, and to the south of Walsh's land (Watson's land), was available for sale. Mrs Gutwenger had become aware of Watson's land as a result of a telephone call made to Karen Holloway by an estate agent (Mr Crowder) who had been the agent handling the proposed sale of Walsh's land and who had thereby become aware of the Gutwengers' interest in purchasing land in the area.

19. Watson's land comprised about 20 acres on which the only improvements were some fencing and a water tank. At its highest point the land provided a view across Port Phillip Bay and to Melbourne in the distance. The asking price was $175,000.

20. Mrs Gutwenger was impressed by Watson's land and thought it an ideal site on which to build a large family home. The applicant agreed that she should purchase it although he had no recollection of the land.

21. On 5 February 1982 Mrs Gutwenger signed a sale note whereby she agreed to purchase Watson's land for $175,000, Mrs Gutwenger is named in the sale note as the purchaser. An initial deposit of $1,000 was paid on signing the sale note and a further $4,000 was payable within 14 days. The balance of an agreed deposit of $50,000 was payable within 30 days. The balance of purchase price, namely $125,000, was payable on or before 6 February 1984, with interest at 12% payable quarterly. Possession was to be given on payment of the full deposit money.

22. The printed form of sale note used for the transaction contains the following:

This sale is conditional upon the land sold being now zoned

under -

(a) the Planning Scheme of the Melbourne and Metropolitan

Board of Works for

____________________________________________________________

_____________________________________ or,

(b) the Municipal Authority for ______________________

23. In the document as executed the words immediately following "(a)" were struck through, and immediately after the words printed in paragraph (b) the following was inserted -

City of Frankston. Residential "D".

24. The initial $5,000 deposit was paid by Mrs Gutwenger from funds that were available to her in Australia. The balance of deposit was remitted to Australia by the applicant from joint funds in Germany.

25. Mrs Gutwenger returned to Germany on 12 February 1982. Before doing so, she executed a Power of Attorney in favour of Holloway to enable him to complete the purchase of the land on her behalf.

26. The balance of purchase price was paid in February 1984 out of funds available to Mrs Gutwenger from her father's estate. The title was transferred into her name on 24 February 1984.

MRS GUTWENGER'S INTENTION

27. I accept Mrs Gutwenger's evidence to the effect that her intention in purchasing the land was to use it for the purpose of building on it either a single large house sufficient to accommodate both the Holloway family and her own (when in Australia) as well as providing the facility for her father to visit. Apart from Mrs Gutwenger's direct testimony which I accept as both credible and probative of the facts, there were a number of other aspects of the evidence which support it. First, the Gutwengers were not actively looking for land in 1982. It was only after they had seen an auction sign on Walsh's land that their interest was excited. When that sale was cancelled they took no other steps to acquire land. The initiative in relation to the purchase of Watson's land came from the agent who had become aware of their interest in purchasing land as a result of enquiries made concerning Walsh's land. The main attractions of Watson's land were first the location in an area of Frankston in proximity to the Holloway's home, to the nursing home where Mr Holloway Snr lived and to Mrs Gutwenger's close friend Mrs Streader; and second, the views over Port Phillip Bay and beyond that the land offered.

28. Without having any knowledge of the land apart from the fact that it was said to be adjacent to Walsh's land, the applicant was happy for his wife to purchase it.

29. It appears that no professional advice was sought at the time of purchase. Had the future development of the land into subdivisional lots been contemplated it would be reasonable to expect that some enquiry would have been made as to zoning and related matters. Furthermore, some investigation as to the cost of development and the likely market for the subdivided lots would have been made. All of these matters would be fundamental to the commercial exploitation of the land. But, it is the fact that no such investigations were made. Indeed, it appears that Mrs Gutwenger simply paid the price that was being asked for the land. Furthermore, nothing was done at the time to protect Mrs Gutwenger's interest as purchaser of the land by way of lodging a caveat against the title. The manner in which the land was acquired bore none of the hallmarks of a commercial enterprise. At the time the land was purchased the applicant and his wife were actively considering the prospect of taking up the residence in Australia. It was not until about a year later that they became aware that the applicant's long term future with Ferrero was to be in Germany.

30. All things considered, the manner in which Mrs Gutwenger went about acquiring the land is not indicative of a person acquiring property for the purpose of turning it to a profit. I have no hesitation in finding that Mrs Gutwenger purchased the land with the intention that it should be used as a site for a home for her immediate family and possibly for her brother and his family. I find that the land was not acquired by Mrs Gutwenger for the purpose of profit-making by sale.

THE ZONING OF THE LAND

31. As indicated above, the sale note executed by Mrs Gutwenger on 5 February 1982 contained a condition that the land be zoned as Residential "D". The question of zoning was not a matter to which Mrs Gutwenger adverted at the time of purchase.

32. The evidence tendered by the respondent in the form of an affidavit sworn by Richard Neil Head, a town planner employed by the City of Frankston, establishes that between 3 November 1971 and 30 October 1985 the land was governed by a planning scheme ordinance under which it was zoned Residential "D". The land is currently, and has been since 16 February 1988, zoned under a scheme by which it is designated Residential "4". The conditions which applied at the time the land was purchased were such that a detached house could not be erected on an allotment smaller than 0.26 of a hectare (unless the allotment was already in existence at 3 November 1971), only one detached house could be erected on an allotment (except for an attached flat via a permit from the council) and no building could be constructed to a height greater than two storeys except in accordance with a permit. Further, the land could not be subdivided into allotments smaller than 0.26 of a hectare. (It is common cause that 0.26 of a hectare is equal to two-thirds of an acre).

33. Head's evidence is silent as to the zoning restrictions applicable after 30 October 1985 and before the current scheme came into force on 16 February 1988. As he was not required for cross-examination it was not possible to question him as to the requirements which applied during that period. However, as the restrictions which have applied since February 1988 are substantially the same as those which applied between 1971 and October 1985, I infer that similar conditions applied between October 1985 and February 1988. The parties to the proceedings appear to have proceeded on the basis of that proposition although it was not formally conceded to be so.

THE DEVELOPMENT OF THE LAND

34. Several months after Mrs Gutwenger purchased the land, possibly in April or May of 1982, the applicant spoke to Holloway by telephone and asked him to investigate the possibility of being able to build two houses on the land and to ascertain whether there were any legal restrictions on building. Following this conversation Holloway visited the office of the City of Frankston. He says he was told that the area had been zoned for two-thirds of an acre lots and that he understood this to mean that it would be impossible to build a single house on the land, rather, the land had to be subdivided before it could be built on.

35. Subsequently, on or shortly prior to 2 September 1982, Holloway approached a firm of land surveyors and civil engineers, Breese Pitt Dixon Pty Ltd, to ascertain what could be done with the land. Enquiries were made by Breese Pitt Dixon including inquiries addressed to the State Rivers and Water Supply Commission of Victoria and the Frankston Sewerage Authority.

36. When Breese Pitt Dixon made their enquiries they sought advice as to the likely requirements that would be placed on a subdivision of the land into either 0.3 hectare lots, 1 hectare lots or 3 lots of approximately 2.5 hectares each. The State Rivers and Water Supply Commission replied by letter dated 14 September 1982 in which each of the three alternatives were specifically dealt with. A copy of the Commission's reply was sent to Holloway on 21 September 1982. The Frankston Sewerage Authority did not reply until 12 November 1982. The Authority did not go into the kind of detail that the Commission had done but rather simply said that it was likely that a sewerage requirement would be placed on any proposed subdivision of the land into lots of an area less than approximately 2.5 hectares. A copy of the Authority's reply was sent to Holloway on 18 November 1982. In their letter of 18 November 1982, Breese Pitt Dixon said, inter alia:

From the enclosed, and previous correspondence from the

State Rivers and Water Supply Commission, it would appear that

the requirements that would be placed on any subdivision

would be such that development was uneconomical.

37. Whatever understanding Holloway may have obtained from his verbal enquiries from the Frankston Council, there can be no doubt that by the end of November 1982 he had been made aware of the fact that it was not a zoning requirement that the land had to be subdivided into two-thirds of an acre lots before it could be built on. The nature of the responses obtained by Breese Pitt Dixon made it patent that there were alternatives to a two-third acre lot subdivision.

38. Late in 1982 Holloway advised the applicant by telephone that the land was zoned for two-thirds of an acre blocks of land but as the applicant was unsure of what that meant insofar as building on the land was concerned any decision about building was postponed until the Gutwengers' next visit to Australia.

39. During the early part of 1983, Mr Holloway Snr's health deteriorated and his prognosis was poor. Mrs Gutwenger returned to Australia on 18 June 1983. The applicant followed on 16 July 1983. It was apparent to Mrs Gutwenger upon visiting her father that Mr Holloway Snr did not have long to live. He died in August 1983.

40. In the latter part of July 1983 the applicant consulted a local estate agent one Abel about what could be done with the land. By that time the applicant had become aware that his career with Ferrero was about to take off and the idea that he may move to Australia was no longer a realistic prospect. The state of health of Mr Holloway Snr had put an end to the prospect that he would be able to stay with his daughter during her visits to Australia.

41. Mr Abel was of German origin and the applicant felt comfortable in speaking with him. Abel knew of the land and his initial response to the applicant was to the effect "Oh, you are the unlucky one who bought that property". Abel's advice was to contact the firm of O'Connor and Beveridge, who were civil engineers. The applicant took Abel's advice and contacted O'Connor and Beveridge at the firm's Frankston office where he spoke to Messrs Max Cooper and Phillip Charlton. He asked them what could be done with the land. Cooper suggested the possibility of subdividing it, and as this concept was foreign to the applicant he asked Cooper to explain what it involved. It appears that Cooper was already familiar with the land, he having investigated the possibility of subdivision on behalf of a previous potential owner. Cooper prepared a summary of estimated costs and a cash flow. According to his estimate it would cost about $491,000 to develop the land into 26 subdivisional lots over a period of 12 months. Cooper also explained that there were two particular matters which required attention before a subdivision could be approved. One related to drainage and the other had to do with the ownership of a private road along the southern boundary of the land.

42. Although it had all the appearances of an ordinary street, and was known as Somerset Road, the strip of land immediately adjacent to the southern boundary of the land was privately owned and remained registered in the name of Mr Watson, the person from whom Mrs Gutwenger had purchased the land. Before the land could be subdivided it was necessary for Somerset Road to be either declared a public road or brought into common ownership with Mrs Gutwenger's land.

43. Holloway was not present during the applicant's initial visit to O'Connor and Beveridge, but before returning to Germany, the applicant introduced him to Cooper and Charlton. The matter was left on the basis that O'Connor and Beveridge would see what could be done about solving the perceived problems confronting a subdivision and that in this process they would deal with Holloway on behalf of the owner. In addition, an arrangement was made for a Mr John Corp, an acquaintance of Holloway's, who was experienced in subdivisional developments, to assist in the role of project manager. O'Connor and Beveridge had some dealings with Corp in the early stages of their involvement but it seems that Corp played only a minor part in the overall process of subdividing the land.

44. On 6 August 1985 Mrs Gutwenger executed a general power of attorney in favour of Holloway authorising him to do on her behalf anything that she could lawfully authorise an attorney to do.

45. The process of acquiring the title to Somerset Road was a tortuous business. It is unnecessary to chronicle all of the steps that were taken. It is sufficient to record that it was not until October 1985 that the title to the road was acquired by Mrs Gutwenger and that in July 1986 the City of Frankston gazetted it as a public road. The drainage problem was also ultimately solved but not without a considerable degree of effort over a long period. An agreement was reached with the adjoining owner for the granting of a drainage easement. The subdivision of the land into 27 lots was dealt with in three stages. Stages 1 and 2 were approved by the City of Frankston on 18 April 1986 and stage 3 on 18 July 1986. Tenders were called for street construction in July 1986 and work started some weeks later. The subdivisional plans were registered at the Land Titles Office on 7 April 1987 when separate titles were issued for each lot. At that point in time the land was ready to be placed on the market for sale as 27 subdivided lots.

FINANCING THE DEVELOPMENT

46. Throughout the whole period from the acquisition of the land by Mrs Gutwenger in February 1982 until the land was transferred to the applicant, Holloway held a power of attorney from Mrs Gutwenger which enabled him to act on her behalf in relation to the acquisition and development of the land. One of Holloway's tasks was to arrange finance in Mrs Gutwenger's name for the development.

47. The Gutwengers are, and at all relevant times were, people of some substance. The applicant holds a position which attracts a very substantial salary. Nevertheless, it was decided that it would be prudent, for the most part, for the cost of developing the land to be financed with funds borrowed in Australia.

48. In about May 1986 Holloway approached the Westpac Bank to enquire about finance. By letter dated 8 May 1986 the District Commercial Manager of Westpac at Frankston advised Holloway that the bank would be prepared to consider an application for $600,000 to assist with the subdivision. The bank recommended a commercial bill facility over a period of 12 months. An application for finance was duly made by Holloway on behalf of Mrs Gutwenger on 9 May 1986 but no further action was taken until September 1986 when a valuation was obtained for security purposes and on 30 September 1986 the bank formally approved finance as a commercial bill facility for 12 months. A mortgage in favour of Westpac was executed on behalf of Mrs Gutwenger and same was registered against the title to the land on 3 November 1986.

49. Correspondence put in evidence shows that from time to time commercial bills were discounted and the proceeds credited to an account in the name of Mrs Gutwenger. The costs associated with the development were paid out of the same account on which Holloway had authority to operate. There is no evidence as to the precise amount of finance actually provided by the bank but it appears to have been somewhat less than $600,000. In addition to the borrowed funds, other funds were provided from the Gutwengers' joint funds in Germany. In due course, following the transfer of the land to the applicant, as and when sales of lots were settled the bank progressively discharged its mortgage and credited the sale proceeds in reduction of the liability due to the bank by Mrs Gutwenger. On 28 August 1987 the bank agreed to totally discharge the mortgage as (according to an internal bank memorandum) all liability under it "had long been cleared from proceeds of settlements".

50. An internal bank record of Holloway's first approach to Westpac on 2 May 1986 records the following:

Mrs Judith Ann Gutwenger, a non resident Australian (resides

permanently in Frankfurt West Germany) is the owner of 20 acres at

Overport Rd., Frankston.

sub-division of the property into 27 2/3 acre lots. Plan has been

sealed by the Council and Mr Holloway is now in the stage of seeking

funds to cover subdivision costs.

Estimates have been prepared by O'Connor and Beveridge Williams and Co

Consulting Engineers, and total costs are set at $690 approx.

Mrs Gutwenger has $100 on deposit at Karingal and is therefore

seeking $590 to cover the 3 stages of the subdivision.

Estimated income from sale of land -

Lots 1-9 say $80 each = $720

Lots 10-14 say $75 = $375

Lots 15-22 say $70 = $560

Lots 23-27 say $55 = $275

David Holloway seeking indication from Bank that funds would be

available. Funding by way of Commercial Bills was then explained to

Mr Holloway.

Proposed Limits

BDL $600

12 months with clearance in full from sale of subdivisional land.

Lots 1-9 will be sufficient to clear debt.

Fees

E/F .5%

SECURITY

Offered - Reg'd T/L/A M'tge over 20 acres, Overport Rd.,

Frankston

Subdivided value estimated - $1500

51. Subsequently, when Holloway arranged for a valuation to be provided from John Crowder and Sons Pty Ltd, a local firm of estate agents and property consultants, the valuer (Mr G.K. Crowder) estimated that the gross realisation from the sale of the 27 lots would be $2,075,000.

52. The applicant's income tax returns show (and it is common ground) that in the 1987 financial year 12 lots were sold for a total of $1,065,500; and in the 1988 year a further 14 lots were sold for a total of $1,030,113. (Only lot 11 remained unsold as at 30 June 1988).

53. The gross realisation from the 26 lots sold to 30 June 1988 was therefore $2,095,613.

54. There is a remarkable consistency between the "estimated income from sale" recorded in the bank record made on 2 May 1986 ($1,930,000), the valuer's estimated gross realisation in the valuation dated 15 September 1986 ($2,075,000), and the actual gross proceeds of the 26 lots sold in the two financial years ($2,095,613). This consistency demonstrates that when Holloway called on the bank on 2 May 1986 he was not some kind of novice dabbling in real estate on his sister's behalf. He had clearly in one way or another, undisclosed by the evidence, obtained advice as to the commercial viability of the proposal to subdivide the land and was well aware that the development of the land had the potential to yield a profit in excess of $1,000,000. It is incomprehensible that Holloway would not have passed on this information to his sister and her husband.

THE TRANSFER TO THE APPLICANT

55. In her affidavit Mrs Gutwenger says (at paragraph 23):

Up until early 1987 my husband and I had made no firm decision on

what to do with the sub-divided lots and one of the possibilities

which we seriously considered and discussed was building houses to

let. However in early 1987 my husband and I decided to sell the

lots at which stage I had decided that I really did not want to be

involved in decisions concerning the sale of the lots as the thought

of selling the land was really quite depressing. Consequently I

decided that I would transfer by way of gift all of the land to my

husband. This was effected by a transfer of the land which I signed

in March 1987.

56. The applicant deals with the same subject matter at paragraph 18 of his affidavit thus:

Before 1987 Judith had not made any firm decisions about what she

would do with the land when it was sub-divided, however we did

seriously consider building houses on the land to let. In early

1987 Judith and I for the first time decided to sell the lots. She

simply did not want to be involved in decisions about the sale of

the sub-divided lots and transferred the land to me by way of gift.

This occurred in March 1987 I then instructed Crowders a firm of

estate agents to sell the lots on my behalf. I also gave David

Holloway a Power of Attorney to act on my behalf, a true and correct

copy of the Power of Attorney is now produced and shown to me and

marked with the letters 'JG2'.

57. Having had the opportunity to hear both witnesses cross-examined I do not accept their evidence on this issue. There is an overwhelming preponderance of evidence to demonstrate that from mid-1983 onwards a decision had been made to sell the land as subdivided lots. This was the basis upon which Holloway sought finance for the project and it was the basis upon which finance was granted by Westpac.

58. Given the high hopes she had when the land was purchased, and the unfortunate circumstance of her father's death which in effect removed one of the primary reasons for the plan she had originally contemplated, it is understandable that Mrs Gutwenger may have regarded the idea of selling the land depressing, but I am satisfied that the decision to sell was made well before early 1987. Mrs Gutwenger had not at any stage been actively involved in the process of subdividing the land. From the very outset she had acted through her brother as her attorney. There is no evidence that she had ever made any decision concerning the land although I do not doubt that her husband and brother had from time to time kept her informed. Had she retained the land in her own name she would have had no more involvement in the sale of the lots than she had had in relation to its subdivision. Indeed, once the land was transferred to the applicant, Holloway continued to act under a power of attorney from the applicant in the same way as before, but as the applicant's attorney rather than as Mrs Gutwenger's.

59. The decision to transfer the land to the applicant was obviously conveyed to Holloway. On 6 March 1987 Holloway's solicitors Messrs James J. McCarthy and Associates wrote to him advising, inter alia, as follows:

We refer to our recent telephone discussions in relation to the

proposed Transfer of the premises to Dr Joseph Gutwenger.

60. We confirm that this Transfer can be carried out without Victorian Government stamp duty being applicable on the basis of it being a Transfer between husband and wife which is exempt from stamp duty in Victoria. There will be some delay however because the Transfer will need to be lodged with the Comptroller of Stamps for opinion and it will be necessary to obtain Declarations from Mr and Mrs Gutwenger that they were married and are still married to one another.

61. And on 18 March 1987 the solicitors wrote directly to the applicant and Mrs Gutwenger in these terms:

Dear Mr and Mrs Gutwenger,

re: Transfer - Gutwenger to Gutwenger

Sale of Land at Mount Eliza in Victoria

We have received instructions through the Attorney of Judith Ann

Gutwenger, Mr David Holloway, to prepare a Transfer in accordance

with the enclosed documents. The purpose of the enclosed Transfer

is to transfer the freehold land currently in the name of Judith Ann

Gutwenger to her husband Josef Gutwenger. Your execution of these

documents will be construed by this firm as being instructions in

order to complete this transaction in its entirety on the

instructions of Mr Holloway.

Please find enclosed the following documents:

1. Transfer of Land.

2. Declaration.

3. Power of Attorney.

The Transfer of Land should be signed by each of you where indicated

by the pencil crosses with your usual signatures in the presence of

independent adult witnesses. The Declaration must be made by you

both jointly before a Commissioner for taking Affidavits (or his

German equivalent).

The Power of Attorney for Josef Gutwenger is to be executed in the

presence of an independent adult witness.

All of these documents should be immediately returned to this office

so that we can attend to the necessary Victorian Government stamping

procedures.

Yours faithfully,

JAMES J. McCARTHY and ASSOCIATES

62. The transfer is expressed to have been made in consideration of "a gift between husband and wife" and was executed on 24 March 1987. On the same day, the applicant executed a power of attorney in favour of Holloway whereby he authorised Holloway to do on his behalf anything that he may lawfully authorise an attorney to do in relation to the sale, disposal or any other dealings in relation to the land.

63. The transfer was registered at the Titles Office on 9 April 1987, two days after the registration of the subdivisional plans, and two days before the offering for sale at public auction of the first 10 lots.

64. In order to register the transfer it had been necessary for the bank to give its consent. The consent was duly given on the basis that the debt to the bank did not increase and that no further funds could be drawn without a new mortgage being executed. Notwithstanding the transfer of the land into the applicant's name the liability to the bank remained in the name of Mrs Gutwenger and same was subsequently satisfied by proceeds from the sale of the subdivided lots by the applicant. There is no evidence of any specific arrangements having been made as between the applicant and his wife concerning Mrs Gutwenger's liability to the bank. From the facts established I draw the inference that the applicant accepted the transfer of the land subject to an understanding that he would discharge Mrs Gutwenger's liability to the bank from the sale proceeds.

65. There is no evidence to suggest that the gift of the land was solicited by the applicant or that it was made pursuant to any preconceived plan or arrangement between the Gutwengers. Nor is there any evidence to suggest that any advice was obtained in advance as to the taxation or other consequences that may flow from the gift apart from the advice that Holloway's solicitor gave concerning stamp duty.

66. Until she transferred the land to her husband, Mrs Gutwenger was clearly the beneficial owner of the land. It had been purchased in her name. She had paid the initial deposit of $5,000. Apart from the balance of the deposit ($45,000) which was paid from joint funds remitted from Germany, she had paid the balance of purchase price from her own resources in Australia. Some of the interest payments were remitted from Germany but later interest was paid out of Mrs Gutwenger's funds in Australia. To a substantial extent the cost of the subdivision was met from money borrowed in her name, with the balance paid out of joint funds from Germany. At all times prior to 24 March 1987 Holloway had acted as her agent pursuant to powers of attorney granted by her. He had incurred liabilities in her name and had met them from funds drawn from her account.

67. Throughout this whole process the applicant had taken an active interest in what was being done. Clearly, Mrs Gutwenger relied upon her husband's advice and assistance. But until he became the owner of the land the applicant's role was consistent with the role that may be played by many husbands in like circumstances. His involvement prior to March 1987 does not give rise to any inference that he had, or expected to receive, any proprietary right in the land or to the proceeds from its sale. Whilst the course of conduct engaged in by Mrs Gutwenger, the applicant and Holloway from mid-1983 onwards may be indicative of Mrs Gutwenger being involved in the carrying out of a profit-making undertaking or scheme in relation to the land, there is no basis upon which it can be said that the applicant was similarly involved. At least, not until after the land was transferred to him.

THE APPLICANT'S 1987 AND 1988 INCOME TAX RETURNS

68. On 17 June 1988 the respondent issued a notice of assessment of income tax in the name of the applicant which was purported to have been issued pursuant to s 168 of the Income Tax Assessment Act. A taxable income of $1,110,111 was shown in the notice in relation to the year ending 17 June 1988. Tax was assessed in the sum of $538,659. A notice of objection was lodged on behalf of the applicant. In August 1988 the applicant lodged income tax returns for the years ending 30 June 1987 and 1988. The 1987 return contains the following statement:

PROFIT/LOSS ON SALE OF LAND

During the year of income the taxpayer disposed of land which had

been acquired on 24th March 1987 for no consideration by way of gift

from Judith Ann Gutwenger. The land was originally acquired by

Judith Gutwenger on 24th March 1982 for the purpose of building a

residence. Judith Gutwenger decided not to proceed with building

the residence when her father who lived in Frankston, where the land

is situated, died.

As the land was acquired for no consideration the cost base of the

land for capital gains tax purposes will be the market value of the

land at the time of acquisition.

The land gifted consisted of Lots 1 to 27 of subdivision Nos LP

206079C, LP 2063667 and LP 206367W being part of the land described

in Certificate of Title Volume 6694 Folio 713. No formal valuation

was obtained at the time of the gift however estimates of the market

values of the lots were received from various estate agents as

indicated on schedule 1.

As the estimates were received in January 1987 and as real estate

values were rising at this time, they are likely to be lower than

the actual values on 24th March, 1987. Consequently as the land

which was sold in the 1987 year of income was sold within a short

time after the gift it is considered that the consideration received

on the sale of each block is a closer approximation of the market

value at the time the land wasgifted. Consequently, when

commissions etc are taken into consideration no profit will have

resulted.

69. The schedule referred to in the above statement gives particulars of estimated values of the 27 lots obtained from five named estate agents. In four cases the values are shown as a single sum whereas in one case a range is shown. For each lot an average of the valuations has been calculated.

70. The statement is followed by particulars in relation to the sale of 12 lots. In each case the deemed cost of the lot is shown as the same amount as the gross sale price and in each case after deducting agents' commission, solicitors' fees and other adjustments, a net loss is shown. The total net loss claimed for the financial year was $35,956.

71. The applicant's 1988 return contains the following statement:

PROFIT ON SALE OF LAND

Refer details contained in Income Tax Return year ended 30th June,

1987.

For the purposes of calculating any losses in the 1988 return of

income the taxpayer has used the estimates of value obtained in

January, 1987. Although these estimates are likely to be lower than

the actual market value on 24th May, 1987 it can be seen that no

profit resulted from the sales by the taxpayer.

72. The statement is followed by particulars of the sale of 14 lots. In each case the deemed cost is shown as the average of the valuations set out in the schedule to the 1987 return. After deducting agents' commission, solicitors' fees and other adjustments a net capital loss of $76,158 is claimed.

73. On 21 December 1989 the respondent issued an amended assessment for the year ended 30 June 1988 and an assessment for the year ended 30 June 1987.

74. The assessment for 1987 included as assessable income the sum of $660,204 being "profit on sale of land subdivision in the 1987 financial year". The taxable income was adjusted to $624,248 (after deducting the loss of $35,256 returned by the applicant), and tax was assessed in the sum of $349,119.01.

75. The amended assessment for 1988 adjusts the original taxable income of $1,110,111 by deducting the sum of $584,728 "to reflect profit on sale of land subdivision in the 1988 financial year", leaving a taxable income of $525,383 on which tax in the sum of $252,142.67 was assessed.

76. Neither the 1987 assessment nor the amended assessment for 1988 is accompanied by any particulars indicating the precise details of the calculations made by the respondent in assessing the taxable income for those years.

THE APPLICANT'S CASE

77. The notices of objection lodged by the applicant are couched in the widest possible terms, and necessarily so, as the respondent's assessments were not accompanied by detailed particulars. However, the applicant's case is more precisely particularised in his response to the respondent's request for particulars of the grounds of objection. Apart from the money sums involved, the particulars for the two years of income are the same.

78. In summary, the applicant's case is that the amounts assessed were not taxable income under any provision of the Act and should not be included as assessable income.

79. It is said that the amounts assessed do not represent a profit derived by the applicant during the year of income, further no part of any profit derived by the applicant during the year of income in connection with the sale of the land was income according to ordinary concepts and usages and is not deemed to be assessable income under any of the provisions of the Act.

80. The applicant denied both that the land realized during the year of the income was trading stock and that it was acquired by him as part of or in the course of any business carried on by him. Further, he said theland was not acquired for the purpose of sale and neither the acquisition nor the selling of the land was a part of any business of the applicant.

81. The applicant further denied that he had carried on during any material time a business of, or which involved the buying and selling of land and the land was not procured, manufactured, acquired or purchased by the applicant for the purpose of sale or exchange.

82. It was further asserted that as the land was acquired by the applicant by way of gift on or about 24 March 1987, it could not be disposed of so as to give rise to a profit which would be assessable under any provisions of the Act, and further that the provisions of s 25A of the Act have no application. Nor are the amounts in question assessable under ss 26AAA(2) of the Act notwithstanding that part of the land may have been sold within twelve months of acquiring it by way of gift. The applicant says that if the realization of a gift within twelve months of its acquisition can give rise to a profit so as to be assessable under s 26AAA(2) of the Act (which is denied) such profit should properly be ascertained by deducting from the net sale proceeds of any of the land sold prior to 24 March 1988, the value of the land as at 24 March 1987 and the alleged assessable income ought to be recalculated accordingly.

83. Alternatively, it is said that if any profit in respect of the sale of the land is assessable income of the taxpayer under s 25 or any other provision of the Act such profit should be calculated by deducting from the net sale proceeds of the land the value of the land as at 24 March 1987.

84. It is the applicant's case that any liability to income tax in respect of the acquisition and disposal of the land should be determined solely under Part IIIA of the Act and that the land should properly be deemed to have been acquired at market value pursuant to s 160ZH(9) of the Act.

THE RESPONDENT'S CASE

85. The respondent has supplied particulars of the grounds upon which the applicant's objections were disallowed. Apart from the money sums involved the particulars are the same for each of the two years of income in question.

86. The respondent asserts that the respective amounts assessed are assessable income of the applicant pursuant to s25(1), s 25A and/or s 26AAA of the Act.

87. The particulars supplied by the respondent were in response to particular paragraphs of the notices of objection lodged by the applicant. Set out below are the relevant portions of the particulars furnished in respect of the 1988 assessment:

1. The sum of $525,383 was income according to ordinary concepts

and usages within the meaning of s 25(1) of the Act because:

(a) the said sum was the profit derived by the applicant in the

1988 year of income from causing, procuring or soliciting,

with the intention of deriving a profit thereby, property to

be purchased in the name of his wife, the property to be

subdivided and the subdivided lots to be transferred by gift

into the name of the Applicant and then sold at a profit;

(b) alternatively, profit of not less than $525,385 was derived

by the applicant in the 1988 year of income from causing,

procuring or soliciting with the intention of deriving a

profit thereby, property registered in his wife's name to be

subdivided and the subdivided lots to be transferred by gift

into the name of the applicant and then sold at a profit;

(c) alternatively, profit of not less than $525,385 was derived

by the applicant in the 1988 year of income by causing,

procuring or soliciting, with the intention of deriving a

profit thereby, the subdivided lots, registered in his

wife's name, to be transferred by gift into his own name and

then sold at a profit.

2. (a) Profit of equal to or not less than $525,385 was derived by

the applicant in the 1988 year of income from the carrying

on or carrying out of a profit making undertaking or scheme

within the meaning of s 25A of the Act which was constituted

by the applicant causing, procuring or soliciting with the

intention of deriving a profit thereby:

(i) property to be purchased in the name of his wife,

the property to be subdivided and the subdivided

lots to be transferred into his own name and then

sold at a profit;

(ii) alternatively, property registered in his wife's

(iii) alternatively, subdivided lots, registered in his

(b) Profit of not less than $525,385 was derived by the

3. The acquisition of the subdivided lots by gift was a purchase

within the meaning of s 26AAA of the Act. Accordingly, the

profit derived from sales of the subdivided lots within twelve

months of the date on which they were gifted to the applicant is

assessable under s 26AAA. There is no provision in the Act

which permits or requires the profit assessable under s 26AAA to

be ascertained by deducting from the net sale proceeds the

market value of the lots as at the date of the gift to the

4. There is no provision in the Act which permits or requires that

the amount of the profit which is assessable under s 25, s 25A

and/or s 26AAA should be calculated by deducting from the net

sale proceeds of the lots the value of the lots at the date of

the gift to the applicant.

5. By virtue of s 160L(3)(b) and s 160ZA of the Act, Part IIIA is

inapplicable because the profit is assessable under s 25(1), s

25A and/or s 26AAA of the Act.

THE APPLICATION OF PART IIIA

88. Part IIIA of the Act (Capital Gains and Capital Losses) applies in respect of the disposal of certain assets acquired on or after 20 September 1985. The applicant says that the provisions of Part IIIA should be applied in relation to the proceeds from the sale of the land.

89. The respondent refers to two provisions contained within Part IIIA which have a bearing upon its application. First, s 160L(3)(b) provides that Part IIIA does not apply in respect of a disposal of an asset if as a result of the disposal an amount has been or will be included in the assessable income of the taxpayer by virtue of s 26AAA. Second, s 160ZA provides to the effect that Part IIIA does not apply to a capital gain which is or will be included in the assessable income of the taxpayer under a provision of the Act other than a provision of Part IIIA.

90. It follows from the above that before the provisions of Part IIIA are applied, it is necessary to determine whether the amounts in question are assessable under some other provision, for example, under s 25, 25A or s 26AAA.

INCOME ACCORDING TO ORDINARY CONCEPTS AND USAGES (S 25).

91. The applicant denies that any return from the sale of the land is properly to be regarded as income according to ordinary concepts and usages. The respondent's case is to the contrary and in the particulars of the grounds for rejecting the applicant's objections, the respondent relies in this context upon various assertions to the effect that the applicant caused, procured or solicited, with the intention of deriving a profit thereby, the purchase and subdivision of the land and the transfer of same into his own name.

92. The evidence does not support many of the assertions of fact contained in the respondent's particulars. I have already expressed my findings as to the intention Mrs Gutwenger had at the time she purchased the land and to her subsequent development of the land. There is no evidence to suggest that the applicant either caused, procured or solicited the purchase of the land by his wife. The idea was clearly her own. The applicant had never seen the land. His only involvement in the decision to purchase was to agree with Mrs Gutwenger that she should do so. At the time of purchase neither Mrs Gutwenger nor the applicant had any profit-making intention with respect to the land. And there is certainly no evidence to suggest that before early 1987 either party had the intention that the land should be transferred to the applicant, nor that the applicant solicited the transfer. However, I think that whilst the language used may not be entirely apt it can reasonably be said that by being a party to the transfer of 24 March 1987 the applicant "caused" the land to be transferred into his own name.

93. In his final submissions, counsel for the respondent identified the respondent's primary submissions as relying on s 25(1), the contention being that the profit derived by the applicant in 1987 and 1988 arose from a business of dealing in land. The issue was put on this basis. Did the applicant's profit result from the mere realisation of a capital asset or did it result from an activity in the nature of a trade or business? If the answer to the second question posed is in the affirmative, then the profit is properly to be regarded as being income according to ordinary concepts and usages and thus assessable as income under s 25(1). Counsel for the applicant took issue with the manner in which this submission was formulated in that it did not appear to fall comfortably within any of the particulars pleaded. Whilst there may be some merit in the applicant's complaint there was no question that the primary issue between the parties has always been whether the applicant's profits were income assessable under s 25 and clearly if the applicant had "caused" the land to be transferred into his own name with the intention that it be sold at a profit, then it may be proper to treat him as having been engaged in an activity in the nature of a business.

94. On the view I have taken, the facts found establish that prior to March 1987 the applicant's involvement with the land was not personal, but rather was on behalf of his wife. He had until that time no expectation that he would profit by the sale of the land. However, in the period since mid-1983 the land had been subdivided with the intention to sell it at a profit, albeit the intention then being that of Mrs Gutwenger. It was only after the transfer in March 1987 that the applicant's activities were conducted on his own behalf. By that time, the land had been subdivided and arrangements were in hand for an auction of part of the land to be held on 11 April 1987.

95. Although the transfer of the land from Mrs Gutwenger to the applicant has been called a gift it was not in fact made without consideration. Certainly, the transfer document identifies the consideration for the transfer as "a gift between husband and wife" and there is no evidence to suggest that any consideration passed between the parties to the transfer other than the acceptance by the applicant of the responsibility to discharge Mrs Gutwenger's liability to Westpac out of the sale proceeds. I have earlier referred to this matter and have found by inference from the facts that the transfer was made conditional upon the applicant accepting that liability. That being the case the transfer should not properly be regarded as a gift but rather that the applicant should be treated as having given valuable consideration for the land to the extent that he undertook to discharge the debt to the bank.

96. The applicant was under no obligation to accept the transfer from his wife, but having done so, and having, as I have found, undertaken to discharge Mrs Gutwenger's liability to the bank, he continued with the process of putting the land on the market for sale. The arrangements for the initial auction were already in place and the sale was but a couple of weeks off. Indeed, the particulars accompanying the applicant's 1987 income tax return show that lot 22 was sold by the applicant on 23 March 1987 "in anticipation of the gift on 24 March 1987".

97. The characterisation of the proceeds of the sale of an asset as income depends upon the application of a well established test. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Whitfords Beach Pty Ltd [1982] HCA 8; (1981-2) 150 CLR 355 Gibs CJ said at p 367:

The question whether the profits were income within ordinary

concepts depends on the application of the tests laid down in

Californian Copper Syndicate v. Harris (1904) 5 Tax Cas 159 and

in the cases that have followed that decision. Was what was done

merely a realization of the taxpayer's asset, or was it something

done in what was truly the carrying on or carrying out of a

business? In other words the question is "whether the facts

reveal a mere realization of capital, albeit in an enterprising

way, or whether they justify a finding that the "(taxpayer) "went

beyond this and engaged in a" (business of profit-making) "in land

albeit on one occasion only": see McClelland v. Federal

Commissioner of Taxation (1970) 120 CLR at p 496 where however the

words used are "a trade of dealing in land"; the words which I

have ventured to substitute seem more consonant with the

Australian authorities. The words "merely" and "mere" in these

statements seem to me to be an important part of the definition of

the line between profits that are taxable and those that are not.

If the taxpayer does no more than realize an asset, the profits

are not taxable. It does not matter that the taxpayer goes about

the realization in an enterprising way, so to secure the best

price. As I have said in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v.

Williams (1972) 127 CLR at p 249:

"The situation is not altered by the fact that the landowner

seeks and acts upon the advice of an expert as to the best

method of subdivision and sale or by the fact that he

carries out work such as grading, levelling, road building

and the provision of reticulation for water and power to

enable the land to be sold to its best advantage".

Further, the mere magnitude of the realization does not convert it

into a business: Commissioner of Taxes v. British Australian

Wool Realization Association (1931) AC 224 at p 252. But if the

taxpayer does engage in an operation of business, the proceeds are

income and taxable.

I do not understand subsequent authorities to say anything to the contrary.

98. Unlike the relatively casual and unbusinesslike process whereby Mrs Gutwenger originally acquired the land, the applicant's response upon the acquisition of it in his name was both immediate and decisive. He acquired the land in a state ready for sale; arrangements had already been made to auction lots 1 to 10; he had presold lot 22; the auction was duly held on 11 April 1987 and a number of lots were sold; thereafter he caused the land to be marketed effectively so that by 30 June 1988 all but one of the 27 lots had been disposed of. All of these facts are indicative of something more than a mere realisation of an asset.

99. There can be no other explanation than that the applicant acquired the land with the intention of selling it at a profit. Although it was a one-off transaction, it falls comfortably within the concept of carrying on a business of dealing in land. There is no question of the applicant having merely realised a capital asset.

100. In my opinion any profit which accrued to the applicant from the sale of the land should properly be regarded as income according to ordinary concepts and usages. The applicant's profit from the sale of the land is assessable under s 25(1) of the Act.

SECTION 25A

101. As an alternative, the respondent relies upon s 25A(1) of the Act. In view of the conclusion I have reached in relation to the assessability of the profits under s 25 it is not essential to deal with the respondent's alternative argument but I propose making some short comments.

102. The relevant portions of s 25A are as follows:

25A(1A) This section does not apply in respect of the sale of

property acquired on or after 20 September 1985.

25A(1) The assessable income of a taxpayer shall include profit

arising from the sale by the taxpayer of any property acquired by

him for the purpose of profit-making by sale, or from the carrying

on or carrying out of any profit-making undertaking or scheme.

The property in question is the 27 lots transferred to the applicant on 24 March 1987.

103. As the property was acquired by the applicant after 20 September 1985, the section does not apply to the sale of the property.

104. The respondent says that in the present case, the applicant had been carrying out a profit-making undertaking or scheme since mid-1983, thus well prior to 20 September 1985, and that subsection (1A) has no application in those circumstances.

105. In my view, this submission must fail for two reasons. First, on the facts found the applicant was not carrying out a profit-making undertaking or scheme prior to his acquisition of the land and accordingly, even apart from subsection (1A) the section has no application. Second, even if that be not so, any profit- making undertaking or scheme which the applicant carried out was in respect of the sale of the land by himself and accordingly, as he acquired the land after 20 September 1985, the section can have no application in respect of such sale.

SECTION 26AAA

106. As a second alternative, the respondent relies upon the provisions of s 26AAA of the Act. So far as relevant that section provides:

26AAA(1A) This section does not apply to a sale of property, or

of an interest in property, that occurs after 25 May 1988

(including a sale that occurs after that date but is required by

subsection (3) or (3A) to be treated as if it had occurred on or

before that date).

26AAA(1) For the purposes of this section -

(a) ...

(b) ...

(c) an arrangement or understanding, whether formal or informal

and whether express or implied, shall be deemed to be an

agreement;

(d) ...

(e) ...

(f) if property is transferred from a person or persons to another

person or other persons in exchange for other property or

without consideration, the transfer shall be deemed to

constitute the sale of the property by the first-mentioned

person or persons and the purchase of the property by the

second-mentioned person or persons;

(g) if the land is sold to or purchased by a person in pursuance

of a contract, the date on which the contract was made shall be

deemed to have been the date on which the land was so sold or

purchased;

...

26AAA(2) Where

(a) a taxpayer has purchased property after 21 August 1973 and

before the commencement of this section or purchases

property after the commencement of this section; and

(b) the taxpayer has, whether before or after the commencement of

this section, sold the property or an interest in the property

before the expiration of the period of 12 months from the date

on which he purchased the property,then, subject to this

section, the assessable income of the taxpayer includes any

profit arising from the sale ofthe property or interest.

107. The applicant says that if s 26AAA applies then the profit derived by the taxpayer, in a case when the property has been acquired by way of a gift, is to be calculated as if the property had been purchased at market value. Unlike Part IIIA, s 26AAA does not contain any deeming provision applicable to the case of a gift of a property to which the section applies. The question of how any profit should be calculated in the case of the sale of property that has been acquired by way of gift does not in the circumstances arise and it would be inappropriate to deal further with that issue.

108. Whether the applicant acquired the land without consideration, or whether he acquired it in consideration of his undertaking to accept liability for Mrs Gutwenger's liability to Westpac, the position so far as s 26AAA is the same. The transfer from Mrs Gutwenger to himself would, for the purposes of s 26AAA constitute a sale by Mrs Gutwenger and a purchase by the applicant.

109. As ss (2) applies only to sales made before the expiration of 12 months from the date on which the property was purchased, on the basis that the land was purchased on 24 March 1987, ss (1A) has no effect in the facts of the case.

110. It is common cause that the applicant sold 20 lots within 12 months from the date he acquired the land. No argument has been put as to why the provisions of s 26AAA should not be applied to profits made from sales made after 24 March 1987 and before 24 March 1988. But for the fact that the profit from the sale of the land is assessable under s 25, it is my opinion that s 26AAA would apply to those sales.

PART IIIA

111. It follows from the conclusion that I have reached in relation to the assessability of the profits from the sale of the land under s 25 that there is no scope for the application of Part IIIA.

CONCLUSION

112. The applicant is liable to income tax on the basis that profits derived from the sale of the land were income according to ordinary concepts and usages.

113. The material presently before the Court is not adequate to enable the Court to determine the assessable income of the applicant for the financial years ending 30 June 1987 and 1988. It is therefore not possible at this stage to determine whether the respondent's assessments for those years are excessive. However, it is my present understanding that on the basis that the profits are assessable under s 25 the parties are not in dispute as to the appropriateness of the assessments as they currently stand. In case my understanding is faulty I propose to stand the matter over to enable the parties to consider these reasons and make further submissions as to the formulation of the orders which will flow from these proceedings.
