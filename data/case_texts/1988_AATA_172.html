Income tax Assessable income - section 23FB fund – Termination of fund - Fund terminated contrary to approved terms and conditions - Whether benefits assessable income - Effect of approval of trust deed - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936-1984, secs 79, 23FB(2) (f), 26AF(1).

Pursuant to subsection 43(1)(a) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the Tribunal affirms the objection decision in relation to the assessments for the years of income ended 30 June 1983 and 1984.

(Sgd) P. Gerber

DEPUTY PRESIDENT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) WT87/165, WT87/46, WT85/307, WT85/793

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And: Commissioner of Taxation

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Dr P. Gerber (Deputy President)

Date: 7 June 1988

1. The four applications presently before me concern four taxpayers, each of whom became a member of a what became known as "one-man" superannuation funds, each of which, it was conceded, qualified for tax relief under (the then) section 79 of the Income Tax Act 1936-1983. The Fund in each case was established on the same day, with the same trustee, and on similar terms and conditions. At the hearing, evidence was only called in one of the four applications (WT85/307), it being conceded that the outcome in that case would govern the result in the other three. It subsequently transpired that the "test" case was governed by the Income Tax Assessment Amendment Act (No.3) of 1984, which removed section 79, while the other applications were governed by the earlier legislation which dealt with the so-called "section 79 funds". In the end, I have concluded that the amending legislation has not, in the events that occurred, affected the fiscal result in any of these four cases.

2. Dealing with the evidence of the "test" case, it was proved that on 26 June 1965, a superannuation fund was established (henceforth referred to as "the Fund") for the benefit of the taxpayer/applicant. A Mr Bryden was appointed as trustee. Clause 9 of the trust deed provided for the payment of benefits as set out below;

(a) upon retirement not before 60 years of age;

(b) ceasing to carry on employment by reason of permanent ill health;

(c) upon the 70th birthday;

(d) if death occurs before reaching the age of 60 years.

Clause 20 contained the usual dissolution provision, giving a life span to the Fund of 21 years after the death of the last survivor of the descendants of King Edward VII, living on 26 June 1965. In addition, such oddities as intervening insanity, bankruptcy and similar visitations operated as a disqualification for membership. It is common ground that these exclusionary provisions are not relevant to these applications.

3. In the events that occurred, the trustee Bryden died in April 19B3 and was replaced, as I find, by Mr E.J. Harty. I further find that the said Mr Harty was requested by this taxpayer (and each of the other applicants) to terminate the fund and to pay to her (and to the other applicants) the total balance held by the Fund and to retain any amounts due for expenses and income tax on behalf of the Fund. In the result, and after some misunderstanding with an officer of the Sydney Tax Office, Mr Harty terminated the Funds and distributed the amount held by each Fund to each of the four taxpayers, each being the only member. Although the taxpayer in the "test" case requested that payment be made before 30 June 1983, it is common ground that the payment to her was not, in fact, made until the 1984 tax year, that is after the passing of the Income Tax Assessment Amendment Act (No. 3) of 1984, whilst the payment in the other three cases was made in the 1983 tax year.

4. Turning to the facts in the "test" case, the Commissioner submits that the amount received in by the applicant from the Fund ($25,522) is, in all the circumstances, assessable in full pursuant to s.26AF, whilst Mr Aristei, who appeared for the applicant, strongly contends that what was received by his client did not constitute a "benefit received from the Fund". It is conceded that s.23FB (the successor of s, 79) introduced by the 1984 amending legislation, applies to the Fund.

5. . To complete the factual narrative, it should be stated that when the trustee put an end to the Fund, the taxpayer was alive, continuing in her employment, in good health and had not yet reached the age of 60 years. In short, none of the provisions of cl. 9 of the trust deed applied so as to "open the door", so to speak, to the termination of the Fund and a payment out to the member of the amount of the benefit.

6. The relevant statutory provisions are set out below. A (former) s.79 Fund qualified for tax relief provided it satisfies certain tests set out in the section and was approved by the Commissioner. One of the conditions for "recognition" of a fund by the Commissioner for purposes of tax relief and set out in s. 79(2)fl, is that the fund does not permit a member to receive any benefits from the fund "otherwise than in the event of the death, sickness or permanent incapacity for work: of the member ... (or) before the sixtieth anniversary of the birth of the member". These conditions are, for present purposes, repeated in similar terms in the amending Act of 1984; the reduction from 60 years to 55 years as a qualification for payment having no impact on the present application. It will thus be seen that the Fund in the instant case made a payment to its member which contravened (i) s.79 as it stood before its removal, (ii) S.23FB of the amending Act of 1984, and (iii), cl.9 of the deed which established the Fund. The effect of the amending Act of 1984 (with effect from 1 July 1983) was, inter alia, to replace the former s.79 with S.23FB.

7. In the result, The respondent taxed the amount received from the Fund in accordance with s. 26AF (1) as amended. However, where the former s.26AF (1) refers to a s.79 Fund, the amended S.26AF (1) refers to a S.23FB fund. The new section 26AF (1) provides:

(1) Where -

a) in a year of income and after 19 August 1980, a taxpayer received or obtains a benefit of any kind out of, or attributable to assets of, .... a section 23FB fund;

(b) the benefit is received or obtained otherwise than in accordance with approved terms and conditions applicable to the fund at the time when the benefit is received or obtained; and

(c) the Commissioner is satisfied that the taxpayer received' or obtained the benefit -

(i) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, a member of the fund;

(ii) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, a dependant of a person who was, or had been, a member of the fund; or

(iii) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, associated with a person who was, or had been, a member of the fund,

the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income shall include the amount or value of that benefit.

Sub-section (3) provides -

(a) ....

(b) ....

"section 23FB fund" means -

(a) a fund the income of which of any year of income is or has been exempt from tax by virtue of section 23FB or would, but for the provisions of Division 9C, be, or have been, exempt from tax by virtue of that section; and

(b) a fund that was a section 79 fund for the purposes of this section as in force at any time before the commencement of the Income Tax Assessment Amendment Act (No. 3) 1984.

The effect of the above amendment is that it comes full circle and takes this taxpayer back to the former s.79. In short, the respondent, in applying sub-section 26AF (1) as amended, concluded that the benefit was received "otherwise than in accordance with the terms and conditions" applicable to a section 23FB fund - in the instant case - a fund that was a section 79 fund for purposes of section 26AF as in force at any time before the commencement of the Income Tax Assessment Act (No. 3) 1984.

8. I now turn to the various submissions advanced on behalf of the applicant. In the first place, it was urged upon me that the evidence did not justify the conclusion that Mr Bruce Harty had been duly appointed as trustee of the Fund after the death of Mr Bryden. In the result, so it is said, not having been properly appointed, he took it upon himself to intermeddle with trust matters and to act characteristically in the office of a trustee, thereby constituting himself as a trustee de son tort; cf. re Barney, Barney v. Barney [1892] 2 Ch. 265; Mara v. Brown [ 1896] 1 Ch. 199; Myler v. Fitzpatrick (1822) 6 Madd 360; Carl Zeiss Stiftung v. Herbert Smith & Co (No2) [1969] 2 Ch. 276.

9. On the whole of the evidence, having heard the sworn testimony of the taxpayer as well as the evidence of Mr Bruce Harty, I am satisfied that the taxpayer herself appointed Mr Harty as trustee. Mr Harty confirms, in a letter to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation dated 23 May 1983, that "(the) beneficiary has asked for the fund to be terminated and has appointed me trustee in accordance with the deed in order to give effect to it." For good measure, the taxation return for the Superannuation Fund for the 1983 tax year names Mr Bruce D. Harty as the trustee. I find that whatever confusion is said to have arisen is attributable to a letter, written by this taxpayer, (and, semble, by all the other taxpayers), a copy of which was attached to the above return, which was addressed to "The Trustee, EJ Harty" requesting that "you exercise your power as Trustee to terminate the Superannuation Fund and pay the total benefits remaining to me before June 30 1983 ...". In fact, there is a Mr EJ Harty, who happens to be the son of Mr Bruce Harty and who practises as an accountant in partnership with his father. For good measure, Mr EJ Harty was called (literally, on the telephone) to give evidence at the hearing. I am therefore satisfied that what was done was effective in appointing Mr Bruce Harty as trustee of the Fund in succession to the late Mr Bryden, and that the reference to "The trustee EJ Harty" in the taxpayer's letter of 25 May 1983 was an error which gave rise to no misunderstanding by any of the parties involved in this application and has no legal effect. I find furthermore that the trustee acted within his authority at all times.

10. In an alternative submission, it was urged upon me that the payment was not authorized by the trust instrument, so that the monies could only be lawfully paid out under the rule of in Saunders v. Vautier (18415 Cr & Ph 240; cf. Queen Street Hotels Pty Ltd v. Byrne [1980] ACLC 24, hence the payment of the vested interest to the beneficiaries in the instant case, and the subsequent extinguishment of the trust, was said to be different in kind from the "benefit" contemplated by S.26AF. It is an enterprising submission, but one to which I cannot accede. It is, of course, perfectly correct that a trustee must administer the trust according to its express terms, nevertheless, adult beneficiaries who together are absolutely entitled to the trust property may terminate the trust and demand that the fund be handed over to them, and this is so whether or not this is expressed in the trust document. However, no persuasive reason was advanced why a payment made pursuant to the rule in Saunders v Vautier (supra) was not "caught" by the provision of s. 26AF.

11. In the alternative, it was submitted that if the rule in Saunders v. Vautier (supra) did not apply, the acts of the trustee de son tort are only explicable in terms of a breach of trust, the effect of which is that no benefit was lawfully transmitted to the four beneficiaries, so that the monies paid to them remain the property of the trust. Mr Aristei went on to submit that as the trust instrument did not authorize the trustee de son tort to pay the income and corpus of the trust property to the beneficiaries, who were at the time of payment (i) not beneficially entitled, (ii) not absolutely entitled, nor (iii) presently entitled to the trust property, it was his duty to call for the return of the monies from the beneficiaries; cf. Watts v. Turner (1831) Russ & M 634; Willis v. Hiscox [1839] EngR 386; (1839) 4 My & Cr 197. It followed from this that the monies paid to the taxpayers, being illegally obtained, did not constitute a "benefit" which was caught by S.26AF. It is an enterprising submission, and I deal with it out of respect to Mr Aristei, who presented his interesting and thoroughly researched arguments persuasively and not without a little elegance. It is with no disrespect that I am unable to agree with him that the trustee was improperly appointed and/or acted in breach of trust.

12. I find that what occurred here is that the trustee paid to this beneficiary 'out of an approved 23FB fund a benefit contrary both to the terms approved by the Commissioner of Taxation at the time of the creation of the Fund as well as the relevant legislation at the time the benefit was received. The consequence of such a payment is that the sum so paid became part of the assessable income of the taxpayer in the year in which it was paid pursuant to s.26AF (1).

13. Having examined the law, both before and after the commencement of the amending Act of 1984 and concluded that the amendments have no impact on the fiscal consequences in any of these applications, notwithstanding that in each of the other three cases the benefits were paid in the 1983 tax year, it follows that the decision on the objection in each case will be affirmed.

This is to certify that this and the preceding 9 pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons for Decision of Dr P. Gerber (Deputy President) in matter Nos. WT87/165, WT87/46, WT85/307 and WT85/793.

Date: 7 June 1988
