Re Valentine Arnold Little and Commissioner of Superannuation [1986] AATA 272 (10 September 1986)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: VALENTINE ARNOLD LITTLE

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. Q85/104

AAT No. 2874

Superannuation Act, 1920

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

The Hon. J.B.K. Williams CBE (Senior Member)

Dr A.P. Renouf (Senior Member)

Dr N.C. Davies (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation Act, 1920 - Superannuation Act, 1976 - Superannuation Act Amendment Act, 1976 - applicant in 1975 following medical examination admitted to the Provident Fund - essence of present application is that decision to place him in the Provident Fund was wrong and that he should have been accepted for the Superannuation Fund under the 1920 Act - question whether that course now open by reason of provisions of 1976 legislation, particularly section 46 of Act No 32 of 1976.

Superannuation Act, 1922 - ss.5(1), 79(2)

Superannuation Act, 1976 - ss.16, 154(2), 183

Superannuation Amendment Act, 1976 - ss.45, 46

Superannuation (Former Provident Account Contributor) Regulations

HEARING

CANBERRA

10:9:1986

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

The applicant was formerly employed as a mail officer with Australia Post. He retired from that position on 6 December 1985, and is in receipt of superannuation upon the basis that he was a former contributor to the Provident Account set up under the Superannuation Act, 1922 ("the superseded Act"). He retired voluntarily at the age of about sixtyone (61) years, and not on medical grounds.

2. On 6 March 1975, he underwent a medical examination by a Commonwealth Medical Officer for the purpose of it being decided under the superseded Act whether he should be accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund or to the Provident Account established under that Act.

3. The Commonwealth Medical Officer furnished a report dated 6 March 1975 of the result of his examination. It contains the recommendation that the applicant be accepted for the Provident Account rather than for the Superannuation Fund. The reason given in the report for this recommendation was that the applicant suffered "mild labile hypertension".

4. On 4 December 1975, a delegate of the Superannuation Board exercising powers conferred by the Superannuation Act, completed a document containing the heading "DETERMINATION OF MEDICAL FITNESS ON APPOINTMENT *SUPERANNUATION FUND/*PROVIDENT ACCOUNT". The Document contains the name and date of birth of the applicant. The words "*SUPERANNUATION FUND" were struck out on the heading of the form, leaving the words "*PROVIDENT ACCOUNT". The document goes on to state "The abovenamed appointee has been accepted as meeting the requirements of Section 42(2A) of the Postal Services Act and is also accepted as a contributor to either the Superannuation Fund or Provident Account as indicated above." It appears that the effect of this document is that the applicant was accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account.

5. On 12 October 1976, the applicant wrote a letter addressed to an officer of Australia Post containing the following:

"With regards to our conversation re my revaluation of my

rating from Provident to full Superanuation I hereby enclose

my Doctors report from Nov 1969 until Dec 1975 with the

intention of having the Govt medical set aside in favour of

my own doctors report who assures me my B.P. is normal.

I have also donated Blood to the Blood Bank on 4 or 5

occasions in the last 15 months. (The Doctors report normal

B.P)" (sic)

6. A copy of the applicant's letter was forwarded by Australia Post to the Commissioner of Superannuation under cover of a letter dated 28 October 1976. In this letter, it was stated that "the applicant was medically accepted for the Provident Account on 4 Dec 1975. However, he disputes this decision and is requesting the removal of the Benefit Classification Certificate."

7. It should also be stated that the applicant enclosed with his letter to Australia Post referred to earlier a copy of a certificate from his medical practitioner listing blood pressure readings taken in November 1969, April 1974, April 1975, and December 1975. The certificate also contains the opinion that the applicant was normotensive.

8. The Provident Account established under the superseded Act was abolished by the Superannuation Amendment Act, 1976 (Act No 32). The Superannuation Act, 1976 (Act No 31) provided in s.16 for the issue of benefit classification certificate to an employee who, following medical examination, was in the opinion of the Commissioner not likely by reason of or for a reason connected with a physical or mental condition referred to in medical reports to continue to be an eligible employee until the person attained his maximum retiring age.

9. Under the Superannuation Act, 1976 (Act No 31) there was deemed to be in force on 1 July 1976 in respect of a former Provident Account contributor a benefit classification certificate issued by the Commissioner specifying the physical or mental conditions by reason of which the former Superannuation Board did not accept the person as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund established under the Superannuation Act. The applicant, as a former Provident Account contributor, was a person with respect to whom there was, on 1 July 1976, deemed to have been in force a benefit classification certificate.

10. It appears that the applicant's letter of 12 October 1976 was treated as a request made pursuant to s.16(6) of the Superannuation Act, 1976 (Act No 31) to revoke the benefit classification certificate deemed to be in force in respect of him. The applicant was again examined by a Commonwealth Medical Officer who recorded his blood pressure as 130/80 and recommended that the benefit classification certificate be revoked. A Senior Medical Officer in the Department of Health agreed with this recommendation. As a result, a delegate of the Commissioner on 28 April 1977, revoked the benefit classification and the applicant was notified of this in a letter dated 2 May 1977.

11. It appears that the matter rested there until 1984. On 28 June 1984, the applicant wrote to the Commissioner enquiring as to his entitlements should he retire in October of that year aged sixtyone (61). The Commissioner replied to this letter on 4 September 1984 setting out the applicant's entitlements on alternative bases.

12. On 10 October 1984, the applicant again wrote to the Commissioner. In this letter, he stated:

"I joined the superannuation scheme in 1975 as a provident

contributor. However, my Benefit Classification Certificate

was revoked. I thought that I would then become an eligible

superannuation contributor and that my retirement pension

would be based on the same conditions as those pertaining to

superannuation contributions.

Would you please advise me fully in writing on my standing

in the Superannuation Scheme with particular reference to

the effect of the withdrawal of the Benefit Classification

Certificate."

13. The Commissioner replied to this by letter dated 13 November 1984 in which it was stated:

"2. Your retirement benefits are calculated on the basis

that you were a Provident Account contributor prior to the

Commencement of the new scheme on 1 July 1976 and the

revocation of your Benefit Classification Certificate does

not alter this situation.

3. The revocation of your Benefit Classification

Certificate will only be a factor in the calculation of your

retirement benefit should you be retired on invalidity

grounds.

4. Prior to the revocation of your Benefit Classification

Certificate, had you retired on invalidity grounds with a

condition specified on your Benefit Classification

Certificate, your benefit would have been reduced on medical

grounds."

14. It appears that in 1984 a delegate of the Commissioner considered that the applicant's letter of 12 October 1976 may have been intended as indicating an intention to dispute the decision taken by the delegate of the Superannuation Board under s.5(1) of the superseded Act. On 4 December 1975, the delegate granted an extension of time until 21 December 1984 under s.154(2) of the Superannuation Act, 1976 for the lodgment of a request for reconsideration of the decision taken under s.5(1) of the superseded Act.

15. The Commissioner then proceeded to reconsider that decision. In the result, the Commissioner, on 5 June 1985, confirmed the delegate's decision to accept the applicant as a contributor to the Provident Account. The Commissioner considered himself to be limited to exercising the same powers as were available to the delegate of the Superannuation Board when making his decision in 1975 and that by virtue of s.5(1)(c) of the superseded Act, he was constrained to consider the matter with reference only to the medical report furnished pursuant to s.5(1)(a). He did not consider himself entitled to have regard to any subsequent medical evidence.

16. The applicant now seeks a review of that decision by this Tribunal. For him, it is contended that the original decision of the delegate of the Superannuation Board was wrong; alternatively it was wrongly based on incomplete information in that the medical examination did not meet the requirements of the guidelines laid down for examining medical officers.

17. Act No 32 of 1976 brought about radical changes to the superannuation scheme. By s.45 of that Act which came into operation on 1 July 1976 the Provident Account was abolished by the repeal of s.75 of the superseded Act which set up the Provident Account. Part XII of Act No 31 of 1976 contained transitional provisions whereby funds assets and liabilities of the Superannuation Fund under the superseded Act became funds assets and liabilities of the new Fund. Division 4 of Part XII dealt with existing contributors. In effect eligible employees under the old scheme became eligible employees under the new. S.183 empowered the making of regulations to make provision for modifying the Act or a provision of the Act specified in the regulations in the application of the Act or that provision and in relation to a person to whom this section applies or to and in relation to a prescribed class of persons in relation to whom the section applies. It was further provided that the regulations may provide for the payment of contributions in addition to or in substitution for provisions that would otherwise be payable under the Act and for benefits in addition to or in substitution for benefits provided for by the Act. Pursuant to that power, the Superannuation (Former Provident Account Contributor) Regulations were made.

18. S.79(2) of the superseded Act introduced by Act No 102 of 1959 and amended by Act No 97 of 1965 conferred a right for contributors to the Provident Account to seek to be transferred to the Superannuation Fund. It provided:

"(2.) Where--

(a) a person who has become a contributor to the Provident

Account otherwise than by virtue of section eighty-one

of this Act applies to the Board to be allowed to

contribute to the Fund under Part III. of this Act;

(b) the person has undergone a medical examination by a

legally qualified medical practitioner approved by the

Board and a report of the result of the examination has

been furnished by the medical practitioner to the

Board; and

(c) the Board is satisfied, after considering the report of

the medical practitioner, that the health and physical

fitness of the person are such as to justify his being

accepted as a contributor to the Fund under Part III.

of this Act,

the Board may allow him to contribute to the Fund under Part

III. of this Act; and"

19. By s.46 of Act No 32 of 1976, subsections were added to s.79 in the following tems:

"(4) The Board shall not exercise its powers under sub-

section (2) of this section in relation to a person who is a

contributor to the Provident Account except in pursuance of

an application made by the person before 13 February 1976.

(5) Where, on or after 13 February 1976 but before 30 April

1976, a person who is a contributor to the Provident Account

makes application to the Board for the Board to exercise its

powers in relation to him under sub-section (2) of this

section and the application arose out of or is otherwise

related to a communication made to the Board by the person

before 13 February 1976, the Board may, for the purpose of

sub-section (4) of this section, treat the application as

having been made before that last-mentioned date."

20. As has been said, the 1976 legislation brought about radical changes to the superannuation scheme. The old Provident Account was abolished and elaborate provisions were introduced to determine the rights and liabilities of contributors as between former contributors to the Fund and former contributors to the Provident Account and the disposition of monies held in the Fund.

21. In essence, the Commissioner, upon his review was, and now this Tribunal is, asked to set aside the original decision made in 1975 to admit the applicant to the Provident Account and instead to direct that he should have been accepted as a contributor to the Fund. It appears from the evidence that the first intimation from the applicant that he was dissatisfied with the decision made in placing him in the Provident Account was contained in his letter of 12 October 1976. Insofar as it may be taken to be an application for transfer from the Provident Account to the Fund, it was made subsequent to the time prescribed by subsections (4) and (5) of s.79. To transfer the applicant from the old Provident Account to the Superannuation Fund would, in our view, be contrary to the plain legislative intent behind the 1976 legislation, particularly the statutory direction given by subsections (4) and (5) of s.79. It appears to us that the legislative intent behind the enactment of subsections (4) and (5) of s.79 was that there should be a cutoff date at which the rights and liabilities of contributors under the superseded Act were to be determined. It was, it appears to us necessary to determine a point of time at which those former contributors to the Provident Account who considered themselves eligible for acceptance to the Fund could establish their eligibility.

22. It appears that under the superseded Act a right of review of a decision taken by the Board or its delegate was given. This right of review still exists in another form under the current legislation. However, it does not seem to us that the right of review extends to the making of determinations, the effect of which would be to bring about an alteration to the applicant's status from an original contributor to the Provident Account to a contributor to the Superannuation Fund.

23. In the years intervening between the original decision in 1975 and today, substantial legislative changes have been made affecting relationships between contributors and the Fund and relationships between contributors themselves. To disturb those relationships now would, in our view, be contrary to the legislative intent which was to bring about certainty and finality in those relationships.

24. One must feel sympathy for persons in the position of the applicant who could not reasonably be expected to appreciate the implications of the very complex legislation governing the subject; however, the matter falls to be determined in accordance with that legislation.

25. In effect, the decision under review was that there should be no alteration of the status of the applicant from that of a contributor to the Provident Account to that of a contributor to the Superannuation Fund. For the foregoing reasons (which differ from those of the primary decision-maker), we would affirm the decision under review.
