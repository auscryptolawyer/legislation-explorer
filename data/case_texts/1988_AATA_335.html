Re Mark Hastings Rohde and Commissioner of Superannuation [1988] AATA 335 (25 November 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: MARK HASTINGS ROHDE

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. S87/66

AAT No. 4773

Administrative Appeals Tribunal

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.A. Layton(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Administrative Appeals Tribunal - Applicant seeking payment of legal expenses and witness fees incurred in appeal - whether Tribunal has power to award costs, or to make recommendations to the Attorney-General under the Administrative jurisdiction - discussion of circumstances in which the Tribunal is empowered to order costs and circumstances giving rise to exercise of discretion.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, ss.67, 69

Superannuation Act 1976

Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees) Act 1971, s.64

Freedom of Information Act 1982, s.66

Re Rohde and Commissioner for Superannuation S87/66, delivered 4 November 1988

Re Sullivan and Secretary, Department of Transport No.3) (1979) 2 ALD 502

Re Smedley and Director-General of Social Security (1983) 5 ALD 387

Re Ricci and Director-General of Social Services (1982) 4 ALN No. 44

Re Blyth and Repatriation Commission (1982) 4 ALN No. 86

Re O'Brien and Repatriation Commission (1983) 5 ALN No. 148

HEARING

ADELAIDE

25:11:1988

ORDER

The applicant's application for costs and witness fees is refused.

DECISION

Following the Tribunal decision in Re Rohde and Commissioner for Superannuation S87/66 delivered 4 November 1988 in which the Tribunal upheld the applicant's appeal from a decision of a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation refusing to revoke his Benefit Classification Certificate ("B.C.C."), the applicant sought a direction from this Tribunal that the respondent, or alternatively the Commonwealth, pay his costs incurred in seeking review of the aforementioned decision.

2. The Directions Hearing was conducted by telephone and submissions were made by the applicant and by Mr Eadie for the respondent. The applicant contended that as a Benefit Classification Certificate should never have been issued in respect of himself, it was in the interests of justice that his legal expenses and witness fees incurred in instituting his appeal should be re-imbursed. The respondent drew to the Tribunal's attention the limited powers conferred on the Tribunal under the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, in the absence of any specific provisions under the Superannuation Act 1976, empowering the Tribunal to award costs to successful applicants, but submitted that there were precedents for the Tribunal recommending to the Attorney-General that costs be awarded in the appropriate case.

3. Unlike the Compensation jursidiction which empowers the Tribunal to award costs to successful applicants (pursuant to s.64 of the Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees) Act 1971) there is no similar provision under the Superannuation Act (supra) which empowers this Tribunal to award costs in these circumstances.

4. Under the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act (supra) itself, the Tribunal's powers are limited to the provisions contained in s.67 which provides:

'67.(1) A person summoned to appear as a witness

before the Tribunal is entitled to be paid fees, and

allowances for expenses, fixed by or in accordance

with the regulations in respect of his attendance.

(2) Subject to sub-section (3), the fees and allowance

shall be paid -

(a) in a case where the witness was summoned at the

request of a party other than the person who made

the decision subject to review - by that party;

and

(b) in any other case - by the Commonwealth.

(3) The Tribunal may, in its discretion, order that

the fees and allowances of a witness referred to in

paragraph (2)(a) shall be paid, in whole or in part,

by the Commonwealth."

Sub-s.67(3) therefore empowers the Tribunal at its discretion to order the Commonwealth to pay in whole or in part the fees and allowances of witnesses falling within para.(2)(a) of the Act. But as pointed out by the Tribunal in Re Sullivan and Secretary, Department of Transport (No.3) (1979) 2 ALD 502, the Tribunal's discretion may only be exercised where a witness is summoned to appear before the Tribunal. If a witness appears voluntarily at the request of a party, the Tribunal cannot order the payment of fees in respect of that witness. In this case the witnesses in relation to which the applicant seeks payment of fees, were not summoned and the applicant must therefore fail for that reason alone.

5. However, even if a witness has been summoned to appear, a decision by a Tribunal to exercise the discretion should only be made if there is sound reason for doing so. The fact that the applicant is successful in his application for review is insufficient ground for an exercise of the discretion, see Re Smedley and Director-General of Social Security (1983) 5 ALD 387. Precedents such as Re Ricci and Director-General of Social Services (1982) 4 ALN No 44, Re Blyth and Repatriation Commission (1982) 4 ALN No 86, and Re O'Brien and Repatriation Commission (1983) 5 ALN No 148, suggest that a party must be able to establish that refusal by the Tribunal to order payment of fees would result in hardship to that party.

6. In the present case even if the witnesses had been summoned, I am not satisfied the applicant would suffer hardship if the Tribunal did not make an order for payment, nor that the decision the subject of review was made vexatiously or without reasonable cause and as previously stated a successful review of itself is insufficient ground for the exercise of discretion. The general principle in this jurisdiction that each party bears their own costs should apply. I also draw attention to the fact that the Tribunal decision in the applicant's case was a majority decision, the remaining member was not satisfied that the B.C.C. should be revoked.

7. I make these comments in response to the applicant's submissions only in passing as in the present case the Tribunal cannot order payment of witness fees as the applicant's witnesses Dr Mills and Mr Tomlian were not summoned to appear before the Tribunal, but appeared voluntarily at the request of the applicant. Dr Goldney, for reasons which appear in the decision, was subpoenaed by the Tribunal of its own motion and therefore the applicant will not have any expense for that doctor's appearance.

8. I also draw to the applicant's attention that he may make application to the Attorney-General for legal assistance, pursuant to s.69 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act (supra). But it should be noted that the Attorney-General may grant the application only "if he is satisfied that it would involve hardship to that person to refuse the application and that, in all the circumstances, it is reasonable that the application should be granted.". The discretion to refuse or grant an application lies with the Attorney-General and not the Tribunal. There is no provision as found in s.66 of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 which allows the Tribunal in its discretion to make recommendations to the Attorney-General on behalf of the applicant. Therefore in the absence of such provision it is not only inappropriate but beyond the competence of this Tribunal to make any recommendation to the Attorney- General with respect to costs.

9. For these reasons I find that the Tribunal has no power to order the payment of witnesses fees in this particular case on any costs in relation to the review.
