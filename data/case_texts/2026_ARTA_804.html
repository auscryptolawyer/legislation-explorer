Tribunal Number: 2025/4468

Tribunal: Senior Member M Harrowell

Decision: The decision under review is affirmed.

CATCHWORDS

Tax Agents – Tax Practitioners Board – registration as a tax agent – whether applicant satisfies relevant experience criteria – Decision to refuse application for registration affirmed

LEGISLATION

Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth)

Tax Agent Services Act 2009 (Cth)

Tax Agent Services Regulations 2022 (Cth)

CASES

Dennis and Tax Practitioners Board [2025] ARTA 1001

Statement of Reasons

  1. By application dated 15 December 2024 (registration application), Mr Hakim, the applicant in these proceedings, applied to the respondent (Board/respondent) for registration as a tax agent pursuant to s 20-25(1) of the Tax Agent Services Act 2009 (Cth) (TAS Act). Mr Hakim contended he satisfied the requirements for registration including those found in cl 203 of Sch 2 of the Tax Agent Services Regulations 2022 (Cth) (TAS Regulation).
  2. On 27 March 2025 the Board rejected the application. This was because the Board was not satisfied Mr Hakim had the relevant experience as required by cl 203(d) of the TAS Regulation. Mr Hakim was advised of this decision and reasons therefore by letter dated 23 May 2024 (Board’s Decision).
  3. The reasons for rejecting the Registration Application were as follows:
    Reasons for decision

    The Committee rejected your application under subsection 20-25(1) of the TASA on the basis that you did not satisfy the Committee that you met the requirements prescribed by the regulations in relation to relevant experience in accordance with paragraph 20-5(1)(b) of the TASA. The Committee made this finding on the basis that you failed to evidence that you had undertaken at least 2 years of full-time relevant experience (or part time equivalent) in the last 5 years as required under Item 203(d) of Schedule 2 to the TASR. The Committee particularly noted:

    1. you did not provide sufficient evidence regarding the amount of relevant experience you received under the supervision and control of a registered tax agent.

    2. the statements provided to the Board by the nominated supervising tax agent Selina Nasrin (RN 25949383) did not support the amount of relevant experience claimed by yourself and there were discrepancies in the information provided to the Board.

    Ms. Nasrin stated regarding relevant experience you had completed 3 to 4 hours on average per week.” The calculation based on the 4 years and 20 weeks presented is as follows: 4x (4x44) + (4x20) = 4x176 + 80 = 784 hours.

    You contested this and stated that you had in fact 6000 plus hours which is calculated as (1540 x 4 years) + (35x20) = 6160 + 700 = 6860. You confirmed the basis for this calculation as follows:

  • that you work for NSW Government from 8am to 4pm Monday to Friday in a non-tax agent services role
  • you work 5 hours min at night Monday to Saturday in a contractor role.
    3. You may re-apply in the future, but this will need to be accompanied by evidence to support any statements as to your relevant experience to show you meet the registration requirements, specifically, the relevant experience requirement in Item 203(d) of Schedule 2 of the TASR.
  1. By application dated 19 June 2025 (review application), Mr Hakim applied to the Tribunal for review of the Board’s Decision.
  2. For the reasons that follow, I have decided that the decision to reject the application for registration should be affirmed.

HEARING

  1. This review application was heard on 23 April 2026.
  2. Mr Hakim represented himself and the Board was represented by Mr Hanna of Counsel.
  3. Pursuant to the directions of the Tribunal, each of the parties had provided a Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions (SFICS) in support of their respective positions together with various documentary evidence originally provided to the Board by Mr Hakim in support of his application.
  4. The documentary material provided to the Tribunal is contained in two volumes entitled “Joint Hearing Bundle” (hearing bundle/HB), and “Joint List of Authorities”. The hearing bundle also contained various witness statements and submissions.

CONSIDERATION

  1. Part 2 of the TAS Act regulates the registration of tax agents.
  2. In respect of individuals, s 20-5 (1) provides:
    20-5 Eligibility for registration as registered tax agent or BAS agent

    Individuals

    (1) An individual, aged 18 years or more, is eligible for registration as a *registered tax agent or BAS agent if the Board is satisfied that:

    (a) the individual is a fit and proper person; and

    (b) the individual meets the requirements prescribed by the regulations (including, but not limited to, requirements relating to qualifications and experience) in respect of registration as a registered tax agent or BAS agent; and

    (c) in the case of registration which is not a renewal—the individual maintains, or will be able to maintain, professional indemnity insurance that meets the Board’s requirements; and

    (d) in the case of a renewal of registration—the individual:

    (i) maintains, at the time of applying for registration, professional indemnity insurance that meets the Board’s requirements; and

    (ii) has completed continuing professional education that meets the Board’s requirements.

    Note 1: An individual in the capacity of trustee of a trust can be registered: see section 70-15.

    Note 2: Subsection (4) provides for an exception to paragraph (1)(b) for pre-1988 tax agents and nominees.

  3. Subdivision 20-B – Applying for registration of the TAS Act sets out the provisions concerning applying for and determination of an application for registration.
  4. Section 20-25 provides:
    20-25 Registration

    Grant of application for registration

    (1) If you have applied to the Board for a type of registration, the Board must grant your application if you are eligible for registration of that type. Otherwise, the Board must reject your application.

    (2) The Board must decide your application within 4 months of receiving it.

    (3) If the Board does not decide your application within 4 months of receiving it, the Board is taken to have rejected your application. However, this does not apply if your application is for renewal of your registration.

    Note: For renewals of registration, see section 20-5

  5. As is evident from s 20-25(1) of the TAS Act, the Board must reject the application if the eligibility requirements of the TAS Act and TAS Regulation are not met.
  6. Schedule 2 Part 2 of the TAS Regulation prescribes requirements applicable to individuals seeking registration. There is a no dispute clause 203 that applies to the application of Mr Hakim. Clause 203 states:
    Diploma or higher award

    203 This clause applies if the individual:

    (a) has been awarded a diploma or higher award in accounting from a registered training organisation or an equivalent institution; and

    (b) has successfully completed a course in Australian taxation law that is approved by the Board; and

    (c) has successfully completed a course in commercial law that is approved by the Board; and

    (d) has undertaken at least 2 years of full time relevant experience (or part time equivalent) in the last 5 years.

    Note 1: The Board may approve a course by an approval process, an accreditation scheme, or by other means.

    Note 2: For the definition of relevant experience, see clause 212 of this Part.

  7. The sole issue in this case is whether the applicant has “undertaken at least 2 years of full-time relevant experience [or part-time equivalent] in the last 5 years” as required by clause 203(d).
  8. “Relevant experience”, this is defined in cl 212 of the TAS Regulation which provides:
    relevant experience means work by an individual:

    (a) as a registered tax agent; or

    (b) as a tax agent registered under Part VIIA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 as in force immediately before 1 March 2010; or

    (c) under the supervision and control of a registered tax agent; or

    (d) under the supervision and control of a tax agent registered under Part VIIA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 as in force immediately before 1 March 2010; or

    (e) as an Australian legal practitioner; or

    (f) of another kind approved by the Board;

    that includes substantial involvement in the provision of one or more of the types of tax agent services described in section 90-5 of the Act, or substantial involvement in the practice of a particular area of taxation law to which one or more of those types of tax agent services relate.

    Note: The terms registered tax agent and tax agent service are defined in the Act.

  9. Presently, I am concerned with the “relevant experience” of Mr Hakim “under the supervision and control of a registered tax agent” (as provided in the definition in cl 212(c) of the TAS Regulation) on a “part-time equivalent” basis, which experience must include “substantial involvement” in the provision of tax agent services described in s 90-5 of the TAS Act or in a particular area of taxation law to which such services relate.
  10. Section 90-5 of the TAS Act defines tax agent services as follows:
    90-5 Meaning of tax agent service

    (1) A tax agent service is any service:

    (a) that relates to:

    (i) ascertaining liabilities, obligations or entitlements of an entity that arise, or could arise, under a *taxation law; or

    (ii) advising an entity about liabilities, obligations or entitlements of the entity or another entity that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law; or

    (iii) representing an entity in their dealings with the Commissioner; and

    (b) that is provided in circumstances where the entity can reasonably be expected to rely on the service for either or both of the following purposes:

    (i) to satisfy liabilities or obligations that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law;

    (ii) to claim entitlements that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law.

    (2) A service specified in the regulations for the purposes of this subsection is not a tax agent service.

    Note: For specification by class, see subsection 13(3) of the Legislation Act 2003.

  11. In Dennis and Tax Practitioners Board (Dennis) I said: [1]
    55. Three concepts are involved in determining whether the requirements of clause 201(d) of the TAS Regulation are satisfied by Mr Dennis. These are:

    (a) Time spent in gaining experience: an applicant must undertake one year of full-time relevant experience in the last five years or an equivalent period on a part-time basis;

    (b) Need for supervision and control: the required experience must be gained under the supervision and control of a registered tax agent; and

    (c) Work done in gaining relevant experience must include substantial involvement in the provision of tax agent services or in the practice of taxation law relating to those particular services. Tax services are defined in section 90-5 of the TAS Act.

  12. Similarly, these concepts apply to clause 203(d) of the TAS Regulation, save that the requirement is for part time experience equivalent to 2 years full-time.
  13. In the present case, the parties agree that 2 years full-time means 3080 hours.
  14. The issue is what time was worked under the supervision and control of Ms Nasrin and does it meet the required 2 years.
  15. The respondent’s position can be summarised as follows.
    (a) Based on the SRE and the information provided by Ms Nasrin, that is her estimate that the applicant worked 3 to 4 hours on average per week in providing tax agent services, the respondent said this amounted to 784 hours.[15]

    (b) There is insufficient evidence for the Tribunal to be satisfied that the applicant had substantial involvement in completing the work identified in the SRE being the lodgement of returns[16].

    (c) Neither the bank statements, invoices nor the spreadsheet provide sufficient evidence of relevant experience and do not substantiate the hours claimed; and

    (d) in any event, the Tribunal could not be satisfied that all hours identified by the applicant was work under the supervision and control of Ms Nasrin.

  16. The applicant says he has “consistently worked 20 hours per week over a period of 4 years and 5 months, amounting to approximately 3920 hours”.[17] This work is said to have been performed between 5 pm and 11 pm Monday to Friday and on Saturday from 9 am to 4 pm. In relation to working on Saturdays, I note the applicant originally contended in his SFICS that work on the weekend was performed on Sunday between 9 am to 4 pm and, as noted above, the Spreadsheet records time worked on Saturdays was in the evening.[18]
  17. In connection with the SRE, the applicant says the declaration by Ms Nasrin incorrectly stated only 4 hours per week was worked.
  18. As to evidence to support work experience, reliance was placed on invoices provided to clients of Shelley Global as well as the Spreadsheet which was described as “a detailed work logbook”[19]
  19. Reliance was also placed on statutory declarations including “client references confirming [the applicant’s] work experience and professional conduct” which are found in the hearing bundle.[20]
  20. In my view the evidence is not sufficient to establish that an equivalent number of the required hours of “relevant experience” as defined by clause 212 of the TAS Regulation, namely 3080 hours, has been gained by the applicant.
  21. My reasons are as follows.
  22. The evidence of Ms Nasrin establishes 784 hours of relevant experience as calculated by the respondent. Her statement to the Board demonstrates work to which she was referring was tax agent services as defined in s90-5 of the TAS Act. It is unclear whether the 1-2 hours of supervision were in addition to the 3-4 hours identified as having been worked by the applicant. Even if this was the case, the total hours are substantially less than 3080 hours.
  23. The applicant’s Spreadsheet establishes that the applicant was “available” to take calls from clients and undertake work for them for which they were invoiced. However, the evidence does not establish that the applicant was in fact working under the supervision and control of Ms Nasrin providing tax agent services during this time.
  24. In this regard, the Spreadsheet is not a contemporaneous document, the applicant saying that entries were made at the end of the day or well after the period to which particular entries relate. The entries do not identify particular clients and cannot be related back to any invoice by which services to particular clients were billed.
  25. More particularly, the evidence of the applicant is that, aside from reviews of documents to be lodged with the ATO through the portal and some enquiries he might raise with Ms Nasrin if he needed assistance, he was not supervised. Rather, he would give advice to and undertake tasks for those he described as “my clients” on numerous issues which was neither reported to or subject to review, let alone supervision, by Ms Nasrin.
  26. The oral evidence of the applicant, which I have set out above, makes this clear.
  27. It is possible some of the entries in the spreadsheet involve matters about which he consulted was Nasrin for assistance. However, such possibility is far from sufficient to establish the agreed requirement of 3080 hours “under the supervision and control of a registered tax agent” has been met.
  28. In reaching this conclusion, I should not be taken as deciding what is adequate supervision and control or how much time needs to be spent in the supervisor and the applicant for registration interacting with each other. In this case, the applicant has a long history of providing accounting related services and tax agent services to clients. One would reasonably expect that what is adequate supervision and control needs to be assessed having regard to the experience of the applicant and what is required in the particular case.
  29. However, in this case, much of the work relied upon as relevant experience was not work done under the supervision and control of Ms Nasrin.

Conclusion

  1. The decision of the respondent to refuse the application for registration is affirmed.
