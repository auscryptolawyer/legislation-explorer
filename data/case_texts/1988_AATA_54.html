REASONS FOR DECISION

10 February 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. On one view the issue in these references is simple and uncomplicated, and the solution clear. That is the way the matter is put for the Applicant. A company (TCO) contends that it borrowed substantial sums of money in order to purchase income-producing assets. The assets were company shares and interests in partnerships. Having borrowed the moneys, they were wholly applied in the purchase of those assets. In the years of income in question the assets generated substantial assessable income and the Company became liable for substantial sums by way of interest. TCO says that the interest for which it became liable constituted "losses and outgoings ...... incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income (of TCO) ......" and, accordingly, the interest liability so incurred is to be allowed as a deduction pursuant to s.51 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). Accordingly, TCO contends that the result is that the taxable income of TCO is to constitute a figure derived by deducting that interest (and other items not in dispute) as allowable deductions from the assessable income derived by way of dividends which has been taken into account in the computation of its taxable income (of. s.48).

2. When TCO lodged its returns of income for the years ended 30 June 1974 and following (Column 1), it returned as income the dividends and profits which it asserted it had received as assessable income (Column 2); claimed interest expenditure as an allowable deduction (Column 3); and declared its taxable income (Column 4). The Commissioner disallowed the amounts claimed as deductions by way of interest and assessed taxable income (Column 5).

  1. As the matters were presented before the Tribunal, the Commissioner defends his assessments on two quite different bases. First, he contends that TCO has not produced sufficient evidence before the Tribunal to enable TCO to discharge the burden it bears of proving that the assessments are excessive: the reason being that the Commissioner contends that TCO has not established that it incurred the interest obligations it speaks of in any way such as would make them allowable as income tax deductions pursuant to s.51 of the Act. Alternatively, it is contended that any expenses which were so incurred, were incurred at least in part for a purpose other than that of carrying on business or deriving assessable income and that, accordingly, the expense is not allowable as a deduction pursuant to s.51 of the Act.
  2. The second basis for defending the assessment is quite different. It is that, even if the evidence was sufficient to satisfy the Tribunal that there had been such outgoings, the outgoings are not allowable as deductions, either because the transaction said to constitute the loss or outgoing claimed which is, as a deduction, a sham and, therefore, a nullity; or it is void against the Commissioner pursuant to the provisions of s.260.
  3. To understand the issue as to whether TCO has presented sufficient evidence in support of its objection to enable it to satisfy the burden of proof, and whether that evidence has persuaded the Tribunal, it will be convenient to first outline the context which the Commissioner contends is the full context in which the issues fall to be determined.
  4. The applications arise out of the affairs of two individuals: M and N. Over many years they had worked in collaboration in providing accountancy services and financial advice within the hotel industry. By 1974 they were jointly interested in a range of enterprises in two States. Both men suffer major health problems and, although both were alive at the date of hearing, at least one of them was not capable of attending to give evidence. I am not persuaded that it would have been unreasonable for the other to attend.
  5. Being concerned, among other things, to make provision for their respective families in the event of death M and N consulted a solicitor and he advised them. The advice was intended when effectuated to result in a situation whereby:

(a) investments previously controlled by M and N (or by companies which they controlled) would come to be vested in a new company at an initial cost to that company of $1,668,770. It was intended that the new company would be TCO;

(b) TCO would be owned as to one-half by M and the other half by N;

(c) TCO would finance its purchase of the investments by borrowing (at interest) the purchase price from one its own subsidiaries (LENDCO).

10. To that extent the proposal reflects the scenario relied on by Counsel for TCO. However, the Commissioner puts the matter on a broader basis. It is contended for the Commissioner that the transaction whereby TCO borrowed and became liable to pay interest forms part of a larger scheme involving the affairs of M and N and their families whereby the purchases by TCO; the borrowings by TCO; and the "payment" of interest by TCO were all either a sham or directed to such an end as to have "the purpose or effect" of avoiding tax. Accordingly, it is contended, the transactions are pro tanto ineffective or void against the Commissioner, and the entitlement of TCO to the deductions sought is defeated.

11. The Commissioner presented in support of his case a diagram in the way of a flow-chart which, when read in an anti-clockwise direction, demonstrated the place of the moneys allegedly borrowed within a circular flow of money.

  2. The chart is in three parts. It is the raid-section which reflects the transactions within the respective groups of M and N which enabled the money-flow to proceed. It is a characteristic of both the diagram and the tabular representation that their presentation could commence at any point, provided it continued until it returned to that point. It is also to be noted that in as much as the flow table merely traces a money cycle which reflects a re-organization of a company group, it does no more than could be done within any groug re-organization, provided the group is conceived of sufficiently widely to enable the re-organization to take effect without the introduction of funds from outside the group. Similar charts could be presented to account for external funds by enlarging the scope of the chart.
  3. If the money-flow indicated no more than that borrowers borrowed from lenders and paid interest to those lenders on the moneys so borrowed, it would not be remarkable. However, the case for the Commissioner was presented on the basis that the transaction to which TCO was a party had an overall tax avoidance significance because it was part of a complex of events which resulted in tax revenue being lost to Australia as a result of the circulation of Australian-sourced moneys through, a Hong Kong company (HKCO).

16. It is to be noted that the deductions claimed relate only to moneys borrowed from LENDCO - an Australian company. The evidence did not establish anything as to the "borrowings" (if any) from A, B and C.

S.51 DEDUCTION CLAIMS

  1. The Applicant claims that it is entitled to have certain amounts in respect of interest deducted from its assessable income in order to determine its taxable income in each of the years under reference (s.48). In making that claim it contends that the amounts claimed for interest constitute "...losses and outgoings... incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or...incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income..." [s.51(1)]. The claims fall into two parts.
  2. The first is a claim by TCO to deduct interest in relation to a loan alleged to have been made by LENDCO to TCO on or about the 14th day of October 1974. The principal of the loan ("the Initial Loan") is alleged to have been $1,168,770. Together with one amount of $100,000 and two amounts of $200,000 each lent to A, B & C respectively, it is alleged to make up a total of $1,668,770 advanced by LENDCO. It is alleged that the moneys so borrowed from LENDCO were wholly applied by TCO in the purchase of shares in other companies with a view to the generation of assessable income. It is claimed that the borrowing was for no other purpose such as would cause only portion of the interest to be allowable as a deduction. Further, it is claimed that there was an entitlement to a deduction in relation to interest on the Initial Loan on the basis that interest was calculated at the rate of 7% and was capitalised annually in the years of income ended 30 June 1975, 1976 and 1977; and that thereafter the Initial Loan was treated as part of the Current Loan (cf. below).

19. The second claim relates to interest said to be allowable from time to time as interest in respect of a Current Loan. The Current Loan is said to have commenced during the year of income ended 30 June 1975 and the balance outstanding at any time is alleged to have been determined by bringing to account moneys advanced to or to the use of TCO and applied in the purchase of interests in partnerships (interests acquired with a view to the generation of assessable income); on other moneys advanced and applied to income-producing purposes; by crediting moneys received to the credit of the account by way of returns on investment as dividends upon shares and profit distributions arising from partnerships; and by the debiting of interest charges.

21. The Applicant bears the burden of proving that the assessments are excessive [s.190(a)]. It is therefore incumbent upon the Applicant to persuade the Tribunal - although only on the balance of probabilities - that it incurred liabilities by way of interest in such circumstances as to satisfy the requirements of s.51(1) of the Act. S.5K1) provides:-

"All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income."

  1. To the extent to which it is established that a deduction is allowable under s.51(l) of the Act in relation to any of those items, then subject to the operation of other provisions of the Act, the relevant assessment is excessive and should be reduced. Whether any of those claims to a deduction are established depends upon the evidence advanced in relation to each such claim. Although a finding as to entitlement in one year to some extent will tend to establish an entitlement in years following, nonetheless the entitlement must be made out in each case and in each year. Therefore, it is appropriate to consider what needs to be established in relation to each head of claim as contended for by the Applicant; and then to consider whether, upon the evidence presented before the Tribunal, the claim is made out.
  2. It is contended that the interest deductions claimed in relation to the Initial Loan relate to a borrowing made by TCO in the sum of $1,168,700 in October 1974. It is claimed:-
    • (a) that the moneys were borrowed from LENDCO;
      • (b) that the moneys borrowed were immediately applied in the purchase of shares in subsidiaries with a view to deriving dividend income;
        • (c) that, pending repayment of the principal, the Applicant was liable for interest at the rate of 7% per annum on the principal sum until such time as it became obliged to suffer some other rate of interest;
        • (d) that, pursuant to that obligation, the Applicant suffered a "loss or outgoing" by way of interest amounting to $58,606 which was "incurred" during the year of income ended 30 June 1975;
        • (e) that that liability was "incurred" despite the circumstance that no money was paid to LENDCO;
        • (f) that the liability was "incurred" by TCO in that a journal entry was made in the Books of Account of the Applicant as of June 30, 1975 crediting LENDCO with "interest to 30/6/75 $58,606". (The journal entry was duly posted in the ledger of the Applicant. As a result the Applicant, in its own records, acknowledged its liability to LENDCO in the sum of $1,227,376);
        • (g) that a further liability was "incurred" by a similar journal entry as of June 30, 1976. That entry was duly posted to the ledger of the Applicant, and thereby indebtedness to LENDCO was acknowledged as increased to $1,314,857 by reason of crediting an account of LENDCO with interest in the sum of $87,481?

(h) that a similar crediting of interest took place as of 30 June 1977, acknowledging a further interest liability in the sum of $107,754 and that the balance due to LENDCO was $1,425,583.26. (That balance was established by bringing to account after interest to 30 June 1976 was debited, unexplained credit entries of October and December 1975 and further credit entries to 30 June 1976 which were also unexplained).

No further interest was credited or claimed under this head. According to the accounts of the Applicant the balance due at 30 June 1977 remained unchanged until 30 June 1981 when that particular account was closed and, by journal entry, the indebtedness on the Initial Loan (then standing at $1,425,583.26) was transferred to the Current Loan account.

  1. In the years following, a substantially similar system was followed so that, in so far as accounting entries in the books of the Applicant could establish it, the interest expenses claimed were borne.
  2. What is the most striking feature of the evidence in this case is that no witness came forward to give evidence on oath or affirmation as to any events giving rise to the claimed liability or even as to the writing up of the accounting records. An accountant gave evidence which summarized the effect of what was recorded in the primary records but he did not claim to have any knowledge as to whether or not any of the representations made in those primary records were in any sense true. In the course of the hearing the question was raised with Senior Counsel for the Applicant as to what evidence there was of the actual facts claimed to establish the entitlement. It was answered with the contention that, a company's books (including the minute book) are prima facie evidence of the matters recorded in them; and that the evidence had sufficiently established that the records produced were the books of account and minute books of the Applicant.
  3. As a result, the findings of fact which have to be made in order to determine whether or not the requirements of s.51(1) of the Act are prima facie satisfied, must be drawn from such documents as are in evidence unvouched by the testimony of any witness. Once that is recognized it can be seen that the available evidence of the "losses and outgoings" is limited to:-
    • (a) entries in the accounting journal of the Applicant, all made as of 30 June in each year;
    • (b) copy tax returns for the Applicant prepared and presented in each instance following the 30 June to which the return relates hut before the expiration of the immediately following year, which income tax returns are consistent with the primary records and annual Accounts of the Applicant?
    • (c) annual Accounts for TCO produced in evidence by an accountant who obtained them from the custody of TCO;
    • (d) the annual Accounts and income tax returns of LENDCO prepared on a similar basis which are consistent with the amount acknowledged by TCO as interest receivable by LENDCO (including the moneys credited by the Applicant), and which, by schedules to its Balance Sheet, acknowledges increasing indebtedness on the part of the Applicant in sums equal to the amounts represented by the Applicant to be the sum of its indebtedness to LENDCO for principal and interest; and
    • (e) inferences to be drawn from the actions of the Commissioner in issuing assessments based upon the statements in the income tax returns of TCO that it had derived the income referred to therein. (I have deliberately used the terra "statement" rather than "assertion" or "admission". The "statements" in the returns may serve as "admissions" in one context - an acknowledgment of indebtedness – and at the same time for other purposes constitute a self-interested assertion - a claim to an income tax deduction. Such statements are not necessarily true or false. Nor are they necessarily to be classed as true or false because they can be characterized as admissions rather than assertions.

28. I find that there is no evidence at all as to the terms of any borrowing as agreed upon before or at the time of borrowing; and no direct evidence of any agreement at any time as to the payment of interest. Against that background, I am satisfied that the only basis for contending that any loss or outgoing was "incurred" in relation to any interest lies in the journal entries. As to that, I am not persuaded that the entries were made on or before 30 June in the years to which they relate but I am satisfied that such entries were made at some time unknown during the period of twelve months following the date to which the journal entry relates. It follows that, if anything at all is allowable, it will only be so in so far as the making of the journal entry effects the incurring of a liability in the amount of that entry in relation to the year of income current when the journal entry was made.

29. What suffices to constitute a loss or outgoing as an expense which has been "incurred" has often been referred to in decisions of the High Court of Australia, but usually, only in the context of considering whether an expense proved to have been suffered was "incurred" in one year rather than another.

30. Consideration of those and other authorities indicates that payment is not essential ("no proof of payment or actual disbursement need be established" Beaumont, J. in F.C. of T. v. Lau 84 A.T.C. 4929 at 4940). But "what is clearly necessary is that there should be a presently existing liability [Nilsen Development Laboratories Pty. Limited v. F.C.T. [1981] HCA 6; (1981) 144 C.L.R. 616 at 627 - per Gibbs, J.] Although incurred "...does not mean only defrayed, discharged or borne, but rather includes encountered, run into or fallen upon... It does not include a loss or expenditure which is no more than impending, threatened or expected" New Zealand Flax Investments Limited v. F.C. of T. [1938] HCA 60; (1938) 61 C.L.R. 179 at 207 - per Dixon, J.: a comment approved of by Barwick C.J. in Nilsen (ante) at page 624

when, having cited the passage with approval, his Honour said:-

"and I would for myself add 'no matter how certain it is in the year of income that that loss or expenditure will occur in the future'."

31. A loss or outgoing may be incurred even though it remains unpaid, provided the taxpayer is "definitively committed" [F.C.T. v. James Flood Pty. Limited [1953] HCA 65; (1953) 88 C.L.R. 492 at 5063, or if "the taxpayer has completely subjected himself to (the expense)" (ibid), although "it may be going too far to say that he must have come under an immediate obligation enforceable at law whether payable presently or at a future time" (ibid). Again, "Commercial and accountancy practice may assist in ascertaining the true nature and incidence of the item as a step towards determining whether (the expense) answers the test laid down by s.51(l) but it cannot be substituted for the test" (ibid).

32. In Nilsen, Barwick C.J. said (at 625):

"Of course, it may very well be that in the keeping of commercial accounts it would be proper to make provision against the annual gross profits of some sum related to the amount of the liability which must in due course arise because of the service by the employee during the year of accounting; and to do so before arriving at the profits or gains of the period during which the qualification of the employee is taking place. But the prudence and commercial propriety of such a course has little bearing on the question whether there is present in the year of income a loss or outgoing within the meaning of S.5KD." (cf. Nilsen, Barwick C.J. at 625).

33. But that statement refers to principles. It does not refer to what was done by unknown hands at unknown times, working under the direction of persons unknown, and/or in accordance with unknown criteria. What is presented upon the financial records before us does not, in my view, establish the facts and events which those accounting records purport to record and which the methodology of accountancy processes is designed to reflect. Accounts prepared in accordance with accountancy principles ought record the financial implementation and consequences of other facts and events in such a way as to make possible the clear and precise expression of the consequence of those other facts and events in summary form in the way of Profit and Loss Accounts and Balance Sheets. However, in my view there is no presumption for the purposes of taxation law, that financial accounts of a taxpayer, prepared in purported compliance with the obligation of any Companies Act or Code (as in force from time to time) are correct, or that the facts and events which they purport to record ever happened.

  1. Measured by those standards, the terms of the numerous loans said to have been made to this Applicant are unknown. Further, what liability TCO had in relation to the payment of interest upon those loans, is unknown. Furthermore, in those circumstances, I am not persuaded that at any time within the period in question TCO became so "liable" for interest as to have "incurred" a loss or outgoing - using that term in the manner outlined above. Accordingly, I am of opinion that the Applicant has not discharged the onus it bears of establishing any existence of facts which in law entitle it to any of the deductions which are in dispute.
  2. In my view that conclusion is sufficient to dispose of the issues upon these reference. However, regard must be had to the possibility of error in the findings so reached and, therefore, to consider whether the other arguments advanced upon the hearing ought be considered in these reasons. For my part I have decided that it is not appropriate to consider those alternative arguments. The conclusion I have reached is based upon an assessment of the evidence presented. If that view should happen to be wrong it would call for such a fundamental revision of the assessment of the evidence, that consideration of the other issues would necessarily be influenced by that revised appreciation. In the circumstances, I decline to consider the alternative arguments.
