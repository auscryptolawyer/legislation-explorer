Re Jeffrey VC Spencer and Beverley A Spencer v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1990] FCA 126 (24 April 1990)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: JEFFREY V.C. SPENCER and BEVERLEY A. SPENCER

And: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. NP240 of 1989

FED No. 308

Bankruptcy

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEW SOUTH WALES DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Wilcox J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Bankruptcy - Petition by Deputy Commissioner of Taxation - Whether signatory to petition was a properly appointed delegate of the Commissioner - Whether signatory was a person temporarily appointed to act in office of Deputy Commissioner - Power of Commissioner to make appointments necessary for the proper administration of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

Taxation Administration Act 1953, s.8(1).

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s.8.

Acts Interpretation Act 1901, s.33A.

HEARING

SYDNEY

24:4:1990

Counsel for the Petitioning Creditor: Mr M Aldridge

Solicitors for the Petitioning Creditor: Australian Government Solicitor

Counsel for the Debtors: Mr J Johnson

Solicitors for the Debtors: Johnson and Co

ORDER

1. A sequestration order be made against the estate of each debtor.

2. Costs, including any reserved costs, be taxed and paid according to the Bankruptcy Act 1966.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules. (See also Order 37 rule 2(3)).

DECISION

A notice of opposition has been filed in this matter. It is agreed between the representatives of the parties that there is only one question requiring resolution. That question arises out of the circumstance that the petition was signed by one Michael John McHugh who stated in the petition, as it was originally framed, that he was "the person performing the duties of the office of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation". The petitioner named on the petition is the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation.

2. When the matter came on for hearing today counsel for the petitioning creditor sought leave to amend the petition by adding some additional words. This application was not opposed. Accordingly, I granted leave. In the result, the opening words of the petition, as amended, are: "I, Michael John McHugh of 28 Railway Street, Chatswood in the State of New South Wales, the person performing the duties of and acting as Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, petition the Court for a sequestration order against the estate of Jeffrey V C Spencer and Beverley A Spencer, etcetera."

3. There is evidence before the Court that the permanent Deputy Commissioner of Taxation stationed at Chatswood at the date of the petition was Edward John Withers. Mr Withers went on recreation leave for two weeks from 16 January 1989 to 27 January 1989. The petition was executed on 23 January 1989; that is to say, during Mr Withers' absence on leave. Annexed to an affidavit sworn by Mr Withers is a photocopy of a telex which he sent to the Commissioner of Taxation, Mr Trevor Boucher, on 17 November 1988 which read as follows:

"Recreation leave

As discussed yesterday I would like to take

two weeks' recreation leave commencing Monday,

16 January 1989.

Brian Flynn has commitments at that time and

would prefer not to relieve me. Would you

please let me know if you would like to have

somebody from national office relieve for that

period."

Written on the telex immediately after the above words, which are typewritten, is the following:

"Mr McHugh to act.

TB 3/12/88".

4. Mr Withers has given evidence that he is familiar with the signature of Mr Boucher, the Commissioner of Taxation, as he has seen his signature on numerous documents, and that the letters "TB" appearing on the telex in handwriting are Mr Boucher's. The affidavit also annexes a telex of 5 December 1988 written by an officer of the Taxation office to the Deputy Commissioner stating:

"The Commissioner has given approval for Mr

Mick McHugh to act as Deputy Commissioner,

Chatswood, while you are on recreation leave

for two weeks commencing 16 January 1989."

5. There is also a document called Actual Movement Temporary Performance which sets out details of Mr McHugh's acting appointment. This document is signed by a person who is identified as a staff clerk.

6. I am satisfied that Mr McHugh was appointed by Mr Boucher to act as Deputy Commissioner of Taxation at Chatswood during the period from 16 January to 27 January 1989; that is, the period during which the petition was executed.

7. On behalf of the debtors the submission is made that the documents do not comply with s.8 of the Taxation Administration Act 1953. Section 8(1) reads as follows:

"The Commissioner may, either generally or as

otherwise provided by the instrument of

delegation, by writing signed by the

Commissioner, delegate to a Deputy

Commissioner or any other person all or any of

the Commissioner's powers or functions under a

taxation law or any other law of the

Commonwealth or a Territory, other than this

power of delegation."

8. The section goes on to deal further with the matter of delegation of power. I have not heard counsel for the petitioning creditor; but, as it seems to me at the present time, s.8 has nothing to do with this case. This is not a case in which the Commissioner delegated to Mr McHugh any particular functions. This is a case in which the Commissioner appointed Mr McHugh to act temporarily in the office of Deputy Commissioner.

9. Section 33A of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 provides that, where a provision of an Act confers on a person a power to appoint a person to act in a particular office, then:

"(e) while the appointee is acting in the

office:

(i) the appointee has and may exercise

all the powers, and shall perform

all the functions and duties, of the

holder of the office; and

(ii) that or any other Act applies in

relation to the appointee as if the

appointee were the holder of the

office."

10. Section 8 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 gives to the Commissioner the general administration of the Act. This must include the power to make the appointments necessary for the proper administration of the Act.

11. The moneys, the subject of the bankruptcy petition, are moneys in respect of which a judgment has been recovered in an action brought in the name of "Deputy Commissioner of Taxation" in the District Court of New South Wales at Sydney. The moneys comprise partly moneys the subject of taxation assessment and partly moneys the subject of tax instalment deductions.

12. Section 209(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act provides that any tax unpaid may be sued for and recovered in any Court of competent jurisdiction by the Commissioner or Deputy Commissioner, suing in his official name. A like provision is made in relation to tax instalment deductions in s. 221R(1) of the Act. The effect of these provisions is that an action may validly be brought in the name of either the Commissioner of Taxation or the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation for moneys due to the Commonwealth in respect of either taxation assessed or tax instalments. A person who occupies the position of the officer in whose name the action is brought is empowered to take all necessary steps to prosecute the action and to recover the moneys. It may be that an action for recovery of tax is commenced in the name of a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation during the time when a particular person holds that permanent appointment. That person may retire and another person may be permanently appointed to the office. As was conceded by the solicitor for the debtors, in such circumstances the new appointee would be entitled to exercise the powers of the office in respect of the pending claim. I see no difference between that situation and a case where the appointment is a temporary one. I agree that it is necessary that the appointment be made by the Commissioner; but that requirement is satisfied in the present case. In my opinion there is no merit in the objection which has been taken.

13. It is agreed between the parties that there is no other matter in issue. Accordingly, I propose to make a sequestration order.

Orders

14. The orders that I make as are follows: I am satisfied that each of the debtors committed the act of bankruptcy alleged in the petition, namely that each of them failed on or before 21 December 1988 either to comply with requirements of the bankruptcy notice served on him or her on the 30th day of November 1988 or to satisfy the Court that he or she had a counter-claim, set-off or cross-demand amount equal to or exceeding the sum specified in paragraph A of the bankruptcy notice. I am satisfied that the petitioning creditor has proved the other matters of which s.52(1) of the Act requires proof. I make a sequestration order against the estate of each of the debtors. I order that costs, including any reserved costs, be taxed and paid according to the Act. I direct that a draft of this order be delivered to the registrar within seven days in accordance with rule 174(2).
