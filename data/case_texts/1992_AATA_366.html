Re Vyb and Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner [1992] AATA 366; (1992) 24 ATR 1199 (1992) 29 ALD 560 (30 November 1992)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: VYB

And: INSURANCE AND SUPERANNUATION COMMISSIONER

No. V92/933

AAT No. 8396

Number of pages - 9

Superannuation

[1992] AATA 366; (1992) 24 ATR 1199

[1992] AATA 366; (1992) 29 ALD 560 (extract)

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mrs R.A. Balmford(1) (Senior Member) and J.T. Brassil(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - reasonable benefit limits - whether applicable to payments made to members of public sector superannuation funds who retired before 1 July 1990

Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 ss.15, 16, Part IIIA

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s.27A

Re Superannuation Appeals N91/439-442 [1992] AATA 189; (1992) 15 AAR 539

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 2 October 1992

30:11:1992

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

This is an application under the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 ('the Act') for review of a decision of the respondent made on 14 July 1992 on reconsideration under sub-section 16(3) of the Act of six decisions made on 28 March 1992, and described further at paragraph 13 infra. Those decisions were made under section 15N of the Act, and thus are decisions referred to in paragraph (aa) of the definition of 'reviewable decision' in sub-section 3(1) of the Act. Accordingly, the reconsideration was authorised by sub-section 16(1A), and the decision on reconsideration is reviewable by this Tribunal under sub-section 16(6).

2. The Tribunal was originally constituted to hear this application with three members. One member was associated with a public sector superannuation fund, not directly involved in the matter. He disclosed this association at the commencement of the hearing. Some discussion took place, and shortly after the commencement of the hearing that member decided to take no further part therein pursuant to sub-section 14(1) of the AAT Act, and thus ceased to be available for the purposes of these proceedings. With the agreement of the parties the hearing and determination of the proceeding was completed by the Tribunal constituted by the remaining two members, pursuant to sub-section 23(1) of the AAT Act.

3. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ('the AAT Act') and numbered T1 to T23, together with other material lodged at the hearing. Evidence was given at the hearing by the applicant,who was unrepresented, and by Mr Butts, operations manager of what will be referred to as 'the XYZ Superannuation Scheme'. The respondent was represented by Mr Gillespie of the Legal Section of the Insurance and Superannuation Commission ('the Commission').

4. Sub-section 16(9) of the Act reads:

'16.(9) The hearing of a proceeding relating to a reviewable

decision shall take place in private and the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal may, by order:

(a) give directions as to the persons who may be present; and

(b) give directions of a kind referred to in paragraph 35(2)(b) or (c)

of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal 1975.'

Pursuant to that provision, the hearing of this matter took place in private. An order was made under paragraph (a) directing that only certain named persons there at the commencement of the hearing and other staff of the Commission be permitted to be present.

5. In Re Superannuation Appeals N91/439-442 [1992] AATA 189; (1992) 15 AAR 539, after referring to sub-section 16(9), the Tribunal said:

'No provision seems to have been made in the Act for observance of

privacy in the publication of the result of any such proceeding.

Section 14ZF of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 provides -

"14ZF Notwithstanding section 35 of the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal 1975, the hearing of a proceeding before the Tribunal for the

review of an objection decision or an extension of time decision or on

an extension of time application shall not be in public unless the

party who requested the review or made the application requests that

the proceeding be heard in public".

This section specifically modifies the terms of s 35 of the AAT Act.

Sub-section 16(9) of the Act appears to be inconsistent with the

requirement of s 35 of the AAT Act that the hearing of a proceeding

before the Tribunal shall be in public. We have taken the Act, as

later legislation, to have impliedly repealed sub-section 35(1) of the

AAT Act in so far as it applies to any proceedings arising under the

Act (cf s 25(6)(b) of the AAT Act). It would frustrate the privacy of

a hearing if the result were to be published so as to disclose the

identity of the applicant. We see no distinction between the phrase

"shall take place in private" used in the Act and the phrase "shall

not be in public" used in the Taxation Administration Act.'

6. We would, with respect, adopt that passage. These reasons have been drafted with the intention of preserving the privacy not only of the applicant, but also, so far as possible, of the superannuation scheme of which he was formerly a member. We have directed that, for the purposes of the publication of these reasons, the applicant be referred to as 'VYB'.

7. It is appropriate to begin by setting out some general historical background to the specific facts with which we are concerned. Between 1 July 1988 and 1 July 1990 there were a number of changes in the treatment, for taxation purposes, of public sector superannuation funds, in order to ensure that payments from both public and private sector funds would in future be treated in the same manner. The amendments were enacted over a period, in such a way as to enable payees and potential payees from public sector superannuation funds to arrange their affairs appropriately in the two years prior to 30 June 1990. The changes were publicised, but it appears from material before the Tribunal that there was significant confusion during that period in relevant quarters about the effects of the provisions which were enacted.

8. Since 30 June 1990, payments from public sector superannuation funds have been subject to 'reasonable benefit limits ('RBL's'), i.e. limits on the amount to be treated, for taxation purposes, in a manner more beneficial to the recipient than the manner of treatment of ordinary income received in the financial year of retirement. Prior to 30 June 1990 there had been no limit of that kind on payments from public sector superannuation funds. The RBL is calculated, in accordance with the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations made under the Act ('the Regulations'), by multiplying the 'final average salary' of the payee by a 'reasonable benefits multiple'. However, if 'eligible termination payments' ('ETP's') received from the fund are paid into an 'approved deposit fund ('ADF') or into a fund which provides a deferred annuity, then the payment is not subject to an RBL. The material set out in this and the previous paragraph is not in issue and we find accordingly.

9. Administration of the RBL's is the responsibility of the respondent under the Act. Section 15G of the Act provides that a payer (as defined) making an ETP to a person is required to give prescribed information to the respondent. Sub-section 15K(1) then requires the respondent to determine whether the payment is in excess of the person's RBL and if so, the extent to which it is in excess. However, if the whole of the payment is rolled over to an ADF, sub-section 15K(3) provides that the respondent must not make such a determination and thus no RBL applies to the payment. These provisions, which are contained in Part IIIA of the Act, came into operation on 1 July 1990. 'Eligible termination payment' is defined in section 15E of the Act as having, in Part IIIA, the same meaning as in section 27A of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ('the Tax Act'). 'Payer' is relevantly defined in section 15E as meaning, in Part IIIA, 'any person or other entity (other than a continuously non-complying ADF) that makes, or is liable to make, an eligible termination payment . . . '.

10. The specific facts of this matter are not in dispute, and from evidence before the Tribunal we find them to be as set out in this and the following three paragraphs. On 1 May 1988 the applicant took early retirement after service of 31 years and 7 months with a Victorian government instrumentality. He was a contributing member of the XYZ superannuation scheme, which was established by the Victorian government, separately from the State Superannuation Fund, to provide superannuation for staff of a specific group of State instrumentalities. The XYZ superannuation scheme is a 'public sector superannuation fund' within the meaning of clause 1 of Schedule 2 to the Regulations. The scheme permitted payment of lump sum benefits on early retirement after 30 years service, and the applicant took his benefits accordingly.

11. The XYZ superannuation scheme also offered the option of retaining a benefit in the scheme in a 'beneficiary account', rather than receiving it or rolling it over to an ADF. The applicant took advantage of this option. He subsequently made a number of withdrawals from his beneficiary account. The six decisions which were affirmed by the decision here under review relate to six such withdrawals made after 1 July 1990. Those withdrawals were ETP's within the meaning of paragraph (b) of the definition of 'eligible termination payment' in sub-section 27A(1) of the Tax Act, each one being 'a payment made from a superannuation fund in respect of the taxpayer by reason that the taxpayer is or was a member of the fund' and not being any of the payments listed in sub-paragraphs (i) to (iii) of paragraph (b). By virtue of the definition in section 15E of the Act, the payments were thus ETP's for the purposes of Part IIIA of the Act, and accordingly the XYZ superannuation scheme, not being an ADF, was a 'payer' for those purposes.

12. The information relating to those payments which was given by the XYZ superannuation scheme to the respondent in pursuance of section 15G did not include the applicant's tax file number. In those circumstances, the respondent is required by section 15L of the Act to make an interim determination 'to the effect that the whole of the amount of the payment that is counted towards the reasonable benefit limits is in excess of the reasonable benefit limits'. Such an interim determination was duly made in respect of each of the six withdrawals. The respondent was then informed of the applicant's tax file number, and on 28 March 1992 made six 'final determinations' by amendment of each of the interim determinations pursuant to section 15N of the Act.

13. The form of each final determination was as set out below:

'INSURANCE AND SUPERANNUATION COMMISSION

Lodge this page with your tax return,

quoting reference number ( )

Reasonable Benefit Limits

Final Determination

for an eligible termination payment paid on (date)

notified by (XYZ) SUPERANNUATION BOARD

Our reference number: ( )

(Name and Address of Applicant) Your tax file number

( )

Reasonable Benefit Limits (RBL) Determination Details:

Total RBL amount counted from previous benefit(s) $

Total RBL amount counted from this benefit $

Lump Sum Reasonable Benefit Limit $

Determined excessive component $

Eligible Termination Payment (ETP) Components:

Pre July 1983 component $

Post June 1983 taxed component $

Post June 1983 untaxed component $

Undeducted contributions $

Non-qualifying component $

Concessional component $

Excessive component $

Total of eligible termination payment $

Explanatory Information

A brochure called Your ISC Determination is enclosed. Please

read it carefully as it will help to explain this notice.

Richard M Beetham

Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner'

In each case the figure '0' appeared opposite the line 'Determined excessive component'. Thus in each case the determination constituted a decision that the applicant's ETP was not in excess of his relevant RBL. The total of the six ETP's did not exceed the figure shown opposite the line 'Lump Sum Reasonable Benefit Limit'. Those six decisions were, as we have said, confirmed on 14 July 1992 by the decision here under review.

14. The submission of the applicant was that, because he had retired before 1 July 1990, RBL's should not be applied to his withdrawals made after that date from the XYZ superannuation scheme. To apply RBL's to those amounts was to give retrospective effect to the amending legislation, a course of action which had not been in the contemplation of Parliament, and was not within the power of the respondent. He conceded that the six decisions in question were correct, and that as the payments did not exceed his RBL, he suffered no taxation detriment. His concern was that he perceived that the legislation was being given retrospective effect. RBL's were not intended to apply to payments made to people who retired before 1 July 1990.

15. Mr Gillespie submitted that there was no basis on which the Tribunal could do anything but affirm the decision under review. We accept that submission. Even had the applicant not conceded that it was correct, there is no evidence before us on which we could find otherwise. As set out in paragraph 11 supra, the XYZ superannuation scheme was a 'payer', and the amounts received were 'eligible termination payments', thus invoking the provisions of Part IIIA, to which we have referred.

16. We appreciate that the applicant considers that it was not the intention of the legislature that Part IIIA of the Act should apply to his benefits. However, the provisions of the Act to which we have referred are not ambiguous. There is no basis on which we could refer, under section 15AB of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901, to any extrinsic material to determine their meaning.

17. Had the applicant not retained his benefits in the scheme at the time of his retirement in May 1988 and beyond 1 July 1990, his position may well have been different, but that is not the situation with which we are concerned. As we have said in paragraph 7 supra, it appears that there was at the time significant confusion about the effects of the amending provisions.

18. For the reasons given, the decision under review will be affirmed.
