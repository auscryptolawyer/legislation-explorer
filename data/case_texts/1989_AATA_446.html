ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION - exempt income - whether income not exempt from income tax where derived - whether any tax paid

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s.23(q)

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v French [1957] HCA 73; (1957) 11 ATD 288

Australian Machinery and Investment Co Ltd v Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1946] HCA 65; (1946) 8 ATD 81

REASONS FOR DECISION

21 June 1989 Mrs R.A. Balmford, Senior Member

  1. This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent disallowing an objection by the applicant to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of his taxable income for the year ended 30 June 1980 of an amount of $7199 derived from a source outside Australia.
  2. The applicant requested that the objection be referred to a Board of Review, and that request was complied with by the respondent. By virtue of section 223(1) of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 and sub-section 189(2) of the Income Tax. Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") that referral of the decision on the applicant's objection is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision.
  3. Evidence was given at the hearing by the applicant, who was unrepresented. The respondent was represented by an officer of the Australian Taxation Office. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 as modified by section 14ZG of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 and numbered folios 1 to 37 together with one document lodged at the hearing by the respondent.
  4. The applicant submitted that the income in question was exempt income by virtue of section 23(q) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") as it stood at the relevant time, and was thus excluded from his assessable income by the exception to sub-section 25(1) of the Act.
  5. Section 23(q) then read, so far as relevant:

"The following income shall be exempt from income tax:-

(q) income ... derived by a resident from sources out of Australia and Papua New Guinea, where that income is not exempt from income tax in the country where it is derived ...

Provided that this paragraph shall not apply to exempt any income unless -

(i) where there is a liability for payment of income tax in the country where that income is derived - the Commissioner is satisfied that the tax has been or will be paid .. ."

6. On the basis of the documents before me and the evidence of the applicant, which was not challenged and which I accept, I find the facts to be as set out in this and the following paragraph. In January 1980, as a student engineer, the applicant was engaged to work for an international engineering company. He signed a document which read as follows, so far as relevant:

" EQUALISATION SYSTEM

Since July 1971 an Equalisation System has been in operation. The rules are as follows :

  1. The Equalisation System consists of a 10% payroll deduction to cover the personal income tax to be paid on behalf of the employee by [the company], in their country of assignment.
  2. The Equalisation System applies to all Field International Staff not stationed in their country of origin.
  3. The Equalisation System does not apply to Headquarters personnel.

I, the undersigned, having read the above, hereby agree to all the terms thereof.

I authorize and direct my employer to deduct from my salary, on a monthly basis, the contributions as specified above.

...

Date : 7/1/80 Signature :

The purpose of this arrangement was that all staff members would contribute the same percentage of salary regardless of whether they were serving in a low tax or a high tax country, and income tax as levied in the country of service would be paid on behalf of each staff member by the company.

  1. The applicant was employed from February to April 1980 in Brunei and in May and June 1980 in France. His gross earnings paid in respect of services performed in Brunei were $5340, and in France $1859. I am satisfied from-salary advice forms that 10 per cent of his salary was withheld under the heading "tax". It is not in issue that he has been at all material times an Australian resident.
  2. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v French [1957] HCA 73; (1957) 11 ATD 288 the High Court held that, for the purposes of section 23(q), the salary of a taxpayer resident in Australia was derived in New Zealand, where the services for which it was paid were performed, and not in Australia, where it was paid. Thus in the present case, $5340 of the amount in question was derived in Brunei, and $1859 in France.
  3. Both parties were in agreement, and I find, that income tax is not levied on the income of private individuals (as opposed to companies) in Brunei. Dixon J (as he then was) said in Australian Machinery and Investment Co Ltd v Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1946] HCA 65; (1946) 8 ATD 81 at 100, that "exempt" in the expression "exempt from income tax" in section 23(q) meant "not subject to tax".
  4. Accordingly, that part of the income in question which was derived in Brunei does not satisfy the initial requirement of section 23(q), and in respect of that part of the income the objection decision under review will be affirmed.
  5. So far as the income derived in France was concerned, there was before the Tribunal a certificate dated 14 April 1982 from the Direction Generale des Impots at Paris to the effect that the applicant was at that date not liable for tax on his income for the year 1979. Leaving on one side the question of whether in French taxation practice "the year 1979" refers to what would in Australia be described as "the year ended 30 June 1980", this document is open to several interpretations. It may mean that the applicant's income in that year was not subject to tax, for any one of a variety of reasons, such as that he was a foreign resident, or that it was below the taxable minimum. It may mean that he was assessed for tax in that year and the tax had been paid. Thus, unfortunately it cannot be relied upon as evidence either that the applicant's income was not exempt from income tax in France, or that any tax for which there was a liability has been paid.
  6. The applicant informed the Tribunal that he was satisfied that the company which employed him would have carried out any obligations to pay any tax levied in respect of him by the French Government. He had spoken in 1975 to an Australian engineer who had been taken on to the staff of the company at the same time as he, and who was still in its employ. That engineer told him that the company carried out such obligations.
  7. In Case M14 (1961) 12 TBRD 86, a Board of Review found income to be exempt where the taxpayer's employer had paid tax for which the taxpayer was liable under the law of India. However, in that case there was evidence available to the Board upon which it was able to satisfy itself as to the matters required to be shown for the application of section 23(q).
  8. If the applicant is to succeed in this matter, he must discharge the burden imposed upon him by section 190 of the Act of "proving that the assessment is excessive". To do so, he must show, first that his income derived in France was not exempt from income tax in that country, and second, that any tax for which he was liable in that country has been (or will be) paid.
  9. While I appreciate that he is personally convinced of both these matters, I regret that the evidence before me is not such as to satisfy me, on a balance of probabilities, of either of them. Accordingly, the objection decision under review will be affirmed.
