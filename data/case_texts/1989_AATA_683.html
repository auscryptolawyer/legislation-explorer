ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

INCOME TAX - allowable deduction - interest incurred on a loan secured by a mortgage over the applicant's farming property purpose of the loan unconnected with the running of the applicant's farming business.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 S37

REASONS FOR DECISION

7 November 1989 Deputy President G L McDonald

The applicant is appealing against an assessment of the Commissioner disallowing a deduction claimed for interest in the sum of $4,478 in the applicant's 1984 tax return.

The Tribunal heard from the applicant and his accountant. Documents filed for the purposes of S37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and other exhibits were tendered along with an agreed statement of facts. The undisputed facts are as follows.

The applicant and members of his family owned and operated a farm in the South West of Western Australia. In August 1982 a proprietary limited company ("the Company") was incorporated and the land transferred to the company. The shares in the company were held by members of the family including the applicant and together they operated a business as primary producers on the land pursuant to a partnership.

The applicant was married but his wife, unlike the spouses of his brothers, had no financial interest in the partnership or in the company.

Subsequently the applicant issued proceedings in the Supreme Court of Western Australia against his two brothers arising from a dispute over the transfer by the applicant to his brothers of some shares in the company.

During 1982 the applicant reached agreement with his brothers whereby he discontinued his Supreme Court action and in exchange 1260 acres of the farming property were excised in favour of the applicant and registered in his name. The deed provided that:

(a) the applicant retire from the partnership

(b) he transfer his remaining shares in the company

(c) he discontinue an action in the Supreme Court of Western Australia for an order for the dissolution of the partnership

(d) he agree to lease the excised land to his brothers for a period commencing on the date of transfer and terminating on 1 March 1990, at a rental of $10.00 per annum payable yearly in advance on each anniversary of the date of the lease

(e) the applicant had the right to occupy and reside in the homestead erected on the land and utilise the immediate surroundings of approximately three acres for his own use, and

(f) the applicant give first option of purchase to the lessees, up to 1 March 1990.

The applicant lived on the farming property between 1974 and 1979 and 1982 and 1986. In the period 1982-86 the applicant, in accordance with the agreement reached with his brothers, lived in a homestead located on the property and made use of the 3 acres surrounding the homestead. He told the Tribunal that during this period he worked as a truck driver and shearer.

He utilised the 3 acres surrounding the home to grow vegetables but it was not suggested that he did this on a commercial basis. It is evident the applicant could not utilise the land to farm as it was leased to his brothers. His activities were those of a shearer and truck driver. Whilst those activities are not inconsistent with work carried out by a farmer they are not, per se, descriptive of the business of a farmer. There is no objective evidence for the Tribunal to conclude that the applicant was in 1984 engaged in activities which would warrant his occupation being characterised as that of "a farmer".

It is not suggested by the applicant that the $10 per annum paid by his brothers for the lease of the property constitutes an income earning activity such that it would allow the applicant's income earning activity to be characterised as that of "a lessor". Having regard to his total income for the year the $10 paid by way of rent is insignificant.

In 1982 the applicant separated from his wife and entered into an agreement with her pursuant to S87 of the Family Law Act (1975) in which he was to pay her the sum of $45,000 in three instalments as follows:

(a) $23,000 on or before 27 May 1982

(b) $10,000 on or before 8 July 1982

(c) $12,000 on or before 8 October 1982

In default of payment interest at the rate of 15% would accrue on any outstanding sum.

On 9 November 1983 a Writ of Summons was issued by his wife's solicitors through the West Australian District Court demanding payment of $22,000 plus interest arising from the non-payment of the latter two instalments under the Family Court Agreement.

On 18 November 1983 the applicant applied to Bridging Finance Co of Australia Ltd and obtained an interest only loan of $40,000 for the period of one year secured by a first mortgage against the 1260 acre property. However, the loan funds were not to be released to the applicant until the Title Deed evidencing the transfer from the company into the applicant's name issued from the Titles Office (see exhibit 2) thereby enabling the finance company to register a first mortgage in its favour. It would appear that the mortgage in favour of Bridging Finance Co of Australia Ltd was not registered until 14 February 1984 (see exhibit C).

On 23 February 1984 the applicant secured a further loan from the same company on the same terms for the amount of $15,000 per annum. Exhibit 2 shows a second mortgage in favour of the same company being registered on 12 March 1984.

$27,041.30, being capital plus interest, was remitted on 26 February 1984 to the applicant's wife's solicitor in full settlement of the outstanding amount owing pursuant to the Family Court Agreement.

In his evidence to the Tribunal the applicant said he could not recall why he took out a second loan other than he needed the money to meet the interest and legal expenses connected with both his Family Court action and the action brought in the Supreme Court against his brothers.

S5(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1937 is in the following terms:

"All losses as outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which the losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income."

The applicant through his accountant claims that if he had not secured the loans he would have been forced to sell the 1260 acre property which would have led to him losing the means which he hoped to earn his living after the expiry of the lease in favour of his brothers (1 March 1990). He claims that the expenditure was necessarily incurred in order to protect the means by which he would gain or produce assessable income.

The applicant points to Begg v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1937) 4 ATC 257 where interest on monies borrowed to pay debts accruing to a deceased estate rather than use the capital of that estate because of the unduly depressed market prices was allowed as a deduction. It was a term of the Will that the assets could be used to earn income. In that circumstance Mr Acting Justice Reed found that the monies were:

"...wholly and exclusively laid out and expended for the production of assessable income and were outgoings actually incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income for the year".

In other words there was a finding of a direct connection between the interest claimed by way of deduction and the income earning asset.

In Travelodge Papua New Guinea Ltd v Chief Collector of Taxes 85 ATC 4432 the National Court of Papua New Guinea held that interest payments were an allowable deduction where they were incurred during the period of construction of a hotel on the basis that the payments formed an integral part of the taxpayer's business of building and running an hotel.

In Snowden and Willson Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1958] HCA 23; (1958) 99 CLR 431 the Court allowed a deduction for legal expenses incurred by a company appearing before a Royal Commission to defend itself against allegations concerning the integrity and fairness of the way in which it conducted its business. In that case Dixon CJ said (page 436) :

"...it appears to me the carrying on of the business of the nature described brought with it the attacks against which the taxpayer company sought to defend itself. The attacks touched its business nearly; they disparaged the methods by which it was conducted; they were calculated to deter intending or likely customers from dealing with it and destroy the faith of existing customers in their current relations with the company".

In the present case the respondent argues that the interest payments are not connected to the gaining or production of assessable income in the sense that they are not "incidental and relevant to that end" (Ronpibon Tin NL & Tongah Compound NL v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47 at 56). The respondent also argues that an objective examination of the expenditure as against the taxpayer's income earning activities would lead to the conclusion that there is no relationship between the two (Brennan J in Magna Alloys & Research Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 80 ATC 4542 at 4551). Alternatively, the respondent argues that the expenditure is of a private or a domestic nature and thus falls within the exception provided in S51.

The respondent claims that the circumstances are similar in the instant case to those in cases such as -

(i) Case F34 74 ATC 192 - where the taxpayer claimed a deduction for interest and legal costs incurred in borrowing money against the security of Blue Chip Industrial shares pending an increase in the stock market in order to finance the purchase of a home unit where the Board of Review held that the claim for interest was not both incidental and relevant "in gaining or producing" assessable income

(ii) Case B11 70 ATC 46 - where the taxpayer purchased a new home with borrowed monies but retained his former home and leased it to a tenant. The taxpayer claimed a deduction against the rent received from the tenant for the interest paid on the borrowed monies. It was held the deduction was not allowable as "the deductability of interest depends directly and only on the use to which the principle is put" and here the principle was put towards the purchase or a new private residence.

There are other examples where interest incurred has not been an allowable deduction even although the interest was incurred against money borrowed to protect the future of an income producing asset (Sheil v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 86 ATC 4731, Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Munro [1926] HCA 58; (1926) 38 CLR 153).

In each of the cases cited by the applicant the Court found a direct connection between the business or income producing activity of the applicant and the claimed deductions. In each of the cases cited by the respondent no such connection was found and consequently the deduction claimed was not allowed.

In the present case, the applicant borrowed monev in order to satisfy an order of the Family Court. It may be true that he would not have been able to satisfy the order of the Family Court except by the sale of the property if he had not been successful in securing a loan. There is however no connection between the loan and any interest incurred in relation to it and the income earning activities in which the Tribunal has found that the applicant engaged (ie truck. driver and shearer). It is a case where the applicant has used as collateral security an asset (the farm registered in his name) to raise money in order to satisfy a debt totally unconnected with the means by which he earned his income. Consequently any interest incurred in relation to the loan is not tax deductible. For that reason his claim must fail and the decision under review be affirmed.

In view of that decision it is not necessary to address the alternative argument advanced by the respondent that the expenditure was of a "private or domestic nature" and therefore fell within the exception provided in S51.

CONCLUSION

For the reasons given above the decision under review is affirmed.
