Donald Carne and Tax Agent's Board of Victoria [1998] AATA 692 (4 September 1998)

Last Updated: 9 September 1998

Administrative

Appeals

Re Donald Carne

Applicant

And Tax Agents' Board of Victoria

Respondent

Decision No 13257

Tribunal Deputy President B.M. Forrest

Mr D.L. Elsum, AM, Member

Decision The decision under review is affirmed.

CATCHWORDS

TAX AGENTS' - application for registration as a new additional nominee for a company registered as a tax agent - registration refused - whether discretion to disregard a conviction for a serious taxation offence - distinction between an applicant for registration and an applicant for re-registration in exercise of discretion - anomaly deserving of attention by the legislature - decision under review affirmed.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 251A, 251BC(1), 251BC(2), 251BC(3), 251JA(1)(a)(i), 251JA(2), 251KC(1) and 251KC(2)

Crimes Act 1914 (Cth) s. 29D

Hogan v Tax Agents' Board of Victoria (1996) 96 ATC 2066.

REASONS FOR DECISION

4 September 1998 Deputy President B.M. Forrest

Mr D.L. Elsum, AM, Member

Mr C.G. Woodard, Member

The applicant Donald Carne has applied for review of a decision of the Tax Agents' Board of Victoria ("the Board") on 24 March 1998 to refuse his registration as a nominee for Tax Affair Pty Ltd ("Tax Affair") a company registered as a tax agent.

The jurisdiction of the Tribunal to review the decision of the Board arises pursuant to s. 251QA(e) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITA Act").

The background facts are as follows.

On 26 July 1996 the applicant was convicted in the County Court of Victoria following a guilty plea of one charge under s. 29D of the Crimes Act 1914 (Cth) of attempting to defraud the Commonwealth of $49,475 and sentenced to six months imprisonment, wholly suspended upon his entering into a recognisance in the sum of $200 to be of good behaviour for three years.

On 19 November 1997 Tax Affair, applied to the Board to register the applicant as a new additional nominee for the company under s. 251KC(1) of the ITA Act.

On 19 February 1998 the Board wrote to the directors of Tax Affair inviting submissions as to why the applicant ought to be registered having regard to his conviction and to the provisions of s. 251BC(1) and s. 251JA(1) of the ITA Act.

In a reply to the Board on 3 March 1998 Mr Hosking a director of Tax Affair in what amounted to a reference on behalf of the applicant detailed the applicant's experience. The Secretary of the Board in a letter dated 25 March 1998 notified Tax Affair of the decision to decline to grant registration. In his letter the Secretary cited s. 251BC(1) of the ITA Act and added that the reason the Board is unable to grant registration to the applicant as a nominee is because it does not have the discretion to disregard the conviction in the County Court on 26 July 1996.

LEGISLATION

The legislative provisions dealing with the registration of tax agents are contained in Part VIIA of the ITA Act. These provisions so far as relevant provide:

"251A In this Part, unless the contrary intention appears:

...

"conviction", in relation to a person, in relation to an offence, includes the making of an order under section 19B of the Crimes Act 1914 in relation to the person in respect of the offence;

...

"serious taxation offence" means:

(a) an offence against section 29D or 86A of the Crimes Act 1914, being an offence that relates to a tax liability within the meaning of the Taxation Administration Act 1953;

....

251BC(1) [Disqualifying factors] Without limiting the generality of an expression used in this Part, but subject to this section, a person is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters, as at a particular time, if:

(a) the person is not a natural person;

(b) both of the following conditions are satisfied:

(i) the person was not registered as a tax agent, or as a nominee, for the purposes of this Part immediately before the commencement of section 39 of the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No. 2) 1988;

(ii) the person does not hold such qualifications (whether academic, by way of experience or otherwise) as are prescribed;

(c) the person has not attained the age of 18 years;

(d) the person is not of good fame, integrity and character;

(e) the person has been convicted of a serious taxation offence during the previous 5 years; or

(f) the person is under sentence of imprisonment for a serious taxation offence.

251BC(2) [Generality of para. (1)(d)] Nothing in paragraph (1) (e) or (f) limits the generality of paragraph (1) (d).

251BC(3) [Discretion of the Board] Where:

(a) a Board is required, in considering an application for:

(i) re-registration as a tax agent; or

(ii) re-registration of a nominee of a tax agent;

to decide whether the Board is satisfied that a particular person is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters;

(b) the person is not under sentence of imprisonment for a serious taxation offence; and

(c) the Board is satisfied that, because of special circumstances:

(i) a conviction of the person;

(ii) the doing of an act or thing by the person; or

(iii) an omission of the person;

should be disregarded;

the Board may, in making the decision referred to in paragraph (a), disregard the conviction, the doing of the act or thing or the omission, as the case requires.

...

251JA(1) [Requirements for registration] The Board shall register the applicant as a tax agent if the applicant satisfies the Board that:

(a) if the applicant is a natural person:

(i) the applicant is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters;

...

251JA(2) [Refusal of registration in other cases] The Board shall refuse to register the applicant in any other case.

...

251KC(1) [Requirements] The Board shall register or re-register the proposed nominee as a nominee of the tax agent for the purposes of this Part if the Board is satisfied that the proposed nominee is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters.

251KC(2) [Refusal of registration in other cases] The Board shall refuse to register or re-register the proposed nominee in any other case."

Section 29D of the Crimes Act 1914 (Cth) reads:

"29D. A person who defrauds the Commonwealth or a public authority under the

Commonwealth is guilty of an indictable offence."

DISCUSSION

The offence for which the applicant has been convicted, an offence against s. 29D of the Crimes Act 1914 (Cth) is a serious taxation offence in accordance with the meaning given to the terms "conviction" and "serious taxation offence" in s. 251A of the ITA Act. Under the legislative scheme, the Board has the power to register or re-register the proposed nominee as a nominee of the tax agent provided the Board is satisfied the proposed nominee is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters: s. 251KC(1), and in any other case shall refuse to do so: s. 251KC(2). A person is not a fit and proper person if any of the factors in paragraphs 1(a) to (f) of s 251BC(1) apply to the person. For present purposes paragraph (e) operates as a disqualifying factor, a conviction of a serious taxation offence during the past five years. Section 251BC(3) gives the Board a discretion in considering an application for re-registration whether as a tax agent or a nominee of a tax agent to disregard a conviction.

It is apparent from an examination of the language of the scheme that in relation to the discretion a distinction is drawn between an applicant for registration and an applicant for re-registration in that the discretion available to the Board when considering an application in the latter category is not available in the former category. On this point see also Hogan v Tax Agents' Board of Victoria (1996) 96 ATC 2066.

However that distinction has in the present case created an incongruous situation. The applicant's position in regard to the subject application can be contrasted with his individual registration as a tax agent.

In September 1996 the Secretary of the Board wrote to the applicant a "show cause" letter as to why his registration should not be cancelled or suspended in view of his conviction.

After considering the applicant's reply, the Board (in November 1996) decided not to cancel or suspend the applicant's registration. In doing so the Board was acting pursuant to the powers contained in s. 251K(2) which relevantly for present purposes confer upon the Board a discretion whether to suspend or cancel the registration of a tax agent if the Board is satisfied the person is "not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters". The Board exercised its discretion in favour of the applicant when he applied for re-registration as a tax agent. His registration is current.

In the applicant's present circumstances while the Board does not have the power to grant an application for original registration as a nominee of a tax agent (which the applicant who represented himself at the hearing did not dispute), it has the power to grant re-registration irrespective of whether it be for an individual registration or as a nominee of a tax agent. That a discretion may be exercised to enable the applicant be re-registered as a tax agent in his own right while at the same time be fettered in considering an application relating to the same person in another capacity has created an anomalous situation deserving of attention by the legislature.

For these reasons the decision under review is affirmed.
