Re Taxation Appeals [1990] AATA 53; 21 ATR 3344 (13 March 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. WT86/3070

AAT No. 5801

Income Tax

[1990] AATA 53; 21 ATR 3344

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

G.L. McDonald(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Investments in unit trusts - computer based commodity futures trading system - receiver appointed - units purchased to reap "quality dividends" - units capital in nature - taxpayer not "participating in or carrying out any undertaking or scheme".

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 S51, 52

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 S37

Hallstroms Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1946] HCA 34; (1946) 72 CLR 634

Clowes v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1954] HCA 10; (1954) 91 CLR 209

Milne v The Commissioner of Taxation and the Commonwealth of Australia (1976) 113 CLR 526

HEARING

PERTH

13:3:1990

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

The applicant is claiming a deduction of $10,000 for the tax year ended 30 June 1984 in connection with losses made trading on the futures market. He claims the loss under the provisions of S51 and/or S52 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").

2. The applicant represented himself and gave evidence. Additionally the documents filed for the purposes of S37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and other documents were admitted into evidence. The respondent called no evidence. An agreed Statement of Facts was filed. I am satisfied that the facts are as follows.

3. In May 1983 the applicant attended a seminar conducted by a Director of Central Commodities Services Pty Ltd, Mr R Segler, in which a computer based commodity futures trading system known as "Cybercast Action Trading System" ("the Cybercast system") was promoted. Central Commodities Services Pty Ltd had acquired the rights to use the Cybercast system on behalf of Australian investors.

4. In order to obtain sufficient investor funds Central Commodities Services Pty Ltd established ten unit trusts and the applicant subscribed $10,000 to one of those trusts known as the Cypart Unit Trust No 6. Kratock Pty Ltd was the Trustee of the Trust and there were 150,000 units subscribed at $1 per unit. The minimum subscription was 10,000 units. The applicant paid two amounts of $5,000 (one on 17 May 1983 and one on 3 June 1983) and became a registered unit holder on 27 June 1983.

5. By September 1983 following investigation from the New South Wales Corporate Affairs Commission a receiver was appointed to Central Commodities Services Pty Ltd. The applicant was advised by the receiver to submit a proof of debt form. In accordance with the formula set out by the official receiver in his letter of 5 October 1983 the applicant's debt was quantified in the sum of $8,837.69. From a letter received by the Official Receiver dated 5 October 1983 (exhibit D) it would seem Mr Segler and another Director had disappeared after substantial sums of investors money had been withdrawn.

6. In his income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1983 lodged by the applicant on 26 August 1983 the applicant stated:

"I have recently commenced Commodity Futures

Trading with the firm of Central Commodities

Services Pty Ltd, 13-15 O'Connell Street, Sydney,

and the S52 notification for both shares and

commodities is hereby renewed for the year 1983/4".

7. It was not contested that the applicant had been involved with Commodity Futures Trading during the tax years ended 30 June 1981 and 30 June 1982. Both of those years he had claimed and was allowed deductions for losses with respect to the Commodity Futures' transactions which were carried out on his behalf at the discretion of a firm known as Darlington Futures Ltd.

8. Despite an undertaking given in the Cypart Unit Trust No 6 Deed that audited accounts for each three month period would be provided to each unit holder at no stage did the applicant receive any copy of the audited accounts of the trust fund nor did he receive any distribution of income from the trust fund.

9. At the commencement of the proceedings and with the consent of the respondent the applicant amended his grounds to raise the possibility of claiming a deduction under the provisions of S51(1) of the Act. In the light of the notice of objection against assessment filed by the applicant on 17 April 1985 the Tribunal felt it was appropriate that the amendment be allowed.

Were the costs incurred in purchasing the units capital costs?

10. A determination of whether or not the money expended by the applicant for the purchase of the units in the Trust depends on an examination of what the expenditure is calculated to effect from a practical and business point of view (per Dixon J. in Hallstroms Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1946] HCA 34; (1946) 72 CLR 634 at 648).

11. Whilst the applicant could not preclude the possibility of selling the units it is plain from his evidence that he purchased them because he hoped to reap "quality dividends". Whilst, as the applicant said, "everything that earns has value" from the point of view of selling value at the time he purchased the units, he purchased them on the basis that he would retain them indefinitely so long as they earned income. As the units were not purchased for the purposes of resale but for the purposes of earning income they are therefore capital in nature and fall within the exception provided for in S51(1). The applicant cannot therefore succeed under that sub-section.

Does S52 apply?

12. The fact that the applicant has satisfied the proviso as required by S52(1) does not qualify the applicant under the provisions of paragraph 1 of S52(1). In fact the reverse is the case, the proviso only applies after the applicant has otherwise satisfied the provisions of S52(1).

13. The first paragraph of S52(1) is in the following terms:

"S52(1). Any loss incurred by the taxpayer in the

year of income upon the sale of any property or

from the carrying on or carrying out of any

undertaking or scheme, the profit (if any) from

which the sale, undertaking or scheme would have

been included in assessable income, shall be an

allowable deduction".

S52(1) only allows a deduction where, had there been a profit rather than a loss, the profit would have been assessable under S25A of the Act.

14. The case was argued before me on the basis that the applicant was not engaged in an "undertaking or scheme" in the way in which that phrase has been interpreted by the courts. In Clowes v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1954] HCA 10; (1954) 91 CLR 209 at 218 in considering the assessability of the difference between the price paid initially for forestry bonds and the total amount arising from marketing of the timber produced from the land in relation to which the bonds were held the Chief Justice said:

"To enter into a contract to provide a specified

sum on such terms, to pay it and then to await

results cannot in my opinion be properly described

as 'carrying on or carrying out a scheme or

undertaking'. Even if the case is considered

apart from S26(a), then I think the taxpayer's gain

should be held to be a mere enlargement of capital.".

15. His Honour Mr Justice Kitto said (page 224):

"...it must be a profit arising to the taxpayer,

must be a profit arising from the carrying on or

carrying out by him or on his behalf of an

undertaking or scheme, that is to say by him or on

his behalf either alone or with others. The

appellant's profit cannot be said to have arisen to

him from the carrying out by the company of its scheme".

16. The decision in Clowes v Federal Commissioner of Taxation was affirmed by the High Court in Milne v The Commissioner of Taxation and the Commonwealth of Australia (1976) 113 CLR 526.

17. In the instant case the taxpayer subscribed $10,000 towards the purchase of 10,000 units in the Cypart Unit Trust No 6. Kratock Pty Ltd was the Trustee. The Trust Deed excluded any suggestion that there was a relationship of principal and agent between the Trustee and the unit holders (see Clause 4). Consistent with that no unit holder was personally liable for any debt, loss, liability or outgoing incurred by the Trustee with respect to the administration and management of the trust fund (Clause 32(B)).

18. It is quite plain from the fifth schedule to the Trust Deed that the Trustee entered into a management agreement with Robert Lee Segler pursuant to which Segler was to carry on the business of commodity futures trading using the Cybercast system on behalf of the Trustee (see Clause 2 of the fifth schedule). There is nothing in the documentation to suggest that the applicant taxpayer, was himself participating in the "carrying on or carrying out of any undertaking or scheme". He was merely an investor in Cypart Unit Trust No 6 and as such had no power to participate in trading on the futures market whether by using the Cybercast system or otherwise. In those circumstances and having regard to the authorities cited above, he does not qualify for a deduction with respect to his $10,000 investment.

19. In passing it should be noted that the Trustee of the Cypart Unit Trust No 6 and the beneficiary unit holders, one of whom was the applicant, would be subject to the provisions of Division 6 of Part III of the Act, which makes no provision for allowing a beneficiary a deduction for losses incurred by the Trustee of the trust estate.

20. Finally, the applicant pointed to the fact that he succeeded in the 1981 and 1982 tax years in claiming deductions in relation to losses made in commodity futures trading conducted on his behalf by Darlington Futures Ltd. The Tribunal is unaware of the circumstances between the applicant taxpayer and Darlington Futures Ltd, which may have been one giving rise to an agency relationship. Those circumstances are not now before the Tribunal and are not in my view relevant in considering this matter.

21. For the above reasons the decision under review is affirmed.
