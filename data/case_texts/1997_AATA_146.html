Peter Andrew Scherer and Commissioner of Superannuation [1997] AATA 146 (2 May 1997)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

PETER ANDREW SCHERER v. COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. A95/337

AAT

No. 11825

Number of pages - 13

Superannuation



COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

P BURTON (Senior Member), J BRASSIL (Member) AND GRATION (Member) (dissenting)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - Right of "eligible employee" to transfer membership to Public Sector Superannuation Scheme - whether precluded by act of resignation from exercising the right - consequences of resignation on right to elect within prescribed time - whether deemed "eligible employee" until right exercised or expiration of prescribed time - whether s244 of the Superannuation Act 1976 (Cth) open to two constructions - construing legislation in favour of the recipient of the benefit where meaning not clear.

LEGISLATION

Superannuation Act 1976 (Cth)

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth)

Superannuation Act 1990 (Cth)

HEARING

CANBERRA, 13 February 1997 (hearing), 2 May 1997 (decision)

2:5:1997

Representative for the B Dube

ORDER

The decision under review is set aside and the tribunal directs that the respondent recognise the declaration made by the applicant on 19 November 1993 as a validly executed declaration and election under s244 of the Superannuation Act 1976.

DECISION

P BURTON, J BRASSIL AND GRATION (dissenting)

MAJORITY REASONS FOR DECISION

1. This is an application for review of a decision made by the Commissioner of Superannuation on 22 November 1995 refusing the applicant's request made on 19 November 1993 to make an election under s244(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 (Cth) to transfer to the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme (the PSSS).

2. The tribunal had before it the documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth). The parties were represented and it was indicated at the outset that no oral evidence would be offered. Counsel for the parties handed up written submissions and spoke to them.

3. The facts of the matter are not in dispute. The applicant, Dr Scherer, is a 52 year old man. He joined the Australian Public Service in 1979 and became an "eligible employee" for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") on 21 February 1979. On 24 September 1986 he commenced leave without pay (LWOP) from his employer, the Department of Employment & Industrial Relations ("the Department"), to begin work with the OECD in France. His LWOP ceased on 19 August 1993. Dr Scherer returned to duty with the Department for the period 19 and 20 August 1993, having notified his intention to resign to take up work with the OECD. He was an "eligible employee" under the Act up to and including this time.

4. On 20 August 1993 Dr Scherer tendered his resignation and completed Superannuation Election Form (SE1) in which he indicated his intention to remain a member of the CSS. He, or someone on his behalf (see note on the form at T19, f83) also completed Benefit Application Form (Form SBA) in which he elected to preserve all his superannuation rights. Had he not completed the SE1 form he would have remained a member of the CSS in any event, by default, and it is agreed by the parties that nothing turns on the fact that this election was made, other than the fact that this purported election was made before his resignation and while he was an "eligible employee". On 19 November 1993 the applicant wrote to the Commissioner for Superannuation requesting permission to make an election to join the PSSS (T21 f95). In a letter of 19 January 1994 (T26 f105) Dr Scherer further explained:

... I did not have the opportunity to make a fully informed choice as to whether I should elect for the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme. It has now become very clear to me that the correct election in my case was to transfer to the PSS scheme - both for the technical reasons set out in (the letter of 19 November 1993) and because in view of my present financial commitments and the pension scheme of my current employer, continuing with the deferred benefit provisions of the CSS makes no sense. I therefore ask that you treat me as having made an election for the PSSS on 20 August.

5. By letter of 24 January 1994 the Delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation informed the applicant of his decision to refuse the applicant a transfer to the PSSS. The Delegate reasoned:

As your request to transfer to the PSSS was made on 19 November 1993, some three months after your resignation, you cannot be considered to be an "eligible employee" under the Act at the time your request was made and therefore you cannot transfer to the PSSS.
It is not disputed that the notification of 19 November 1993 was within the three months allowed under s244(3)(c) of the Act. The dispute centres on whether the applicant was entitled under the legislation to make the election after his resignation, though no longer an "eligible employee"; or alternatively, whether the applicant was deemed to have the status of an "eligible employee" for the purpose of making a final election before the expiration of the prescribed period after which time the applicant was precluded from making the election under s244(3)(c).

Legislation

6. The relevant provisions of the Act are as follows:

s3. "eligible employee" means:

(a) a person who, immediately before the commencement of this Act, was an employee for the purposes of the superceded Act and has continued to be, and is, such an employee; (b) a person who is a permanent employee....

s244. (1) Subject to this section, an eligible employee who is not precluded by or under the Superannuation Act 1990 (other than by paragraph 6(2)(a) of that Act) from being a member of the Superannuation (1990) Scheme may, in writing addressed to the Commissioner: (a) declare that he or she wishes to become a member of that scheme; and (b) elect to cease to be an eligible employee. (2) An eligible employee may not make an election and declaration under subsection (1) during any period when the eligible employee is absent from duty on leave of absence without pay. ..... (3) An eligible employee may not make an election and declaration under subsection (1): (a) if the eligible employee was, on 31 March 1991 or any later day, precluded by or under the Superannuation Act 1990 (other than by paragraph 6(2)(a) of that Act) from being a member of the Superannuation (1990) Scheme - after the period of 3 months commencing on the day on which the eligible employee ceased to be so precluded; or (b) if the person becomes an eligible employee after 1 April 1991 and paragraph (a) does not apply - after the period of 3 months commencing on the day on which the person becomes an eligible employee; or (c) if the eligible employee is precluded under subsection (2) from making the election and declaration during a period that ends after 31 March 1991 - after the period of three months commencing immediately after the termination of that period; or (ca) if the eligible employee is precluded under subsection (2B) from making the election and declaration during a period that ends after 31 March 1991 - after the period of 3 months commencing immediately after the termination of that period; or (d) in any other case - after 30 June 1991.

s245. A person who makes a declaration and election under section 244 is taken to have ceased to be an eligible employee at the end of the day on which the declaration and election are made.

The relevant provisions of s6 of the Superannuation Act (1990) are as follows:

s6. (1) Subject to subsection (2), each of the following persons is, by force of this section, a member of the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme: (a) a permanent employee; (b) the Commissioner; (c) the holder of a statutory office who has made an election under section 7; (d) a temporary employee whose election under section 8 has effect; (e) ... (2) In spite of subsection (1), a person is not a member of the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme if the person: (a) is an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976; or (b) is a person to whom the Judges Pensions Act 1968 applies; or (ba) is a person: (i) to whom invalidity pension, or a deferred benefit by way of invalidity pension, is, or but for the suspension of payment would have been, payable to him or her under the Superannuation Act 1976; or (ii) to whom a pension of a kind mentioned in section 64A or 65 of the Superannuation Act 1922 as in force immediately before the repeal of that section, or a deferred benefit by way of a pension payable for the reason mentioned in subsection 119ZA(1) of that Act, is, or but for the suspension of payment would have been, payable to him or her; or (iii) to whom deferred benefits are applicable under the Superannuation Act 1976; or (iv) to whom deferred benefits are applicable under the Superannuation Act 1922 and who has not become an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976; or (v) who is a re-employed former contributor with preserved rights for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 and who has not become an eligible employee for the purposes of that Act; other than such a person who is a casual employee within the meaning of the Rules or who became a member of the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme before the commencement of section 68 of the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act 1991 and has continued to be such a member; or (c) is declared, in writing, by the Minister to be a person to whom this section does not apply. (3) Paragraph (2)(a) does not exclude a person from membership of the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme in relation to particular employment if the person is an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 in relation to other employment.

The parties' contentions

7. The applicant contends that on 19 November 1993 he made a valid declaration and election pursuant to the Act to effect a transfer of his membership of the CSS to the PSSS. The applicant says that on 19 August 1993, pursuant to s244(1), he acquired the right to declare that he wished to become a member of the PSS scheme and to elect to cease to be an eligible employee. He had been precluded by s244(2) from making this declaration and election in the period he was on LWOP, and by s244(3)(c) was precluded from making such declaration and election after the expiration of three months commencing at the end of his LWOP. Thus, the applicant had from the resumption of duty on 19 August 1993 to 19 November 1993, that is three months commencing after the termination of LWOP, to make the relevant declaration and election. He contends that this right continued until the expiration of that period notwithstanding that he had resigned in the meantime. He contends further, or in the alternative, that s245, in specifying that eligible employment ceases at the end of the day on which the declaration and election are made, supports his submission that his status of eligible employee was maintained until the date he declared his wish to transfer to the PSSS.

8. The respondent says that once the applicant had resigned he was no longer an "eligible employee" as defined by the Act, and, not being a "permanent employee" as required by s6(1)(a) of the Superannuation Act 1990 ("the 1990 Act") for the purposes of membership of the PSSS he has no right to elect to become a member of the PSSS. The respondent contends that s245 only applies to persons who were "eligible employees" at the time of making the declaration and election.

The issues

9. The issue to be determined is whether s244(1) requires a person to be an "eligible employee" at the time of making a declaration and election, as against being an "eligible employee" at the time when the right to make the declaration and election was acquired. That is, whether the right given by s244(1) is unfettered for the prescribed period in which it could be exercised, or whether the right was extinguished by the act of resignation. Resignation would ordinarily extinguish the status of an "eligible employee", unless, as the applicant contends, s245 deems a person to retain the status of "eligible employee" for the purposes of the section notwithstanding his or her resignation. This gives rise to the further issue raised by the applicant of whether s245 intends to preserve that status until a declaration and election are made by the eligible employee, or until the expiration of three months, whichever is the earlier.

Applying the legislation

10. Subsection 244(1) is a permissive provision. It allows an eligible employee the option of transferring to the PSSS. The subsection does not distinguish between the giving of the right and its exercise. It permits the option to transfer to the PSSS by providing for the way in which the option is to be communicated to the Commissioner. To effect that choice an eligible employee is required to both declare the wish to become a member of the new fund, and to elect to cease to be an "eligible employee" in writing addressed to the Commissioner. However, ss(2) and (3)(c) are cast in restrictive terms. Ss244(2) precludes the exercise of the right to declare and elect by an "eligible employee" while he or she is on LWOP, and ss244(3)(c) precludes that "eligible employee" on LWOP to whom ss(2) refers from making the election and declaration outside the prescribed period of three months commencing from the end of the LWOP period. The effect of these subsections is to grant a window of three months in which an eligible employee on LWOP can, on the termination of LWOP, exercise the right given in ss(1). Thus, within s244 a distinction is made between the granting of the right to opt to transfer to the new fund, and the timing of making the election.

11. The mechanism provided in s244(1) to effect the transfer to the new fund requires two distinct steps to be taken. Both the declaration and election are essential to effect the transfer. While the right to transfer is open only to an eligible employee, that person must cease being an eligible employee (and a contributor to the CSS), before becoming a member of the new fund. Thus, while the requirement that the person must elect to cease being an eligible employee appears to presuppose that the person declaring and electing is an eligible employee at the time of taking those steps, the primary action is the declaration of a wish to transfer, and the secondary, consequential action is the election to cease to be an "eligible employee".

12. The legislation does not expressly require the declaration and election to be made simultaneously, though it is cast in terms which assume that to be the case. The prescribed mechanism for an eligible employee to take both steps simultaneously ensures that the eligible employee who makes the declaration does not remain an eligible employee. The rationale for ensuring that the person does not remain an eligible employee is to ensure that the person ceases to be a contributing member of the CSS on becoming a member of the PSSS. The applicant's act of resignation, causing him to cease being an eligible employee, did not interfere with that object. Does the section intend to extinguish an eligible employee's right to transfer, if the person resigns within the prescribed time before exercising that right? Was the applicant's act of resignation fatal to the exercise of the right he acquired as an eligible employee?

13. A person has to be an "eligible employee" to acquire the right to transfer to the PSSS. Once that person has acquired the right and a consideration period of not more than three months, then on one view, all the person has to do is to make the declaration. As that declaration would be negated if that person remained an "eligible employee", the legislation requires the further necessary action of election if that impediment still exists. If the person had already ceased being an eligible employee, such an election would be of no effect. Put another way, once a member of the CSS acquires the right to transfer to the PSSS there must first be a declaration of a wish to transfer and then, in order to validate that declaration, that person must elect to no longer claim the status of "eligible employee" because not to do so would conflict with the proper administration of the respective funds. Consistent with this view is that the second leg of the legislative requirement only becomes necessary after the declaration is made and would not be necessary if, during the period granted by ss244(3)(c) to the applicant, he had rid himself of this impediment by his resignation.

14. The meaning of ss244(1) when read alone, seems to require that the person must be an eligible employee at the time of exercising the right given. However, ss244(3)(c), though cast in restrictive terms, in effect grants an eligible employee on LWOP a three month period commencing at the end of the LWOP period in which the declaration and election can be made. The object of ss(3)(c) is to provide a three month period in which that person can consider his or her situation in relation to the choice of fund membership. This is in line with the prescribed periods granted elsewhere in the Act to retiring members in relation to elections to preserve superannuation benefits or the taking of a lump sum. When ss244(1) is read in conjunction with ss244(3)(c) the section is open to a construction which allows for an eligible employee who acquires the right to transfer his or her membership to the PSSS, to exercise that right at any time within the prescribed period, notwithstanding the person resigns in the meantime.

15. S244 is also open to the construction that, despite the granting of the prescribed period in ss(3)(c), an eligible employee who acquires the right to make a declaration and an election and who then resigns before exercising that right, is precluded from so doing, having lost his status as an eligible employee. It might be argued that the legislation intended this consequence, on the ground that having resigned, the person no longer has the status of permanent employee. S244 clearly caters for those eligible employees who remain in permanent employment in the service. On the other hand, the legislation is not concerned with the employment as such, but with eligibility for fund membership. S244 requires a person to cease to be an eligible employee before becoming a member of the PSSS, as an eligible employee for the purposes of the Act is required to be a contributor to the CSS (except whilst on LWOP when exempted by s51 from so contributing), and a person cannot be a contributing member of both funds.

16. The legislation granted the applicant three months from termination of the period of his LWOP to make the prescribed declaration and election. It is not disputed by the parties that it is open under the provisions of the Act for a person resuming duty after a period of LWOP, though having notified of the intention to resign, from making the requisite declaration and election on the day of their return, a week later, or any time within the prescribed time, and then to resign. That is, the legislation does not state that the right to transfer to the new fund is conditional on a person remaining a permanent employee for any particular period of time.

17. In the applicant's case, on 20 August 1993 whilst he was an eligible employee, he indicated his intention to resign and to remain a member of the CSS. The effect of this declaration is the same as if he had made no declaration or election at all. He resigned effective from that day, and on 19 November 1993, within the time prescribed by ss244(3)(c), he reconsidered his situation and declared his wish to transfer his fund membership to the PSSS. It is not disputed that had the applicant declared this wish on 19 or 20 August 1993, and notwithstanding his foreshadowed imminent resignation, the transfer of fund membership would have been recognised. Both Dr Scherer's resignation, and his later declaration of his wish to transfer to the PSSS, took place after he acquired the right as an eligible employee to make the declaration and election, and within the prescribed time. The ss244(1) requirements, taking into account the time allowed by ss(3)(c) in which the required steps are to be taken, were then fulfilled by Dr Scherer's actions, though not simultaneously. If the section is construed to mean that the declaration of a wish to transfer to the new fund is effective only if the person notifies of the declaration and election simultaneously, (and within the prescribed period), that is that he is an eligible employee at the time of making the declaration and election, then the applicant has failed to meet the requirements of ss244(1). The respondent submits that the section is open to no other construction.

18. Such a construction is only open if one construes the requirements in ss244(1) to be a mandatory requirement that the declaration and election are to be made simultaneously. The terms of s245 are open to support such a construction. In further support, the respondent points to the requirement of s6 of the 1990 Act, that only permanent employees are eligible for membership of the PSSS. That section declares that a permanent employee (other than an eligible employee) is a member of the PSSS by force of the section. The respondent argues that the applicant lost his status of an eligible employee by the act of resignation, and not being a permanent employee, is unable to become a member of the PSSS, and that s244 must be construed in that light.

19. However, as we have indicated above, s244 of the Act is open to an alternative construction, namely that an eligible employee who acquires the right to make a declaration and election under s244(1), is given an unfettered right to effect the transfer to the new fund within the time prescribed under ss(2) and (3)(c), the act of resignation being no barrier to the exercise of these rights. On one view, the applicant fulfilled both these requirements. By his act of resignation he ceased to be an eligible employee, albeit at the beginning of the prescribed period, and he fulfilled the other requirement by declaring his wish to transfer, though he did this at the end of the prescribed period, having reconsidered his situation in the period of grace given by ss244(3)(c). In any event, the election requirement is a consequential one to the primary requirement of making the declaration of the wish to transfer, the election only being necessary to remove the impediment of CSS fund membership.

20. The respondent referred the tribunal to the Explanatory Memorandum. It refers to ss244(2) as precluding an eligible employee from making an election while on LWOP until the person's "return to work". While that may have been the intention of the legislature, we note the subsection states "on termination of the period of LWOP". Whether or not the section is open to the construction that an eligible employee on LWOP who resigns before returning to work has the benefit of the prescribed period in which to make an election is not in issue in this case. The situation is distinguishable from the applicant's case, as the person who resigns whilst on LWOP is precluded by ss(2) from acquiring the right to declare and elect, which right the applicant did acquire prior to his resignation. The Explanatory Memorandum also refers to the option of transferring to the new fund being available to a person whilst an eligible employee. The legislation does not use the word "whilst", and while it may have been the intention of the legislature to effect that outcome, for the reasons outlined above, the provisions are open to the construction that only the right has to be acquired whilst the person is an eligible employee. The Explanatory Memorandum does not directly address the issue of whether the two legs of ss244(1) must be simultaneous and the inference the respondent asks us to draw is not an obvious one.

21. Where beneficial legislation is open to two constructions when applied to particular factual situations, it should be construed in favour of the recipient of the benefit, unless there is a specific exclusion or extinguishment of the right. The construction of the legislation urged upon the tribunal by the respondent, when applied to the applicant's circumstances, would grant the applicant three months in which to consider his situation, or until his resignation, which ever first occurred. In the applicant's case such a construction has the arbitrary consequence of granting the applicant a twenty-four hour period to consider his superannuation options in lieu of the three month period prescribed by ss(3)(c). If the legislation intended a person in the applicant's position to have less than the prescribed period in which to consider the option given, it should say so expressly; that is, it should expressly state that the prescribed time is three months, or until resignation, which ever first occurs; or that the granting of the prescribed period is conditional on the option being exercised and completed prior to resignation. It does not do so. There is no provision expressly precluding a person from exercising the right to transfer fund membership by the intervening act of resignation.

22. Section 244 is a transitional provision. It provides an option for certain "eligible employees" to transfer to the PSSS, a newer scheme, and it imposes time limits in relation to the exercise of that option. The status of "eligible employee" is relevant to the employee's status as a contributor to a superannuation scheme, the CSS. Resignation and early retirement affects that status and impacts on available benefits. A retiring scheme member is permitted under the Act to make certain elections in respect of his or her fund entitlements. The applicant had already indicated that he wished to preserve his benefits, and in the absence of an election he remained a member of the CSS. Superannuation benefits are payable on resignation, retirement, and invalidity. Retirement and superannuation benefits go hand in hand. The legislation clearly permits a person to take advantage of the right to transfer to the new fund prior to resigning. Having indicated his intention to resign and work for the OECD, the applicant was under pressure to tender his resignation (see T14). If it is open to construe the legislation consistent with its object of allowing the applicant a period in which to consider the transfer option, that construction is to be preferred to the construction which has the consequence of depriving him of the benefit of the legislation, particularly in the absence of any specific intention to the contrary.

23. What then is the effect of s245? By prescribing the time at which the person ceases to be an eligible employee this section ensures there is no overlap of fund membership. The words of the section are cast in terms which assume that the declaration and election take place on the same day. That would normally be the case. The section is a mechanical one, deeming the opting employee to cease being an eligible employee at the end of the day upon which he makes the declaration and election. The applicant submits that s245 deems a person to be an eligible employee up to the time he or she makes the relevant declaration and election. In support of this interpretation the applicant points to the use of the word "person" rather than "eligible employee". We doubt this is its effect. The use of the word "person" cannot in our opinion be construed as deeming a person to retain the status of an eligible employee, that is a contributing member of the CSS, when that person is no longer a permanent employee. The section cannot be construed as extending the time in which the status of an eligible employee is to be maintained. The section must refer to persons who were, but for their election, eligible employees. It determines the time by which the status of eligible employee is to have ceased. Membership of the PSSS cannot commence until the person has ceased to be a member of the CSS. S245 is intended to regulate the timing of the person ceasing to be a member of one fund and commencing membership of the other to ensure no overlap of fund membership.

24. The applicant ceased being an eligible employee on 20 August 1993, but he remained a member of the CSS fund. In declaring his wish to be a member of the PSSS on 19 November 1993, he ceased his CSS membership by the end of that day by force of s245.

25. The respondent put submissions in relation to the effect of s157(1) of the Act, which provision deals with late elections in relation to superannuation benefits. It reads:

s.157 (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, where an election under this Act is made by a person after the expiration of the period allowed by or under this Act for the making of the election, and the Commissioner is satisfied that in all the circumstances of the case it is desirable that the election should be recognized, the Commissioner may direct that the election be treated as if it had been made within the period allowed and the election shall have effect accordingly.
The section does not apply in the applicant's case. His declaration and election were within the prescribed time. He is not seeking to make a late election.

26. We set aside the decsion under review and decide that the declaration should be recognised as a validly executed declaration and election under s244 of the Act and direct the Commissioner to treat the declaration and election as being validly made on 19 November 1993.

DISSENTING REASONS

Air Marshal Gration AO AFC, Member

1. The applicant's case rests on a reading of the relevant Acts which would allow a person, once he has established a right to transfer from the CSS to the PSSS, to do so within the prescribed period irrespective of any change of "eligible employee" status at the time of seeking transfer. This reading of the Acts relies, in the applicant's case, specifically on the s3 definition of "eligible employee" and the transfer provisions of the 1976 Act at s244 (1), (2), (2A), (3)(c) and s245, and the definition of membership of the PSSS in the 1990 Act s6(1a).

2. At this point it is useful to review the relevant structure of the 1976 Act and the particular transfer provisions. At s3, "eligible employee" includes, at (b),"a person who is a permanent employee". It follows that a person, who was considered an "eligible employee" because of his "permanent employee" status, would no longer be an "eligible employee" under (b) if that status were lost. At s80, provision is made for persons who cease to be "eligible employees" and who are not entitled to receive any of the retirement, invalidity or death benefits, to receive a lump sum of accumulated contributions. The Act then develops all the various rights and considerations, including, at s131, the general provision that, after resignation or retirement, a period of grace of 3 months after such resignation or retirement is allowed during which an election involving deferred benefits can be made. Further, at s157, the Commissioner is given the discretion to consider such elections beyond the period of grace if the circumstances so warrant. Then, in Part XII Transitional Provisions, provision is made for "`eligible employees"' to transfer to the PSSS if they so wish, including in general a period of grace of 3 months for those "eligible employees" who would otherwise have less time than this during which to consider such transfer. However, in contrast with the provision of s131, nowhere in Part XII (or elsewhere) is the Commissioner given any discretion to consider transfer of persons who have ceased to be "eligible employees".

3. When the new PSSS was created, provision was made for current contributors to the CSS to have the option of transferring to the PSSS. As described in the respondent's written submission, the Explanatory Memorandum for the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Bill 1990 makes clear that this transfer option was to be available only to "eligible employees" and only to those "eligible employees" who were otherwise eligible to become members of the PSSS under the prescription of s6(1) and (2) of the 1990 Act. Accordingly, the Transitional Provisions of Part XII of the 1976 Act are relevant only to an "eligible employee". Specifically, the wording of s244(1) allows an "eligible employee" to have the transfer option and, by the drafting convention of exclusion, only an "eligible employee" may exercise that option. That this must be so is apparent from the precise wording of the second leg of the option at s244(1)(b): "elect to cease to be an eligible employee".

4. Having provided, at subsection(1), for the transfer option to be available to "eligible employees", subsection (2) then precludes that option from being exercisable while an "eligible employee" is "absent from duty on leave of absence without pay". Subsection 2A then allows an "eligible employee" who has returned from such leave without pay to exercise the transfer option only if the period of leave without pay commenced before 1 July 1991.

5. Subsection (3) then prescribes the terminating point beyond which the transfer option may no longer normally be made. For the majority of "eligible employees" this is 1 July 1991, after a period of grace of one year. For an "eligible employee" for whom subsection 2A is relevant and whose period of leave without pay concludes after 31 March 1991, the transfer option cannot be exercised after the expiration of 3 months commencing the day after the return to work. The intent of sub-subsections (3)(a) to (3)(ca) inclusive, presumably, is, consistent with the general intent of s131, to allow a period of grace of 3 months to those "eligible employees" whose circumstances would otherwise allow only a lesser period for consideration of the transfer option.

6. It is worthwhile noting at this point that, as was brought out in questioning of the applicant's counsel by the tribunal, the precise wording of subsection (3)(c) does not guarantee a period of three months in all cases: it merely precludes exercise of the transfer option by an "eligible employee" after the expiry of 3 months; it says nothing about persons who, during that period, cease to be "eligible employees".

7. Finally, s245 describes the timing effect of the election to cease being an "eligible employee", viz, "at the end of the day on which the declaration and election are made". Taken in isolation from the context of the Act, the use of the words "A person" might be construed to refer to a person no longer an "eligible employee" who nevertheless makes a declaration and election. However, considering that s244(1) allows only an "eligible employee" to exercise the transfer option, such a reading of s245 is clearly untenable. The correct reading is, in my opinion, that s245 simply specifies the last day of contribution to the CSS before transfer as being the day on which the declaration and election is made.

8. I turn now to analysis of the applicant's case.

9. It is perhaps worth noting that, apart from a reference to the respondent's letter of 24 January 1994 and an immediate rejection of the relevant statement in it, the applicant's written submission avoids any acknowledgement of Dr Scherer's resignation on 20 August 1993. This is probably not surprising since the applicant's application must fail if the effect of such resignation is to preclude subsequent transfer.

10. Taking the applicant's points in order as made in the written submission, it is first submitted that Dr Scherer made a valid declaration and election on 19 November 1993. In my opinion this could not be so on two counts: firstly, not being an "eligible employee" on that date he was precluded by the 1976 Act, s244(1), from making a valid declaration and election; and secondly, not being a member of the class of persons prescribed by the 1990 Act, s6(1), he was ineligible to be a member of the PSSS.

11. The applicant's submission then claims that, since 19 November 1993, Dr Scherer has been a member of the PSSS. For the reasons just stated, this cannot be so. Following through the numbered points in the applicant's submission, points 1-3 are agreed. As a generalised expression of the intent of the Act in respect of "eligible employees" to whom s244(2),(2A) and (3)(c) apply, it is agreed; but, in Dr Scherer's case, his status as an "eligible employee" ceased with effect 21 August 1993 when he resigned. Hence, his option to transfer was extinguished. Point 5 is agreed, but then the chronology should record Dr Scherer's resignation and consequent change of status. Point 6 is rejected as an incorrect reading of the Acts: firstly, s244(3)(c) was inapplicable because Dr Scherer was no longer an "eligible employee"; secondly, Dr Scherer was no longer entitled, under s244(1), to declare his wish to join the PSSS, nor was he entitled to be a member thereof; and thirdly, Dr Scherer's letter of 19 November 1993 did not meet the requirements of s244(1). Point 7 is rejected for the reasons described at para 7 above. And consequently Point 8 is rejected in its entirety.

12. During the hearing, it was suggested that, as the Superannuation Acts are held to be beneficial to members, where a matter of interpretation arises, the interpretation in favour of the member should - other things being equal - be preferred. However, for the reasons already given, it is my opinion that there is no question here of interpretation. At the most, there has been speculation that Parliament may have intended that resignation should not have the effect of extinguishing an otherwise available period of 3 months for consideration of transfer. It is my belief that, if indeed such was intended, then the 1976 Act, Part XII, would have been worded or constructed differently, perhaps with a provision like s131 to apply to transfer. But such possibilities are, in my opinion, irrelevant, because the wording of s244 is unambiguous in its applicability to "eligible employees" only (consistent with the Explanatory Memorandum) and leaves no room for the interpretations made by the applicant.

13. Accordingly, it is my opinion that the Commissioner for Superannuation and his delegates were correct in their rejection of Dr Scherer's application that his election to remain within the CSS be treated as if it had been an election to transfer to the PSSS. As concluded by the respondent in its written submission: "There are no provisions within the 1976 Act which give the respondent the discretion (and therefore the tribunal by virtue of Section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975) in which to allow the applicant's request to transfer to the PSSS." It is my opinion, therefore, that the decision under review should be affirmed.
