ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Assessable Income - pastor's use of a church-supplied manse - value of benefit - reduction of value to account for business usage of manse

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s.26(e), s . 26AAAA, S.26AAAB

DECISION AND REASONS

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No: QT83/50

AAT Decision No 5342

Date: 17 August 1989

Place: Brisbane

Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. QT83/50

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Tribunal: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

The Tribunal decides to set aside the objection decision under review and allow the objection in part by reducing the assessable income by an amount of $57.

(Sgd) K L Beddoe

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. QT83/50

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 17 August 1989

Place: Brisbane

It is my practice to commence reasons by stating the essence of the question or questions to be decided in the particular application. I do not do so in this case because it would be the first time in these proceedings that the question to be decided had been formulated.

  1. The applicant is a lay pastor of the Uniting Church of Australia. The claim arises in the following circumstances.
  2. As part of the conditions of his appointment the applicant is granted use of a house, described as a manse, for the duration of the applicant's appointment to a particular parish. During the year ended 30 June 1980 the applicant was appointed to a coastal town ("S") for the first half of the year and to an inland town ("C") for the second half of the year. It is relevant to note that the coastal town ("S") is within Zone B for the purposes of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act"). The inland town was not in either Zone A or Zone B. Although I have described S as a coastal town that town is not a town renowned for its location nor as a tourist attraction - its raison d'etre is as a centre of commence. It is merely proximate to the coast.
  3. In his return of income for the year ended 30 June 1980 the applicant disclosed salary and other items of income including value of residences provided free by his employer in an amount of $585. That amount was returned as income on advice from church authorities, who, it seems, were relying on advice from the Taxation Office. The applicant claimed a number of

    deductions, including the following

$

Rent of Study (30% of $585) 175

Lighting of Study 35

Cleaning of Study 26

The amount of $175 claimed as rent was disallowed in the assessment made by the respondent and notified to the applicant.

  1. The applicant lodged an objection which, so far as is relevant, reads as follows:

"I hereby lodge an objection to my tax assessment. My claim for $175 for rent of study for professional purposes has been disallowed. I consider this to be quite unfair as this claim has been allowed in previous years and is still an allowable deduction.

A manse is set up as part of the running of a parish and is therefore very different to a private home. It is used quite extensively by the public both day and night and includes being called out at any hour during the night with no overtime, penalty rates or time off.

My wife is involved with interviews and counselling (without any pay) when I am out of town on parish work or out visiting in town, as well as when a number of people arrive at the same time. When a number of people come at the same time the lounge and kitchen are also used.

The telephone is also used by parishioners for both incoming and outgoing calls. The toilet and bathroom are regularly used and sometimes the bedroom. Out of town parishioners are made welcome, all this is part of ministry - business from which my income is derived. I therefore consider my claim more than justifiable."

  1. Given that the applicant did not pay rent for the use of the respective manses (this was made clear to the respondent by the fact of the return of the value of the use of the manses as assessable income) the claim must be understood to be a claim for reduction of the value of the benefit arising from the use without charge of each manse. The respondent understood that the claim was made on that basis. In document T12 which forms part of the respondent's response to the applicant's objection it is stated that the "value of $585 included in your assessable income takes into account the business usage of the residence provided to you".
  2. The evidence established that in each case the manse was used for what was described as "public purposes". That use arose it seems from the fact that the manses were used by members of the Church when visiting the particular town and by travellers. That use also included use by the applicant in the course of his work.
  3. At S the study was an accessible room off the lounge room and from the evidence seems to have been adequate for the purpose. What was clearly not adequate for the purpose was the house itself. Photographs of the house are in evidence before the Tribunal and those photographs reveal a house literally bounded by roads on three sides without any useful curtilage. The weekly rental value of such a house in February 1978 was, on the evidence, no more than $20 per week. The manse was in a poor position in the town being adversely affected by heavy transport and close to the main railway line and the cane train line. The valuer for the respondent did not take particular use of the manse into account when fixing the rental value.
  4. At C the manse was located on the same property as the church building. There again a study was provided which was accessible and adequate. There appears to have been more general use of the house by a greater number of visitors at C. The manse was in a better locality and a superior standard of building than the manse at S. However, in no sense could the manse be said to be more than an average low set house of the style built in inland towns during the sixties. Unlike the house at S this house was well maintained. A valuation made by a valuer employed by the respondent put the rental value at $40 per week after making allowance for the situation of the house next to the church building and the fact that church members used the premises to make phone calls and for overnight accommodation. That valuation was later revised to $42 per week to take into account a fourth bedroom. That is somewhat surprising if the fact of providing accommodation to church members was considered to be a detriment. I will return to the fourth bedroom which was, in effect, the study.
  5. It will now be apparent that the question at issue is whether the respondent has given appropriate weight to the use of a room in each manse for the conduct of church business when determining the value of the benefit for the purposes of paragraph 26(e) of the Act.
  6. The relevant statutory provisions are paragraph 26(e), sections 26AAAA and 26AAAB of the Act. The respondent asserted in the section 37 statement that sub-section 51(1) of the Act was the relevant statutory provision - that is clearly not the case.
  7. By an amended assessment notice of which was dated 23 June 1982 the respondent reduced the amount to be included in assessable income in respect of the manse at C to $4.20 per week (Documents T30 and T31). The adjustment was made following the respondent's acknowledgment that section 26AAAB applied in respect of the manse at C.
  8. The result of the respondent's adjustments can only be described as manifestly absurd and unreasonable. The benefit to the applicant of the use of a poor standard of house located in a Zone B town is said to be more than 2.5 times the benefit of a better house located in a non zone town.
  9. That adjustment had the effect of allowing to the applicant a reduction in the taxable income of $184. The adjustment is based upon section 26AAAB which was not effectively raised by the notice or objection. The respondent, in making the amended assessment, did not purport to change the earlier objection decision.
  10. The effect of that amended assessment was to reduce the weekly value assessed in respect of the manse at C to $4.20. Any adjustment which could be open to the Tribunal in respect of the manse at C cannot exceed 175/585 of that amount which is $32 for the six months the applicant was at C.
  11. In respect of the house at S the maximum adjustment open to the Tribunal in terms of the notice of objection is $87.
  12. There is merit in the applicant's case because it is established by the evidence that the respondent did not give adequate consideration as to the discount that should be applied to recognise use of the respective manses by the applicant in the business of the Church. That is different to use of the manses by members of the Church - a disadvantage which was acknowledged by the respondent. That the respondent failed to give sufficient weight to the use of the office in the manse for church business is demonstrated by the revised valuation made in respect of the manse at C. The revised valuation specifically ignored the use of the fourth bedroom as a study used for church business and increased the value to $42 per week.
  13. Although there is not a specific valuation in respect of the house at S, I am satisfied that the respondent failed to give sufficient weight to the use of the study in that manse for church business. In my view, and drawing support from the revised valuation made in respect of the house at C, the rental value for both manses should be reduced by $2 per week. The reduction to assessable income will be $52 in respect of the house at S and $5 in respect of the house at C.
  14. The objection decision under review will be set aside and the objection allowed in part by reducing the assessable income by an amount of $57.
