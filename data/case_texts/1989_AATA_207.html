ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

TAXATION - s.167 Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 (Cth.) whether or not Commissioner's ascertainment or judgment of income excessive - internal evidence in Special Examiner's Report. Power of Tribunal to give procedural directions. Summons to Commissioner to produce internal reports.

REASONS FOR DECISION

18 September 1989 Deputy President C.J.Bannon, Q.C.

The applicant taxpayer seeks review of decisions of the Commissioner for Taxation disallowing objections by the taxpayer to amended assessments of tax for the years ended 30 June 1979, 1980, 1981, 1982 and 1983. By consent the matters were heard together. For the year ended 30 June 1979 there were two amended assessments dated respectively 4 April 1986 and 10 March 1988. Nothing really turns upon the second amendment to the assessment for the year ended 30 June 1979, nor upon the lengthy, voluminous and almost incomprehensible notices of objection lodged in each matter. If they serve no other purpose, they highlight the difficulties of an unsophisticated system of review when applied to the sophisticated terminology of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 (Cth.) ("the Act"), and the sophisticated arrangements i made by taxpayers. In an attempt to unravel the matter for review, I directed the taxpayer to lodge points of claim setting out the matters of fact on which he relied in support of his objections (Exhibit 1). Mr A. Slater of Counsel made some demur to this course after the event, but I notice it has some support in the views expressed by Fisher J. in Evans v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1988). 88 ATC 4771. In any event, I consider the Tribunal has ample power to give such a direction pursuant to s.33 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 (Cth.) ("the Tribunal Act") as modified by Part IVB of the Taxation Administration Act, 1953 (Cth.).

The Commissioner's documents produced under s.37 of the Tribunal Act became Exhibit A. In response to a request for particulars, the Commissioner advised the taxpayer's representatives by letter dated 7 August 1989 (part of Exhibit B) i that he relied upon s.260 of the Act, that certain contracts or transactions entered into by the taxpayer were not intended to have legal effect, and were a mere disguise for some other and real contract or transaction, and that the assessments were made pursuant to s.167 of the Act. Furthermore, the Commissioner relied upon his statement pursuant to s.37 of the Tribunal Act, forming part of Exhibit A. That statement made some of the points in his answer to the request for particulars, and additionally alleged that the commissions and fees, the subject of the amended assessments, were correctly included in the assessable income of the taxpayer for each year under review, under the terms of s.19, sub-s.25(1), s.26, paragraph (a), S.26AAA or sub-s.36(1) of the Act. In addition, the Commissioner imposed additional tax upon the taxpayer for failing to correctly disclose his income, and gave his reasons for that imposition under s.226(2) of the Act, subject to certain remissions under s.226(3) ibid.

The real point of these proceedings emerged because Mr Slater, who was then appearing for the taxpayer, tendered as an exhibit an extract from the Commissioner's file (Exhibit D) which convinced me that I should summon the Commissioner to produce certain documents, which included a Special Examiner's Report from which the extract was taken, a submission regarding additional tax, the comments of a Supervisor on the Special Examiner's Report, proposed alterations to Assessments, and a report on penalties. (In doing this I had regard to the comments of Lockhart J. in John Tanner Holdings Pty. Ltd. v Federal Commissioner of Taxation unreported 14 December 1987.) The Commissioner, through his counsel Mr P. Graham, Q.C., and Mr McGovern, produced these documents which became Exhibit 3, and the whole of the proceedings turned upon the proper construction of the Special Examiner's Report, and possibly upon one additional document, Exhibit 5. I am indebted to Counsel for their assistance in dealing with the relevant matters in dispute. Exhibit 5 is part of the transcript of evidence given by the taxpayer in proceedings in the Supreme Court of New south Wales, upon which Mr Graham sought to rely as containing an admission. Copies of the taxpayer's I come Tax Returns became Exhibit 4.

The argument which Mr Slater presented during his brief appearance on the first day of the proceedings, and which was adopted by Mr Raphael, his instructing solicitor, who appeared on his own in place of Mr Slater after his departure, was that Exhibit D and Exhibit 3 showed that instead of assessing the taxpayer's income in accordance with s.167 of the Act, the Commissioner had in reality assessed the taxpayer with the income of other entities with which the taxpayer was concerned. Exhibit 3 was marked as a confidential exhibit until further direction, not only because it reveals the name of the taxpayer and the entities with which he was associated, but also because it deals with the affairs of another taxpayer.

Both parties have referred me to authorities. In particular my attention was drawn to the comment of a Full Court of the High Court in George v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1952] HCA 21; (1952) 86 CLR 183, 204 that s.167 of the Act is epexegetical to s.166 and the court's statement:

"The fact is that unless the taxpayer discharges the burden laid upon him by s190(b) of proving that his ascertainment or judgment is excessive, he cannot succeed and it can be no part of the duty of the commissioner to establish affirmatively what Judgment was formed, much less the grounds of it, and even less still the truth of the facts affording the grounds."

However, Counsel also referred me to the judgments in Dalco v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1988) 88 ATC 4649 a case in which a majority of the Federal Court decided that if it appeared that an assessment had been made on a wrong basis or upon a wrong principle it may be impeached. Sheppard and Gummow JJ. at p4656 said:

" If it appears that an assessment has been made in purported but not justifiable exercise of statutory power, so that it is excessive in the necessary sense, then the appropriate course is for the Commissioner to make a new assessment in the light of the Court's decision."

Mr Graham submitted the majority decision was erroneous, more particularly in the passage in the right hand column on p.4659 which reads:

" In our view, there is no basis for a judgment that, in respect of the 1976 tax year, income tax ought to be levied upon Dalco in the amount of $92,043 arrived at by Mr Kidd. Even if Mr Kidd is to be treated as having relied upon a view as to the applicability of sec. 19 of the Tax Act (a matter which is by no means clear) that still would not do. The material available to Mr Kidd did not provide a basis for a judgment that Martine (still less the taxpayer) was to be treated as having derived income in the 1976 tax year in the sum of $92,043 on the footing that in 1976, whilst $92,043 was not actually paid over to Martine (or the taxpayer), that sum was dealt with on behalf of Martine (or the taxpayer) or as it (or he) directed. In truth, the matter was approached by Mr Kidd on a basis which looked through legal entities (and taxpayers) standing between the taxpayer and Mr Maher's company CSL; that basis was a wrong basis."

In general Mr Graham supported the dissenting views expressed by Wilcox J. He pointed out that the judgment was under appeal to the High Court. If it is possible otherwise, it appears basically undesirable in the light of the delicate interplay of juristic opinions as to the meaning of the ordinary words in s.167 of the Act which have vexed the judicature for so many years, to proffer any opinion, especially if there appears to be an alternative route to decision.

In examining the Special Examiner's Report, part of Exhibit 3, I will refer to the taxpayer and his partner as Alka and Seltzer respectively. Alka and Seltzer, it appears from the report, were in partnership as consultants, promoting and dealing in tax avoidance schemes, Alka was a former officer of the Taxation Department and Seltzer an accountant. Alka and Seltzer were two of the members of the partnership business carried on under a name which I shall term Prevulcan (see paragraphs 32, 35 and 87 of the report). It should be noted that the partnership deed was not tendered in evidence by either party and that the taxpayer did not give evidence. The only information before me as to the business arrangements of Alka and Seltzer comes from Exhibit 3, and the evidence given in the Supreme Court, Exhibit 5.

The crux of the Special Examiner's Report is to be found in paragraphs 85, 86 and part of 87, the confidentiality of which is removed to the extent necessary for the purposes of this decision, but with suitable transposition of names.

"Arguments in Defence of the Assessments

85. Apart from any question of quantum, the central point in raising the assessments to Alka and Seltzer, hereinafter called the taxpayers, is whether the fees and commissions derived in connection with tax avoidance activities are properly assessable to the taxpayers themselves. In this regard it is felt the assessments can be defended by using one or a combination of the following:-

(a) The taxpayers are deemed to have derived the income by operation of section 19 and such income is accordingly assessable to them in terms of sub-section 25(1).

(b) Alternatively, the taxpayers derived the income personally and such income is assessable to them in terms of sub-section 25(1).

(c) Alternatively, the creation and use of the various vehicles through which the income was diverted was in fact and in law a sham and that such income was the product of the personal services of the taxpayers and therefore had been derived by them.
  • (d) alternatively, the creation and use of the various vehicles through which the income was diverted represents arrangements and/or agreements that are void against the Commissioner by the operation of section 260 or part IVA where applicable.
  • (e) Alternatively, the taxpayers can be said to have derived a profit from the carrying out of a profit-making undertaking or scheme and such profit should be included in their assessable income in accordance with the former section 26(1).

Derivation - power to carry on business

86. It can be argued that tax avoidance fees and commissions were dealt with on the promoters behalf and as they directed by the channelling of such income through Prevulcan and other entities controlled by Alka and Seltzer. It is necessary at the outset to determine whether or not the trustee of (name omitted), a partner in Prevulcan, had the power to carry on the business of Business and Financial Advisers and Managers'. Clause 8(c) of the relevant trust deed allows the trustee, to carry on either solely or in partnership such trade or business as the Trustees shall in their absolute discretion think fit and for the purposes thereof to borrow moneys and employ such moneys in or towards the carrying on of such business or trade. See folio 61 annexure G. Except as provided here, there is no specific grant of power given to the trustees to conduct a business of Business and Financial Advisors and Managers. In Gulland v F.C. of T.83 ATC 4352 Kennedy J, in reviewing the functions of the Gulland Medical Clinic, a trust which purported to carry on the business of a professional medical practice, concluded that the relevant trust deed contains no express power to carry on the business of a medical practitioner even though, in a clause of the deed the trustees were able to carry on the business of providing medical services or any activity identical thereto whether alone or in partnership or as agent or otherwise . . . (supra at p. 4355).

Accordingly, in our case, it seems that the trust deed does not allow for, or anticipate, the specific income earning activity claimed to have been pursued by the trustee on behalf of the trust. It should also be noted that there is no evidence of any amendment to the deed to allow for this activity.

Personal Exertion

87. It can be argued that Alka and Seltzer were in fact carrying on and controlling the business. The fact that there was a partnership agreement between two parties who did not appear to have any control over the business is beside the point. Prevulcan was merely the designated recipient of the income directed to it by the promoters, such income was the product of their personal efforts and not of the activity said to have been carried on by the partnership."

In my opinion, the references to arguments in paragraphs 85, 86 and 87 do not amount to judgments that the income attributed to Prevulcan and other entities was really the income of Alka and Seltzer. In the Supervisor's Comments dated 18 March 1986 (part of Exhibit 3), the Supervisor, after commenting on the Special Examiner's Report, says in part:

"In the section of the report dealing with Arguments in Defence of the Assessment (paragraph 85), Mr Mankarios has correctly identified the several grounds upon which the Commissioner will place reliance."

A holograph note at the foot of the Supervisor's Comments signed by a person designated "Director" and dated 24 March 1986 states in part

"The submission is recommended. The inclusion of distributions to third parties is due to the taxpayers failure to be frank and honest.

"That note is followed by a further holograph note dated 25 March 1986:

"Approved"

Notwithstanding an element of ambiguity in the Director's note, it appears to me that the final note "Approved", coupled with the Supervisor's Comments, amount to approval of a judgment that the Commissioner makes in raising an assessment under s.167 of the Act, that the income attributed to Prevulcan and the other entities mentioned in the Special Report, was in reality the income of Alka and of Seltzer. This view derives support from the adoption in effect of the Special Examiner's argument in paragrah 86 of a reference to the judgment of Kennedy J. in Gulland v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1983) 83 ATC 4352.

With reference to Exhibit 5, Mr Graham relied upon the evidence of Alka in the Supreme Court where he stated that from 1978 to 1982 he practiced originally on his own account and later in partnership with Seltzer under the name of Alka and Company, and that the major part of the work of that firm was consulting in taxation and financial matters. Alka gave further evidence as follows in those proceedings:

"Q. What was the advice that you gave them following those decisions? (Objected to; allowed). A. It was various depending on the client's situation. We tried to disuade [sic] them from going into arrangements that were overly artificial or depended on purely paper arrangements.

Q.Did you have some criterion yourself for determining the advice you would give in relation to taxation arrangements? A. Yes. (Objected to ; allowed).

Q. What was your criteria? A.Yes, we try to maintain a business basis to anything that we either recommended or suggested to our clients they might consider.

Q.What do you mean by a business basis PA. We like to look at companies that would stand on their own as a business arrangement without a tax implication if it came down to it."

It appears to me, accepting as I should, the reasoning of the majority in Dalco, that there is a basis for a judgment that in respect of the tax years in question, income tax ought to be levied upon the taxpayer in the amounts arrived at by the Special Examiner. Plainly the Examiner was not satisfied that income derived as the result of advising and consultation work of the accountant taxpayer and his partner was really the income of the entities mentioned in the Special Examiner's Report (Exhibit 3). There was room for the view that the Commissioner was not lifting I the corporate veil, but merely forming the opinion that the taxpayer was attempting unsuccessfully to divert what was in fact his own income, to other entities. Such a view gains some support from the taxpayer's evidence set out in Exhibit 5.

Bearing in mind that the overall onus of upsetting the assessments lies on the taxpayer, I do not consider the document (Exhibit 3) contains internal evidence to negate the assessments, and consider there is evidence to support the judgment of the Commissioner. No evidence has been placed before me on behalf of the taxpayer to displace the Commissioner's assessment. The taxpayer, as is his right, elected not to give evidence. No evidence was placed before me to suggest the penalties assessed were excessive or erroneous.

In the event I uphold the objection decisions under review and affirm the assessments.
