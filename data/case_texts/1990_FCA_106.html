Re Qpsx Communications Limited v the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1990] FCA 106 (9 April 1990)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: QPSX COMMUNICATIONS LIMITED

And: THE DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

No. WA G143 of 1989

FED No. 134

Sales Tax

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

WESTERN AUSTRALIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

French J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Sales Tax - judicial review - sales tax appeals procedure - cancellation of registration as manufacturer - rejection of status as manufacturer - assessments for tax and additional tax based on such rejection - objections to assessments lodged - judicial review and injunction proceedings in relation to cancellation of registration - cancellation of registration revoked - assessments maintained - no need for injunctive relief re cancellation - controversy still on foot - availability of appeals procedures - whether Court has jurisdiction to entertain application for declaratory relief - appropriateness of exercising jurisdiction.

Sales Tax Regulations

Sales Tax Assessment Act (No.1) 1930 s.3(4), sub-s.16(4)

Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977

Judiciary Act 1903 s.39B

Fencott v Muller (1983) 152 CLR 570

R v Bevan Ex parte Elias [1942] HCA 12; (1942) 66 CLR 452

HEARING

PERTH

9:4:1990

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr W. Martin

Solicitors for the Applicant: Mallesons Stephen Jaques

Counsel for the Respondent: Ms I. Petersen

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

1. The respondent's motion filed 27 March 1990 is

dismissed.

2. The respondent pay the applicant's costs of the motion.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

On 22 December 1989, QPSX Communications Limited ("QPSX") filed an application in this Court seeking injunctive and declaratory relief against the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation. According to a statement of claim filed with the application, QPSX was incorporated as "a joint venture company" under the terms of a joint venture agreement made 6 May 1987 between the Australian Telecommunications Commission ("Telecom") and Unicom Research Pty Ltd. QPSX says it carries on the activity of embodying computer programes into EPROM micro chips (which I take to be a species of silicon chip) and produces printed circuit boards which are used in the production of products known as "Clusters" for use in advanced telecommunications networks. QPSX claims that from 22 October 1987 to December 1989 it produced 822 PCB's and 48 Clusters. 303 of the PCB's and 12 of the Clusters are said to have been made for use by QPSX as aids to manufacture or auxiliaries to aids to manufacture as defined in Regulation 4 of the Sales Tax Regulations. The remaining 36 Clusters are said to have been supplied to Telecom under an agreement made 4 March 1988. Remaining PCB's were placed in stock. According to paras. 5 and 6 of the statement of claim, the provision of the PCB's and Clusters to Telecom was or was deemed to be the sale of goods under s.3(4) of the Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) 1930, and the production of the programmed chips, PCB's and Clusters constituted the manufacture of goods for the purposes of that Act. Further, it was said that their production by QPSX constituted it a manufacturer. On 22 October 1987 the company was so registered by the Commissioner.

2. On 24 October 1989 the Commissioner is said to have formed the view that QPSX was not a manufacturer under the Act and wrote confirming that it was not required to be registered as such and requested that it return its certificate of registration for cancellation. The statement of claim asserts that QPSX is a manufacturer as defined in the Act and is entitled to be registered and to continue to be registered as such.

3. The application as filed, claims injunctions restraining the Commissioner from revoking QPSX's registration as a manufacturer and from serving on it any notice of revocation under sub-s.16(4) of the Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1). In addition declaratory relief is sought that between 22 October 1987 and 24 October 1989 QPSX was a manufacturer as defined in s.3 of the Act, that it was such a manufacturer between 24 October 1989 and the date of the application and declarations that it was during those periods entitled to be registered as a manufacturer under s.11 of the Act.

4. It is not disputed that notice of the revocation of QPSX's registration coincided with the issue of assessments for sales tax and additional sales tax amounting to $905,936.99 which were to become payable on 7 November 1989. It is also common ground that the assessments so issued were based upon the Commissioner's view that contrary to its contention and inconsistently with its registration as such, QPSX was not a manufacturer under the Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1). A notice of objection was filed on or about 22 December 1989 at which time the present proceedings were also commenced along with an application under the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 seeking review of the Commissioner's decision to cancel the registration. At the present time it appears that the notice of objection against the assessments has not been dealt with.

5. On 29 March 1990 the Australian Government Solicitor wrote to the solicitors for QPSX referring to the two applications in this Court and saying:

"I refer to the above and the decisions made on the

24 October 1989 the subject of your applications to

the Federal Court.

I advise that my client has instructed me that the

decisions made on 24 October 1989 have been revoked

and that no action will be taken in respect of the

decisions.

I am instructed that my client is of the view that

the revocation of the decisions made on 24 October

1989 does not preclude him from making fresh

decisions.

Please advise the writer of your client's

instructions in light of the above and my client's

motions filed in these applications dated 27 March

1989."

The motions referred to were filed on 27 March 1990 and each sought the summary dismissal of each of the applications in light of the Commissioner's decision revoking his earlier decision to cancel the registration.

6. In respect of the motion to dismiss the application brought under the ADJR Act, it was ordered by consent that the application be dismissed but that the Commissioner pay QPSX's costs of the application. A similar motion brought in respect of these proceedings which invoke s.39B of the Judiciary Act 1903 was however contested. By that motion the Commissioner seeks orders that the application be stayed or generally dismissed as an abuse of the process of the Court. The substantive argument in support of the motion was, in effect, that the decision which engendered the application had been revoked, that there was no relevant controversy between the parties and that the question of QPSX'S status as a manufacturer could be dealt with on its merits in appeal proceedings following disallowance of the objection to the assessments referred to.

7. The Commissioner is proceeding on the basis that it is the character of a taxpayer as a manufacturer rather than the fact of its registration as such that determines liability. That seems to be common ground. As is apparent from correspondence tendered during argument on the motion and statements made by counsel for the Commissioner, he reserves the right to cancel QPSX's registration at some future date depending upon the outcome of appeal proceedings in relation to the assessments. In essence, he contends that the appropriate procedure for resolving the question of the status of QPSX is by way of an appeal under the provisions of Pt. VII of the Act.

8. It cannot be contended at this stage that the Court lacks jurisdiction to entertain the present application on the basis that only declaratory relief may be appropriate. Given that the jurisdiction invoked was that under s.39B of the Judiciary Act "with respect to any matter in which...an injunction is sought against an officer or officers of the Commonwealth", the fact that injunctive relief may now be unnecessary does not dispose of the matter or controversy underlying the application. And in this case it is clear that that controversy is on foot. That being the case, the Court continues to have jurisdiction. It is unnecessary to repeat here the many authorities which lead to that conclusion for they have been frequently canvassed, but I refer particularly to Fencott v Muller (1983) 152 CLR 570 and R v Bevan Ex parte Elias [1942] HCA 12; (1942) 66 CLR 452.

9. The question remains whether it is appropriate that the application now proceed having regard to the availability of the appeal mechanism under the statute. In my opinion the applicant is entitled to an early answer to the controversy which undoubtedly casts a shadow of some uncertainty over its present activities, not only in respect of the liability asserted by the assessments under challenge but also in respect of any new liabilities which may be incurred in the continuation of those activities. The objections have not yet been determined by the Commissioner and the timing of any subsequently requested reference to this Court or the Administrative Appeals Tribunal is partly in his hands (see ss.41, 42C and 42D of the Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1)).

10. The present proceedings have been instituted as a result of what the Commissioner now evidently regards as an unnecessary decision to cancel registration. They can be programmed to an early trial which may be able to proceed on facts which are in part agreed or may at least be the subject of affidavit evidence. In my opinion, the application having been commenced and the controversy being capable of resolution in this way more expeditiously than under the appeal procedure, it ought to be allowed to continue.

11. For these reasons the Commissioner's motion will be dismissed with costs and I will hear from the parties in due course as to appropriate programming orders.
