HEARING

SYDNEY

11:5:1988

ORDER

That by consent the decision of the Commissioner upon the objection under review shall be varied and that the objection of the Applicant shall be wholly allowed.

DECISION

INTRODUCTION

The hearing giving rise to these reasons for decision was held in public at the request of the Applicant. The application related to the affairs of Peter R. Simons, a solicitor of Sydney. (I shall refer to the Applicant as "Simons"). It arose out of an assessment to tax which issued against Simons at the instance of the Respondent, the Commissioner of Taxation, on 24 July 1984. The assessment issued pursuant to the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("the T(UCT)A Act") and was based upon a promoter's taxable amount of $120-92 (sic). The promoter's taxable amount having been assessed at that figure, the tax assessed against Simons was also $120-92: by force of s.4 of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax-Promoter's) Act 1982 ("the Promoter's Tax Act"). A letter which accompanied the notice of assessment advised Simons:-

"On the basis of information held in this office it is

considered that you are a member of the eligible

promoters' class for the purposes of the abovementioned

Act as you were a Director of Jensen Mining &

Investments Limited on 24 June 1975 when Jensen Mining &

Investments Ltd. purchased the shares in V.T.H.

Investments Pty. Ltd."

2. Having been so assessed, Simons became indebted to the Crown in the sum of $120-92 (s.208 Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - "the Assessment Act"). He then had but sixty days within which to object to the assessment so made. The objection which was delivered was most detailed. If one page in the objection selected at random provides a fair indication, the objection comprised over 40,000 words: very few of which came to be of any relevance in the course of the hearing. However, that is understandable. It was delivered at a time when, save for the important decision of the High Court of Australia on the constitutional validity of the legislation (cf. MacCormick v. F.C. of T. [1984] HCA 20; (1984) 158 C.L.R. 622)) there was no judicial authority as to the scope or meaning of the T(UCT)A legislation. Furthermore, by the procedural rules of the period there could be no amendment of any grounds of objection: no matter what the circumstances. That was rectified with effect from 1 July 1986 (Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986), but not so as to avail Simons had his grounds of objection been deficient (ibid, s.221).

3. Simons objected to the assessment on 30 July 1984 and his objection was disallowed by the Commissioner on 24 July 1985. He requested reference to a Taxation Board of Review for review 16 September 1985 and that request was complied with 26 March 1986. This Tribunal assumed responsibility for determination of the objection on 1 July 1986 by force of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986. The matter was called over before a member of the Tribunal on 3 December 1986 and was listed for hearing on 4 August 1987, but only after Directions Hearings had been held on 3 April and 20 May 1987.

4. When the hearing was adjourned at the close of sittings on 4 August 1987, the evidence in chief of Simons - at that stage the only witness to be called - was incomplete. On 5 August Counsel for the Commissioner requested an adjournment of the matter but the application was at that point refused. Simons was allowed to complete his evidence and the hearing was then adjourned until the following day. Upon the resumption Counsel for the Commissioner sought a further adjournment and the application was then granted to allow the Commissioner a little over a month in order to further investigate the matter. On 15 September 1987, when the matter was listed to resume, Counsel for the Commissioner advised that he did not wish to cross-examine; that he proposed to call no evidence; and that, without admitting the accuracy of any of the evidence which had been given or any of the arguments which had been advanced, he conceded that the objection should be allowed. The reasons which provided the basis for that concession were not disclosed.

5. At that point Counsel for the Commissioner made application that the application be disposed of in favour of the Applicant in exercise of the power conferred by s.42A of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 which provides:-

"(1) Where all the parties to an application before the

Tribunal for a review of a decision consent, the

Tribunal may dismiss the application without proceeding

to review the decision or, if the Tribunal has commenced

to review the decision, without completing the review."

That application was opposed on behalf of Simons. But in any event, it was an application which was without merit because the only application which might have been dismissed was that of the Applicant: not that of the Commissioner.

6. An alternative submission for the Commissioner was that the Tribunal should direct that the Commissioner's determination upon the objection under review be set aside and that that should be done without providing any reasons. The response by the solicitor for the Applicant was to request that that Order be made, but only after the Tribunal had made appropriate findings of fact and had stated its reasons on a number of issues and thereby determined that the Applicant was entitled to the relief claimed. The Commissioner, by his Counsel, argued against the making of any such findings on the basis that once the Tribunal had determined to set aside the decision under review - a decision properly reached because the Commissioner had conceded the entitlement of the Applicant - the making of findings of fact and the issue of reasons in writing for those findings could serve no useful purpose. It was not suggested that publication of such reasons would be in any way to the detriment of the Commissioner, but only that in no way could such findings advance the interests of the Applicant.

7. I reserved my decision on that question and proceeded to hear the submissions of the parties as to the findings of fact which ought be made and as to the reasons for decision which should attend them. I was addressed on these matters at length by the solicitor for the Applicant but Counsel for the Commissioner reserved his position. That course of action provided no assistance to the Tribunal. On reserving my decision I announced that I would give my attention first to the question whether I should determine the matter in accordance with the request of the Commissioner. I have decided that I should not do so. The protests of the Applicant, represented by the objection and by other submissions to the Commissioner, fell on deaf ears. The Applicant sought an independent review and it was only after a full day of hearing in these proceedings that the Commissioner first sought an adjournment to further investigate matters. By then the Applicant's objection had been before the Commissioner for over three years, and over two years had passed since the Commissioner had "considered" the objection and determined to disallow it. It was only after Simons was allowed to complete his evidence in chief and an adjournment for a month had been availed of, that the Commissioner by his Counsel announced that the objection should be allowed.

8. Even if the Tribunal in those circumstances could act properly in determining this matter without providing reasons, the views of the Applicant on the issue as to the publication of reasons are as much entitled to consideration as those of the Commissioner. The wish of the Applicant is that he should have the benefit, not only of the Order sought, but also of having the reasons of the Tribunal for its findings based on the materials presented to it. In my view, particularly having regard to the history of this litigation; the circumstances giving rise to the assessment; and the manner in which the Commissioner has responded to the objection and to the Applicant's requests for information, he is entitled to that much.

THE BACKGROUND

9. It will assist in an understanding of what follows, if I indicate at this point that it is not disputed that in June 1975 Simons was a Director of Jensen Mining & Investments Limited ("Jensen"). The unchallenged evidence of Simons is that:-

(1) Jensen was at all material times, and at date of

hearing remained, a listed Company on the Stock

Exchange;

(2) in June 1975 Simons was but one of several

Directors of Jensen;

(3) in June 1975 Simons was not Chairman of Directors

of Jensen;

(4) in June 1975 Simons was not an Executive Director

of Jensen;

(5) Simons had no personal knowledge of any transaction

whereby Jensen purchased shares in V.T.H.

Investments Pty. Ltd. (VTH);

(6) Simons retired as a Director of Jensen in 1981;

(7) Simons had severed all association with Jensen,

including any association through shareholdings in

companies as well as as an individual, prior to the

issue of the assessments in this case;

(8) at no time since assessment has Simons had any

entitlement to access to any records of or

information held by Jensen; and

(9) since assessment, the relationship between Simons

and Jensen has been such that no information has

been made available to him despite his requests in

that regard.

10. It is also appropriate to observe that the unchallenged evidence - which relates to the assessed liability in the sum of $120-92 - was that:-

(a) the number of persons jointly and severally liable

as Directors of Jensen is at least four;

(b) the number of persons jointly and severally liable

as shareholders in Jensen probably exceeds 1,000;

and that

(c) if the group was extended to include all of the

beneficiaries of trusts which held shares in Jensen

at any material time, then the number of persons

jointly and separately liable may well constitute

many more (cf. s.7(8)).

11. It is significant to note that, according to the unchallenged evidence of Simons, the Commissioner, as at August 1987, had not instituted proceedings to recover the tax which the Commissioner claims should have been paid by him as early as 27 August 1984. In consequence, the Applicant has not been able to make

"an application to the Federal Court for a declaration

that, by reason of special circumstances, it is not just

and equitable, for the purposes of those prescribed

proceedings, that the person be included in the eligible

promoter's class to which the promoter's recoupment tax

relates"

as provided for by s.11(1) of the T(UCT)A Act.

12. Had it been possible for the Applicant to make such an application, it would have been the responsibility of the Federal Court

"in determining whether or not it is just and equitable

that a person be included in the eligible promoter's

class in relation to a promoter's taxable amount, (to)

have regard to:-

(a) the nature and extent of any participation of the

person in the scheme...;

(b) the extent of any benefit that the person has

obtained, or may reasonably be expected to obtain,

as a result of the carrying out of the scheme...;

and

(c) any other relevant matters. (S.11(5) - T(UCT)A

Act).

13. Furthermore, assuming for the moment that the Applicant would not have been excluded from or deemed not to be and never to have been a member of the eligible promoter's class, a further consequence is that, by not instituting proceedings to recover the tax, the Commissioner has denied the Applicant the right to seek contribution - as provided for by s.10 - T(UCT)A Act - from any one or more of the hundreds of persons who might - on the Commissioner's argument - be jointly and severally liable with Simons for $120-92 in tax.

14. It seems not unreasonable to think that if the same facts were presented to the Court as have been established before me, the prospect for Simons of being excluded from the "eligible promoter's class" or of being entitled to indemnification by Jensen must have been very good.

15. In addition it is to be observed that, as the evidence stands before me, the Applicant had no entitlement or capacity to secure any information from Jensen which would enable him to learn whether or not he has the liability contended for by the Commissioner.

16. Bearing in mind the finding that Jensen at all material times has been a listed company; and that is of the essence of the case for the Commissioner that it is the actions of Jensen, albeit effected by its officers (but not the Applicant), which is the source of the liability assessed to the Applicant - jointly and severally with many others - it seems remarkable that the assessment did not issue against Jensen.

PROCEDURAL MATTERS

17. That being so there is yet one other matter which I consider to be of considerable significance. In preliminary proceedings before this Tribunal, and also independently of those proceedings, the Applicant had sought information from the Commissioner. In particular he asked why he, and apparently he alone, had been chosen as the person within the eligible promoter's class to be assessed. It does not seem unreasonable for a person in the position of the Applicant to have made such a request - or that his request should have been fully responded to - so that he might be told why he, of all the persons feasibly liable, and why he, rather than the Company the actions of which gave rise to his alleged liability, should have been singled out for assessment. The response for the Commissioner was to deny the request as made.

18. The stance of the Commissioner may be discerned from a letter written on his behalf by the Australian Government Solicitor of 11 May 1987. In that letter it was said:-

"... by letter of 31 March 1987 you requested a lengthy

and detailed request particulars (sic) and intermingled

with this sought certain documents but did not state

with any particularity which documents you required.

As you are aware the Administrative Appeals Tribunal, by

its members has no power to order that particulars or

discovery be given (please also see Sutton v.

Commissioner of Taxation [1959] HCA 3; 100 C.L.R. 518)."

19. The letter went on to point out - quite correctly - that many of the documents relevant to these proceedings were freely available to the Applicant through the office of the Director of Public Prosecutions and other public bodies such as the Corporate Affairs Commission. (Such documents would not be likely to shed any light on the question why Simons was assessed rather than others). The solicitor for the Commissioner then went on to provide gratuitously copies of the relevant Sale Agreement and Balance Sheet. On this occasion the Agreement provided was not modified to conceal the identity of the vendors of the shares in VTH. Previously even that information had been withheld.

20. The reference in that letter to the decision in Sutton is a reference to a joint decision of a Full Bench of the High Court of Australia in the case of that name decided in [1959] HCA 3; 1959 (100 C.L.R. 518). It was a decision relating to the powers of the former Taxation Boards of Review as administrative tribunals reviewing the work of the Commissioner in dealing with objections of taxpayers to assessments of tax. No doubt what the solicitor had in mind was the passage in the joint decision (at p.523) in which the High Court said:-

"Of course the Board deals with the cases before it in a

judicial spirit, and decides them by ascertaining the

facts and applying the law as it sees it. That is a

duty belonging to quasi-judicial as well as judicial

bodies. Moreover it is armed with the powers given in

Part V of the Regulations and is governed so far as they

go by the directions contained in that Part. But it is

not exercising judicial power. The importance of this

is that the basis does not exist for implying a power

over the Commissioner as a litigant party to impose on

him a legal obligation to formulate his case by

pleadings or particulars or to give discovery by

answering interrogatories or otherwise or to fulfil some

other procedural requirement borrowed from the Courts of

justice. To enable the Board to impose such

obligations upon the Commissioner express authority

would be necessary."

21. However, having expressed that view, the High Court went on to say:-

"But the relations of the Board to the Commissioner

should not and doubtless do not begin and end with legal

obligation. As an administrative body reviewing his

decisions upon objections, if the Board finds that it

needs the assistance of this or that information as to

what the Commissioner did, there is no reason why the

Board should not ask for it, nor is there anything to

prevent it asking for the information in some specific

form which the Board might find useful for the

facilitation of the proceedings. Natural justice, it

is needless to say, requires that the taxpayer shall

know the course that is taken and what is placed before

the Board. But the procedure of a Board is not

necessarily a formal or technical affair.

The Commissioner is unlikely to withhold any information

or assistance which a Board might reasonably require,

and it would serve no purpose to speculate as to the

effect that might legitimately be produced upon the

Board's determination of the case by a refusal on the

Commissioner's part to supply information in his

possession without which the Board could not get at a

just conclusion on the merits. The truth is that the

course taken in the present case treats the proceedings

with an unreal formalism which should not, and one may

be sure really does not, attend the performance of the

duties of a Board of Review."

22. It is appropriate to state immediately that, in these proceedings, this Tribunal did not "ask for" or "require" that the Commissioner provide any of the information sought by the Applicant. That was for the simple reason that, in this particular case, the Applicant elected to proceed with such information as the Commissioner chose to provide: preferring that course to embarking upon an exercise which might entail another prolonged and expensive litigious journey in order to test the assertion made for the Commissioner that this Tribunal had no power to require the Commissioner to provide particulars or make discovery. After all, the amount of tax involved in the particular case was only $120-92.

23. For my part, I would merely express the hope that the Commissioner will not follow in other cases the course which was followed in the instant case. The course of co-operation which is commonly and readily followed is to be preferred. But, should circumstances prompt the Commissioner to again adopt the stance adopted in this case, it is to be hoped that he will be willing to at least indicate in outline why he has done so. The mere existence of a power is not alone a sufficient reason for its exercise. For my part, I would have thought that the greater the extent of any discretionary power, such as is exercised by the Commissioner under the T(UCT)A legislation, the greater the need to act reasonably and to be seen to act reasonably in its exercise; and the greater the need to be able to present an explanation for its exercise when reasonably called upon to do so, especially by persons adversely effected.

24. Recognizing that questions as to the extent of the powers of the Tribunal have not been argued, I express no view as to the extent to which the procedural powers of this Tribunal differ from those of the Taxation Boards of Review.

THE T(UCT)A LEGISLATION

25. The constitutional validity of the T(UCT)A Act was considered and affirmed by a Full Bench of the High Court of Australia in MacCormick v. F.C. of T. ((1984) [1984] HCA 20; 158 C.L.R. 622). In the course of its summary of the legislative schema established by the T(UCT)A Act, their Honours Gibbs C.J., Wilson, Deane and Dawson JJ. said of promoters recoupment tax (at pp. 632-635):-

"A promoters taxable amount exists where shares in a

company were sold in circumstances which are the

equivalent of those giving rise to a primary taxable

amount. The promoters taxable amount is equal to

20 per cent of the overdue company tax or, where no

vendors recoupment tax has become payable or will, in

the opinion of the Commissioner, become payable, equal

to the overdue company tax. The promoters taxable

amount is not, however, to exceed the value of the

assets of the company before it was stripped, less the

sum of its liabilties including company tax and the

total consideration given for the sale of the relevant

shares. This limit upon the promoters taxable amount

appears to be designed to ensure that a promoter is not

liable to be taxed upon an amount which is greater than

the amount he received by way of remuneration for his

participation in the scheme.

The persons liable to pay tax in relation to a promoters

taxable amount are those who constitute the eligible

promoters class. That class comprises each person who

purchased the relevant shares, each person who acted as

agent for the purchaser or broker, each person who was

an associate of the purchaser at the time of the

purchase and a number of other specified persons.

Where a promoters taxable amount exists, a tax is

imposed upon that amount equal to the whole amount by

the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax - Promoters) Act.

This tax is called promoters recoupment tax.

The persons included in an eligible promoters class are

jointly and severally liable to pay the promoters

recoupment tax in relation to which the class exists

and, if one such person pays any of that tax, he may

recover by way of contribution from any of the other

persons included in the class such part of the amount

paid as a court considers just and equitable. He may

also join as co-defendants the other persons included in

the class in any proceedings for recovery of promoters

recoupment tax. Where proceedings are taken against a

person for recovery of promoters recoupment tax, that

person may apply to the Federal Court for a declaration

that, by reason of special circumstances, it is not just

and equitable that he be included in the eligible

promoters class to which the promoters recoupment tax

relates. Where such a declaration is made, the person

is deemed not to have been included in the relevant

eligible promoters class.

Provision is made for the reduction of the liability of

a person liable to pay recoupment tax where the overdue

company tax or recoupment tax is paid by some other

person or where there is a payment of promoters

recoupment tax based on the same overdue company tax.

It is also provided that an amount of promoters

recoupment tax will be reduced where a payment of the

corresponding company tax is made. There is, however,

no provision for the reduction of promoters recoupment

tax consequent upon payment of vendors recoupment tax

and in this way the liability falls first upon the

promoters within the limits provided by the legislation.

The purpose of these provisions is evidently to ensure

that the amount of the overdue company tax is only

collected once and that the benefit of any payment of

company tax or recoupment tax will flow through to

persons under a related liability. Payment of

recoupment tax does not, however, extinguish the

liability of the target company for the overdue company

tax.

Whilst a penalty is imposed for late payment of

recoupment tax, provision is made that recoupment tax

shall not be increased on account of any additional tax

payable by the target company as a penalty for late

payment that accrues after the assessment for recoupment

tax is made.

The Act contains provisions which enable those who may

become liable for recoupment tax to contest an

assessment of company tax in relation to the relevant

target company......

. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

Where a notice of assessment of company tax has not been

served on the target company and promoters recoupment

tax is payable or, in the opinion of the Commissioner

likely to become payable and if payable, is likely to be

the only recoupment tax payable, notice of assessment is

to be served on the person or any of the persons liable,

or likely to become liable, to pay the promoters

recoupment tax.

. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

Where a notice of assessment of company tax has been

served on the target company and promoters recoupment

tax is, in the opinion of the Commissioner, likely to

become payable and if payable, is the only recoupment

tax likely to be payable, the Commissioner is required

to serve a copy of the notice upon the person or any of

the persons likely to become liable to pay the promoters

recoupment tax and if service was not validly effected

upon the company, then service in this manner shall be

deemed to be service on the company.

The 1982 Assessment Act is a complex piece of

legislation, ......"

26. It is tax so described which the Commissioner claims to have assessed in accordance with the Act.

THE PROMOTER'S TAXABLE AMOUNT

27. The first argument which was raised on behalf of the Applicant in the course of the hearing related to the quantum of tax sought to be recovered by the Commissioner. The amount assessed by the Commissioner on 24 July 1984 as due and payable on 27 August 1984 was $120-92. The Applicant contends that the amount sought to be recovered should not on any view have exceeded $80-32. The difference in views is to be resolved by a consideration of the concept of "promoter's taxable amount".

28. By his notice of assessment, the Commissioner provided "details of unpaid company tax" in which he identified as "ordinary company tax" $307-70 and as "additional tax for late payment of the company assessment" $296-93, to give a total of "unpaid company tax": $604-63. As a result, in the circumstances, the assessment fixed the "promoter's taxable amount" at 20% of "unpaid company tax" so calculated, thereby assessing the amount payable at $120-92. From the figure brought to account as "additional tax for late payment of the company assessment", it would seem that the Commissioner had calculated additional tax at the rate of 10% per annum of $307-70 from 30 April 1976 until 13 February 1983; and at 20% per annum from the latter date until shortly prior to the issue of the recoupment tax assessment against Simons. $307-70 was the amount of income tax assessed against VTH for the year of income ended 30 June 1975. It was assessed by a notice of 10 March 1976 claiming the sum to be due and payable on 30 April 1976.

29. It is to be observed that, if the Commissioner is correct in his contention, then the liability of Simons was nearly doubled at time of his assessment by reason of the circumstance that the assessment against VTH happened to have issued in 1976 rather than only after the passage of the T(UCT)A legislation (proclaimed effective on 13 December 1982). That the liability of any individual should be so substantially influenced by what was probably no more than an accident of history does not seem to be reasonable. However, the question is not whether the result is reasonable, but whether it is a result provided for by the Parliament by the terms in which it enacted the T(UCT)A legislation.

30. The tax in question was imposed pursuant to the provisions of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax - Promoters) Act 1982: "an Act to impose a tax in respect of certain unpaid company tax" - which was assented to 13 December 1982. That Act (the Promoter's Tax Act) was to come into operation on the day on which the T(UCT)A Act came into operation (s.2 Promoter's Tax Act). The Promoter's Tax Act went on to provide:-

S.4. "Where at any time a promoter's taxable amount

exists under the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax)

Assessment Act 1982, tax is imposed, and shall be levied

and paid, on that promoter's taxable amount."

S.5. "The amount of the tax imposed by this Act in

respect of a promoter's taxable amount is an amount

equal to the promoter's taxable amount."

31. S.7 of the T(UCT)A Act is the principal Section providing for the concept of a "promoter's taxable amount". S.7 provided that, when all of the conditions specified in either s.7(1) or s.7(2) (but not both - cf. s.7(7)) existed:-

"A promoter's taxable amount ... shall be taken to exist

at the relevant time in relation to the eligible

promoter's class".

The same sub-sections went on to identify the quantum of "a promoter's taxable amount" as being, in the circumstances of this case, "20% of the overdue company tax".

32. "Overdue company tax" was defined by ss.7(1)(f) and 7(2)(f) of the T(UCT)A Act which provide that one of the conditions necessary to found the liability for promoter's recoupment tax is that:-

". . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

(f) at any time (in this sub-section referred to as the

'relevant time') after the commencement of this Act

there remains an unpaid amount (in this section

referred to as the 'overdue company tax') of

ordinary company tax ..... due and payable by the

company in relation to the relevant year of income;

. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . "

33. S.3 of the T(UCT)A Act in turn provides that:-

"(1) In this Act, unless the contrary intention

appears -

. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

'company tax' means ordinary company tax or

undistributed profits tax;

. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

'ordinary company tax', in relation to a company,

means -

(a) income tax assessed under the (Income Tax)

Assessment Act and levied on the taxable income of

the company being income tax imposed as such by an

Act; and

(b) additional tax payable by the company under s.207

of the (Income Tax) Assessment Act in relation to

tax referred to in paragraph (a);

. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . "

34. Despite the fact that s.3(1) of the T(UCT)A Act provides that, unless the contrary intention appears, "ordinary company tax" shall include "additional tax", the Applicant contends that a contrary intention does appear from the Act. In particular, he points to s.3(11) of the T(UCT)A Act which provides:-

"For the purpose of ascertaining the amount of ordinary

company tax ... that ... is payable at a particular time

by a company in relation to a year of income in a case

where a copy of a notice of assessment in respect of

ordinary company tax ... payable by the company in

relation to that year of income has been served on a

person under sub-section 18(5), the assessment shall be

deemed not to have been served on the company until the

day on which the copy was served on the person."

35. In appraising the submission, it is important to bear in mind that the T(UCT)A Act was framed so as to embrace all the varying circumstances within a defined area which had resulted in non-recovery of company tax and in doing so, to have regard to all the various stages which might have been reached in the assessment process before recovery became impossible. In some instances the tax had not only been assessed but it became due and payable before the target company ceased to exist (cf. s.15(2)(b) T(UCT)A Act). In others, the company had ceased to exist only after the tax had been assessed, but before it had become due and payable (cf. s.15(2)(a) T(UCT)A Act). In other cases, no assessment had issued against the target company as at the date of enactment of the T(UCT)A Act. In those cases, in some instances the companies still subsisted and in others they had ceased to exist (cf. s.15(4) T(UCT)A Act). In consequence, the T(UCT)A Act made provision for completing or perfecting the assessment process against the target company. At the same time, it made provision for giving notice to vendors prospectively liable to vendor recoupment tax of the assessment against the company - whether pre- or post-T(UCT)A Act - so that they might have the opportunity to test the soundness of the assessment against the company (ss. 15 and 18 T(UCT)A Act). Provision was also made for services of notice of the assessment on a promoter (ss.15(6) and 18(4) T(UCT)A Act), but not so as to enable any promoter to challenge the soundness of the assessment against the company unless the only recoupment tax was promoter's recoupment tax (cf. s.15(6) T(UCT)A Act). That being so, where - as here (cf. post) - the "promoter" had ceased to control the target company before its original assessment, the promoter would be dependent on others to establish that the target company was not liable in respect of any "overdue company tax".

36. As a result, s.18(1) provided that:-

"Where -

(a) under the (Income Tax) Assessment Act, the

Commissioner is required or permitted to serve a

notice of assessment on a company in relation to

the liability of the company to pay ordinary

company tax...; and

(b) vendors recoupment tax is payable, or the

Commissioner is of the opinion that vendors

recoupment tax is likely to become payable ...

the notice shall, notwithstanding s.174 of the

Assessment Act, be served on the company by being served

on .....

. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . ."

the person or one of the persons, if more than one person is prospectively liable, with copies of the notice being served on each other person prospectively liable in the latter case.

37. In the circumstances of this case there was no scope for the application of s.18(1) because the assessment against the company had issued in 1976 with the result that the tax assessed became due and payable on 30 April 1976. (Bearing in mind the presumption of regularity, I am satisfied that the notice of assessment was duly and promptly served following its issue). A further consequence was that, by force of s.207 of the Assessment Act, interest was accruing against VTH from that date. That being so, s.18(1) had no application to the assessment of the company and no place in the notification process in relation to vendors.

38. Instead s.18(5) applied. It provided:-

"Where -

(a) in a case to which sub-section (1) did not apply, a

notice of assessment in respect of the liability of

a company to pay ordinary company tax ... in

relation to a year of income has been served, or

purported to have been served, on the company,

whether before or after the commencement of this

Act; and

(b) the Commissioner is of the opinion that vendors

recoupment tax is likely to become payable...,

the following provisions have effect:"

The sub-section proceeded to specify those provisions.

39. Seen against that background it is clear in my view that the first objective of s.3(11) of the T(UCT)A Act was to ensure that, as against vendors, the amount to be recovered by the Commissioner by way of additional tax should only relate to the period from assessment of the company after 13 December 1982 if effected pursuant to s.18(1) by notice to the vendor or a representative vendor; or, where the assessment of the company had been previously made, to ensure that the liability of vendors for additional tax should not commence to run until such time as notice of an earlier assessment was served pursuant to s.18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act. S.15 of the T(UCT)A Act is consistent with that view.

40. Accordingly, although not having had the advantage of hearing argument from Counsel for the Commissioner, I have concluded that the argument of the Applicant as to quantum is, in substance, correct. As no notice of assessment could have been served upon any vendor pursuant to s.18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act prior to 13 December 1982, I find that liability to additional tax could not have commenced to run until 13 January 1983. That being so, the only additional tax which might have been recoverable from Simons would have represented one-fifth of interest on the sum of $307-70 at the rate of 10% per annum from 13 January 1983 to 13 February 1983 ($2-61), and at 20% thereafter calculated to 24 July 1984 ($88-68), thereby at least reducing the unpaid company tax from $604-63 to $398-99 and promoter's taxable amount from $120-92 to $79-80.

WAS ANY AMOUNT UNPAID?

41. The second submission for the Applicant was that I should hold that there was not any evidence which would enable the Tribunal to find that at any time after 13 December 1982 there had remained unpaid any amount of ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax due and payable by the target company in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1975. In consequence, it was contended that, as the existence of such a liability is essential to the liability of a promoter for recoupment tax, the objection should be upheld. I reject the submission. As the evidence stands before me, I find that an assessment issued requiring company tax in the sum of $307-70 to be paid on 30 April 1976. There is simply no evidence by or on account of VTH indicating that that sum or any other sum has been paid since; for the Applicant, it was argued that I should find that, because the Commissioner failed in response to a summons to produce any evidence indicating that any notice of assessment or copy of any notice of assessment has yet been served on anyone under s.18(5), I should conclude that there was no attempt at service because there was no relevant indebtedness. I am not persuaded that that is the appropriate inference to be drawn.

WAS THERE A SCHEME?

42. The third contention for the Applicant is that there was no "scheme" such as would give rise to Simons being a member of any "eligible promoter's class" in relation to the target company. I have been asked to find that, although there is sufficient evidence to support the proposition that Jensen bought the shares in VTH and did so at a time appropriate to make applicable the T(UCT)A legislation, the evidence also establishes that Jensen sold the same shares within the same year of income - that is, prior to 30 June 1975 - and that, having done so, it left VTH with capacity to pay the income tax which was later to be assessed as due and payable in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1975. The evidence of the purchase is centred around the production of an Agreement for Sale; entries in the Registers of the target company; and the published accounts of Jensen for the year of income ended 30 June 1975. Leaving out of consideration the three redeemable preference shares having a paid-up value of $1 each, all of that evidence points to Jensen having acquired the entire shareholding in VTH for $2,531,125 - that company then having net tangible assets of $2,533,125.

43. The published accounts of Jensen also record that, in the same period and before 30 June 1975, 100% of the paid-up capital (excluding from consideration three redeemable preference shares) in VTH was disposed of by sale. Despite the reference in those accounts in that context to "net tangible assets acquired" it would seem to be appropriate to understand the schedules to the accounts as asserting that the net tangible assets of the target company at point of disposition of its shares by Jensen were $2,500 and that the shares were disposed of for a consideration of $2,542. However, that apart, there was no evidence as to the net worth of the target company at point of sale; no evidence as to the terms of sale; no evidence as to the identity of the purchaser; and no evidence of its or their relationship to Jensen. In consequence, there is no evidence which would enable me to say either that the acquisition and sale were not part of a relevant "scheme" or that the capacity of the target company to pay was not diminished.

44. In so holding I am conscious of the dictum that "all evidence is to be weighed according to the proof which it was in the power of one side to have produced, and in the power of the other to have contradicted (Lord Mansfield in Blatch v. Archer [1774] EngR 2; (1774) 1 Cowp. 63 at p.65 - cited by Isaacs, J. in Moreau v. F.C. of T. [1926] HCA 28; (1926) 39 C.L.R. 65). But the difficulty which arises in the application of the T(UCT)A legislation in circumstances such as this is that neither the Respondent nor the Applicant has any significant capacity to control any of the evidence relating to any "scheme" which might be presented to the Tribunal. The practical consequence of the legislation is that the Commissioner raises assessments against vendors and promoters upon the assumption that the inability of a target company to pay tax assessable against it has been occasioned by a "scheme" involving a sale evidenced by an Agreement for Sale. Sometimes consideration of additional evidence will establish that there never was a liability in the target company to pay tax (cf. Case U142 87 A.T.C. 307). At other times the evidence will establish that the sale which seemed to have been given effect to by an Agreement for Sale produced in evidence was not effected at all (cf. Case U205 87 A.T.C. 1159). But, in those cases, the applicants were in a position to produce substantial relevant evidence. In a case such as this, as I have found, the Applicant has no such knowledge or information and no capacity to procure full disclosure of the relevant circumstances to this Tribunal or to himself without calling upon witnesses to attend to give evidence without prior knowledge of what that evidence might be.

45. So it is that a person who has no knowledge or information about the transactions giving rise to his liability and who derives no advantage from the actions said to occasion that liability, has had cast upon him a liability to pay a tax. The tax to be paid is a function of tax unpaid by another. The basis for being so liable to that tax does not depend upon the taxpayer having done any particular thing or having had any knowledge or having derived any benefit from the transaction. His liability is complete even though, in practical terms, it is likely to be impossible for him to establish the circumstances which, should they be established to have existed, would have resulted in him having no liability. The result may be harsh, but to the extent to which the result is occasioned by an inability of the Applicant to persuade as to the findings of fact beyond his knowledge, it is a result occasioned by the substantive law rather than by any deficiency in the principles to be applied in the course of the finding of facts.

46. It is also appropriate to refer to the circumstance that the quality of the evidence establishing a "sale" to Jensen under a liability generating "scheme" is little different to the quality of the evidence available to establish the sale of the same shares by Jensen before 30 June 1975. For reasons expressed in Case V38 88 A.T.C. 325, I consider that the evidence presented before me would have been insufficient to establish the case for such sales by Jensen at the instance of the party bearing the onus of proof. So it is that at the end of the day Simons has failed to present sufficient evidence to persuade that there was not "a sale under a scheme" such as could attract liability to the tax or that the tax due by the company was in fact unpaid at all.

47. Having said as much, it is appropriate to observe that the difficulties which faced Simons in relation to such matters would not be likely to have arisen for Jensen if it, as the promoter, had been assessed.

THE SCHEME AND INABILITY TO PAY

48. The fourth argument to be considered is that the Tribunal should find as a fact that no attempts have been made by the Commissioner to recover the tax of $307-70 at any time since it was assessed on 10 March 1976 and that, in consequence, the Tribunal should hold that a necessary basis for liability, namely that "the taxable company was unable, having regard to other debts in the taxable company, to pay to the Commissioner all the company tax due and payable by the taxable company", has been found not to be made out. As the evidence stands before me and for the reasons advanced in considering the previous argument, I am not so persuaded. I simply have no evidence at all as to the financial position of VTH at 10 March 1976.

CONCLUSION

49. That being so, although I have been persuaded that the assessment is excessive, I have not been persuaded that it is wholly so. In consequence, but for the Commissioner's concession, I might have reduced the assessment. But what I now have to consider is whether I should direct that the Commissioner's determination upon the objection be varied and that the objection be wholly allowed for no other reason than that the Commissioner has consented to such an order being made.

50. In the remarkable circumstances of this particular case I consider it appropriate to so act in making the Order which both parties agree be made and, in doing so, to rely upon the consent of the Commissioner. Accordingly, that the Order of the Tribunal will be that the objection should be allowed. However, as a matter of caution, I would merely note that I would not wish it to be thought that the provision of such a consent alone will in all cases and at all times be sufficient to justify the making of such an Order.
