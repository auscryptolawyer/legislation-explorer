REASONS FOR DECISION

Tribunal: T.R.Hartigan J. (President)

Place: Brisbane

Date: 6 September 1989

I have been requested by both parties to this application for a review of objection decisions by the Commissioner of Taxation to refer a question of law to the Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia.

The parties make their request pursuant to section 45 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 (Cth). That provision where relevant states:

"45(1) The Tribunal may of its own motion or at the request of a party, refer a question of law arising in proceeding before the Tribunal to the Federal Court or Australia for decision

(a) in the case of a proceeding before the Tribunal constituted by 2 or more members at which a presidential member presides - a question shall not be so referred without the concurrence or that presidential member; or

(b) in the case of a proceeding before the Tribunal at which a presidential member does not preside - a question shall not be so referred without the concurrence of the President.

(2) The Federal Court of Australia has jurisdiction to hear and determine a question of law referred to it under this section and that jurisdiction shall be exercised by that Court constituted as a Full Court.

(3) Where a question of law arising in any proceeding has been referred to the Federal Court of Australia under this section, the Tribunal shall not, in that proceeding-
  • (a) give a decision to which the question it relevant while the reference is pending; or
  • (b) proceed in a manner, or make a decision, that is inconsistent with the opinion of the Federal Court of Australia on the question."

A comparable provision of the Repatriation Act 1920 (Cth), namely that "the Tribunal may ... refer a qustion of law" to he Federal Court conferred a discretion upon the Tribunal concerned. The words did not mean that a duty to refer the question of law was imposed upon the Tribunal: Hassell; ex parte Pride (1984) 2 FCR 319 at 322-323. As Toohey J. observed (at 323): "Since there is no obligation on the Tribunal to refer a question of law to the Federal Court, there is no statutory duty to be performed."

I turn now to the exercise of that discretion in this case

Mr. Handley QC and Mr. Slater of counsel appeared for the respondent commissioner, Mr. Sullivan appeared for the taxpayer Mr.Handley informed me that the request to state a case was the suggestion of his client. Mr.Handley submitted that this was an appropriate matter in which to state a case. He also informed me that both parties have had the chance to explore the possibility that there are outstanding issues of fact. In Mr Handley's words, what is left separating the parties in this case is a pure question of law. That question, he submitted, will not only determine this case, its resolution will be relevant to a number of other cases where a similar legal question is raised.

Mr. Handley QC submitted that a decision in the stated case would dispose finally of the issue between the parties before the Tribunal. He also submitted that in the circumstances of this case costs would be saved and a final decision expedited. Mr.Handley submitted that should one or other party wish to take this matter further the decision of the Full Court on the case stated would be one of a final nature such that an application for special leave to appeal to the High Court could be entertained. This was not a case such as Swiss Aluminiun Australia Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation and Ors. [1987] HCA 43; (1987) 73 ALR 584.

Mr. Sullivan for the applicant joined in the request to state a case. He submitted that the facts are within a narrow band and they are not in dispute. He also submitted that the answer to the case stated would allow the matter to be disposed of thereafter. Mr. Sullivan pointed to the question of costs ant some economy would be gained by his client if a case was stated Both counsel referred me to a number of decisions where justice: of the High Court of Australia, sitting on taxation appeals, ha< referred questions of the sort in issue here to the Full High Court. There are other decisions where the High Court heard case: of this sort referred by the Taxation Board of Review.

The question at issue here involves the question whether a sum of money paid to the taxpayer as the result of a deed of restraint was correctly included in the taxpayer's assessable income as a capital gain as defined in Part IIIA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 pursuant to the operation of S.160M. I was told by counsel that the question has not received the attention of the Full Court and, as I have said, I was told that a number of other cases depend on this question.

Not without some hesitation I have decided to state a case to the Full Court as requested by the parties. In taking that course I have borne in mind what was said by the Full Cour of the Federal Court of Australia in Mayor Councillor and Citizens of the City of Doncaster and Templestowe v. The Minister for Community Services (Full Court, Sweeney Keely and Ryan JJ unreported, Melbourne 23 June 1987).

Generally speaking, an appropriately constituted Tribunal, in the light of its knowledge of the relevant facts, may well choose to decide itself any question of law which may arise. AS their Honours said (at 9 of the Court's reasons for judgement):

"Indeed the Tribunal has generally chosen to do so however difficult the question of law may have appeared to be."

Their Honours also pointed out (at p.9) that two important matters flow from the Tribunal resolving at first instance all the questions before it. These two matters are the advantage or expedition and the advantage of allowing an unsuccessful party an opportunity to decide whether to appeal in the light of all the findings made in the case by the Tribunal, having regard to the financial and other consequences for that party of a decision which is against him. The court pointed out that such an assessment can usefully be made only by such a party.

Finally, the Court pointed to the prematurity of the reference before the Court. This refers to the fact that a deputy president had referred the case at a directions hearing and not at a substantive hearing at which time the Tribunal will be much better placed usually to identify when and in what factual context a question of law, if appropriate, should be referred (see p.9 of the reasons). That is not the case here. The matter has come on for hearing and the parties have requested the reference on the basis of the narrowness of the facts and the lack of any dispute in relation to them.

In this case expedition is not advanced by my not stating a case. Furthermore in the circumstances of this case in the light of the attitude to the question of law taken by both parties, this case does not have the usual advantage of leaving it to the unsuccessful party to decide to appeal or not, having regard to the financial and other consequences. Indeed, Mr. Sullivan identified financial advantages for the taxpayer where an appeal seems inevitable, regardless of who is successful before the Tribunal, to have the case go to the Full Court at this stage.

I respectfully consider that the approach taken by the Full Court in the City of Doncaster case (supra) is a valuable guide in the exercise of discretion under s.45 of the Act. Adopting that approach may well mean that in the majority of references which arise under s.45 of the Act the Tribunal will, in the sound exercise of its discretion, not state a case to the Court. Indeed it may well be fairly rare to find a properly constituted Tribunal take this course while it prefers to decide the question of law itself.

Having said that I am satisfied that this is a case where I should exercise my discretion in favour of the request of both parties to refer the question of law identified here to the Full Court under s.45 of the Act. I have weighed the considerations submitted to me by counsel in the light of the observations of the court in the Mayor of Doncaster case (supra). I am satisfied that this is one of the relatively rare cases where considerations of expedition and economy and the administration of justice require this important and difficult question of lav to be decided by the Full Court at the earliest opportunity. Experienced counsel have submitted that no dispute as to the facts arise and on the material before me that is the position. Accordingly I shall direct that the question of law arising upon the facts agreed to by the parties as set out in the annexure hereto is to be referred to the Full Court of the Federal court of Australia pursuant to s.45 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

STATED CASE TO THE FEDERAL COURT UNDER SECTION 45(2) OF THE ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ACT

Case stated by Hartigan J., at the request of the parties, pursuant to section 45{2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975, for the decision of the Full Federal Court of Australia upon the following facts as agreed to by the parties herein :-

1. In or about late August or early September 1985 the Applicant was first employed by A Limited ( “A” ). He was employed as the Marketing Director/General Manager of the Window Furnishings Division of that company. On or about 1 September 1985 the Applicant and A entered into a written agreement ("the Employment Agreement") governing the terms and conditions of the employment.

  1. In the course of his employment with A the Applicant was responsible for management of the operations of the Window Furnishings Division of A. The business activities of that Division accounted for a very significant proportion (approximately two-thirds) of the total business activities of A
  2. On or about 27 June 1986, the Applicant entered into a Deed with A the purpose of which was recited in the Deed as being "to record the terms upon which has agreed, L consideration of the covenants of A herein contained and from and after the date of his ceasing to be employed by A to be restrained from carrying on or being interested in any business or other undertaking or activity as more particularly herein referred to" (hereinafter referred to as "the Restrictive Covenant Deed"). A true copy of the Restrictive Covenant Deed is annexed to this Stated Case and marked "A".
  3. It was orally agreed between the Applicant and A that the restraint of trade imposed by the Employment Agreement should cease to have any effect from the date of entry into the Restrictive Covenant Deed.
  4. On or about 27 June 1986, and before 30 June 1986, A paid to the Applicant an amount of $40,000, pursuant to the terms of the Restrictive Covenant Deed.

6. The taxpayer did not incur any capital loss during the year of income ended 30 June 1986.

The parties request that the following questions of law be determined by the Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia:-

(A) Was there, in consequence of the facts recited herein, included in the assessable income of the Applicant for the year of income ended 30 June 1986 -

(a) an amount of $40,000; or

(b) some other amount, and if so, what amount,

pursuant to sub-section 160ZO(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936?

(B) Who should pay the costs of these proceedings?
