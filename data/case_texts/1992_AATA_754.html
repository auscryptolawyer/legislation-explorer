John Pope Electrical Pty Ltd and Commissioner for ACT Revenue [1992] AATA 754 (25 August 1992)

Last Updated: 1 August 2008



Payroll Tax - review of objection decision - extension of time in which to lodge application for review - criteria to apply in exercise of Tribunal's discretion.



Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 : S.29

Pay-roll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971 : Ss.21, 21E, 40, 40A(2)(3), 40C(1)(2)(3)(4), 40D(1)(2)

Pay-roll Tax Act 1987 : S.12

Taxation (Administration) Act 1987

Re Holstar Agencies Pty Ltd v Collector of Customs (1981) 4 ALD 308

Hunter Valley Developments PtyLtd and Others v Minister for Home Affairs and Environment (1985) 58 ALR 305

Re Ardesia Pty Ltd (in liq) v Chief Minister for Australian Capital Territory (1991) 23 ALD 255



DECISION AND REASONS



Re: JOHN POPE ELECTRICAL PTY LTD

(formerly HADOTOP PTY LTD)

And: COMMISSIONER FOR ACT REVENUE



RESPONDENT



No: A91/222

AAT Decision No 8194

Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

The application for extension of time is refused.



(Sgd) K L Beddoe

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. A91/222

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )



Re: JOHN POPE ELECTRICAL PTY LTD

(formerly HADOTOP PTY LTD)

APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER FOR ACT REVENUE

RESPONDENT



REASONS FOR DECISION



Tribunal : Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date : 25 August 1992

Place : Canberra



The matter before the Tribunal is an application for the Tribunal to extend time in which to lodge an objection to a Payroll Tax assessment.



2. The relevant decision for the purpose of the application was notified to Gillespie-Jones and Co, the agent for the relevant companies, by the Delegate of the Commissioner for ACT Revenue on or about 22 April 1991 [Annexure N]. This decision disallowed an objection to an assessment of Payroll Tax and advised that application could be made within 60 days to the Tribunal for review of the matter if the taxpayer were dissatisfied with the decision.



3. The applicant did not apply to the Tribunal within the 60 days, in fact there has been considerable delay. Hence there is the present application for extension of time to the Tribunal. The delay involved is nearly six months. Notwithstanding that the hearing of this matter occupied two days no explanation of this delay has been given to the Tribunal (cf paragraph 22 of Mr Gillespie-Jones' affidavit).



4. The chronology of and correspondence on the matters are set out as follows in the affidavit of Ian Gillespie-Jones:



13 October 1988: ACT Revenue Collections notified Climatrol Air-Conditioning Pty Ltd of its 1 July 1986 - 30 June 1987 Group Assessment under the Pay-roll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971

Total: $17,558.95

and



1 July 1987 - 31 July 1987 Group Assessment under the Pay-roll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971

Total: $ 2,195.65

[Annexure A]



8 November 1988: Gillespie-Jones & Co, Solicitors, advised they acted for Climatrol Air-conditioning Pty Ltd and Hadotop Pty Ltd but stressed the view with reasons re the inappropriateness that group assessment should issue to two of the four companies

[Annexure B)



25 November 1988: Gillespie-Jones & Co, in a letter to ACT Revenue Collections, again referred to matters it raised in its letter of 8 November 1988 and requested answers

[Annexure C]

24 April 1989: Gillespie-Jones & Co again requested a response

[Annexure D]



21 July 1989: Change of name of Company : Hadotop Pty Ltd changed to John Pope Electrical Pty Ltd

[Annexure E]



6 September 1990: ACT Revenue disallowed the objection. It was said that in terms of s.21 of the Pay-roll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971 individual grouping existed between Climatrol and Emalec, Emalec and Hadotop, Hadotop and Hannan so that by s.21E of the Act these individual groupings formed a single group. Dissatisfaction with the decision, it was noted, required application to be made to the Tribunal within 60 days

[Annexure F]



19 September 1990: Delegate for the Commissioner ACT Revenue Collections letter to Gillespie-Jones & Co: this communication confirmed that individual groups existed in terms of s.21 of the Pay-roll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971 for the period 1 July 1986 to 31 July 1987 inclusive



- in addition it stated that a group also existed between Hadotop Pty Ltd and Emalec Pty Ltd for the period 1 August 1987 to 30 June 1988 inclusive by virtue of s.80 of the Taxation (Administration) Act 1987



- stated that original assessments may have issued in error as they were not issued to the individual companies addressing their liability. Hence amended assessments would have to be prepared



- preparatory to the issue of amended assessments, it was requested that a designated group employer be nominated by virtue of s.21 of the Pay-roll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971 and s.12 of the Pay-roll Tax Act 1987. This established exemption entitlement otherwise a flat rate of 5% applied to all company assessment

[Annexure G]



27 September 1990: Letter from Gillespie-Jones and Co to the ACT Revenue Collections - sought clarification of error in dates contained in earlier communications



- stated inappropriateness to refer the previous objection decision to the Tribunal

[Annexure H]



13 November 1990: ACT Revenue letter to Gillespie-Jones requested nomination of a designated employer exemption [Annexure I]



16 November 1990: Gillespie-Jones & Co letter to ACT Revenue opined that every employer was entitled to exemption entitlement whether or not a designated group employer had been nominated. It also disagreed with the applicability of a 5% flat rate in the absence of a nomination

[Annexure J]



5 December 1990: ACT Revenue to Gillespie-Jones and Co as agent for Hadotop Pty Ltd: noted the failure to nominate a designated group employer and notified an amended assessment said to replace the 13 October 1988 assessment. It applied the 5% flat rate and was in respect of:

1 July 1986 - 30 June 1987

1 July 1987 - 31 July 1987

1 August 1987 - 30 June 1988



Total Tax Due : $13,496.87



Advice was included that objection could be lodged with the Commissioner within 60 days after service of this notice

[Annexure K]



25 January 1991: Communication from Gillespie-Jones & Co to ACT Revenue advised of dissatisfaction with the assessment and objected relying on 14 grounds

[Annexure L]



5 February 1991: From ACT Government Solicitor to Gillespie-Jones & Co sought to justify the assessments notified

[Annexure M]



22 April 1991: ACT Revenue to Gillespie-Jones and Co : notified disallowance of objections and advised application could be made within 60 days to the Tribunal for review of the matter

[Annexure N]



16 May 1991: From Gillespie-Jones and Co to ACT Revenue : sought further and better particulars on a number of matters

[Annexure O]



22 August 1991: From Gillespie-Jones and Co to ACT Revenue : noted no reply to letter of 16 May 1991

[Annexure P]



17 September 1991: From ACT Government Solicitor to Gillespie-Jones & Co: offer of the Commissioner's officers to discuss any particular aspect of the case

[Annexure Q]



28 October 1991: Gillespie-Jones & Co to ACT Government Solicitor: noted the lack of particulars in answer to queries contained in 16 May 1991 letter, and upon the particulars being furnished, the possibility of the conference offer being taken up

[Annexure R]

22 November 1991: ACT Government Solicitor to Gillespie-Jones & Co: reiterated the conference offer; refused to furnish the particulars and noted that the communication did not operate as consent by the Commissioner for an application to be made to the Tribunal out of time

[Annexure S]



20 December 1991: Application for extension of time filed in this Tribunal



5. For the purposes of this matter which relates to an application for extension of time, the Tribunal had first to consider the validity of notification of the objection decision and whether the Tribunal had jurisdiction to extend time for lodging an application for review. Reasons in writing have already been given for that decision.



6. As a preliminary point I decided that the objection decision notified on 22 April 1991 by Delegate of the Commissioner for ACT Revenue was a valid notification of the objection decision.



7. Sub-section 40 of the Payroll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971 ("the Act") sets out the procedure by which an employer seeks review of an objection decision by the Commissioner.



"40. An employer who is dissatisfied with a decision under section 39 on an objection by the employer may, within 60 days after service on the employer of notice of the decision, lodge with the Commissioner a request in writing to refer the decision to the Tribunal.



40A. (2) Where the period for the lodgment by an employer of a request under section 40 has ended, the employer may, notwithstanding that the period has ended, send the request to the Commissioner together with an application in writing asking that the request be treated as having been duly lodged.



40A. (3) An application under sub-section (1) or (2) shall state fully and in detail the circumstances concerning, and the reasons for, the failure by the person to lodge the objection or request as required by this Act."



8. In particular, sub-section 40A(2) is the specific provision whereby applications may be made out of time. The procedure for consideration of the application for extension of time for lodging objections is then set out in sub-sections 40B, 40C and 40D.



"40C. (1) Where the Commissioner receives an application under sub-section 40A (2), the Commissioner shall, as soon as practicable, send the application to the Tribunal.



(2) The sending of an application to the Tribunal under sub-section (1) shall, for the purposes of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, be deemed to constitute the making by the employer concerned of an application to the Tribunal to extend the time within which the request may be lodged with the Commissioner.



(3) The Tribunal may grant or refuse the application.



(4) Where an application under sub-section 40A (2) has been granted, the employer shall, for the purposes of this Part, be treated as having duly lodged the request to which the application relates.



40D. (1) Where an employer duly lodges, or is to be treated as having duly lodged, a request under section 40, the Commissioner shall comply with the request.



(2) The referral of a decision on an objection to the Tribunal shall, for the purposes of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, be deemed to constitute the making by the employer of an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision."



9. The reason I set out these provisions and their machinery in full is to make the point that the legislative procedure has not been followed. In these matters applications were sent directly to the Tribunal; in effect the procedural provisions of the Act were bypassed. This was acknowledged in the hearing before me [Transcript 46].



10. The Payroll Tax (Territories) Assessment Act 1971 ("the Assessment Act") is a Commonwealth Act. With the passing of the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No 2) No 62 1987 the termination date of 31 July 1987 was set to apply to the Assessment Act [Transcript 50]. Certain administrative changes were included : the Federal Commissioner of Taxation who had the general administration of the Assessment Act was replaced by the respondent as the administrator. The Taxation (Administration) Act came into operation on 1 August 1987. The Assessment Act ceased to have effect after 31 July 1987.



11. As regards the application for extension of time having been sent to the Tribunal and not to the Commissioner, it was the understanding of the applicant that the Tribunal had accepted the responsibility of serving the Commissioner [Transcript 51-52] and this is what has happened. Since this has occurred there is open the path by which the jurisdiction of the Tribunal may be exercised and there is no impediment to the examination of the merits of the matter although the procedure laid down by the machinery provisions of the Act has not been followed.



12. Sub-section 29 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 sets out the procedure whereby an applicant may apply for review of a decision. The operation of that provision has been excluded by sections 40, 40A and 40C insofar as those sections prescribe the time for lodgment of an application for review with the Commissioner and provide a procedure for extension of time for lodgment of such applications (see Re Holstar Agencies Pty Ltd and Collector of Customs (1981) 4 ALD 308.



13. There is no doubt in my mind that this Tribunal has a discretion under the Assessment Act to grant the extension of time sought and I did not understand Mr Walker to suggest otherwise.



14. The criteria to be applied in the exercise of such a discretion are best summarised in the judgment of Wilcox J in Hunter Valley Developments Pty Ltd and Others v Minister for Home Affairs and Environment (1985) 58 ALR 305 at 310-311. These criteria will be adopted in the exercise of the Tribunal's discretion in respect of this application since they have been recognised in many cases as the appropriate basis for consideration of similar applications under the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.



15. His Honour said at 310:



"(a) Although the section does not, in terms place any onus of proof upon an applicant for extension, an application has to be made. Special circumstances need not be shown but the Court will not grant the application unless positively satisfied that it is proper to do so."



The applicant does, however, bear the onus of rebutting what his Honour said at page 310 was "the prima facie rule that proceedings commenced outside that period will not be entertained . . . It is a precondition to the exercise of discretion in his favour that the applicant for extension show an `acceptable explanation of the delay' and that it is `fair and equitable in the circumstances' to extend time".



16. The applicant's evidence reposes in the affidavit of Ian Gillespie-Jones sworn on 26 February 1992. This affidavit reiterates the matters that are raised in the chronology of correspondence between Gillespie-Jones and Co and the respondent. The affidavit therefore canvasses inter alia issues that are raised in the course of correspondence : initial misspelling of the Company name; the unresolved matter of company grouping for taxation purposes; the flat rate tax of 5%; delay in reply to queries; whether Gillespie-Jones and Co was the agent for the companies; the question of ambiguous time periods; nomination of designated group employer.



17. The affidavit states [paragraph 26] "(a) hearing is important to resolve the conflict between the various assessments as well as affording justice to the Applicant". As well, the application to this Tribunal for an extension of time dated 20 December 1991 sought an extension of time so that it might contest the assessments and stated as its reason "It had hoped to be able to resolve the matter through correspondence with the ACT Government Solicitor as it has since November 1988 but this now does not seem possible".



18. With respect, it was already apparent that a hearing for resolution of the dispute in relation to the assessments would be required if there were disagreement with the Delegate's decision [Annexure N] on 22 April 1991; further, there was sufficient evidence from the previous and existing correspondence between the parties that the Delegate's objection decision notified on 22 April 1991 would not be acceptable to the applicant.



19. Furthermore the decision of the Delegate of the Commissioner for ACT Revenue of 5 December 1990 [Annexure K] gave notice, the tenor of which foreshadowed finality in this matter: "It should be noted that this assessment replaces the assessment, which may have issued in error, dated 13 October 1988". It also indicated that the Commissioner no longer awaited nomination of a designated group employer: "As the group members have failed to nominate a designated group employer, the following amended assessments have been prepared as outlined in the abovementioned letters".



20. It is tendentious on the part of the applicant to assert that it had hoped to resolve the matter by correspondence. The correspondence had been drawn out and the respondent had clearly attempted to bring the matter to finality. It was then the responsibility of the applicant to follow the usual course in the process by lodging an application for review of the objection decision notified on 22 April 1991.



21. The issue raised by the objection is in the nature of a commercial matter during the course of which the applicant has been in receipt of legal advice. In Re Ardesia Pty Ltd (in liq) and Chief Minister for Australian Capital Territory (1991) 23 ALD 255 at 255 the ACT Tribunal (Todd President) said:



"In dealing with this issue I find it unnecessary to decide whose version of the events that occurred is correct. An application for review should have been lodged, notwithstanding that discussions were proceeding. The applicant was aware of the right of review, a right contained in a legislative provision which specifically stated the time available for lodgment . . . this is not a case of an unrepresented would be applicant in a social security or employees' compensation case. It is a commercial case and in such matters it seems to me that there is a higher and indeed a high,duty to be timely in the making of applications."



22. With respect, the Tribunal endorses these comments in this analogous matter about which there is no dispute in relation to the facts. There is really no explanation of the delay so that it would not be fair and equitable to the respondent in the circumstances for the Tribunal to grant the extension requested. The continued correspondence, commenced on 8 November 1988, constituted notice to the Commissioner that his decision was being contested but given the terms in which the decisions were couched and to which I have adverted, the forum for review of the decision was always going to be in this Tribunal.



23. In respect of any prejudice that may affect the respondent : fiscal legislation is not beneficial in character as, for example, social security legislation might be construed. The applicant was in receipt of legal advice. The dispute had become drawn out and had caused the respondent to revise its position. In these circumstances it is in the interests of the community that a conclusion be reached in matters of this nature.



24. In considering the matter of fairness as between the applicants and other persons in like position, as Wilcox J notes at 311 in Hunter Valley Developments (cited above) "the mere absence of prejudice is not enough to justify the grant of an extension . . . in this context, public considerations often intrude".



25. The applicant contends a pattern of correspondence had been established and that the taxpayer had not, in the course of that correspondence, been disabused of its position. With respect I disagree for reasons already given. While it appears that the applicant wished to resolve the matter without resort to the Tribunal nevertheless, it was incumbent on the applicant to protect its position and the applicant had been advised by the respondent on how this could be done.



26. The words of the ACT Tribunal in Re Ardesia Pty Ltd (1991) 23 ALD 255 are appropriate in this context:



"Finally, there is the question of prejudice to the respondent. No doubt the respondent would not in such a case be prejudiced in conducting its case by the grant of an extension of time. But the case, involving as it would valuation evidence, would involve the respondent and thus the revenue, and ultimately the taxpaying community, in considerable expense even if the applicant's claim, having gone to a hearing, were to fail. If the applicant fails, that expense is thrown away even though the respondent has succeeded. I raise the question whether the Tribunal should not be given power to grant an extension of time on the footing that the applicant must pay the costs of the respondent if the application for review is unsuccessful."



27. While there is no doubt that the respondent was well aware that the applicant was dissatisfied with the objection decision it does not follow that the respondent was aware at all relevant times that the applicant intended seeking a review in this Tribunal. It would have been resonable to infer from the evidence before me that the applicant was seeking to avoid an application for review in the Tribunal.



28. In the circumstances the respondent was entitled to treat its communication of the objection decision on 22 April 1991 as the final formal matter from which an application for review could be made to the Tribunal. The applicant was not justified in treating that communication as being somehow negotiable by correspondence. Nor was the applicant entitled to assume that the Tribunal would extend time. The application by the applicant for an extension of time to lodge an application for review of the objection decision will be refused.



I hereby certify that this and the preceding eleven (11) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of JOHN POPE ELECTRICAL PTY LTD (formerly HADOTOP PTY LTD) and COMMISSIONER FOR ACT REVENUE - A91/222



(Signed) M McLennan (Associate)



Date: 25 August 1992
