REASONS FOR DECISION

8 July 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

THE TAXPAYER

1. The Applicant, who was born in 1927, migrated to this country from Belfast in 1949. He married in the following year and six children were born of that marriage. In 1976, as a result of proceedings initiated by him, he was divorced following a substantial period of separation. Although at the time of separation he left the family home to take up residence in another property he owned, I accept that he continued thereafter to visit the family home quite frequently. In the latter part of 1987, he returned to the family home to occupy a flat there.

2. Once established in Australia, the Applicant went into business on his own account, starting with the manufacture of proprietary medicinal lines. The initial business succeeded and, over the years, the scope of his business interests expanded substantially. Business was carried on principally through the medium of proprietary companies controlled within the family. A broader range of interests extended to motel ownership and the ownership of a greyhound track.

3. The Applicant has been a wealthy man. (His present financial position was not explained). To what extent that was contributed to by income-earning activities during the period under review is very much in dispute. However, in at least large measure, that wealth was the consequence of the generosity of the Applicant's father. During his lifetime he distributed very large sums of money to his children. When and how much was distributed to the Applicant is a matter of some uncertainty but the distribution was clearly complete well before the commencement of the period in question. Similarly, again prior to the period in question, large sums of money had been realized from the sale of business assets.

THE ASSESSMENTS

4. The proceedings before me arose out of an investigation which commenced in about July 1981. In the course of that investigation his taxable income was calculated by the "assets betterment" method. (Despite the criticisms of that phrase fairly made in L'Estrange v. F.C.T. 78 A.T.C. 4744, I will use it - rather because of its widespread use and familiarity than anything else.) It resulted in an amended assessment for the year of income ended 30 June 1976 issuing on 6 June 1983, and amended assessments for the years of income ended 30 June 1977 to 1980 (inclusive) issuing on 22 February 1984. Additional tax was also levied. In relation to the 1977 and 1978 years, those amended assessments followed earlier amended assessments which had issued shortly after the original notices of assessment. In relation to the 1980 year, despite the fact that the Applicant had returned a loss of $2,600 for that year, in 1981 the Commissioner issued an original assessment fixing taxable income at $6,251 with a penalty of $31.61 for late return. No explanation for the adjustment was offered. The result of the assessments before me can be summarized as follows:-

THE OBJECTIONS

  1. The Applicant objected to all amended assessments and, in doing so, in each instance objected to the amount of additional tax imposed.
  2. When the objection to the 1976 assessment was lodged it was cast only in general terms and did not point to any particular error in the Commissioner's contentions. Inter alia, it said:-

"that the statement of my assets (at cost price) and liabilities as at 1st July, 1975, prepared by your officers is incorrect."

When the objections were filed to the later amended assessments, they too were in general terms and did not point to any specific errors on the part of the Commissioner. It is to be noted that, with the exception of the 1980 year, the accuracy of the previous assessment was asserted. For the 1980 year, although income had been assessed on 20 July 1981 at $6,251, the Applicant in his objection contended:-

"that in this year I suffered a loss of $2,600": a statement reflecting what appeared in his income tax return for that year.

THE ISSUES

7. When Counsel for the Applicant opened his case, he identified the issues to be determined by reference to assets betterment calculations which had been placed in evidence as Exhibit "B". The issues so nominated were:-

(a) that the amounts identified as moneys lent to W Laboratories Pty. Limited; BB Salts Pty. Limited; W Hotel Investments Pty. Limited; and possibly, A Motel Pty. Limited were over-stated;

(b) that an item described as "unidentified asset" $60,000 should be excluded from the calculation;

(c) that an item "loan to Dianne" of $2,000 should be excluded;
  • (d) that the loss on the sale of a residence should be reduced;
  • (e) that the amount owing to PV Pty. Ltd. had been understated;
  • (f) that no provision had been made for receipt of capital by way of gifts from one of the Applicant's daughters amounting to approximately $25,000; and
  • (g) that no provision had been made for capital receipts by way of many substantial sums gifted to the Applicant by his wife during the period under review.

To those issues there is to be added the question of the penalties for incorrect returns.

THE CHOICE: REVIEW OR APPEAL

17. In due course on 22 June 1984 the Commissioner gave notice that the objections were wholly disallowed. The Applicant responded by requesting that the matters then be referred for independent review before a Taxation Board of Review. He contended that he made that choice in the knowledge that his matter would be adjudicated on by a multi-member tribunal rather than before a single Supreme Court Judge. The Commissioner did not comply with the request for reference of the Applicant until 15 May 1986. However, before any steps could be taken to provide the independent review sought by the Applicant, the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 had been passed. As a result the Taxation Boards of Review were abolished; this Tribunal had conferred on it the responsibility for determining the objections of the Applicant; and in due course responsibility for conducting the hearing and deciding the application of the Applicant fell to me.

18. In his closing address in reply, the Applicant, in a totally inoffensive way, complained that he had been unfairly treated in that regard. He thought it unfair that his case should come for determination before a single member of this Tribunal when that was not a course which he had been able to consider when making his election in 1984. In putting his matters of complaint, the Applicant was in some respects in error. Taxation Boards of Review were not constituted, as he seems to have thought, by five members; it was not the Commissioner who denied him a Board of Review hearing, except in so far as the Commissioner's delay in transmitting the request for reference would have denied any possibility of a hearing prior to the Boards of Review being divested of their responsibilities; and, contrary to one submission of the Applicant, members of this Tribunal, whether sitting alone or with others, are not in any sense representative of the Commissioner, or anything but totally independent of the Commissioner.

19. But for all that it remains true that, whereas his original choice was to proceed before an independent tribunal of three members, rather than before a Judge of a Supreme Court of one of the States, the Applicant was not provided with any opportunity to consider whether, following the abolition of the Taxation Boards of Review, he might prefer to elect a hearing before a Supreme Court Judge in preference to a hearing before this Tribunal. I observe that now any election to have such matter dealt with before a Supreme Court Judge of one of the States has been denied all applicants in that all such appeals now fall within the jurisdiction of the Federal Court of Australia, a Commonwealth Court and soon will fall within the jurisdiction of Judges of the Family Court of Australia to the extent to which the Federal Court so determines but - in relation to pending matters - only with the consent of the parties [Family Court of Australia (Additional Jurisdiction and Exercise of Powers) Act - No. 8 of 1988].

20. I note that the Applicant expressed as one basis for his concern that in his view no individual sitting alone could fairly decide questions of fact needing to be determined. I infer that in his view that limitation did not extend to the person of any judge sitting alone but, beyond noting the matter, there is little I can say about it. I must discharge ray duty as I see it. It would be nice to think that the Applicant was content that at least he had been fairly heard, even if not correctly judged, but any views he might have to the contrary cannot deter me from the course I must follow.

PREPARATION FOR THE HEARING

21. The history of the proceedings before this Tribunal is material to some of the many problems which were to arise at the hearing. For some years prior to 1984 the Applicant had retained for different purposes the services of two separate solicitors, F and H. He consulted H in relation to the 1984 assessments and, through him, Counsel was retained to draw objections to the 1984 assessments. Those objections were duly prepared and lodged with the Commissioner. They put in issue the entirety of the 1984 assessments but they did not point to any specific errors alleged to be involved in the Commissioner's calculations. Nor was it suggested on behalf of the Applicant that up to, or at, or about that time any representations had been made to the Commissioner such as have been made in the proceedings before me. In view of some of the allegations to be considered hereafter that is a matter of some significance, albeit not great.

22. On 16 September 1986 the matter was first mentioned before the Tribunal. The Tribunal was then notified by H, the solicitor to the Applicant, that the only matter in dispute was to be the matter of penalties by way of additional tax. Accordingly, an afternoon was proposed for the Hearing and a Preliminary Conference was scheduled for 25 November 1986. It was then said that quantum would be in issue. No particulars were then provided. It was then that the Commissioner advised that the betterment figures (other than for 1976) had been lost and were being recalculated (cf. post). The estimated time for hearing was increased to two days.

  1. The next development came on 7 April 1987 when H appeared to represent the Applicant. It was then directed that the Hearing should take place on 27 and 28 August 1987; that there should be mutual disclosure of all relevant documents; and that by 1 July 1987 the Applicant should identify to the Commissioner the errors in and omissions from the Commissioner's calculations which were to be contended for on behalf of the Applicant. By early August 1987 the Commissioner's solicitors were complaining that the last direction had not been complied with. By then H was overseas, as he had previously advised he would be. He was not due to return until shortly prior to the Hearing.
  2. As a result, the matter was re-scheduled for Hearing on 3, 4 and 5 November, 1987 with further Directions Hearings set for 22 September and 8 October 1987. Both Directions Hearings took place but at the latter, due to illness, H did not appear and the Applicant represented himself. In consequence, the matter was stood over until the following day to enable H to attend. The order was then confirmed as to the disclosure of documents to be relied on at the Hearing.

25. Shortly before 3 November 1987 Counsel for the Applicant was briefed and the hearing then commenced. It continued for three days, during which the evidence of the Applicant was completed. The matter was then adjourned until 3 December 1987. An adjournment to 14 December 1987 was later granted on the basis that the Applicant had been hospitalised for seven days suffering from an acute anxiety state. On 14 December 1987 a further adjournment was granted because of reported illness, despite the failure of the medical practitioner concerned to attend as arranged with Counsel for the Applicant. On 21 December 1987 a further application for adjournment was refused but, on the basis that only the evidence-in-chief of the witnesses for the Respondent would be heard; that Commonwealth Reporting Service would be requested to provide a transcript of the proceedings to the Applicant well in advance of any resumed hearing date so that he could be fully informed as to what had occurred; and that the matter would then stand adjourned following the evidence-in-chief of the Commissioner's witnesses for a prolonged period in the hope that by that date the Applicant would have fully recovered. At that point Counsel, being without satisfactory instructions, withdrew from the hearing. Next day the Applicant appeared. He was manifestly unwell and very stressed. Having learned accurately how the Tribunal was proceeding, he retired and the Tribunal proceeded to complete taking the evidence-in-chief.

The hearing was then adjourned until 16 May 1988. The request of Commonwealth Reporting Service was duly made and the transcript was in due course provided.

THE MISSING CALCULATIONS

29. There was yet a further complication in the preliminary proceedings. The Commissioner advised the Tribunal that he was unable to locate copies of the asset betterment calculations which had been prepared for the 1977-1980 years and which had issued with the 1984 assessments. In consequence the Commissioner undertook the task of attempting to reconstitute his calculations in support of the determinations of taxable income expressed by the amended assessments. Revised figures were in due course prepared and made available to the Applicant. The Applicant presented them as the second exhibit to be tendered in support of his case. I shall hereafter refer to those revised figures as "Exhibit B". In aggregate they accounted for the taxable income assessed. However, they differed in that the figure for taxable income in 1978 in Exhibit B exceeded that assessed by $27, but on the other hand, the figure in Exhibit B for taxable income to 30 June 1980 was $27 less than that assessed for 1980.

  1. Against that background it was a matter of surprise when, during the hearing, the Applicant not only produced the notices of assessment which had issued to him on different dates in 1983 and 1984, but also the two sets of asset betterment calculations which had attended the assessments which had issued on those separate dates. They were all received as "Exhibit R". As a result, the Tribunal now had two sets of calculations Exhibit B and Exhibit R - with Exhibit B being constituted by a single set of documents embracing all years and Exhibit R by the original two sets of documents - each in two pages relating to, on the one hand, the year of income ended 30 June 1976 and, on the other, to the years of income ended 30 June 1977 to 1980.
  2. Not only did the Applicant produce Exhibit R at the hearing but, in fact, he also produced a further document in the form of a draft set of asset betterment calculations which had been prepared in the course of the investigation and which had come into his possession during that period. No submissions were made as to it and I do not propose to take its terms into account in any analysis of the calculations.

COUNSEL

32. However, there were yet further complications. At a conference held shortly prior to the then appointed hearing date, mention was made of the identity of Counsel said to have been retained for the Applicant for the hearing. He was the same person who had drawn the objections. I suggested that the status of the brief ought be promptly checked. My understanding was that Counsel so named was ill and would be unlikely to be available for any hearing on the appointed date. I mention the matter only because that circumstance suggests that, even as late as that date, little had been done to prepare effectively for a hearing. I make no judgments as to who might have been responsible for that state of affairs but, one result was that at a very late stage before the hearing commenced another member of the Bar, Counsel conversant with the intricacies of assets betterment calculations, was briefed to represent the Applicant.

33. When the hearing commenced, Counsel set about the discharge of his duties in a most responsible and professional way. But obviously he was working under considerable difficulties, even to the point where I was advised that H, his instructing solicitor, had failed to appear at conferences scheduled throughout the two days of the weekend immediately prior to the hearing.

THE HEARING

  1. Finally the hearing commenced on 3 November 1987. Thereafter the matter proceeded as detailed earlier until on 22 December 1987 the matter was adjourned until 16 May 1988.
  2. When the hearing resumed after a break of nearly five months, the Applicant appeared for himself. He had discharged his solicitor and Counsel. In the two and one-half days of hearing which remained that proved to be singularly unfortunate because the Applicant consistently demonstrated his inability to understand what was in issue; the significance of the evidence; the methodology of assets betterment calculations; and the significance of the varying figures which had been presented. I now proceed to mention some of those difficulties.

BURDEN OF PROOF

36. The Applicant took the view that what was in issue was not the soundness of the assessments of taxable income but rather the soundness of the calculations of the Commissioner which led the Commissioner to fix upon the amounts of taxable income assessed. Further, he held the view that it was the Commissioner's obligation to prove the assessments to be correct by proving his calculations in support to be correct. He was, of course, in error. The Act provides, by s.190 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") that the onus of proving that an assessment is excessive lies upon the taxpayer. That that is so is for a very good reason. Ordinarily, the taxpayer is in a far better position to establish what his taxable income was than the Commissioner. He was also wrong on a second point a conclusion which follows readily enough from the first proposition. It will not assist a taxpayer to demonstrate that there is an error in the Commissioner's figures unless the error establishes that, in consequence, the assessment of taxable income is excessive. [F.C.T. v. Trautwein (1936) 56 C. L. R. 633.

THE LOST RECORDS

37. Closely related to the burden of proof point is the obvious concern of the Applicant about "lost documents". There were two aspects to his concern: the first was that it was said that his records had become lost to him; the second was a contention that it was the Commissioner who had failed to return them to him. As to the contention that records were lost by the Commissioner, I am not persuaded that any records alleged to have been lost and which ever came into the hands of the Commissioner were not returned to D by 0.

SECONDARY AND OTHER EVIDENCE

38. The loss of records, assuming that there was any, may have been unfortunate but, when the best evidence (the allegedly lost records) is not available, then it becomes the responsibility of the Tribunal to determine the matter according to the next best evidence available. To the extent to which originals relating to the relevant period had ever been under the control of the Commissioner, that evidence was to hand. It was in the form of analyses of the original records prepared by 0. Whenever challenged, he could produce the appropriate record. It is no answer to the Commissioner's assessments to contend, as the Applicant repeatedly did, that without the original records he could not verify the accuracy of the calculations made by 0.

WHAT WAS IN ISSUE?

39. I have recounted earlier how the matter moved through stages from it being said that only penalty was in issue, through to the point at which, shortly prior to hearing, the solicitor to the Applicant nominated a particular set of issues. Then, upon the commencement of the hearing in May 1988, Counsel for the Applicant identified with reasonable specificity a band of issues, one of the most substantial of which was later abandoned. Then, upon the resumption of the hearing, the Applicant contended that "everything" was in issue, and always had been. At that stage, particularly having regard to all that had been done to try to ensure a hearing on terms fair to all concerned, the Commissioner representing the community not less than the Applicant, I refused to allow the whole case to be re-opened. I ruled that I would only treat as matters in issue before me the matters identified by Counsel for the Applicant (other than the issue which had been abandoned). That ruling proved ineffectual to prevent many references by the Applicant to other issues that might have been. Having regard to the health of the Applicant and the obvious stress he was suffering I allowed him to be heard as to his views.

The reference which attracted the attention of the Applicant was a reference to moneys expended by way of interest. The error did not exist. There was no discrepancy.

42. The second example was when the Applicant turned his attention to a set of figures added by way of sub-total in Exhibit "R" for the 1979 year. The figures appearing on the document included one of $1,719. Together the figures only accounted for a sub-total of $113,791 although a sub-total of $119,991 appeared. But the seemingly obvious conclusion drawn by the Applicant happened to be in error. What caused the problem was not a mistake in addition as the applicant thought, but a mistake in typing. The figure in the set which appeared as "private expenditure by cheque $1,719" should have been typed as "private expenditure by cheque $7,919".

43. A third problem was in the failure to understand that the figures appearing in the betterment are a function of moneys expended and not of value. Where the Commissioner had recorded an investment of, say $100,000 in a company, and preserved that figure from the date of its expenditure until the close of the betterment, it is not of any help to any applicant to merely contend that the asset had lost its value - even assuming that to be true, and to have been proved to be true. Similarly, he could not understand that, if an item appeared as an asset (or liability) throughout the betterment at a constant figure, merely to establish that the asset (or liability as the case may be) had never existed, did not help him. Such errors have no effect upon the computation of taxable income. Then again, similar difficulties arose in relation to assets which, for want of any other information 0 had treated as being the property of the Applicant. Reference to one parcel of real estate referred to in the calculations provides an example. 0 established that funds had flowed from the Applicant and been expended, seemingly in the purchase of that property. 0 identified the asset so generated by the identified expenditure as the named real estate. The Applicant contended that he was incorrect and that the asset really belonged to one of his daughters. It may be that it did. I find it unnecessary to decide the point. But if any amendment were to be made to the calculations to reflect such a finding, it would be to change the description of the item by deleting "asset real estate" and substituting either "asset - loan" or "non-deductible expenditure - gift". The change would not effect the calculation of taxable income.

44. A different example appeared in relation to another house property which was bought and later sold. 0 brought to account as the purchase price of that property a sum of money which was the total of moneys he could identify as expended by the Applicant and which seemed to be traced into that particular purchase. He may have been wrong. He probably was. But, if it be true that some of the money he treated as the purchase price of that property had in fact been expended for some other purpose, that additional expenditure still had to be accounted for. Either it generated a further asset - being an asset not otherwise brought to account - or it was expended by way of undeductible expenditure - for example, by way of gift or in living expenses not brought to account.

ANSWERS TO THE BETTERMENT?

45. In his closing address, the Applicant contended that it had been established that the net worth of he and his wife exceeded $1,000,000 in 1971. I accept that he had persuaded the Commissioner to that effect in relation to another matter. Therefore, he contended, it was at the least highly improbable that his net assets had fallen to be so low as the Commissioner contended at the opening of the betterment ($473,309 as the "cost" - not value - of all assets less liabilities). I find nothing improbable about such a diminution in wealth. I would have thought that history provides ample evidence that fortunes can be more swiftly lost than they have been made. But, for all that, it may have been so, but if it was so it needed to be established by evidence. Had the Applicant presented a betterment calculation tracing his financial fortune from 1971 through to 1980, he might have demonstrated that his explanation was more probably correct than that of the Commissioner. However, no such explanation was forthcoming.

46. Then, in the closing minutes of a prolonged hearing, the Applicant referred to a racehorse which had been brought into account by 0 in the betterment as an asset. To that point it had never been suggested that the horse or its activities could ever provide an answer to the allegations of the Commissioner. So it was that it was somewhat surprising to hear the Applicant contend that the particular horse had generated "winnings" in excess of $500,000 of which he had received half. It was not an issue before me, and accordingly I make no finding about it. I would merely observe that it by no means automatically follows that, if he had derived such moneys from that source, that the moneys would not have constituted assessable income. I simply have no evidence which would enable me to say one way or the other.

MISSING WITNESSES

47. Counsel for the Commissioner pointed to the circumstance that the Applicant had not called as witnesses persons said to have had a capacity to corroborate his evidence and yet who were not said to be unavailable. The Applicant's response was to say that he had been unable to call two male employees because they were dead. Until then it had never been suggested that either of them could give evidence in relation to any matter in issue. The failure to call his lady bookkeeper was explained: she had moved out of the State and, it was said, that she could not be located. But the substance of the criticism remained unanswered. It was made particularly in relation to a daughter of whom it was said that she was the donor to him of sums in total of the order of $25,000. As to her, at no time was any explanation for her absence proffered.

48. However, some further comments are appropriate in relation to the wife of the Applicant. The Applicant claimed that there were prolonged periods in which substantial moneys were held in her name but wholly under his control. He did not call her as a witness. He attributed that to the circumstance that she is in extremely poor health, such that, had she had to appear in the witness box, she might have suffered a fatal heart attack. He said that she had attended upon Counsel in his chambers and that Counsel had advised him that there was no relevant evidence that she could give. However, he also claimed that he had been informed by his solicitor that it was "common practice" for this Tribunal to attend upon such a witness at his or her home in order to spare them the task of physically attending before the Tribunal. It was not suggested that attendance before the Tribunal was in any way impossible or impractical. Nor was the contention that attendance before the Tribunal might have been a life-threatening exercise supported by any medical evidence.

49. At a late stage in the hearing application was made that I should adjourn the hearing to reconvene at her home. At the time it was pointed out that the course proposed is not so common a practice before the Tribunal that it should be regarded as one to be followed as a matter of course. On the other hand, for my part I would certainly say it is a course I would implement in appropriate cases. Application was made that that course should be followed in this case. However, the application was refused, not because it was thought in any way inappropriate for the Tribunal to attend upon witnesses incapable of attending before the Tribunal, but because - and only because - it was the assertion of the Applicant that the lady knew nothing, and could say nothing, relevant to any of the matters in issue.

THE WIFE'S MONEYS

50. It was suggested that, among the records lost by the Commissioner, were pass-books in the name of the wife which would have established at the very least a substantial capacity on her part to have made gifts to the Applicant. I am satisfied that no such bank accounts were ever sighted by 0. Accordingly, I am satisfied that there is no basis for any contention that those passbooks were lost by officers of the Commissioner and that thereby actions of the Commissioner have denied the Applicant the opportunity to disprove the case against him.

51. Further, I am not persuaded, as a matter of probability, either that such sums existed or that such gifts were made. In reaching that conclusion I take into account the circumstance that, as the evidence stands before me, no such assertion of substantial gifts on the part of the wife was ever made at any time until shortly prior to this hearing and, in particular, that it was not made at the time of the interview of 30 March 1982. Furthermore, I note that the Applicant has not presented any income tax returns and assessments such as might have been expected to exist, even if no other source of income had existed for the wife than interest on the moneys allegedly held, with the level of such interest income being of such magnitude as to suggest a capacity for making gifts in substantial amounts.

THE INCONSEQUENTIAL ERROR

52. There was another point in the calculations to which A attributed undue significance. By the time of hearing 0 had left the service of the Commissioner. When his figures, recorded in the primary records composed during the investigation, were re-worked, a mathematical error was discovered. The cost of an opening asset taken into account in the calculations at $82,051 was overstated by $2,000. The asset was held until after 30 June 1977. As the opening error was not corrected in the original calculations, the fact was that, so long as the error remained, it had the effect of causing income as calculated to be understated by $2,000. In short, it had no effect on taxable income for the years of income ended 30 June 1976 or 1977. Nor would it have had any effect upon the Commissioner's calculations at all had the asset been held and brought to account at 30 June 1980 at the figure at which it had been incorrectly introduced. But that did not happen. In the 1978 year of income, the property was sold at a loss. Since the loss was calculated by 0 by reference to an overstated cost price, there were self-cancelling adjustments to be made in the calculations for the 1978 year. The loss on sale brought to account to the detriment of the taxpayer had been overstated by $2,000 but the value of assets introduced at 1 July 1977 had been overstated by $2,000. CONCLUSIONS

53. I proceed now to express my conclusions in relation to each of the items in issue as identified in pa. 7:-

(a) Loan Accounts: no evidence has been presented which has persuaded me that there is any error in either the Commissioner's workings or methodology;

(b) Unidentified Assets during the hearing Counsel abandoned this $60,000 item without having presented any evidence suggestive of error on the part of the Commissioner;

(c) Loan (Dianne): the Applicant contended that the loan of $2,000 he referred to was repaid in cash. Whether or not it was repaid it does not have any bearing on the calculation of taxable income. If the loan was not repaid, the calculations are quite unaffected. If it was repaid, the cash whereby repayment was effected has not to this point been brought to account in the Commissioner's calculations. In consequence, if the sum of $2,000 is to be brought to account on the basis that the indebtedness of Dianne no longer exists, it is also necessary to bring $2,000 to account as cash received and not brought to account through any of the other assets brought to account in the present calculations. That might be either by way of bringing to account a further asset hitherto unknown or by increasing private expenditure;

(d) Loss on Resale of Residence: the evidence does not establish any error other than that relating to the $2,000 previously considered in pa. 52. As that error had no effect on the calculations and as the evidence does not support any finding that there was any other error, no case for any adjustment in this item has been made out;
  • (e) The Debt Under-stated: no evidence has been placed before me which persuades me that it was so under-stated;
    • (f) Gifts from Daughter: not only was the daughter not called as a witness nor her absence explained, but the evidence of the Applicant is so vague as to payments, and the likelihood of such gifts being made is so improbable, that I am not persuaded that there were any such gifts.


(g) Gifts from Wife: I have considered this issue earlier (pa. 50-51) and I now consider that there is nothing to be usefully added. I am not persuaded that, in the relevant period, there were any gifts or other transfers of money made in such circumstances as would warrant findings that any of the assessments are excessive.

(h) Penalties by way of Additional Tax: the imposition of additional tax for incorrect returns constitutes a substantial part of the burden of the assessments. Even so, my decision in relation to that aspect of the reference can be shortly stated. Applying the principles stated in Case U36 (87 A.T.C. 266) I find no reason to think that the penalties imposed by the Commissioner in the circumstances of this case were excessive.

THE ORDER

54. When the case was opened, it was accepted on both sides that the issues to be determined were to be tested by reference to the calculations set forth in Exhibit "B"; and that in consequence it would be necessary to reduce the 1980 assessment of taxable income by $27, thereby leaving open the possibility of increasing the 1978 assessment by $27. Although subsequently the Applicant produced the Commissioner's original calculations (Exhibit "R") I do not consider that that circumstance should occasion a change in the implementation of those initial expectations. Accordingly, I will direct a reduction in taxable income for the year of income ended 30 June 1980 by $27. However, as to the 1978 year of income, I do not consider this to be an appropriate case in which to exercise the power which the Tribunal has to increase an assessment.

55. Accordingly, the order of the Tribunal will be that the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection relating to the year of income ended 30 June 1980 shall be varied so that taxable income will be reduced to $11,421 and additional tax reduced to $1,138; but in all other respects the assessments of the Commissioner and his determinations upon the objections under review shall be affirmed.
