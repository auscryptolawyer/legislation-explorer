Re Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia v Bowron Australia Pty Ltd [1992] FCA 577; (1992) 92 Atc 4781 (30 November 1992)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

And: BOWRON AUSTRALIA PTY LTD

No. V G268 of 1991

FED No. 892

Number of pages - 16

Income Tax

(1992) 92 ATC 4781

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Northrop(1), Jenkinson(2) and Ryan(3) JJ.

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s51(1) - contract to supply natural gas - whether facilities charge deductible

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s51(1)

HEARING

MELBOURNE

30:11:1992

Counsel for the Appellant: Dr P. Buchanan QC and Mr E. Power

Solicitors for the Appellant: Australian Government Solicitor

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr B.J. Shaw QC and Mr T.P. Murphy

Solicitors for the Respondent: Blake Dawson Waldron

ORDER

THE COURT ORDERS THAT:

1. The appeal be dismissed.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the The Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

NORTHROP J. I agree with the order proposed by Jenkinson J and concur in his reasons for so ordering.

JENKINSON J. Appeal from a judgment of a single Judge of the Court upon an appeal from a decision of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal.

2. The Tribunal had for review a decision of the appellant Commissioner wholly to disallow the respondent's objection against an income tax assessment in respect of the respondent's substituted year of income ended 1 October 1985. The respondent claimed to deduct, pursuant to s.51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, all the expense that it had incurred in that year in respect of the supply of natural gas to it by the Gas and Fuel Corporation of Victoria. The gas was required to fuel boilers and dryers which the respondent employed in its tanning business. Shortly before the respondent commenced to carry on that business in 1982 the Corporation had laid a gas main to the respondent's premises and had there installed a meter and regulators. The Tribunal found, and the parties have not challenged the finding, that the contract (not wholly in writing) in performance of which the Corporation supplied gas to the respondent at those premises until 1984 provided for charges expressed thus:

"Supply Charge: $42.50

Commodity Charge:

First 35 000 MJ @ 0.646c/MJ

Next 265 000 MJ @ 0.493c/MJ

Over 300 000 MJ @ 0.274c/MJ

Minimum Bill: $42.50"

3. The charges were computed, and were payable, monthly. The Tribunal made the following further unchallenged findings:

"(a) the monthly 'supply charge' contained in Tariff 14/22 was

designed to recoup to the Corporation the average cost of

equipment and installation necessary to supply gas under that

tariff to consumers over and above the capital cost to the

Corporation of having the necessary infrastructure in place to be

able to offer supply under that tariff to consumers;

(b) that equipment included the mains, meter and regulators; and (c)
the 'supply charge' was not payable if a special tariff (referred

to as 'Tariff 95') was entered into."

("Tariff 14/22" was an expression signifying the terms, of the first contract, which specified the charges payable by the respondent to the Corporation.) On 1 October 1984 the respondent and the Corporation made a written contract for the supply of gas, in lieu of the first contract. This written contract gives expression to what in the Tribunal's statement of its findings is called "Tariff 95". Clause 1 of the written contract provides:

"1. In consideration of the payments to be made to the Seller by the

Buyer as hereinafter provided and subject to the provisions of

this agreement, the Seller agrees to have available, sell and

deliver Gas to the Buyer throughout the Supply Period for the

operation of the Buyer's fuel-using plant and equipment installed

at the Buyer's premises."

4. The expression "the Supply Period" is defined to mean the period of one year ending on 1 September 1985 and a further period from that date until termination by written notice by either party to the other. Clause 3 of the contract dated 1 October 1984 commences thus:

"Subject to sub-clauses (b), (c), (d) and (e) of this clause the price

of Gas payable by the Buyer in each Billing Period of each year of the

Supply Period shall be a sum made up of:"

5. The rest of Clause 3 prescribes the circumstances by reference to which "the price of Gas" is determined. Clause 4 provides:

"FACILITIES CHARGE

(a) In consideration of the Seller having performed all such works as

were necessary to enable Gas to be supplied to the Buyer through

the Seller's Gas distribution system, the Buyer shall pay to the

Seller for the performance of the said work (excluding the cost

of excess service, concrete base, meter by-pass and security

enclosure for the Meter/Regulator installation) a sum of

$10,418.00.

(b) The said sum shall be payable on a terms basis by 36 consecutive

monthly instalments of $382.00, payable concurrently with the

charge for Gas commencing on the first Billing Period of the

Supply Period. The Seller shall, without prejudice to the

foregoing, be entitled to make demand for payment concurrently

with the rendering by it of its periodic accounts for the supply

of Gas (which may at its discretion be monthly, bi-monthly or at

such other intervals as it may determine).

(c) TITLE TO EQUIPMENT

Nothing herein contained or implied shall operate to vest

property or any other rights in any of the alterations,

extensions or improvements to the Seller's Gas distribution

system in the Buyer notwithstanding that such equipment may be

installed on the Buyer's premises and title to all mains, service

pipes, meters, other apparatus or equipment to the outlet of the

Seller's metering and regulating equipment shall at all times

remain in the Seller.

(d) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Agreement the whole of

the said sum at the time remaining unpaid at the time this

Agreement is terminated shall at the option of the Seller become

immediately due and payable upon demand for the same being made

by the Seller."

6. Notwithstanding the provision contained in the first sentence of Clause 4(b), the respondent paid, and the Corporation accepted payment of, the whole sum of $10,418 shortly after the contract was made. (A letter from the Corporation to the respondent before the contract was made had offered the alternative, single payment.) Another person shared the premises with the respondent and consumed some of the gas delivered to the premises through the main. That person paid the respondent $2,256 in respect of the $10.418 paid by the respondent to the Corporation, and the respondent claimed to deduct $8,162 from its assessable income of the year of income ended 1 October 1985. The claim having been wholly disallowed in assessment, the Tribunal allowed deduction of part of the $8,162. The Tribunal found that in determining the amount to be specified in Clause 4(a) the Corporation had aggregated several components. Gas and Fuel Corporation of Victoria had by its "Chief Solicitor" responded in writing to written questions directed to it by the appellant Commissioner, in exercise, it was represented, of power conferred on the Commissioner by s.264 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. Those two documents were put in evidence before the Tribunal and oral evidence was adduced from a witness named Telkis, who described his occupation as "Principal tariffs analyst" employed by the Corporation. Upon that evidence the Tribunal made the following findings:

"8. The following breakdown of the amount of $10,418.00 was contained

in the Corporation's response to one of the respondent's requisitions of

18 February 1991 ('the requisition answers'):

Main $ 1,090 (Depreciated Value)

Meter $ 1,425 (Depreciated Value)

Regulators $ 3,309 (Depreciated Value)

M.D.I. $ 1.156

$ 6,980

Tax $ 3,438*

Total $10,418

*The Tax referred to was levied by the State of Victoria on the revenue

of the Corporation pursuant to the Public Authorities (Contributions)

Act 1966.

9. Although cl 4(a) of the agreement couches the 'facilities charge'

in terms of it being payment for the Corporation's performance of all

works necessary to enable gas to be supplied through the Corporation's

distribution network (excluding certain specific items), the answer to

Question 1 of the respondent's requisitions, as well as the oral

evidence of Mr Telkis, clearly establish that, in fact, the $10,418.00

represented the full recovery of the cost of equipment which could be

identified as being specifically required to deliver gas to the

applicant - which included equipment installed at the time of entering

the Gas Supply Agreement and equipment installed within the previous ten

years with the later (sic) being included at its depreciated value -

together with recovery of 'public authorities contribution' - a tax

levied by the State of Victoria on the revenue of the Corporation

pursuant to the Public Authorities (Contributions) Act 1966 (Vic.)."

7. The Tribunal concluded that the payment of $10,418 was, to the extent of the components thereof other than the $3,438, "of a capital .... nature", and so not deductible under s.51(1). The $3,438 component the Tribunal held to be deductible under that sub-section and accordingly the Tribunal decided that the respondent's taxable income should be reduced by the appropriate proportionate part of the $8,162 claimed deduction, that is by $2,693. The single Judge by whom the Commissioner's appeal to this Court was heard discerned no error of law in that decision and dismissed the appeal.

8. There is no appeal by the respondent. But Mr Shaw QC, who appeared with Mr Murphy for the respondent, submitted that no enduring benefit had been sought or gained by the expenditure of the $10,418. The respondent obtained no property in, or other right in respect of, any of the structures with which Clause 4 of the contract is concerned. The contract was terminable by either party on three months' notice given to the other at any time on or after 1 June 1985, that is at the expiration of eight months from the date on which the contract was made. Three of the four structures had been installed in 1981 : only the maximum demand indicator ("M.D.I.") was installed for the purposes of the 1984 contract. The Tribunal made the findings which it expressed thus:

"As a result of discussions between representatives of the Corporation

and the applicant, it became apparent from projections that an overall

cheaper cost of gas might be achieved by the applicant by going onto

'Tariff 95'. To adopt that tariff, it was necessary for the applicant

to enter into a written agreement ('the agreement') with the

Corporation, which it did on 1 October 1984. The only material addition

required in the way of extra equipment was the installation of a

'maximum demand indicator' on the applicant's premises. However, no

increased supply capacity was provided as a result of the agreement."

And, Mr Shaw submitted, the overall cheaper cost of gas was the advantage sought by the respondent in making the contract. The respondent neither sought nor gained any lasting advantage, he submitted.

9. In its consideration of the question whether the expenditure was of a capital nature the Tribunal had regard to subsequent events, which it narrated in its reasons for decision thus:

"10. As evidenced by a letter dated 1 June 1985 from the Corporation

to the Secretary of the applicant, the Corporation gave notice of their

intention to terminate the agreement on 1 September 1985 in accordance

with cl 6 of the second schedule of the agreement.

11. On 11 October 1985, the applicant entered in a new agreement

('the second agreement') with the Corporation (effective from 1

September 1985) on terms virtually similar in effect to those in the

first agreement. For the purposes of this application, the only

relevant differences between the two agreements (other than increases in

various charges relating to commodity usage and demand) was the change

of the minimum term of the agreement from one year to three years, and

the deletion of a facilities charge in the second agreement.

12. At the expiry of the second agreement on 1 September 1988, a

third agreement between the applicant and the Corporation took effect,

in substantially similar terms to that of the second agreement (again

apart from increases in various charges relating to commodity usage and

demand). Again the contract was for three years (thus still on foot),

and again did not include a facilities charge. The reason for the lack

of a facilities charge in the second and third agreements was explained

by the requisition answers by reason of the fact that 'the costs of

identified equipment required to deliver gas to (the applicant) were

fully recovered in Agreement No. 1088 (the first agreement)'. The

Corporation further advised in those requisition answers that although

'facilities charges were abandoned in 1989 in respect of all new

contracts entered into .... no facilities or similar charge is included

in the consumption (commodity) charge for such consumers'."

10. The Tribunal's general conclusion about the expenditure is expressed thus:

"17. Looking at the circumstances which followed the termination of

the one year agreement under which the fee was payable (a legitimate aid

to navigation; cf F.C. of T. v Total Holdings (Australia) Pty Ltd 79 ATC

4279), that is, the immediate entering into of two back-to-back

contracts with the Corporation totalling a further six years - the last

of which is still on foot - in terms of very similar effect (with the

notable lack of a 'facilities charge' in either of the subsequent

agreements), I am satisfied that the components of the 'facilities

charge' relating to the recoupment of the cost of the mains, meter,

regulators and maximum demand indicator ('the recoupment components')

are capital in nature. To use the terminology of the various

characterisation 'tests', the recoupment components of the payment,

viewed from a practical and not a juristic analysis, 'procured a right

of a permanent character', 'created a lasting advantage', had an

'enduring benefit' and were 'once and for all'."

11. The expenditure could not be regarded, at the time the contract was made, as "calculated to effect from a practical and business point of view" the elimination from any future agreement, between the Corporation and the respondent for the supply of gas at the premises, of any charge designed to recoup the Corporation any of the cost to it of the work specified in Clause 4 of the contract. The findings of the Tribunal about the "Supply Charge" included in Tariff 14/22, which I have quoted above, together with another finding by the Tribunal establish that, if after the contract had been terminated in 1985 the Corporation and the respondent had agreed upon supply under the terms of that Tariff, the Supply Charge would have been payable by the respondent and thereby the respondent would have been subjected again to a charge designed to recoup the Corporation part of the cost to it of the work specified in Clause 4 of the contract. That other finding was expressed in the following passage in the Tribunal's reasons for its decision:

"18. It was submitted on behalf of the applicant - properly so in my

view - that cases such as W Nevill and Co Ltd v F.C. of T. (1937) 56 CLR
290

and Metals Exploration Ltd v F.C. of T. 86 ATC 4505 are authority for the

proposition that the mere fact that a payment is made in a lump sum does

not of itself alter the character of the series of payments which the

lump sum seeks to commute. However that principle can afford the

applicant little comfort with respect to the recoupment components of

the 'facilities charge'. Had it paid the 'facilities charge' by 36

monthly instalments (which had an inbuilt interest component), the

character of the recoupment components would still have been of a

capital nature.

19. It was sought to use the same argument on the basis that the

recoupment components of the 'facilities charge' were a commutation of

the monthly 'supply charge' payable under the general industrial tariff,

and therefore of an income or revenue nature. Although it is true that

the monthly 'supply charge' was not payable once the applicant had

entered into the Tariff 95 agreements, I do not consider that the

evidence establishes that the 'facilities charge' was in commutation of

that monthly charge. This is borne out by the fact that, were either

the applicant or Corporation to terminate the current 'Tariff 95'

agreement, and the applicant to resume gas consumption under the general

industrial tariff, it would again have to pay a monthly 'supply charge'.

This leads me to conclude that it is unnecessary to characterise that

'supply charge'. However, I note that the Board of Review No 1 in 9

CTBR (NS) Case 103 considered that a flat rate service charge payable by

all consumers of a particular electricity tariff at the same time as

charges for the quantity of electricity consumed was clearly an outgoing

on revenue account."

The incurring of expenditure for a consideration expressed as Clause 4(a) is expressed no doubt made it improbable that the Corporation and the respondent would again bargain, or contract (except in terms of a general tariff such as Tariff 14/22), for the supply of gas to those premises, in terms which identified as a component of the charge any recoupment of the cost of any of the works identified in Clause 4(a). And so far they have not contracted in any such terms, as the Tribunal pointed out. But there was no evidence to suggest that the elimination of that bargaining counter would be productive of any economic advantage to the respondent, or that any officer or servant of the respondent thought that it would. It is notorious that in 1984 in Bendigo, where the respondent's premises were situated, the Corporation monopolised the vending of natural gas, and that the only constraints on the price for that commodity were the political policy of the Parliament and the Executive Government of the State of Victoria and the prices of other fuels. The only advantage, from a practical and business point of view, which the expenditure gained for the respondent was, according to the contract, a supply of the gas it expected to need until 1 September 1985 for a price, including that expenditure, lower than the price of that supply on any other tariff. A minute of the respondent's directors' resolution authorising execution of the contract dated 1 October 1984 describes the contract as "an agreement for a high volume gas usage for the next three years for a once only payment of a facility charge of $10,418 and a reduction of tariff rate from $0.0032c. per megajoule to $0.002324c. per megajoule". If there was, dehors the written contract, some agreement or understanding between the respondent and the Corporation that Tariff 95 would be available for 3 years and would continue to afford a price advantage over Tariff 14/22 - as the Corporation's letter to the respondent before the making of the contract suggests that there was - that would not in my opinion be so enduring an advantage as to enable the expenditure to be characterised as of a capital nature. There was no evidence that anything of the kind which the Tribunal described in paragraph 17 of its reasons for decision (quoted above) as "two back-to-back contracts with the Corporation totalling a further six years" had been promised or otherwise foreshadowed by the Corporation or expected by the respondent before the contract dated 1 October 1984 was made. In those circumstances there was in my opinion no warrant for attributing to those subsequent events significance in determining whether the expenditure was of a capital nature.

12. $3,438 is thirty three hundredths of $10,418. That was the fraction of revenue payable under the Public Authorities (Contributions) Act 1966. The Corporation chose to include that sum in the amount to be specified in Clause 4(a) of the contract. The other components of that amount not being in my opinion of a capital nature, there is no reason to give any different characterisation to the $3,438. That it matched the amount the Corporation expected to have to pay under the Public Authorities (Contributions) Act 1966 in respect of its receipt of the $10,418 and that such a payment would be regarded as on revenue, not capital, account in the Corporation's accounting are not in my opinion considerations in favour of regarding the $3,438 as deductible under s.51(1), which requires consideration of the business entity, structure, or organization set up or established by the payer for the earning of profit and the process by which the payer operates to obtain regular returns by means of regular outlay.

13. There being no appeal by the respondent to this Court, the appropriate order is in my opinion that the appeal be dismissed. Mr Shaw submitted that the wide terms of s.44(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 - "The Federal Court of Australia shall hear and determine the appeal and may make such order as it thinks appropriate by reason of the decision" - authorised an order setting aside the Tribunal's decision and directing that there be substituted a decision that the respondent's taxable income be reduced by the whole amount of the claimed deduction of $8,162. Assuming, but without deciding, that the submission is correct, yet in my opinion appropriateness, in the sense intended by s.44(2), is not to be determined solely by reference to what the Court decides should have been considered the amount of the respondent's taxable income. The conduct of the parties is to be considered in determining what is appropriate. The respondent neither cross-appealed pursuant to O.53 R.13 when the appellant appealed against the Tribunal's decision nor cross-appealed pursuant to O.52 R.22 when the appellant appealed against the judgment of the single Judge. Those decisions by a respondent represented by very experienced solicitors and counsel make it inappropriate, in my opinion, that any order but the dismissal of the appeal be made.

14. Counsel indicated that no order concerning costs would be sought, the parties having made an agreement concerning costs.

RYAN J. I have had the advantage of reading in draft the reasons for judgment of Jenkinson J. I agree with those reasons and with the orders proposed by his Honour and have nothing to add.
