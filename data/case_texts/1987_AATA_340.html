Last Updated: 29 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - whether carrying on business - fisherman

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss.

Martin v FC of T [1953] HCA 100; (1952) 90 CLR 470

Case D54, 72 ATC 333

14 CTBR Case 75

Tweddle v FC of T [1942] HCA 40; (1942) 2 AITR 360

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No AT86/666

AAT Decision No 3796

Deputy President RK Todd

25 September 1987

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. AT86/666

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Deputy President RK Todd

Date : 25 September 1987

Place : Canberra

Decision : The tribunal sets aside the objection decision under review and in substitution therefor decides that the sum of $131 that was returned as assessable income be treated as assessable income and that the sum of $6,570 claimed as an allowable deduction be allowed as such.

(sgd.) RK Todd

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. AT86/666

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

25 September 1987 Deputy President RK Todd

  1. This is the review of an objection decision- the effect whereof was to decide to exclude from assessable income of the applicant in the year ended 30 June 1985 an amount returned by the applicant as such, and to disallow, in respect of that year, certain deductions that were claimed as having been incurred in carrying on a business from which that income was said to have been derived.
  2. The applicant has been a fisherman for about forty years. For about thirty-two years, to June 1983, he owned a vessel called the "M", which he used to catch salmon, mullet and tuna. In the year with which we are concerned there was no question of him fishing for tuna, and indeed he had sold the "M" in June 1983. With the "M", and with the help of what he called a net boat, he had for a very long time been involved in "beach seining". This is a time honoured method of catching fish, in this case salmon and mullet, from the beach. A net boat is used to carry the net out from the shore and back again as required, and the fish are caught by hauling in the net to the shore. It is, on the evidence, a mode of fishing which is often carried out' by several fishermen if the school which has been identified is too large to be caught and handled by a single fisherman and is in fact enough to support several.
  3. The applicant said that it was necessary, for the purpose of finding where to set the net, to sight schools of salmon or mullet, and that for that purpose it was always necessary to spend a very great deal of time driving to various places along the coast to attempt to make such sightings. It might, the applicant said, be necessary to spend many days in such attempts. When a sighting was made, the vessel and equipment would be taken to where the fish were, since the fish would often remain in the one place for some days.
  4. In 1983 the applicant was aged about 60, and he said that he had been finding the maintenance of the "M", which was a forty foot boat, too much trouble on his own. A trusted deck-hand who had been with him for many years had died, and although without the "M" the applicant could not go to all the places to which he could have gone in the "M", he decided that he could sell the "M" and make a living from a sixteen foot net boat. This particular net boat had been acquired by him in about September 1982 that is to say before the disposal of the "M".
  5. In the year ended 30 June 1984 the applicant appears to have devoted most of his time to working on his son's vessel, the "O", helping his son to develop expertise in fishing. Indeed the applicant's only income from fishing in 1984 appears to have been from a share in the son's operations of the "0". The applicant obviously had a wealth of experience with which to assist his son, and around this time he made two trips along the Great Australian Bight as far as Esperance.
  6. In the year ended 30 June 1985 the applicant certainly had a very unsuccessful year. His only income from fishing was the sum of $131, which was derived from one small catch only. Against this the expenditure in relation to the business, which he claimed to have been continuing to carry on, was $6,570, producing a net loss of $6,439. Having regard to this result, and bearing in mind the apparent pause in the applicant's fishing activity in the previous year, the Commissioner viewed the applicant's activities in the 1985 year as either involving no more than a hobby or pastime on the part of a man previously engaged in carrying on a business but now approaching, if not reaching, retirement, or as constituting the working up of a business which had been formally carried on but which had ceased to exist.
  7. In the year ended 30 June 1986 the business improved to the extent that the applicant's income from the catch of fish had increased to $5,195 against expenditure of $7,804, producing a net loss of $2,609. In respect of that year the Commissioner accepted that the applicant was carrying on a business, treated the amount received as assessable income, and allowed deductions of the expenditure claimed to have been incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of producing such income.
  8. Primarily, the Commissioner submits that the amount of $131 returned in the 1985 year is not assessable income and that no expenses are deductible against that sum. Alternatively it-was submitted that if that amount is to be seen as constituting assessable income, some apportionment is necessary, and the expenditure incurred should be allowed by way of deduction only to the extent of the income received. Ms Lanigan, who appeared for the Commissioner, and who in a clearly put submission said everything that could be said in support of the objection decision, reminded the Tribunal that the question whether a business is being carried on is one of fact, involving both subjective and objective considerations and depending much on the "large or general impression gained" (see Webb J. in Martin v FC of T [1953] HCA 100; (1952) 90 CLR 470 at 474). In accordance with this approach I consider that the question comes down to this:

Are the reduced activities of the applicant in 1984-1985 to be seen as reflecting a space between the cessation of an undoubted old business and the commencement of a new, or rather as reflecting a temporary downturn in the continued carrying on of the old business?

I am of the opinion, in all the circumstances, that it should be seen as the latter. The applicant said, and I accept, that when he disposed of the "M" he intended "wholly and solely to go on with the net boat". The applicant had not in the 1985 year changed his method of fishing, but had rather continued to search for fish in the manner described, a manner which on the evidence may be old-fashioned, but which was for the applicant one of very long standing.

  1. As Mr Kellow, who appeared for the applicant, submitted, the reduced activities in 1984, and the reduced income in 1985, should not in my opinion lead to a conclusion that the applicant had retired from his old business and was merely "keeping his hand in". It is to be remembered also that at the point when the applicant confined himself to the use of the net boat he was, as he said and I accept, hampered by bad weather and poor supplies of fish. It is nothing new to hear of the difficulties placed before fishermen in all sorts of ways, but the applicant told for instance of the frustrations of large schools of salmon being "baulked", as he put it, from entering the large bay which is a favoured site for his operations by the presence of a large ship at a particular jetty, and by the noise and vibration from the operation of the mechanisms used to load the ship.
  2. The decision in Case D54, 72 ATC 333, to which I was referred, is distinguishable. In terms of the financial performance of the boat owner in that case, there may be said to be some similarities with the present case, but as I read Case D54 it was critical to that decision that the taxpayer was a medical practitioner whose capabilities of earning income from his fishing activities should not be seen as amounting to a commercial activity when set against his potential earning power as a doctor. While not denying that it is open to carry on concurrent callings or businesses, the Board did not consider that the taxpayer in that case had sustained the necessary degree of continuity and system for his activities to amount to the business of fishing. In this case, on the other hand, the Commissioner's case requires me to find that a man who has led -his whole working life as a fisherman should be found to have ceased to be carrying on as such during a year of adverse conditions and during a time when, although his equipment had changed, he was carrying on his activities as he always had.
  3. In 14 CTBR (NS) Case 75 the taxpayer was a retired grazier who had taken up deep sea fishing as a way of occupying his time, and who went to sea with a non-professional crew of up to four persons, gathered from local residents and holiday-makers who were allowed to take home a "feed of fish". The Board concluded that the taxpayer's fishing operations could never operate at a profit, and referred to Tweddle v FC of T [1942] HCA 40; (1942) 2 AITR 360, although I must say with respect that the principles expressed by Williams J in Tweddle's Case appear to be more in conflict with the Board's decision on that particular aspect than the Board supposed. At all events, the case is, in my opinion, clearly distinguishable.
  4. In all the circumstances I am of the opinion that, the applicant was in the 1984-1985 year continuing to carry on, albeit unsuccessfully, his long established business of searching for and catching fish, and that accordingly the objection decision under review should be set aside. The sum of $131 that was returned as income of the fishing business should be treated as assessable income and the expenditure of $6,570 claimed as a deduction against that income should be allowed as such accordingly.
