Butler and Commissioner for Superannuation [1987] AATA 757 (25 August 1987)

Butler and Commissioner for Superannuation [1987] AATA 757 (25 August 1987)

Last Updated: 1 February 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

SUPERANNUATION - benefit classification certificate - delay in issuing certificate - death of employee prior to issue of certificate - consideration of reasons for delay - consideration of prejudice suffered by employee - Neal applied.

Superannuation Act 1976 (Cth.) ss 16, 81, 154

Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978 (No 17 of 1978)

Atra v Farmers and Graziers Insurance (unreported decision of NSW Court of Appeal, 24 October 1985)

Re Miller and Commissioner for Superannuation (No 2) (1986) 11 ALD 1

Neal v Commissioner for Superannuation No ACT G51 of 1986 (unreported decision of the Federal Court, 4 June 1987)

Respondent REASONS FOR DECISION

25 August 1987 Deputy President C J Bannon PC

Dr A.P. Renouf (Senior Member)

Dr H.D. Browne (Member)

The applicant, Miriam Stella Butler, seeks a review of a decision of a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the respondent") to confirm the decisions made by a delegate of the respondent on 13th August 19S2 to issue a benefit classification certificate in respect of her late husband, Professor Stuart Thomas Butler, pursuant to section 16(10) of the Superannuation Act 1976 (Cth.) ("the Act") and that the benefit payable to the applicant be determined pursuant to section 81(2) of the Act.

Professor Butler died on 15th May 1982 (Exhibit B). Prior to 1977 Professor Butler held a chair in Theoretical Physics within the University of Sydney. In 1977 he took up an appointment as Chief Executive Officer and Director of Research Establishment with the Australian Atomic Energy Commission (AAEC) at the Lucas Heights Research Laboratories near Sydney. Professor Butler took up that appointment on 2nd or 5th October 1977. Prior to that date he belonged to the University of Sydney Professorial Superannuation Scheme. Endowment assurance policies taken out by that scheme were transferred to Professor Butler and subsequently surrendered by him as set out in the applicant's affidavit (Exhibit B). The applicant gave evidence about this at transcript pp. 9 and 9a.

It was open to Professor Butler to gain certain benefits under the Act by making payments of moneys related to the period of his previous employment with the University pursuant to Part IX of the Act. This was referred to in correspondence (Exhibits H and G) but Professor Butler decided against that course. He did, however, decide to join the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme (Exhibit G) after receiving a letter (Exhibit E) of 15th July 1977 from the secretary to the AAEC which said:-

"I enclose some literature on superannuation in the Commonwealth which may be of interest to you. When the superannuation authorities have done their calculations on the basis of the data you will be sending to me, we will ask for an expert from Canberra to come to Sydney to discuss with you the options available to you and to answer questions you may have."

We are satisfied from the affidavit of George Patrick Hayes (an officer of the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office) sworn 28th January 1987 (Exhibit 2) and from the evidence of Mr TH Lyon, a retired personnel officer of the AAEC, that the possibility of a benefit classification certificate being issued pursuant to section 16(4) of the Act, because of a pre-existing heart ailment affecting Professor Butler, was discussed between Mr F. Marryat, an officer of the respondent and Mr Lyon. A file note, annexure B to Mr Hayes' affidavit (Exhibit 2) notes the benefit classification certificate conditions affecting Professor Butler as being "Myocardial disease. Left bundle branch block". The note states in part: "I informed Mr Lyon that we will not issue a BCC at this stage but will wait until Professor Butler officially advises whether he will accept appointment."

Mr Lyon gave evidence that he informed Professor Butler of the advice coming from the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office that should he accept the appointment with the AAEC, they would be obliged to issue a benefit classification certificate (transcript p 14) and would issue one (transcript p 15a) or one might be issued (transcript p 26). However the precise condition to be specified in the certificate is not stated to have been drawn to Professor Butler's attention, although he knew it was associated with his heart ailment (transcript p. 20). We accept Mr Lyon's version of the facts and that Mr Lyon's conversation with Professor Butler took place at Coogee some time between August and October 1977.

The plain fact is that no benefit classification certificate was issued by the respondent in accordance with section 16(4) of the Act during Professor Butler's lifetime. Interesting questions as to the effect of informal communication of knowledge of decisions are discussed in the unreported judgment of McHugh JA in the NSW Court of Appeal in Atra v Farmers and Graziers Insurance (24th October 1985). However whatever the state of Professor Butler's information and whatever the intentions of officers in the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office, the respondent did not in fact issue a benefit classification certificate until 13th August 1982 (T8/1) following Professor Butler's death on 15th May 1982. It should also be noted that a file note by Mr Lyon of 28th July 1977 (Exhibit M) indicates that it was a Dr Richards who stated he would have to issue a benefit classification certificate, not the respondent.

Mr GP Hayes swore a second affidavit on 20th March 1987 (Exhibit 3) and gave oral evidence concerning the reasons for the delay in issuing a benefit classification certificate with respect to Professor Butler. His view was that the reason for delay was because the AAEC never informed the respondent that Professor Butler had accepted the appointment. He also pointed out that originally the respondent kept two files regarding Professor Butler which were later merged.

Mr RC Withear for the respondent realistically and properly accepted that there had been some neglect but claimed it was by the AAEC. We are unable to take such a view. We believe the delay was on the part both of the AAEC and the respondent's office. Unfortunately in offices dealing with large scale public activities, instances of delay and neglect are bound to occur from time to time. If the Act requires the respondent to issue a benefit classification certificate within a reasonable time of the medical examination, as discussed by this Tribunal in Re Miller and Commissioner for Superannuation (No 2) (1986) 11 ALD 1 then we are obliged to say that the respondent failed to so do.

Once the respondent has instituted the process of medical examination pursuant to section 16(2) of the Act, it does not appear to be a proper answer, if he was under a duty to proceed within a reasonable time, to say that he is postponing consideration pursuant to section 16(4) pending a notification by the AAEC as to acceptance of employment.

The argument that Professor Butler suffered no detriment by the failure to issue the benefit classification certificate within a reasonable time because he knew of his heart ailment and of the likely issue of the benefit classification certificate, in our opinion fails. No one communicated the terms of the proposed benefit classification certificate to Professor Butler and it may have been open to him to dispute the description "Myocardial disease. Left bundle branch block" if that had appeared on a benefit classification certificate. He may have been able to show that his heart ailment was something of a lesser nature. If the delay in issuing the certificate until after Professor Butler's death some five years later had not occurred, Professor Butler might have undergone other medical examinations and obtained evidence to review the diagnosis in a review application pursuant to section 154(2) of the Act. The fact that the applicant may seek a review is no answer to the loss of opportunity to perpetuate and obtain evidence.

The death certificate issued in respect to the late Professor Butler (T8/1) states as causes of death:-

"1 (a) Pulmonary oedema 24 hours

(b) Cardiomyopathy (post viral) 7 years

11 Cerebrovascular accident 48 hours"

The application to this Tribunal for review, follows a reconsideration by a delegate of the respondent presumably in accordance with section 154(2) of the Act, of a decision that Professor Butler's death was caused or substantially contributed to by a condition specified in the benefit classification certificate. The grounds on which review was sought were six in number as set out below (T1/5):

"(i) That the Delegate misdirected itself that in purporting to issue -a Benefit Classification Certificate as defined by Section 3 of the Act, it applied the test introduced by Act No. 17 of 1978 and not as Section 16(10) was prior to that amendment.

(ii) That to issue pursuant to Subsection 16(10) of the Superannuation Act a Benefit Classification Certificate in respect of Professor Stuart Thomas Butler was in all the circumstances a denial of natural justice in that it occurred purportedly some four years and 10 months after the deceased had undergone a medical examination by a Commonwealth Medical Officer and subsequently a specialist purportedly pursuant to subsection 16(2) of the Superannuation Act or alternatively the Commissioner by delaying making any decision for so long prejudiced incurably all rights that the deceased had to object, appeal and/or seek to vary or modify the views that may have been held by the Commissioner in relation to the issuing of a Benefit Classifications Certificate.

(iii) That there was no power in the Commissioner to issue such a certificate on 26th August 1982.

(iv) That the Commissioner failed to exercise within a reasonable time the statutory functions provided for by section 16(4) of the Act, namely to consider the report of the examinations that the deceased underwent in 1977 within a reasonable time and further, assuming the Commissioner formed the requisite opinion, to issue an appropriate certificate within a reasonable time.

(v) That the Commissioner directed himself in law in applying the test as laid down by Section 66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 as amended by Act No 17 of 197B and not section 66(2) as it stood at the time that the deceased became an eligible employee namely 5th October 1977.

(vi) That the Delegate should have found that the deceased's death was not caused and was not substantially contributed to by a physical or mental condition that existed at the time the person became an eligible employee.

No evidence was placed before us in support of ground (vi). We are unable to accept grounds (i) and (v) as correct. The Act prior to amendment in 1978 did not in our opinion create vested rights except perhaps those considered in Part XII of the Act. It appears to us that in any event the amendments introduced by the provisions of the Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978 (No 17 of 1978) were intended to be retrospective. The applicant's real argument is that set out in ground (iv) and presumably ground (iii). While ground (ii) is cast in language more apt for judicial review it may also be considered if the Tribunal considers the purported decision should be set aside for the reasons referred to in ground (iv).

While this Tribunal applied the dicta expressed in the Federal Court in the Tribunal decision in Re Miller and Commissioner for Superannuation (supra) that decision is the subject of an appeal to the Federal Court which is as yet undetermined. Meanwhile in the case of Neal v Commissioner for Superannuation No ACT G51 of 1986 (unreported decision of the Federal Court, 4 June 1987), the Federal Court by majority decided that neither section 16(4) nor section 16(10) of the Act is the subject of a time limitation, and that there is no implication of a reasonable time to be read into the provisions.

Both parties requested the Tribunal to finalise these proceedings notwithstanding the non-exhaustion of possible appellate proceedings in other cases. Accordingly the Tribunal, as in duty bound, applies the decision in Neal (supra), rejects the grounds submitted by the applicant and affirms the decision under review.
