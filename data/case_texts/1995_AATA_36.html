TAXATION - application to amend grounds of objection - applicable principles - relevance of delay - whether any prejudice - liberal approach - interests of justice - leave to amend granted



Income Tax Assessment Act s 190(a)

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Applicants

Respondent



REASONS FOR DECISION



16 February 1995 Mr BJ McMahon (Deputy President)



  1. These are 2 applications made pursuant to s 14ZZK of the Taxation Administration Act for orders extending the grounds stated in the applicants’ sales tax objections.
  2. Assessments issued on 22 March 1993, the first being for the period from 1 June 1988 to 23 July 1989 and the second being for the period 24 July 1989 to 31 May 1991. The assessments were made in default of returns.
  3. Objections were lodged in each case on 18 May 1993. They are in relatively simple terms, although presumably they were prepared with professional help. The first ground of objection raised the validity of the assessments. The second ground raised certain issues of fact which might suggest that there was no liability to sales tax in the circumstances. The third and fourth grounds (if they have any relevance) appear to go to that element of the assessments dealing with additional tax.
  4. The objections were disallowed on 25 October 1993 and an application was made to this Tribunal on 23 December 1993 for review of the objection decisions.
  5. Following the usual preliminary conference procedure adopted in the Tribunal, statements of facts, issues and contentions were filed and served by both parties. On 17 May 1994 the applicants’ statement raised, for the first time, notice that the applicants intended to apply to extend the grounds of their objection. The proposed additional objections were then set out in paragraph 30 of that document. This was responded to on 24 June 1994. The respondent indicated that it objected to the proposed extension of grounds, firstly, because the applicants had not shown grounds for the exercise of the discretion of the Tribunal in their favour, and secondly, because if the discretion was so exercised, the respondent would have lost the opportunity of giving a notice complying with s 12B(1) of the Sales Tax Procedure Act 1934. That section prohibits the remission of sales tax if the Commissioner has required payment of the tax prior to the expiration of the 3-year period set out in the section. The issue here would be whether what passed between the parties amounted to a requirement to pay the tax.
  6. The applicants now seek an order extending the grounds of their objections, not only in accordance with the foreshadowed amendments in the statement of facts and contentions, but also in relation to 2 other matters.
  7. The additional 2 matters deal with an allegation that the Commissioner failed to form a view as to the prices at which the goods could reasonably be expected to have been sold under arm’s length sales and alleges certain consequences of that failure. The second new ground is an allegation that the Commissioner failed to form a view of the fair market value of the goods sold by indirect marketing sale and alleging certain consequences as a result of that failure. These allegations clearly follow from the judgment of a Full Court of the Federal Court in Copperart Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 94 ATC 4259. It was only when the judgment of the court in that case was published, that the consequences of failures of the type sought to be alleged in the new grounds were declared to be the law. The judgment was handed down on 22 April 1994.
  8. The Commissioner was unable to point to any prejudice that might flow from the granting of leave to extend the grounds of objection, except that in so far as some of the grounds alleged a failure on the part of the Commissioner to give a valid notice under s 12B, he would be precluded by the time limitations in that section from rectifying the fault.
  9. The principles to be applied in granting leave under s 14ZZK have been authoritatively stated in relation to an identical predecessor of that section in Lighthouse Philatelics Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 103 ALR 156. A Full Court unanimously observed (at 164) that the Commissioner can not be said to be confined in the course of considering the taxpayer’s objection to the matters raised by the taxpayer in that objection. He has an obligation to administer the Act and may determine to allow the objection for grounds totally unrelated to those raised by the taxpayer, if that be the correct course, just as he could form the view, based on a reconsideration of the matter, that the assessment should be confirmed for reasons which he had not previously considered.
  10. Their Honours went on to observe that the same liberal approach should be extended to taxpayers so as to give effect to the amendment to the former s 190(a) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. Whereas previously a taxpayer was rigidly bound by the terms of his objection, the remedial legislation allowing amendments should be construed in accordance with well established principles relating to ameliorating legislation. Their Honours said (at 165) —

“It follows that the Tribunal or the court has power to permit the taxpayer to argue that the taxable income and tax payable are incorrect and “excessive” for reasons not initially advanced, even if those reasons involve, as in the present case, entirely fresh grounds in substitution for the original grounds, or even if they require consideration of matters not considered by the Commissioner in the original assessment process...The decision whether to allow an amendment ought to be made on the same considerations of justice upon which such decisions are regularly made in litigation.”

  1. It is not necessary for the applicants to demonstrate grounds upon which the Tribunal should exercise its discretion, except that they must deal with any question of mala fides or prejudice, or considerations of justice militating against a benign exercise of the discretion. Nor is there any rule that the provision of an acceptable explanation for the delay is an essential precondition to the granting of leave to amend (Comcare v A’Hearn [1993] FCA 498; 119 ALR 85 at 88). Gross unexplained delay could, however, be a consideration in deciding whether overall justice pointed to the desirability of giving leave.
  2. In Gilder v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 22 ATR 872 Davies J refused leave to amend principally because of considerations of justice and the fact that proceedings had reached an advanced stage. In my view, no such contrary considerations are present in the facts before me.
  3. As to the 2 additional grounds, the taxpayers ought not to be precluded from raising them, simply because their availability was not apparent until after a judgment of the Full Court had declared the law for the first time. As was pointed out in Lighthouse, the Commissioner (and this Tribunal standing in his shoes) has an obligation to administer the Act. If there were aspects of the Act that were formerly unclear and have now to some extent been clarified, then it is the duty of the Tribunal to take these matters into account.
  4. As to the additional grounds except those relating to s 12B, no prejudice to the Commissioner has been shown and considerations of justice would require a liberal approach to the exercise of the discretion.
  5. As to those grounds relating to s 12B, I am of the view that the applicants should be allowed to raise them and have the matters decided at the hearing. It may well be that, as the Commissioner contends, valid s 12B notices have already been given, either in the form of the assessments, or in the form of intercourse between representatives of both parties and referred to in correspondence between the solicitors that was shown to me. The applicants should be entitled to test this approach. If it should be held that no valid notice requiring payment of the tax prior to the expiration of the relevant period has been given, then the prejudice to the Commissioner would be nil in relation to the first period of assessment (the 3-year period having already expired) and be only to a minor degree in relation to the second period of assessment. The interests of justice and the interests of having all outstanding issues resolved at the hearing, without a proliferation of proceedings in other forums, outweighs any consideration of this nature raised by the respondent.
  6. I will therefore order that both applicants be granted leave to amend the grounds stated in their objections, in terms of the 5-page document handed to me at the hearing and which I have signed for the purpose of identification and placed with the papers.
