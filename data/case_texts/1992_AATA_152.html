Re Trustees of the C and M Baldwin Pension Fund and Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner [1992] AATA 152; (1992) 92 ATC 2063 / (1992) 23 ATR 1220; (1992) 15 AAR 470 (20 May 1992)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TRUSTEES OF THE C. and M. BALDWIN PENSION FUND

And: INSURANCE AND SUPERANNUATION COMMISSIONER

No. V92/40

AAT No. 7971

Occupational Superannuation Standards - Ombudsman

(1992) 92 ATC 2063 / (1992) 23 ATR 1220

(1992) 15 AAR 470

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

I.R. Thompson(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Occupational Superannuation Standards - obligation of Commissioner to make decision if return lodged in approved form - form approved for 1990/1991 year - return for 1990/1991 lodged in form approved for 1989/1990 year - significant difference in information required to be included - no reviewable decision

Ombudsman - substantial delay in providing approved return form - trustees of superannuation funds inconvenienced and having to incur additional expense - Tribunal having no power to provide remedy - referred to Ombudsman

Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 ss. 3, 5, 7, 12, 13, 16

HEARING

MELBOURNE

20:5:1992

ORDER

1. The Tribunal has no power to hear and determine the application in these proceedings and therefore directs that the application be removed from the hearing list.

2. The Tribunal directs the District Registrar to send to the Ombudsman a copy of the application in these proceedings, a transcript of the hearing in the Tribunal and a copy of each of the documents tendered in evidence.

DECISION

1. On 5 November 1991 the applicants, the trustees of the C. and M. Baldwin Pension Fund ("the Fund") through their tax agent, Mr C.W. Baldwin, lodged with the respondent a return providing information relating to the Fund, their certificate as trustees of the Fund and a certificate by an approved auditor. The purpose of their doing so was to obtain from the respondent a notice in writing under the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 ("the Act") that the respondent was satisfied that the Fund satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in relation to the 1990/1991 year of income. The respondent returned the form to the tax agent and declined to state whether or not he was satisfied that the Fund satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in relation to that year of income. Mr Baldwin, on behalf of the applicants, requested the respondent to reconsider his decision. The respondent declined to do so. Mr Baldwin then applied to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of the decision of the respondent to refuse to give notice in writing stating that he was satisfied that the Fund satisfied the superannuation fund conditions.

2. The respondent informed the Tribunal that he considered that the Tribunal had no power to hear and determine the application. The matter was then listed for hearing of the preliminary issue of jurisdiction. At the hearing Mr Baldwin appeared on behalf of the applicants; the respondent was represented by Ms M. Petery, a public officer employed in the Insurance and Superannuation Commission. At the hearing a number of documents were tendered in evidence. One was a chronology of events. Oral evidence was not given but the facts relevant to the preliminary issue were agreed.

3. Section 12 of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987, so far as is relevant at present, provides as follows:-

"12. (1) Where, after the end of a year of income of

a fund, the trustees of the fund give to the

Commissioner, in relation to the year of income;

(a) a return, in an approved form, providing

information relating to the fund and to

fund's satisfaction of the superannuation

fund conditions during the year of income; and

(b) a certificate by the trustees of the fund,

in the prescribed form; and

(c) a certificate by an approved auditor, in

the prescribed form;

subsection (3) applies in relation to the fund in

relation to the year of income.

. . . . .

(3) Where this subsection applies in relation to a

fund in relation to a year of income, the Commissioner

shall . . . give notice in writing to the trustees of the fund

stating whether the Commissioner is satisfied that the

fund satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in

relation to the year of income, having regard to:

(a) the return and certificates given under

subsection (1); and

(b) any other information available to the

Commissioner."

4. Section 13 of the Act, so far as is relevant at present, provides as follows:-

"13. (1) Where, in relation to a fund in relation to a

year of income of the fund:

(a) the trustees of the fund have not given

the Commissioner the return and

certificates referred to in subsection 12(1),

or the trustees of the fund have given the

Commissioner the return and certificates

referred to in that subsection but the

Commissioner is not satisfied that the fund

satisfied the superannuation fund

conditions; and

(b) the trustees of the fund satisfy the

Commissioner that, because of special

circumstances that existed in relation to

the fund during the year of income, it would

be reasonable for the fund to be treated as

if it had satisfied the superannuation fund

conditions; and

(c) if the trustees have not lodged a return

for the year of income under subsection

12(1) - the trustees satisfy the

Commissioner that the failure of the

trustees to lodge the return, and any

failure of the fund to satisfy the

superannuation fund conditions, were not

part of a levy avoidance scheme;

the Commissioner shall give notice in writing to the

trustees of the fund stating that the Commissioner is

satisfied that the fund should be treated as if it had

satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in

relation to the year of income."

5. Section 5 of the Act states what is required for satisfaction of superannuation fund conditions; only part of sub-section (2) is relevant in these proceedings. That part reads:-

"5. (2) A reference in this Act to a fund satisfying

the superannuation fund conditions in relation to a

year of income is a reference to the following

conditions being satisfied in relation to the fund in

relation to the year of income;

. . . . .

(b) at all times during the year of income

when the fund was in existence and there

were in force regulations for the purposes

of subsection 7(1) prescribing standards

applicable to the fund, the fund complied

with those standards;

. . . . ."

6. Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations were made in 1987; matters prescribed by them included matters referred to in section 7 of the Act. Those regulations have been amended on several occasions since 1987. Substantial amendments were made in June 1991. The Act was significantly amended by Act No. 55 of 1991; a superannuation supervisory levy was introduced. That Act came into force on 24 April 1991.

7. Section 16 of the Act, so far as is relevant at present, provides as follows:-

"16. (1) The trustees of a fund or unit trust that is

affected by a reviewable decision of the

Commissioner, being a decision referred to in

paragraph . . (a) . . . of the definition of 'reviewable

decision' in subsection 3(1), may, if dissatisfied with

the decision, by notice given to the Commissioner

within the period of 21 days after the day on which

the trustees of the fund first receive notice of the

decision, or within such further period as the

Commissioner allows, request the Commissioner to

reconsider the decision.

. . . . .

(3) Upon receipt of the request, the

Commissioner shall reconsider the decision and may,

subject to subsection (4), confirm or revoke the

decision or vary the decision in such manner as the

Commissioner thinks fit.

(4) Where the Commissioner does not confirm,

revoke or vary a decision before the expiration of

the period of 21 days after the day on which the

Commissioner received the request under

subsection (1) to reconsider the decision, the

Commissioner shall, upon the expiration of that

period, be deemed to have confirmed the decision

under subsection (3).

. . . . .

(6) Applications may be made to the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of

decisions of the Commissioner that have been

confirmed or varied under subsection (3)."

8. "Reviewable decision", so far as is relevant in these proceedings, is defined in section 3 as follows:-

"'reviewable decision' means:

(a) a decision of the Commissioner to give a

notice in relation to a fund under section 12,

13 . . . stating that the Commissioner is not

satisfied that the fund complied with the

superannuation fund conditions or the

approved deposit fund conditions, as the

case may be, in relation to a year of income;"

9. Mr Baldwin is an accountant and tax agent; he is responsible for preparing the accounts and the taxation returns of a considerable number of superannuation funds. He has acquired considerable experience of preparing returns for the purposes of section 12 of the Act. He prepared the return with which we are concerned in these proceedings using a form approved by the respondent for the purposes of section 12 for the 1989/1990 year of income. However, during the 1990/1991 year of income the regulations prescribing standards applicable to the Fund had been amended significantly and the superannuation supervisory levy had been introduced. Consequently, the form approved by the respondent for the purposes of section 12 for the 1989/1990 year of income was not appropriate to elicit all the information required to establish whether the Fund satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in relation to 1990/1991 year of income.

10. The respondent arranged for a new return form to be printed and by an Information Circular issued in May 1991 invited those responsible for the administration of superannuation funds to complete a request form, which was attached to the Information Circular, for supply of the 1990/1991 return forms. Mr Baldwin's firm completed the request and sent it back to the Commissioner; it requested 100 superannuation fund forms. However, there was a considerable delay in the printing and distribution of the forms. Ms Petery said that they were distributed in September; Mr Baldwin said that his firm did not receive any until October. In any event they were received by Mr Baldwin's firm some time before the return with which the Tribunal is concerned in these proceedings was lodged on 5 November 1991. It is significant, I consider, that the certificate of the auditor, which was endorsed on the return, was given in terms of the information to which the return related. It did not extend to all the information which was required to be given in the form approved for the 1990/1991 year of income.

11. Mr Baldwin submitted that the return which he lodged was "in a form approved by the respondent for the purposes of" section 12 because returns in that form had been approved for the 1989/1990 year of income. He said that in an earlier year he had been permitted to lodge a return in a form which was not approved that year but which had been approved for the year before it. However, when regard is had to the purpose for which the return was lodged, it is clear that for the 1990/1991 income year the respondent needed information to be provided which would not be included in a return in the form approved for use for the 1989/1990 year. If Mr Baldwin had lodged the return before the form approved for the 1990/1991 year of income had become available, there might have been some force in his argument that the form was a form approved by the Commissioner for the purposes of section 12, there being no later form approved and available. I doubt whether that argument is sound; but in any event by the time that the return was lodged the form for the 1990/1991 year of income had been distributed and was the only form approved by the Commissioner for the purposes of section 12 for that year of income. Further, the certificates of the trustees and of the approved auditor were required to relate to the information contained in the return in that form.

12. The obligation imposed by subsection (3) of section 12 comes into existence only upon a return and certificates being given to the Commissioner in accordance with subsection (1). In its context in the Act it is clear that compliance with the provisions of subsection (1) must be, at least, substantial. That is to say, the return must be substantially in the approved form, and the certificates in the prescribed forms. Because of the significant changes made in the 1990/1991 year of income to the regulations and the introduction of the levy, the form approved for the return for that year was different from the form approved for the previous year.

13. More importantly the information, represented by entries in the form of "Yes" or "No" or in the form of an alphabetical code, required to be provided in the return was, in some respects, significantly different from the information represented by entries made in those forms in the previous year's return. An illustration of the significant changes in the information requirement is provided by a note on Question 3(a) contained in the Guide issued by the respondent for completion of the return. It reads:-

"Trustees should note that for the purposes of Section 5

of the OSS Act the proclaimed day was 1 July 1990.

This means that the former superannuation

provisions of the Tax Act were incorporated in the

OSS Regulations from that date. ISC Occupational

Superannuation Group Information Circular No 18

contains details of the effect of this change and a

number of other changes previously announced by

Government and reflected in the OSS regulations

from 1 July 1990. Trustees should also note that the

amendments to the OSS Act and OSS regulations,

effective from 1 July 1990 which introduced the

reasonable benefit limit administration by the ISC

included Regulation 18A which limits the factor which

may be used to commute pensions to lump sums

during the year and Regulation 18B which establishes

a standard limiting the amount of deductible

contributions that may be accepted by a fund during

a year."

The return given to the respondent by the applicants in the form approved for the previous year was not substantially in the form approved for the 1990/1991 year, that is to say information required to be included in the return was not included in it. No obligation arose, therefore, under section 12(3).

14. If the respondent had accepted the return being given in a form other than that currently approved, as he apparently did in respect of an earlier year, it would have been necessary for additional information to be sought from the applicants and for supplementary certificates to be provided by them and by the approved auditor. If the respondent had adopted that course, he would have implicitly approved the form of the return; but he did not do that. He was under no obligation to do it. Because the return was not in the approved form, the Commissioner rejected it. No obligation to make a decision under section 12(3) arose and no reviewable decision was made.

15. Mr Baldwin submitted that the Commissioner nevertheless had an obligation to make a decision under section 13 of the Act whether to give notice that he was satisfied that the Fund should be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in relation to the 1990/1991 year of income. The first limb of paragraph (a) of section 13(1) was satisfied; that is to say, the respondent had not been given a return such as is referred to in section 12(1). However, the applicants took no steps whatsoever to satisfy the respondent that special circumstances existed in relation to the Fund during the 1990/1991 year of income or that the failure to lodge the return was not part of a levy avoidance scheme.

16. When asked by the Tribunal whether any special circumstances existed, Mr Baldwin submitted that there were special circumstances in relation to lodging a return under section 12, namely that return forms in the form approved for use for returns in respect of that year of income were not available at the time when he was preparing the accounts for the Fund; but he did not suggest that, as required by section 13, special circumstances existed in relation to the Fund or that they existed during the 1990/1991 year of income. In the absence of any information that such special circumstances might exist, no obligation arose for the respondent to make a decision under section 13 whether or not to give notice that he was satisfied that the Fund should be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions. The same result followed also from the absence of any steps being taken to satisfy the respondent of the matter referred to in section 13(1)(c). The respondent made no decision under section 13. There is no basis for finding that a reviewable decision was made.

17. As there was no reviewable decision of the respondent in respect of which a request for reconsideration could be made under section 16(1), the failure of the respondent to deal with the request made by Mr Baldwin did not bring section 16(4) into play. There was no decision or deemed decision in respect of which application could be made to the Tribunal under section 16(6). The Tribunal's power to review any decision depends upon there being an enactment which provides that application may be made to it in respect of that decision (Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, section 25(4)). As section 16 of the Act does not provide for application to be made to the Tribunal except in respect of a reviewable decision, the Tribunal has no power to hear and determine the application in these proceedings.

18. Mr Baldwin drew to the Tribunal's attention the practical consequences for trustees of superannuation funds of the late approval and availability of section 12 return forms. He said that an accountant who had accounting and taxation responsibilities in respect of a large number of funds, such as his firm had, needed to begin work on the preparation of the annual accounts and the taxation returns of those funds immediately after the end of each year of income. The section 12 return in respect of each fund had to be prepared at the appropriate stage of the overall accounting work in relation to that fund. Not to be able to complete all the work on one fund before proceeding with the work on another increased the cost and resulted in delay. He said that the same considerations applied to obtaining certificates from the approved auditors.

19. Essentially, Mr Baldwin's concern was that the respondent was administering the Act without proper regard for the reasonable interests of the trustees of the funds and for the additional expense to which it put them. Ms Petery informed the Tribunal that the reason why the forms were not printed so as to be available by the end of the 1990/1991 year of income was that some regulations prescribing matters referred to in section 7 of the Act for that year were made only on 19 June 1991. The returns had to be in a form which elicited information about those matters. There is no reason to doubt that that was the reason why the form was not printed earlier; but it does not, in my view, excuse the failure of the respondent and those responsible for the administration of the Act to have proper regard for the interests of the trustees and beneficiaries of superannuation funds. Such regard requires that amendment to regulations that will necessitate changes being made to the return form previously approved be made sufficiently early in a year of income for the forms to be prepared and printed so that they are available and ready for use by the end of the year.

20. It is difficult to see why, once a bill to amend the Act has been drafted, any amendments to regulations which will need to be made if it is enacted should not then be drafted provisionally. Similarly, once amendments to the regulations have been drafted, even provisionally, it would appear that a draft could be prepared of the form to be approved for section 12 returns if the regulations are made. If that course were followed, and the printing work given to a printer willing to undertake it without delay, it might be expected that only in exceptional circumstances the forms would not be available by the end of the year of income. However, it is apparent from information given to the Tribunal about problems in previous years that the situation which arose at the end of the 1990/1991 year of income has occurred before. It has apparently caused considerable dissatisfaction to trustees of superannuation funds and their accountants. For that reason I consider it is a matter which should be referred to the Ombudsman for investigation why matters have not been better arranged over the years, with a view to appropriate action being taken to see that they are properly arranged in future years.

21. Accordingly, I have directed the District Registrar to send to the Ombudsman a copy of the application in these proceedings, copies of the documents tendered as exhibits, a transcript of the hearing and a copy of the decision of the Tribunal and of this statement of the reasons for that decision.
