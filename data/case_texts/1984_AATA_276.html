Re Barry Knowles and Commonwealth of Australia (Taxation) [1984] AATA 276 (18 July 1984)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: BARRY KNOWLES

And: COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA (TAXATION)

No. N81/217

Compensation (Australian Government Employees)

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

The Hon. J.B.K. Williams CBE (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Compensation (Australian Government Employees) Act - claim for compensation - applicant employed in Taxation Department as a clerk - claim of aggravation of condition of both neck and shoulder by lifting files from floor - decision to refuse claim affirmed.

HEARING

CANBERRA

18:7:1984

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review of the delegate of the respondent.

DECISION

The applicant is employed in the Taxation Department at Lismore. On 14 January, 1981, he lodged a claim for compensation under the Compensation (Australian Government Employees) Act. In it he described the nature of the injury or disease which he suffered as "aggravation while sitting at a desk of arthritis and spondylitis of spine and left shoulder". It was also stated in the claim form that his symptoms first became apparent in 1956. It also appears from the claim form that the applicant resumed work on 2 January 1981, "by order of the Commonwealth Medical Officer" and continued to 7 January 1981, during which time the aggravation claimed occurred.

It appears that the applicant was off work for approximately 12 months prior to the 2 January 1981, occasioned by pain in the back, neck and shoulder and a hearing problem. However, following consideration of reports from Dr. Brodziak, a physician and a Commonwealth Medical Officer, it was decided that the applicant was "fit for re-employment" in duties not involving the use of the telephone and which would not require continuous bending but would allow him to move around from time to time. It was following that decision that the applicant returned to work on 2 January 1981. As stated earlier he ceased work again on 7 January, 1981. It further appears that the applicant again resumed work on 26 March 1981, and asserts that the applicant's employment during the period 2-6 January 1981, bought about incapacity for work by reason of aggravation of a pre-existing back and neck condition. In the determination under review, a delegate of the respondent disallowed the claim and determined that the applicant did not sustain personal injury arising out of or in the course of his employment and that he did not suffer a disease or the aggravation, acceleration or the recurrence of a disease to which his employment was a contributing factor.

It appears from the evidence of the applicant given before the Tribunal that prior to his absence from work of about 12 months, his work involved sitting at a desk and operating an Olivetti receipting machine. Bending over the machine created problems. However, upon his return to work on 2 January 1981, he was placed in another position, that of correspondence clerk. This involved checking the accuracy of correspondence that had been typed. It did not involve the use of the Olivetti receipting machine nor the use of a telephone, which appear to have been his main causes for complaint earlier. He said that in his new position of correspondence clerk it was his practice to place bundles of files on the floor beside his desk. He would pick up a bundle, place it on his desk and work on individual files which would then be returned to the floor. He said that this involved repetitive bending throughout the day. The 2 January 1981, was a Friday and he said that he experienced back and neck pain throughout the day. This also occurred on the following Monday and Tuesday. On Wednesday he consulted Dr. Percy who certified him as being unfit for work. It further appears that upon his return to work on 26 March 1981, his duties were the same as those during the period 2-7 January 1981, and that since his resumption on 26 March 1981, he has been able to cope with the work and has not since had time off on sick leave although it was said by the applicant that since the resumption on 26 March 1981, he "took things much slower and let the work pile up a bit".

It appears that the applicant's return to work on 26 March 1981, followed an examination on 10 March 1981, by Dr. Bloch, a surgeon, who in a report expressed the opinion that the applicant was "certainly fit for the duties of a Taxation Officer and can always make minor postural adjustments". Following Dr. Bloch's report, a Commonwealth Medical Officer, Dr. McMahon expressed the opinion that he was fit for continued employment.

There is evidence before the Tribunal of the applicant's medical condition. Dr. Percy, to whom previous reference has been made, noted degenerative changes of a fairly marked degree in the cervical spine with narrowing of joint space and with subarticular sclerosis. In the thoracic and lumbar spine a number of minor osteophytes are present on many of the vertebral bodies. In a radiological report by Dr. Williams dated 28 October 1983, it is stated that there are changes of disc degeneration complicated by osteospondylitis at the posterior part of the C3/4 discal joint and at the C5/6 and C6/7 discal joints where the involvements extends more uniformly through the entire depths of the joints. Osteophytic spurs have formed at the posterior margins of the joints at C3/4 and C5/6, and further osteophytes are noted at the neuro-central joints at C4/5 and C5/6 on the right side. Tomographic studies of the facetal joints of the cervical region show well advanced osteoarthritic changes which involve the C2/3 and C5/6 joints with the greatest severity and with lesser arthritic changes at C4/5 and C6/7. The changes noted would create limitation on the range of neck movements. In a report dated 28 October 1983, Sir Keith Jones, a surgeon, expressed the view that the applicant suffered from cervical thoracic and lumbo-sacral spondylosis. There was also some osteoarthritis of the left shoulder causing some limitation of movement and pain but which is not severe. The opinion was also expressed that the work described by the applicant would have caused aggravation of his spondylosis of the cervical and thoracic spine and that it was consistent with the history that aggravation continued from approximately 7 January 1981, to the middle of March 1981. It should be said that the opinion of Sir Keith Jones followed an examination of the applicant at a time substantially later than the occurrence of the events with which this case is concerned. He was not called to give oral evidence and thus was not available to cross-examination.

It was put to the applicant in cross-examination that after a period of approximately 12 months off work he was "more than a little disappointed" when he found that he was required to resume work on 2 January 1981, which information was apparently conveyed to him towards the end of December 1980. The applicant did not agree that he was disappointed but agreed that he had entertained hopes that he would be permanently retired from the Public Service on superannuation. He said that he had been informed by a Commonwealth Medical Officer that he was "85%" incapacitated. Also in cross-examination he agreed that at the time he was required to return to work his view was that he should not have been so required but that he should have been retired.

Upon the view I form of the evidence, the applicant received strong support in this view from Dr. Percy. I should say that I found the evidence of Dr. Percy unsatisfactory in many respects. His evidence was given in obvious anger and upon analysis contains a number of inconsistencies. It was not given with detachment. He agreed in cross-examination that he thought that the applicant should have been superannuated and that this had been a topic of discussion between himself and the applicant. The view expressed by him, may I think, be summed up in the following passage from his evidence:

"No - the approach adopted by the Commonwealth

Medical Officers was such that they sent him

back to this job picking up papers which I

think was a really very disgusting thing to do.

They did not give credence to the fact that

his war disabilities and other disabilities

were causing him trouble. They just reckoned

he was a bludger. I do not agree with them".

It is plain upon the evidence that the applicant suffers degenerative

conditions in the area of his back, neck and shoulder. He also suffered a hearing problem which apparently has been greatly alleviated by the use of a hearing aid. These problems extend back for a number of years. His absence from work for a period of about 12 months was apparently attributed to constant bending over a Olivetti receipting machine and a hearing problem associated with the use of telephones. However, upon his resumption of work on 2 January 1981, his duties were changed to that of a correspondence clerk which as has been said involved the checking of correspondence. The view I have formed of the evidence, is that the applicant returned to work in a very unwilling frame of mind. He had been led to believe that he might be retired from the Public Service and the requirement to return to work surprised and disappointed him. These attitudes received support from Dr. Percy. It appears that on his return to work on 2 January 1981, it would have been quite open for the applicant to have positioned files on a chair or some other object rather than on the floor, which would have eliminated a lot of bending, a factor which in the opinion of Dr. Williams, expressed in the course of his evidence, would have relieved the situation. Dr. Williams also expressed surprise that severe pain would have developed in the applicant so shortly after his resumption of work on 2 January 1981, as usually the onset is slow and progressive, although it can happen that the onset of pain can be sudden. I am unable to accept that the onset of disabling pain occurred as described by the applicant on resumption of work on 2 January 1981. Rather, I think that the truth of the situation was that the applicant was mentally conditioned to demonstrating that the decision to direct his return to work rather than to retire him was a wrong decision. Support for this view is I think, to be found in the fact that he resumed work some 6-7 weeks later and has been able to continue in the same job although he says that he has slowed down in the performance of it. It is difficult to see what might have occurred in that period of 6-7 weeks which would have produced a dramatic improvement in his condition. Dr. Percy attributed this to treatment given to the applicant in that period. There is no evidence that the treatment during this period differed from that given during the period of 12 months preceding his return to work. I have difficulty in seeing why it should have proved more effective that the treatment given over a much longer period. During that time, following further medical examinations, he had learned that it was not intended to retire him from the Service, a decision to which I think he ultimately became reconciled and which was productive of a changed attitude to his work.

There was evidence from the applicant that at some time the applicant attended what was termed "a laying on of hands ceremony" associated with the Mormon religion. This he said had been productive of a substantial improvement in his condition. He said that the improvement was not spontaneous but occurred over a period of 3-4 months. At the end of this period "he realised that the pain was gone. I could do things I had not realised I could do before". The applicant was unable to say when the ceremony occurred so that it is not possible to say if it had any bearing upon the apparent improvement of his condition between 6 January 1981, and 26 March, 1981.

I am not satisfied that, on the balance of probabilities, the work performed by the applicant on his return to work between 2-7 January 1981, resulted in incapacity for work by reason of aggravation of his pre-existing physical conditions and that accordingly the determination under review should be affirmed.
