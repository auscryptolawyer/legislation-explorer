Brown and Commissioner of Taxation [1999] AATA 892; (1999) 43 ATR 1199; 99 ATC 2369 (26 November 1999)

Last Updated: 8 October 2009





DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION [1999] AATA 892

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No VT1999/1-3

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re RONALD VIVIAN BROWN

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal Mr B. H. Pascoe, Senior Member



Date 26 November 1999

Place Melbourne

Decision The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.



........(Sgd) B. H. Pascoe...........

Senior Member

CATCHWORDS

INCOME TAX – deduction for marketing fee – script writer – whether fee incurred – whether contingent on sales

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936

New Zealand Flax Investments Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1938) [1938] HCA 60; 61 CLR 179

Nilsen Development Laboratories Pty Ltd & Ors v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 81 ATC 4031



REASONS FOR DECISION



26 November 1999 Mr B. H. Pascoe, Senior Member



1. This was an application to review a decision of the respondent to disallow an objection against amended assessments of income tax for the years ended 30 June 1993, 1994 and 1995. The applicant's objection sought allowance of $12,000 as a deduction in each of the relevant years which deductions had not been claimed in the returns lodged. The objection claimed that the applicant was a film and video script writer and he had incurred a marketing fee of $12,000 in each of the years.

2. At the hearing of this application the applicant, Mr Brown, was unrepresented and the respondent was represented by an officer of the respondent. Mr Brown gave evidence and tendered a statutory declaration of a Ms S. Richardson, a resident of the USA.

3. Mr Brown said that he entered into an arrangement with Ms Richardson effective from 1 July 1992 to market his scripts and film concepts for a fee of $1,000 per month. He said that the arrangement was made by telephone and confirmed in writing although he could not now locate a copy of the written agreement. He said that under the agreement he was not required to make payments progressively as Ms Richardson proposed that the fee be deducted from the proceeds of sales. He accepted that, while efforts were still being made, no sales had yet eventuated although there were some recent indications of possible success. Mr Brown maintained that he did not claim the fees initially but subsequent advice from a tax agent that the fee should be deductible in each relevant year led him to lodge the objection.

4. In his letter of objection Mr Brown stated that the marketing fee was "payable when the first script was sold". In response to a request from the respondent for some confirmation of the agreement, Mr Brown provided a letter dated 15 August 1988 headed "R & R Films" of Century City, California, USA signed by Ms Richardson. This letter stated:

"...I can confirm the contract we have had since 1993 in which we actively market on your behalf for a fee of One Thousand (Australian) Dollars per calendar month.

Regrettably, we have not placed any of your projects with a network or studio here, but we continue to try."

Mr Brown said that he had no prior relationship with R & R Films and met Ms Richardson in Australia as a person known to his father. He said that while she was in Australia she gave him her telephone number and address in the USA.

5. Mr Brown produced a statutory declaration signed by Ms Richardson on 3 September 1999 (three days prior to the hearing) at Brighton, Victoria. He said that she was unwilling to attend to give evidence. The statutory declaration confirmed a marketing relationship with Mr Brown effective 1 July 1992 and stated:

"...the agreed fee of $Aust 1,000 per month is still owing and that while I agreed that all or part of the fee could be offset against future sales, this was at my indulgence, and in no way could the debt be avoided. I can at any time demand full or part payment of the debt. It is not in my view, in any way contingent on any other outcomes."

6. When the respondent put to Mr Brown in cross-examination that a search of US telephone numbers and addresses showed the telephone number and address of Ms Richardson were listed under the name Ron V. Brown, Mr Brown said that this was not possible and he had no knowledge of such a listing. However, Mr Brown later confirmed that the Ron V. Brown listed at the Californian address was his father. While Mr Brown was somewhat evasive about the relationship between his father and Ms Richardson, he said that it was preferable to use someone whom you can trust. He said that his father was established in film and television distribution and both he and Ms Richardson had a wide network of contacts in the industry. He confirmed, also, that he has had an agent in Australia who charges a percentage of sales proceeds.

7. It was submitted by Mr Brown that the monthly fee was deductible as being incurred on a monthly basis. He maintained that it was not contingent on future sales but a liability to which he was definitively committed although unpaid by consent of the other party. The respondent submitted that the liability was contingent on sales of scripts which may arise in the future and was not incurred within the meaning of section 51 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").

8. There is little to support Mr Brown's argument that the fee was deductible in each of the relevant years as being incurred on a monthly basis. His original statement in his objection that the fee was "payable when the first script was sold" appears to be the true fact of the liability. The letter of Ms Richardson of 15 August 1998 makes no reference to when the liability equal to $1,000 per month will come home. Her statutory declaration is somewhat ambivalent in that she says that "I agreed that all or part of the fee could be offset against future sales" but also that "I can at any time demand full or part payment of the debt". Unfortunately, Ms Richardson did not appear to amplify this apparent contradiction or to be cross-examined. Given the reluctance of Mr Brown to admit to a connection with Ms Richardson other than as an arm's length marketer of film and video scripts whilst admitting that her address was the same as his father's, it is difficult to accept that the limited evidence provided from her should be determinative of the issue. A clear further difficulty in the way of Mr Brown is that the arrangement was said to have commenced on 1 July 1992. Some seven years later no payment of the alleged liability has been made. Mr Brown made some reference to discussions relating to offsetting the alleged liability against sales of other matters such as a TV series, however, it does not appear to have progressed beyond discussions.

9. For Mr Brown to succeed he has to demonstrate that the expenditure claimed as a deduction was incurred in each of the relevant years. In the oft quoted judgement of Dixon J in New Zealand Flax Investments Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1938] HCA 60; (1938) 61 CLR 179 (at page 207), His Honour said:

" 'Incurred' does not mean only defrayed, discharged or borne, but rather it includes encountered, run into or fallen upon. It is unsafe to attempt exhaustive definitions of a conception intended to have such a various or multifarious application. But it does not include a loss or expenditure which is no more than impending, threatened, or expected."

In Nilsen Development Laboratories Pty Ltd & Ors v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 81 ATC 4031, Barwick CJ (at page 4035) said that he would add to this last sentence "no matter how certain it is in the year of income that that loss or expenditure will occur in the future."

10. In this case, I am satisfied that the marketing fee is contingent on the sale of scripts and, in the relevant years, was no more than impending, threatened or expected. As such it was not incurred in any of the three years in question. It follows that the decision under review must be affirmed.
