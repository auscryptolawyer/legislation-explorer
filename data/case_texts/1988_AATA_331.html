Re Weir Pumps Limited and Commissioner of Patents and Stork Pompen BV (Joined Party) [1988] AATA 331 (24 November 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: WEIR PUMPS LIMITED

And: COMMISSIONER OF PATENTS and STORK POMPEN B.V. (Joined Party)

No. N88/373

AAT No. 4764

Patents

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

C.J. Bannon QC(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Patents - a pump housing and a method of detecting cooling conditions in an automated machine - advertised as accepted in the Official Journal of Patents - application for an extension of time - no error or omission - party to opposition proceedings - in effect claim a retrospective priority date - had policy of not checking applications

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 (Cth.) s.30(1A)

Patents Act, 1952 (Cth.) ss.59, 160

Trade Marks Act, 1958 (Cth.)

Australian Paper Manufacturers Ltd. v CIL Inc. [1981] HCA 64; (1981) 37 ALR 289

Danby Pty. Ltd. v Commissioner of Patents (1988) AIPC 90-489

Groko Maskin A.B. v Sebastion Engineering (1981) Pty. Ltd. (1984) AIPC 93-221

Lehtovaara v Acting Deputy Commissioner of Patents [1981] FCA 218; (1981) 39 ALR 103

Lyons v Registrar of Trade Marks [1983] FCA 236; (1983) 50 ALR 496

Re Stibbard and The Commissioner for Patents, 29 August 1986, No. 2833

Rohrmoser v Registrar of Trade Marks (1987) 70 ALR 613

HEARING

SYDNEY

24:11:1988

ORDER

The decision of the Supervising Examiner of Patents is affirmed, which includes the decision that the applicant is to pay the costs of the proceedings in the Patent Office;

Time for sealing of the patent to mature from Application No. 563613 is extended until the expiry of six calendar months from the date of this decision.

DECISION

Weir Pumps Limited ("Weir") is a Scottish corporation organised and existing under the laws of the United Kingdom. Stork Pompen B.V. ("Stork"), which possibly is Dutch or Scandinavian, applied for Letters Patent under appliction No.563613 for a claimed invention for a pump housing and a method of detecting cooling conditions in an automated machine. The application was advertised as accepted in the Official Journal of Patents on 16 July 1987, and the three month period for lodging notice of opposition provided in s.59(1) of the Patents Act, 1952 (Cth.) ("the Act") expired on 16 October 1987. No application to extend the time was made to the Commissioner of Patents within the three month period. On 8 December 1987, Weir lodged a notice of opposition, together with an application for an extension of time of two months within which to lodge the notice of opposition. The application for extension of time (T7) was stated to be made pursuant to s.160(2)(a) of the Act.

2. The application states:

"We have only very recently become aware of the

existence of the patent application as a result of the

letter of notification sent on behalf of the applicants

to Weir Engineering Pty. Ltd., a New South Wales

Corporation which is a wholely owned subsidiary of Weir

Pumps Limited and we had omitted to instruct any search

or watch in relation to patent applications in a field

in which we are active. Having regard to our general

knowledge of state of the art we would have opposed the

present application within the normal time had we become

aware of it."

In a statutory declaration in support of the application made by David Peter Elliott, Assistant Secretary of Weir, it is stated in Paragraphs 5 and 5:

"(5) My company has never commissioned a search or

watch of patent applications in the present field.

However, had my company been aware of the patent

application at an earlier date, it would have filed

opposition on the basis that claim 1 at least was not in

respect of a novel and patentable invention over the

state of the art in Australia before 12 August 1982

(which I am informed is the earliest priority date

claimed).

(6) The reason my company did not commission any

search or watch in respect of patent claims relating to

concrete volute pumps is that it did not occur to myself

or to the best of my knowledge any other officers of my

company, that any valid patent claims could be made in a

field with long standing prior art. I accept that this

was an error of judgement and that there was an omission

to take competent professional advice until the period

for opposition to Australian Patent Application

No 563613 had expired."

The application was opposed by Stork, and after a hearing, Mr T.R. Bruhn, a Supervising Examiner of Patents, made a decision on 10 March 1988, refusing the application (T2). In accordance with s.151 of the Act, Weir sought a review of the decision by this Tribunal. On 17 June 1988, I directed, pursuant to sub-s.30(1A) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 (Cth.), that Stork be joined as a party to the proceedings. The application came on to be heard before me, and has proceeded since by written submissions from all parties.

3. The Supervising Examiner of Patents concluded that Weir never had any intention of opposing the patent application on or before 16 October 1987, and consequently there was no error or omission on its part or on the part of its patent attorneys within the terms of s.160(2) of the Act (T2).

4. Before me it was submitted by Mr J.G. Hinde, patent attorney, but who appeared in his role as solicitor for Stork, that, in any event, there was no power to grant an extension under s.160(2) of the Act. As originally enacted, s.160 provided:

" 160-(1.) Where, by reason of-

(a) an error or action on the part of an officer

or person employed in the Patent Office;

or

(b) circumstances beyond the control of the person

concerned,

an act or step in relation to an application for a

patent or in proceedings under this Act (not being

proceedings in a court) required to be done or taken

within a certain time has not been so done or taken, the

Commissioner may extend the time for doing the act or

taking the step and permit the act to be done or the

step to be taken.

(2.) The time required for doing an act or taking a

step may be extended under this section although that

time has expired."

As at relevant times, so far as material, s.160 provided:

"160.-(1.) Where, by reason of an error or omission

on the part of an officer or person employed in the

Patent Office, an act or step in relation to an

application for a patent or in proceedings under this

Act (not being proceedings in a court) required to be

done or taken within a certain time has not been so done

or taken, the Commissioner shall extend the time for

doing the act or taking the step.

"(2.) Where, by reason of-

(a) an error or omission on the part of the person

concerned or his agent or attorney; or

(b) circumstances beyond the control of the person

concerned,

an act or step in relation to an application for a

patent or in proceedings under this Act (not being

proceedings in a court) required to be done or taken

within a certain time has not been so done or taken, the

Commissioner may, upon application by the person

concerned, but subject to this section, extend the time

for doing the act or taking the step.

"(3.) The time for the doing of an act or the taking

of a step may be extended under either of the last two

proceeding sub-sections although that time has expired.

"(4.) Where an appliction for extension of time

under sub-section (2.) of this section is made more than

three months after the expiration of the time allowed

for the doing of the act or the taking of the step, the

Commissioner shall advertise the application in the

Official Journal."

It was submitted on behalf of Stork that the amendment to s.160 had made it clear that extensions could only be granted once opposition proceedings were duly instituted. In this respect I agree with the submissions from the Australian Government Solicitor on behalf of the Commissioner, and from Mr Terry, patent attorney for Weir, that the amendments have not altered the fundamental provisions of s.160 so as to change the original wording which referred to "in proceedings" in s.160(1). The fundamental change introduced into s.160 was to differentiate between errors or omissions in the Patent Office in s.160(1) and those made by a person concerned, his agent or attorney, in s.160(2) of the Act.

5. Leaving that point aside, Mr Hinde's fundamental submission as to power to make an order under s.160 in favour of a person who is not an applicant for a patent or a party to opposition proceedings, remains.

6. Dealing with the Trade Marks Act, 1955 (Cth.), Beaumont J. in Lyons v Registrar of Trade Marks [1983] FCA 236; (1983) 50 ALR 496 held that a power to extend time provided in s.131 provided an independent source of power to extend time, notwithstanding an inbuilt and exhaustive code for extension contained in s.49 of the same Act. In Rohrmoser v Registrar of Trade Marks (1987) 70 ALR 613 at 616 Jenkinson J. found some difficulty with treating s.130 of the Trade Marks Act as overriding s.49 thereof. It seems to me the matter of the correct construction of s.160 of the Act should not be passed upon in this Tribunal, having regard to the High Court's decision in Australian Paper Manufacturers Ltd. v CIL Inc. [1981] HCA 64; (1981) 37 ALR 289 in relation to Convention patent applications and to Federal Court decisions. Although I am impressed by Franki J's dissent in Lehtovaara v Acting Deputy Commissioner of Patents [1981] FCA 218; (1981) 39 ALR 103, a case concerning time for acceptance of a standard patent, I am prepared to assume for the purposes of this application, that s.160 of th Act applies to give power to extend time, even in the case of "Code" sections with their own inbuilt time limits.

7. However, I prefer Mr Hinde's submission that regardless of the alleged independence of s.160 of the Act from other provisions thereof, s.160(2) is not sufficiently wide to enable a person who is not a party concerned as being an applicant for a patent, or a party concerned as being a party to existing proceedings under the Act (not being proceedings in a court), to apply for an extension of time. Reference has been made to some case called Groko Maskin A.B. v Sebastion Engineering (1981) Pty.Ltd. [1984] APO 14; (1984) AIPC 93-221; 3 IPR 614; which may well be a patent office decision. The library of the Tribunal in Sydney does not possess any series of intellectual property reports whatsoever, notwithstanding the Tribunal's jurisdiction under the Act. It does not seem expedient to hold up this decision to hunt for it from libraries situated elsewhere, and, in any event, I have reached a firm conclusion upon the ultimate point. In Re Stibbard and The Commissioner for Patents, 29 August 1986, No.2833, I pointed out the disastrous consequences to the patent system if s.160 could be used to enable an applicant for a patent to obtain an extension of term so as to in effect claim a retrospective priority date, earlier than the date of actual application. I have also been referred to a judgment of Pincus J. in Danby Pty.Ltd. v Commissioner of Patents (1988) AIPC 90-489, but I make no comment on that in view of my ultimate conclusion in this matter. I also note that there are substantial differences between patent and trade mark laws, which make it dangerous to draw analogies from trade mark decisions.

8. It appears to me that it would also cause a serious upset to the patent system to permit persons who have not sought to oppose, to intervene after advertisement of acceptance and to say that although they had a policy of not checking on applications, they now wish to oppose. Loss of opportunity to oppose is not disastrous. A patent when granted is often not worth the paper it is written on until tested judicially, in revocation proceedings in a court of competent jurisdiction. That opportunity remains open to Weir.

9. Accepting for the purposes of argument that I have a discretion to extend time to lodge notice of opposition, in terms of s.160(2) of the Act, it seems to me that in the circumstances of this case it is proper to decline to so do. As the Supervising Examiner of Patents observed, it is due to a deliberate policy of Weir that it did not maintain a watch for pending patent applications or for their acceptance. I do not regard Weir's actions as falling within the phrase "error or omission on the part of the person concerned" nor as involving "circumstances beyond the control of the person concerned".

10. It seems to me proper to affirm the decision of the Supervising Examiner of Patents, and to affirm his decision that Weir pay the costs of the proceedings in the Patent Office. There is no power to award costs of the hearing before this Tribunal. Time for sealing of the patent to mature from Application No.563613 is extended until the expiry of six (6) calendar months from the date of this decision.
