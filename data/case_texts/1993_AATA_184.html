Re Taxation Appeals [1993] AATA 184 (16 June 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. NT86/12137 - 9, NT90/117 - 122

AAT No. 8787

Number of pages - 6

Income Tax

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

BEAUMONT J (Presidential Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - objections to assessments where imposed - exercise of Commissioner's discretion to remit additional taxes - scope of review by Tribunal - whether Commissioner considered mitigating circumstances of conviction and sentence - whether Commissioner took into account relevant considerations.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s.226(3)

HEARING

SYDNEY, 23 April, 10 and 11 June 1993

16:6:1993

Counsel for the Applicant: F.P. Carnovale instructed by

Peter Wise and Co.

Solicitors for the Applicant: Peter Wise And Co

Counsel for the Respondent: G.K. Downes QC, with S.W. Gibbs

instructed by Australian Government

Solicitor

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

DECISION

BEAUMONT J The background to this matter is explained in my reasons dated 22 December 1992 and this day in the associated Federal Court proceedings. In my reasons dated 22 December 1992, I deal with the 1987 assessments, which are now before the Tribunal, except insofar as additional tax is concerned. I have now heard argument on that issue. I propose to deal with it in these reasons and then make a decision which will dispose of the whole matter before the Tribunal.

The subject assessments of additional tax

3. As will be noted, "aggravating factors" (a) (50%) and (c) (20%) were applied, thus giving a total component, for those factors, of 70%.

The scope of review by the Tribunal

4. The role of the Tribunal in a case as the present, is of course quite different from the limited function undertaken by the Court in an exercise of judicial review of administrative action. The approach to be taken by the Tribunal was recently discussed by French J in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Swift 89 ATC 5,101 at 5,112. I respectfully agree with those observations. As French J points out, the settled course of authority indicates that the Tribunal is bound to consider the relevant facts proved on the evidence before it and to decide on those facts proved on the evidence before it and to decide on those facts what was the correct or preferable decision. Moreover, as French J observed, although the Tribunal is not bound by the Commissioner's Taxation Rulings, the Tribunal is generally entitled to take them into account as a relevant factor in the achievement of reasonable consistency in decision-making.

The decision-making process

5. The Commissioner's statement of findings on material questions of fact, evidence and reasons for decision made under s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, dated 14 April 1990, stated the following reasons, in summary form, in respect of the additional tax:

"(iii) Additional Tax for Incorrect Return

The additional tax imposed on the taxpayer consists of two components

being:-

(a) a per annum component and

(b) a culpability component.

As the understatement of taxable income resulted from an omission of

assessable income which was not voluntarily disclosed by the

taxpayer the per annum component was calculated at 10% of the tax

avoided for the period from the date the tax was originally due up

to and including 13 February 1983 and at 20% per annum thereafter

until the date of preparation of the amended assessments. The per

annum component also included an amount of 40% of the tax avoided in

each income tax year.

The calculation of the per annum amount is in accordance with the

Commissioner's policy on additional tax as outlined in paragraph 8 and

Note 3 of Income Tax Ruling Number 2012.

The culpability component of the additional tax amounted to 70% of the

tax avoided for reach income tax year. The culpability component was

determined in view of the following circumstances:-

(1) Deliberate steps had been taken by the taxpayer to conceal

omitted income, namely the maintenance of the two sets of record

keeping books. As the purpose of the 'second set of books' was to

conceal income for the evasion of income tax the extent of the

penalty was 50%.

(2) As prior investigations by the Australian Taxation Office into the

taxpayer's affairs had revealed previous deliberate evasion, a further

penalty rate of 20% was imposed.

The amount of the culpability component was determined after

consideration of Notes and 7 in Income Tax Ruling Number 2012.

6. This was a summary of the reasoning process adopted by Mr. J. Hassarani, Taxation Auditor, in his report, dated 11 February 1987, later adopted by the Commissioner. In his report, Mr. Hassarani stated, relevantly, the following:

"PENALTY RECOMMENDATIONS AND SUBMISSION

31 It is submitted for approval to:-

(a) raise amended assessments for each of the years ended 30 June,

1974 to 1979 inclusive, to include the omitted income summarised in

para 5 and,

(b) remit in terms of sub-section 226(3) of the ITAA, additional tax

imposed by sub-section 226(2) of the said Act, to effectively impose

additional tax (up to a maximum of 200%) of:-

(i) 10% p.a. up to 13/2/83

(ii) 20% p.a. from 14/2/83 to 13/2/87

(iii) 40% flat and,

(v) a 70% aggravating factor

on the tax avoided.

32 The 70% aggravating factor is based upon the approval given in

relation to the previous amended assessments raised in 1984 concerning

the 1977 - 1982 income years. (vide folio 33 for a copy of the

auditor's recommendation). Factor (e) does not apply in these present

amendments hence, the aggravating factors total 70% as against 85% in

1984."

7. Mr. Hassarani's supervisor, Mr. Peter Brooks, said in his comments dated 23 February 1987:

"ADDITIONAL TAX

In the previous investigation, additional tax was imposed at the

standard per annum and the culpability rates plus 85% for the

aggravating factors which were seen to apply (copy at folio 33).

Factors (a) and (c) apply equally to this investigation and I agree

with Mr. Hassarani's submission that they should be applied at the

same rate i.e. 50% for factor (a) and 20% for factor(c).

In this instance the application of the recommended rates takes the

additional tax to more than 200% for every year and accordingly it is

submitted that the calculated penalties be reduced to the 200% maximum

allowed by section 226(2).

Matters in Dispute

The previous amendments to the taxpayer's assessments led to

objections and subsequent appeals. It is anticipated that these

further amendments will also be contested. Since the first

amendments issued in March 1984, there have been a number of

developments in the case, particularly in relation to the criminal

charges against the taxpayer, which have tended to strengthen the

Commissioner's case through the emergence of evidence both oral and

documentary which has pointed to the validity of the black books and

aided in their interpretation. I understand that the present

position in relation to the appeals is that they will not be heard

before the criminal proceedings are complete."

8. Mr. Hassarani late dealt with the taxpayer's objection in 1988. In his report, he referred to an approach made to the Deputy Commissioner on behalf of the taxpayer by Mr. Tim McCarthy, a lawyer. In his report, Mr. Hassarani said:

"The 200% penalty was imposed with the Executive Officer's approval

and his stated view was that the additional tax of 200% imposed

under sub-section 226(2) should not be remitted in whole or in part

under sub-section 226(3). Recent AAT decisions have been critical

of the ATO's failures of not fully considering what was considered

to be mitigating circumstances and cited the following, inter alia,

as instances where due consideration should have been given -

. The taxpayer's honesty,

. Whether there had been a lack of co-operation during the course of

investigation

. Whether the taxpayer had retained the services of a tax agent and to

whom blame for the omissions could be attributed

. The complexity of the tax issues in question

. How deliberate or flagrant the avoidance was.

. Whether the taxpayer was learned in matters of accountancy and tax

law or had substantial commercial experience.

Being mindful of the above and the facts relating to the taxpayer's

circumstances it is submitted that no reduction in the

Statutory penalties imposed should be entertained.

In rejecting the assertions raised by Mr McCarthy, as mitigating

circumstances, the following factors are submitted as the reasons for

the decision reached:-

(a) The penalty imposed by the Court in view of the criminal

conviction was not related to any Taxation Prosecution and hence

should be distinguished for that purpose in reviewing administrative

penalties imposed under the I.T.A.A.

(b) There is no precedent upon which a judgment may be made in relation

to Mr McCarthy's untested view.

(c) The fact that recent amendments to the Taxation Administration Act

provide for even harsher pecuniary penalties where Taxation

Prosecutions are successful.

(d) That although the taxpayer has been sentenced to three years'

jail, with a minimum non-parole period of two years and three

months, he is yet to be incarcerated as he has yet exhausted all

avenues of appeal, which his is most likely to do, and the end

result of which is far from certain.

(e) The fact that the taxpayer and his family have suffered

traumatically as a result of the criminal proceedings cannot be

denied, however, that in conjunction with the $300,000 reputedly

incurred is not sufficient reason to reduce the Taxation

administrative penalty when one has regard to the contrived manner

in which the taxpayer has defrauded the Revenue of monies properly

payable to it for so many years.

(f) The extent of the taxpayer's taxes and penalties, levied by the

ATO, were made known to Judge Loveday by counsel for the taxpayer

prior to sentencing. However, the learned Judge still saw fit to

impose the maximum sentence available to him under the law and even

went so far as to remark in effect that in view of the criminality

involved he felt that the sentence imposed was still inadequate.

Submitted for approval to disallow the objections, in full, in

relation to the 1974 to 1979 years inclusive."

9. In supporting this view, Mr. Brooks said, in a report dated 1 March 1988:

"ADDITIONAL TAX

Whilst I consider that from a moral if not legal view point, Mr.

McCarthy's arguments in favour of remission have some merit, I

nevertheless agree with Mr. Hassarani's view that the serious factors

which point to the taxpayer's culpability cannot be easily put aside.

In addition, when considering Mr McCarthy's view it is difficult to

ignore the fact that even though the taxpayer's counsel made the trial

judge fully aware of the tax outstanding, the judge saw fit to impose

the maximum sentence, which in his opinion, was inadequate given the

criminality involved.

Another indirect consideration is the taxpayer's apparent intention

to make no attempt to put Apsley Investments Pty Ltd in funds so

that it might meet its tax liability. Without wishing to dwell on

this point, it should be noted that it was the company's distribution

of black cash to the taxpayer which largely contributed to its

current poor financial position.

If it is determined that some concession is to be made I submit that

now is not the time. In my opinion the 'Double Jeopardy' aspect can

only be considered fully once the outcome of the taxpayer's appeal

against his conviction and/or sentence is known."

10. In giving approval to disallow the taxpayer's objections, Mr. K. Taylor, Complex Audit Technical Specialist, in a report dated 31 March 1988, said:

"With regard to the additional tax under section 226, the taxpayer

submits 'there are good and sufficient reasons why such additional tax

should be fully or alternatively, almost fully, remitted, and the

Commissioner has not properly taken into account such reasons or

alternatively, has not given proper weight to such reasons.' Amongst

other things, Mr McCarthy, on behalf of the taxpayer, put forward the

following in support of a remission of additional tax:-

(a) The taxpayer was convicted in the District Court of NSW,

Criminal Jurisdiction on 23 October 1987 of having conspired with

others between 1 January 1969 and 30 June 1981 to defraud the

Commonwealth and sentenced to three years jail;

(b) The criminal proceedings have been traumatic for the taxpayer and

his family; and

(c) The taxpayer's legal costs in the criminal matter were in excess

of $300,000.

Regarding the application of section 226, I have taken the following

factors into account:-

(a) The taxpayer omitted assessable income from his return.

(b) The seriousness of the omission and period over which it occurred.

(c) The deliberate steps taken to conceal the omitted income.

(d) The concealment involved collusion with others.

(e) The taxpayer has previously been involved in tax evasion.

(f) The taxpayer failed to provide information requested on a number

of occasions; and

(g) The taxpayer has had the benefit of failing to pay tax on the

omitted income for many years.

After taking into account the Income Tax Rulings covering the

remission of additional tax and the matters mentioned above as well as

all the circumstances known to me, I consider there are not sufficient

reasons to remit the additional tax or any part thereof in terms of

sub-section 226(3)."

The taxpayer's challenge

11. The principal contention advanced on behalf of the taxpayer is that the Commissioner's decision failed to give appropriate weight, or any real weight, to the mitigating circumstances involved in the fact that the taxpayer served a substantial term of imprisonment as a result of his conviction on a offence arising out of the same facts as were relied on to justify the amended assessments.

12. In my opinion, there is force in this contention.

13. It is plain from the reports to which reference has been made that the Commissioner was not prepared to take this matter into account in the taxpayer's favour, notwithstanding that his appeal having been dismissed, the taxpayer was, at an advanced age (69 - 70 years) imprisoned from October 1988 until March 1990.

14. In my view, it is appropriate to take into account, in reduction of what should otherwise be payable as penalty tax, the "odium" of public prosecution and the "stigma" arising from his conviction (see Case No. C108 (1953) 3 TBRD 639 per H.H. Trebilco at 642 and per J.A. Nimmo at 645).

15. It will be recalled that, at one stage of the Commissioner's review process, it was suggested that it was then premature to rely upon the conviction of the taxpayer because the appellate processes had not then been exhausted. But looking at the matter now, as the Tribunal does, I have the benefit of the knowledge that the taxpayer's appeal failed. It follows, in may view, that the Commissioner failed to take into account a consideration which was relevant and ought to have been taken into account. On this ground, I propose to set aside the Commissioner's decisions but to remit them for reconsideration of the exercise of the discretion conferred by s.226(3)

16. Reference should be made to some further submissions put on behalf of the parties.

17. For the Commissioner, it was put that the sentencing Judge, Loveday J, took into account in the taxpayer's favour on sentence the circumstance that the taxpayer had been assessed for substantial additional tax.

18. The relevant passage in the remarks on sentence is as follows:

"The tax evasion was achieved by the keeping of two sets of books.

One was for the accountants for the purposes of the preparation of

tax returns and of course for inspection by tax officers if ever

that occurred. The other set provided a true record for the

information of those running the businesses. A large amount of money

is alleged to have been involved.

The accountant who was called in the Crown case to analyse the books

and who did so in relation to those years of the conspiracy which

he felt he could properly analyse show that some amount well in

excess of one million dollars was understated in income between the

years 1975 and 1980. For the period of the conspiracy, that is, for

the whole of the period, the amount understated would have been far

greater. Mr. Barker has put before me a summary of amounts which

are now claimed to have been evaded by you and I am told that there

is a present assessment which including penalty and interest claims

from you approximately one and a half million dollars. Whatever the

true figure it was obviously a very large sum of money. Numerous

false returns and declarations were filed and made and the

conspiracy involved a number of businesses and a number of people,

all of which were necessarily implicated in this fraud. In my view

the maximum penalty of three years is inadequate, having regard to

the criminality which is demonstrated by a short statement of these

facts. No mitigating factors were put to me in relation to the

crime itself."

19. I cannot accept the Commissioner's argument. When read in context, his Honour's remarks were intended to emphasise the magnitude of the crime, not to discount it.

20. On the other hand, I agree with the Commissioner's contention that, for present purposes, no weight should be given to the fact that the taxpayer incurred substantial expenditure in seeking, unsuccessfully, to defend the criminal charges.

21. I reject also argument advanced on behalf of the taxpayer that the previous assessments did not involve tax "evasion" for the purposes of the "aggravating factors". As I have held in my December 1992 reasons in respect of the 1984 assessments, the taxpayer has not shown that the Commissioner was wrong in assessing the taxpayer on the basis that income had been deliberately omitted from the taxpayer's returns for the years of income the subject of the 1984 assessments.
