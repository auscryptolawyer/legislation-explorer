ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISIONS



INCOME TAX - Shares acquired under employee share scheme - whether eligible termination payment - whether payment of call and removal of restrictions a transfer of property - whether payment made in consequence of termination of employment.



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936



McIntosh v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1979) 45 FLR 279

Reseck v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1975] HCA 38; (1975) 133 CLR 45



AAT No 10136



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No VT94/467

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:      

Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal : Mr B.H. Pascoe, Senior Member

Date : 13 April 1995

Place : Melbourne

Decision : The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

......Sgd. Mr B.H. Pascoe................

Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No VT94/467 TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re: Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION Respondent



REASONS FOR DECISION

13 April 1995 Mr B.H. Pascoe, Senior Member



1. This application seeks a review of a decision of the Commissioner of Taxation to disallow an objection lodged by the applicant against an assessment of income tax based on income of the year ended 30 June 1990. The matter in dispute is the assessability of a benefit derived by the applicant on the acquisition of 10000 shares in P Ltd through participation in the P Ltd Executive Share Plan.

2. The facts of the case were not in dispute. The issue was whether the relevant benefit was assessable under the provisions of Section 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (“the Act”) or whether it was an eligible termination payment within the provisions of Sub-division AA of Division 2 of Part III of the Act.

3. On 13 May 1988 the applicant having accepted an invitation to participate in the P Ltd Executive Share Plan, subscribed for and had allotted to him 10000 shares paid to one cent each. Under the rules of the Executive Share Plan, until the shares were fully paid up, the company was entitled to retain the share certificate, the shares could not be mortgaged or encumbered without consent of the Directors, were not transferable and did not rank for dividend or carry voting rights. The amount of the call on these shares was normally forty-nine (49) cents per share and was to be made no earlier than five years from the date of issue of the shares. If the holder of the Plan shares ceased to be an employee of any company within that five years then the call would be made after one year (or earlier at the request of the employee) from the date of cessation. Where an employee ceased employment by reason of death, retrenchment or retirement the amount of the call was to be forty nine cents. However if employment ceased for any other reason the call would be equal to the then current market value less the initial payment of one cent. The applicant was made redundant on 3 July 1989. On 7 August 1989 the applicant paid the call of 49 cents per share and, on 21 August 1979 was issued a new share certificate showing him as the holder of 10000 fully paid ordinary shares in P Ltd.

4. With his 1990 Income Tax Return the applicant requested a ruling pursuant to Section 169A of the Act and submitted that the benefit of the difference between the fifty cents paid for each share and the market value constituted an eligible termination payment and should not be assessed under Section 26AAC of the Act. The respondent issued a ruling that the benefit was assessable as a consequence of the applicant’s participation in the P Ltd Executive Share Plan and not a consequence of termination of employment. The assessment which then issued included an amount of $47,200 as assessable income. The applicant had included an amount of $1,925 as income arising from the partly paid shares resulting in taxable income being increased by $45,275. After objection the respondent reduced the assessable amount to $44,800 as the initial assessment had been based on market value of $5.22 per share on 21 August 1989 rather than the value of $4.98 at 7 August 1989.

5. The applicant did not seek to dispute that, if he was wrong in his contention that the benefit constituted an eligible termination payment, the benefit amounting to $44,800 being the difference between the $49,800 market value of the shares at 7 August 1989 and the cost of $5,000 was correctly assessable under Section 26AAC(5) of the Act. The relevant provisions of Section 26AAC provide:-

“26AAC(1) For the purposes of this section, a taxpayer shall be taken to have acquired a share in a company, or a right to acquire a share in a company, under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees if-

(a) in the case of a share, the share was acquired by the taxpayer-

(i) in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer; or

(ii) as a result of the exercise or operation of a right to acquire the share, being a right that was acquired by the taxpayer in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer; or

(b) in the case of a right, the right was acquired by the taxpayer in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer.

.....

26AAC(5) Where a taxpayer has acquired during the year of income a share in a company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees, the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income includes the value of that share at the time when it was acquired by the taxpayer less the sum of-

(a) the amount, if any, paid or payable by the taxpayer as consideration for the share; and

(b) if the taxpayer acquired the share as a result of the exercise or operation of a right (whether that right was unconditional or subject to conditions) to acquire the share - the amount, if any, paid or payable by the taxpayer as consideration for the right.

.....

26AAC(15) Where -

(a) a taxpayer acquires a share in a company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees; and

(b) by reason of any conditions or restrictions (being conditions or restrictions applicable only to shares in the company acquired under such a scheme) attached to, or to the issue of, the share (including conditions or restrictions in relation to the payment of moneys in respect of the share) the right of the taxpayer to dispose of the share is restricted or the taxpayer is liable to be divested of his ownership of the share,

the acquisition of the share by the taxpayer shall be deemed for the purposes of this section (other than subsections (4A), (4B), (4C), (4D) and (4F)) to have taken place at the time when the right of the taxpayer to dispose of the share ceases to be so restricted, the time when the taxpayer ceases to be so liable to be divested of his ownership of the share or the time immediately before the taxpayer disposes of the share, whichever first happens.”

Clearly the applicant acquired shares in P Ltd under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees and the time of that acquisition for income tax purposes was 7 August 1989 when any restrictions on disposing of the shares ceased.

6. However, the applicant argued that the benefit fell within the provisions of Subdivision AA of Division 2 of Part III of the Act and these provisions should take precedence over those of Section 26AAC. His primary contention was that the fully paid shares constituted a ‘payment made in respect of the taxpayer in consequence of the termination of any employment of the taxpayer’ so as to come within the definition of an “eligible termination payment” in Section 27A(1). It was said that the shares constituted “property” and deemed to be a payment pursuant to Section 27A(8) which states:-

“27A(8) For the purposes of this Subdivision, a transfer of property to, or for the benefit of, a person shall be deemed to be a payment to, or for the benefit of, the person of an amount equal to the value of the property immediately before the transfer.

27A(8A) Where-

(a) a transfer of property to, or for the benefit of, a person is deemed by subsection (8) to be a payment to, or for the benefit of, the person; and

(b) but for this subsection, the payment would be an eligible termination payment under paragraph (a) of the definition of “eligible termination payment” in subsection (1),

the amount of the payment shall be reduced by the amount or value of any consideration provided, paid or payable by the person in respect of the transfer”.

Section 27(1) provides a definition of property as:-

““property” includes-

(a) a chose in action; and

(b) any estate, interest, right or power, whether at law or in equity, in or over property;”

The applicant considered that his circumstances were within paragraph 24 of Income Tax Ruling IT 2609 which states:-

“24. Where an employee acquires a parcel of shares as a consequence of termination of employment and in such circumstances that the other prerequisites for the operation of Subdivision AA of Division 2 of Part III of the Act are satisfied, the shares would constitute an eligible termination payment as defined in that Subdivision. The income tax liability in respect of the acquisition of those shares would therefore fall to be determined under Subdivision AA and not section 26AAC”.

Whilst such rulings are merely an expression of opinion on the law by the Commissioner of Taxation the statement made in this ruling appears to be an appropriate interpretation where such circumstances exist. However the question here is whether the applicant acquired shares as a consequence of termination of employment and/or was there a transfer of property in consequence of termination of employment.

7. The applicant contended that the fully paid shares evidenced by the certificate issued on 21 August constituted different and distinct property from the partly paid “Plan Shares” issued to him on 13 May 1988. He argued that a partly paid share did not confer the same rights upon its owner as a fully paid share when it cannot be sold, is not listed on a Stock Exchange, has no voting rights or rights to dividend. Consequently, it was said, there were two different properties. Support for this view was taken from manner in which the company maintained its share register. The register has two sections. One section records the partly paid Plan Shares. When a call is made and the shares are fully paid an entry is made to reverse the listing in the partly paid shares section of the register and a new entry is made in the fully paid shares section of the register. The applicant maintained that the reversal of the entry in the partly paid section “reflected the fact that the Plan Share had fulfilled its role as a temporary vehicle and ceased to exist in full accordance with its definition as a partly paid share”.

8. Unfortunately, in my view, the applicant’s argument ignores the true legal position relating to these shares. Ordinary shares in the company were allotted and issued to him on 13 May, 1988. Whilst there remained uncalled capital outstanding, the holder was restricted from exercising certain rights which normally attach to a share in the company. On neither 7 August 1989, when the balance of the uncalled capital was paid up, or 21 August, 1989, when the entry in the register was made and a new share certificate issued, was there any allotment or issue of a new share. The applicant simply requested and was permitted to pay the further amount of 49 cents per share so that the restrictions on exercising the rights which would normally attach to the shares were lifted. There was no transfer to him of property. The shares which he held after 7 August 1989 were the same shares as he had held since 13 May 1988. There was no conversion into a different class of share as would happen where the holder of a convertible preference share converts it to an ordinary share. There was no issue of a new share or a reissue of a share. The shares when issued in May 1988 were issued in accordance with the rules of the Executive Share Plan and by which rules the applicant agreed to be bound when making the original application. The subsequent payment of the call, again within the rules of the Executive Share Plan, caused the restrictions upon the rights attaching to the shares to cease. Even if the lifting of the restrictions could be regarded as property, which I do not accept, it is difficult to regard it as having been transferred to the applicant. The applicant did not “acquire” a parcel of shares in August 1989. He acquired the shares in May, 1988. In August 1989 the restrictions on exercising certain rights ceased as a result of him causing the shares to be fully paid up. Whilst Section 26AAC(15) may deem the acquisition to have taken place on 7 August for the purposes of that section, the actual date of acquisition under general law was May, 1988.

9. If I am wrong in my view and there was a deemed payment by transfer of property in August 1989, it is still necessary for the applicant to demonstrate that the payment was made in consequence of the termination of employment of the applicant. The respondent took the view that it was not. It was argued that the termination of employment must be a precondition to making the payment in the sense that if the payment could occur irrespective of any termination of employment, then the payment is not relevantly one made in consequence of the termination of employment. It was said that, on and from the date when the company accepted the applicant’s application for shares, the applicant was entitled to have the Plan shares allotted to him. The subsequent call and payment of unpaid capital was a consequence of that acceptance of the original application and participation in the Executive Share Plan. The call and removal of restrictions could take place for any one of several reasons not only on cessation of employment. It could happen after the expiration of five years from date of issue of the shares or prior to that date with the consent of the executive on the winding up of the company, at any time after the making of a takeover offer for the company or certain other events or at the expiration of one year from the executive ceasing to be employed by the group for any reason.

10. The meaning of the words “in consequence of retirement from employment” was canvassed in the case of McIntosh v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1979) 45 FLR 279. In that case the Full Federal Court was considering the words of the former Section 26(d) of the Act which required a payment to be “in consequence of retirement from, or the termination of, an office or employment”. The taxpayer retired and became entitled to a pension, part of which he exchanged, pursuant to the rules of the superannuation fund for a lump sum payment. The taxpayer contended that the lump sum was capital and not assessable whilst the Commissioner of Taxation assessed five per cent of the payment pursuant to Section 26(d). The Court held that the payment was a payment within the meaning of that section. In the decision of Brennan J., after referring to decision of Gibbs and Jacobs JJ., in Reseck v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1975] HCA 38; (1975) 133 CLR 45, His Honour said (at pp 282)-

“To say that a payment ‘follows as an effect or result of the termination’ imports causation as the relevant nexus between the termination and the payment, but it is clear that termination need not be the dominant cause of the payment”.

And later (at p. 283)

“His Honour denies the necessity to show that retirement is the dominant cause, but he does not allow a temporal sequence alone to suffice as the nexus. Though the language of causation often contains the seeds of confusion, I apprehend his Honour to hold the required nexus to be (at least) that the payment would not have been made but for the retirement.

.....

Was the taxpayer’s retirement the occasion of and a condition of entitlement to the payment of $27,006.84? On and by reason of his retirement, and at the time when he retired, he acquired an entitlement to a pension (r. 17a). One-half of that pension was commutable and the taxpayer then had a month in which to exercise his right to require commutation (r. 22). Prior to retirement, the taxpayer might have elected to require commutation of the pension presumptively payable, but until retirement there was no entitlement to a pension upon which the right to require commutation might operate. On and by reason of retirement, a contributory who has earlier elected to require commutation is entitled to payment of a lump sum; on and by reason of retirement, a contributor who has not elected to require commutation, acquires the right to become entitled to payment of a lump sum. In either case retirement is the occasion of and a necessary condition of becoming so entitled, and a payment made to satisfy the entitlement is made in consequence of retirement. The taxpayer’s retirement was the occasion and a necessary condition of his becoming entitled to $27,006.84. Though entitlement awaited the exercise of the taxpayer’s election to require commutation his entitlement to payment did not depend upon the occurrence of any other event or upon the act or consent of any other person. When the payment was made in discharge of the entitlement, it was made “in consequence of” his retirement, and the payment thus falls within the statute.”

It is relevant to note that, in that case, until retirement there was no entitlement to a pension from which the right to an entitlement to a lump sum payment arose. In this case the entitlement to have the call made and the shares becoming fully paid arose out of the issue of partly paid shares in May 1988. Termination of employment was one of several possible points of time when the call could be made. It is not possible to regard the call and removal of restrictions as something that would not have been made but for the retirement. Retirement affected timing of the event only. Consequently, I am of the view that, even if the making of the call and removal of restrictions on the shares could be regarded as a transfer of property, such transfer was not a consequence of termination of employment of the applicant.

11. The decision of the respondent Commissioner of Taxation is affirmed.
