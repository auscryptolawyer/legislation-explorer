Re Y (Confidential) and Commissioner of Superannuation Relationship [1982] AATA 32 (5 March 1982)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re : Y (confidential) And: COMMISSIONER FOR

SUPERANNUATION

Relationship

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mr R.K. Todd (Senior Member)

Dr A.H. Marsh (Member)

Mr B.C. Lock (Member)

CATCHWORDS

relationship between s.35 and s.43(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 _ ambit of order restricting publication of decision and reasons for decision

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, ss.35, 43(2)

HEARING

CANBERRA

5:3:1982

ORDER

Confidential Information

DECISION

The following paragraphs are taken from the Reasons for Decision in an Application for Review in which an order was made prohibiting publication or disclosure of inter alia the remainder of those Reasons.

------ CONFIDENTIALITY

27. The question now arises as to what order should be made pursuant to s.35(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. That an order in some form should be made is in our opinion beyond question. Reference to the transcript and the exhibits will show that the applicant's problems shortly prior to his commencing employment with the Commonwealth were such that revelation of them now, as he asserted and we accept without reservation, would have a catastrophic effect upon his life. They must not be revealed to anyone beyond those who have already properly come to know them for the purposes of the conduct of this application unless that becomes absolutely necessary. The Tribunal at the hearing directed, after the applicant had opened his case and given some introductory evidence, that the hearing take place in private (see transcript p.24-27). The Tribunal was and remains conscious that publication of the name of the applicant could lead to the revelation of matters which should not be disclosed. It also made interim orders prohibiting disclosure of the transcript or of certain exhibits except as permitted by those orders.

28. When the Tribunal had completed the preparation of its Decision and the Reasons therefor, but before handing them down, a hearing was at the Tribunal's request held before Mr Todd at which submissions were made as to the form and ambit of the order in relation to confidentiality which should be made. Following that hearing the Tribunal has given anxious consideration to the matter and come to a clear conclusion as to what orders should be made. The questions at issue may be stated as follows:

(i) What evidence given before the Tribunal, and what documents lodged with the Tribunal or received in evidence by it, should be made the subject of an order pursuant to s.35 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act?

(ii) What order, if any, should be made in relation to the publication of the Decision and Reasons for Decision herein?

(iii) To what persons should disclosure be permitted of any evidence and other matter so made the subject of the direction?

There was no dispute that in terms of s.35 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act some limitations should be placed upon the publication or disclosure of evidence given before the Tribunal and of matters contained in documents lodged with the Tribunal or received in evidence by it.

29. First, as to (i), it was agreed at the hearing that it was appropriate that publication or disclosure of the whole of the transcript of evidence, all of the documents in the Tribunal file, and all of the exhibits tendered at the hearing should be restricted. To this extent the direction would go beyond the terms of the interim order that was made but it is plain that the facts of the matter are so interwoven that there would be no purpose in segregating what may with safety be disclosed from what may not. So much then is without difficulty. But there was one major area of disagreement in this context, to which we now turn.

30. Mr Wilde submitted that it was not open to the Tribunal to direct that there be non-disclosure of the Tribunal's Decision and Reasons for Decision. For the purpose of considering this submission s.35(2) and s.43(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act should be set out:

'35(2) Where the Tribunal is satisfied that it is desirable to do so by reason of the confidential nature of any evidence or matter or for any other reason, the Tribunal may, by order _

(a) direct that a hearing or part of a hearing shall take place in private and give directions as to the persons who may be present;

(b) give directions prohibiting or restricting the publication of evidence given before the Tribunal, whether in public or in private, or of matters contained in documents lodged with the Tribunal or received in evidence by the Tribunal; and

(c) give directions prohibiting or restricting the disclosure to some or all of the parties to a proceedings of evidence given before the Tribunal, or of the contents of a document lodged with the Tribunal, in relation to the proceeding."

'43(2) Subject to sections 35 and 36, the Tribunal shall give reasons in writing for its decision and those reasons shall include its findings on material questions of fact and a reference to the evidence or other material on which those findings were based.'

31. Mr Wilde contended that because of the words 'Subject to sections 35 and 36' appearing at the commencement of s.43(2) the effect of that provision is that, where the Tribunal considers that what may be described as an order for confidentiality should be made under s.35(2) in relation to certain evidence or other material, the only course open to it is to make such order. It must then, he said, proceed to exclude from its Reasons for Decision findings on material questions of fact and references to the evidence or other material on which those findings are based to the extent that publication of such findings etc would involve publication or disclosure forbidden by the order made under s.35(2). In other words, the obligation to give Reasons, being 'Subject to sections 35 and 36' is, it was said, attenuated to that extent.

32. The consequence of this submission as we see it however would be that in a case where applicant and respondent are well aware of the contents of the confidential evidence or other material the Tribunal must nevertheless give Reasons for Decision which may well be virtually meaningless because of the need to exclude from those Reasons the evidence upon which the Tribunal has relied. One of the principal features of the new system of Australian administrative law has been the primacy of the obligation of administrators and in turn of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal to give reasons for decisions. The result of the submission made, if it be accepted, would seem strange indeed would certainly be inconvenient and unfortunate, and equally certainly would run counter to the principle that meaningful reasons should be given. The Tribunal should, as the applicant in effect submitted, be free to consider its reasons for its decision in a manner untrammelled by the exterior problem of an order for confidentiality of those reasons made after the reasons have been brought into existence.

33. The difficulty in reconciling the provisions of s.35(2) with those of s.43(2) stems from the fact that s.35(2) does not, in terms, refer to the making of an order restricting the publication of reasons. It must be conceded therefore that if s.35(2) stood alone it might not authorise the making of an order restricting the publication of reasons. Section 43(2) relates to the giving of reasons and to the inclusion therein of a reference to the evidence or other material on which those findings were based. A possible literal reconciliation of the two sections may be as Mr Wilde contended, but Parliament cannot in our view have intended that where an order for confidentiality is made under s.35(2) the efficacy of that order can nonetheless be destroyed by a requirement to publish reasons which refer directly or indirectly to the confidential evidence or material. Nor can it have been intended that where a need for confidentiality arises the Tribunal is constrained, so as to protect sensitive evidence from disclosure, to express reasons using language so guarded or uninformative that the statutory intention that there be provision of proper reasons is thwarted. Such constraints may well prove intolerable and impossible to meet. Accordingly, if s.43(2) is to have effect subject to s.35, it must be a necessary implication that the ambit of any confidentiality order extends to all forms of disclosure including disclosure through publication of the reasons for decision, or of portion thereof if that is all that is required. Were this not so, the power conferred by s.35(2) could be nugatory. It is all an example of the maxim that a grant of power includes a grant of all those matters that are incidental to the exercise of the power. As was said by Griffith CJ delivering the judgment of the High Court in D'Emden v Pedder [1904] HCA 1; (1904) 1 CLR 91 at 109-110:

'It is only necessary to mention the maxim, quando lex aliquid concedit, concedere videtur et illud sine quo res ipsa valere non potest. In other words, where any power or control is expressly granted, there is included in the grant, to the full extent of the capacity of the grantor, and without special mention, every power and every control the denial of which would render the grant itself ineffective. This is, in truth, not a doctrine of any special system of law, but a statement of a necessary rule of construction of all grants of power, whether by unwritten constitution, formal written instrument, or other delegation of authority, and applies from the necessity of the case, to all to whom is committed the exercise of powers of government."

34. An alternative construction is that s.43(2) should be seen simply as making the requirement to give reasons subject to s.35 and s.36 in the sense that those reasons may themselves be made subject to an order pursuant to s.35 and/or s.36. On this basis, to make s.43(2) 'subject to' s.35 and s.36 is to make s.43(2) and things done pursuant to its provisions subject to the totality of the provisions of s.35 and s.36 and what may be done pursuant to those sections. It does not mean that Reasons for Decision are automatically subject to a particular order made under, for instance, s.35. They may be subjected to such an order, but this does not mean that their form, scope and content must be circumscribed by the provisions of such an order. They should on the contrary be drawn so as to comply with the requirements of s.43(2), but may then themselves be made subject to an order for confidentiality.

35. The intention of the legislature as displayed in the provisions of s.35(2) and s.43(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act must surely have been on the one hand to secure the confidentiality of sensitive matter consonantly with the principle of the desirability of proceedings being heard in public and of evidence and documents being made available to the public (see s.35(3) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act), and on the other hand to secure (not least for the purposes of an appeal) the provision to applicants and decision-makers alike of Reasons for Decision which will expose the reasoning of the Tribunal and the findings of fact underlying those reasons, and will refer to the evidence or other material on which those findings of fact are based. These two legislative aims appear to us to be entirely compatible, and if the legislation is to be found, as Mr Wilde's submission effectively invited us to find, to have set them in opposition there would have to be said to have been serious error or omission in its drafting. It follows from all that we have said that we conclude that we may direct that the whole or part of our Reasons for Decision be not published or disclosed save as we may direct.

36. In fact we consider that there is no purpose to be served by segregating from these Reasons for Decision such portion thereof as consists of evidence and/or documents in respect of which an order for confidentiality should be made. It would be theoretically possible to make paragraphs 9, 16 and 17 an annexure to the Reasons as was done by Brennan J, the then President of the Tribunal, in Re Pochi and Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs [1979] AATA 64; (1979) 2 ALD 33 at p.57. It would seem of course that including them in an annexure to the Reasons makes them no less part of those Reasons, but rather makes restricted publication of the Reasons a simpler task. It is no doubt a convenient course where, as in Re Pochi, what is to be placed in an annexure is a discrete section of evidence related to some particular matter. In this case the paragraphs mentioned should certainly not have unrestricted publication but having made such an order there is little point in publishing the remainder of the Reasons, save for paragraphs 27 to 40 which deal not with the facts at all but only with the question of confidentiality presently under discussion. Furthermore, as we have already indicated, in this particular case the facts are so interwoven that we do not consider that it would be possible to separate what may with safety be disclosed from what may not and preserve the required confidentiality. In any event, this is a case dependent upon its own highly individual facts. The only real point of principle that arises in it is one that has been clearly stated by the Tribunal in Re Taylor and set out in paragraph 10 above. Also, there seems to us to be no reason why the respondent cannot give instructions to his officers and to his medical advisers within the Department of Health based on Re Taylor and summarising what we have here said as to the insufficiency of a certificate referring only to 'history of nervous disorder'. We therefore conclude that an order should be made restricting the publication of the Decision and of the whole of the Reasons for Decision save for paragraphs 27 to 40 thereof. Even then the name of the applicant is not to be disclosed.

37. We can now turn to the question of the persons to whom disclosure may be permitted. Mr Wilde submitted that, in relation to the 1976 Act, the following were such persons:

(i) The Commissioner and his advisers.

(ii) A Minister of the Crown.

(iii) A Commonwealth authority or instrumentality and an official of a Commonwealth authority or instrumentality who requires the relevant information for official purposes.

38. We consider that (ii) and (iii) above are too widely expressed. We can see no reason why every Minister of the Crown should be included. Nor can we see at this stage why Commonwealth authorities or instrumentalities in general or their officials should be included. It is in our view preferable to limit the range of persons to whom disclosure may be made and to grant leave to apply should such further disclosure become necessary. This is in accordance with previous practice, including the direction made by Brennan J in Re Pochi. If the order which we have made can be shown to be too limiting in some particular instance or for some particular purpose, the Tribunal can act to cure the problem pursuant to the grant of leave to apply.

39. In the result a direction will be made restricting publication or disclosure of the transcript of evidence given before the Tribunal, of the documents lodged with the Tribunal and received in evidence by the Tribunal and of the Decision and Reasons for Decision herein to the following persons:

(i) The applicant and his advisers.

(ii) The Commissioner, his legal advisers and such officers of the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office as may require the same for official purposes pursuant to the Superannuation Act 1976.

(iii) The Minister for the time being administering the 1976 Act.

An exception will however be made permitting the publication of paragraphs 27 to 40 inclusive of the Reasons for Decision, for the reason already stated but without any reference being made to the name of the applicant. The order will also reserve to either party leave to apply to the Tribunal to extend the exceptions referred to or to revoke the order of prohibition therein contained. Any such application should in the first instance be made to the Tribunal in writing, stating the nature of the disclosure sought to be made and the reasons upon which the application is based.

40. Apart from the originals of the Decision, the Reasons for Decision and the Order under s.35(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the parties when making any publication or disclosure permitted by that Order are requested wherever possible to base such publication on prints of the documents in question identifying the applicant only as 'Y'. Copies of the documents so printed will be made available to the parties at the time of handing down of the Decision. It is hoped by this procedure, which has been previously adopted by the Tribunal, to lessen the impact of any inadvertent release, in unforeseen circumstances, of the full Decision etc to a third party.
