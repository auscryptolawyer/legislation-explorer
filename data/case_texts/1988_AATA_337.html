NT85/2228 and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 337; (1988) 20 ATR 3055; 88 ATC 1134 (25 November 1988)

Last Updated: 7 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - Rates of tax - whether a "non-resident" a "prescribed non-resident" - whether subject to a lower tax threshold than a "resident" - whether any "income related directly to activities undertaken by that person during a visit to Australia".

Income Tax (Rates) Act 1982 - ss. 3(1), 4, 6, 7(1),

Schedule 1;

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s. 6.

Applicant COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

No. NT85/2228

AAT Decision No 4770

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

25 November 1988

Sydney

(Sgd.) PETER M. ROACH,

Applicant And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

25 November 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. The Applicant in this reference is a man now in his sixties. He first came to Australia with his parents as a teenager. That was in 1941. He later commenced his working career in Sydney and, after some years, left Australia in 1952 to pursue his career overseas. For many years prior to 1986 he held a position of considerable distinction and great responsibility overseas. He then returned to permanently reside in Australia.
  2. In all those years he maintained his association with Australia. In 1965 he bought his first real estate in Australia - an undeveloped residential suburban lot. Later he sold that property and bought first one and later other residential properties and, with one exception, let them to third parties. The exception was another residential property which came to be the home in Sydney for his children when they were undertaking tertiary studies here. It also provided accommodation for the Applicant on his wife on the occasion of their visits to Australia.

3. The appointment held by the Applicant was in Asia. By the terms of his engagement, each year he was entitled to recreation leave and, in exercise of that right, he travelled for some four weeks - generally alternating visits to Australia with trips to either the United Kingdom or North America. When in Australia he would visit with his children, with his family and with friends. At the same time he would attend upon his accountant, his estate agents and, if appropriate, his solicitors. He also took advantage of the opportunities afforded by his presence to inspect his properties and to review his investment in them and their management. He would attend meetings of strata companies should any be held during his visit. Routine matters of property management were entrusted to his agents, but matters of greater moment were directed by him from his Asian base.

4. During the year of income ended 30 June 1983 he held two rental properties in Sydney and a third residence occupied by members of his family. From his rental properties he derived assessable income of $9,381 and bore expenses incurred in the derivation of that assessable income amounting to $2,794. That gave rise to a taxable income returned of $6,567 assessed at $6,547. He made no claim for expenses by way of interest, or travelling expenses, or other outgoings alleged to directly arise out of any visit to Australia, or his management of his properties while in Australia.

  1. There is no dispute but that he derived a taxable income for the year of income ended 30 June 1983 of $6,547. Had that level of taxable income been derived in the previous year then, whether he was to be considered as a "resident" or "non-resident" of Australia, the tax payable would have amounted to $752.64. But, because that level of taxable income was derived in the year of income ended 30 June 1983, the Commissioner contends that, because the Applicant was not an "Australian resident", instead of the tax leviable being $639.47 it amounts to $1,828.54: an increase of $1,189.07 - said to be wholly attributable to the circumstance that he was a "non-resident" of Australia.
  2. That such a claim should be made is attributable to the enactment by the Parliament of the Income Tax (Rates) Act 1982 ("the Rates Act"). According to the Second Reading Speech of the Honourable the Treasurer on introducing the Bill which was to become the Rates Act: -

"...The Bill will implement the Budget proposals to alter the personal income tax rates scale and to withdraw the benefit of the tax-free threshold from non-resident taxpayers generally."

Later in the same speech he said:-

"The Bill will withdraw the threshold from non-residents with effect from 17 August"1982 (Budget Day), except in relation to those non-residents who came to Australia on or before that date for a short working visit or who had made firm arrangements for such a visit by that date."

No explanation was offered in that Second Reading Speech or in the Explanatory Memorandum tabled with it as to why the benefit of the tax-free threshold should be withdrawn from non-resident taxpayers.

  1. It should be noted immediately that, it is quite clear from the terms of the Rates Act, the benefit of a tax-free threshold was not to be wholly withdrawn from non-resident taxpayers. Despite the words of the Second Reading Speech and the Explanatory Memorandum, what was to happen was that, whereas previously there had been no distinction between resident and non-resident taxpayers in the matter of tax-free threshold, new thresholds were to be established for each with the threshold for some non-residents being reduced to $584; with the threshold for other non-residents being reserved at the same rate as for residents; and with the threshold for residents being set at $4,462. The question for determination upon the reference is whether the terms of the Rates Act were such as to entitle him to the benefits of the same tax-free threshold as a resident.
  2. The relevant provisions of the Rates Act are as follows:-

"6. The rates of tax declared by this Part ..., apply for the financial year commencing on 1 July 1982."

"7.(1) Except as otherwise provided by this Part, the rates of tax are as set out in Schedule 1.

"SCHEDULE 1

GENERAL RATES OF TAX--FINANCIAL YEAR 1982-83

PART 1--RESIDENT TAXPAYERS

The rate of tax in respect of each part of the taxable income of a resident taxpayer specified in column 1 of the following table is the rate set out in column 2 of that table opposite to the reference to that part of the taxable income:

Column 1 Column 2

Parts of Taxable Income Rate per centum

The part of the taxable income that-

exceeds $4,462 but does not exceed $17, 894 ..............................30.67

...........................................................................................................................

........................................................................................................

PART II--NON-RESIDENT TAXPAYERS The rate of tax in respect of each part of the taxable income of a non-resident taxpayer specified in column 1 of the following table is the rate set out in column 2 of that table opposite to the reference to that part of the taxable income:

Column 1 Column 2

Parts of Taxable Income Rate per centum

The part of the taxable income that-

exceeds $585 but does not exceed $17,894........................................30.67

..........................................................................................................

..........................................................................................................

The issue is whether, for this taxpayer, the relevant tax-free threshold is $585 (Part II— Non-Resident Taxpayers) or $4,462 (Part I--Resident Taxpayers).

9. That question must be determined by reference to s.3(1) of the Rates Acts which, so far as is material, provides:-

"(1) In this Act, unless the contrary intention appears--

'non-resident taxpayer', in relation to a year of income, means a taxpayer who is a prescribed non-resident in relation to that year of income;

'prescribed non-resident' means--

(a) in relation to the year of income that commenced on 1 July 1982, a person who, at all times during that year of income, was a non-resident, not being--

( i).....................................................; or

(ii) a person who derived income (other than exempt income) directly or indirectly from sources in Australia, being income that was related directly to activities undertaken by that person during a visit to Australia, where the visit commenced on or before 17 August 1982 or the Commissioner is satisfied that the arrangements for the visit were completed on or before 17 August 1982; and

(b) ..........................................................................................

'resident taxpayer, in relation to a year of income, means a taxpayer who is not a prescribed non-resident in relation to that year of income;"

  1. The significance of the date 17 August 1982 derives from the circumstance that that was the date upon which the Budget Speech was delivered in 1982. As a result, the Rates Act was cast so as to make that date a significant date, even though the Rates Bill was not introduced to the Parliament until 14 October 1982 and did not receive the Royal Assent until 30 October 1982.
  2. Consideration of the terms of the definition of "prescribed non-resident" initially identifies as a prescribed non-resident all the persons who at all times during the year of income ended 30 June 1982 were "non-residents". By reason of s.4 of the Rates Act which provides that:-

"The Assessment Act is incorporated, and shall be read as one, with this Act." references otherwise undefined in the Rates Act are to be understood in the sense provided for in the Assessment Act (s.6). By that standard the Applicant was a "non-resident" of Australia at all material times during the year of income ended 30 June 1983, notwithstanding the circumstance that he was physically present in Australia from 19 August 1982 to 15 September 1982. It follows that, were it not for the possible application of further provisions within the definition of "prescribed non-resident", the Applicant would have been a "prescribed non-resident" and, for the purposes of the Schedule to the Rates Act, "a non-resident". But the relevant qualification to the definition of "prescribed non-resident" excludes from the scope of that concept such persons as satisfy three tests. To satisfy those tests the Applicant must persuade me

* that he is "a person who derived income (other than exempt income) directly or indirectly from sources in Australia";

* that that income was "income that was related directly to activities undertaken by that person during a visit to Australia"; and

* that "the visit commenced on or before 17 August 1982 or (that the Commissioner should have been) satisfied that the arrangements for the visit were completed on or before 17 August 1982.

12. I am satisfied - and it is not really disputed by the Commissioner - that the first and third elements of the tests are satisfied. As to the second test, it is to be noted

* that the derivation of income is linked to the presence of the non-resident in Australia;

* that it is not necessary that all of the relevant income be so linked;

* that relevant income must have been "related directly to activities undertaken by that person during" the visit;

* that the test of presence is not conditional upon the date the visit concluded; but

* that either the visit must have commenced on or before 17 August 1982 or the Commissioner must be satisfied -

which upon review means that the Tribunal must be satisfied - that the arrangements for the visit were completed on or before 17 August 1982.

As I have said, I am satisfied that the latter test was satisfied. In considering the other tests it is appropriate to recognize that the income derived from the properties by the taxpayer during the year can be considered in relation to three periods: prior to 19 August 1982 - before the Applicant's arrival; from 19 August 1982 to 15 September 1982 - while he was in Australia; and after 15 September 1982 - following his departure. For this Applicant, the only period which could have generated relevant income was the second..

13. It was argued for the Commissioner that for any of the assessed income to constitute "income that was related directly to activities undertaken by that person during the visit to Australia" required two things: that the income be the product of personal exertion; and that the personal exertion which generated the income should have been personal exertion during the visit. The Commissioner's representative pointed to references in the Second Reading Speech and in the Explanatory Memorandum to the exception being directed to those who came to Australia for "a short working visit". In my view, it is not necessary to adopt that basis for determining the application. I am of opinion that the difficulty facing the Applicant is that the requisite causal nexus between activities of the Applicant in Australia and the income was missing. It cannot be said that the rental income which was derived "during (the) visit to Australia" was any more "related directly to activities undertaken by (the Applicant) during a visit to Australia" than the income derived when the Applicant was not in Australia whether before or after 17 August 1982 - and whether before his arrival or after his departure.

14. In considering whether the test of income being "related directly to activities (of the taxpayer) during a visit to Australia", it is appropriate to recognize that the income flow was not related to the presence or absence of the Applicant. His presence may have led to improved future returns but upon the evidence it had no influence on the income, gross or net, which was derived while he was in Australia.

.15. That being so I am persuaded that the correct interpretation of the Rates Act requires that the Applicant be treated as a "non-resident" for the purposes of s7(1) and the first Schedule to the Act. For those reasons the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection under review shall be affirmed.
