Re Jacqueline Lockwood and Commissioner of Superannuation [1990] AATA 124; 5908 Superannuation 22 ALD 57 (22 May 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JACQUELINE LOCKWOOD

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. Q89/127

AAT No. 5908

Superannuation

22 ALD 57

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Miss S.A. Forgie(1) (Deputy President), K.J. Lynch(1) (Senior Member) and Miss A.M. Brennan(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - jurisdiction of Tribunal - Tribunal empowered to review reviewable decisions that have been confirmed or varied - whether power to review revocation of reviewable decision - determination of which decision constituted the reviewable decision - whether decision-maker had revoked decision or varied decision - decision - Tribunal has no power to review.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 - Sections 25(3), 37, 43

Superannuation Act 1976 - Sections 3(1), 81(1), 154, 154(1), 154(2), 154(6)

Social Services Act 1947 24A(1)

Re Gee and Director-General of Social Services (1980-81) 3 ALD 132

Director-General of Social Services v. Hales [1983] FCA 81; (1982-83) 47 ALR 281

Ricegrowers Co-operative Mills Ltd v. Bannerman [1981] FCA 211; (1981) 38 ALR 535

HEARING

BRISBANE

22:5:1990

ORDER

The Tribunal decides that it has no power to review the decision of the delegate dated 27 January, 1989 to revoke a decision dated 24 March, 1987. Any party is at liberty to apply on three (3) days' notice to the other.

DECISION

On 27 January, 1989 Mr Peter James Gunther, the Delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the Commissioner"), made the following decision:

"I, Peter James Gunther, Delegate of the Commissioner for

Superannuation, pursuant to subsection 154(4) of the

Superannuation Act 1976 (the Act), REVOKE the decision

taken by a delegate on 24 Mar 1987 that, following the

death of Mr Kenneth William Lockwood, Ms McMurray was not

entitled to a benefit under the provisions of the Act as

she did not fall within the definition of "spouse"

contained in subsection 3(1) of the Act."

(T2/2 of the documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act")

and admitted in evidence at the hearing ("the T

documents")).

Mrs Lockwood became aware of that decision on 8 March, 1989 and, within the time limits prescribed by section 29 of the AAT Act lodged an application to review it on 29 March, 1990. There was no issue raised by the parties that Mrs Lockwood is not a person whose interests are affected by the decision and, on the material before us, we are satisfied that she is such a person and so entitled to apply within the meaning of section 27 of the AAT Act.

2. To understand the delegate's decision, it is necessary to set out the earlier decision and certain facts which were not in dispute between the parties. On the basis of the material before us we make the following findings. The late Mr Kenneth William Lockwood died on 28 October, 1986 as a result of injuries he received in a motor vehicle accident. At the time he died he was an "eligible employee" within the meaning of sub-section 3(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act"). On 20 February, 1984, he had been divorced from Mrs Jacqueline Lockwood and there were two children of the marriage, Felicity Anne Lockwood and Andrew Kenneth Lockwood ("the children"). Mrs Lockwood made an application for benefit under the Act in respect of those two children. Ms Christine McMurray also made an application on 3 November, 1986 on the basis that she satisfied the eligibility requirements to be considered as Mr Lockwood's "spouse" within the meaning of sub-section 3(1) of the Act (T5/37).

3. On 24 March, 1987, a delegate of the Commissioner determined that each of the children satisfied the criteria of "eligible child" within the meaning of sub-section 3(1) of the Act (T3/33). At the same time, the delegate also decided that

"pending a decision whether Christine McMurray satisfies

eligibility criteria to be regarded as an eligible spouse

and depending on medical investigation pension in respect

of children be at rate of 11% (each) or standard pension

only. Refer Folio 32 where Mrs McMurray has indicated that

she will be electing for refund of accumulated

contributions." (T3/33)

The Commissioner notified Mrs Lockwood's solicitors of this decision in a letter dated 13 April, 1987 (T14/62). He stated that Ms McMurray had 30 days from the notification of his determination within which to appeal against the decision.

4. A decision was also made on 24 March, 1987 in relation to Ms McMurray's application and the delegate decided that she did not meet the criteria to be recognised as an eligible spouse under the Act (T3/33-34). The Commissioner notified Ms McMurray of the decision in a letter dated 13 April, 1987 (T13/61).

5. By letter dated 28 April, 1987, Ms McMurray's solicitors sought a statement setting out the delegate's findings on material questions of fact, the evidence on which those findings were based and the reasons for his decision. They also advised that they were instructed to lodge an application for review of the decision with this Tribunal (T15/63). By a further letter also dated 28 April, 1987, Ms McMurray's solicitors requested a reconsideration of the decision (T16/64). They put forward further information in support of her case. The Commissioner notified them that their letter of 28 April, 1987 had been accepted as a formal notice in writing pursuant to subsection 154(2) of the Act. Following the reconsideration, the decision which is set out in paragraph 1 above and which is under review was made. A copy of that decision and the Commissioner's reasons for reaching it was sent to the solicitors acting for Mrs Lockwood (T39/139) and those acting for Ms McMurray (T39/138). Mrs Lockwood subsequently applied for review of that decision and, on 18 May, 1989, Ms McMurray was joined as a party. No payment of benefits has yet been made to Ms McMurray and the children are continuing to receive pension at the standard rate.

6. With the consent of the parties, this hearing has been divided into two. The first relates to a legal issue. Depending on a decision in relation to that legal issue, the second will relate to a review of the merits of the decision after evidence is led and submissions made on behalf of the parties. We are concerned now with the legal issue, which revolves around the proper interpretation of subsection 154(6) of the Act which deals with applications for review to this Tribunal and provides:

"(6) Applications may be made to the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal for review of reviewable decisions that have

been confirmed or varied under sub-section (4) and for

review of decisions of the Superannuation Board, or

delegates of the Superannuation Board, made under

section 141 of the superseded Act."

7. It is also relevant to set out the following sub-sections of section 154.

"154. (1) In this section-

"decision" has the same meaning as in the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal Act 1975;

"reviewable decision" means a decision of the Commissioner,

or a delegate of the Commissioner, under this Act, under

the superseded Act or under the regulations made under

either of those Acts, and includes a decision of the

Superannuation Board, or a delegate of the Superannuation

Board (other than a decision under section 141 of the

superseded Act);

"Superannuation Board" means the Superannuation Board

established by the superseded Act.

(2) A person affected by a reviewable decision who is

dissatisfied with the decision may, by notice in writing

given to the Commissioner within the period of 30 days

after the day on which the decision first comes to the

notice of the person, or within such further period as the

Commissioner allows, request the Commissioner to reconsider

the decision.

(3) There shall be set out in the request the reasons for

making the request.

(4) Upon receipt of the request, the Commissioner shall

reconsider the decision and may confirm or revoke the

decision or vary the decision in such manner as he thinks

fit.

(5) Where the Commissioner confirms, revokes or varies a

decision, he shall, by notice in writing served on the

person who made the request, inform the person of the

result of his reconsideration of the decision and his

reasons for confirming, revoking or varying the decision,

as the case may be.

(6) ...

(7) ..."

8. The issue for consideration in this case is whether the delegate's decision of 8 March, 1989 is a reviewable decision that has been confirmed or varied under sub-section 154(4). The decision was expressed in terms that the delegate had "revoked" the earlier decision. On its face, sub-section 154(6) makes no reference to the review of reviewable decisions which have been revoked or to the review of decisions revoking reviewable decisions.

9. The parties made written submissions and spoke to them. Mr Newton of Counsel appeared for Mrs Lockwood, Mr Whithear for the Commissioner and Mr Buxton, solicitor, for Ms McMurray, the party joined. All parties relied on the T documents.

10. Mr Newton for the applicant submitted that

"5. The delegate in reviewing the original decision of 24

March, 1987 has done more than merely revoke that decision

despite the use by the Delegate of the term "revoke".

6. If the Delegate in reviewing the original decision had

merely revoked the original decision then no finding would

be currently on foot as to whether or not Ms McMurray falls

within the definition of "spouse". The situation, had the

earlier decision merely been revoked, would be that there

was no finding at all of whether Ms McMurray does or does

not fall within the relevant definition.

7. By proceeding beyond a mere revocation of the original

decision the Delegate has, in effect, purported to vary

that original decision by making a finding that Ms McMurray

does fall within the definition of "spouse". Such a

variation of the original decision allows the

Administrative Appeals Tribunal to review that decision

under Section 154(6) of the Superannuation Act."

11. Mr Buxton, on behalf of Ms McMurray, argued that there are, in effect, two lines of decisions made by the Commissioner. The first originates from Mrs Lockwood's application for orphan's benefit made on behalf of her two children. The second originates from Ms McMurray's application for a spouse's benefit. As a result of the review, of the first decision made on her application, Ms McMurray was deemed to be a spouse and therefore eligible for spouse's benefit under section 81(1) of the Act. Mr Buxton argued that we are concerned only with the second line of applications and resulting decisions. Further, he argued that sub-section 154(6) should be given a very narrow construction and that this Tribunal may only review decisions which have been confirmed or varied. It may not review decisions which have been revoked as, Mr Buxton argued, the decision of 27 March, 1987 had been revoked. In his written submissions, he went on to say

"15. The rationale for limiting the scope of decisions and,

in particular for deleting the review of revocation

decisions is that, in the normal scheme of things, an

original decision subsequently revoked on review would, as

in this case, deprive the original decision of its adverse

quality insofar as the person affected by the decision is

concerned. A confirmation or variation, however, of the

original decision would, by definition, retain the whole or

some of the adverse quality of the original decision.

Other than in perverse circumstances a person directly

affected by a decision would not seek a review upon

revocation.

16. In this instance the effect of the revocation has been

to grant to the secondnamed respondent a spouse's benefit

and to limit the orphan's benefit otherwise payable to the

children of the applicant."

12. Mr Whithear for the Commissioner stated at the outset that

"It was certainly not the delegate's intention that this

decision could not be further reviewed by application to

the Tribunal. Therefore, with the benefit of hindsight, it

would perhaps have been preferable if the delegate had

"varied" the reviewable decision by substituting for it a

decision that Ms McMurray was a "spouse". There is some

doubt whether the latter course of action is open to a

delegate in the absence of an express power to do so, see

section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

However, because of the difficulties in cases involving

third parties such as the present, it is submitted that the

balance of convenience favours the adoption of a more

purposive approach in relation to the powers under section

154 thereby allowing the reviewable decision to be varied

by substituting another decision. Unless advised

otherwise, we would intend to handle any future similar

cases in this fashion.

4. If the purposive approach is accepted, it is submitted

that the powers of the delegate were not extinguished by

the reconsideration of 27 Feb 1989 and a delegate may now

exercise the residual power to decide whether Ms McMurray

is a "spouse". This would mean, in effect, that the

reviewable decision will have been varied and hence empower

the Tribunal to review the result of the reconsideration in

accordance with subsection 154(6) of the Act."

13. With regard to the question of law presently before this Tribunal, Mr Whithear submitted

"6. Whatever view is taken of the above questions of law,

it is submitted that the application made on behalf of Ms

Lockwood for review of the decision taken on 27 Jul 1989 is

a matter in which the Tribunal does not presently have

jurisdiction. The provisions of subsection 154(6) of the

Act are clear and unambiguous and reflect the intention of

the Parliament to impose certain condition precedents on

the jurisdiction of the Tribunal. This is recognised by

Subsection 25(3) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act

1975 which provides that an enactment "may specify

conditions subject to which applications may be made". One

of the requirements specified by the Parliament is that the

Tribunal should only have jurisdiction where a reviewable

decision has been confirmed or varied under section 154(4)

of the Act but this is not so in relation to the result of

the reconsideration of 27 Jul 1989."

14. A response was submitted on behalf of Ms McMurray to the submissions on behalf of the Commissioner and of Mrs Lockwood. With reference to the Commissioner's submission, Mr Buxton's submissions may be summarised under two headings. First, it is irrelevant to consider the delegate's intention when he reviewed the decision of 24 March, 1987 and it is now illogical for the delegate to say that he should have characterised it as a "variation" and not as a revocation. Second, the delegate's powers are now extinguished. There can be no distinction between a preliminary and primary decision. The effect of the decision of 27 January, 1989 is that Ms McMurray is deemed to be Mr Lockwood's spouse and the payment of benefits follows as a matter of course. No further decision is required. The payment of reversionary benefits to children is conditional upon the existence or otherwise of a spouse but the contrary is not the case.

15. In relation to the submissions made on behalf of Mrs Lockwood, Mr Buxton submitted that the use of the word "revoke" by the delegate must be viewed in context and that it is a fresh determination of the initial application made by Ms McMurray. The original decision that Ms McMurray was not a spouse was revoked (i.e. annulled) and it follows that Ms McMurray is now determined to be the spouse. It is illogical to say that revocation would leave no finding.

16. A response was also submitted on behalf of the Commissioner. Mr Whithear argued that it is the decision made on 24 March, 1987 and not that of 27 January, 1989 which is under review in these proceedings. In doing so, he relied on the decision of this Tribunal in Re Gee and Director-General of Social Services (1980-81) 3 ALD 132 at 141 (Davies J, President, Mr Cusack and Mr Prowse, Members).

17. We turn first to consider the decision which is under review in this case and have considered first Gee's case to which Mr Whitear referred. The Tribunal there considered section 24A(1) of the Social Services Act 1947 which provided

"24A(1) Where the Director-General of Social Services -

(a) has, in pursuance of section 14 or 15 of the Social

Services Act 1947, affirmed, varied or annulled a

determination, direction, decision or approval of an

officer under that Act, being a determination,

direction, decision or approval that has been reviewed

by a Social Security Appeals Tribunal; and

(b) has so affirmed, varied or annulled that

determination, direction, decision or approval

otherwise than in accordance with the decision of the

Social Security Appeals Tribunal in relation to that

determination, direction, decision or approval,

an application may be made to the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal for a review of the decision of the

Director-General so as to affirm, vary or annul that

determination, direction, decision or approval."

18. The facts with which it was concerned are summarised in the headnote in the following terms:

"The sequence of events relating to G was that the original

decision to recover overpaid benefit was taken in February

1979 by M. This decision was varied by M in July 1979

following on an appeal to the Director-General pursuant to

s.15 of the Social Services Act. G then appealed to an

SSAT against the decision as varied and in September that

Tribunal recommended that the appeal be upheld. This

recommendation was considered but rejected in March 1980 by

W. The form of rejection constituted the endorsement by W

of the word "Approved" on a document recommending dismissal

of the appeal. Following intervention on G's behalf by a

Member of Parliament the matter was again considered by W

in September 1980 who varied the amount to be recovered by

reducing it by $100. The decision to seek recovery of the

benefit affecting G was that of W in March 1980 and no

appeal could be brought to the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal against this as it had not been appealed against

to an SSAT."

(page 132)

19. The following submission was made on behalf of the respondent in that case:

"Mr Wryell's decision of 1 September 1980 was a review

undertaken pursuant to s.14 of the Social Services Act,

that it reviewed Mr Wryell's decision of 24 March 1980 and

that that latter decision had not been the subject of

review by an SSAT. It was submitted that the decision of

Mr Wryell on 1 Septemeber 1980 did not review Mr Martin's

decision, taken in February 1979 and varied in July 1979,

to reduce the benefit to recover the overpayment. Mr

Martin's decision had been considered by an SSAT but, in

the submission of the respondent, it was not the subject of

review by Mr Wryell on 1 September 1980."

(page 138)

20. The Tribunal said that the first step is to identify the decision which affected Mrs Gee's rights. It identified that decision by looking at the effect of each of the decisions taken in the process in the following fashion:

"A decision was taken by Mr Martin pursuant to s.140(2) in

February 1979 which affected Mrs Gee's rights. That

decision was varied by Mr Martin in July 1979 to reduce the

deduction to $16 per fortnight and to reduce the

overpayment claimed to $1310. The varied decision affected

and reduced Mrs Gee's rights to a benefit by reducing her

benefit by $16 per fortnight for the purpose of recovering

an overpayment. Thus, there was a decision made by Mr

Martin in February 1979 and varied in July 1979 which

remained operative affecting Mrs Gee's rights unless set

aside by a court of law or affected by a decision of the

Director-General or his delegate pursuant to ss.14 or 15 or

otherwise under the Social Services Act or by a decision of

the Administrative Appeals Tribunal.

On appeal under s.15, Mr Martin's decision was reconsidered

by Mr Wryell on 24 March 1980. Section 15 authorised Mr

Wryell to affirm or vary or set aside that decision. Mr

Wryell approved a recommendation in effect that, "the debit

of $1310 be confirmed" and "the appeal be dismissed". It

is a proper inference that, although Mr Wryell did not use

the word "affirm", nevertheless by his decision he affirmed

Mr Martin's decision."

(pages 138-139)

21. The Tribunal then went on to consider what is meant by an "affirmation" at page 139

"What is the effect of an affirmation? Does a decision

which affirms an earlier decision supplant and extinguish

the earlier decision so that it takes its place? Or does

the earlier decision, when affirmed, continue to operate?

Clearly, if an earlier decision is set aside, that is the

end of that decision, it no longer operates. But if it is

affirmed, does it not continue in full force and effect?

We see nothing in the Social Services Act from which an

inference should be drawn that a decision, once

reconsidered on appeal or review, ceases to have effect and

is replaced by the decision taken on appeal or review

affirming it. Nor is there anything in the general law

which suggests that a decision ceases to have effect once

it is affirmed and that its place is taken by the decision

of affirmation. Indeed, it was specifically held in Ridge

v. Baldwin [1963] UKHL 2; (1964) AC 40 that a decision affirming an

earlier decision is itself of no effect if that earlier

decision is void. Cases such as Twist v. Randwick

Municipal Council [1976] HCA 58; (1976) 12 ALR 379 which have

distinguished Ridge v. Baldwin do not suggest that a

decision on appeal entirely supplants the earlier decision.

In Commissioner of Taxation v. Mantle Traders Pty Ltd

[1980] FCA 156; (1980) 33 ALR 276, Bowen C.J., Franki and Brennan JJ.

considered the procedure to be adopted on an appeal to the

Supreme Court from a decision of a Board of Review in

favour of a taxpayer does not necessarily result in the

making of a new assessment and that, in the case under

appeal, it was at least doubtful whether the Board,

although it had upheld the taxpayer's claims, had purported

to make a new assessment or to reduce the assessment made

by the Commissioner. His Honour said,

"The powers conferred by ss193 and 195 may be

exercised to vary or create an assessment which takes

effect according to its tenor, but the powers so

conferred are'form only' ..."

His Honour said that, 'The decision of a Board is not, so

to speak, a new root of litigation from which new issues

grow'."

22. The Tribunal in Gee's case decided, therefore, that the affirmation of a decision simply leaves the original decision in place. It is that original decision which remains operative and which is under review rather than the affirmation itself. It does not follow from this, however, that the original decision is necessarily the decision under review in every case. As the Tribunal said in that case, where a decision is set aside, that is the end of that decision and it no longer operates. What is clear from Gee's case is that the decision under review must be determined according to the relevant legislation and the facts of each case.

23. Turning then to sub-section 154(6) of the Superannuation Act, this Tribunal may review a reviewable decision which has been affirmed or varied. The "reviewable decision" is that of 24 March, 1987 which was made by a delegate of the Commissioner within the meaning of sub-section 154(1) (see paragraph 7 above). Had we been considering a case similar to that in Gee's case in that the delegate had affirmed the original decision that Ms McMurray was not Mr Lockwood's spouse, then we would be reviewing the original decision dated 24 March, 1987 as the operative decision. Clearly the delegate has not affirmed the earlier decision in this case. If the delegate has varied the decision then the decision under review would be the reviewable decision as varied. On the face of his decision, he has not varied the decision. He has stated that he has "revoked" the earlier decision. No reference is made in sub-section 154(6) to review a decision which has been revoked. We are of the view that there is no power given by the Act to the Tribunal to review a reviewable decision which has been revoked. This not only accords with the wording of sub-section 154(6) from which the term "revoke" is omitted but also with logic. Once revoked, there is nothing left to review as the decision no longer operates (see the passage from Gee's case at paragraph 21 above).

24. The delegate stated that he "revoke(d)" the decision taken by the delegate on 24 March, 1987. We have considered whether he "revoked" it or, despite the use of the word "revoke", whether he varied it. What is meant by revoke? "Revoke" means, inter alia,

"3. To recall; to call, to summon back 1521. 4. To annul,

repeal, rescind, cancel, late ME. |5 To retract, withdraw,

recant - 1671" (Shorter Oxford English Dictionary, 3rd

edition, 1978).

We have also considered the meaning ascribed to the word "revocation". In Words and Phrases Legally Defined, (2nd edition) the following passage appears:

"The word revocation has two meanings - the one, if I may

use such an expression, in the probate sense, that is where

one instrument revokes another originally entitled to

probate, - the other I will call the chancery sense, that

is, where a subsequent paper renders an earlier paper

inoperative wholly or partly, though both parties may have

received probate" Brenchley v. Lynn (1852) 2 Rob Eccl 441,

per Dr Lushington, at p462."

25. The word "vary" is defined in the Shorter Oxford English Dictionary in part as meaning

"II. Trans. To cause to change or alter, to introduce

changes or alterations into (something)."

26. Has then the decision of 24 March, 1987 been revoked or varied? Certainly, the delegate expressed his decision in terms of revocation. The document containing the delegate's decision on the review is contained in a very lengthy document which has been very carefully and thoroughly prepared. It is divided into two main headings - "Result" and "Reasons". Under "Reasons" he has used a number of sub-headings - "Relevant Legislation", "Events leading to the delegate's decision", "The reconsideration", "The evidence", "Application of section 3 of the Act" and "Conclusion". It is clear that the delegate was satisfied on the evidence before him that Ms McMurray is a spouse within the meaning of the Act. He stated this in paragraph 22 which was the final paragraph under the heading "The evidence". Under the heading of "Conclusion" appears the final paragraph (23), in which he goes on to say

"I have therefore revoked the delegate's decision as shown

in paragraph 1 above." (T2/29)

27. If we look only at the unambiguous words of this paragraph, we must conclude that the delegate has revoked, or set aside, the earlier decision but has not replaced it with another. Can we look beyond the clear words of paragraph 23? The meaning of a "decision" has been considered by the Full Court of the Federal Court in Director-General of Social Services v. Hales ((1982-83) [1983] FCA 81; 47 ALR 281, McGregor, Lockhart and Sheppard JJ) and is of assistance in determining what the delegate has done in this case. At page 293-294, McGregor J said:

"As to what is a "decision", Director-General of Social

Services v. Chaney [1980] FCA 87; (1980) 31 ALR 571; 3 ALD 161 is of

assistance, though the circumstances were different. There

a widow's pension had been sought and refused. She sought

to have a decision whether she was entitled to a widow's

pension for which provision is made in Pt IV of the Act.

There was a preliminary hearing before the President of the

Tribunal. He held the Tribunal had jurisdiction to review.

There was an appeal to the Full Federal Court against this.

Deane J, one of the majority in the judgment, said (ALR at 590):-

"The word 'decision' is a word of indeterminate

meaning. In some contexts, it can refer to the mental

process of making up one's mind. In the context of

judicial or administrative proceedings, the word will

ordinarily refer to an announced or published ruling

or adjudication. In such a context, the word

'decision' may be apt to include the determination of

any question of substance or procedure, including, for

example, rulings on procedural questions such as

whether particular evidence should be received, or the

meaning of the word may be limited to a determination

effectively resolving an actual substantive issue.

When the word 'decision' has the last-mentioned

limited meaning, it can refer to any such

determination whether final or intermediate (see, eg

Registrar of Workers' Compensation Commission v. FAI

Insurances Ltd (1971) 1 NSWLR 422 at 448) or be

limited to referring only to a determination which

effectively disposes of the matter in hand (see, eg

Winter and Winter (1933) NZLR 289 at 295; Penniel v.

Driffill (1980) WAR 30 at 32)."

...

Northrop J, who dissented, said at page 584: "In its

general sense the meaning of the word 'decision' appearing

in the Shorter Oxford dicitionary is: 'The action of

deciding (a contest, question, etc); settlement,

determination; (with a and pl) a conclusion, judgment: esp

one formally pronounced in a court of law 1552'; see also

Re Kent County Council and Council of Dover (1891) 1 QB 725

and Reidy v. Herry (1897) 23 VLR 508, where at p510,

Williams J said: 'Now I take it that an adjudication or

decision of justices is something which is announced but

not what is passing in the minds of the justices. It is a

decision announced in open court. That is their decision

or adjudication.'"

I do not understand that his impressions in this area

were different from the opinions of the other members of

the court; nor do I imply that decisions which are subject

to review by the AAT are only those announced in courts.

As to further comments on the meaning of "decision" see

also per Northrop J in Ricegrowers Co-operative Mills Ltd

v. Bannerman [1981] FCA 211; (1981) 38 ALR 535 at 542-3; (1981) ATPR 43,332

at 43,338."

28. Northrop J in Ricegowers Co-operative Mills Ltd v, Bannerman [1981] FCA 211; (1981) 38 ALR 535 considered the same passages from Chaney's case and concluded at page 544 that in the case before him he did

"not find it necessary to give any definitive meaning to

the word 'decision' appearing in s13(1) of the Judicial

Review Act. The mere thought processes taking place in the

mind of the person when considering whether or how to

exercise a power or to perform a duty of an administrative

character under an enactment do not, in my opinion,

constitute a decision. In addition to thought processes,

there must be some overt act by which the conclusions

reached as a result of those thought processes are

manifested. The manifestation may take many different

forms. It may take the form of a verbal or written

communication of the conclusion to the person affected, It

may take the form of action taken to give effect to the

conclusion. It may take the form of no action being taken

when otherwise definite action would have been taken."

29. In the case before us, we have the clear words of the delegate both under the heading of "Conclusion" and under "Result". Both statements are expressed in terms of "revocation" without suggestion that another decision has been substituted or that the original decision has been varied. That the delegate had formed the opinion that Ms McMurray was entitled to be regarded as Mr Lockwood's spouse for the purposes of the Act is clear from the other material in T2 to which we have already referred (see paragraph 24 above). From the careful way in which he framed T2, we are satisfied that he understood the various steps involved in reaching a decision - gathering the evidence, considering the evidence, making findings based on the evidence and making a decision. He understood the reasoning process and the need to make a decision and the need to distinguish between the two. The fact that he has shown such a clear understanding, leads us to the conclusion that we must conclude that his decision has been to revoke the earlier decision. Even though we are also of the view that his process of reasoning shows that he meant to make a decision that Ms McMurray was a spouse within the meaning of the Act, and his decision does not reflect his finding, this cannot alter what he has already stated to be his decision. In reaching this conclusion, we have also taken account of the fact that no benefit has yet been paid to Ms McMurray and no action taken to do so. The letter written on behalf of the Commissioner to Ms McMurray's solicitors on 3 March, 1989 (T38/138) states that

"Ms McMurray will be advised of action resulting from the

enclosed decision as soon as possible after the 30 day

period in which the deceased's former wife may appeal

against the decision has expired."

30. No "action" has been taken since Mrs Lockwood lodged her application for review. What is apparent from this letter is that action to pay her was contemplated if Mrs Lockwood had not applied. However, such contemplation alone cannot, in our view, take away from the clear words of the delegate's "conclusion" to revoke the decision. Had there been some action taken it may have been arguable that the action in itself was evidence of a decision that Ms McMurray was a "spouse". As it is, the earlier decision of 24 March, 1987 has been revoked or set aside and nothing has been substituted for it.

31. We have also considered whether the two decisions made on 24 March, 1987 were in fact one decision so that the revocation of one may be regarded as a variation of the one decision overall. Those decisions are referred to in more detail in paragraphs 3 and 4 above. They were made on separate applications from separate applicants. It would also appear that the delegate regarded them as separate decisions when he decided that

"pending a decision (emphasis added) whether Christine

McMurray satisfies eligibility criteria ... pension in

respect of children be at rate of 11% (each) or standard

pension only."

The delegate goes on to say that

"in regard to Ms McMurray's application there is

conflicting advice relating to the date de facto

relationship commenced."

Although in relation to the same estate, we are of the opinion that the decisions were separate. Consequently, we have concluded that the revocation of the decision made with regard to Ms McMurray's application cannot be regarded as a variation of a decision.

32. We do not consider that the issue of whether the Commissioner may make another decision in relation to Ms McMurray's application or whether he is functus officio has been fully argued. Nor has consideration been given to whether Mrs Lockwood could seek reconsideration of the delegate's decision in relation to the children. Presumably, the status of Ms McMurray as a spouse or not is relevant to the entitlement of the children under the Act. Any such request for reconsideration would necessarily be subject to the Commissioner's allowing further time to lodge the request pursuant to subsection 154(2). These issues should be considered further by the parties.

33. The Tribunal therefore decides that it has no power to review the decision of the delegate dated 27 January, 1989 to revoke a decision dated 24 March, 1987. Any party is at liberty to apply on three (3) days' notice to the others.
