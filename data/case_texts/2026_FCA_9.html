THE COURT ORDERS THAT:

Extension of time for resuming the second meetings of creditors

  1. Order 3 made on 26 September 2025 be varied such that, pursuant to s 447A(1) of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), Part 5.3A of the Act is to operate in relation to each of Transtar Linehaul Pty Ltd ACN 123 148 774, Edgely Pty Ltd ACN 089 763 646 and Fleet Repairs & Maintenance Pty Ltd ACN 151 744 697 (all administrators appointed) (together, the Companies) as if:
(a) s 75-140(3) of the Insolvency Practice Rules (Corporations) 2016 (Cth) (IPR) omitted the words “that is more than 45 business days after the first day on which the original meeting was held” and, in lieu thereof, included the words “later than 24 March 2026”;

(b) Part 5.3A of the Corporations Act allowed adjournment of the meetings convened under s 439A of the Corporations Act in relation to the Companies to a day not later than 24 March 2026, notwithstanding the operation of s 75‑140(3) of the IPR; and

(c) the requirement to hold a meeting of creditors of the Companies within the convening period specified in s 439A(2) of the Corporations Act is satisfied by holding the adjourned meeting no later than 24 March 2026,

and provided that the requirements of s 75-140 of the IPR are otherwise complied with in respect of such meetings.

Confidentiality orders

  1. Order 1 made on 26 September 2025 be varied by replacing "27 January 2026" with 24 March 2026".
  2. Subject to Order 4 below, pursuant to ss 37AF(1)(b), 37AG(1)(a) and 37AJ of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth), on the ground that the order is necessary to prevent prejudice to the proper administration of justice, the documents (Documents) identified as “Confidential Exhibit AS-4” referred to in the Affidavit of Adam Shepard sworn on 16 January 2026 be marked “Confidential” on the Court’s file and not be published, disclosed or accessed by any person, except pursuant to an order of the Court, until 24 March 2026 or further order.
  3. Order 3 above does not prevent the Plaintiffs, the Plaintiffs’ legal representatives or the Plaintiffs’ servants, agents or employees, from disclosing, publishing or accessing the Documents and the information contained therein.

Liberty to apply

  1. The Plaintiffs have liberty to apply in respect of the voluntary administration of the Companies.
  2. Any person who can demonstrate sufficient interest to modify or discharge Order 1 has liberty to apply on three business days' written notice to the Plaintiffs.
  3. The Plaintiffs give notice of these orders to the creditors of each of the Companies by sending a circular letter to creditors of the Companies (by email in respect of those creditors for whom there is an email address in the books and records of the Companies or who have otherwise notified the Plaintiffs that email is their preferred method of communication, and by post in respect of all other known creditors) informing them of the substance of these orders.

Costs

  1. The Plaintiffs’ costs of this application be costs in the administration of the Companies.

Note: Entry of orders is dealt with in Rule 39.32 of the Federal Court Rules 2011.

REASONS FOR JUDGMENT

Delivered ex tempore, revised from transcript

YOUNAN J

INTRODUCTION

  1. By Interlocutory Process dated 15 January 2026, the first plaintiff, Adam Shepard and Cameron Gray (as voluntary administrators of the second to fourth plaintiffs) (Administrators) apply for orders pursuant to s 439A(6) and/or s 447A(1) of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) extending the convening period for the second meeting of creditors of each of the second to fourth plaintiffs (the Companies). The Administrators do not seek any orders in respect of the fifth plaintiff by this application.
  2. The second meetings of creditors were initially held on 11 August 2025 and adjourned for up to 45 business days. On 26 September 2025, the Court made Orders in this proceeding deferring the statutory requirements for the resumption of the second meetings until 27 January 2026. The Administrators now seek a further deferral to enable the remaining assets to be sold, and a deed of company arrangement proffered, which may provide for a better return to creditors than would ensue from an immediate winding up of the Companies.
  3. The Administrators seek that the second meetings be deferred to 24 March 2026. Absent a deferral, those meetings would otherwise need to be resumed by 27 January 2026, and notice of the resumption given to creditors by 19 January 2026.

EVIDENCE

  1. In support of their application, the Administrators rely on:
(a) an affidavit sworn 25 September 2025 and the documents contained in exhibit AS‑1;

(b) a confidential affidavit sworn 25 September 2025 and the documents contained in confidential exhibit AS‑2;

(c) an affidavit sworn 15 January 2026 and the documents contained in exhibit AS‑3; and

(d) a confidential affidavit sworn 15 January 2026 and the documents contained in confidential exhibit AS‑4.

BACKGROUND

  1. The Companies operated as part of a group carrying on transportation and freight logistics services. The cause of the demise of the Companies’ business is not presently relevant.
  2. On 7 July 2025, the Administrators were appointed as voluntary administrators of the second to fifth plaintiffs. Administrators continued trading the business until 5 September 2025.
  3. On 25 September 2025, the plaintiffs commenced this proceeding.
  4. On 26 September 2025, Markovic J made orders in this proceeding, inter alia, extending the time for resuming the second meeting of creditors of each of the second to fifth plaintiffs until 27 January 2026 (First Extension Orders).

DISPOSITION

  1. The legal principles are well trodden and do not warrant repeating.
  2. I am satisfied that an extension of time to convene the second meetings of creditors promotes the objects of Part 5.3A of the Act; specifically s 435A(b), on the basis that it is not possible for the Companies or their business to continue in existence, and an extension would allow the Companies to be administered in a way that results in a better return for the Companies’ creditors and members than would result from an immediate winding up of the Companies.
  3. I am satisfied on the evidence presented to the Court, which includes a non-binding Heads of Agreement, that the ongoing administration of the Companies is likely to enable the sale of the Arndell Park Property, owned by the third plaintiff, on terms that are more favourable than those that might ensue in a more immediate and potentially discounted sale if the third plaintiff were wound up. I am satisfied further that the sale process is advanced, and that the outcome of this process will be a significant consideration in any proposed Deed of Company Arrangement.
  4. I accept that an extension will permit the Administrators to continue to sell the balance of the Companies’ assets, and allow them further time to resolve any contested claims to ownership of certain assets.
  5. Based on the evidence presented to the Court, given the size and scope of the Companies’ business (which was evidenced by summarised receipts and payments from 7 July 2025 to 12 January 2026 for the second to fourth plaintiffs), I also accept that the Administrators have acted with diligence since the First Extension Orders in:
(a) facilitating the sale of the Arndell Park Property;

(b) realising many of the Companies’ various assets;

(c) resolving various related-party and unrelated creditor claims, including the discharge of a secured debt owed to the National Australia Bank in the sum of $4,976,193.91; and

(d) discharging almost all priority creditor claims, including paying-out all but one of the second plaintiff’s employee entitlements, excluding superannuation.

  1. On that basis, I am satisfied that a deferral of approximately 2 months – which I consider reasonable given what is sought to be achieved in that time – would not materially prejudice the remaining creditors.
  2. I am edified in that conclusion by the fact that notice of the Administrators’ application was provided:
(a) to ASIC (on 15 January 2026), without objection; and

(b) to all known creditors of each of the second, third and fourth plaintiffs (on 14 January 2026), without substantive response to date.

CONCLUSION

  1. I make the orders in substantially the same form proposed by the Administrators, with an amendment to proposed Order 1, which has the effect of varying Order 3 of the Orders made by Markovic J on 26 September 2025, and substantive editorials to proposed Order 3.
  2. Having viewed Confidential Exhibit AS-4 to the affidavit of Adam Shepard sworn on 15 January 2026, I am satisfied that, given the commercially sensitive nature of the information contained therein, the orders sought are necessary to prevent prejudice to the proper administration of justice.
I certify that the preceding seventeen (17) numbered paragraphs are a true copy of the Reasons for Judgment of the Honourable Justice Younan.

Associate:

Dated: 16 January 2026
