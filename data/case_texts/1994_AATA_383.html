Re Croner Trading Pty Ltd and Commissioner of Patents [1994] AATA 383; (1994) 21 AAR 35 (1994) 37 ALD 235 (22 December 1994)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: CRONER TRADING PTY LTD v. COMMISSIONER OF PATENTS

No. V94/468

AAT No. 9927

Number of pages - 8

Patents

[1994] AATA 383; (1994) 21 AAR 35

[1994] AATA 383; (1994) 37 ALD 235

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

J. HANDLEY (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Patents - direction made by respondent's delegate that certain particulars filed in support of an opposition to amendment of patent application could not be relied on in the opposition - whether the opposition was "dismissed" or "determined" - jurisdiction of the Tribunal to review the direction.

Patents Act 1952 s.59

Patents Act 1990 s.104(4)

Administrative Decisions Judicial Review Act 1977

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975

Patents Regulations 1990 5.5, 5.6, 5.10, 22.26

Mobay Corporation v The Dow Chemical Company [1992] APO 25; (1992) 24 IPR 379

Norwood Industries Pty Ltd v MacBird FloraPrint Pty Ltd [1992] APO 39; (1992) 24 IPR 368

Ferocem Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Patents [1994] FCA 981; (1994) 28 IPR 243

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 21 November 1994

22:12:1994

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr McGowan

Solicitors for the Applicant: Chambers and Associates

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr Hess

Solicitor for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

It is decided that the Administrative Appeals Tribunal does not have jurisdiction to-

1. Determine whether the Commissioner acted within power to make the relevant direction; and

2. Review the direction made on 4 May 1994.

The application is therefore dismissed.

DECISION

J. HANDLEY The applicant applied for review of a decision of a delegate of the Commissioner of Patents made on 4 May 1994. The delegate directed that particulars 7-13 and 16-22 of the applicant's Statement of Grounds and Particulars filed in support of opposition to amendment of patent application 571375 were prima facie incapable of providing support to the grounds of opposition and could not be relied upon in the opposition.

2. At the hearing of the application Mr McGowan appeared on behalf of the applicant and Mr Hess appeared on behalf of the respondent.

The Facts

3. From a case management point of view, the opposition by the applicant in the patent application before the respondent, as may be gleaned from the T-documents, has an inglorious history.

4. Although the history of the application was not alluded to at the hearing of the application, the T-documents indicate that patent application number 571375 in the name of Concorde Trading Pty Ltd ("Concorde") was advertised by the respondent on 14 April 1988. On 13 July 1988 the applicant filed a notice of opposition pursuant to s.59 of the Patents Act 1952 ("the 1952 Act").

5. Thereafter a number of preliminary applications were heard and considered, however, on 17 June 1992, the opposition was heard and a written decision was subsequently delivered on 25 September 1992. The respondent's delegate decided that the opposition succeeded on the ground of non-compliance within the meaning s.40 of the 1952 Act as to the absence of novelty, but concluded that there was patentable subject matter disclosed in the specification and gave Concorde 60 days to propose amendments to its particulars.

6. By application dated 18 November 1992, Concorde proposed certain amendments to the complete specification and the proposed amendments were advertised as being open to opposition in the Australian Official Journal of Patents on 11 March 1993. In response, Croner filed on 11 June 1993 a Notice of Opposition to the allowance of the amendments under s.104(4) of the Patents Act 1990 ("the Act").

7. This precipitated Concorde making an application to dismiss the opposition pursuant to Regulation 5.5(1). The opposition was heard in Canberra on 15 December 1993 and the written decision was issued on 4 May 1994.

8. For the reasons set out in the decision, (T-1), the respondent's delegate decided, inter alia, that the opposition could not be dismissed, but gave directions that certain particulars (7-13 and 16-22) could not be relied on in the opposition.

9. On 31 May 1994, the applicant applied to this Tribunal for review of those directions.

The legislation

10. The Administrative Appeals Tribunal ("the AAT") has jurisdiction to review certain decisions of the Commissioner of Patents pursuant to Regulation 22.26 of the Patents Regulations 1990 ("the Regulations"). Regulation 22.26 provides that:-

"22.26 (1) In this regulation:

"decision" has the same meaning as in the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal Act 1975.

(2) Application may be made to the Tribunal for review of a

decision of:

(a) the Commissioner under the following provisions:

(i) paragraph 3.24(1)(b) ("Commissioner may request samples and

viability statement");

(ii) subregulation 3.25 (2) ("request for Commissioner's

certificate authorising release of sample of a micro-organism");

(iii) subregulation 5.5 (3) ("dismissal of opposition");

(iv)subregulation 5.6 (3) ("determination of opposition");

(v) subparagraph 6.2 (1)(b)(ii) ("prescribed period: grant of

standard patent");

(vi) subregulation 22.21(5) ("protection or compensation of

certain persons"); or

(b) the Patent Attorneys Professional Standards Board under

subregulation 20.21 (7) ("authorisation to bring proceedings

against a patent attorney"); or

(c) the Patent Attorneys or Disciplinary Tribunal under

subregulation 20.23 (2), (4) or (5) ("decision of Disciplinary

Tribunal")."

....

11. Other Regulations, relevant to this application and relied on by Counsel are as follows:

"5.5 (1) An applicant may:

(a) within 1 month of being served with a copy of a statement by

an opponent under subregulation 5.4 (1) ("filing of statement");

or

(b) if the complete specification in relation to an opposed

application is re-examined under subsection 97(1) of the Act

("re-examination of complete specifications")-within 1 month from

the day when the re-examination is completed under regulation 9.5

("completion of re-examination");

request the Commissioner in the approved form to dismiss the

opposition.

(2) As soon as practicable after a request is made, the

Commissioner must inform the opponent of the request having been

made.

(3) The Commissioner may dismiss the opposition whether or not the

applicant has requested dismissal of the opposition."

"5.6 (1) If the complete specification in relation to an opposed

application is re-examined under subsection 97(1) of the Act

("re-examination of complete specifications"), the opponent may,

within 1 month from the day when the re-examination is completed

under regulation 9.5 ("completion of re-examination"), request the

Commissioner in the approved form to determine the opposiiton.

(2) As soon as practicable after a request is made, the

Commissioner must inform the applicant of the request having been

made.

(3) The Commissioner may determine the opposition whether or not

the opponent has requested determination of the opposition."

........

"5.10 (1) The Commissioner may, on his or her own motion or on the

application of a party:

(a) give a direction that is not inconsistent with the Act or

these Regulations for the conduct of proceedings to which this

Chapter applies; or

(b) determine that a period prescribed in this Chapter (except in

regulation 5.3 ("filing of opposition") or paragraph 5.4 (1)(a)

("filing of statement")), is to be extended by such further period

as the Commissioner reasonably allows.

(2) The Commissioner may:

(a) on the application of a party in the approved form; and

(b) on such reasonable terms (if any) as the Commissioner

specifies;

extend the time within which the party may take a step prescribed

in this Chapter, not being a step that is taken under regulation

5.3 or paragraph 5.4 (1)(a).

(3) If section 107 of the Act ("amendments directed by

Commissioner: patent applications") applies to an opposed

application, the time within which a party may take a step

prescribed in this Chapter is extended by a period that is equal

to the period from the day when the applicant is given an

opportunity to be heard that is referred to in subsection 107(2)

of the Act to the end of the day when the decision is made under

107(4) of the Act whether or not the amendment is allowed.

(4) The Commissioner may:

(a) on the application of a party; and

(b) on such reasonable terms (if any) as the Commissioner

specifies;

permit the party to serve further evidence on the other party.

(5) The Commissioner must not give a direction under subregulation

(1) or grant an application under subregulation (2) or (4) unless

the Commissioner:

(a) if he or she proposes to grant an application by a party - is

reasonably satisfied that the other party has been notified of the

application; and

(b) if he or she proposes to act on his or her own motion -

ensures that the parties are notified of the proposed action; and

(c) in either case:

(i) gives the parties a reasonable opportunity to make

representations concerning the application or proposed action; and

(ii) is reasonably satisfied that a direction, an extension of

time or the serving of further evidence is appropriate in all the

circumstances."

The Respondent's Submissions

12. Mr Hess, who appeared on behalf of the respondent, objected to the Tribunal hearing the application. He submitted that regulation 22.26 of the Regulations empowered the Tribunal to only review decisions as provided under (2)(a-c) of regulation 22.26. He said that the direction sought to be reviewed was made pursuant to regulation 5.10 and was not reviewable by the AAT.

13. Having regard to regulation 22.26(2)(a), he submitted that the delegate did not "dismiss" or "determine" the opposition, (sub-regulation 5.5(3) and 5.6(3)) and that any challenge to the direction made by the delegate could only be made to the Federal Court.

14. He said that the Commissioner's power originates in ss.102 and 104 of the Patents Act 1990 and the delegate's direction in effect decided that the opposition by Croner proceed only upon lawful grounds, being grounds properly relevant to the opposition under sections 102 and 104.

15. Mr Hess submitted that the grounds directed not to be relied on in the opposition were not "lawful" within the meaning of ss.102 and 104 because those grounds did not deal with the amended specifications and some of the opposition grounds purported to deal with matters which had been the subject of findings of fact in previous proceedings and could not therefore be reopened.

16. In conclusion, Mr Hess did not submit that there were no rights available to Croner to challenge the direction made by the delegate, although he maintained that such a right was not available to it before the AAT. If the AAT were to review the direction, it would amount, he said, to the Tribunal giving itself a jurisdiction which is not conferred by the Regulations.

The Applicant's Submissions

17. On behalf of the applicant, Mr McGowan submitted that the direction of the delegate to limit the grounds of opposition amounted to either a partial dismissal or a partial determination of the opposition and was a decision reviewable by the AAT under Regulations 5.5(3) and 5.6(3) respectively, as originating in Regulation 22.26(2)(a)(iii) and (iv).

18. He submitted that the decision of the delegate was mischievously characterised as a direction because it had the substantial effect of striking out particulars of the opposition or dismissing the opposition to the extent that those particulars could not be relied upon. He further submitted that there is no power within the Regulations or the Act for the Commissioner to act by directing that grounds of opposition not be relied on and no such power may relevantly be found under Regulation 5.10 as was asserted by the respondent.

19. Whilst acknowledging that the delegate did not dismiss or determine the opposition outright (which would give the applicant a right of review by the AAT), the delegate limited or restricted the opportunity of the applicant to oppose the patent application to such an extent that it could properly be characterised as a virtual dismissal or a determination.

Conclusion

20. Some clarification is needed as to what was sought to be reviewed in this application.

21. The applicant sought, upon initial application, a review of the delegate's decision that certain particulars could not be relied on in the opposition. This pleading would suggest that the applicant challenged the direction made. On closer analysis, however, the applicant asserted an additional or alternative basis for review, namely the absence of power of the delegate to make the direction. Its statement of issues filed at the commencement of the hearing on 21 November said in part:

"4. The decision under review is not a direction within the

meaning of Regulation 5.10. This is because the direction here

was inconsistent with the regulations because there is no power

to make such a direction. Because the "direction" was made

without any power whatsoever and is not contemplated anywhere in

the Patents Act or its Regulations, it cannot be regarded as

something contemplated by Regulation 5.10. Putting it

differently, the "direction" was inconsistent with the regulations

in that it purported to achieve something by way of direction

(namely, the effective partial dismissal or determination of the

opposition) instead of by means of a normal opposition hearing

under, for example, Regulation 5.6 or 5.5 where all the

appropriate material must be considered in coming to the decision.

The delegate here made a specific finding that there were no

grounds to dismiss the opposition (pages 13.8, 14.9 and 15.1).

Where such findings have been specifically made it is incompetent

for the same decision maker in the same decision to reverse part

of that finding by the somewhat furtive use of disguising that

reversal in the shape of a "direction".

22. The applicant therefore claimed that the decision was made without power.

23. The respondent's case was that the decision was made within the power conferred by regulation 5.10 and that any review or challenge of the direction, if at all, could only be made to the Federal Court under the Administrative Decisions Judicial Review Act 1977, and not the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act").

24. The alternative submission of Mr McGowan, that the direction was made without power, is puzzling. If it was made without power, why did the applicant in its initial application to the AAT, and as an alternative argument in this hearing, seek to review it?

25. A party may conceivably argue absence of power to make a direction, but if unsuccessful, then seek to review the direction. I did not understand the applicant to have put its case on that basis. The issue of whether the delegate was empowered to make the direction made is, in any case, and for reasons which follow, not a matter reviewable by this Tribunal.

26. Both representatives indicated that, to their knowledge, this was the first time that a decision of the Commissioner or his delegate, to make such a direction had been challenged. Whilst there have been comments made by delegates of the Commissioner concerning the powers that are said to be open to them, no review external to the Commissioner's Office of those powers has been conducted (refer Mobay Corporation v The Dow Chemical Company [1992] APO 25; (1992) 24 IPR 379 and Norwood Industries Pty Ltd v MacBird FloraPrint Pty Ltd [1992] APO 39; (1992) 24 IPR 368. The Federal Court however in Ferocem Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Patents [1994] FCA 981; (1994) 28 IPR 243 did hear an appeal against a refusal to extend time pursuant to a power under Regulation 5.10).

27. The powers of review given to the AAT are found in regulation 22.26 of the Regulations, and are in respect of certain decisions only. Regulation 22.26 refers to review by the AAT of "decisions" of the Commissioner and decisions (of the Commissioner) are to have the same meaning as found in the AAT Act. Although "decisions" are not defined, s.3(3)(b) of the AAT Act says a decision "... includes a reference to" "...... giving ... a.... direction ...".

28. Regulation 22.26, whilst conferring power on the AAT to review decisions does so, and by specific reference, to decisions made under nominated regulations. There is no power, therefore, to review decisions of the Commissioner, or his delegates, at large.

29. Mr Hess submitted that the relevant direction was made under Regulation 5.10 and if that is right, it is not reviewable pursuant to Regulation 22.26 because it is not a decision therein referred. Mr McGowan said the direction, to the extent that it was made, amounted to a partial dismissal or partial determination and was therefore reviewable under Regulation 5.5 and Regulation 5.6 respectively.

30. I am satisfied that there has not been a dismissal of opposition within the meaning of sub-regulation 5.5(3) nor has there been a determination of opposition within the meaning of sub-regulation 5.6(3) of the Regulations. There has been no more than a direction to limit the grounds of opposition.

31. The "opposition" referred to at 5.5 and 5.6 is not the grounds of opposition pleaded to oppose or to act as a defence to the particulars of claim lodged by Concorde. The dismissal or determination of "opposition" is the decision made on the merits, by the delegate, having considered the application, the opposition and the evidence.

32. Both parties to these proceedings are clearly aware that the opposition proceeding will continue, indeed it was listed for hearing before the Commissioner on 16 September 1994. Prior to that date, the applicant applied to this Tribunal to have that proceeding Stayed, pending the hearing of this application. The respondent consented to the Stay of that proceeding. The making of the Stay Order by me, on 29 August, was in error just as it was not appropriate for the respondent, then, to consent to the making of that Stay Order.

33. I have decided that this Tribunal does not have jurisdiction to hear this application. It follows that the Tribunal did not have jurisdiction also to make the Stay Order.

34. If any further attention on this issue is warranted the parties need look no further than the reasons of the delegate at T-1 where at p.3 he in part said-

"I indicated that I was not willing to dismiss the opposition at

this early stage and would prefer to have full submissions from

both parties on the matter before invoking any discretionary

powers. I now find that while it is possible for the Commissioner

to dismiss an opposition without representation from the

applicant, the requirements of natural justice dictate the

opponent should be given an opportunity to be heard on the matter.

In the present circumstances, therefore, and making no assumptions

about the likely outcome of this action, I refuse the applicant's

request for this opposition to be dismissed forthwith."

At page 17 the same delegate said-

"I have found that the Patents Act 1990 makes specific provision

for the course of action followed by the opponent in this case.

Even though some of the opponent's particulars are prima facie

incapable of supporting their cause of action, there are no

grounds for dismissing the opposition".

35. The submissions of Mr McGowen with respect to the power of the AAT to review the decision therefore are not upheld.

36. The jurisdiction of this Tribunal to review decisions is, as recorded earlier, conferred by Regulation 22.26. Nothing in that regulation purports to give the AAT jurisdiction to review or hear an application with respect to the powers exercised by the Commissioner or his delegate in making a direction, as was made here. Any challenge to the exercise of the power of the delegate to make the relevant direction is more within the scope of judicial review, not merit review. Whilst I make no finding that the Federal Court is the venue to make such a challenge, I do find that the AAT is not the appropriate forum.

37. I have therefore decided that the AAT has no jurisdiction to -

1. Determine whether the Commissioner acted within power to make

the relevant direction; and

2. Review the direction made on 4 May 1994.

The application is dismissed.
