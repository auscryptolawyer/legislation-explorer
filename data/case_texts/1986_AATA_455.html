Bewley and Commissioner for Superannuation [1986] AATA 455 (7 February 1986)

Last Updated: 22 January 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Freedom of Information - Four separate applications for correction of records.

Freedom of Information Act 1982 - s49.

Re Kearney 55 ALR 545;

Applicant

COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION ) A85/103 and A85/107.

Re : PHILIP JAMES BEWLEY

Applicant

And： COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent DECISION

Tribunal : The Hon Sir William Prentice (Senior Member)

Mr R A Sinclair (Member)

Mr C J Stevens (Member)

Date : 7 February 1986

Place : Sydney

Decision : On Application A85/58:

(a) the Tribunal directs that in the Commissioner for Superannuation's minute of 18 January 1985 on the applicant's personal file, the word "determined" where secondly used in paragraph 2 thereof, be altered to read "considered";

(b) in all other respects the Tribunal affirms the decisions not to amend the records as sought.

On Application A85/96:

(a) the Tribunal directs that in the Commissioner's minute of 10 April 1985 on the applicant's personal file the phrase in paragraph 2 thereof "was conditional upon" be altered to read "was in expectation of being provided with";

(b) the Tribunal directs that an annotation be placed on the Commissioner's minute of 10 April 1985 on the applicant's file as to paragraph 5 thereof, that:

"Mr. Pearlman of Archives subsequently recollected as to this interview that Mr. Bewley was asked whether he preferred contract employment, fixed term or permanent, and that he did reply that for a number of reasons he preferred permanent employment and among these reasons was superannuation";

(c) in all other respects the Tribunal affirms the decisions not to amend the records as sought.

On Application A85/103:

The Tribunal affirms the decision not to make the amendment sought.

On Application A85/107;

The Tribunal directs that an annotation be placed on G. Wharton's file note of 13 March 1985 on the appellant's personal file, as to points 3 and 4 of paragraph 2 thereof, that:

"Mr. Pearlman of Archives subsequently recollected as to this interview that Mr. Bewley was asked whether he preferred contract employment, fixed term or permanent, and that he did reply that for a number of reasons he preferred permanent employment and among these reasons was superannuation".

On Application A85/68:

The Tribunal affirms the decision not to grant the applicant access to documents on the applicant's personal file, noted as folio 184 and folios 191 to 193.

(Sgd.) W T PRENTICE,

Sir William Prentice,

Presiding Member.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

Nos. A85/58, A85/68, A85/96,

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION ) A85/103 and A85/107.

Re ： PHILIP JAMES BEWLEY

Applicant

And : COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The applicant was from 1976 to 1978 a member of the Commonwealth Public Service. (Paragraph 3 of the Reasons for Decision in AAT matter A84/186 refers). From 1978 to 1983 he was a member of the Tasmanian Public Service. On 16 August 1983 he was permanently appointed to the Australian Public Service with effect from 1 September 1983, which involved a move with his family to Canberra and compulsory re-entry into the Commonwealth Superannuation Fund. Following medical examination on 15 August 1983. and his completion of the requisite form in which he stated that he had had a small melanoma removed from his shoulder then some three years before, a decision was made on 15 March 1984 by a Delegate of the Respondent to issue a Benefit Classification Certificate ("BCC") regarding him, with specification of the condition "history of superficial spreading melanoma". This decision to issue the BCC was set aside by a decision of the Tribunal on 18 October 1985. In the intervening period lengthy acrimonious discussion and correspondence had taken place between the applicant and the Commissioner's officers much of it relating to the Commissioner's desire to have Mr Bewley medically examined again, and Mr Bewley's attitude thereto.

  1. Mr Bewley contends that as a result, many erroneous details remain in documentary form upon his personal records. He has made four separate applications under s49 of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the FOI Act") for amendment of the records concerned. The desired amendments having been refused, there being also an application where access to documents was refused, he has made five applications for review of the decisions of refusal. All five applications were, as a matter of convenience, taken at the one hearing which extended over four days. Evidence and submissions were taken in each matter in turn and decisions reserved. The transcript covered some 233 pages.
  2. Determination of many of the questions raised has been rendered more difficult by the failure of the applicant at times to specify with clarity the actual amendments sought - many of the requests being in argumentative form and amounting to demands that reports be in effect re-written, so as to agree with the applicant's understanding of events as contrasted with that of the Respondent's officers.

In these Reasons the requests will be referred to by number with the Tribunal application number in brackets, for example, Request 1 (58), Request 1 (96)...

The Requests in Application A85/5B

  1. The requests for amendment covered by this application are fourteen in number. They are to be found in an attachment to Mr Bewley's letter of 28/2/85 (reproduced as folios T52/2-8 of the s37 documents). The Respondent's decisions thereon and his reasons are reproduced as folios T4/1-10 of those documents.
  2. Request 1 (in application 58) refers to a four page minute dated 18 January 1985 by the Respondent Commissioner to his Minister (T43/1-4) purporting to explain the circumstances of the issue of a BCC, and reasons for delay in answering Mr. Bewley's correspondence. It is stated in the minute that the Commissioner's Senior Medical Officer Adviser had "determined" as delegate of the Public Service Board that the health and fitness of Mr Bewley justified his acceptance for permanent appointment under the Public Service Act but "determined" in his capacity as Adviser that he did not meet the general medical standard for superannuation purposes. The document upon which the Commissioner constructed this portion of his minute was briefed as T5/1-4. It is dated 16/8/83. It is a Departmental form which has been filled in; in the boxes thereon which the Senior Medical Officer concerned could tick, he has selected that marked "determined" in relation to fitness to join the Public Service. But the boxes which are offered for ticking as to meeting the general medical standard for superannuation purposes, each refer only to "considering" not "determining". Plainly the form envisages that the determination as to superannuation has to be made by the Commissioner and not by his "Adviser", The Senior Medical Officer Adviser's recommendation was in the form of an opinion rather than a determination in the sense of a "decision".

We decide therefore that this portion of the Commissioner's minute is inaccurate and misleading; and that the word "determined" where secondly used in paragraph 2 thereof should be altered to read "considered".

  1. Request 2 (58) complained that the use of the phrase "as soon as administratively possible" in paragraph 7 of the same minute, was misleading because a delay of 7 months occurred between the medical examination concerned and appointment to the Public Service on the one hand, and the issue of a BCC on the other. We have perused the text of the Commissioner's letter to Mr Bewley of date even with the minute (T42/1), copy of which was sent to the Minister with the minute, and find it amplifies the reference complained of in paragraph 7 by explaining what was meant by the phrase in question. Accordingly, we do not agree that the phrase in its setting is misleading or calls for amendment.
  2. Request 3 (58) relates to paragraph 6 of the Commissioner's letter of 18/1/85 to Mr. Bewley (T42/2) in so far as it refers to a proposed further medical examination in July 1984 in connection with a request to revoke the BCC then in existence, and refers to the CMO's being asked "to report also on any other condition known by you" (i.e. the applicant) "but not disclosed at your initial examination". It is contended that the phrase misleads any reader of it by implying that there may have existed other medical conditions which the applicant had not reported.

On a study of the paragraph we feel that the words "which might have been" perhaps could for greater precision have been included before the allegedly misleading "known by you". However, we consider that the whole paragraph as it stands does not convey the suggestion that Mr Bewley in fact had had other conditions and had not disclosed them. We consider the paragraph appropriate to the briefing of the CMO on such a matter; and not therefore to call for amendment.

8. Request 4 (58) refers to paragraph 9 of the Commissioner's letter of 18/1/85 (T42/3) to Mr. Bewley in which reference is made to "a request to cancel the medical examination that had been arranged". and the fact that in the writer's view "with hindsight this is seen as a conditional request for cancellation of the appointment rather than an absolute request". It is contended that this would mislead for in Mr. Bewley's view there was no request made by him to cancel the appointment – it was cancelled by the Commissioner's officer.

A reading of the whole paragraph indicates to us that the Commissioner was stating the view he took of the effect of certain correspondence. It appears in our opinion to be a true statement of the views formed and not to be misleading as to such. In so far as the last sentence of the paragraph states that "the appointment had to be cancelled..." neither it nor the paragraph appear misleading.

9. Request 5 (58) challenges the correctitude of the use of the phrase "the general medical standard requires" and the reference to material justifying "the opinion for requiring the issue of a BCC" - the words "requires" and "requiring" being the gravamen of the request for amendment; in paragraph 14 of the Commissioner's eight page confirmation of 7/12/84 of the decision to issue a BCC regarding Mr Bewley (T36/8).

The paragraph in question indicates the circumstances when the general medical standard used in such considerations and decisions is met. We are of the opinion that as a matter of English usage it could have been better expressed. And we note that the insertion of the word "requiring" could be regarded as amounting to verbosity - the phrase "for the issue of a" (BCC) would have sufficed. We do not regard the paragraph however as misleading; and do not envisage s49 of the FOI Act as contemplating or requiring an exercise in the correction or improvement of English.

  1. Request 6 (58) also challenges the wording of paragraph 14 of the decision of 7/12/84 (T36/8). It is claimed that the phrase "based on statistics" should be eliminated, in that it infers that one advice is superior to another. We consider that it is necessary only to say that Dr. Carney's opinion, that in question (Exhibit 2 before the Tribunal), does on its face purport to be based on or rely on statistical analysis. We are unable to conclude a misleading element calling for correction, exists as claimed.
  2. Request 7 (58) refers to page 5 of a Departmental memo of 23/11/84 (before the Tribunal as T35/5) which comments on Mr. Bewley's letter of 3/9/84 to the Commissioner (T26/1-2). The contested paragraph is regarded as objectionable and misleading because it states (Mr. Bewley) "had complained to the Ombudsman and would not be medically examined until..." The particular letter from Mr Bewley had stated inter alia "I believe it not to be in my interests to undergo medical examination as requested by you until the Ombudsman has reported on my complaints or until you can give a satisfactory answer to questions put to you in my letters of 1st and 17th August 1984...Given that you continue to ignore my questions, and that you may not have had the benefit of the Ombudsman's conclusions before 28/9/84, the appointment with the CMO for that date should be cancelled. In the alternative, given answers to my queries and an amended instruction to the CMO the appointment will be kept by me..."

We consider the comment the subject of this request, to constitute a fair interpretation of the letter (T26/1-2) as amounting to a refusal to attend the arranged medical examination until stipulated conditions were fulfilled - those conditions being unacceptable to the Commissioner. We feel no alteration is called for.

  1. Request 8 (58). Mr. Bewley agreed did not need to be considered in the application.
  2. Request 9 (58) takes exception to the accuracy and propriety of the words "altogether dramatic" in the phrase "it is altogether dramatic to suggest that he has been deprived of a vital condition of employment for over 14 months" - in a Departmental minute of 23/11/84 (T35/9 in our documents). Mr. Bewley submits that he understood when he was being employed that he would get the usual conditions, and the issue of a BCC had a most serious effect on his career after he had left the Tasmanian Public Service,

We see no reason to regard the use of the phrase as other than the expression of an opinion honestly held and which might reasonably be included in a minute of the kind being made. As such, we do not think it can be or should be construed as misleading in the setting in which it occurs.

14. Request 10 (58) impugns the relevance of and inclusion of the sentence "so far as I know he did not seek information or assurances (which could not have been given contrary to the provisions of the Act) or request a decision before taking up the position" in the minute of 23/11/84 (T35/11). It is contended that the minute implies that Mr Bewley was in some way obliged to seek information or assurances about superannuation as a condition of service.

We do not agree that the sentence carries the imputation as asserted. In our opinion the document does not call for correction or annotation.

15. Request 11 (58) refers to a comment as to "Q.7" in the minute of 23/11/84 which refers to the matter of whether a medical examination as arranged would go ahead. The contention is similar to that raised under Request 4 (58).

We have had regard to the correspondence involved, and believe it to be unnecessary to say more than that the paragraph appears to reflect the Departmental officer's honestly-held view; one which we consider plainly open on the tenor of the letters written.

  1. Request 12 (58) also relates to a paragraph of the minute of 23/11/84 under the heading "Q.8" in which the officer states "However it is not true that no effective action was taken in the period specified". The request is based on Mr. Bewley's view of the word "effective" as contrasted with that of its user. The words occur in a statement of fact relative to the progress of various interconnected matters involving Mr. Bewley. There is nothing which suggests that it does not represent an expression of opinion genuinely and reasonably held. No misleading elements such as calls properly for a correction appears to exist in it in our view.
  2. Request 13 (58) relates to a short file note (T34/1) made on 21/11/84 by the Commissioner apparently in relation to Mr. Bewley's letter to him of 19/11/84 (T33/1). That letter included the following expressions:-

"Please discuss this letter with ASRR"; (Assistant Secretary Reconsideration and Review). "My initial reaction is that some parts of the letter leave Mr. Bewley open to action.

He may wish to discuss the matter with A-G's. Meanwhile other matters should proceed as expeditiously as possible."

Mr. Bewley submits that the file note is misleading (1) in that it implies that the Attorney-General's Department can advise individuals on private matters; and (2) because he contends his correspondence did not leave him open to action.

We consider the objection taken to the minute as completely misguided. It appears to the Tribunal to be quite clear that the Commissioner is instructing the particular Assistant Secretary to consider and possibly to discuss with the Attorney-General's officers, whether some action disciplinary or otherwise might or should be brought against the applicant. As such the minute appears to be quite within the competence of the Commissioner, not misleading and unexceptionable in our view.

18. Request 14 (58) which appears at T52/8, relates to a file note made by the Commissioner (reproduced at T29/1). Mr. Bewley submits that he was not informed by the Auditor-General's officer that a matter he had raised was not a matter for the Auditor-General, and that the Commissioner had created a fantasy for the file for his own purposes. The portion of the note objected to is the sentence "I understand that Mr. Bewley was informed that it was not a matter for the Auditor-General". The Tribunal is of the view that the note does not refer to Mr Bewley's "personal affairs" within the meaning of s48 of the FOI Act, and merely records the Commissioner's understanding of some factor on information given him. Amendment we consider, is not called for.

The Requests in Application A85/96

19. Request 1 (96) and four other such are to be construed from the applicant's letter of 24/4/85 to the Minister (before us as T78/1). All are related to the Commissioner's minute of 10/4/85 to that Minister (T5/1). Request 1 states that paragraph 2 of the Commissioner's letter is misleading and inaccurate in that it states:-

"Mr. Bewley claimed that his acceptance of permanent appointment with the Commonwealth Public Service was conditional upon being provided with full superannuation cover..."

Mr. Bewley contends that he has never made such a claim but that his acceptance of permanent appointment with the Commonwealth Public Service was made (as he had repeatedly stated) with the understanding that he would be eligible for full superannuation rights. After consideration of all the correspondence touching upon the point, to which we have referred, which included T30, T28/1, T49, T62 and T29 in the s37 documents, we accept Mr Bewley's statement in this regard. And accordingly we form the view that the sentence in question is incorrect and misleading, and calls for correction by the deletion of the words "was conditional upon" and insertion of "was in expectation of being provided with".

20. Request 2 (96) relates to paragraph 3 of the Commissioner's letter (T5/1) and submits it is incomplete and should have included a reference to the fact that the advertisements therein referred to did not include a notation as to possible restrictions.

We are of the opinion that the statement is a reasonable one and not such as calculated to mislead the Minister or any other person.

21. Request 3 (96) relates to paragraph 5 of the Commissioner's letter (T5/1) and submits it is inaccurate and misleading in that it states-

"In fact the subject of superannuation was not canvassed at the selection interview..."

Originally a Mr. Pearlman of Archives, who was present at the interview referred to in paragraph 4 of the Commissioner's letter, approved of the form of this paragraph 5. However, Mr. Pearlman subsequently asked the Commissioner's Advocate (Mr. Peters) at these proceedings, to say that he had now recollected that at the interview Mr. Bewley was asked whether he preferred contract employment, fixed term or permanent - "and that he did reply that for a number of reasons he preferred permanent employment and among those reasons was superannuation" (transcript at page 170 refers).

In effect both Mr. Bewley and Mr. Peters were in agreement on this point.

In the circumstances we consider the minute incomplete and misleading in view of Mr. Pearlman's reported subsequent recollection, and that an annotation should be annexed to the minute by way of explanation and correction.

22. Request 4 (96) which is before us in amended form as T4/3, complains that paragraph 7 of the minute of 10/4/85 (T5/2) being incomplete, should be amended to reflect the fact that the BCC was issued 7 months after employment commenced.

We note that the minute in question had attached to it a copy of a report dated 19 February 1985 from the Commissioner to the Minister. Having regard to that annexure and in particular paragraph 3 thereof (which is before us at T56/1) in which the matter of the 7 months delay is canvassed, we do not consider the particular paragraph misleading. It stands on its own as a statement of fact that BCC's are issued after employment is entered upon.

23. Request 5 (96) challenges (T4/33 refers), the propriety of the Commissioner's recommendation to the Minister in paragraph 8 of the minute of 10/4/85 that Mr. Bewley's claim should not be dealt with on an act of grace basis. It is suggested that an alternative should have been recommended.

The paragraph in question clearly represents the Commissioner's recommendation and is factually correct in that regard. No basis exists in our view for any requirement of alteration.

The Request in Application A85/103

24. The request herein relates to a telex to Mr. Bewley (before us as T7) concerning a proposed appointment for an examination of him by Dr. Brown on 22 April 1985. Inter alia the telex stated:-

"Your refusal to attend the examination for S16(6) purposes until after 24 April is noted. Arrangements will be made for an examination to take place after that date".

The request for alteration in the documentation of the telex is based on the assertion that at no time had Mr Bewley stated that he would refuse to attend a medical examination.

We have examined the correspondence and voluminous file of documents on this subject put before us, and find of particular relevance the telegram of 15/4/85 from Mr Bewley to the Commissioner. We conclude that the conduct depicted in that correspondence and documentation clearly indicate that Mr Bewley did in effect refuse to attend a medical examination appointed for S16 (6) purposes until after 24 April. We find the telex to be a correct statement and not to call for annotation or amendment,

The Request in Application A85/107

25. The request in this matter of 4/7/85 (exhibited as T4) relates to a file note by a GJ Wharton of 13/3/85, and in particular the alleged inaccuracy and misleading nature of points 3 and 4 of paragraph 2 thereof, which state that the matter of superannuation was not raised at the interview for employment of Mr Bewley. The note related to a meeting to discuss whether availability of superannuation was a condition of Mr Bewley's employment.

For reasons which appear in our paragraph 21 above, we consider that the points made are misleading and inaccurate in the light of the subsequent remarks by Mr. Pearlman and that an annotation by way of correction should be made to this file note.

The Request in Application A85/68

26. Certain other documents on Mr. Bewley's file to which access was sought, were not made available. By the time of the hearing of this matter the only documents to which access continued to be refused, were four pages, one numbered folio 184 being a typed file note by Mr. Carberry an officer of the Commissioner's staff, and three numbered folios 191 to 193 being handwritten notes made by Mr. Peters another of his officers. Copies of these documents have been made available in confidence to the Members constituting the Tribunal.

The ground eventually relied on to support the claim that these folios were exempt documents, was that provided for in s42(1) of the FOI Act - it being submitted that they were of such a nature that they would be privileged from production in legal proceedings on the ground of legal professional privilege.

  1. Mr. Carberry's file note was described in the schedule annexed to the affidavit of Mr. R. D. McKillop in evidence as "note of conversation with legal officer in Freedom of Information Branch, Attorney-General's Department, to obtain legal advice for the purpose of proceedings then pending". Mr. Peters' file note was described therein as "note of conversation with legal officer, Freedom of Information Branch Attorney-General's Department, to receive legal advice for the purpose of proceedings then pending". It is averred in Mr. McKillop's affidavit that the sole purpose of Mr. Carberry's telephone conversation which his note recorded was to obtain the aforementioned legal advice, and that the sole purpose of Mr. Peters' file note was the recording of that legal advice as given on the telephone at a later date. On their face the documents appear to be as averred by Mr. McKillop.
  2. The statement in the Tribunal decision Re Boehm and Department of Industry, Technology and Commerce V83/351 of 14 March 1985 (at paragraph 42) that "The Attorney-General's Department vis a vis another Commonwealth Department, is for this purpose" (that of attracting legal privilege to advice given) "in the same position as a private legal practitioner vis a vis a private client", for which the case of Re Kearney (55 ALR 545 at 551) was cited as authority, does not appear to have required modification following the decision on appeal in the Kearney case to the High Court where an exception to the normal legal privilege rule was being canvassed (Re Kearney [1985] HCA 60; 61 ALR 55 at 60-61). In Re Lianos and Department of Socjal Security, Deputy President Hall propounded a similar view (N83/893 of 19 February 1985 at paragraphs 8, 9 and 10), as did the Tribunal presided over by Davies, J., President, in Re Dwyer and Department of Finance (A84/163 of 11 November 1985) in which it was also enunciated that a document which would of its nature be privileged, retains that nature whether proceedings are or are not in foot in which that privilege may be claimed.

29. We are of the view that legal professional privilege still attaches to the documents concerned and that the decision to refuse access to them as being exempt documents under s42(1) of the Act should be affirmed.
