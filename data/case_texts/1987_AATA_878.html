ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - deduction claimed for losses carried forward -applicant had entered into a deed of assignment pursuant to the Bankruptcy Act 1966 - whether losses were incurred by the applicant or by the trustee under the deed of assignment

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s80 Bankruptcy Act 1966 s229

Re Thomas: ex parte Commissioner of woods and forests (1888) 21 QBD 380

In re Johnson (1946) 8 ATD 201

Respondent REASONS FOR DECIAION

  1. This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent disallowing an objection by the applicant to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of his taxable income for each of the years ended 30 June 1979 and 1980, of an amount claimed as a deduction in respect of losses carried forward,
  2. The applicant requested that each of the objections be referred to a Board of Review, and these requests were complied with by the respondent. By virtue of Section 223(1) of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 and subsection 189(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") that referral of these decisions on the applicant's objections is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of applications to the Tribunal for review of the decisions. As the issue was the same in respect of each year of income, both applications were heard together. No oral evidence was called by either party, but a number of documents were before the Tribunal.
  3. On 25 February 1975 the applicant and his wife entered into an agreement to carry on business in equal partnership as domestic appliance retailers. The business, which was carried on in a suburb of Melbourne, did not prosper, and on 18 October 1977 the partners executed a Deed of Assignment under Part X of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 in the following terms:

"the debtors convey and assignn to the Trustee all their divisible property within the meaning of Part X, of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 UPON TRUST to deal with the same in accordance with Part X of that Act for the benefit of the creditors of the Debtors and as to any surplus after paying in full the several debts and liabilities of the Debtors proved under this Deed together with the costs charges and expenses of or incidental to the execution of the trusts of this Deed (including the remuneration and expenses of the Trustee) UPON TRUST for the Debtors their executors administrators or assigns;"

  1. The business continued to be conducted for some time, and the applicant continued to take an active part in its operations. His house was sold in order to provide funds for the creditors, and a dividend of 33.45c in the dollar was paid. All stock., fixtures and fittings of the business were sold on 23 June 197S. The partnership income tax return for the year ending 30 June 1978 shows a loss of $11,655, borne as to $5827 by each partner.
  2. In his personal return for each of the years ending 30 June 1979 and 30 June 1980, the applicant claimed a deduction in respect of losses of the business carried forward pursuant to Section 30 of the Act. The relevant provisions of the Act read as follows at all relevant times:

"80(2) ... so much of the losses incurred by a taxpayer in any of the seven years next preceding the year of income as has not been allowed as a deduction from his income of any of those years shall be allowable as a deduction

.....

(4) Notwithstanding any other provision of this section, where, prior to the year of income, a taxpayer has become a bankrupt, or, not having become a bankrupt, has been released from any debts by the operation of an Act relating to bankruptcy, no loss incurred by him prior to the date on which he became a bankrupt or the date on which he was so released, as the case may be, shall be an allowable deduction.

(4A) Where, in the year of income, a taxpayer pays an amount in respect of a debt incurred by him in one of the seven years next preceding the year of income, being a year in which the taxpayer incurred a loss to which the last preceding sub-section applies, the amount paid by the taxpayer in respect of the debt shall, subject to the next succeeding sub-section, be an allowable deduction to the extent that it does not exceed so much of the debt as the Commissioner is satisfied was taken into account in ascertaining the amount of the loss."

Sub-section (4B) is not relevant to this matter.

  1. No issue arises in respect of the losses incurred prior to 18 October 1977, when the business was clearly being conducted by the partnership. However, counsel for the applicant submitted, that after 18 October 1977 the business continued to be, in effect, operated by his client, who was accordingly entitled to the benefit of sub-section 80(4A) in respect of the losses of the business incurred after that date.
  2. We are unable to accept this submission. Sub-section 2 29(1) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966, which is contained in Part X of that Act, provides;

"229. (1) Subject to this section, the due execution by a debtor of a deed of assignment that is entered into in accordance with this Part and complies with the requirements of this Part operates to vest in the trustee forthwith, upon the trusts and for the purposes of the deed, all the divisible property of the debtor."

The definition of "divisible property" in sub-section 137(1) of that Act, taken with Section 116, to which that provision in effect refers, is clearly such as to comprehend within its terms the applicant's interest in his house and his share of the assets of the business. The proceeds of the sale of the house, the profits of the business and the losses of the business must accordingly have been received or borne, as the case might be, by the trustee.

8. In Re Thomas: ex parte Commissioner of Woods and Forests (1888) 21 QBD 380 at 383, Cave J considered the effect of the Eng1ish Bankruptcy Act 1883, and said:

"The property is transferred to the trustee, and the remedies against the person and property of the debtor are taken away; in substitution for those remedies there is a vesting of the property in the trustee, so that he may dispose of it for the benefit of the creditors, and attached to that is the right to disclaim where the property is onerous. Looking at these sections as a whole, their effect is that the creditors cannot any longer get at the property of the debtor, there being substituted a right to prove against the estate, which is vested in the trustee. It would be outrageous to say that the Crown has any right which would interfere with the vesting of a Crown lease in the trustee for the benefit of the creditors."

That passage was relied on by Clyne J in the Federal Court of Bankruptcy in In re Johnson (1946) 8 ATD 201, which concerned a deed of assignment. His Honour said (at page 202):

The trustee as the statutory assignee of the bankrupt's property has a statutory duty to distribute this property amongst the creditors who have proved their claims and these creditors have a corresponding right to receive a ratable share of this property.

Having regard to these considerations I think it would be palpably absurd to say that a trustee who pays or is bound to pay dividends to creditors of a bankrupt who have proved for wages was an employer of these creditors and that in paying them these dividends he was paying them wages.

It is equally absurd to suggest when a trustee of a deed of assignment under Part XII pays dividends to creditors who have proved for wages that he is paying wages to these creditors or that he is an employer of these creditors.

9. It is clear, therefore, that after 18 October 1977 the business was not being carried on by, or for the benefit of, the applicant, and he was not in a position to claim a deduction under sub-section 80(4A). Accordingly, the objection decisions under review will be affirmed.
