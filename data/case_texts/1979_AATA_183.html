Hare and Commissioner for Superannuation [1979] AATA 183 (30 November 1979)

Last Updated: 12 March 2007

Superannuation - reference to previous Reasons for Decision - application brought on for further hearing - retirement of Member constituting Tribunal -proceeding completed by remaining Members - making of final decision.

Superannuation Act 1976, ss 66, 154, 184

Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978, s 77

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s 20, 25, 43

Re: G.A. J. HARE

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. 78/12027

AAT Decision No 318

Mr R.K. Todd (Senior Member)

Dr M. Glick (Member)

30 November 1979

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No.78/12027

Re: G.A.J. HARE

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

DECISION

The Tribunal decides to set aside the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation taken on his behalf by his delegate on 27 February 1978 and confirmed upon re-consideration under s 154 of the Superannuation Act 1976, which decision was that the benefit entitlement of the applicant on his ceasing to be an eligible employee on 22 November 1977 by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity should be determined under s 66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976, and further decides to remit the matter of the benefit entitlement of the applicant to the Commissioner for re-consideration in accordance with a recommendation that he consider the applicability or otherwise of the provisions of s.184 of the Superannuation Act 1976.

DATED this 30th day of November 1976.

(Sgd) R.K. Todd

(R.K. Todd) Senior Member

(Sgd) M. Glick

(M. Glick)

Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No.78/12027

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: G.A.J. HARE

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

1.We refer to the previous Reasons in this matter which were published on 11 May 1979 and which should be read in conjunction with these Reasons.
2.On 24 September 1979 Mr WJ Smith, who was one of the Members appointed to hear this application for review, and who joined in the previous hearing and in the earlier Reasons for Decision referred to above, resigned as a Member of this Tribunal upon his taking up another appointment. No order has been made pursuant to s20 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 for the reconstitution of the Tribunal, and upon the matter coming for further hearing both the applicant and Mr Huttner, who appeared for the Commissioner for Superannuation, agreed that the proceeding might be completed by the Tribunal constituted by the remaining Members.
3.The application for review came on for further hearing as a result of a letter dated 23 October 1979 from the Commissioner for Superannuation to the Registrar of this Tribunal. That letter should be set out in full omitting only formal parts:
"1 refer to the Decision and Reasons handed down by the Tribunal in this matter on 11 May 1979, and pursuant to the leave therein reserved do now hereby request that the Tribunal reinstate this Application for Review for further hearing.

2. Having carefully considered both the findings of fact and the reasons expressed in the Reasons for Decision, and on the question of the applicability of section 184 of the Superannuation Act 1976, I do hereby consent to the Tribunal setting aside my decision that, by reason of the benefit classification certificate deemed to be in force under sub-section 184(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 immediately before Mr Hare's retirement, specifying the condition "hypertension", Mr Hare's entitlement should be determined under sub-section 66(2).

3. Should the Tribunal agree to this course of action it is proposed, immediately after the decision has been so set aside, to invoke the provisions of sub-section 184(5), and to issue pursuant to those provisions a benefit classification certificate specifying "nervous condition" as the condition which would have been specified in the benefit classification certificate deemed to have been in force under sub-section 184(2) immediately before Mr Hare's retirement on 22 November 1977, had he not furnished false information in connection with the medical examination which he underwent for the purposes of the superseded Act, on 27 February 1975.

4. Upon the issuing of that benefit classification certificate Mr Hare's benefit entitlement will then fall to be determined pursuant to sub-section 66(2), on the basis that the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a condition specified in the said benefit classification certificate, or by a condition connected with such a condition."

4. As apparent from the Reasons for Decision previously given, the decision handed down by the Tribunal on 11 May 1979 was not a final decision in terms of s43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act. It was the intention of the Tribunal that no such final decision in respect of the only Application for Review then properly before the Tribunal should be made until the Commissioner had considered his attitude to the applicability or otherwise of s184 of the Superannuation Act 1976. The question of such applicability was not, and still is not, before the Tribunal, having regard to the provisions of s154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 and of s25 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act.

5. The position now however, as the letter set out above indicates, is that the Commissioner has determined to apply the provisions of s184(5) of the Superannuation Act 1976, that is to say the provisions of s184 as amended by s77 of the Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978 (No.17 of 1978). We were assured by Mr Huttner that everything possible has been done to ensure that the speediest action will now be taken to progress the matter, but it is conceived that a decision to apply the provisions of s184 could hardly be taken given the continued existence of the previous decision. We emphasise that we make no comment whatsoever upon the correctness or otherwise of the Commissioner's decision to apply 184(5), but we consider that the time has now come to proceed to set aside his former decision. This will leave the Commissioner free to make a new decision as to the applicability or otherwise of s184(5). If he does decide in favour of its applicability the applicant will be equally free to challenge that decision. In order to achieve these objectives the form of decision which we propose is that the decision of the Commissioner which is the subject of this application for review be set aside and that the matter of the benefit entitlement of the applicant be remitted to the Commissioner for re-consideration in accordance with a recommendation that he consider the applicability or otherwise of the provisions of s184 of the Superannuation Act 1976.

6. It may be noted that while the Commissioner's letter referred to above has expressed his consent to the Tribunal setting aside the previous decision, there is no power conferred by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act upon the Tribunal to set aside a decision by consent. For this reason the decision which we make is a decision of the Tribunal made by it upon the grounds and for the reasons set out in the Reasons for Decision published on 11 May 1979.

7. It is a matter for regret that the benefit entitlement of the applicant remains unresolved. In terms however of the complex of applicable statutory provisions in relation to the procedures for administrative review, and of the particular circumstances of this case, there is in our opinion nothing that the Tribunal can do other than to make the decision which we have outlined above.
