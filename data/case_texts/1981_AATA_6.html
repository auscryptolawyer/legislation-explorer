Re Alice Homsy and Commissioner of Superannuation [1981] AATA 6 (23 January 1981)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re : ALICE HOMSY

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. N80/98

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mr Ewart Smith (Senior Member)

Dr M. Glick (Member)

Mr L.G. Oxby (Member)

CATCHWORDS

superannuation - determination of retirement benefits - retrospectivity of benefit classification certificate _ absence of time limit on power to issue benefit classification certificate _ physical or mental conditions specified _ whether incapacity caused by or connected with condition specified in certificate _ no jurisdiction to make order for reinstatement _ irrelevance of advice as to probable superannuation benefits

Superannuation Act 1976 _ s.16(10), s.66(2), s.154(4)

HEARING

CANBERRA

23:1:1981

ORDER

The Tribunal hereby affirms the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation made on 18 August 1980, confirming the decision of his delegate made on 15 November 1979:

(i) to issue under sub-section 16(10) of the Superannuation Act 1976, in respect of Mrs Homsy, a benefit classification certificate specifying the condition 'nervous disorder'; and

(ii)that he was of the opinion under paragraph (c) of sub-section 66(2) of the Act that the incapacity which was the ground for Mrs Homsy's retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a mental condition specified in the benefit classification certificate or by a mental condition connected with such condition.

DECISION

1. The applicant, Mrs Alice Homsy, has appealed to this Tribunal against the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation made on 18 August 1980 under s.154(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 confirming the decision of his delegate made on 15 November 1979:

(i) to issue under sub-section 16(10) of that Act, in respect of Mrs Homsy, a benefit classification certificate specifying the condition 'nervous disorder'; and

(ii)that he was of the opinion under s.66(2)(c) of that Act that the incapacity which was the ground for Mrs Homsy's retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a mental condition specified in the benefit classification certificate or by a mental condition connected with such condition.

2. The relevant facts, established by the documents contained in Exhibits T1, T2, T3 and T5 (received in evidence without objection) and by the evidence of Mrs Homsy herself and Dr Thomas Gerald Quinn, a Commonwealth Medical Officer called as a witness by the Respondent, are set out in the succeeding paragraphs.

3. Mrs Homsy, who is now 32 years of age, commenced employment with the Health Insurance Commission in July 1975. The facts provided by the Respondent (Exhibit T3, p.7) show that Mrs Homsy commenced to be a contributor under the Superannuation Act as from 2 August 1976. She became an 'eligible employee' within the meaning of that Act with effect from that date. She continued in employment as a Claims Officer with the Health Insurance Commission, and continued to be a contributor, and an eligible employee, under the Superannuation Act until she was retired on the ground of invalidity on 13 February 1979.

4. Mrs Homsy underwent a medical examination for the purposes of permanent appointment on 19 August 1976 and the usual Medical Report Form was completed for the purpose of this examination (Exhibit T5, pp.15-18). In answer to question 1(b) in the Applicant's Statement of Personal Medical History on this form, i.e., whether she is or has ever suffered from 'Mental or nervous conditions', Mrs Homsy answered 'Yes', and in Part C, which required applicants to furnish details in all 'Yes' answers in questions on personal history, stated simply '75. needed rest'. In answer to question 12(c) concerning absences from work through sickness over the previous 2 years, Mrs Homsy answered 'Yes', gave the duration as 4 weeks, and again detailed the absence in Part C as 'needed rest'. She also stated in answer to question 12(d) that she was (at the time of completing the form) in good health.

5. Dr Craggs, the Commonwealth Medical Officer who carried out the examination, on the basis of his examination and the applicant's answers to the questions, expressed the opinion that she met the general medical standard for permanent appointment and Dr Hobart, Adviser to the Commissioner for Superannuation, on the basis of Dr Craggs' report, signed a recommendation that he considered the applicant met the general medical standard.

6. We mention here that Dr Quinn produced the clinical notes of Mrs Homsy's examination on 19 August 1976 and those notes (by Dr Craggs) stated 'No psychiatric history'. Mrs Homsy stated that she did not volunteer any information as to her prior nervous problems other than as mentioned in paragraph 4 above. She said that she was not asked. We are therefore not clear as to what was the basis of Dr Craggs' note referred to above. However, as the issue before the Tribunal did not turn on the source or accuracy of this note, but rather on Mrs Homsy's medical condition at the time of the original examination, we did not pursue the matter further.

7. The Statement of Mrs Homsy's Sick Leave (Exhibit T5, p.23) shows that she was absent from work on account of 'Nerve disorder', 'Depression', 'Nerve Depression' and 'Nervous Disorder' for various periods, the earliest of which commenced on 26 November 1975 and the latest of which ended on 20 December 1978. No absence, referable to any nervous problem, was recorded during 1976, the year in which she was medically examined for permanent appointment. The absences referred to were supported by Medical Certificates; in particular, the certificate of Dr Roger Kiely in respect of her absence of some 2 weeks commencing 26 November 1975 (subsequently extended for a further 2 weeks) stated Mrs Homsy to be unfit for work as she was found to be suffering from 'Nervous disorder'. Dr Kiely is a specialist psychiatrist practising in Macquarie Street, Sydney and Mrs Homsy has been under his care, to use his own words in his letter of 18 March 1980 to the Director of Health (NSW Division), before us as Exhibit T5, p.50, '... almost continuously since 1973'.

8. On 23 January 1979, a form initiating a medical examination for continued employment or retirement on invalidity grounds was signed by Mrs Jane Styles, who was Personnel Officer (Salaries), in respect of Mrs Homsy (Exhibit T5, p.20) and she was accordingly examined by a Commonwealth Medical Officer (Dr Thomas Gerald Quinn), who reported (Exhibit T5, p.21) that she was suffering from 'Schizo-Affective Paranoid Psychosis' and that she was unfit for continued employment and should be retired on the ground of invalidity. Dr Quinn also indicated his opinion that Mrs Homsy 'may, in due course, partially or completely recover and become fit for some form of Commonwealth employment'.

9. The date determined for Mrs Homsy's retirement was, with her agreement, fixed at 13 February 1979. She signed an application for invalidity retirement pension on 9 February 1979.

10. Following consideration of this application, a Delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation, on 15 November 1979, issued a benefit classification certificate under s.16(10) of the Superannuation Act 1976 in respect of Mrs Homsy. This certificate stated that the Delegate was not satisfied that Mrs Homsy's incapacity which was the ground for retirement was not caused, and was not substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions that existed at the time she became an eligible employee, or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or conditions. The relevant condition was specified in the certificate as 'Nervous Disorder' (Exhibit T5, p.26). The Delegate then recorded, under s.66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 (Exhibit T5, pp.27-28), that he was of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for the retirement of Mrs Homsy was caused or substantially contributed to:

(i) by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the benefit classification certificate; or

(ii)by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or conditions.

The physical and mental conditions specified in the benefit classification certificate were identified by the Delegate as 'Nervous disorder'. The Delegate also stated (Exhibit T5, p.28) that in forming his opinion he had had regard to the condition 'Schizo-Affective Paranoid Psychosis" stated to be the condition contributing to the incapacity in the Medical Officer's report on her fitness for continued employment and the comments of the Adviser to the Commissioner for Superannuation, who agreed with the Medical Officer.

11. It is convenient at this stage to set out the relevant provisions of s.16 and s.66 of the Superannuation Act 1976:

'16(1) In this section, 'approved medical practitioner' means a medical practitioner approved by the Commissioner for the purposes of this Act.

(2) The Commissioner may, for the purposes of this section, require a person (other than a person to whom section 184 applies) who proposes to become or becomes an eligible employee to undergo, within such period as the Commissioner specifies, such medical examination or examinations by an approved medical practitioner or practitioners as the Commissioner determines.

(3) A report or reports of the result or results of the medical examination or examinations shall be furnished to the Commissioner.

(4) The Commissioner shall consider the report or reports, and such other matters (if any) as the Commissioner considers relevant, and, if he is of the opinion that the person is not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with a physical or mental condition or conditions referred to in the report or reports, to continue to be an eligible employee until the person attains his maximum retiring age, the Commissioner shall issue a benefit classification certificate to that effect, being a certificate in which the relevant condition or conditions is or are specified.

(5) In the application of this Act to an eligible employee at any time, a reference in this Act to a benefit classification certificate that is in force in respect of an eligible employee shall, in the case of an eligible employee whose period of contributory service would, if he ceased to be an eligible employee at that time, be not less than 20 years or an eligible employee who has attained his maximum retiring age, be read as not including a reference to a benefit classification certificate that has been issued in respect of him.

......

(10) Where _

(a) a person who is an eligible employee dies or ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity _

(i) before he has undergone a medical examination or examinations as required under sub-section (2);

(ii) if he has undergone such an examination or examinations _ before the report or reports of the result or results of the examination or examinations has or have been considered by the Commissioner;

(iii)if the report or reports has or have been considered by the Commissioner _ before the Commissioner has reached a decision as to whether he should issue a benefit classification certificate in respect of the person under sub-section (4); or

(iv) if the Commissioner has decided to issue a benefit classification certificate in respect of the person under sub-section (4) _ before the certificate has been issued; and

(b) the Commissioner is not satisfied that his death or the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement, was not caused, and was not substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions of the person that existed at the time the person became an eligible employee, or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions,

the Commissioner shall issue in respect of the person a benefit classification certificate in which there is or are specified the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person which, in the opinion of the Commissioner, existed at the time the person became an eligible employee and, for the purposes of this Act, the certificate shall be deemed to have been in force in respect of the person immediately before his death or retirement.

(11) Where _

(a) a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of death or retirement on the ground of invalidity;

(b) his period of contributory service is less than 20 years and, on the day on which he ceases to be an eligible employee, he has not attained his maximum retiring age;

(c) a benefit classification certificate is not in force in respect of the person immediately before his death or retirement or, if a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of him at that time, the Commissioner is not of the opinion that the death or incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions of the person specified in the certificate, or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions; and

(d) the Commissioner is satisfied _

(i) that, at or in connexion with a medical examination which the person was required to undergo under this section, the person failed to furnish any information required to be furnished by him or furnished false information; and

(ii)that, if the person had not failed to furnish that information or had not furnished that false information, there would have been in force in respect of the person, immediately before his death or retirement, a benefit classification certificate in which there would have been specified the physical or mental condition or conditions which caused, or substantially contributed to, the death or retirement, or a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions,

the Commissioner shall issue in respect of the person a benefit classification certificate in which there is or are specified the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person which, in the opinion of the Commissioner _

(e) in a case where a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of the person at the time he dies or retires _ would have been the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person specified in the certificate at that time; or

(f) in any other case _ would have been the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person specified, at the time the person dies or retires, in the benefit classification certificate that would have been in force in respect of the person,

if the person had not failed to furnish that information or had not furnished that false information and, for the purposes of this Act, the certificate shall be deemed to have been in force in respect of the person immediately before his death or retirement.

(11A) Where a benefit classification certificate is in force in respect of a person in respect of whom a benefit classification certificate is issued under sub-section (11), the first-mentioned certificate shall be deemed to have been revoked by the second-mentioned certificate with effect from the time immediately before the death of the person or the retirement of the person by reason of which the second-mentioned certificate was issued."

'66(1) Subject to sub-sections (3), (3A) and (4) of this section and to section 79, where a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity before attaining his maximum retiring age then, except in a case where sub-section (2) of this section applies, the person is entitled _

(a) if the person does not make an election under section 68 or 69 _ to invalidity pension in accordance with section 67 and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, a lump sum benefit in accordance with that section;

(b) if the person makes an election under section 68 _ to invalidity pension, and a lump sum benefit, in accordance with that section; or

(c) if the person is entitled to make an election under section 69 and makes such an election _ to a lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 69(2) and, where t e person has paid supplementary contributions, an additional lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 69(3).

(2) Subject to sub-sections (3), (3A) and (4) of this section and section 79, where _

(a) a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity before attaining his maximum retiring age;

(b) there was in force in respect of the person, immediately before the person's retirement, a benefit classification certificate; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the certificate or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions,

the person is entitled _

(d) where the period of contributory service of the person is not less than 8 years _

(i) if the person does not make an election under section 71 or 72 _ to invalidity pension in accordance with section 70 and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, a lump sum benefit in accordance with that section;

(ii) if the person makes an election under section 71 _ to invalidity pension, and a lump sum benefit, in accordance with that section; or

(iii) if the person is entitled to make an election under section 72 and makes such an election _ to a lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 72(2) and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, an additional lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 72(3); or

(e) where the period of contributory service of the person is less than 8 years _ to a lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 73(2) and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, an additional lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 73(3).

....."

12. Sub-section (1) of s.66 provides for the normal benefits applicable to a person who ceases to be an eligible employee (Mrs Homsy was of course such an employee) by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity and, these benefits being substantially greater than the benefits available under sub-section (2) of the section, it would be in Mrs Homsy's interests to be treated as a person to whom sub-section (1) was applicable. Sub-section (2) applies where a benefit classification certificate was in force in respect of the person concerned immediately before the person's retirement and the Commissioner (or his delegate) is of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the certificate or by a physical or mental condition connected with such a condition or conditions; in a case where the contributory service of the person is less than eight years (which is the position in respect of Mrs Homsy), a lump sum as provided by s.73 is the only benefit, that is to say, no pension is payable.

13. Turning to s.16, it will be seen that sub-section (2) enables the Commissioner to require a peson who proposes to become or becomes an eligible employee to undergo, within such time as the Commissioner specifies, such medical examination or examinations by a medical practitioner approved by the Commissioner as the Commissioner determines. Sub-section (4) requires the Commissioner to consider the report or reports of the examination or examinations, and such other

matters as he considers relevant, and, if he is of the opinion that the person is not likely, by reason of or for a reason connected with a physical or mental condition or conditions referred to in the report or reports, to continue to be an eligible employee until the person attains his maximum retiring age, to issue a benefit classification certificate to that effect and to specify in it the relevant condition or conditions.

14. Sub-section (1) of s.16 imposes on the Commissioner a duty to issue a benefit classification certificate in respect of a person where, inter alia:

(a) that person, being an eligible employee, ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity before the report of the result of his or her medical examination under sub-section (2) has been considered by the Commissioner; and

(b) the Commissioner is not satisfied that the incapacity which was the ground for the retirement was not caused, and was not substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition of the person that existed at the time the person became an eligible employee, or by a physical or mental condition connected with such a condition.

15. It is clear from sub-section (10) not only that a benefit classification certificate can be issued after the eligible employee's retirement, but that it is required to be issued once the circumstances specified in the sub-section exist. One of these circumstances is that the result of the original medical examination has not been considered (whatever the reason) before the employee's retirement and the Commissioner is not satisfied of the matter set out in paragraph (b) of the sub-section.

16. In fact, whilst Mrs Homsy had undergone the medical examination required on entry to the Superannuation Scheme on 19 August 1976 (see paragraph 4 above), neither the Commissioner nor his delegate had, before her retirement on 13 February 1979, considered the report of the Commonwealth Medical Officer on that examination (Exhibit T1, p.1, para.3(b)) and the benefit classification certificate in respect of her was issued after her retirement, on 15 November 1979, as noted in paragraph 10 above. Mr Wilde, representing the Commissioner, explained the reason for the delay as due to the very great number of cases requiring consideration following the introduction of the new (1976) Superannuation Scheme. (In the first year of operation of the new Act, the Tribunal was informed some 27,000 new contributors had to be processed and this number was at least matched in each of the succeeding years.)

17. The only time limit the Act appears to impose on the issue of a benefit classification certificate is that the employee shall not have completed 20 years' contributory service, and the specific provisions included in s.16 seem to the Tribunal to prevent any construction of the section that would read into it a requirement that the Commissioner's powers must be exercised within a reasonable time. The absence of any other time limit means that even though an eligible employee may have answered all questions honestly to the best of his knowledge and belief and even though he may have continued in the employment of the Commonwealth or a Commonwealth authority for a very long period (up to 19 years) in the belief and expectation that he was covered for normal superannuation benefits, he could be deprived of those benefits by the issue of a benefit classification certificate under sub-section (10). The Tribunal notes in this connection the limitations placed by section 84 of the Life Insurance Act 1945 on the avoidance of a life policy on the ground of an incorrect statement in the proposal where the statement was not fraudulently untrue.

18. Mr Beltrami stated in his opening address (Transcript p.54) that Mrs Homsy 'did not wish to challenge the fact that she did suffer from this nervous condition prior to being admitted into the superannuation scheme' and Mrs Homsy, in her evidence, admitted that she had been attending Dr Kiely for many years for a nervous disorder. Her explanation of not providing more details of her illness in the form was that there was only one line for the details and she was not asked for more. From Mrs Homsy's own evidence, the report of Dr Kiely on 18 March 1980 (Exhibit T5, p.50) and the evidence of Dr Quinn, particularly as to the history of her illness given to him by Mrs Homsy (Transcript, p.77), the Tribunal is satisfied that Mrs Homsy was, at the time she became an eligible employee, suffering from a nervous disorder of a schizo-affective nature. On the basis of the report of Dr Quinn on his examination of Mrs Homsy in January 1979 for continued employment or retirement on medical grounds, in which he recommended her retirement on medical grounds, specifying her condition as Schizo-Affective Paranoid Psychosis and her incapacity as 100%, confirmed by Dr Quinn's evidence before the Tribunal, the Tribunal is of the opinion that the Commissioner had no option but to confirm the issue of a benefit classification certificate under s.16(10) of the Superannuation Act in respect of Mrs Homsy by his delegate specifying the condition 'nervous disorder' and to confirm the opinion of the delegate under s.66(2)(c) of the Act that the incapacity which was the ground for her retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by the mental condition specified in the certificate.

19. Because of the course the proceedings took, the Tribunal considers it desirable to comment briefly on some matters raised by Mrs Homsy in her evidence.

20. Much of her evidence was directed to establishing that she had been truthful in disclosing her medical condition for the purposes of her medical examination for permanency in August 1976. That would be a matter in issue if the benefit classification certificate had been issued under s.16(11). However, as already mentioned, the certificate in Mrs Homsy's case was issued under s.16(10) and whether she made full disclosure of her pre-existing illness is not relevant to such a certificate. Mr Beltrami, in his closing address, drew attention to difficulties he saw caused by the vagueness of the questions seeking information in the form required to be completed for the initial medical examination, especially in the case of persons who may be suffering from psychiatric disorders. We say no more than that in Mrs Homsy's case the structure of the form did seem to cause some difficulties and Mr Beltrami's comments in the transcript at pages 108-9 will no doubt be studied by the appropriate authorities.

21. From Mrs Homsy's evidence, it seems that her chief complaint was that she was induced to agree to seek retirement on medical grounds by advice given to her that she would receive a 70% pension. However, what she may have been led to expect in the way of superannuation benefits can have no bearing on her entitlement under the Act and Mr Beltrami conceded this. For this reason, the Tribunal did not consider it necessary to pursue this aspect further.

22. Finally, in her formal appeal to this Tribunal, Mrs Homsy sought as an alternative to the grant of a pension, reinstatement as an officer of a Federal Department at the same work level as she had before retirement. This Tribunal has, of course, no jurisdiction to make an order in relation to that matter in these proceedings.

23. For the reasons set out above, the Tribunal affirms the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation.
