Taylor and Commissioner of Taxation (Taxation) [2026] ARTA 543 (10 April 2026)

Applicant: Mark Anthony Taylor

Respondent: Commissioner of Taxation

Tribunal Number: 2025/6722

Tribunal: General Member McBride

Place: Brisbane

Date: 10 April 2026

Decision: The Applicant’s application under section 32 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) requesting the Tribunal grant a stay order of the Respondent’s decision to refuse to release the Applicant from his tax-related liabilities under Division 340 of Schedule 1 of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 (Cth) is refused.

Statement of Reasons

  1. On 26 September 2025, the Commissioner of Taxation (the Respondent), made a decision to disallow Mr Mark Anthony Taylor’s (the Applicant) objection under s.14ZY of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 (TAA).The Applicant lodged in the Administrative Review Tribunal (the Tribunal) a Tax Questionnaire (Taxation Review) dated 10 March 2025.[1] On 7 December 2025 the Applicant lodged an Application seeking a review of the Commissioner’s objection decision not to release the Applicant from his tax related liabilities on the grounds of ‘serious hardship’ under section 340-5 TAA, matter number 2025/6722 (the Substantive Proceedings).
  2. On 7 December 2025, the Applicant lodged an application requesting the Tribunal grant an order staying implementation of the Respondent’s decision (Stay Order).
  3. That application stated the grounds for the request were:
    "I have requested that the Australian Taxation Office afford me debt release on my back tax liability, as release will allow me to continue trading and will still provide tax revenue now, and into the future. I do not believe it is in the best interest of the Taxation Office, or anyone, for me to be forced into bankruptcy over health issues. I cannot see that my bankruptcy would benefit taxpayers, as bankruptcy would result in me paying no tax at all. Not this back debt, or any ongoing, as my only choice would be to retire and go on the pension. The net result, to the Australian public, will be a loss. Whereas some help now will enable me to pay ongoing tax and not be a burden on the pension system. Thank you for your consideration and I am available to provide any further information you require"
  4. On 13 March 2026, this matter was constituted to me.

THE JURISDICTION ISSUE

  1. The Tribunal is a creature of statute. Section 12 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) (ART Act) limits the Tribunal's jurisdiction. A decision is only reviewable by the Tribunal if an Act or a legislative instrument makes a provision for a decision made under that Act or legislative instrument to be reviewed by the Tribunal.[2] Absent such express conferral, the Tribunal, being an administrative decision-making body, has no inherent jurisdictional entitlement or general powers of review.
  2. Under Section 17(1) of the ART Act a "person whose interests are affected by a reviewable decision may apply to the Tribunal for review" of that decision.
  3. Ordinarily, a decision made by the Respondent is made under a "taxation law"[3] that enlivens the Tribunal's jurisdiction to review the Respondent's decision under Part IVC of the TAA. Part IVC sets the procedure by which a Taxpayer may lodge an objection with the Commissioner and if dissatisfied with that decision ultimately file an application for review in the Tribunal or appeal to the Federal Court of Australia.
  4. Not all decisions made by the Respondent under a taxation law are capable of being objected to under Part IVC. A Hardship Application for release of eligible tax liabilities is provided for under s340-5 TAA: that section is in the following terms:

“340 – 5 Release from particular liabilities in cases of serious hardship

Applying for release

(1) You may apply to the Commissioner to release you, in whole or in part, from a liability of yours if section 340 - 10 applies to the liability.

(2) The application must be in the approved form.

(3) The Commissioner may release you, in whole or in part, from the liability if you are an entity specified in the column headed "Entity" of the following table and the condition specified in the column headed "Condition" of the table is satisfied.

Entity and condition
Item
Entity

an individual
you would suffer serious hardship if you were required to satisfy the liability

(4) If the Commissioner:

(a) refuses to release you in whole from the liability; or

(b) releases you in part from the liability;

nothing in this section prevents you from making a further application or applications under subsection (1) in relation to the liability.

Notification of the Commissioner's decision

(6) A failure to comply with subsection (5) does not affect the validity of the Commissioner's decision.

(7) If you are dissatisfied with the Commissioner's decision, you may object against the decision in the manner set out in Part IVC.”

  1. The Respondent's decision not to release the Applicant from his tax related liabilities under section 340-5 TAA is a reviewable decision under section 340-5 (7) TAA. The substantive application is listed for a case management direction hearing before a registrar in June 2026.

The Stay Request

  1. In addition to the substantive application for review, the Applicant also filed a 'Stay request' in the Tribunal. Although not articulated on the form, presumably the Applicant intended to request the Tribunal consider whether it might exercise its powers to stay any possible recovery action by the Respondent while the substantive proceedings were before the Tribunal.
  2. For the benefit of the Applicant who is self-represented, as a general rule, a reviewable decision the subject of an application to the Tribunal, continues to operate while the Tribunal undertakes its review.
  3. Section 32(2) ART Act provides an exception to that rule and gives the Tribunal a discretion to stay or otherwise affect the operation of the decision if the Tribunal considers that it is desirable to do so for the purposes of ensuring the effectiveness of the review.
  4. The operation of s 32(2) ART Act is modified in taxation cases. The starting position is section 14ZZM TAA. That section permits the Respondent to continue to recover a tax liability while a taxpayer seeks review of his objection decision in the Tribunal subject to a limited exception for small business taxation assessment decisions. (emphasis added)
  5. Section 14ZZM is in the following terms
    “14ZZM Pending review not to affect implementation of taxation decisions

    (1) The fact that a review is pending in relation to a taxation decision does not in the meantime interfere with, or affect, the decision and any tax, additional tax or other amount may be recovered as if no review were pending.

    (2) However, the application of subsection (1) in relation to a small business taxation assessment decision is subject to an order made under subsection 32 (2) of the ART Act.

    Note: An order made under subsection 32(2) of the ART Act is subject to section 14ZZH (limitation on orders staying or affecting reviewable objection decisions relating to small business taxation assessment decisions) of this Act.”

  6. Sections 14ZZB (5) TAA provides that section s 32 ART Act does not operate in relation to a reviewable objection decision or an extension of time refusal decision. Notwithstanding subsection (5), subsection 14ZZB (6) provides a carve out in relation to an objection decision that relates to a small business taxation assessment decision, subject to the modifications set out in subsection 14ZZH TAA.
  7. Section 14ZZH (2) TAA permits the Tribunal to make a stay order in very limited circumstances. Section 14ZZH (2) is in the following terms:
    “(2) Despite the ART Act, the ART must not make the order unless the party satisfies the ART that, when considered in the context of both the particular circumstances of the decision under review and the overall taxation system the application for review and the request for making the order are not frivolous, vexatious, misconceived, lacking in substance or otherwise intended to unduly impede, prejudice or restrict the proper administration or operation of a taxation law"
  8. For the benefit of the Applicant the words “frivolous” and “vexatious” are legal terms of art, used to describe legal proceedings or applications that lack merit or have no prospects of success.
  9. The 'circumstances' referred to in the exception to s14ZZH TAA are only considered if the Tribunal is satisfied the reviewable objection decision relates to a small business taxation assessment decision.

What is a small business entity?

  1. Section 328-110 Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (Cth) (1997 Act) is in the following terms:
    “(1) You are a small business entity for an income year (the current year), if:
    (a) you carry on a business in the current year; and

    (b) one or both of the following applies:

    (i) you carried on a business in the income year (the previous year) before the current year and your aggregate turnover for the previous year was less than $ 10 million;

    (ii) your aggregate turnover for the current year is likely to be less than 10 million”

What is a small business taxation assessment decision?

  1. Section 14ZQ TAA defines a "small business taxation assessment decision" as a taxation decision that is:
    "(a) an assessment of tax-related liabilities (as defined in s 225-1 schedule 1), relating in whole or in part to carrying on a business and;

    (b) made in relation to a small business entity (within the meaning of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997).”

The review decision is not a small business taxation assessment decision

  1. The substantive application to the Tribunal concerns the Applicant's request the Respondent exercises his discretion to discharge the Applicant’s existing taxation liability, and the Respondent's decision to refuse an application for release of liabilities. The objection was not an assessment made by the Respondent. The substantive proceedings, do not relate to a small business taxation assessment decision. It follows; the exception in s 14ZZB (6) TAA is not engaged.
  2. The Respondent submits and I accept that this means the Tribunal does not have the power to order a stay of the Respondents decision under s 32 ART Act.

CONCLUSION

  1. The Application for a stay is refused
