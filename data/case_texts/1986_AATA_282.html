Re Katrina Le Breton Rumley and Commissioner of Superannation [1986] AATA 282 (26 September 1986)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: KATRINA LE BRETON RUMLEY

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNATION

No. N86/116

AAT No. 2900

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

E. Smith (Senior Member)

Ms L.S. Rodopoulos (Member)

Dr D.B. Travers (Member)

CATCHWORDS

SUPERANNUATION - officer of Parliament House Construction Authority formerly officer of Australia Council - whether eligible employee for purposes of Superannuation Act - whether "employed in a permanent capacity" - whether excepted by Superannuation (Eligible Employees) Regulations.

Words and Phrases - "eligible employee" "employed in a permanent capacity"

Superannuation Act 1976, s. 3(1) "eligible employee", permanent employee", temporary employee", ss. 45, 46

Superannuation (Eligible Employees) Regulations, reg. 4

Re Montgomery and Commissioner for Superannuation (1983) 3 AAR 69; 7 ALN N239

McClelland v. Northern Ireland General Health Services Board (1957) 2 All ER 129

Clark v. Independent Broadcasting Co. Ltd (1974) 2 NZLR 587

HEARING

CANBERRA

26:9:1986

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

The applicant has sought review of the decision of the delegate of the respondent, confirming the decision conveyed to the Parliament House Construction Authority ("the Construction Authority") by letter dated 30 July 1984, that the applicant became an eligible employee under the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Superannuation Act") on 27 September 1983, when she took up employment with that Authority.

2. The applicant had, before accepting employment with the Construction Authority, been a full-time officer of the Australia Council for several years but had not been required to contribute to the Commonwealth Superannuation Fund while an officer of the Australia Council. This was because she was given the option, when the Superannuation Act came into operation (1 July 1976), of being treated as an eligible employee by virtue of the Superannuation (Eligible Employees) Regulations, reg. 4(b), but had not so opted. For reasons specified in her letter dated 30 October 1983, she wished to continue her employment with the Construction Authority without participating in the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme.

3. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged in pursuance of s. 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, as Exhibits T. 1 to T. 14. It also received in evidence as Exhibit 1 additional documents submitted by the respondent which were numbered AT. 1 to AT. 13. In the circumstances it did not hear any oral evidence, the matter being dealt with by submissions from Mr R. Refshauge, for the applicant, and Mr R. Davidson, for the respondent, by common consent.

4. The applicant was employed by the Construction Authority in pursuance of s. 28 of the Parliament House Construction Authority Act 1979 from and including 27 September 1983. She resigned from her office with the Australia Council as from the close of business on 26 September 1983. The applicant did not take any steps in relation to becoming a member of the Commonwealth Superannuation Fund; in fact, there seems to have been a misunderstanding on the matter as the Construction Authority was seemingly under the impression that she was already contributing to the Superannuation Fund by virtue of her appointment with the Australia Council. That was not the case and the matter of superannuation, therefore, was not the subject of discussions that took place between the applicant and officers of the Construction Authority. The applicant has claimed that she was not informed at the time of negotiating the new appointment with the Construction Authority that she would not be able to continue the option she had since 1975 of not participating in the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme. She thought that in her very special circumstances compulsory joining should be waived. The respondent's delegate took the view that she was an eligible employee and as such was bound to contribute to the Fund under the Superannuation Act.

5. The definition of "eligible employee" in s. 3(1) of the Superannuation Act, so far as relevant, reads as follows:

"eligible employee" means -

(a) a person who, immediately before the

commencement of this Act, was an employee

for the purposes of the superseded Act

and has continued to be, and is, such an

employee;

(b) a person who is a permanent employee;

(c) a person who is a temporary employee and

is specified in a direction given under

section 11, 12 or 13;

(d) a person who is the holder of a Statutory

Office and is specified in a direction

given under sub-section 14(1) in relation

to that statutory office;

(e) a person -

(i) who is a temporary employee;

(ii) who is the holder of a statutory

office to which he was appointed on a

full-time basis; or

(iii) who is the holder of a statutory

office to which he was appointed

otherwise

than on a full-time basis and who is

specified in a direction given under

sub-section

14(2) in relation to that

statutory office,

and who, immediately before the

commencement

of the employment by reason of which

he is such a temporary employee or his

appointment to that office, was an

eligible employee by virtue of another

paragraph of this definition or a

previous application of this paragraph;

. . . . . . . . . . .

but does not include -

(i) . . . . . . . .

(j) any person included in a prescribed class

of persons;

(k) . . . . . . . .

. . . . . . . . . . .

The definitions of "permanent employee" and "temporary employee" in s. 3(1) are also relevant. They provide as follows:

"permanent employee" means -

(a) a person who is an officer for the

purposes of the Public Service Act; and

(b) any other person employed by the

Commonwealth or by an approved authority

in a permanent capacity,

but does not include a part-time employee who

is not an approved part-time employee.

"temporary employee" means -

(a) a person who is an employee for the

purposes of the Public Service Act; and

(b) any other person employed by the

Commonwealth or by an approved authority

otherwise than in a permanent capacity,

but does not include -

(c) a part-time employee who is not an

approved part-time employee; or

(d) a person who is engaged or appointed for

employment outside Australia only, not

being a person who is included in a class

of persons approved by the Minister, in

writing, for the purposes of this

definition.

Section 28 of the Parliament House Construction Authority Act 1979 reads, so far as relevant, as follows:

28. (1) The Authority may appoint such officers

and engage such employees as are necessary for the

performance of its functions under this Act.

(2) The terms and conditions of service or

employment (in respect of matters not provided for

by this Act) are such as are, subject to the

approval of the Public Service Board, determined by

the Authority.

6. Mr Refshauge submitted, late in his argument, that the applicant was a person included in a prescribed class of persons within the meaning of the exception in paragraph (j) of the definition of "eligible employee" and is consequently not eligible or required to contribute to the Superannuation Fund. As, if this submission is upheld, it would be unnecessary to examine the other submissions made on her behalf, it is convenient to deal first with this submission.

7. In order to determine whether the applicant is a person within a prescribed class of persons, it is necessary to examine the Regulations made under the Superannuation Act prescribing such persons. The relevant Regulations are contained in the Superannuation (Eligible Employees) Regulations, Statutory Rules 1976, No. 140, as amended, and include in regulation 4 several classes of persons who are to constitute a prescribed class of persons for the purposes of paragraph (j) of the definition of "eligible employee". There is only one class of persons relevant to the applicant, namely, that contained in paragraph (b) of regulation 4. Paragraph (b) prescribes persons who -

"(i) immediately before the commencement of the

Act, were employed by an approved Authority in

a permanent capacity and have continued to be,

and are, such employees;

(ii) immediately before the commencement of this

Act were not employees for the purposes of the

superseded Act;

(iii) immediately before the commencement of this

Act, were not members of a superannuation

scheme applying to persons employed by the

approved authority and have not, at any time

since the commencement of the Act been, and

are not, such members;

(iv) have not requested that they be treated as

eligible employees."

The reference in the regulation to the superseded Act is a reference to the Superannuation Act 1922.

8. This provision would make the applicant's case, if the regulation were to be construed as applying to persons who were employed immediately before the commencement of the Superannuation Act 1976 (1 July 1976) by an approved authority and who at some time after that date move to permanent appointment with another approved authority. We do not think, however, that such a construction can be sustained. Sub-paragraph (i) is concerned with persons employed in a permanent capacity by an approved authority before the commencement of the Superannuation Act who continued to be employees of that authority after the commencement of that Act. We are of the view that "such employees" must be read as "employees employed in a permanent capacity by that authority" and while she certainly answered that description after the commencement of the 1976 Act while she remained an officer of the Australia Council, she ceased to do so when she took up employment with the Construction Authority. She resigned from her employment with the Australia Council immediately before her employment by that Authority, and at that point her status changed. Section 3(3) of the Superannuation Act, which deems an eligible employee who ceases to be such but who immediately thereafter again becomes an eligible employee, not to have so ceased, does nothing, of course, for a person who was not an eligible employee before ceasing, as was the applicant's case.

9. Mr Refshauge submitted that the decision requiring the applicant to contribute to the superannuation Fund under the Superannuation Act is a matter for the Construction Authority, not the Commissioner for Superannuation, by virtue of the terms and conditions under s. 28 of the Parliament House Construction Authority Act 1979. While we agree that superannuation may in general be said to be a term and condition of employment, and may in some circumstances be a purely contractual matter, where, as here, there is a statute providing for superannuation for Commonwealth employees - including Commonwealth authorities - and the relevant authority has been prescribed as an "approved authority" under that Act, as in the case of the Construction Authority (see Statutory Rules 1980, No. 286), we see the provisions of the Superannuation Act as mandatory. If an employee of the Construction Authority comes within the definition of "eligible employee" in s. 3(1) of that Act, then that employee must contribute as required by ss. 45 and 46, unless excepted by some action authorized by the Superannuation Act itself. That position must yield, of course, to any contrary intention expressed in the Parliament House Construction Authority Act 1979; but we do not read s. 28 or any other provision of that Act as indicating that the Superannuation Act is not to apply according to its terms. The mere fact that it was enacted after the Superannuation Act does not determine the issue. But the terms and conditions applicable to the applicant under that Act, and indeed the Act itself in the special circumstances of this case, must be examined to determine whether the applicant can be said to have been employed "in a permanent capacity".

10. Dealing with this matter, Mr Refshauge argued that, while a termination date for the Parliament House Construction Authority Act (known as a "sunset clause") was not included in the Act itself, the intrinsic nature of the undertaking meant that it necessarily had a limited life. This affected the nature of the employment that could be offered to persons employed by it and such employment could not be said to be "permanent". Nor, he said, could the applicant continue to be employed under the Act after the construction of the new Parliament House had been completed. Whatever they were called, persons employed under the Act were in truth temporary employees and only came within the Superannuation Act after a direction under s. 13 of that Act and no such relevant direction had been given. Mr Refshauge cited a number of authorities as to the meaning of "permanent" in other situations, but interesting as they are we cannot accept them as having application in relation of employment of the kind in question in the present case. We refer only to the case of McLelland v. Northern Ireland Health Services Board (1957) 2 All ER 129. That was a case involving the right to terminate employment, and consequently not wholly applicable to the present issue. He quoted from the judgments of Lord Goddard and Lord Keith. The former said, at p. 133 -

"That an advertisement offers permanent

employment does not, in my opinion, mean thereby

that employment for life is offered. It is an

offer, I think, of general as distinct from merely

temporary employment, that is that the person

employed would be on the general staff with the

expectation that, apart from misconduct or

inability to perform the duties of his office, the

employment would continue for an indefinite period.

But, apart from a special condition, in my opinion

a general employment is always liable to be

determined by reasonable notice."

Lord Keith said, at p. 137 -

"I turn, then, to the question of the

permanence of her appointment . . . . . . From August 16

to September 3 neither the Board nor the appellant

were under any conditions other than those

specified in the Board's letter of July 29, and I

think that counsel was right in his contention for

the Board that, during the period and subject to

proving her fitness for the post during a

probationary period of service, the appellant held

her post under a contract of general hiring. That

is to say, she held a permanent post in the sense

of an appointment on the Board's permanent staff of

indefinite duration. . . . . . . It is quite impossible,

in my opinion, to hold that, during this period,

she was holding a lifelong appointment."

It should be noted that Lord Keith was in the minority in this case, but the quoted remarks are consonant with the views of the majority.

11. Mr Refshauge drew the inference from McLelland's case that the appointment of a permanent officer was not a lifelong or interminable appointment but a general hiring for an indefinite period. That, he submitted, was clearly not the situation here, where the employees were employed for a limited period of time which must end in or about 1988. The concluding date may not be specifically ascertained, he submitted, but it was nevertheless not indefinite.

12. It may be observed that McLelland's case was cited with approval by Moller J. in Clark v. Independent Broadcasting Co. Ltd (1974) 2 NZLR 587 In that case, Moller J stated -

"I am of the view that, properly construed,

this contract means no more than that the

plaintiff's employment was, to use Lord Goddard's

words, 'general as distinct from merely temporary'

or to use Lord Tucker's words in McLelland's case,

that the plaintiff would be 'on the regular

establishment staff' of the defendant."

13. The question of permanency in relation to the Superannuation Act was considered by this Tribunal, presided over by Deputy President Jennings Q.C., in Re Montgomery and Commissioner for Superannuation (1983) 3 AAR 69; 7 ALN N239 Each party relied on statements made in that case to support their arguments. Mr Refshauge saw the applicant as being akin to the research assistant to a Royal Commission posed by the Tribunal as an example of a temporary employee; Mr Davidson (for the respondent) quoted paragraphs 24, 25 and 26, referring in particular to the Dictionary definition in the Shorter Oxford Dictionary and the question of the employment lasting indefinitely. The relevant paragraphs of Re Montgomery read:

". . .

The question as to whether his employment was "in a

permanent capacity" therefore turns on the meaning

of that phrase in the context of the Act.

"Permanent" is defined in the Shorter Oxford

Dictionary as:

"Lasting or designed to last indefinitely without

change; enduring; persistent; opposite to

temporary."

"Temporary" is defined as:

"Lasting for a limited time, existing or valid for

a time only; transient; made to supply a passing

need."

25. If there is no reference to the period of time

for which an employment by the Crown is to last the

inference is that it will last indefinitely but not

of course, forever. The right of the Crown or for

that matter any employer, to dismiss an employee

without cause is well understood in the community

subject of course to reasonable notice and

statutory rights, if any. But when an employment

is described as permanent it is generally either a

reference to the normal retiring age, intervening

sickness or misconduct excepted, or at least to the

expectation of the employee that unless exceptional

circumstances supervene he or she may anticipate

continuous employment for an indefinite time.

26. If on the other hand an employment is

described as temporary it is a reference to some

specific period of time or some future event which

will obviously result in the termination of the

contract. For example, a research assistant

engaged to assist a Royal Commission would

obviously treat his employment as being bound to

conclude when the Commissioner delivered its

report."

14. It was also stated in Re Montgomery, at paragraph 20:

"20. Whether the applicant was engaged "in a

permanent capacity" within the meaning of that

expression as it occurs in the definitions of

permanent and temporary employees in s. 3(1) of the

(Superannuation) Act is a question which must be

determined by reference to the manner in which the

applicant was appointed and the terms of his

engagement in so far as they can be inferred from

the facts set out or referred to in paragraphs 14

to 19 inclusive."

15. We are of the view that in deciding whether the applicant was a "permanent employee" as defined in s. 3(1) of the Superannuation Act the words "in a permanent capacity" must be considered in their context and as a qualitative, and not merely a quantitative, expression. The hall-mark of an officer for the purposes of the Public Service Act (see paragraph (a) of the definition of "permanent employee" in s. 3(1) of the Superannuation Act) is not merely that he may - unless retrenched or dismissed or he resigns or is invalidated out of the Service - remain until he reaches his retiring age: he is afforded different conditions of service, including rights to be considered for promotion and rights of appeal and provisions with respect to leave of various kinds not available to temporary employees. And he is ordinarily subject to requirements e.g. a period of probation, to which employees are not subject because their services can ordinarly be dispensed with more readily. Commonwealth Authorities (including the Construction Authority) have followed this pattern in providing employment for their employees, dividing them into at least two categories, namely, officers, on the one hand, and employees, on the other. We turn now to the circumstances and the incidents of the applicant's employment with the Construction Authority.

16. In our view, there is no doubt that the applicant was appointed as an officer under s. 28(1) of the Parliament House Construction Authority Act 1979. Although s. 29 provides for the engaging of consultants, we do not see the applicant's employment falling within that provision. The terms and conditions applicable to her as an officer are set out in terms and conditions approved by the Public Service Board for the National Capital Development Commission. This strange situation came about by reason of the adoption of those terms and conditions, as the correspondence indicates, "as an interim measure". The interim measure appears to have been allowed to develop into a more permanent state, but, reading those terms and conditions mutatis mutandis, as we think we must, they constitute the terms and conditions applicable to employment by the Construction Authority, reading references to the Commission as references to that Authority. Those terms and conditions make separate provision for officers and employees, and in relation to the former provide them with the trappings and the rights and obligations broadly corresponding to those of an officer of the Australian Public Service. The applicant's appointment, it may be noted, followed an advertisement in the National Press seeking applications for the position of Curator Grade 2. The conditions of service were stated as follows:

"Similar to those applying to the Australian Public

Service including eligibility to contribute to the

Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme. In certain

cases superannuation, service for long service

leave, sick leave and recreation leave are

transferable."

The applicant had discussed with Mr Fowler of the Construction Authority, before final acceptance of the position as Curator, the terms of the duty statement applicable to her, as a result of which a new duty statement was prepared. She accepted her appointment by letter dated 22 August 1983 in the following terms:

"Following my letter of 10 August 1983 and

discussions with Mr Ian Fowler, I wish to confirm

my acceptance of the position of Curator Grade 2 to

the Parliament House Construction Authority.

I attach the final duty statement as agreed to by

the Authority and myself. I look forward to

commencing work at the end of September."

She was not required to serve a period of probation in view of her earlier service with the Australia Council (see Exhibit 1, AT 13/1). As mentioned earlier, the question of her constributing to the Superannuation Fund did not form part of the discussions, due to misunderstanding on both sides as to her position in respect of superannuation.

17. While the view of the Construction Authority as to whether the applicant was obliged to contribute to the Superannuation Fund cannot itself determine the question, it is significant that, in a letter to the Commissioner for Superannuation dated 12 September 1985 (Exhibit 1, T.13/1) and signed by the Administrative Officer, it was stated, in part, as follows:

"Reference your letter NC5111 of 11 June 1985 it is

confirmed that Miss Rumley was permanently

appointed to the Authority. Staff appointed under

the provisions of Section 28 of the Parliament

House Construction Authority Act 1979 are permanent

staff."

It is thus clear that the Construction Authority itself regarded the applicant as having been appointed to their permanent staff.

18. We have concluded from an examination of the terms and conditions applicable under the Parliament House Construction Authority Act 1979 that there is ample evidence that a distinction is drawn between officers (their "permanent staff") and employees (their "temporary staff"). The distinction is generally along the lines of the Public Service Act 1922 and of other Commonwealth Authorities. The terms and conditions deal with the matter of superannuation in condition 8, which reads as follows:

"8. (1) Officers of the Commission (Authority)

appointed on or after the 1st July 1976 are

required to constribute under the provisions of the

Superannuation Act 1976.

(2) Other persons who may apply to contribute

to the scheme are:

(a) Employees who have been employed for a

continuous period of not less than 12

months whose employment is likely to

continue for a further period of at least

3 years.

(b) Employees whose permanent appointment is

expected within 12 months.

(c) Persons employed under contract for a

period of at least 12 months.

(d) Persons appointed to statutory offices

for a term of not less than one year."

This, however, must, in the view we take, be regarded as declaratory only. As mentioned earlier, eligibility and requirement to contribute under the Superannuation Act is a matter to be determined under the Superannuation Act, not the terms and conditions of employment, and once the employing body has been prescribed as an approved authority, liability to contribute under ss. 45 and 46 follows if the person concerned is employed "in a permanent capacity". In particular, we note that Condition 8(2) merely reflects in a somewhat shorthand way the substantive provisions of sections 11, 12, 13 and 14 of the Superannuation Act. They do not attempt to do more than give a general indication of the classes of person who may become eligible to contribute for Superannuation.

19. We respectfully agree with what Lord Goddard and Lord Keith said in McLelland's case and accept that permanent employment does not mean employment for life. While the House of Lords was dealing with different statutory provisions in that case, it may be accepted that permanent employment ordinarily means of indefinite duration. That view is supported, in relation to the Superannuation Act, by Mongomery's case. Looking at the applicant's employment, we see it clearly fitting into the category of "employment in a permanent capacity" within the definition of "permanent employee" and thus falling within the definition of "eligible employee" in s. 3(1) of the Superannuation Act, unless the Parliament House Construction Authority Act itself is of such a transitory nature as to make that impossible. We accept that that Act will some day be repealed and that the Construction Authority probably will have completed its task, say, by 1989. That cannot, however, be said with complete certainty and the Construction Authority could well be kept in existence longer. The provisions of the Commonwealth Employees (Redeployment and Retirement) Act 1979 make it less likely that an officer such as the applicant will cease to hold office, if she decides to stay in Commonwealth employment, though we do not rely only on that Act. We do not consider that the Construction Authority is comparable with a Royal Commission, the example given in Montgomery's case; the Construction Authority is in a very different category. It is a statutory body established for a public purpose with a considerable staff and a very substantial project to complete. We do not see the fact that at some future but as yet undetermined time it will cease to exist as precluding its officers from qualifying as having been employed in a permanent capacity. We mention that if it were not possible for the Authority to employ officers in a permanent capacity, then the regulation prescribing it as an approved authority for the purposes of the Superannuation Act would not be valid insofar as it applied to the definition of "permanent officer". We are not persuaded that that is the position.

20. We wish to add only that, having arrived at the conclusion that we have, this Tribunal does not have any discretion to exempt the applicant from contributing to the Superannuation Fund. The Superannuation Act requires contributions to be made to the Fund by officers of an approved authority employed in a permanent capacity. The decision under review will accordingly be affirmed.
