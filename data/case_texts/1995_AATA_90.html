Helen Teresa Greenwood (Nee Mamczuk) and Commissioner of Superannuation [1995] AATA 90; (1995) 21 AAR 251 (6 April 1995)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

HELEN TERESA GREENWOOD (NEE MAMCZUK) v. COMMISSIONER FOR

SUPERANNUATION

No. N94/274

AAT No. 10123

Number of pages - 8

Retirement Benefits

[1995] AATA 90; (1995) 21 AAR 251

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

D. GRIMES (Senior Member), N. ATTWOOD (Member) AND A. HORTON (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Retirement Benefits - Superannuation - elections - whether valid election - incorrect information statement - whether informed decision - whether election irrevocable

Superannuation Act 1976, ss 154, 244, 245, 247

Superannuation Act 1990, r 1.3.30

HEARING

SYDNEY

6:4:1995

Solicitors for the Respondent: Comsuper

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the decision under review and finds that the election made by the applicant on 24 June 1991 in accordance with section 244 of the Act is invalid for the purposes of the Act.

DECISION

D. GRIMES, N. ATTWOOD AND A. HORTON This is an application to review a decision of 3 March 1994 made by a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the Commissioner") under section 154(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") to affirm a decision dated 3 March 1993, not to treat the applicant's election to cease to be an eligible employee for the purposes of the Act and declaration that she wished to become a member of the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme ("PSS"), dated 24 June 1991, as ineffective for the purposes of section 244 of the Act.

2. As the applicant is presently residing in the Republic of Seychelles, the parties agreed that the matter be determined by the Tribunal on the papers before it, without the conduct of a hearing. This matter was considered by a single member Tribunal and a decision handed down on 19 January 1995. The composition of this Tribunal did not comply with the requirements contained in section 154(7) of the Act and the decision of 19 January 1995 was therefore void. A new Tribunal was constituted complying with section 154(7) of the Act. The Tribunal had before it the documents produced by the respondent in accordance with section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

3. The applicant had been a member of the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme ("CSS") since its establishment in July 1976, having previously been a contributor for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1922, since December 1972. On 1 July 1990, at the time of commencement of the PSS, the applicant was an eligible employee in the Australian Taxation Office ("ATO") in Newcastle. From 6 February 1989, she had been employed in the capacity of a permanent part-time employee at the ATO and as such had been a partial contributor to the CSS from 14 February 1989. In June 1991 the applicant had just returned to work from 12 months maternity leave.

4. As a member of the CSS at the relevant time, the applicant was required to elect whether or not to remain in the CSS or transfer to the new PSS scheme.

5. Transfer from the CSS to the PSS was effected by the method governed by sections 244 and 245 of the Act which state:

s.244(1)

Subject to this section, an eligible employee who is not precluded

by or under the Superannuation Act 1990 (other than by paragraph

6(2)(a) of that Act) from being a member of the Superannuation

(1990) Scheme may, in writing addressed to the Commissioner:

declare that he or she wishes to become a member of that scheme; and

elect to cease to be an eligible employee.

s.245

A person who makes a declaration and election under section 244 is

taken to have ceased to be an eligible employee at the end of the

day on which the declaration and election are made.

6. Section 244 also deals with the time within which a declaration and election provided for in sub-section (1) was to be made, generally between 1 July 1990 and 30 June 1991.

7. The declaration and election was effected by completion of a form SE1 which was circulated to members of the public service. Part A of the form is head "NOTIFICATION OF DECISION TO REMAIN IN THE CSS". Part B is headed "ELECTION TO CEASE MEMBERSHIP OF THE CSS and TO BECOME A MEMBER OF THE NEW

PSS".

8. The applicant completed Part B of the form on 24 June 1991 in the following terms:

Superannuation Act 1976

ELECTION TO CEASE MEMBERSHIP OF THE CSS

and

TO BECOME A MEMBER OF THE NEW PSS

TO: Commissioner for Superannuation

In accordance with Section 244 of the Superannuation Act 1976,

Given Names Surname

I, HELEN TERESA MAMCZUK (GREENWOOD)

USE BLOCK LETTERS

whose AGS No is 23674307 and date of birth is 21/08/53

DECLARE that I wish to become a member of the Superannuation (1990)

Scheme (known as the PSS)

and accordingly elect to cease to be a member (ie and "eligible

employee") of the CSS;

ACKNOWLEDGE that my decision cannot be reversed;

ACKNOWLEDGE that I have received sufficient information to enable me

to make an informed decision.

(SIGNED H Mamczuk) 24/06/91

Signature Date

9. There is no conflict that the applicant filled out the form SE7 as reproduced in Document T12 at page 75.

10. On 21 December 1992 the applicant wrote to the Taxation Office, in the following terms, seeking to revoke her election:

"I am an employee of the Australian Taxation Office (Newcastle) and

commenced contributions to the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme in

1972. I am of Permanent Part Time status and have been since

February 1989.

In June 1991 I returned from 12 months maternity Leave. On 24 June

1991 I made an election (as required) to change from the CSS to the

PSS. At that time I was not aware that I was entitled to a three

month extension in which to lodge my election due to my resumption

from extended leave, viz, maternity leave.

That decision to change over to the PSS was made at the time because

I believed I had to make one. I had no information on which to base

a decision and opted for the PSS simply because other female

workmates were doing the same. In view of what has transpired since

that time I now wish to revoke my election and have my membership to

the CSS restored.

This request is made in view of the following:-

- Personnel advised that the information shown on 1990 statement was

unreliable, and that as a PPTE I would be given the option of

changing my election on receipt of my 1991 statement. I HAVE STILL

NOT RECEIVED MY 1991 STATEMENT.

Two RBO officers (Iain Somerville and Deborah Wills) attended the

Newcastle Tax Office on 10 March '92 for a super` talk and advised

that I would be able to change my election due to the fact that I

had no information available at the time on which to base my

original one.

On 12 March 1992 I sought a professional opinion from Mr Laurie

Ebert who was of the opinion that as I had not received any reliable

information from the RBO on which to base a decision that I should

be given the opportunity to change back to the CSS just as others

have been given the opportunity to since change from the CSS to the

PSS.

On 17 March '92 my husband (also an employee of the Newcastle ATO)

phoned Mr Iain Somerville of the RBO. He explained to him the

circumstances surrounding my case and indicated that I would be

forwarding a letter to the RBO requesting re-instatement to the CSS.

Mr Somerville advised that information was being put together and

that statements would be issuing in due course. His advice was that

when statements (1991) are issued PPTE contributors would be given

the opportunity to review their elections and that we should wait

until that time to request reversion to the CSS.

Personnel advised to follow the advice of Iain Somerville and

request reversion to the CSS after receipt of 1991 statements.

A series of queries to Personnel over the last nine months have

resulted in numerous conflicting responses - based on the responses

received by them from the RBO; eg, two queries on the one day

resulted in advice that 1) "No one in RBO knows anything about

Helen's case" and 2) "Someone is working on it at the moment".

I believe that from the outset I have been given conflicting advice

and information and have still not received my 1991 statement. I

have endeavoured to do the right thing all the way along but have

still not received the correct information to which I am entitled to

make a balanced decision on my future Fund membership. In view of

what has transpired I believe that my original election should be

regarded as invalid.

At all times since the making of my original election I have

followed the advice of the Personnel Section, ie, wait until I get

my 1991 Statement at which time I would be given the opportunity to

change back to CSS.

I have decided to wait no longer and in the absence of my 1991

Statement and any reliable information from the RBO I now request

that my original election be revoked and that my membership of the

CSS be restored.

Your written confirmation would be appreciated."

11. The letter seeking to revoke the declaration of 24 June 1991, was forwarded under cover of a letter dated 30 December 1992, signed by the Assistant Personnel Manager, ATO, Newcastle, Mrs Sue Woodhouse (Document T18/114) which stated in part:

".... Her reasons for doing so are supported by this office as

numerous requests have been made for the issue of a revised

comparison statement to enable her to make an informed decision and

to date none had been received.

She made her election on the basis of information available at the

time, however she was then told that the information for part timers

could be incorrect and she would be issued with a revised statement.

This office has supplied all the relevant information on Ms Mamczuk

to enable a new statement to be prepared, however when your office

was contacted the information given was that as Ms Mamczuk had

already changed to PSS the computer system was unable to produce a

new statement due to some programming problems.

Ms Mamczuk is only one of many part time officers who have yet to

receive a new comparative statement so they can make an informed

decision. The necessary paperwork on a number of these officers was

sent to your office some time ago and more recently more information

was sought and replies provided. ...."

12. On receipt of the delegates decision dated 3 March 1993 that the election made by the applicant was irrevocable the ATO and the applicant wrote again to the Commissioner which resulted in a reconsideration of the reviewable decision.

13. Ms Woodhouse wrote as follows:

"Attached is a letter from Ms Mamczuk in reply to your letter of

3 March 1993.

Ms Mamczuk made her election to PSS on receipt of information from

both Personnel and RBO.

She made enquires regarding her right to revert back to CSS after

receiving more information from Personnel and RBO.

At each request she was advised by both parties that due to the

incorrect statement on which she based her original decision, she

would be able to revert to CSS but to wait until the correct

information had been received.

Ms Mamczuk has yet to receive her corrected statement despite

numerous requests from the Department.

David Pleasance was the officer who was involved in this case before

me and he will provide a written statement in support of Ms Mamczuk

if necessary.

It is considered to be in the interest of this officer to allow the

reversion given that she made her election on information provided

by your office and was not advised by Personnel that the election

could be delayed in view of her extended leave at the time.

Both parties are at fault and this Office supports Ms Mamczuk's

request. ...."

14. The applicant's letter of 8 April 1993 states:

"I request that the Commissioner review the decision made in

relation to my request of 21 December 1992 and notified to me on 17

March 1993, copies enclosed. I make this request under Section 154

of the Superannuation Act 1976 and set out my reasons below :

- As notified to you in my previous correspondence, at all times I

acted in strict accordance with advice supplied to me by both the

RBO and my Personnel section.

- I have still not received my correct statement.

- I was not advised at the time of making my election that I was

entitled to an additional three months in view of my extended leave.

- I was not advised by anyone that I would be able to lodge a late

election because of non receipt of a correct statement - as

mentioned in your letter of 3 March 1993.

- I have been denied the right to make my preferred election (ie to

remain in the CSS) as I was not given the correct details nor the

correct advice.

- My "election" cannot be treated as valid as I was not in

possession of sufficient information on which to base a decision -

as I explained in my request. Again I still have not received my

correct statement despite the many verbal assurances from staff of

the RBO.

- from the outset I have been advised by both the RBO and Personnel

that I would be able to change my "election" in view of the

circumstances when I received my amended statement. I have not yet

received it.

- Given that I have followed the advice of both the RBO and

Personnel I fail to see how my request can be denied. If I have

followed advice from the RBO how can the RBO make a decision

contrary to that advice?

Accordingly I request that you review your decision and re-instate

my membership to the CSS.

15. Where an election has been made by a contributor to the CSS to cease to be an eligible employee for the purposes of that scheme and to join the PSS, revocation of that decision is permitted in very narrow circumstances. Such circumstances are provided for in section 247 of the Act. Section 247 states:

Where, for the purpose of rule 1.3.30 of the Rules for the

administration of the Superannuation (1990) Scheme in its

application to a person who has ceased to be an eligible employee

under section 245, the CSS average salary has been ascertained by

reference to an amount other than the amount referred to in

paragraph (a) or (b) of the definition of "CSS average salary" in

that rule:

that person may, within 3 months after the person has been informed

that his or her CSS average salary has been so ascertained, by

writing addressed to the Commissioner, revoke the declaration and

election by virtue of which the person had so ceased to be an

eligible employee; and

on the making of the revocation, this Act has effect as if the

declaration and election had not been made.

16. The applicant's "average CSS salary" as at 30 June 1990, was calculated $23,640 in both an incorrect 1990 Information Statement issued to the applicant and in a later issued correct 1990 Information Statement. It does not appear to be disputed by the applicant that her CSS average salary was ascertained by reference to an amount referred to in paragraphs (a) or (b) of rule 1.3.30 of the Superannuation Act 1990. That being so, the applicant has no access to the revocation provisions of section 247 of the Act.

17. It is clear that Parliament strictly limited the opportunities to revoke an election made under section 244(1) of the Act but in the view of the Tribunal this assumes that the election was made in the possession of adequate and correct information.

18. The issue of revocation is only relevant in so far as there exists a valid election made by the contributor. The applicant maintains that the election she made on 24 June 1994 was not a valid election for the purposes of the Act. The Tribunal notes that the respondent, in the decision under review, found the election to be valid, the applicant having signed an acknowledgment that the election was made with sufficient information and was irrevocable.

19. It is difficult to determine from the documents before the Tribunal the extent of the information in relation to the PSS election that the applicant in fact received. The documents produced pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 do not specify whether or not the applicant received either the information kit referred to on the reverse of the election form (Document T5) or the circulars contained in the section 37 documents (Documents T7 - T8). The Tribunal is mindful that the applicant was absent from her workplace on maternity leave until June 1991. The evidence before the Tribunal is that the personnel manager of the Newcastle branch of the ATO, believed the information received by the applicant had been incorrect and "extremely poor". The Tribunal notes that on 10 March 1992 after the election was made the applicant was advised by two Retirement Benefits Officers , Iain Summerville and Deborah Wills, that she would be able to change her election because she had no information available on which to base the original election. This indicates confusion in the minds of officers of the Retirement Benefits Office at the time and is certainly suggestive of a less than efficient operation at the time.

20. There is no dispute that the applicant received a personal information statement prior to making her election, nor that the statement was incorrect. It is agreed that the 1990 Information Statement did not represent a true analysis of the applicant's comparative financial position in either of the schemes in question. It is also clear that it was not until some time after the period for making an election had expired that the applicant received a correct information statement.

21. On 17 June 1991 Superannuation Circular No A15 (Document T11) was issued to employers on the subject of "Information Statements for Members Who Were `Partial Contributors Prior to 1 July 1990". As part of the CSS/PSS choice information campaign, the Retirement Benefits Office had provided employers with members provisional information statements in July 1990 and final information statements in November 1990 for issue to all CSS employees who had the option to transfer to the PSS. In many cases, as far as permanent part-time time employees were concerned, CSS contributor information (provided to the Retirement Benefits Office by employing departments) was incomplete resulting in partial contributor histories being unreliable. In order to produce revised information statements, employers were requested to provide complete details on partial contributors in the form of a "Partial Contributor Record" which was attached to the circular, together with instructions and a completed example. As the Retirement Benefits Office was anxious to provide partial contributors with correct information statements as soon as practicable, co-operation was sought to provide the required information as soon as possible. The circular also advised in part:

"Advice to Partial Contributors Regarding the CSS/PSS Choice

...

10. The legal mechanism for electing to transfer to the PSS

is through making an election under sub-section 244(1) of the

Superannuation Act 1976. In the normal course of events elections

under sub-section 244(1) must be made by 30 June 1991. Sub-section

157(1) of the Act does, however, give the Commissioner for

Superannuation discretion to accept an election made after the end

of the specified period if in all the circumstances of the case he

considers it is desirable that the election be recognised ... in

view of the importance that the member information statements have

in the decision, the Commissioner has agreed that he will give

sympathetic consideration to any partial contributors who delay

making an election to transfer to the PSS until the RBO is able to

provide each with a correct member information statement."

22. A letter to partial contributors explaining the above was attached to the circular and employers were requested to ensure that a copy of the letter was provided to all partial contributor employees. In this regard, it was stated in the circular (and also summarised in the letter):

"13. If members have made, or subsequently make, an election to

transfer to the PSS then, the Act does not permit the election to be

reversed nor does it give the Commissioner any discretion in this

regard. The Commissioner's letter therefore advises those partial

contributors who are unsure of their scheme choice to await the

issue of the revised information statement before taking their

decision. It also advises any partial contributors who may have

already elected to transfer to the PSS who, on receipt and

consideration of the revised information statement, consider that

had they originally received a correct statement they would have

remained with the CSS, to provide the RBO with the details of their

case."

23. The reconsideration notes that the "Partial Contributor Record completed by ATO on 1 September 1992 confirms that the "Commissioner's Advice to Partial Contributors" letter had been issued to Ms Mamczuk" however the Tribunal notes such confirmation (Document T17) is undated.

24. The Tribunal also notes that in her application to revert to the CSS, the applicant is supported by personnel officers of the ATO who agreed with her statement that she had "no information on which to base a decision" to elect to join or the PSS or not.

25. The applicant's evidence is that she made the election soon after returning to work from maternity leave because she believed she had to do so (being unaware of her entitlement to the three month extension) and because other female workmates were doing the same. The incorrect personal information statement supplied to her was, in the Tribunal's view, a significant added factor in the applicant's consideration of her decision, and the uncertainties expressed in Superannuation Circular No A15, added to the concerns.

26. The Tribunal is mindful of the letter of a personnel officer of the ATO dated 9 February 1994, that notified the Retirement Benefits Office that:

"...there is no doubt that information available to Part-time

officers, within this branch, was extremely poor at the time Ms

Mamczuk made her election."

The Tribunal also notes that it was conceded by the ATO (Document T19) that the applicant was not advised by personnel that her election could be delayed in view of her extended leave.

27. Whilst there is no dispute that the applicant signed the acknowledgments on the election form, the question is now whether it can be said that the applicant did indeed receive sufficient information to make an "informed decision". It is this Tribunal's opinion that an informed decision could only be made with possession of not only sufficient but also correct information. In the Tribunal's view, the evidence suggests that prior to making her decision the applicant did not receive all the information available and it is conceded that the information statement she received was incorrect. The applicant signed the election in ignorance of the fact that the personal information statement was incorrect and what information she had was (in the opinion of the ATO) "extremely poor", and that as a permanent part-time employee, she had an additional three months in which to consider the decision. In the circumstances, the Tribunal is not satisfied that the applicant made an informed decision in electing to transfer to the PSS. Without such informed decision making, the Tribunal cannot regard the applicant's election as valid for the purposes of section 244 of the Act.

28. In reaching its conclusion, the Tribunal is particularly mindful that it was recognised by the respondent at the time of election, that the information statements of some part time employees were possibly incorrect, and for this reason such employees were permitted a three month extension on the election period in which to obtain a correct information statement. Such action, appears to this Tribunal to be a recognition by the respondent of the significance of the personal information statement in the making of an election.

29. Accordingly, the Tribunal sets aside the decision under review and finds that the election made by the applicant on 24 June 1991 in accordance with section 244 of the Act is invalid for the purposes of the Act.
