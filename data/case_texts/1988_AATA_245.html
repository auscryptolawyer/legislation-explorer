ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Assessable Income - Employee acquired shares holding company of which his employer was a subsidiary - Cost shares to employee below market value - Share certificate not available to employee until three months after issue of shares - Difficulty could be experienced in sale and transfer – Whether the amount represented by a discount afforded to the taxpayer to be brought to tax within the meaning of section 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act

Sections 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act

Case D59 1972 ATC 364

2 August 1988 The Hon. Mr Justice Rodney N. PURVIS

REASONS FOR DECISION

The applications for review that are now before the Tribunal relate to the years of income 1980 to 1985 inclusive. The matters referable to each of these six years have been heard together as the question at issue in each year is the same. The question is whether the amount represented by a discount afforded to the taxpayer on his acquiring shares in a holding company, the subsidiary of which is his an employer, is to be brought to tax within the meaning of section 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

In respect of each of the years the subject of these applications the taxpayer lodged income tax returns in which he made mention of shares that he had acquired in his employer's holding company, such mention being to the following effect, and for this purpose I refer to the income tax return in respect of the year ended 30 June 1980, exhibit “1” in the proceedings.

In such return and as a schedule to it, the taxpayer sets forth the shares that were acquired pursuant to what is described as an Employees Stock Purchase Plan detailing the dates on which shares were acquired, the number of shares so acquired, and the money equivalent of the discount afforded to him on purchase. As he says in the schedule to his return, the shares were acquired for eighty-five per cent of their market value ruling on the New York market at the end of each month. He did not include in what he regarded as his taxable income the money equivalent of the fifteen per cent alleging that there was no guarantee by the company in which the shares were acquired that an employee would benefit from such purchase. He said the certificate is received two to three months after purchase: local brokerage charges for disposing of the shares would, on the overseas charges, dissipate the fifteen per cent discount given by the company and that the shares purchased were for a long term investment. He made other observations in the schedule in support of the amount not being liable to tax.

In each of the returns for the other years, the subject of the present applications, information to a like effect was contained. On some occasions the taxpayer set forth the extended money equivalent of the fifteen per cent as it related to the number of shares acquired, in each case contending that the "assessable income" in relation to the discount was nil. In respect of each of the said years assessments issued, the Commissioner contending that the taxpayer had obtained an assessable benefit for the shares; that they were acquired in accordance with section 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act and that the dollar equivalent of the discount extended over the number of shares acquired during the particular year should be included as part of the assessable income.

Objections were lodged by the taxpayer to each of the assessments and the objections in respect of each of the subject six years were to the same effect; that is, - and I quote for the purpose from the objection taken on 31 January 1984 to the 1981, 1982 and 1983 assessments-:

(1) [Company] employees may only buy shares with up to 10 per cent of earned income per annum. My maximum contribution for the year 1982 will be approximately $4000;

(2) Shares are bought on a discount of 15 per cent which the Taxation Department claims is assessable income; that is, maximum Taxation Department assessable income is $4000 x 15% = $600;

(3) Scrip is not available from [the company] for purchased shares for two to three months after purchase, thus eliminating any immediate profit-taking;

Various other observations were made in the objection which are not now germane to these applications other than that the taxpayer does make mention again to brokers fees which would be charged on a sale and would, he says, eliminate any profit to be gained from the fifteen per cent discount.

As it is appropriate at this stage I would also make mention of other factors said by the taxpayer to be covered by his objections and as they are set forth in some detail in exhibit "E", a document that purports to set forth the issues raised by him with the Australian Taxation Office. He there makes mention of the fluctuation in share prices; the time taken for scrip to issue; the selling time and the fluctuation in currency prices.

The Commissioner as he is required to do in his statement set forth reasons for disallowing the taxpayer's claims and in respect of each of the subject years says that the taxpayer acquired the shares in his employer's holding company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees and that the amount sought to be brought to tax was correctly included in the taxpayer's assessable income under sub-section 26AAC(5) of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

And so the issues emerge. The taxpayer is an employee of ... Australia Limited and has been so since at least 1965, from which time he has, year by year, month by month participated in [the company] Employees Stock Purchase Plan. As a participating employee the taxpayer is enabled to acquire shares in the United States holding company of his employer. Such shares are listed on the Newt York Stock Exchange but not on any Australian Stock Exchange. The basis upon which the shares are acquired is set forth in the Stock Purchase Plan which is in evidence as exhibit "7".

With reference to what is described as the full text of the Employees Stock Purchase Plan it is noted by clause 3 that the corporation, meaning thereby the United States holding company, will make one or more annual offerings to employees to purchase stock under this plan. I pause here to note that eligibility extends to:-

"a regular or regular part-time employee of [the company] or any of its subsidiaries."

Each offering period shall be of twelve months duration, and during such portion thereof as the employee may elect to participate, amounts received as compensation by an employee shall constitute the measure of such of the employee's participation in the offering as is based on compensation.

Clause 5 of the full text provides that the corporation is to maintain payroll deduction accounts for all participating employees. With respect to any offering made under this plan an employee may authorize a payroll deduction in terms of whole number percentages up to a maximum of ten per cent of the compensation an employee receives during the offering period or during such portion thereof as an employee may elect to participate. I pause here to note that the taxpayer did make an election and such an election extended to the maximum of ten percent.

Clause 9 of the full text is referable to the purchase of shares and makes mention of each employee who participates in an offering being granted an option upon the effective date of such offering for as many shares of [company] stock as the participating employee elects to purchase, and again makes mention of the requisite percentages. It is in the same clause that mention is made of the purchase price for each share, being eighty-five per cent of the average market price on the last day of a participating employee's pay period.

The taxpayer stated that n practice his salary is charged with the ten per cent at the beginning of a month and shares in the company then acquired at an amount calculated on the market price of the shares on the exchange one month before such date on which the deduction takes place. The purchase price is set at the market price less fifteen per cent thereof. It is this fifteen per cent that is sought by the Commissioner to be brought to tax. The share certificates referable to the shares acquired are not, according to the taxpayer, available for some three months after the deduction is made from the salary. It is to be noted that on the evidence the issue of certificates is a matter of convenience to the issuing company and might be the subject of negotiation if thought desirable. Exhibit "B", a note from the pay office of the company to all members of the Stock Purchase Plan recites:

Stockholder relations department now issues certificates every quarter and there is a one month lag between the last month of purchase and the date of issue of the certificate."

Illustrative of the plan in operation is exhibit "C", a document similar to the schedule contained in the income tax return of the taxpayer for the 1981 financial year, and indeed similar to schedules contained in each of the other relevant returns. Exhibit "C" sets forth the dates on which a purchase of shares took place, what is described as the assessable income per share, such amount representing fifteen per cent of the relevant market price, the number of shares acquired at the end of a particular month, and an extended figure representing the fifteen per cent applied to all of the shares so acquired.

Thus, by looking at the purchases that took place at 30 June 1980 for example, it may be seen that the market price of the company's shares one month before was $51, that the taxpayer acquired his shares for $43.35, and the fifteen per cent discount was represented by the sum of $7.65. Thus, for the six shares that he bought at that time, that is 30 June 1980, he was required to pay by way of a deduction against salary some $260 and the fifteen per cent discount was in the sum of approximately $46.

The situation is further illustrated by exhibit "F" which sets forth acquisitions of a more recent date, namely during the 1988 financial year, said by the taxpayer to be illustrative of the situation that existed in the subject years. Here again the money equivalent of the market price less the fifteen per cent of the market price is charged against the taxpayer's salary. At the time of his acquiring the shares and it seems more for convenience than otherwise, certificates did not issue, they emerged some months later. In a practical sense the absence of a share certificate could hinder a sale on a stock exchange. It is said by the taxpayer that the absence of a certificate for this period of time is a restriction consequent on issue or acquisition. I shall make further reference to this aspect later.

The evidence shows that no costs are incurred by the employee associated with his becoming a shareholder. Costs would be incurred on a sale, but in this matter the taxpayer has not disposed of any of the shares acquired by him in the company, certainly not during the years the subject of these references. Various other matters as they emerged in the evidence might be noted. Participation in the Employees stock purchase Plan is open to all of the company employees throughout the world. The taxpayer made mention of two exceptions, New Zealand and South Africa, but those exceptions related more to the policies of the governments in those countries than to the position of any employee as such.

The terms and conditions of issue of the shares have remained substantially the same over the years the subject of these references. The taxpayer agreed that there were not any restrictions on sale of the shares so acquired by an employee, or at least none that he knew of, and that an employee in theory was free to sell the shares as soon as they were issued.

As I have already mentioned, the Commissioner seeks to bring to tax the money equivalent of the fifteen per cent allowed on the purchase of shares in each of the respective years. He seeks to bring these amounts to tax pursuant to the provisions of section 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act. Sub-section (1) of section 26AAC provides:

For the purposes of this section a taxpayer shall be taken to have acquired a share in a company or a right to acquire a share in a company, under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees if -

(a) in the case of a share, the share was acquired by the taxpayer -

(i) in respect of, or for, or in relation directly or indirectly to any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer; or

(ii) as a result of the exercise or operation of a right to acquire the share, being a right that was acquired by the taxpayer in respect of, or for, or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer; or

(b) in the case of a right, the right was acquired by the taxpayer in respect of, or for, or in relation directly or indirectly to, any employment of, or services rendered by, the taxpayer or a relative of the taxpayer."

Sub-section (5) of section 26AAC so far as it is relevant provides:

Where a taxpayer has acquired during the year of income a share in a company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees, the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income includes the value of that share at the time when it was acquired by the taxpayer less the sum of -

(a) the amount, if any, paid or payable by the taxpayer as consideration for the share; and

(b) ......

The sub-section continues by making provision for the situation where a taxpayer has acquired the share as a result of the exercise or operation of a right to acquire the share. The sub-section sought to be brought in aid by the taxpayer, sub-section 15 of section 26AAC provides that:

“ Where -

(a) a taxpayer acquires a share in a company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees; and

(b) by reason of any conditions or restrictions (being conditions or restrictions applicable only to shares in the company acquired under such a scheme) attached to, or to the issue of, the share (including conditions or restrictions in relation to the payment of moneys in respect of the share) the right of. the taxpayer to dispose of the share is restricted or the taxpayer is liable to be divested of his ownership of the share,

the acquisition of the share by the taxpayer shall be deemed for the purposes of this section to have taken place at the time when the right of the taxpayer to dispose of the share ceases to be so restricted, the time when the taxpayer ceases to be so liable to be divested of his ownership of the share or the time immediately before the taxpayer disposes of the share, whichever first happens."

In the present case the restriction, if it be a restriction, namely the non-availability of the share certificate, would be one that was lifted within, at the most, three months of the share being issued and acquired.

Thus, looking at the relevant statutory provisions and accepting that the taxpayer carries the onus of satisfying the Tribunal that the assessments issued by the Commissioner were wrong, it is incumbent upon him to establish inter alia that the shares were not acquired by him in respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to any employment or that the shares were not. acquired under a scheme for the acqusiition of shares by employees and that his income should not include the value of the difference between, or the sum represented by the difference between the amount that would be entailed in the acquisition of the share on the market and the amount that he is required to pay, that is, the sum that is deducted from his salary or that conditions or restrictions were attached to the issue of the shares the like of those referred to in sub-section 15 of section 26AAC.

Looking at the evidence as it is before the Tribunal there is clearly material available on which the Tribunal is able to conclude that the taxpayer did acquire the shares in the company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees and that the same was in respect of or for, or in relation directly to, employment of the taxpayer with ... Australia Limited. Accordingly, the taxpayer in this regard has not established the contrary situation.

Turning to sub-section 5 of section 26AAC, as I have already mentioned, on the evidence I am satisfied that the taxpayer acquired these shares in the company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees hence his assessable income pursuant to sub-section 5 is to include the value of that share at the time when it was acquired, less the sum of the amount paid by the taxpayer as consideration the share. The value of the share at the time when it was acquired is the market price of the share about one month before the acquisition takes place. There is evidence that the taxpayer paid for each share the market prices less fifteen per cent. The sub-section does not, and it is understandable that it does not, make mention of a sale situation or of costs and expenses that might be incurred in connection with a sale. That would be another matter. If, in due course, a person in the position of the taxpayer should seek to sell the shares then the amount that might be brought to tax would be the difference between the cost of acquisition, any expenses incurred in connection with a sale, the total of such cost and expenses being then deducted from a sale price. It does not seem to me to be relevant to the present issue to consider questions of costs incurred on sale nor is it relevant as I see the position to consider currency or exchange fluctuations nor fluctuations in market price of shares over periods.

The other matter raised by the taxpayer in support of the shares being allegedly issued with conditions or restrictions attached does not seem to me to be of moment in the context of these applications. Even be it that the unavailability of the share certificate is a condition or a restriction within the meaning of sub-section 15, such condition or restriction no longer applies some three months after issue and the relevant time would then be no more than three months beyond that deemed applicable by the Respondent Commissioner. However, I do not consider that the lack of availability of the shares certificate as such be it by agreement as appears from exhibit "B" or be it by reason of convenience to the holding company is a condition or restriction attached to, or to the issue of, the share. As was submitted by Mrs Hastings who appears on behalf of the Respondent Commissioner, the taxpayer upon issue of the shares which approximates the date of payment, becomes entitled on the evidence to receive dividends and vote on the shares so issued. The taxpayer is the owner of the shares consequent upon their being issued and title is acquired to them on the evidence as at the date of issue, which date approximates the date of purchase.

Reference was made by Mrs Hastings to a decision of the No. 2 Board of Review in Case D59 1972 ATC 364 which was a case dealing with an employee shareholder who had been issued with shares and a delay taking place in the shares so issued being listed on a stock exchange. By reason of their not being listed, the shares could not be sold through that medium up until the time of listing. Case D59 (supra) was a case under sub-section 26E of the Income Tax Assessment Act and not under 26AAC. Section 26E contains what seems to me to be a different emphasis so far as value is concerned in that the statute there makes reference to "the value to the taxpayer of all allowances, etcetera". Section 26AAC refers to, "the value of that share at the time when it was acquired by the taxpayer less the sum of any amount paid". There is a distinct difference in orientation between the two provisions. I do not consider that any difficulty arising in the disposition of the shares as a consequence of a share certificate not being available is a condition or restriction within the meaning of sub-section 15 of section 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

Accordingly, I do not consider that the taxpayer has discharged the onus that rests upon him to show that the assessments issued by the Commissioner in respect of the years the subject of these references were excessive and accordingly, I affirm each of the assessment decisions under review.
