PATENTS ACT - extension of time - payment of renewal fee - error or omission within "grace period" - "prescribed action" - "relevant act" - "certain time" - decision affirmed.

Patents Act 1990 sub-ss. 223(2), 223(3), 223(7), 223(11)

Patents Regulations 13.6(1), 13.6(3), 22.11(3)(b)

Re Sanyo Electric Co Ltd and Commissioner of Patents (1996) 36 IPR 470

Scaniainventor v Commissioner of Patents [1981] FCA 84; (1981) 36 ALR 101

Newcastle City Council and GIO General Limited [1997] HCA 53; (1997) 191 CLR 85

Re Foldi and Others [1997] APO 9; (1997) 38 IPR 131

REASONS FOR DECISION

27 November 1998 Deputy President B.M. Forrest

Mr C.G. Woodard, Member

This is an application for review of a decision made on 12 November 1997 by Deputy Commissioner of Patents Mr Herald to refuse to grant a request under s. 223 of the Patents Act 1990 ("the Act") for an extension of time to pay a renewal fee for a patent.

The application to the Tribunal is made pursuant to the power contained in s. 224 of the Act which includes a review of a decision made under s. 223.

In addition to the material lodged by the respondent pursuant to s. 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 the Tribunal also had before it the 1984 report of the Industrial Property Advisory Committee "Patents, Innovation and Competition in Australia", a copy of the Government Response to the Report, the Second Reading Speech and the Explanatory Memorandum to the Patents Bill 1990.

BACKGROUND

The applicant Beckswift Limited ("Beckswift") is the proprietor of Patent No. 614373 for "Shielded Electrodes". The patent was granted on 21 April 1989 and renewal fees are payable each year on the anniversary of the date of the patent. The applicant did not pay the seventh year renewal fee due on 21 April 1996.

The background facts and circumstances set out in a statutory declaration of Peter John Smart made on 24 October 1996 (which was lodged in support of the application for an extension of time within which to pay the renewal fee) are not in dispute and are accepted by the Tribunal to be as follows.

Mr Smart, a Chartered Patent Agent and European Patent Attorney is a partner in Beck, Greener & Co London and has responsibility for the patent affairs of Beckswift within the multinational Tetra Laval group of companies of which Beckswift is part.

In his statutory declaration Mr Smart stated that the Tetra Laval organisation decided in early 1996 to close down the operation of Beckswift. As a result of this it was determined that the Australian patent would not be maintained.

When the due date in April 1996 for the payment of the renewal fee approached, Beck, Greener & Co instructed Australian agents Davies Collison Cave ("DCC") to mark their records that the patent would not be renewed.

On 1 August 1996 Tetra Pak sent a request by facsimile to Mr Smart enquiring as to the precise status of the patent and whether it was possible to revive it.

On 10 September 1996 Tetra Pak instructed Mr Smart by facsimile to pay the renewal fee. According to Mr Smart's declaration a staff member in his office failed to carry out the instruction. This failure was not noticed until 21 October 1996 whereupon instructions were immediately sent to DCC but by virtue of the time difference between England and Australia, it was already too late to effect payment.

On 23 October 1996 the renewal fee was paid and at the same time a request was made to the respondent under s. 223 of the Act for an extension of time to pay that fee.

LEGISLATION

Section 223 of the Act relevantly provides:

"223. (2) Where, because of:

(a) an error or omission by the person concerned or by his or her agent or attorney; or

(b) circumstances beyond the control of the person concerned;

a relevant act that is required to be done within a certain time is not, or cannot be, done within that time, the Commissioner may, on application made by the person concerned in accordance with the regulations, extend the time for doing the act.

(3) The time allowed for doing a relevant act may be extended, whether before or after that time has expired.

...

(7) Where:

(a) a patent application lapses, or a patent ceases, because of a failure to do one or more relevant acts within the time allowed; and

(b) the time for doing that act or those acts is extended;

the application or patent must be treated as having been restored.

...

(11) In this section:

"relevant act" means an action (other than a prescribed action) in relation to a patent, a patent application, or any proceedings under this Act (other than court proceedings)..."

Regulation 13.6 of the Patents Regulations stipulates the prescribed period for the purposes of s. 143(a) of the Act for payment of a renewal fee for a standard patent as being the twelve months ending at the anniversary of the date of the patent. Under s. 143 of the Act a standard patent ceases if the patentee does not pay a renewal fee within the prescribed period. If the renewal fee is paid within six months of the anniversary date, reg. 13.6(3) extends the prescribed period to the date of payment.

"13.6 (1) For the purposes of paragraph 143(a) of the Act ("ceasing of patents"), subject to subregulation (2), the period of 12 months ending at the end of an anniversary of the date of the patent (being an anniversary specified in column 2 of item 7 of Part 2 in Schedule 7) is prescribed for the payment of renewal fee.

...

(3) If a renewal fee is not paid in the period referred to in subregulation (1), but is paid within 6 months after the anniversary concerned, the prescribed period is extended to the day on which the fee is paid."

Regulation 22.11(3) provides that certain actions are prescribed actions for the purposes of the definition of "relevant act" in sub-s. 223(11):

"22.11 (3) For the purposes of the definition of "relevant act" in sub-section 223(11) of the Act, each of the following actions is prescribed:

...

(b) paying a fee in a period that is extended because of...subregulation 13.6(3) ("time for payment of renewal fee for standard patent")."

CONSIDERATION

The present application is concerned with the proper construction of s. 223 of the Act. The issue for consideration is whether s. 223 is available to extend time to pay a renewal fee when the error or omission occurs within the period allowed by reg. 13.6(3) and payment is not made until after the expiry of that period hereinafter referred to as "the grace period". In deciding not to grant the extension of time sought (with the consequences that the patent has irrevocably ceased) the Deputy Commissioner was satisfied that s. 223 did not permit an extension of time to pay the renewal fee in the present circumstances where it was paid after the expiry of the grace period.

The Deputy Commissioner held that the action to be taken, that is, the payment of the renewal fee for which an extension of time was sought, was not a "relevant act" for the purposes of sub-s. 223(2) by reference to the definition of "relevant act" in sub-s. 223(11) read with the provisions of reg. 22.11(3)(b).

The submissions of Mr Carne, Counsel for the respondent supported the approach taken by the Deputy Commissioner.

Counsel for Beckswift, Mr Ryan submitted that an extension of time may be granted pursuant to sub-s. 223(2). It was said that reg. 22.11(3)(b) has no application to the present case because at no time was there a period which was extended because of reg. 13.6(3). The period could only have been extended because of reg. 13.6(3) if the renewal fee had in fact been paid within the grace period. Further it was said that the Deputy Commissioner, has read reg. 22.11(3)(b) as if it said that a prescribed action is paying a fee in the period that is referred to in sub-regulation 13.6(3).

We do not accept this submission as in our view it would have the effect of rendering reg. 22.11(3)(b) nugatory. On the facts in this matter there was no error or omission prior to the anniversary date as by that date there was no intention on the part of Beckswift to renew the patent. Beckswift subsequently changed its intention but did not pay the renewal fee within the grace period which ended on 21 October 1996 as provided by reg. 13.6(3). The failure to pay the renewal fee within the grace period occurred because of an error or omission by Beckswift's attorney during that period.

Section 223, which in its terms is a general extension of time provision, provides a means whereby rights otherwise lost may in certain circumstances be restored to the extent provided by the Act. To enliven the powers in sub-s. 223(2) of the Act to extend time the use of the expression "because of" requires there be a causal link between the "error or omission" and the "relevant act that is required to be done within a certain time...". Clearly the relevant act required to be done is payment of the renewal fee. The question then arises whether the payment of the renewal fee is a relevant act for the purposes of s. 223.

The term "relevant act" is defined in sub-s. 223(11) of the Act. The definition excludes a prescribed action. Regulation 22.11(3)(b) categorises the payment of a renewal fee for a standard payment in the grace period as a prescribed action. Sub-regulation 13.6(3) as observed earlier, operates to extend the prescribed period, that is the period ending on the anniversary date (21 April 1996) for payment of a renewal fee, to the date of payment provided the fee is paid within the grace period. Thus the action described in reg. 22.11(3)(b), paying a renewal fee in the grace period is a prescribed act and not a relevant act to which s. 223 applies.

If the renewal fee is paid within the grace period s. 223 has no work to do. This is so because of the operation of reg. 13.6(3) (a provision which gives effect to Australia's acceptance of the obligations of the Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property by providing a grace period of not less than six months to pay fees for the maintenance of patent rights). If the renewal fee is paid within the grace period, reg. 13.6(3) provides an automatic extension of the prescribed period to the date of payment exclusive of any rights under s. 223.

It was submitted on behalf of Beckswift that as payment of the renewal fee was required to be made by the end of the grace period, the "certain time" referred to in sub-s. 223(2) within which a relevant act is required to be done ended on 21 October 1996, and not as the Deputy Commissioner held, on 21 April 1996. There is no reason in principle, it was said, to read the expression "certain time" in sub-s. 223(2) as being restricted to the period ending on the anniversary date of the patent.

We do not agree. The term "certain time" is not defined in the legislation. However its meaning as derived from the statutory framework is the time, that is, the prescribed period within which the "relevant act" in s. 223 is required to be done. In this case the payment of the renewal fee is the act required to be done to which the "certain time" has to relate.

The causal link between the error or omission and the relevant act also requires the error or omission to have occurred before the "certain time" to provide a basis for the operation of s. 223.

In our view the "certain time" refers to the prescribed (anniversary) date in reg. 13.6(1). Even if this view be incorrect, the contrary view cannot determine the issue given that the "certain time" has to relate to the relevant act which in this case is excluded from the definition in s. 223.

Mr Ryan further submitted that the legislation should be read in a manner beneficial to patentees because of the otherwise serious consequences. He referred to Re Sanyo Electric Co Ltd and Commissioner of Patents (1996) 36 IPR 470. Sanyo is not of assistance here. It was a matter involving an exercise of discretion on its particular facts. The question of whether payment of the renewal fee is a relevant act in sub-s. 223(11) was not the issue.

In Re Reilly and Commissioner of Patents (1996) 40 ALD 791 at 796; (1996) 36 IPR 314 at 320 the Tribunal (Deputy President Forgie) remarked:

"The definition of a "prescribed action" in subreg 22.1193)(sic) of the Patents Regulations is not relevant for there was no question at the hearing, and I accept, that the payment of the renewal fee is a relevant act and not a prescribed act for the purposes of s 223(11)."

This passage may be thought to provide some support for Mr Ryan's submission. However the learned Deputy President did not have to consider the issues currently before the Tribunal and her remarks are obiter. Reilly was concerned with issues of the proprietorship of the patent and whether the circumstances surrounding the failure to pay the renewal fee were beyond the control of the applicant.

The Tribunal accepts s. 223 is a remedial provision. In Scaniainventor v Commissioner of Patents [1981] FCA 84; (1981) 36 ALR 101 the Federal Court considered sub-s. 160(2) of the Patents Act 1952, a provision in substantially similar terms to sub-s. 223(2) of the Act. The Court (Fox ACJ, Franki and Northrop JJ) (at 105) observed that:

"Section 160 is a remedial section and should be applied where it appears to be applicable unless there is some clear indication to the contrary."

The applicant's submission is in substance a request for a favourable exercise of discretion. In the decision under review the Deputy Commissioner did not exercise the discretion because of the view taken as to the scope of s. 223. While the Tribunal is well aware of the consequences that flow to Beckswift from payment of the renewal fee two days after the expiry of the grace period, the Tribunal cannot disregard an established principle of statutory interpretation, that if the express words of the legislation are reasonably capable of only one construction and neither the purpose of the provision nor any other provision of the legislation throws doubt on that construction, it cannot be ignored: Newcastle City Council and GIO General Limited [1997] HCA 53; (1997) 191 CLR 85 per McHugh J. at 109.

In our opinion the language of the legislation does not permit of an alternative construction. In Re Foldi and Others [1997] APO 9; (1997) 38 IPR 131 a similar view, with which we agree, was also expressed.

The decision under review is affirmed.
