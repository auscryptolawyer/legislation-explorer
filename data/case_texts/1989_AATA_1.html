TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

REASONS FOR DECISION

9 January 1989 Deputy President I. R. Thompson

1. All five objection decisions under review relate to the taxation year which ended on 30 June 1980. On 12 September 1978 a discretionary family trust was established. It provided for specified beneficiaries and general beneficiaries. The specified beneficiaries were a man ("H"), his wife ("W") and their children. During the 1980 taxation year there were two children, a son ("S") and a daughter ("D"). The deed of trust provided also for general beneficiaries, among whom were included the specified beneficiaries. The trustee appointed was a proprietary company ("A Co."). The directors of that company were H and W. The trust was named "the A Family Trust". A Co. as trustee for the A Family Trust carried on business. The trust deed empowered the trustee to determine to distribute or to accumulate income of the trust during any accounting period. It provided also that, in the event that any part of the net income of the trust fund for any accounting period was not the subject of a determination to distribute or to accumulate it, the trustee was to hold that part of the income in trust for the specified beneficiaries in equal shares.

2. In the early part of the 1980 taxation year A Co. as trustee of the A Family Trust was carrying on business in a joint venture with another company ("M Co."); M Co. had no connection with H or his family apart from carrying on business with A Co. in a joint venture. The business of the joint venture was carried on in the name of M Co.; it provided the office staff and maintained the records of the business. Under the terms of the joint venture agreement M Co. was required to pay to A Co. at the end of each month A Co.'s share of the profits of the business for that month. Apparently the payments were not made regularly but by early November 1979 A Co. had received from M Co. $200,000 in respect of profits earned during the 1980 taxation year. Because of a change in the ownership of M Co., and possibly for other reasons, A Co. wished to bring the joint venture to an end and, in its capacity as trustee for the A Family Trust, to establish a business to be carried on in its own name. It needed to have adequate capital to establish the business on its own behalf.

3. H, who provided the management skill and business expertise of A Co., was, therefore, concerned that the $200,000 which A Co. had received from M Co. should be reduced as the result of the exaction of income tax by as little as was lawfully possible. He sought professional advice and thereafter persuaded W that they and A Co. should act in accordance with that advice. The details of the action taken by H, W, A Co. and others is set out more fully later in this statement. At this point it is sufficient to state that A Co. as trustee of the A Family Trust added as a general beneficiary another proprietary company ("B Co.") as trustee of the Z Trust, determined to distribute $200,000 of the income of the trust to B Co. as trustee of that trust and caused a cheque drawn on a hank account of A Co. to be drawn in favour of B Co. and transmitted to B Co. In accordance with the advice received by H a new discretionary family trust ("the A Management Trust") was then established with A Co. as the trustee and with H, W, S and D as the specified beneficiaries. Shortly afterwards, as H had been informed by his adviser would occur, $174,000 was paid to A Co. as trustee of the A Management Trust. The income tax return of A Co. for the 1980 taxation year showed $200,000 of the income of the A Family Trust as having been distributed to B Co. as trustee for the Z Trust. The income tax returns in respect of W, S and D for that year did not include any income derived from the A Family Trust. However, the respondent took the view that the distribution to B Co. had not been effective and that, in default of any determination by A Co. to accumulate or to distribute otherwise the income of the A Family Trust, the deed of that trust had operated to give to each of H, W, S and D in that year a present entitlement to one quarter of the income, that is to say $50,000. He, therefore, issued notices of amended assessment on that basis.

4. W then lodged an objection pursuant to section 185 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") in respect of her assessment. A Co. similarly lodged objections in respect of assessments made under section 98 of the Act in respect of each of S and D. S and D also lodged objections against assessments made otherwise than under Division 6 of Part III of the Act. It is not clear whether H also lodged an objection in respect of his own assessment; from comments made by Counsel at the hearing before the Tribunal it seems that he probably did so. However, the present proceedings are not concerned with the assessment of his income. The objections were all disallowed by the respondent. The applicants then requested the respondent to refer his decisions on the objections to a Board of Review. That occurred before 1 July 1986. However, by that date the Board of Review had not undertaken the reviews. Consequently, by virtue of the provisions of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 the Administrative Appeals Tribunal is now required to undertake them.

  1. At the hearing before the Tribunal the applicants were represented by Mr H. Reicher, of Counsel, and the respondent by Mrs A. Moshinsky, of Counsel. Oral evidence was given by H and by an accountant, now retired, P. In addition to the usual small number of documents lodged with the Board of Review by the respondent when referring the request, numerous documents tendered at the hearing were received as evidence.
  2. Among those documents was a copy of the deed of settlement of the A Family Trust. A number of facts concerning the settlement and the trust are of significance. First, the settlor was a person apparently not connected with the family of H and was not a beneficiary of the trust. Under the terms of the deed she was an "excluded person" and so could not be nominated as a beneficiary of the trust. Second, the trustee was empowered to nominate in writing the trustee of any "eligible trust" as a general beneficiary of the trust. "Eligible trust" was defined in the deed as meaning "any trust or settlement under which any beneficiary or class of General Beneficiary has any interest", subject to the proviso that a trust could be an eligible trust "only if all interests therein must vest before the expiration of the perpetuity period named herein". The perpetuity period was stated to be 80 years from the date of the deed of settlement. A trust of which an excluded person was a beneficiary was not an eligible trust. Third, the deed contained the default provision referred to above in respect of income. Fourth, it provided that "as from the vesting date" the trustee was to stand possessed of the trust fund and its income in trust for such of the general beneficiaries or any one of them as the trustee might appoint by instrument in writing. There was then a provision, similar to that in respect of income, in default of such an appointment being made. Fifth, the trustee was authorised to exercise, by a resolution of the directors of the trustee, any power, discretion or authority conferred by the deed.

7. As already stated, the actions taken by A Co., and by H and W as its directors, were the result of professional advice. H and P gave evidence of the circumstances in which that advice was sought and obtained. In 1979 P was in practice as a public accountant. He prepared the accounts of A Co. and the A Family Trust; he also prepared the income tax returns of A Co., H, W, S and D for the 1980 taxation year. I accept H's evidence that he went to see P to obtain advice regarding the cessation of A Co.'s joint venture with M Co. and the establishment of a business by A Co. as trustee for the A Family Trust. I accept that P arranged for H to see a solicitor ("Y") to discuss those matters, which were to include minimization of tax liability. I accept that Y told H that there was a way in which the liability to pay tax on the income of $200,000 could be totally eliminated but that fees totalling $27,000 would have to be paid to those who would assist in bringing about that state of affairs. I find that P accompanied H to at least one of his meetings with Y to discuss how that was to be achieved and that Y explained fully to H in precise detail the steps which would require to be taken by A Co., and by H and W as its directors and by others, to achieve the desired result. I accept H's evidence that he in turn convinced W as a director of A Co. that they and A Co. should act in that manner in order that A Co. might be able to retain the bulk of the $200,000 as capital for establishing a business to be run by it as trustee of the A Management Trust.

8. In cross-examination of H Mrs Moshinsky suggested to him that some meetings of himself and Was the directors of A Co. had not been held and some documents not executed on the dates shown in the minutes of those meetings and on those documents. She referred him to evidence given in examination-in-chief that he had gone to see Y in the third week of November 1979 and that he had been given full instructions by Y how to proceed and had been supplied with copies of all relevant documents. In cross-examination he said that he could not recall the precise date when he had gone to see Y because it was so long ago. Further, he said that he had met Y in his office in the city on several days during the month, of November and had been given documents and additional instructions by him at each meeting. It was also put to him that he and W had not understood the effect of various resolutions which they had passed as directors of A Co. or of documents which had been executed by them for A Co. He denied that and gave a reasonably accurate account in layman's terms of the effect of most of the actions which he, W and A Co. had taken.

  1. The Z Trust was established on 5 November 1979; Y was the settlor with B Co. as its trustee. That trust was an essential vehicle in the scheme devised by Y for H and A Co. As the first step by H and W as directors of A Co. towards implementing the scheme was taken on 9 November 1979, I am satisfied on the balance of probabilities that the first meeting between H and Y took place very early in November 1979. Copies of bank accounts of A Co. and B Co. show that the cheque drawn on A Co.'s account in favour of B Co was presented on 19 November 1979. I am satisfied on the balance of probabilities that the various actions taken by H, W and A Co. were taken over a period and that they were taken on the dates shown in the relevant documents. There is no dispute as to what those actions, and actions taken by others to give effect to the scheme, were. They were as follows.
  2. On 5 November 1979, when the Z Trust was established, the specified beneficiaries of that trust included S Co. as trustee of the S Unit Trust. The vesting day shown in the schedule to the deed of settlement was 60 days from the date of the execution of that deed. However, the deed provided that the vesting day should be either that day, an earlier date specified by the trustee with the consent of the guardian of the trust or a later date specified by the trustee with the consent of the guardian, being no later than the date on which the perpetuity period named in the deed would expire. The perpetuity period was named as 80 years from the date of the deed of settlement. The deed of settlement of the Z Trust was in substantially the same terms as the deed of settlement of the A Family Trust. It empowered the trustee to nominate additional general beneficiaries but not any person who was an excluded person as defined in the deed. Among the excluded persons was the settlor of the Z Trust but not the settlor of the A Family Trust.
    1. On 9 November 1979 H and W met in their home as directors of A Co. and resolved to amend the trust deed of the A Family Trust. On that same day they executed a deed having that effect. The operative part of the deed was an amendment of the definition of eligible trust in the settlement deed. As amended the definition of "eligible trust" was as follows:-

"'eligible trust' shall mean any trust or settlement under which any General Beneficiary hereof has any interest whether absolute or contingent or by of (sic) expectancy whether liable to be affected by the exercise of any power of appointment or revocation or to be diminished by the increase of the class to which that Beneficiary belongs; PROVIDED HOWEVER that a trust shall be an eligible trust only if all interests or expectancies therein must vest before the expiration of the perpetuity period named herein."

  1. On 12 November 1979 H and W met at their home as directors of A Co. in its capacity as trustee of the A Family Trust and resolved first to nominate B Co. in its capacity as trustee of the Z Trust to be a general beneficiary of the A Family Trust and then to distribute $200,000 income of the trust to B Co. in that capacity and to instruct Y, as signatory of cheques drawn on A Co.'s bank account, to make a payment to B Co. in that capacity in respect of the distribution. In the meantime on 9 November 1979 another discretionary family trust in the same form as the A Family Trust and the Z Trust had been established ("the E Trust"). The settlor was Y. The specified beneficiaries were four charities and the unborn children and grandchildren of H and W. The trustee was E Co. Before 10 November 1979 two persons not associated with H and his family were the directors of E Co. but on that date they resigned and H and W were appointed as directors and W as secretary of the company.
  2. On 12 November 1979 H and W as directors of A Co. in its capacity as trustee of the A Family Trust resolved to make a loan of $140,000 to E Co. in its capacity as trustee of the E Trust, such loan to be interest free and repayable on demand. Relevant bank statements show that $140,000 was debited against the account of A Co. and credited to the account of E Co. on 12 November 1979. Pursuant to the resolution to nominate B Co. in its capacity as trustee of the Z Trust as a general beneficiary of the A Family Trust a nomination was executed on 12 November 1979 by A Co.
  3. On 19 November 1979 the directors of B Co., who included Y, noted that in its capacity as trustee of the Z Trust it had received a distribution of income of $200,000 from A Co. in its capacity as trustee of the A Family Trust and resolved to make an advance of $184,000 to S Co. as trustee of the S Unit Trust. It resolved that the amount paid was to be repayable on demand unless S Co. made a gift in excess of $20 to one of four named charities, in which event S Co. as trustee of the S Unit Trust would thereafter be beneficially entitled to the amount paid to it by B Co. It was resolved also that Y should draw a cheque for the payment of the $184,000 to S Co. and to forward it to S Co. with a certified copy of the minutes of the meeting. The income tax return of the S Unit Trust (under a changed name) for the 1980 taxation year showed $184,000 as having been received from the Z Trust. The return showed that amount as part of a total income in excess of $26 million and that the net income after expenses was in excess of $23 million. It showed also that all but $86 of that income was distributed to the sole beneficiary of the trust, K Co., as trustee of the J Unit Trust.
  4. On 15 November 1979 E Co. applied to L Co. for a loan of $174,000. The directors of L Co. were the same as those of K Co. The loan was duly made. Then on 12 December 1979 L Co., on the application of E Co., assigned to A Co. as trustee of the A Management Trust its right title and interest in the loan made to E Co. The consideration given by A Co. to L Co. for the assignment was a sum of $1,000. A Co. paid the $1,000 to L Co. and, the assignment having taken effect, E Co. discharged its indebtedness to A Co. as the trustee of the A Management Trust by paying $174,000 to it. Thus $173,000 of the original $200,000 came back to A Co. for the benefit of the same beneficiaries as before, $16,000 having been retained by B Co., $10,000 by S Co. and $1,000 by L Co.
  5. On 21 November H and W as directors of A Co. in its capacity as trustee of the A Family Trust resolved to appoint 21 November 1979 as the vesting date of that trust and to distribute the net income of the trust fund in the manner set out in a deed presented to the meeting. Annexed to the minutes as maintained in the minute book was a copy of what purported to be the deed presented to the meeting. On that same day A Co. as the trustee of the A Family Trust executed a deed appointing 21 November 1979 as the vesting day and providing that "the balance of the net income of the Trust Fund for the last Accounting Period as remains at the Vesting Date be divided between, set aside for, and henceforth absolutely belong to the undermentioned beneficiaries (sic) in the following proportions (sic)" and then showing alongside the name of H "the whole of the net income of the Trust Fund" and "the whole of the Trust Fund". That deed was not in precisely the same terms as the copy annexed to the minutes which provided that for "the net income for the last Accounting Period", instead of "the balance of the net income for the last Accounting Period as remains at the Vesting Date", to belong to H on the vesting of the trust.

  1. Mrs Moshinsky argued the respondent's case on two alternative bases, first that the purported distribution by A Co. to B Co. was a sham and second that the scheme constituted an arrangement which was absolutely void as against the Commissioner by virtue of section 260 of the Act. She did not argue that there was a fiscal nullity because the Tribunal would have been bound by decisions of the courts to reject any such argument. However, she reserved the respondent's right to seek to rely on fiscal nullity and to present arguments in support of that at the appropriate stage if there was an appeal against the decision in these proceedings. Mr Reicher conceded that some of the transactions undertaken in order to give effect to the scheme prepared by Y were artificial and could not be regarded as transactions entered into for normal family or commercial purposes. Although he did not expressly concede that the arrangements were caught by section 260, he did not present any arguments that they were not. However, he submitted that, if they were so caught, the result was that A Co. as trustee of the A Family Trust remained liable to pay tax on the income of $200,000, that it was not liable to pay tax as the trustee of S and D and that none of W, S and D was liable to do so personally in respect of any part of the income of the trust.
  2. In Scott v Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth (No. 2) (1966) 40 ALJR 265 at 279 Windeyer J. discussed the nature of "sham" in relation to schemes to avoid or minimize liability to taxation. He said:-

". . . But it is not enough to say that a fund governed by the provisions of a deed such as that we have here could be a superannuation fund within the meaning of the Act. For it to be so in fact the parties concerned must have intended that the deed should take effect and operate according to its tenor; that a fund should be set up subjected to the trusts of the deed; and that Associated Provident Funds should as trustee be bound to carry out those trusts. On the other hand, if the scheme, including the deed, was intended to be a mere facade behind which activities might be carried on which were not to be really directed to the stated purposes but to other ends, then the words of the deed should be disregarded. It was urged for the appellant Associated Provident Funds that it is a real company and that the deed was really executed by it; and that, it was said, is the end of the question. But it is not. A disguise is a real thing: it may be an elaborate and carefully prepared thing; but it is nevertheless a disguise. The difficult and debatable philosophic questions of the meaning and relationship of reality, substance and form are for the purposes of our law generally resolved by asking did the parties who entered into the ostensible transaction mean it to be in truth their transaction, or did they mean it to be, and in fact use it as, merely a disguise, a facade, a sham, a false front - all these words have been metaphorically used - concealing their real transaction ..."

20. In the following year Diplock L.J. said in Snook v ondon and West Riding Investments Ltd. (1967) 2 QB 786 at 802:-

"As regards the contention of the plaintiff that the transactions between himself, Auto Finance and the defendants were a 'sham', it is, I think, necessary to consider what, if any, legal concept is involved in the use of this popular and pejorative word. I apprehend that, if it has any meaning in law, it means acts done or documents executed by the parties to the 'sham' which are intended by them to give to third parties or to the court the appearance of creating between the parties legal rights and obligations different from the actual legal rights and obligations (if any) which the parties intend to create. But one thing, I think, is clear in legal principle, morality and the authorities (see Yorkshire Railway Wagon Co. v. Mac lure (1882) 21 Ch.D. 309, C.A. and Stoneleicrh Finance Ltd. v. Phillips [1965] 2 Q.B. 537), that for acts or documents to be a 'sham', with whatever legal consequences follow from this, all the parties hereto must have a common intention that the acts or documents are not to create the legal rights and obligations which they give the appearance of creating. No unexpressed intentions of a 'shammer' affect the rights of a party whom he deceived. There is an express finding in this case that the defendants were not parties to the alleged 'sham'. So this contention fails." (emphasis added)

21. When H was cross-examined by Mrs Moshinsky, he was asked hat the intentions of himself, his wife and A Co. were when they ook the various steps to give effect to the scheme. He said hat Y had explained to him in detail what the steps and the effects of them would he and had assured him that all other persons who had to take steps to give effect to the scheme would take those steps in accordance with the scheme. That meant that B Co. would deal with the $200,000 income which it received as a result of the distribution by A Co. in a manner which would result in S Co. receiving $184,000 as trustee for the S Unit Trust and that, when K Co. had received the $184,000 as beneficiary of the S Unit Trust, its related company L Co. would make a loan of $174,000 to E Co. and then assign to A Co. its right to be repaid that loan for a consideration of only $1,000. H gave evidence that he would not have entered into the scheme if he had not had great trust in Y that the money paid out to B Co. would not be lost to A Co., save to the extent of the $27,000 which was to be retained in effect as the fees of Y's firm of solicitors and the promoters of the scheme.

22. He clearly did not intend the distribution to B Co. as the trustee of the Z Trust to create the legal right which on its face the transaction gave to B Co. as such trustee to distribute the money in turn to any beneficiary of that trust in accordance with the virtually unfettered discretion which it had under the terms of that trust. He did not intend that S Co., having received $184,000 by way of distribution from the Z Trust should be free to distribute it to its beneficiary free of any obligation on the part of that beneficiary to take the next step required to give effect to the scheme. He did not intend that L Co. should be free to exercise its normal contractual rights to recover the loan from E Co. and to decline to assign its rights to do so upon payment of a totally inadequate consideration.

  1. Y was not called to give evidence. However, from both the circumstances of the scheme and the inferences that can be drawn from the failure of the applicant to call him as a witness, I am satisfied that Y did not intend the acts and documents to create the legal rights and obligations which they gave the appearance of creating. Mr Reicher submitted that the transactions were not to be regarded as "sham" simply because they were artificial. That submission may well be correct; I express no opinion on that question. But in this case there is not simply artificiality. There is, as I have found, a mere appearance of creating legal rights and obligations which the parties had a common intention should not be created by the acts and documents which purported to create them.
  2. However, Mrs Moshinsky did not base her arguments in support of "sham" solely on the intentions of the persons involved in the scheme. She submitted that there was a sham also because the nomination of B Co. in its capacity as trustee of the Z Trust as a beneficiary of the A Family Trust was invalid and that consequently the purported distribution of the $200,000 by A Co. to B Co. as the trustee of the Z Trust was not in fact such a distribution. It was merely an unauthorised payment. She submitted that the nomination was invalid because for two reasons the Z Trust was not an "eligible trust" within the terms of the deed of settlement of the A Family Trust. First, the settlor of the A Family Trust was not an excluded person for the purposes of the nomination of an eligible trust to be a beneficiary of the Z Trust; second, all interests or expectancies in the Z Trust might not vest before the expiration of the perpetuity period named in the A Family Trust.
  3. As to the first of those matters it is to be noted that the amendment made to the deed of settlement of the A Family Trust on 9 November 1979, that is to say the substitution of a new definition of "eligible trust", did not have any effect on the prohibition, contained in the proviso to clause 1.(b)(1)(iii) of the deed, of the nomination of a corporation in which the settlor had any actual or contingent beneficial interest. However, on 9 November 1979 the settlor of the A Family Trust did not in fact have either an actual or a contingent beneficial interest in the Z Trust and there is no evidence before the Tribunal to indicate that there was ever any likelihood that she would be nominated as a beneficiary of that trust. I am, therefore, unable to accept as correct the first basis of Mrs Moshinsky's submission.
  4. However, I am satisfied that the Z Trust was not an eligible trust for the second of the reasons she gave. Mr Reicher submitted that, because of developments in the law relating to perpetuities and also in the attitude of the courts towards the exercise of powers by trustees for the benefit of their trust funds, A Co. had, or should be regarded as having had, the power to nominate the Z Trust, notwithstanding that according to the terms of the settlement deeds it was not strictly within the definition of an eligible trust. I am unable to accede to that submission. First, whatever a court might have done if A Co. had sought its authority to make the nomination, no such authority was in fact sought. Second, the nomination was made as a step in a series of transactions which were artificial in the extreme; their validity, if they were to be valid, depended on each step being taken strictly in accordance with the legal requirements for that step to have its desired legal effect. This is not the sort of situation to which Mr Reicher has referred where the courts have endeavoured to give effect to the equities rather than the legalities. Accordingly, the nomination of B Co. in its capacity as trustee of the Z Trust as a beneficiary of the A Family Trust was not valid and cannot be treated as having been valid. Consequently, the purported distribution to B Co. as such trustee was not a valid distribution and cannot be treated as though it were.
  5. Accordingly, I accept Mrs Moshinsky's submission that, if the transaction should not otherwise be regarded as a sham, it certainly was not effective as a distribution of income of the trust fund. Whether that can properly be called a "sham" I rather doubt; however, that is immaterial. What is important is that there was no valid distribution of income of the trust fund. By 21 November 1979 no other determination to distribute the income had been made. Consequently the whole of the income of the trust fund remained undistributed on that date. It is, therefore, necessary to consider the effect of what was done by H, W and A Co. on 21 November 1979.
  6. As already noted, H and W as directors of A Co. resolved to appoint 21 November 1979 as the vesting date of the trust and to distribute the net income of the trust fund as set out in the deed presented to the meeting. Again as I have noted above, the company executed on that day a deed the terms of which were slightly different from those of the copy, annexed to the minutes of the meeting, of the draft deed which was apparently what was presented to the meeting. The first question that arises is whether, in view of that difference, the execution of the deed was within the powers of the trustee. In the circumstances in which the resolution was passed and the deed executed there was no difference in the effect of the two alternatives. As there had been no valid distribution of income by 21 November, the net income of the trust for the last accounting period (which under the provisions of the deed of settlement was the date on which the trust vested) was the same amount as the balance of the net income on that day. I have come to the conclusion, therefore, that prima facie the vesting deed was validly executed.
  7. The second question is whether the execution of the vesting deed was a sham. I am satisfied that it was not. H and W as the directing mind of A Co. intended the deed to have effect according to its terms. Consequently, the whole of the income of the A Family Trust for the 1980 taxation year passed to H on 21 November 1979. W had not, and did not thereafter, become presently entitled to any part of that income; so no liability to pay tax on any part of it arose in her case. Nor had S and D, who were infants and therefore under a legal disability, become presently entitled to any part of it, and they did not do so thereafter; so A Co. was not liable under section 98 of the Act to pay tax on any part of it as their trustee.
  8. I turn now to consider whether the arrangements were caught by section 260 of the Act. As already noted, Mr Reicher went a considerable way towards conceding that they were so caught. I have no doubt that they were. Mr Reicher, however, submitted that, because section 260 only "annihilates" transactions forming part of the scheme and does not reconstruct a different set of facts from that which existed before the transactions were undertaken, and because the transactions were void only as against the Commissioner, only A Co. could be held liable under section 99 or section 99A of the Act to pay tax as trustee on the income of the trust. Any further step to give effect to the default provisions in the deed of settlement would, he submitted, be engaging in reconstruction. In respect of a similar argument advanced in Stamp v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 88 ATC 4803 Pincus J. said at page 4809:-

"In my opinion, the effect one gives to the principle on which Mr Harrison relies depends very largely on how one chooses to describe the transactions in question. It must be admitted that one could contend that there is more than annihilation involved, for if the Commissioner is right, there notionally comes into existence (so far as the appellant's tax affairs are concerned) a right having no previous or other existence. Alternatively, one could say that there is no reconstruction, for one simply strips away the distribution which in fact occurred, leaving exposed a set of facts having adverse tax consequences for the appellant . . . Conceding that these difficulties exist, I am of the view that the 'annihilation only' principle cannot save the appellant here. That is so because one does not have to postulate the occurrence of any additional event or transaction to achieve the result for which the Commissioner contends. It is true that one treats as existing a right to income which would not otherwise exist, but that does not appear to me to require any reconstruction."

The situation in the present case appears to me to be one to which the conclusion of His Honour would be equally applicable.

  1. Mrs Moshinsky submitted that the execution of the vesting deed on 21 November 1979 was part of the arrangement to avoid tax, so that it was void as against the Commissioner. I do not accept that that was so; the arrangement (or scheme, as I have called it above) was intended to cause A Co. as the trustee of the A Management Trust to receive the $173,000 as capital. The vesting of the A Family Trust was unnecessary as a step in the tax avoidance scheme. It could itself have had no effect of enabling any person to avoid a liability to tax which that person would otherwise have had. I am, therefore, satisfied that it was not void as against the Commissioner. Its effect was that the whole of the income of the A Family Trust for the 1980 taxation year passed as income to H. None of it passed to W, S or D and none of them ever became presently entitled to it.
  2. If, contrary to the conclusion which I have reached, the vesting deed was void against the Commissioner, that would still not have resulted in W, S or D becoming presently entitled to any part of the income of the trust in the 1980 taxation year. The default provision in the deed of settlement for distribution of income applied only in respect of any part of the net income of the trust "as shall not be the subject of a determination [to accumulate or distribute] effectively made at or prior to the end of" an accounting period. So it could not have taken effect until after the end of the accounting period. By virtue of clause 1(n) of the deed of settlement, if the trust had not vested before then, the accounting period which contained the month of November 1979 would have ended on 30 June 1980. Consequently the default distribution would have taken place in the 1981 taxation year.
  3. H was assessed as liable to pay $30,754.06 in respect of additional tax under section 226(2) of the Act. In view of the conclusion which I have reached that she was not liable to pay the primary tax to which the additional tax related, she is not liable to pay that additional tax.
