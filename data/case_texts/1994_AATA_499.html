EXTRACT OF TRANSCRIPT OF PROCEEDINGS



MR ALLEN: In this matter the applicant seeks to make what is termed a late election, pursuant to the provisions of section 157 of the Superannuation Act 1976. At the outset it is perhaps appropriate to set out the history of the matter. The applicant became employed by the Australian Capital Territory Health Authority on 23 July 1973. Prior to that date he had been employed for some two years at the Rockhampton Base Hospital which meant, of course, he was an employee of the Queensland State Public Service. Prior to that he had been employed in Malaysia.



He then resigned from the ACT Health Service, with effect from 30 November 1976. He had in the interim, on 1 July 1976, become an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976. After his resignation he accepted a refund of accumulated contributions and that took place on 18 January 1977. On 31 July 1990 he made a late election to preserve the superannuation rights. Those rights may be preserved pursuant to section 137 of the Superannuation Act 1976. Now, taking even the most liberal view of matters in account of the applicant and accepting that his date of service for the purposes of the Superannuation Act began on 23 July 1973, his resignation in November 1976 did not, of course, amount to five years service. That is important, because under section 139(1)(b), it is provided:



Where a person makes an election under section 137 and he is not employed in public employment -



which was the case here:



- and does not become employed in public employment within that period, but at the time when he ceased to be an eligible employee, he had completed five years eligible employment.



Now, as we have said, even on the widest possible view in relation to the applicant, at the date of his resignation he had not completed five years eligible employment. Indeed, it was much less because he did not become an eligible employee for the purposes of the act until 1 July 1976. So that even if the tribunal were to consider the matter and decide in the exercise of its discretion that the applicant should be allowed to make a late election, the effect of that late election is entirely negative because he does not qualify under section 139 as not having the five year service and that is the end of the matter



bhuate 18.5.94 2



The only other reference that needs to be made is to section 132 which was referred to by the applicant. Subsection (c) thereof refers to a period of employment, whether within or outside Australia, during which the applicant was the member of a superannuation scheme, etcetera. The applicant pointed to that and said that he had been a member of a scheme in Malaysia. However, subsection (4) introduces qualifications to that, plus it is also doubtful whether it is applicable to schemes which are not schemes constructed by an Australian Government or body, but notwithstanding that, paragraph 4 makes the matter inapplicable unless there is a lump sum obtained by the applicant which was paid to the commissioner that did not happen in this case. Consequently, that section has no application either. Looking at the whole of the matter stated, pursuant to section 138, there is no way this applicant can qualify. Therefore, the decision under review is affirmed.
