REASONS FOR DECISION



1. The applicant seeks a review of that part of a decision of the Commissioner of Taxation ("the Commissioner") which disallowed the applicant's objection to an amended assessment made by the Commissioner of the taxpayer's income tax liability for the year ended 30 June 1987.



2. At the hearing of this matter, Mr Edmonds, of counsel appeared for the applicant. Dr Sorenson, of counsel, appeared for the respondent. The Tribunal had before it the documents (the T documents) lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. The applicant also tendered a letter dated 4 May 1992 from the Australian Taxation Office to the applicant (Exhibit 1); six letters dated 28 February 1990 to 15 November 1990 between the Australian Taxation Office and Mr A G Harris, Secretary, Ampol Provident Fund Management Pty Ltd (Exhibit 2); a Trust Deed constituting Ampol Provident Trust Fund (Exhibit 3); a trust deed constituting Ampol Provident No 2 Trust Fund (Exhibit 4); Annual Reports of Ampol Provident Fund for the years ending 31 March 1984, 1985, 1986 and 1987 (Exhibit 5); a statement of evidence of the applicant (Exhibit 6); a statement of evidence of Mr A G Harris (Exhibit 7); a bundle of documents headed "[Applicant] - Terminated E/R 3/4/87" consisting of copies of cheque butts, bank statements and one handwritten sheet (Exhibit 8); a statement of Mr M Stevenson (Exhibit 9); a report dated 3 April 1990 by Mercer Campbell Cook and Knight (Exhibit 10); a notice of assessment for year ended 30 June 1987 (Exhibit 11); notices of assessment for 1984 - 1986 and correspondence between Blake Dawson Waldron and the Ombudsman (Exhibit 12); group certificates for the years 1984 to 1986 for the applicant (Exhibit 13); a document prepared by the Taxation Officer at Ampol Ltd as summary of employees' excess benefit in the light of the Mercer Campbell Cook and Knight report (Exhibit 14). The respondent tendered a tax return (Form F) for No 2 Fund for year ending 31 March 1987 (Exhibit A); a tax return (Form F) and revenue account for year ending 31 March 1988 (Exhibit B); a document headed "Ampol Provident Fund No 2" (Exhibit C).



3. Before saying anything further about the circumstances of the application, it is convenient to set out s26AFA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Assessment Act"). The provision in the form relevant to the present application is as follows:

(1) Where--



(a) in a year of income and on or after 7 December 1983, a taxpayer receives or obtains a benefit of any kind out of, or attributable to assets of, a section 23F fund;



(b) the benefit--



(i) ...



(ii) is an excessive benefit; and



(c) the Commissioner is satisfied that the taxpayer received or obtained the benefit--



(i) by reason that the taxpayer was, or had been, a member of the fund;



...,



the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income shall include the amount or value of that benefit.



(2) Where--



(a) sub-section (1) would, but for this sub-section, apply to the amount or value of an excessive benefit received or obtained by a taxpayer out of, or attributable to assets of, a section 23F fund; and



(b) the Commissioner, having regard to--



(i) the nature of the fund;



(ii) the circumstances by reason of which the benefit is an excessive benefit; and



(iii) such other matters relating to the receiving or obtaining of the benefit by the taxpayer as the Commissioner considers relevant,



is satisfied that it would be unreasonable for sub-section (1) to apply to the whole or part of the benefit,



that sub-section does not apply to the benefit, or to that part of the benefit, as the case may be.



(3) ...



(4) In this section--



...



"excessive benefit" means a benefit of any kind that is excessive in amount or value having regard to the matters mentioned in sub-paragraphs 23F(2)(h)(i), (ii), (iii) and iv);



"section 23F fund" means a fund to which section 23F applies, or has applied, in relation to any year of income.



Background to the Review

4. Notice of the original assessment of the applicant's income tax liability for the year ended 30 June 1987 had been given by the Commissioner on 20 October 1987. At that time the Commissioner had treated the sum of $263,908.67 which had been received by the applicant during that year as having been an "eligible termination payment" in accordance with the treatment of that sum by the applicant in his return. On 3 September 1991 the Commissioner gave notice of an amended assessment. In this assessment only $136,439.67 was treated as having been an "eligible termination payment". The balance of $127,469.00 was treated as having been an excessive benefit received by the applicant in the year of income out of, or attributable to assets of, a section 23F fund and received by the applicant because he had been a member of such a fund. In accordance with sub-section 26AFA(2) the Commissioner had considered whether it would be unreasonable for sub-section 26AFA(1) to apply. He had not been satisfied that it would be. He therefore treated that balance as assessable income. The applicant objected to the Commissioner's changed treatment of the balance, but the Commissioner disallowed that objection and it is that disallowance which has led to the present review.



Circumstances of the Receipt of $263,908.67

5. On 3 April 1987 the applicant had retired from his employment with a large public company where he had worked mainly as a storeman for 39 years from the age of 19. He had retired early because of ill health.



6. At the time of the applicant's retirement from his employment, there were two superannuation funds in existence for the employees of his firm. The applicant was a member of both funds. The funds, designated the 'Number One' and the 'Number Two' funds, had a common trustee. At the time of the applicant's retirement, the 'Number One' fund was a section 23F fund within the meaning of sub-section 26AFA(4); the 'Number Two' fund was not.



7. Shortly after the applicant's retirement, the trustee drew two cheques on an account holding moneys to the credit of the 'Number One' fund, one cheque in favour of the applicant for $116,018.47 and the other in favour of the 'Number Two' fund for $147,890.20. The latter cheque was deposited into an account holding monies to the credit of the 'Number Two' fund. The trustee then drew a cheque for $147,890.20 in favour of the applicant from the 'Number Two' fund. The applicant received from the trustee both cheques drawn in his favour which together represented the sum of $263,908.67. A statement declaring that the whole of the sum of $263,908.67 paid to the applicant was an "eligible termination payment" was received by the applicant from the trustee.



8. It was not in dispute before the Tribunal that when he lodged his return for the year ended 30 June 1987 the applicant had believed that the entire sum of $263,908.67 was an "eligible termination payment". It was accepted by the applicant before the Tribunal that this belief was wrong and the $127,469.00 treated by the Commissioner in his amended assessment as assessable income pursuant to sub-section 26AFA(1) had been an excessive benefit within the meaning of section 26AFA.



9. The applicant argued before the Tribunal however that the Commissioner's changed treatment of the $127,469.00 was unjustified on two alternative grounds: first, that at the time when he purported to do so the Commissioner had no longer the power to amend the original assessment so as to increase the applicant's liability; and, secondly, that the Tribunal ought to be satisfied that it would be unreasonable for sub-section 26AFA(1) to apply to that sum. If the Tribunal was so satisfied, by operation of sub-section 26AFA(2) (read together with sub-section 43(6) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act of 1975), sub-section 26AFA(1) would not apply to that sum. The applicant further argued that the Tribunal ought at least be satisfied that it would be unreasonable for sub-section 26AFA(1) to apply to part of the sum of $127.469.00, namely $64,400.00.



10. The first question which the Tribunal must consider is, therefore, that of the power of the Commissioner to amend the applicant's assessment. The determination of that question depends upon which of two former sub-sections of section 170 of the Assessment Act applied in the circumstances.



11. The former sub-sections are 170(2) and (3) which read as follows:

(2) Where a taxpayer has not made to the Commissioner a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment, and there has been an avoidance of tax, the Commissioner may--



(a) where he is of the opinion that the avoidance of tax is due to fraud or evasion--at any time; and



(b) in any other case--within 6 years from the date upon which the tax became due and payable under the assessment,



amend the assessment by making such alterations therein or additions thereto as he thinks necessary to correct the assessment.



(3) Where a taxpayer has made to the Commissioner a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment, and an assessment is made after the disclosure, no amendment of the assessment increasing the liability of the taxpayer in any particular shall be made after the expiration of 3 years from the date upon which the tax became due and payable under the assessment.



12. The Commissioner submitted before the Tribunal that former sub-section 170(2) in particular paragraph (b) thereof was the provision that applied in this case and thus he had power to amend the applicant's original assessment. However, the applicant submitted that the former sub-section 170(3) was the applicable provision in which case the Commissioner had no power to amend. Given the applicant's acceptance before the Tribunal that, of the sum of $263,908.67 which he had received after his retirement, $127,469.00 was an excessive benefit and that there had occurred an "avoidance of tax" within the meaning of that term used in sub-section 170(2), the only issue between the applicant and the Commissioner was whether the applicant had made to the Commissioner in his taxation return for year ended 30 June 1987 a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment.



13. On this issue the applicant bears the burden of persuasion: see McAndrew v FCT [1956] HCA 62; (1956) 98 CLR 263. At the same time, however, it was said in Boyded Industries v FCT (1987) 18 ATR 180, at 191 that:

The taxpayer's onus of proof in relation to s 170 does not require it [or him or her] to speculate as to every matter and specifically to anticipate every conceivable fact which the Commissioner may subsequently argue has not been disclosed to him. The Commissioner bears at least an evidential onus of raising the relevant issues to enable the taxpayer to deal with them in the discharge of its [or his or her] overall burden of proof ...



14. In the present appeal the Commissioner raised only one issue with which the applicant was required to deal and that was that the applicant had failed to make true disclosure of one material fact necessary for his assessment. The Commissioner identified this one material fact as being that $127,469.00 of the $263,908.67 he had received following his retirement had been an excessive benefit within the meaning of section 26AFA. The Commissioner submitted that the applicant could not satisfy his burden of persuasion on that issue because he had not included the excessive benefit of $127,469.00 at item 13 ("Other Income") in his return for the year ended 30 June 1987.



15. We reject the Commissioner's submission. Our reason is that the one matter of which he has alleged the applicant did not make true disclosure was, in our view, not a material fact necessary for the applicant's assessment as that notion was used in sub-section 170(2). Rather than being a material fact necessary for the applicant's assessment, the matter identified by the Commissioner in our view would have been an assertion by the applicant as to the tax consequences of a material fact necessary for his assessment. This material fact had been disclosed by him, namely his receipt of the sum of $263,908.67. All the applicant was required to do was to disclose "all the material facts necessary for his assessment". Assuming the taxpayer had done so, the circumstance that he failed to assert correctly their tax consequences was irrelevant for the purpose of the application of section 170(2). The distinction for the purpose of this section that we have made was stated by Windeyer J in W Thomas & Co Pty Ltd v FCT [1965] HCA 54; (1965) 115 CLR 58 at 76, in the following way:

[W]hat must be disclosed are the material facts ... It does not seem to me to be correct to say that if these be truly and sufficiently stated the effect of doing so is neutralized by a claim based upon an erroneous view of their legal complexion and consequence.



16. A number of years later, Stephen J, in A L Hamblin Equipment Pty Ltd v FCT [1974] HCA 1; (1974) 130 CLR 159, 175, was able to say concerning the former subsection 170(2), "It is well established that the disclosure required is of the relevant facts and not of the tax consequences which they may produce".



17. The Commissioner relied in support of his submissions upon two authorities, namely Levy v FCT [1961] HCA 92; (1961) 106 CLR 448 and the Boyded Industries case from the latter of which cases a quotation has already been set out above.



18. In our view, neither of those authorities assists the Commissioner in the present application.



19. Levy's case is, of course, a leading decision of the High Court on the notion of a taxpayer's making a true disclosure of all material facts necessary for his assessment, but the distinction referred to above as having been made subsequent to Levy's case both by Windeyer J in the Thomas case and by Stephen J in the Hamblin case was not discussed in Levy's case, no doubt, because the issue simply did not arise in it. In that case there had been a misstatement as to what was clearly a matter of material fact necessary for a taxpayer's assessment, namely, the total amount of moneys paid by a partnership to persons supplying materials to that partnership for the purpose of its business.



20. The Boyded Industries case, the only other authority relied upon by the Commissioner, was a decision of Hunt J of the Supreme Court of New South Wales. Among the issues in that case was whether a partnership had failed to make true disclosure of a material fact necessary for its assessment. The partnership had received a dividend from a company, which receipt it had disclosed in its tax return, at the same time asserting that the dividend was exempt. It was conceded by the taxpayer before Hunt J that the dividend had not been exempt. According to his Honour at 192,

The taxpayer has ... argued that the question of whether the dividend was exempt is one of law and not one of fact, so that the mis-statement did not fall within the terms of s 170(2). That is no doubt so of the resolution of the question in the courts. But here the partnership has stated it as a fact (or, more accurately perhaps, as a conclusion) that the dividend was exempt. That fact or that conclusion has turned out to be incorrect. In my view, that mis-statement does fall within the terms of s 170(2).



The approach taken in the passage just quoted appears to us to be contrary to that taken earlier both by Windeyer and Stephen JJ and also conflicts with Slater Holdings Ltd v FCT (1980) 10 ATR 711. In the Slater Holdings case, Hunt J had stated that "an incorrect claim for deduction based upon an incorrect view of the law does not amount to a failure to disclose", and had relied for that statement on the Thomas case.



21. The Tribunal adopts in this case the approach taken by Windeyer and Stephen JJ in the Thomas and Hamblin cases respectively and concludes that the sole matter identified by the Commissioner as a material fact necessary for the taxpayer's assessment of which he failed to make true disclosure was not a fact at all for the purpose of the former sub-section 170(2).



22. Given that in the present application the Commissioner's case was that there was only one material fact necessary for his assessment of which the taxpayer had failed to make true disclosure and given also that we have rejected the Commissioner's submission in that regard, it is no more necessary for the Tribunal to consider, than it was for the taxpayer to persuade the Tribunal of, the situation regarding other matters which were truly material facts necessary for the taxpayer's assessment. However, we do not believe it inappropriate to mention that a consulting actuary gave evidence before the Tribunal in the present application, upon which evidence he was not cross-examined by the Commissioner, that the Commissioner had before him, at the time he made his original assessment of the taxpayer's income tax liability for the year ended 30 June 1987, all the material facts necessary for him to determine the excessive benefit component, if any, of the sum of $263,908.67 which the taxpayer had received.



23. Having held that at the time when he purported to do so the Commissioner had no longer had the power to amend the applicant's original assessment for the year ended 30 June 1987 so as to increase the applicant's liability, we do not reach the issue of whether it would be unreasonable for subsection 26AFA(1) to apply to the whole or part of the excessive benefit received by the applicant.



24. In so far as the Commissioner decided to disallow the applicant's objection to the Commissioner's treatment of the sum of $127,469 as an excessive benefit, the Tribunal sets aside the decision under review.
