VT86/2275 and Commissioner of Taxation [1990] AATA 323; (1990) 21 ATR 3161; 90 ATC 175 (5 January 1990)

VT86/2275 and Commissioner of Taxation [1990] AATA 323 (5 January 1990); (1990) 21 ATR 3161; 90 ATC 175

Last Updated: 27 May 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - unpaid company tax - copy of notice of assessment of company's tax liability served - opinion that vendors recoupment tax likely to be payable precondition for service - Commissioner subsequently forming opinion vendors recoupment tax not likely to be payable - person served still wishing Tribunal to review assessment of company's tax liability - whether Tribunal has power to do so

Procedure - hearing of preliminary issue - affidavit tendered at hearing containing inadequate evidence - applicant's representative unprepared as to facts - hearing to be resumed -directions given to ensure all relevant evidence presented and parties properly prepared

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ss. 3(1), 5, 6, 18(5) & (8)

Case W53 89 ATC 489

Duggan and Ryall v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 72 ATC 4239

RULING AND REASONS

Re:

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. VT86/2275

AAT Decision No 5618

Deputy President I. R. Thompson

Data: 5 January 1990

) No. VT86/2275

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

Respondent

RULING AND DIRECTIONS

Tribunal : Deputy President I. R. Thompson

Date : 5 January 1990

Place : Melbourne

Decision : 1. On the evidence at present before the Tribunal it would be premature for it to decide whether or not it has power to hear and determine the deemed application for review in these proceedings.

  1. The hearing of the said deemed application is to be resumed, on a date of which the District Registrar is to give written notice to the parties, for consideration of such further evidence as is contained in affidavits, if any, lodged with the Tribunal in accordance with paragraphs 3 and 4 below.
  2. If the applicant does not accept that the opinions expressed by C in paragraphs 6 and 7 of his affidavit sworn on 18 December 1989 are soundly based, he is to lodge with the Tribunal and serve on the respondent not less than 21 days before the date of the resumed hearing an affidavit or affidavits setting out evidence showing that he and the other persons named in the said paragraphs 6 and 7 are persons by whom vendors recoupment tax is likely to become payable in relation to ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax that is payable or may become payable by the company referred to in the said paragraphs 6 and 7 for the years of income referred to in those paragraphs.
  3. If the applicant lodges and serves an affidavit or affidavits in accordance with paragraph 3 above, and if the respondent still intends to maintain that the copy of the notice of assessment should not have been served on the persons referred to in paragraphs 6 and 7 of C's affidavit, he shall, not less than 14 days before the date of the resumed hearing, lodge with the Tribunal and serve on the applicant an affidavit or affidavits stating which of the evidence set out in the affidavit or affidavits lodged and served by the applicant is incorrect and setting out such evidence as he relies on to establish that.
  4. If either party wishes to cross-examine any person whose affidavit has been lodged in accordance with paragraph 3 or paragraph 4 above, that party shall give notice in writing of that to the other party not less than 7 days before the date of the resumed hearing.
  5. Except with leave of the Tribunal, no witness shall give evidence at the resumed hearing unless an affidavit sworn by him or her has been lodged and served in accordance with paragraph 3 or paragraph 4 above.

(Sgd I. R. Thompson)

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. VT86/2275 TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

REASONS FOR RULING AND DIRECTIONS

5 January 1990 Deputy President I. R. Thompson

1. In June 1975 the respondent served on a company, N Pty. Ltd. ("the company"), an assessment, made pursuant to section 166 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITA Act"), of the amount of its taxable income during the taxation year which ended on 30 June 1972 and of the tax payable thereon. The company was at that time being wound up and on 20 August 1975 the liquidator lodged with the respondent an objection in writing against the assessment. Subsequently that objection was withdrawn. The company was then dissolved.

2. In July 1983 the respondent served on three persons, of whom the applicant was one, a copy of the notice of assessment. That was done pursuant to section 18(5) of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("the T(UCT)A Act"). The other two persons then nominated the applicant as the nominated party to sign objections on behalf of the company. However, in the event, an objection was signed by all three of the persons, including the applicant. The objection was disallowed by the respondent. The applicant then requested him to refer the objection to a Board of Review pursuant to section 187 of the ITA Act, as then in force. In February 1986 it was referred to Taxation Board of Review No. 2. It had not been reviewed by that Board by 30 June 1986, when the Taxation Boards of Review were abolished and the Administrative Appeals Tribunal was given power and required to hear and determine objections which had been referred to the Boards but not reviewed (Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986, section 223).

3. Because of the enormous number of objection decisions which and been referred to the Taxation Boards of Review but not reviewed by 30 June 1986, and because also of the enormous number of additional objection decisions subsequently referred by the respondent to the Tribunal, the review of the objection decision in these proceedings was not commenced by the Tribunal until December 1989. At the hearing of the matter the applicant was represented by Mr B. Saunders, an accountant, and the respondent by Mr G. A. A. Nettle, of Counsel. Because of an unusual turn which events had taken in October 1989 it was agreed that the effect of that turn of events should be considered as a preliminary issue. Arguments in relation to it were addressed to the Tribunal by Mr Saunders and Mr Nettle. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (as modified by section 142G of the Taxation Administration Act 1953) and an affidavit sworn on 18 December 1989 by C, the Manager, Recoupment Tax Section, Australian Taxation Office, Melbourne; a number of documents were exhibited to that affidavit.

4. The unusual turn of events to which I have referred was the dispatch to the applicant of a letter dated 10 October 1989 signed by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation which was in the following terms:-

"TAXATION (UNPAID COMPANY TAX) ASSESSMENT ACT 1982

[N] PTY. LTD. (NOW DEFUNCT)

Reference is made to the notice issued to you on 15 July 1983, and to the copy of a copy notice of assessment, served on you as a preliminary step forward to the raising of a recoupment tax assessment.

It is advised the facts and evidence surrounding the issue of the notice on 15 July 1983, have recently been reviewed. As a result of this review, it has been determined the opinion formed, that yourself and Messrs [X] and [Y] were likely to be liable for recoupment tax in relation to the unpaid company tax of [N] Pty. Ltd., was erroneous.

Accordingly, the notices of 15 July 1983, to yourself and Messrs [X] and [Y], are deemed to have been incorrectly issued and are not supported by the relevant provisions of the abovementioned Act.

The 'Notices' issued on 15 July 1983, are hereby withdrawn and it is confirmed your liability as to recoupment tax in [N] Pty. Ltd., is nil."

5. Subsequently, on 18 December 1989 letters in similar terms were sent by the Deputy Commissioner to the other two persons to whom a copy of the notice of assessment of the company's income and tax liability had been sent pursuant to section 18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act.

6. In the affidavit sworn by C he stated that he held an authorisation from the respondent to form opinions for the purpose of section 18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act and was duly authorised to make the affidavit on the respondent's behalf. Paragraph 6 and 7 of the affidavit are as follows:-

"6. I have since that time reviewed the information in the possession of the Commissioner and I have formed the opinion that at the time of service of the copy notices of assessment on upon the Applicant and Messrs [Y] and [X] recoupment tax was not likely to become payable by any of the Applicant or Messrs [Y] and [X] in relation to ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax that was payable or might become payable by [N] Pty Ltd for the years of income ended 30 June 1971 and 1972.

7. I am also of the opinion that there is not now - any basis for service of copies of the notices of assessment which were served on the Applicant and Messrs [Y] and [X] and that none of those persons is liable under TUCT for recoupment tax in respect of [N] Pty Ltd for the years of income ended 30 June 1971 and 1972."

7. The preliminary issue which the Tribunal was required to address was whether or not it now had power to review the assessment of the company's income and income tax liability for the taxation year which ended on 30 June 1972. Mr Nettle submitted, in effect, that as the result of the action taken by the respondent in October 1989 the applicant no longer had any interest in the assessment of the company's income tax liability and could not proceed with his application for review of the objection decision in these proceedings. Mr Saunders, on the other hand, contended that, once a person had been served with a copy of a notice of assessment pursuant to section 18(5) of the T(UCT)A Act, section 18(8) gave him a right, which no subsequent act of the respondent could take away, to object to the assessment and to pursue his objection by way of proceedings in the Tribunal for review of the respondent's decision in respect of it.

8. Section 18(5) and (8) are, so far as is relevant in these proceedings, as follows:-

"18. (5) Where –

.........

(b) the Commissioner is of the opinion that vendors recoupment tax is likely to become payable by a person or persons in relation to ordinary company tax or undistributed profits tax, as the case may be, that is payable or may became payable by the company in relation to that year of income,

the following provisions have effect:

(c) the Commissioner shall serve a copy of the notice of assessment on -

(i) where, in the opinion of the Commissioner, the vendors recoupment tax is likely to become payable by only one person - that person; and

(ii) in any other case - any of the persons referred to in paragraph (b);

.........

(8) Where a copy of a notice of assessment is served under sub-section (5) in relation to the liability of a company to pay company tax in relation to a year of income –

.........

(b) if the copy was served under sub-paragraph (5)(c)(ii) - one person who -

(i) is included in the representative class in relation to the recoupment tax referred to in paragraph (5)(b);

and

(ii) is nominated, by notice signed by more than one half of the persons included in that class, has the same rights under Division 2 of Part V of the Assessment Act in relation to that liability as the company would have had if the notice of assessment had been served on the company on the date on which the copy of the notice was served under sub-section (5) and, for the purpose of the exercise of those rights by the person, the provisions of that Division apply in like manner as those provisions would apply in relation to the exercise of those rights by the company."

9. The Assessment Act referred to in section 18(8) is the ITA Act. Division 2 of Part V of that Act contains the provisions for lodging an objection against an assessment (section 185), for requesting reference of an objection decision to the Tribunal (section 187) and for the referral of an objection decision to the Tribunal to be deemed to constitute the making by the taxpayer of an application to the Tribunal to review the objection decision (section 189(2)).

10. In section 3(1) of the T(UCT)A Act "vendors recoupment tax" is defined as meaning tax assessed under that Act and imposed by the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax - Vendors) Act 1982. The circumstances in which vendors recoupment tax is to be assessed, so far as the primary taxable amount is concerned, are set out in section 5 of the T(UCT)A Act. A person in respect of whom vendors recoupment tax may be assessed pursuant to section 5 may also in certain circumstances be liable to pay a secondary taxable amount by virtue of section 6.

11. As has frequently been emphasized by the Tribunal, the respondent not only has a power to assess any tax payable by a person but he also has a duty to do so if the facts as known to him, or as he believes them to be, are such that by virtue of the provisions of the ITA Act, or of any other Act such as the T(UCT)A Act, tax is assessable. However, he has no power to assess tax as payable when he knows that it is not payable. It appears from C's affidavit, although not expressly stated, that the respondent has now been able to ascertain facts which have caused him to conclude that vendors recoupment tax is not likely to become payable by the applicant and the other two persons on whom a copy of the notice of the company's assessment was served. If he has indeed ascertained such facts, he cannot now properly assess any amount of vendors recoupment tax as payable by any of those persons.

12. Mr Nettle submitted that the proper formation of an opinion that vendors recoupment tax was likely to become payable by those persons was a condition precedent to serving on them a copy of the notice of assessment of the company's tax liability. He based that submission on the decision of the Tribunal in Case W53 89 ATC 489 where it held that, in hearing and determining a deemed application by a person served with a copy of a notice of assessment under section 18(5)(c), it was not restricted to deciding only whether the Commissioner had formed such an opinion as was referred to in section 18(5)(b). If it found that such an opinion could not properly have been formed on the facts as established before the Tribunal, it should decide that the statutory pre-condition for the service of the notice of assessment had not been met, so that there had been no effective service, and it should therefore set aside the objection decision. Mr Nettle submitted further that, if after serving a copy of a notice of assessment pursuant to section 18(5)(c) the respondent formed a new opinion that the previous opinion was wrong, the service of the notice was to be regarded as having been without effect.

13. In Duggan and Ryall v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 72 ATC 4239 Stephen J. set aside assessments made under section 99A of the ITA Act on the ground that the Commissioner's opinion that it would be unreasonable that the section apply in relation to a trust estate in relation to a year of income was vitiated by error. His Honour's view was that that was the equivalent of failure to reach such an opinion.

14. However, in the present proceedings the Tribunal does not have any evidence of the facts on which the respondent formed either his original opinion - if indeed he did form such an opinion - or his new opinion. Mr Saunders informed the Tribunal that he did not know whether the facts were such that the respondent should have formed either of the opinions. The terms in which C's affidavit is expressed leave open the possibility that the evidence on which the respondent's new opinion is based is the same as that on which he based his original opinion and that he has simply changed his view of the legal consequences of the facts disclosed by that evidence. I am unwilling, therefore, at this stage to accept the statements contained in the affidavit as the basis for finding that the original opinion was not, or should not have been, formed and that the copy of the notice of assessment should not have been served.

15. Mr Nettle contended that, because the respondent had now formed his new opinion, the applicant no longer had any interest in the assessment of the company's tax liability. I do not accept that that is so. As the respondent served the copy of the notice of assessment in purported pursuance of section 18(5)(c), there is reason to believe that before doing so he may have formed an opinion such as was prerequisite to his doing so. In the absence of any evidence as to the basis for the new opinion, the possibility cannot be ruled out that he will change his mind again and hold once more an opinion that vendors recoupment tax is likely to be payable by the applicant and the other two persons. Mr Nettle suggested that, if that occurred, it would be oppressive and the applicant would be able to obtain from a court an injunction to restrain the respondent from pursuing that course of action. That may be so but, if the opinion were found then to be soundly based, there is no certainty that an injunction would be granted. In my view, unless the Tribunal is satisfied either that no such opinion as is required by section 18(5) was formed or that the opinion that was formed then had no proper basis, the applicant continues to have an interest in having the assessment of the company's tax liability reviewed.

16. However, C's affidavit has cast sufficient doubt on whether an opinion prerequisite for the service of the copy of the notice of assessment was propenly formed for it to be necessary to decide whether or not the Tribunal has power to review the assessment. The evidence at present before the Tribunal is quite inadequate for it to do that. It is deplorable that, although the respondent wrote to the applicant in October 1989 informing him of his new opinion, C's affidavit was not lodged with the Tribunal or served on the applicant before the hearing of the preliminary issue had commenced and that Mr Saunders had failed to ascertain from the applicant, who is the person best placed to know the extent of his shareholding in the company in 1972 and other relevant matters, facts showing whether," or not the new opinion was soundly based. It was also most unsatisfactory that paragraphs 6 and 7 of C's affidavit were expressed in terms of his now holding certain opinions without any facts being stated in relation to the opinion formed before the copy of the notice of assessment was served or, if such was the case, the failure to form such an opinion, or to show the basis for the opinions now held.

17. The hearing of the preliminary issue will have to be resumed. But I am giving directions which, I hope, will ensure -that on this occasion the Tribunal has available to it sufficient evidence for it to make an informed decision on the issue. First, if the applicant and the other two persons served with a copy of the notice of assessment consider that they are likely to be liable to pay vendors recoupment tax, he should lodge an affidavit or affidavits stating why. If he does not do so, the opinion of C that they are not so liable will stand uncontradicted and may then be relied on by the Trjubunal. If the applicant does lodge an affidavit or affidavits stating facts which can properly form a basis for an opinion that vendors recoupment tax is likely to become payable by him and the other two persons, and if the respondent disputes any of those facts, the respondent must then lodge an affidavit or affidavits stating which of the facts are not correct and setting out any facts which rebut them. If either party wishes to cross-examine the deponent of any affidavit, he must give notice of that before the hearing and the party who lodged the affidavit must then have the deponent present at the hearing. Without leave of the Tribunal no oral evidence will be received from a witness who has not sworn an affidavit lodged with the Tribunal before the hearing and served on the other party in accordance with the directions I am giving, and no adjourment will be granted because a deponent is not available for cross-examination if no notice requesting his attendance has been given by the party wishing to cross-examine him or her.
