Re Taxation Appeals [1991] AATA 96; 22 ATR 3207 (23 April 1991)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. VT88/344

AAT No. 6866

Income Tax

[1991] AATA 96; 22 ATR 3207

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

P.J.B. Burns(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - whether income returned by a family discretionary trust is assessable income of the trust, the taxpayer who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned - whether sec. 260 or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 is applicable - rate of additional tax imposed - deductibility of expenditure items.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 17, 19, 25, 51(1), 170(3), 223, 227, 260, Part IVA

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Forsyth 81 ATC 4157

Handley v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 81 ATC 4165

Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4851

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland; Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation; Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4765

Jolley v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4197

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting 89 ATC 4358

Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4830

Case W 58 89 ATC 524

Income Tax Laws Amendment Bill (No. 2) 1981 - Second Reading Speech

Income Tax Laws Amendment Bill (No. 2) 1981 - Explanatory Memorandum IT 2121

HEARING

MELBOURNE

23:4:1991

ORDER

The Tribunal:

(i) sets aside the decisions under review and remits the matter to the Commissioner to:

(a) allow deductions from the applicant's assessable incomes

for each of the years ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986

inclusive as agreed between the parties and set out in paragraph

16 of the statement of the Tribunal's reasons for this decision;

and

(b) excise from the applicant's assessable income for the year

of income ended 30 June 1985 $1,335 earned by his wife.

(ii) the culpability component in the additional tax imposed is to be calculated at the flat rate of 25%.

DECISION

The primary question at issue in these applications is as to whether income returned by a family discretionary trust assessed by the Commissioner of Taxation ("the Commissioner") to the applicant for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986 inclusive is assessable income of the trust or the applicant who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned.

2. Other matters in dispute which arise from the assessment of the income to the applicant are:

1. The deductibility of certain items of expenditure

incurred during the relevant years;

2. The powers of the Commissioner to amend the

applicant's assessments for the years of income ended 30 June

1984 and 30 June 1985; and

3. The imposition of additional tax for incorrect returns.

3. At the hearing the applicant was represented by Mr T. Murphy of counsel and the Commissioner by Mr G. Davies of counsel. The applicant and his wife were the only witnesses.

4. On issuing amended assessments to the applicant for each of the abovementioned years of income the Commissioner advised him that sec. 260 and/or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") applies to include the gross income disclosed in the return of income of the trust in his assessable income. Further in the event that Part IVA of the Act applies the amounts are includible in his assessable income by virtue of the operation of secs. 17, 19 and 25 of the Act.

5. The applicant lodged objections against the amended assessments and on their disallowance by the Commissioner requested reference of the decision to the Tribunal.

6. The applicant is a computer programmer and consultant analyst and from 1965 to 1980 he was employed by various firms in that capacity. In 1980 he resigned from his employment with a company known as Computer Power because he said he had become tired of being required to do things the way employers wanted things done, which in his view was not always the right way, and also the urgency with which employers wanted to see results without allowing time for proper planning. He also said that from time to time he had ideas which he thought were worth developing but it was difficult to get employers to spend money on these ideas whereas if he were self employed he could risk his own capital in such ventures. As a result he decided to branch out on his own.

7. One of the projects with which the applicant was involved was the development of a rally computer. Both the applicant and his wife are interested in and participate in car rallies with the applicant as driver and his wife as navigator. Two prototype rally computers were developed. The first Mark I in 1980 and an improved model Mark II in 1982. These computers were designed in conjunction with the applicant's wife who advised him of her requirements as a navigator during rallies. His wife also performed a number of tasks in the production of the computers such as "wire wrapping" which is a technique used for connecting the various electrical parts of the applicance. According to the evidence she spent long hours on this procedure but no detailed record was kept of these activities.

8. The completed computer provides two displays. The top one shows the total distance travelled by the vehicle and the lower display shows the trip distance which was said to be the distance between significant navigational points, for example crossroads. Other projects the applicant worked on were a device for providing navigational directional equipment and a further development of that device which if successful would have provided a central control over the movement of ambulances. The development of the navigational directional equipment was carried out in conjunction with another company, Unico Computer Systems Proprietary Limited. Numerous other projects were also carried out by the applicant but none of these yielded any assessable income during the period under review.

9. In his evidence the applicant said he had chosen a company trust arrangement instead of trading in his own name as it offered protection from liability particularly in the case of an accident. This he said had been brought home to him some twelve months earlier when a fellow employee had made a miscalculation which caused a huge boom arm to crash onto equipment causing tremendous damage. He also had the idea that the fact that the company acted as a trustee gave greater protection but he did not really know why that was so and he relied on advice from his wife and his accountant in matters of this kind.

10. On 1 November 1980 a discretionary trust ("E trust") was settled and a company (Q Proprietary Limited) was appointed trustee of the E trust. Copies of the trust deed and the certificate of incorporation of Q Proprietary Limited were tended in evidence.

11. The trust deed was prepared by a firm of solicitors and gives the applicant and his wife wide powers. Each of them was appointed appointor and guardian with the power to remove and appoint any trustee and as a result generally control the trust. Beneficiaries are the applicant, his wife and their children.

12. The issued capital of Q Proprietary Limited comprises two shares each of $1 paid up to that amount and held during the period under review by the applicant and his wife. Each of them was appointed a director of the company and the applicant's wife was also appointed secretary and public officer.

13. The only source of income of the company during the years under review was attributable to the activities of the applicant in the computer field with the exception of an amount of $1,335 paid to the company during the year of income ended 30 June 1985 in respect of secretarial services provided by the applicant's wife to an arms length organisation.

14. Both the applicant and his wife were employed by Q Proprietary Limited with the applicant performing all of the consultancy work undertaken and his wife performing secretarial duties and also being involved in the development of the projects previously mentioned. No formal contracts of employment between them and the company were tendered and examination of the directors' minute book did not disclose any record of minutes of meetings in respect of their employment.

15. The applicant was paid wages for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985 the amount of which was determined at the end of each year after discussions between him, his wife and his accountant but payment was not authorised by a formal directors' resolution. His wife's wages were said to have been based upon hours worked by her both in connection with the inventions referred to earlier and in respect of secretarial duties which she performed. No record was kept of the hours which she worked but she estimated that she worked about ten hours per week during the year of income 30 June 1984, fifteen to twenty hours per week during the year of income ended 30 June 1985 and twenty hours per week during the year of income ended 30 June 1986. Her wages also were fixed at the end of each of the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985 but wages payable in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1986 were paid on a weekly basis as were those paid to her husband.

16. During the hearing it was agreed between the parties that in the event either sec. 260 or Part IVA applies the following amounts should be allowed as deductions:

Year ended 30 June 1984 1985 1986

$8,365 $8,999 $10,971

17. As the company trust arrangement was entered into prior to 27 May 1981 the matter falls for consideration under sec. 260 of the Act.

18. Mr Murphy for the applicant submitted that sec. 260 of the Act did not apply as there was no scheme which had the purpose or effect of avoiding tax and the establishment of the trust was part of a normal family or commercial structure. He said that the use of trusts was wide spread especially in what might be called family businesses and this was not a case where one person goes out and generates income and the other person stays at home and maybe types the odd letter.

19. He said that this case was different because the income of the trust comprised not only the income which was derived by the applicant from fees from computer consultancy but also from secretarial income earned by the applicant's wife. He also submitted that the income derived from the computer consultancy was not income derived solely by the applicant but resulted from the joint efforts of the applicant and his wife with the applicant providing the technical expertise and his wife the "keystroking" function.

20. He submitted that if sec. 260 applies and the company trust arrangement is annihilated what is left is both husband and wife working together in a family business which would otherwise have been a partnership. He said on that basis the applicant's assessable income should be increased by the amount of the profit which was payable to the two children as his wife did not receive any distribution from the trust in the years of income under review.

21. He then sought to distinguish this case in that it was a family business with both the applicant and his wife committed to it with the wife being involved in setting up the trust and also being appointed a joint appointor of the trust. Secondly, he said her involvement with the activities of the trust were not confined to its internal activities but extended to assisting her husband in the development of his inventions, for example the rally computer.

22. He took his submission in respect of his claim in respect of the partnership or joint venture even further by referring to the wages paid by the trust to the applicant and his wife in the year of income ended 30 June 1984 which amounted to $18,000 and $10,000 respectively and submitted on that basis the applicant's assessable income should be two-thirds of the fees derived from the computer consultancy and one-third allocated to his wife because that was the basis upon which the parties themselves valued their respective contributions to the trust.

23. He said that although the various inventions being developed by the applicant did not yield any income and none of them was marketed this did not prevent the Tribunal from coming to a decision the trust was set up for among other reasons to develop and market such products. In further support of this proposition he referred the Tribunal to a photograph of the applicant's rally car which had the name Q Computers painted on it and he claimed that this supported his contention that it was a commercial venture and was not a hobby.

24. Mr Davies for the Commissioner submitted that the substantial purpose of the company trust arrangements was to avoid income tax and it was clearly not referrable to any ordinary or business family dealings. He said that the discretions conferred by the trust deed upon the trustee to distribute the net income of the trust among family members suggested a purpose of avoidance of tax and apart from the sum of $1,335 earned by the applicant's wife from secretarial duties in the year of income ended 30 June 1985 the whole of the trust income was derived from the technical expertise of the applicant. He also referred to the evidence given by the applicant's wife to the effect that the trust structure had advantages in that if millions of dollars were earned from the inventions the income could be distributed among family members.

25. Mr Davies pointed out that the trust income was used by the applicant and his wife as required for private and domestic purposes and the trust had no commercial purpose. The consultancy work was performed by the applicant and the income which was received was deposited into the trust bank account and drawn by the applicant and his wife as required. He referred to the fact that the applicant's wages were determined in arrears at the end of each financial year and were dependent upon the net income of the trust. He pointed out that the monies which were said to have been distributed to the children were actually credited to the accounts of the applicant and his wife as reimbursement for maintenance expenses incurred by them during the year.

26. There were also various submissions made by both Mr Murphy and Mr Davies in respect of what the situation might have been if for example a partnership had been chosen by the applicant and his wife instead of a trust.

27. I do not accept Mr Murphy's submission that the income derived from the computer consultancy work performed by the applicant resulted from the joint efforts of the applicant and wife and should be divided between them on the basis of the wages each was paid by the trust in the year of income ended 30 June 1984. The inventions yielded no income and irrespective of whether their development constituted a hobby or a commercial venture the sole source of income of the trust with the exception of the amount of $1,335 earned by the applicant's wife in the year of income ended 30 June 1985 resulted from work performed by the applicant in the computer field.

28. For that reason I find that the principles laid down in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland; Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation; Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4765; Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4846; Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting 89 ATC 4358; Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4830 apply and sec. 260 applies to strike down the trust.

29. On the annihilation of the trust the whole of the income less $1,335 earned by the applicant's wife in the year of income ended 30 June 1985 is properly assessable to the applicant.

30. Also in dispute is the deductibility of expenditure items as set out below in respect of which agreement was not reached between the parties:

1. Rental paid by Q Proprietary Limited for the use of a

room in the family residence amounting to $1,305 for each of the

years of income ending 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985 and $1,200

for the year of income ended 30 June 1986. On the annihilation

of the trust what is left is the applicant paying rent for the

use of the lounge room in the matrimonial home for the years of

income ended 30 June 1985 and 30 June 1986 and for the use of an

office added to the home during the year of income ended 30 June

1986. The decisions in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v

Forsyth 81 ATC 4157 and Handley v Federal Commissioner of

Taxation 81 ATC 4165 establish that deductions are not allowable

in the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985. Although

the situation is different in the year of income ended 30 June 1986

in that the rent was paid in respect of an office added to the

family home I am of the view that in these circumstances where the

applicant is paying rent to his wife in respect of part of the

matrimonial home a deduction is not allowable.

2. $220 incurred during the year of income ending 30

June 1986 in the installation of deadlocks throughout the

residence. This expenditure is of a capital nature and as such

is expressly excluded as a deduction under sec. 51(1).

3. Wages amounting to $5,000, $10,000 and $10,500 paid

to the applicant's wife in the years of income ended 30 June

1984, 1985 and 1986 respectively. The situation in respect of

wages payable to the applicant's wife presents some difficulty.

No records were kept of the hours which she worked in the

business but in her evidence she stated that she worked for

about ten hours per week during the year of income ended 30 June

1984, fifteen to twenty hours per week during the year of income

ended 30 June 1985 and twenty hours per week during the year of

income ended 30 June 1986. However it is unclear from the

evidence as to the hours which related to the inventions from

which no income was derived and those which related to the computer

business. Further the Tribunal was not provided with hourly rates

of pay applicable during the years under review other than the fact

that she was paid at the rate of $12 per hour for secretarial

services provided to an arms length employer during the year of

income ended 30 June 1985. Although I am of the view that she is

entitled to some wages the evidence is insufficient for me to make a

finding and for that reason the objection decision in respect of

that aspect is affirmed.

31. The next matter for consideration is as to whether the Commissioner's power to amend the applicant's returns of income for the years ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985 was precluded by sec. 170(3) of the Act as it applied during the year. At the time sec. 170(3) read as follows:

"170.(3) Where a taxpayer has made to the Commissioner a full

and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his

assessment, and an assessment is made after that disclosure, no

amendment of the assessment increasing the liability of the

taxpayer in any particular shall made except to correct an error

in calculation or a mistake in fact; and no such amendment shall

be made after the expiration of 3 years from the date upon which

the tax became due and payable under that assessment."

32. As in Daniel's case supra, an examination of the applicant's personal return and the company and trust return would not have disclosed to the Commissioner the details of the arrangement: for example, the circumstances of his employment with the company, the terms of which were not set out in a written contract and the manner in which the income was earned. I find that there had not been a full and true disclosure of all material facts necessary for his assessment, and sec. 170(3) does not preclude the Commissioner from amending the assessments for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 1985.

33. Finally, there is the matter of the additional tax imposed under sec. 223(1) of the Act. The applicant entered into an arrangement on the advice of his accountant and tax adviser, which was one in general use in the profession in which he operated, and in the circumstances the culpability component included in the additional tax imposed should be reduced to a rate of 25 per cent flat for each of the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985.
