VT88/352 and Commissioner of Taxation [1991] AATA 461; (1991) 22 ATR 3144; 91 ATC 240 (23 April 1991)

Last Updated: 27 May 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - whether income returned by a family discretionary trust is assessable income of the trust, the taxpayer who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned or the net income of a partnership of the taxpayer and his wife -whether sec 260 or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 is applicable - rate of additional tax imposed - deductibility of expenditure items

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.17, 19, 25, 51(1), 170, 223, 227, 260, Part IVA

Decision: The Tribunal:

  1. sets aside the decisions under review and remits the matter to the Commissioner to allow deductions from the applicant's assessable incomes as agreed between the parties and set out in paragraph 16 of the statement of the Tribunal's reasons for this decision;
  2. the culpability component in the additional tax imposed in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985 is to be calculated at the flat rate of 25 per cent; and
  3. additional tax imposed under sec 223 of the Act in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1986 is to be reduced to nil.

REASONS FOR DECISION

23 April 1991 Mr P.J.B. Burns, Member

  1. The primary question at issue in these applications is as to whether income returned by a family discretionary trust assessed by the Commissioner of Taxation ("the Commissioner") to the applicant for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 to 30 June 1986 inclusive is assessable income of the trust, the applicant who performed the services in respect of which the income was earned or the net income of a partnership of the applicant and his wife.
  2. Other matters in dispute which arise from the assessment of the income to the applicant are:
    1. the deductibility of certain items of expenditure incurred during the relevant years;
    2. the powers of the Commissioner to amend the applicant's assessment for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985; and
    3. the imposition of additional tax for incorrect returns.

3. At the hearing the applicant was represented by Mr T. Murphy of counsel and the Commissioner by Mr G. Davies of counsel. Two witnesses were called, namely the applicant, and his accountant and tax adviser.

  1. On issuing amended assessments to the applicant for each of the abovementioned years of income, the Commissioner advised him that sec 260 and/or Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") applies to include the gross income disclosed in the return of income of the trust in his assessable income. Further in the event that Part IVA of the Act applies the amounts are includible in his assessable income by virtue of the operation of sees 17, 19 and 25 of the Act.
  2. The applicant lodged objections against the amended assessments and on their disallowance by the Commissioner requested reference of the decision to the Tribunal.
  3. The applicant is a computer analyst programmer. In 1976, after a period of approximately six years as an employee of a local authority superannuation board, the applicant formed a partnership with his wife with the purpose of providing programme analysis services on a contract basis.
  4. The business was conducted as a partnership for some three years, up until 1978. A copy of the partnership return for the year of income ended 30 June 1978, lodged with the Australian Taxation Office ("the ATO"), was tendered in evidence. This return disclosed income from "systems analysis programming" and it was stated that the net income of the partnership was distributed as to two-thirds to the applicant and one-third to his wife. That return was the only partnership return tendered and there was no reference in it to the fact that the business had been taken over by any other entity.
  5. On 28 June 1978 a discretionary trust, R Trust, was settled and on the same day a company was incorporated ("CCC") which was appointed trustee of the R Trust. A copy of the trust deed and the certificate of incorporation of CCC were tendered in evidence.
  6. The trust deed was prepared by a firm of solicitors and gives the applicant wide powers. He was appointed both the guardian and appointor and as a result of being appointor had the power to remove and appoint any trustee. Beneficiaries were the applicant, his wife and their children. The trustee has the discretion to distribute the income as it sees fit and also has the power to remove a person from the class of beneficiaries, to declare trusts in substitution for the trust set out in the document and to amend, revoke or add to the provisions of the deed. However, the trustee was not permitted to exercise any of those powers without giving the guardian or appointor seven clear days notice of its intention to exercise the powers.
  7. The issued capital of CCC comprises two shares, each of $1, paid up to that amount, and held during the period under review by the applicant and his wife. Each of them was appointed a director of the company and the applicant was also appointed secretary and public officer.
  8. The applicant performed all of the computer work undertaken but no formal contract of employment was tendered, nor did the company minute book disclose any record of directors' meetings in respect of his employment. He was paid a wage which was determined at the end of each year after discussions between him and Miss P, his accountant and tax adviser, but its payment was not authorised by a formal directors' resolution. This wage was based upon fees earned during the year and was set at a figure which allowed a margin of profit to the trust.
  9. Each year the applicant borrowed money from the company to meet ongoing personal expenses. These borrowings were charged against a loan account which was credited with the wages payable to the applicant at the end of the year. There was no formal directors' resolution authorising the making of loans to him. His wife was not employed in the business and was not paid wages.
  10. All of the income received by the company during the period under review resulted from computer consulting work performed by the applicant. This work was contracted through an agency and the contracts provided that the applicant supply the specified service. Fees were fixed at a rate determined by the type of work performed by the applicant and the hours involved.
  11. No moneys were actually paid to his children who were beneficiaries of the trust. Distributions were credited to their accounts and then transferred to the applicant's loan account in repayment of education and other expenses incurred by him on their behalf during the year.
  12. In her evidence his accountant and tax adviser (Miss P) said that a large percentage of her clients were in the computer industry and that she was first approached by the applicant and his wife in 1978, and her firm was involved in the establishment of the trust. She said that she had advised them to incorporate and to form a family trust. The advantages of incorporation she said were that they could take on contracts they might not otherwise have been able to take on because certain principals would not deal with partnerships. She also asserted that there was the provision of superannuation which was important and the couple wanted a more professional base from which to expand the business and perhaps involve the children at a later stage. She said that the establishment of a trust provided a more flexible arrangement than a company and as a result the applicant and his wife opted for it.

16. During the hearing, it was agreed between the parties that in the event that either sec 260 or Part IVA applies, the following amounts should be allowed as deductions:

Year ended 30 June

1984 1985 1986

$6560 $6641 $7003

  1. As the company trust arrangement was entered into prior to 27 May 1981, the matters falls for consideration under sec 260 of the Act.
  2. It is clearly established save in perhaps in exceptional circumstances that an arrangement whereby personal exertion income of a taxpayer is diverted to a family trust established for the benefit of his family is caught by sec 260. (Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland, Watson v Federal Commissioner of Taxation, Pincus v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4765; Tupicoff v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 84 ATC 4851; Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Bunting 89 ATC 4358; and Daniels v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 89 ATC 4830.)
  3. There are no circumstances that remove the instant case from that principle and I find sec 260 of the Act operates to void the company trust arrangement. The latter arrangement when it was entered into in 1978 replaced a partnership between the applicant and his wife. Mr Murphy submitted on behalf of the applicant that, if sec 260 of the Act is applied and the company/trust arrangement is annihilated, what is left is a partnership.
  4. As Gibbs CJ said in Pincus's case (supra), at pages 4776-4777:

"Section 260 renders an arrangement void as against the Commissioner only insofar as it purports to have one of the purposes or effects specified in the section. The dissolution of the partnership, in itself, had no such purpose or effect, but it formed an integral part of the arrangement to which section 260 applied. ... However what was left after the annihilation of the arrangement was not the original partnership. The four doctors were in fact no longer practising in partnership and the section does not require or permit fiction to be substituted for fact."

The Tribunal must therefore be satisfied that the partnership continued and that it has not come to an end by the operation of law. This is what the applicant has failed to do in this case. On the evidence, I am satisfied that what remained after the arrangement is annihilated does not satisfy the legal requirements of a partnership, viz an association of persons who, in relation to the outside world, have come together in order to carry on business for profit, or are in receipt of income jointly. The joint bank account was closed, and orders and accounts were channeled through the trust account. For good measure, all of the income was derived from the applicant's personal exertion. On either view, the income is properly assessable to the applicant. Taxable incomes for the years under review should be recalculated allowing deductions from assessable income of $6560, $6641 and $7003 in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1984, 1985 and 1986 respectively as agreed between the parties.

21. The next matter for consideration is as to whether the Commissioner's power to amend the applicant's returns of income for the years ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985 was precluded by sec 170(3) of the Act as it applied during that year. At the time sec 170(3) read as follows:

"170.(3) Where a taxpayer has made to the Commissioner a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment, and an assessment is made after that disclosure, no amendment of the assessment increasing the liability of the taxpayer in any particular shall made except to correct an error in calculation or a mistake in fact; and no such amendment shall be made after the expiration of 3 years from the date upon which the tax became due and payable under that assessment."

  1. As in Daniels's case supra, an examination of the applicant's personal return and the company and trust return would not have disclosed to the Commissioner the details of the arrangement: for example, the circumstances of his employment with the company, the terms of which were not set out in a written contract and the manner in which the income was earned. I find that there had not been a full and true disclosure of all material facts necessary for his assessment, and sec 170(3) does not preclude the Commissioner amending the assessments for the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 1985.
  2. Finally, there is the matter of the additional tax imposed under sec 223(1) of the Act. The applicant entered into an arrangement on the advice of his accountant and tax adviser, which was one in general use in the profession in which he operated, and in the circumstances the culpability component included in the additional tax imposed should be reduced to a rate of 25 per cent flat for each of the years of income ended 30 June 1984 and 30 June 1985.
  3. During the year of income ended 30 June 1986, the applicant became estranged from his wife and in that year he received as a beneficiary the whole of the net trust income, together with wages paid to him by the trust. The amended assessment which was issued to him disallowed all deductions claimed in the trust return of income and as discussed earlier, agreement was reached between the parties as to those deductions which are now allowable. Although the deductions now allowable are less than those claimed in the trust returns lodged, as the whole of the trust income has been returned by the applicant, either as a distribution to him from the trust or as wages, the additional tax imposed under sec 223(1) of the Act for that year should now be reduced to nil.
