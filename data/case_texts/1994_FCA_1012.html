Gordon Raymond Howell v Commissioner of Taxation [1994] FCA 1012; (1994) 94 Atc 4186 (1994) 28 Atr 105 (13 April 1994)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

GORDON RAYMOND HOWELL v. COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. NG769 of 1993

FED No. 177/94

Number of pages - 8

Income Tax

(1994) 94 ATC 4186

[1994] FCA 1012; (1994) 28 ATR 105

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEW SOUTH WALES DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

DAVIES(1), HILL(2) AND WHITLAM(3) JJ

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - assessable income - whether amounts additional to normal sales price of goods paid by purchaser by way of commission to tax-haven entity controlled by director of vendor income - whether Administrativ Appeals Tribunal erred in law in not considering whether amounts made assessable income pursuant to s.26(e), Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth).

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth): ss.19, 26(e)

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (Cth); ss.43(2B)

Bisley Investment Corporation v Australian Broadcasting Tribunal (1982) 40 ALR 233; distinguished.

HEARING

SYDNEY, 2 March 1994

13:4:1994

Counsel and Solicitors BR Pape instructed by

for Applicant: John Cartwright and Co

Counsel and Solicitors AH Slater QC and WG Muddle

for Respondent: instructed by the Australian

Government Solicitor

ORDER

THE COURT ORDERS:

(1) The application be allowed.

(2) The decision of the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal to affirm the Commissioner's objection

decisions for the years ended 1981 to 1986

inclusive be set aside.

(3) The matter be remitted to the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal to be heard and decided again

in accordance with law either with or without

the hearing of further evidence at the

discretion of the Tribunal.

(4) The respondent pay the applicant's costs of the

application.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

DAVIES J I have had an opportunity to read the reasons for judgment prepared by Hill J. I agree with them and with the orders which his Honour proposes.

HILL J Gordon Raymond Howell, the applicant, appeals against a decision of 6 September 1993 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal ("the Tribunal"), constituted by a Presidential Member, Purvis J. That decision affirmed a decision of the respondent Commissioner of Taxation ("the Commissioner") disallowing Mr Howell's objections against amended assessments made by the Commissioner for the years of income ending 30 June 1981 to 30 June 1986 inclusive.

2. Each of the amended assessments appears to have been made on the basis that amounts added by the Commissioner to the taxable income initially assessed to Mr Howell were assessable income because they represented an informal distribution of the profits of a company called Living Fabrics Pty Limited ("Living Fabrics"). Section 108 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth) ("the Act") may have been the inspiration for the amended assessments. Each of the amended assessments imposed additional tax reflecting the alleged omissions of income.

3. Mr Howell duly objected to the amended assessments and, on these objections being disallowed, Mr Howell, being dissatisfied, applied to the Tribunal for review of the decision. The appeal to this Court is an application in the original jurisdiction of the Court, but is heard by a Full Court pursuant to s.44(3)(b)(i) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. The appeal to this Court is an appeal "on", and thus limited to, a question of law: Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s.44(1).

4. By the time the matter was heard by the Tribunal, any suggestion that the amounts added to Mr Howell's assessable income in each year might be income pursuant to s.108 of the Act and s.44(1) of the Act had been discarded. In his statement of findings of material questions of fact, evidence and reasons for the decision given pursuant to s.37(1)(a) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, the Commissioner relied upon the operation of s.25 of the Act, either alone or in conjunction with s.19.

5. Mr Howell was, at all relevant times, the managing director of Living Fabrics, a textile company which purchased yarn from a supplier, Toyama and Co Ltd ("Toyama"), a company incorporated in Japan. Mr Howell was not a shareholder of either Living Fabrics or Toyama.

6. It seems that in or around 1979 Living Fabrics, which had for some years prior to that date purchased yarn from another Japanese supplier, reached an agreement with Toyama to ship yarn from Japan to Australia and that the price payable by Living Fabrics would be the "actual" cost of the yarn increased by an agreed amount. Thereafter the actual cost of the yarn, together with the agreed additional amount, was paid by Living Fabrics to Toyama with Living Fabrics claiming in its return, as cost of purchases, the composite amount. By arrangement with Toyama, the additional amount was then transmitted to Goodenough Trading Pty Limited ("Goodenough"), a company incorporated in Hong Kong. Mr Howell was neither a director nor shareholder of Goodenough. The assessments, the subject of the present appeal, include as assessable income of Mr Howell the total of the additional amounts paid by Living Fabrics to Toyama in the years in question. These amounts, as reflected in the amended assessments, were as follows:

Year ended 30 June 1981 $3520.00

Year ended 30 June 1982 2952.00

Year ended 30 June 1983 12912.00

Year ended 30 June 1984 10743.00

Year ended 30 June 1985 12917.00

Year ended 30 June 1986 11319.00

7. The learned Presidential Member found that the affairs of Living Fabrics were conducted without the holding of formal board meetings of directors and indeed no meetings of directors ever took place, nor were there meetings of shareholders. However, a family company of Mr Howell, in which company he, his wife and children were shareholders and of which Mr Howell was chairman of directors, held a majority of the voting shares in Living Fabrics.

8. The arrangements with Toyama and Goodenough were put in place by Mr Howell without the involvement of any of Mr Howell's co-directors in Living Fabrics. Amounts received by Goodenough were accounted for by that company as commissions and the evidence made it clear that the funds of that company, subject to any fees payable, for example, to directors or auditors of that company, were made available either to Mr Howell or were paid at his direction.

9. Mr Howell gave evidence before the Tribunal seeking to paint the agreements between Living Fabrics and Toyama for the payment of the increased amount for yarn and the agreement with Goodenough for a commission of a similar amount as commercial transactions. Apart from documentary material, his was the only evidence advanced in support of his case. None of his co-directors in Living Fabrics or the secretary of that company or any of the overseas directors or shareholders of Toyama or Goodenough gave evidence.

10. Mr Howell's credit was attacked and the Tribunal having heard the evidence was of the view that without corroboration that evidence should not be accepted. However in the course of the evidence Mr Howell admitted that Goodenough performed no services to earn the amount that was said to be commission paid to it and to that extent agreed that the agreement with Goodenough was a sham.

11. The Tribunal made the following findings of fact:

"1. H. had the de facto control of BC Mills Pty Limited, a

shareholder in LF Pty Limited.

2. H. was able to direct the affairs of LF Pty Limited without

the approval of other directors and/or regardless of their

approval. H. could, if he so sought, procure BC Mills Pty

Limited to exercise its voting rights as he directed. H.

could, if he so sought, direct LF Pty Limited to exercise its

powers as he directed.

3. H. at all relevant times conducted the affairs of LF Pty

Limited as he sought so to do.

4. LF Pty Limited paid to T and Co Limited by way of cost of

purchase of yarn imported from T and Co Limited amounts in

excess of the actual price payable to T and Co Limited

for yarn.

5. H. caused moneys in excess of cost of yarn purchased to be

paid by LF Pty Limited to T and Co Limited, there to be

available as he directed.

6. The amounts in excess of the actual cost price were paid at

H's direction by T and Co Limited to G. Trading Limited.

7. Moneys held and invested by G. Trading Limited were paid or

applied by that company at the direction of H.

8. G. Trading Limited did not play any part in ensuring the

supply of yarn by T and Co Limited to LF Pty Limited. The

payment of moneys by T and Co Limited to G. Trading

Limited was at the instigation of H.

9. H. caused funds of LF Pty Limited to be transferred out of

Australia to Hong Kong with the express intent that the same

should there remain to be dealt with on his behalf or as he

directed, not as LF Pty Limited directed or on behalf of LF

Pty Limited. There was not an appropriation by directors or

shareholders of assets of the company LF Pty Limited and the

moneys received by H. were not of a capital nature (see FC of

T v Blakeley [1951] HCA 17; (1951) 82 CLR 388). H. was the person intended

to be benefited by the transfer of the funds. They were

appropriated to his use (see Bective v FCT [1932] HCA 22; 47 CLR 417, 424;

Parsons, Income Taxation in Australia 2.41, 2.44)."

12. The references in these findings to "H." are to Mr Howell, to "BC Mills Pty Limited" to Mr Howell's family company, to "LF Pty Limited" to Living Fashions, to "T and Co Limited" to Toyama and to "G. Trading Limited" to Goodenough.

13. Having regard to these factual findings the Tribunal concluded that Mr Howell had not satisfied the onus placed upon him by s.190(b) of the Act. The Tribunal then expressed its satisfaction:

"...that the moneys remitted to G. Trading

Limited via T and Co. Pty Limited ex LF Pty

Limited were moneys reinvested and accumulated

or otherwise dealt with on H's behalf and/or as

he directed. They were to his benefit."

14. Accordingly the Tribunal was of the view that the assessments including penalties were correct. Under the heading "PENALTIES" in its reasons the Tribunal said:

"The scheme was deliberately entered into by H.

in order to put out of the reach of the

Australian Taxation Office funds otherwise

assessable in the hands of LF Pty Limited. They

were funds that were then to be available to H.

for his own beneficial use."

15. It would seem that the Tribunal reached its conclusion by reference to the provisions of s.19 of the Act. That section, in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1981 to 30 June 1985 inclusive, provided as follows:

"Income shall be deemed to have been derived by

a person although it is not actually paid over

to him but is reinvested, accumulated, capitalized, carried

to any reserve, sinking fund or insurance fund however

designated, or otherwise dealt with on his behalf or as he

directs."

16. By s.5 of the Income Tax Assessment Amendment (Capital Gains) Act 1988 (Cth) (No 52 of 1986), s.19 of the Act was amended effective from 24 June 1986 by the insertion of the words "or money" after the word "Income".

17. In its unamended form, it was clear that s.19 could only operate in respect of amounts which were income. Nothing in the present case turns upon whether the word "Income" in s.19 is to be construed as meaning "income" in ordinary concepts, or whether it includes any amount which is made assessable income under, for example, s.26(e) of the Act. In its amended form s.19 extends as well to money, an extension made necessary by the insertion into the Act of Part IIIA dealing with capital gains and capital losses. But, at least in the present case, that amendment does not alter the general thrust of the section, for to deem an amount of money to be derived by a person says nothing about whether that amount of money forms part of that person's assessable income. Unless that money, when derived, has the character of income, then the deeming in s.19 will be of no consequence.

18. The purpose of s.19, in its unamended form, was stated by Rich J in Permanent Trustee Co of New South Wales Limited v Commissioner of Taxation (1940) 2 AITR 109 at 110-111 to be:

"...to prevent a taxpayer escaping though his resources have

actually been increased by the accrual of the income and its

transformation into some form of capital wealth or its

utilisation for some purpose."

19. Counsel for Mr Howell submitted that the Tribunal erred in applying s.19 to a case where the moneys, said to have been applied in accordance with the section, were not income of Mr Howell. To the extent that it was open to the Tribunal to find that the amounts in question had the character of income in the hands of Mr Howell, it was submitted that the Tribunal had failed to find, in the present case, that the amounts in question were income. Although the notice of appeal made no challenge to any finding of fact, it was submitted that there was no evidence upon which the Tribunal could have found that Mr Howell was the beneficial owner of the funds held in Hong Kong, or that the funds had been transferred to Hong Kong with the express intention that they should there remain to be dealt with by Mr Howell on his behalf or as he directed. In written submissions counsel for Mr Howell submitted that the Tribunal had made no finding of sham in respect of the transaction and companies other than the label of "commission" to describe the moneys paid to Goodenough by Toyama. This matter was not pursued in the course of argument.

20. The findings that the funds in Hong Kong were available to Mr Howell for his own beneficial use and that he was the beneficial owner of the funds were findings open to the Tribunal on the evidence and accordingly it can not be said that the Tribunal erred in law in so finding. However, because of the point to which I shall turn hereafter, I should also note that it would equally have been open to the Tribunal to find that the funds flowed from a scheme of tax evasion whereby Living Fabrics obtained deductions to which it was not entitled and the moneys ended up in the hands of Goodenough in a relative tax haven. There they were available to be used for the benefit of members of the Howell family and of the Howell group of companies, as should be decided.

21. Senior counsel for the Commissioner properly conceded in the course of argument that for the amounts in question to be included in the assessable income of Mr Howell, those amounts would have to be income falling within s.26(e) of the Act, namely, as being:

"...allowances, gratuities, compensations, benefits, bonuses

and premiums allowed, given or granted to (Mr Howell) in

respect of, or for or in relation directly or indirectly to,

any employment of or services rendered by him...".

22. No finding was made by the Tribunal that the amounts in question did fall within s.26(e). It was necessarily a relevant matter for the Tribunal to consider whether the amounts in question were what might compendiously be described as "remuneration" to Mr Howell paid by Living Fabrics for his employment or services rendered by him. If that were not so it could not be held that any amount was assessable income to him, whether or not the provisions of s.19 were otherwise satisfied. The failure of the Tribunal to consider a question necessary for its conclusion involves an error of law.

23. Senior counsel for the Commissioner submitted that it was implicit in the Tribunal's conclusion that it had considered whether or not the amount in question was remuneration in the sense used above and had decided that matter adversely to Mr Howell, or alternatively that Mr Howell had not satisfied the burden of proof in respect of that issue.

24. It was submitted that the reasoning of the Tribunal should not be subject to too minute a scrutiny. As is clear from Bisley Investment Corporation v Australian Broadcasting Tribunal (1982) 40 ALR 233 at 251; 59 FLR 132 at 152 per Lockhart J, with whom in this respect Sheppard J agreed (see at 252; 153), the decision of the Tribunal will not necessarily be set aside where the Tribunal has not expressly made a finding of fact, although referring to facts in its reasons. It will, as Sheppard J said in the same case (at 255; 157), be sufficient if there is substantial compliance with the requirement of s.43(2B) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act. See also per Foster J in FCT v Cainero (1988) 88 ATC 4427 at 4430-1.

25. But the present is not a case where there has been substantial compliance. It is a case where a matter vital to the conclusion reached by the Tribunal has apparently not been considered at all by it. I should say in defence of his Honour it would seem that counsel appearing for the Commissioner in the Tribunal did not draw to his Honour's attention the need for the amounts in question to have the character of remuneration under s.26(e) of the Act before s.19 could operate upon them.

26. The present is not a case where it could be said on the evidence that it was not open to the Tribunal to conclude that the amounts in question were remuneration paid to Mr Howell. Whether they were involves a question of fact and not of law and is for the Administrative Appeals Tribunal to decide. It follows therefore that the applicant's appeal should be allowed and that the matter should be remitted to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal to be heard and decided again in accordance with law. It will be a matter for the Administrative Appeals Tribunal whether fresh evidence might be adduced.

27. Despite a submission to the contrary from senior counsel for the Commissioner based on what was said to be differences between the grounds of appeal filed on behalf of Mr Howell and the matters argued, I am of the view that as the applicant has been successful the usual rule as to costs should apply and that accordingly the Commissioner should pay Mr Howell's costs of the present application.

28. Accordingly I would propose the following orders:

(1) Application be allowed.

(2) The decision of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal to

affirm the Commissioner's objection decisions for the years

ended 1981 to 1986 inclusive be set aside.

(3) The matter be remitted to the Administrative Appeals

Tribunal to be heard and decided again either with or

without the hearing of further evidence.

(4) The respondent pay the applicant's costs of the application.

WHITLAM J I also agree with the judgment of Hill J.
