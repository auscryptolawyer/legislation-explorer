ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - allowable deductions - Shire Clerk of local council - overseas travel - wife's expenses - whether deductible

Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936, s.51 (1)

Amalgamated & Zinc (De Bavay's) Limited v FCT [1935] HCA 81; (1935) 54 CLR 295 at p.303

Lunney v FCT (1958) 100 CLR 478

REASONS FOR DECISION

5 May 1988 Mr E.T. Perrignon (Senior Member)

  1. During the year of income ended 30 June 1984 the applicant incurred expenditure in respect of overseas travel by his wife and himself. That part of the expenditure which was claimed as a deduction in respect of his wife's travel was disallowed by the Commissioner. The applicant's objection to the assessment was disallowed and the applicant has applied for review of the decision disallowing the objection.
  2. The applicant was the Chief Executive Officer and Shire Clerk of a local government Council. He and his wife, as delegates of the Council, travelled to a city in the United States of America in order to attend the proclamation of, and other ceremonies connected with, the establishment of a sister city relationship between that city and the Council by which the applicant was employed. They spent one week at the sister city. During that week some ceremonies were attended by the applicant alone, and others by his wife alone. Some ceremonies they attended together. They also travelled to Washington, D.C., to meet members of the United States Sister City International organization, for the purpose of arranging a visit to the applicant's Shire of representatives of that organization.
  3. Although the applicant and his wife made visits to other places during their trip overseas, no deduction was claimed in respect of those visits. However, the main purpose of their overseas trip was to attend the making of the proclamation and associated ceremonies at the sister city. The claim which was disallowed was for $1,450 in respect of the applicant's wife, being air fares amounting to $1,250 and meals and accommodation $200, making a total of $1,450. The applicant's claim for $1,450 for the equivalent part of his own travelling expenses was allowed.
  4. The only oral evidence in the appeal was that of the applicant and his wife. I accept their evidence. It establishes that in order to further his career they had for many years engaged themselves extensively in local community activities within the respective areas of the Councils with which he served. Those activities included active participation in the affairs of various clubs and associations, attendance at local functions and the entertainment of visiting public officials. His wife's role was supportive of that of her husband. Amongst other local community organizations, the Girl Guides and Brownies engaged her active support. The applicant was successful in his career and now holds a senior position in the field of local government.

5. It was contended on behalf of the applicant that the applicant's wife played an important part in support of her husband over the years in the furtherance of his career and that both the applicant and his wife had set a pattern of behaviour which resulted in the promotion of the applicant through the local government service. It was said that a nexus had been established between the wife's journey to the sister city and Washington and the gaining by the applicant of his assessable income as a senior local government employee. They had both been invited by the sister city to attend the making of the proclamation and other ceremonies. Together with the Shire President and his wife, they had been appointed as the Council's delegates to so attend. There was, it was argued, a sufficient nexus between the earning by the applicant of his income and the expenditure of $2,900 for his wife and himself on their joint travelling expenses. The question was said to be one of fact. It was pointed out that the Council had paid the applicant's salary for the week when he was in the sister city.

6. Section 51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 allows as a deduction from a taxpayer's assessable income, "all losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing such income". The outgoings must be an expenditure which has an effect in gaining or producing income: Amalgamated Zinc (De Bavay's) Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1935] HCA 81; (1935) 54 CLR 295 at p.303. The fact that the expenditure may have been incurred as a necessary prerequisite to the derivation of assessable income does not make the expenditure deductible: Lunney v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1958) 100 CLR 478 at pp.498-499. There must be a "perceived connection" between the expenditure and the income: Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Hatchett (1971) 71 ATC 4184. The expenditure must be incidental and relevant to gaining or producing the assessable income: Ronpibon Tin No Liability v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47 at pp.56,58.

7. In the present case I am of the opinion that there is no connection between the expenditure in question and the applicant's assessable income which would justify the conclusion that the expenditure was incurred in gaining or producing that income, or any part of that income, whether in the taxation year in which the expenditure was incurred or in any other year. There was, in my view, no perceived connection between the outgoing and the applicant's assessable income. No doubt the applicant's wife was of considerable assistance to him in the support which she gave at the ceremonies in the sister city which were attended by both of them, and also in her attendance at other such ceremonies alone, but I do not think that her participation in such events can rightly be regarded, for the purposes of s.51 (1), as being incidental and relevant to the production of the applicant's income.

8. In the result therefore the application fails and the Commissioner's decision under review is confirmed.
