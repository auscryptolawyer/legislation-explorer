ST87/293 and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 782; (1988) 20 ATR 3117; 89 ATC 201 (20 December 1988)

Last Updated: 8 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - ASSESSABLE INCOME - residence - applicant lived with family in Greece for seven years - travelled to America to complete six years of tertiary study - intention to temporarily return to Australia to ascertain whether to undertake postgraduate course in Australia - whether permanent place of abode outside Australia.

Words and Phrases: "reside", "resident"

"permanent place of abode"

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, sub-s6(1)

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Miller [1946] HCA 23; (1946) 73 CLR 93

R v Hammond [1852] EngR 108; (1852) 117 ER 1477

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Applegate 79 ATC 4307

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Jenkins 82 ATC 4098

REASONS FOR DECISION

20 December, 1988 Deputy President RA Layton

Miss WJ Purcell (Senior Member)

Mr DJ Trowse (Member)

  1. This application for review concerns a reference relating to the financial year ending 30 June 1986 in which the applicant received income from the rental proceeds for five flats which had been transferred to him by his father on 7 November 1985 at a time when he was living in the United States of America. The applicant was assessed at a rate of taxation applicable to a non-resident of Australia, the Commissioner finding him not to be a resident of Australia within the meaning of sub-s6(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Assessment Act").
  2. These Reasons for Decision should be read in conjunction with a review involving the applicant's parents and the findings of fact in that decision are adopted insofar as they are relevant to this decision.
  3. At the review hearing, the applicant was represented by his agent and the Commissioner by one of his officers. The information before the Tribunal consisted of the supplementary documents lodged with the Tribunal and further documentation tendered at the commencement of the hearing, including an agreed statement of facts. In addition, there was a further agreed fact set out in para 6 hereof.
  4. The findings of fact are as follows. The applicant was born in Australia on 3 September 1965. He resided in Australia with his parents until approximately January 1980 when he left with his parents and two other siblings to live in Athens, Greece. Following arrival in Athens, he and his family were joined by his paternal and maternal grandparents, who all resided together in his paternal grandparent's house in Athens. The applicant attended the American Community School in Athens and after completing his secondary education, he applied for and was accepted to study medicine at the Gannon University in Pennsylvania in the United States of America.
  5. The applicant left Greece in 1981 to take up his tertiary study in America and he has lived in a student residential hall at that university from 1981 to the present time.
  6. It is an agreed fact between the parties that the applicant intends to return to Australia for a brief period in December 1988 with a view to ascertaining whether he wishes to study for a Doctorate in Medicine in Australia upon completion of his present Master's of Medicine in June 1989.
  7. The matter which arises in this review is whether or not the income which the applicant received from the rental of the flats should be taxed at a non-resident rate.
  8. We have set out the relevant law in case ST87/210-212,269; ST87/213-215, 270 delivered 20 December 1988 at paras 16 to 22, and it is as follows.
  9. The word "resident" or the phrase "resident of Australia" is set out in the definition clause in sub-s6(1) of the Assessment Act:

"'resident' or 'resident of Australia' means -

(a) a person, other than a company, who resides in Australia and includes a person -

(i) whose domicile is in Australia, unless the Commissioner is satisfied that his permanent place of abode is outside Australia;

(ii) who has actually been in Australia, continuously or intermittently, during more than one-half of the year of income, unless the Commissioner is satisfied that his usual place of abode is outside Australia and that he does not intend to take up residence in Australia? or

(iii) who is an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 or is the spouse or a child under 16 years of age of such a person;"

Paragraph (a) of that definition refers to the word "reside" which has been described by the High Court in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Miller [1946] HCA 23; (1946) 73 CLR 93 by Latham, CJ at pp99-100, as having a wide meaning, one of the dictionary meanings being "to dwell permanently or for a considerable time, to have one's settled or usual abode, to live, in or at a particular place".

  1. Extended definitions of "resident" are contained in sub-paras 6(1)(a)(i), (ii) and (iii). The only definition specifically referable to this applicant is that contained in sub-para (i) which refers to a person "whose domicile is in Australia, unless the Commissioner is satisfied that his permanent place of abode is outside Australia;".
  2. The term "place of abode" has been interpreted in the case of R v Hammond [1852] EngR 108; (1852) 117 ER 1477 at 1480 as "a place of residence" which is in turn accepted as meaning "the place where a person lives with his family and sleeps at night".
  3. The leading case on the meaning of the qualification in sub-para.6(1)(a)(i) is that of the Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Applegate 79 ATC 4307. Franki, J at page 4309 made the following comment:

"Accordingly in my opinion the phrase 'permanent place of abode outside Australia' is to be read as something less than a permanent place of abode in which the taxpayer intends to live for the rest of his life.

There is nothing in the subsection which requires the intent of the taxpayer to be the critical factor even though it is, of course, a relevant factor. Essentially the question is whether, as a matter of fact the taxpayer's permanent place of abode was outside Australia at the relevant time."

The learned Judge then continued by saying that:

"the question is one of degree and bearing in mind the association of the phrase 'permanent place of abode' with the word 'domicile' and the fact that the liability for tax falls to be determined annually"

13. Northrop, J in the same case rejected a contention made on behalf of the Commissioner that in the extended definition of "resident", the phrase "his permanent place of abode is outside Australia" should be construed as applying to a person who "intends to live outside Australia indefinitely, without any definite intention of ever returning to Australia in the foreseeable future, except at some remote albeit specific point of time". In rejecting that argument, Northrop, J made the following comments at page 4313:

"In my opinion that contention should be rejected. The qualification applies to persons who although domiciled in Australia, do not reside in Australia within the accepted meaning of the word 'reside'. The qualification is stated in an affirmative form, namely, where the person has his permanent place of abode outside Australia. The qualification is not concerned with whether a person has abandoned his Australian domicile or has acquired a new domicile or not. The qualification is concerned with the person's permanent place of abode. The phrase 'place of abode' may have many meanings, it can refer to the building or place where a person sleeps and it can refer to the building or place where he is usually found, for instance, 'his place of business'."

14. His Honour then considered the word "permanent" and noted that there could be many shades of meaning of that word, but adopted the view of Lord Justice du Parcq who said that the word "permanent" is indeed a relative term, and is not synonymous with "everlasting". Northrop, J further observed at page 4314, that the word "permanent" must be construed to have a shade of meaning applicable to the year of income under consideration and that it was:

"... unreal to consider whether a taxpayer has formed the intention to live or reside or to have a place of abode outside of Australia indefinitely, without any definite intention of ever returning to Australia in the foreseeable future ... What is of importance is whether the taxpayer has abandoned any residence or place of abode he may have had in Australia. Each year of income must be looked at separately. If in that year a taxpayer does not reside in Australia in the sense in which that word has been interpreted, but has formed the intention to, and in fact has, resided outside Australia, then truly it can be said that his permanent place of abode is outside Australia during that year of income. This is to be contrasted with a temporary or transitory place of abode outside Australia".

15. Fisher, J in the same case of Applegate (supra), reinforced the view that the words "permanent place of abode" do not necessarily direct attention to the taxpayer's state of mind in respect of that or any other place, and whilst such a state of mind is crucial to "domicile", when looking at "permanent place of abode" a greater emphasis must be placed on the nature and quality of the use which a taxpayer makes of a particular place of abode. At page 4317 His Honour said:

"Obviously if his stay is purely temporary and he intends to move on or return to Australia at some definite point of time this denies the place of abode an essential characteristic of a home, namely durability. Moreover it seems appropriate to view objectively the nature and quality of the use which the taxpayer makes of the place of abode to determine whether it has the characteristics of his fixed place of abode, his home. ...

To my mind the proper construction to place upon the phrase 'permanent place of abode' is that it is the taxpayer's fixed and habitual place of abode. It is his home, but not his permanent home. It connotes a more enduring relationship with the particular place of abode than that of a person who is ordinarily resident there or who has there his usual place of abode. Material factors for consideration will be the continuity or otherwise of the taxpayer's presence, the duration of his presence and the durability of his association with the particular place."

  1. The Federal Court's decision in Applegate (supra) has been subsequently endorsed and relied upon in the courts, a notable example being the decision in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Jenkins 82 ATC 4098.
  2. In the present review, we cannot be satisfied that the applicant has resided in Australia in the sense that he has dwelt permanently or for a considerable period of time in Australia as at the relevant taxation year, namely 1986. As at the close of that fiscal year, he had been absent for a period approaching seven years, the first of which he lived continuously in a family environment. He then travelled from there to America to undertake a course of study due to extend over a time span of six years. Additionally, the applicant's plans for the future appear uncertain even at this advanced point in time. Those factors inevitably lead to the conclusion that Australia was not his settled or usual place of abode during the year under review. In other words, he did not "reside" in Australia as that term is used in para6(1)(a) of the Assessment Act.
  3. Turning now to the extended statutory definition contained in sub-para.6(1)(a)(i). It is conceded that the applicant has the relevant domicile in Australia and the only remaining issue is the question of whether the qualification to sub-para6(a)(i) of the statutory definitions of "resident" is applicable to the applicant.
  4. Applying the criteria and discussion of the Full Court of the Federal Court in Applegate (supra), we are satisfied that the Commissioner was correct in finding that the applicant's permanent place of abode was outside Australia for the purposes of the taxation year ending 30 June 1986. In the circumstances of this reference, the matters touching on the question of "resides" are also applicable to this test. They, together with the applicant's intention to remain outside Australia for a prolonged and undefined duration are fatal to his cause.

20. Therefore we are of the opinion that the Commissioner's assessment should be upheld and that the applicant should be assessed at a rate of taxation payable relevant to a non-resident of Australia.
