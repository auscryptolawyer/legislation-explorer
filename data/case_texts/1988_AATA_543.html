REASONS FOR DECISION

15 June 1988 Deputy President R.D. Nicholson

Objection Decision Under Review

The Applicant seeks review of a decision by the Respondent disallowing the Applicant's objection to the amendment of its assessment for the income year ended 30 June 1981 whereby the sum of $159,367 was assessed as income under the provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). In the Applicant's accounts for that year the amount of $159,367 was shown as a current liability and described as "deposits on uncompleted contracts". The amount was not included in the gross sales figure returned.

Evidence

The Tribunal had before it documents filed by the Respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 as modified by section 14ZG of the Taxation Administration Act 1953. It also received exhibits during the hearing. It heard sworn evidence from the director of the company who was the managing director during the relevant year of income; from the present managing director and 30% shareholder who was, in the relevant year of income, a project engineer with the Applicant; and from an officer in the employ of the Australian Taxation Office who is qualified as an associate member of the Australian Society of Accountants.

Facts

The Applicant company provides and provided during the relevant year of income mechanical services and air conditioning contracting and manufacture of hospital sterilisers. In the course of that business it entered into a number of contracts (or sub-contracts) for manufacture of items and delivery of its services in relation thereto.

At a hearing held on 19 February 1988 evidence was adduced to establish the value of the contracts from which the amount in issue was said to be calculated. In the course of that hearing it became apparent that the sum of $89,937 had been deducted twice. The Applicant accepted that to be the position at a directions bearing on 21 April 1988.

At the resumed hearing on 8 June 1988 the Applicant adduced further evidence of contracts into which it had entered and which established a total contract value in excess of that previously contended for. The consequence of that was that the Respondent accepted the Applicant's claim should still be properly treated as relating to the sum of $159,367.

At the first hearing the Tribunal heard evidence in relation to 13 contracts. At the second hearing it heard evidence in relation to a further 11 contracts. It is not appropriate that there should be set out in these reasons a description of each of those contracts. It is sufficient that the general character of those contracts be described. In that respect it is to be noted that counsel for the Applicant did not dispute the description of those contracts given by the Respondent.

It is apparent from the evidence which the Tribunal heard in relation to these contracts that the amounts deducted do not comprise deposits on uncompleted contracts. The evidence establishes that the amounts comprise a deduction from gross sales of 10% of the progress claims made in respect of the contracts.

It is also apparent that under the contracts the Applicant was legally entitled to progress payments as the contracts proceeded. The Applicant carried out work and exercised its right to claim progress payments in respect of the value of work completed to the date of those payments. For example, this may be seen from Exhibits A3 (invoice number 6709) dated 23 June 1981 "Value of work completed $102,770.00"; C2-3 (invoice number 6716 dated 30 June 1981 "Value of work completed $34,350.00"; F5 (invoice number 6710) dated 24 June 1981 "Value of work completed $21,400.00"; G9 (invoice number 6713) dated 30 June 1981 "Value of work completed $2,824.00"; H3 (invoice number 6711) dated 30 June 1981 "Value of work completed $17,700.00" and J3 (invoice number 6712) dated 30 June 1981 "Value of work completed $1,500.00".

In relation to the 11 contracts or sub-contracts referred to in evidence at the resumed hearing, the entitlement to progress payments was achieved in most cases by the provision of a bank guarantee from or on behalf of the Applicant which avoided retentions being made. Whether or not there was a bank guarantee, the position was that the Applicant had an entitlement to the payments which it invoiced. Moneys received from all payments were paid to the Applicant's bank account.

The Applicant returned its income on an accruals basis. Consequently, it is not relevant whether the amounts invoiced were in fact paid.

The evidence of the second director referred to above was that built into each amount claimed by the Applicant was an amount to cover the whole of the defects period, being an allowance for work that may have to be performed. The claim he said, therefore, constitutes an amount of money invoiced for work done and work that may be required to be done. It is because of this allowance for the latter amount that the Applicant claims to deduct 10% in issue.

Application of law

The Applicant contends that the amount of $159,367 in issue is not income pursuant to the concepts of section 19 or section 25 of the Act. In support of that the Applicant contends that the decision in Arthur Murray (N.S.W.) Pty. Limited v. The Commissioner of Taxation [1965] HCA 58; (1965) 114 CLR 314 supports its position. In that decision it was held that fees received in advance of tuition for dancing lessons could not at the moment of their receipt be regarded as assessable income where they applied to lessons to be given over future periods.

The facts in this proceeding are entirely distinguishable. Here there was no payment into a suspense account from which amounts were periodically transferred to the general account. There is no proof of an established accountancy and commercial principle such as that which was agreed to exist in the Arthur Murray case (supra). The Applicant in this proceeding was entitled to claim the various payments which had thus crystallised into debts. The amounts here had reached the situation where they could properly be counted as gains completely made so that there is neither legal or business unsoundness in regarding them without qualification as income derived - Arthur Murray (supra) at 318 per Barwick C.J., Kitto J. and Taylor J. As was said in Henderson v. The Commissioner of Taxation [1969] HCA 14; (1970) 119 CLR 612 at p. 650 per 3arwick C.J. :

"When the service is so far performed that according to the agreement of the parties or in default thereof, according to the general law, a fee or fees have been earned and it or they will be income derived in the period of time in which it or they have become recoverable."

See also H.W. Coyle v. Commissioner of Inland Revenue (NZ) 80 ATC 6012 at pp. 6019-6022 and Minister of National Revenue v. John Colford Contracting Co. Ltd. 26 DLR (2d) 15.

In paragraph 11 of its objection the Applicant stated that the sum in contention represents the difference between "the invoiced value of projects not completed and the work-in-progress of those projects as at the end of the year of income". This objection has not been sustained on the evidence. No calculation was made of the value of work-in-progress of the contracts. The amount deducted in respect of the contracts is simply 10% of the value of progress claims as at 30 June 1981.

Had the objection of the Applicant stated that there should be an entitlement for a deduction for future contingencies, there is authority that no such entitlement is available: Nilsen Development Laboratories Proprietary Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1981] HCA 6; (1961) 144 CLR 616 at 623-4 per Barwick C.J. and at 627 per Gibbs J. applying Emu Bay Railway Company Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1944] HCA 28; (1944) 71 CLR 596 at 606 and New Zealand Flax Investments Limited v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1938] HCA 60; (1938) 61 CLR 179 at 195 and Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. James Flood Proprietary Limited [1953] HCA 65; (1953) 88 CLR 492.

It was contended by counsel for the Applicant in oral argument that the figure of 10% had been arrived at as a figure equivalent to the budgeted profit element on payments received on various contracts current during the year but still ongoing as at the end of that year of income. It was argued that the income was not therefore derived because it was not possible to ascertain at that time the profit element. That, however, was not a ground stated in the Applicant's objection. Furthermore, it is quite contrary to the evidence which establishes beyond dispute that the 10% was unrelated to profit. The evidence of the present managing director on that matter was categorical:

"So that your 10 per cent removal has nothing to do with the fact that you have not made your profit yet?- Nothing to do with it at all. We have not carried out the work in that category against which the claim is made even though it may he claimed out in full."

Entitlement to amend assessment

The Applicant did not discharge the onus of proving that the Respondent did not have authority to amend the assessments: see McAndrew v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1956] HCA 62; (1956) 98 CLR 263 at 269, 274 and 283. It did not address any argument to that end although the matter was raised by its objection. The facts in this proceeding show that there was not full and true disclosure in the return; the double deduction accepted by the Applicant during the hearing establishing this. The description given to the deduction in issue in this proceeding also "left the Commissioner uninformed or incorrectly informed, up to the time of

the assessment, of a material fact": Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Levv [1961] HCA 92; (1961) 106 CLR 448 at 456 per Kitto J. The result was an avoidance by under-assessment (Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Barton [1957] HCA 5; (1957) 96 CLR 359 at 366) so that the conditions of sub-section 170(2) of the Act are satisfied to entitle the Respondent to issue the amended assessment.

Conclusion

For the reasons given the Tribunal will affirm the objection decision under review.
