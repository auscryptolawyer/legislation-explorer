ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - deduction - expenses of attending Wimbledon - journalist - whether in the course of producing income

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s51(1)

Decision No 3920, 27 November 1987

FC of T v Maddalena 71 ATC 4161

Evans v FC of T 86 ATC 4901

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION No AT86/645

AAT Decision No 3949

Deputy President RK Todd

REASONS FOR DECISION

27 November 1987 Deputy President RK Todd

  1. This reference involves expenses incurred by the applicant in attending the annual Tennis Championships of the All-England Croquet and Lawn Tennis Club ("Wimbledon") in 1985. In his taxation return for the year of income ended 30 June 1985 ("fiscal 1985") the applicant returned an amount of $100 as income from "Radio Broadcasts". Deductions totalling $3,454 were claimed as having been incurred in earning this amount.
  2. The applicant is a public servant who, during fiscal 1985, derived as income the amount described above from radio broadcasts, and also income from his public service position and from a rental property.
  3. At the hearing oral evidence was given by the applicant and by the news director of the radio station from which income was earned. These witnesses did, however, give different impressions of what was the major purpose of the trip. In his evidence the applicant stated:

"Well, there were a number of reasons. Number one, first and foremost, to provide coverage for [the radio station] here. Secondly, to undertake a predetermined set of historical cameo interviews with all-time great players, officials, umpires and the like and also as an effort to pre-establish contacts in the journalistic field."

This can be contrasted with the evidence of the news director who stated:

"[The applicant] came to me and told me that he had accreditation to go to Wimbledon. My understanding was that while he had the accreditation he did not have anyone to report to back in Australia. He asked whether we would be interested in receiving reports from him on the tennis matches. My response was basically that that would be tremendous. As a radio news room we get reports from Wimbledon on the tennis matches that are played but usually without any local bias to the report, so in other words, we could keep an eye on how the local players were going as opposed to all the other games that were being played. So it sounded like an attractive proposition. I put the proposition to our manager. I think you have a copy of the memo concerned and the response was positive. We agreed to pay [the applicant] $10 a day for that coverage.

COMMISSIONER'S REPRESENTATIVE: Was any consideration given to the fact that the cost of such a trip would far exceed the income?---Our understanding was that [the applicant] was going over there anyway. The idea of the payment of a fee was as much as anything a token gesture that he was going to the effort to provide us with something specific."

  1. I find it impossible to accept that the primary motivation for the applicant expending the sum of $3,454 was to provide reports to a radio station which would then return him only $100. It is far more realistic, in my opinion, to find that the applicant intended to use the trip primarily as a method of establishing contacts, and secondly to produce the tapes referred to. The provision of services to the radio station was only a minor consideration.
  2. This finding necessitates consideration of whether expenditure in pursuance of either of what I have found to be the two major objectives is deductible.
  3. A discussion of the need to have incurred expenditure "in the course of" gaining or producing income is set out in Decision No 3920 which is also being handed down today. The difficulty for the applicant, in the immediate case, in relying on the establishment of contacts as a basis for deduction is that they were to be used to obtain employment in the future. Specific reference was made to contacts made with the British Broadcasting Corporation which subsequently employed the applicant. This raised the problems faced by the taxpayer in FC of T v Maddalena 71 ATC 4161 in which it was found that expenses incurred in obtaining or securing employment are non-deductible as they are not incurred in the course of but rather are incurred prior to the earning of the income. The same finding must in my opinion be made here.
  4. The remaining basis for the claim is the production of the taped interviews. Although the applicant stated that he believed the tapes were of "commercial value", it became obvious during the hearing that the tapes had not been edited. The editing, the applicant stated, would involve "literally hours and hours of work". Some of the enthusiasm for preparing the tapes for sale seemed to have waned after Mr Boris Becker failed to win a third consecutive Wimbledon title in 1987. The connection between Mr Becker and the tapes was said by the applicant to be the various comments which "all-time greats" had made about Mr Becker that had been recorded by the applicant in personal interviews in 1985. Considering that the expenditure was incurred approximately two years ago, and that there is still no definite indication that income will ever be produced from the tapes, the applicant is "faced with the difficulty that the section [s51(1)] requires that the outgoings be incurred not merely with a view to the obtaining of income in the future, but in 'gaining or producing' assessable income" (Evans v FC of T 86 ATC 4901).
  5. In summary, the only purpose of the trip that can give rise to a deduction is the production of the reports for the radio station. I find that this was a very minor purpose and that the allowance of a deduction of $100, as allowed upon assessment, is reasonable.

9. For these reasons the Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.
