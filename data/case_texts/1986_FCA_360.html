Re Peter Clyne v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1986] FCA 360 (29 October 1986)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: PETER CLYNE

And: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. G210 of 1986

Administrative Law

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEW SOUTH WALES DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Jackson J.

CATCHWORDS

Administrative Law - application for an order of review - notice of assessment of income tax showing provisional tax payable - assessment of taxable income for previous year - objection - taxpayer's statement of estimated taxable income at nil on assumption of nil income or loss for previous year - Commissioner's estimate of provisional tax based on estimate of taxable income for previous year - "reason to believe" taxable income greater than estimated taxable income - whether decision - whether Commissioner limited to accepting taxpayer's estimate of taxable income - whether Commissioner erred in calculating provisional tax an assumption that objection would fail.

Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 s.5.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.221YB, 221YDA, 221YDB.

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v. Saddler (1983) 14 ATR 616

Case P100 82 ATC 489

HEARING

SYDNEY

29:10:1986

Counsel for the applicant: The applicant appeared in person.

Solicitors for the applicant: M.F. Twemlow & Co.

Counsel for the respondent: Mr D.G. Hill Q.C. with Mr A.E. Cramer-Roberts

Solicitor for the respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The application be dismissed.

NOTE: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

This is an application for an order of review pursuant to s.5(1) of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 in respect of a decision of the respondent made under s.221YDA (4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. On 11th September 1986 I overruled the respondent's objection to the competency of the proceedings and it is now necessary to deal with the substance of the application. In so doing I shall turn first to the circumstances giving rise to the matters in question.

2. The applicant's income tax return for the year ended 30th June 1985 showed him as having incurred during that year a loss of $9,009.00, the manner of calculation of that loss being there stated as follows:-

"Gross Fees Received 89665

Less 25% of income paid to trustee

pursuant to order made by Federal 4805

Court on 19 July 1983. 84860

Less amount paid to Bankrupt Estate of

Peter Clyne in lieu of 131 order since

22 January 1985 contribution of estate 2100

Contribution towards legal costs 1000 3100

Gross income before expenses 81760

Less expenses

Advertising 1737

Bank charges 233

Courier expenses 2827

Donations 105

Edgecliff Office Expenses

Petty cash expenses 1029

Rent 4048

Telephone expenses 147 5224

Hire cars 8738

Office expenses 441

Petty cash expenses 1650

Postage 2854

Printing and stationery 7288

Professional consultation fees 17063

Rent paid to Sebel Town House 23188

Roses, long stemmed for office

decoration 1378

Secretarial fees 200

Subscriptions 1074

Taxagent's fees 250

Telephone, telex and cable expenses 5110

Travelling expenses 11409 90769

NETT LOSS (9009) "

======

3. In assessing the applicant's liability to income tax in respect of that year the respondent did not accept the figures as so returned and disallowed:-

(a) the claims to deduct $4,805.00 and $2,100.00, being

payments to the applicant's trustee in bankruptcy;

(b) a claim to deduct $1,000.00, described as a

"contribution towards legal costs", apparently on

the basis that the expenditure was of a private

nature;

(c) the whole of a claimed deduction of $5,224.00 being

"Edgecliff office expenses";

(d) half of a claimed deduction of $8,738.00 for "hire

cars", the amount disallowed being $4,369.00;

(e) $12,140.00 of a claimed deduction of $17,063.00 for

"professional consultation fees";

(f) the whole of a claimed deduction of $23,188.00 for

"Sebel Town House rent";

(g) the whole of a claimed deduction of $1,378.00 for

"roses"; and

(h) $1,703.00 of a claimed deduction of $5,110.00 for

"telephone, telex and cable expenses".

He also added $3,857.00 to the applicant's income by reason of the application of an exchange rate to a fee of $US9,000.00 said to have been paid to the applicant by one E. Solomon.

4. The adoption of these steps had the consequence that the applicant had a taxable income $59,764.00 higher than before and instead of a loss of $9,009.00 in the year, his taxable income was $50,755.00.

5. A notice of assessment was issued by the respondent on 24th January 1986 and the notice showed that an amount of provisional tax calculated at $24,712.00 was payable on or before 1st April 1986.

6. S.221YDA(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act provides that a taxpayer who has received a notice of assessment of income tax, on which is notified an amount of provisional tax payable may, within the times specified in that provision, make an estimate of:-

"(c) the amount of his taxable income for the whole of

that year of income;

(d) the respective amounts included in that estimated

taxable income that represent -

(i) salary or wages;

(ii) income derived from the carrying on of

a business of primary production; and

(iii) other income; "

and of certain other matters and:-

". . . furnish to the Commissioner a statement, in the

form provided by the Commissioner for the purpose,

showing the amounts so estimated together with such

other information as is specified in the form."

7. If a taxpayer adopts the course referred to in s.221YDA(1) and furnishes the Commissioner with the statement required by that provision the amount of provisional tax payable in respect of the year of income to which it relates is by s.221YDA(2), and subject to s.221YDA(4), to be calculated as if the taxable income for the year were an amount equal to the "estimated taxable income". The "estimated taxable income" is the amount of the estimated taxable income shown in the statement furnished to the Commissioner pursuant to s.221YDA(1). See the definition of the term in s.221YA(1).

8. On 3rd February 1986 the applicant sent to the respondent a letter and an application for variation of provisional tax in which he claimed that he estimated his taxable income for the year ended 30th June 1986 as nil. In the letter he stated:-

"Your assessment issued on the 24th January 1986 notifies

provisional tax for the year ended 30th June 1986 as being

$24,712.

This is presumably related to the assessable income for

the year ended 30th June 1985 being $50,755 as stated in

the assessment.

The relevant return showed a net loss of $9,009. My

objection of today's date accepts adjustments totalling

$4,140, so that in effect the return is being amended to

$4,869 as being the net loss.

The rest of your assessment is the subject of objection.

Pursuant to the provisions of Section 221YDA(1) of the

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 you are hereby given notice

that I estimate my taxable income for the whole of the

financial year ending on the 30th June 1986 as being nil.

This estimate is made on the assumption that my objection

is allowed to a sufficent extent to show a nil income, or

a loss, for the year ended 30th June 1985, so that

provisional tax properly payable would be nil.

A statement in the prescribed form is attached, and you

are invited to let me know whether there are any further

details or materials you require."

9. The "objection" to which reference was there made was the applicant's objection to the assessment of income tax in respect of the year ended 30th June 1985, the objection having also been sent to the respondent on 3rd February 1986.

10. Although the Income Tax Assessment Act empowers a taxpayer to make, in effect, his own estimate of provisional tax, the Commissioner is not bound to accept such an estimate and s.221YDA(4) provides that:-

"(4) Where the Commissioner has reason to believe that the

taxable income which will be or has been derived by a

taxpayer in a year of income is greater than the estimated

taxable income, the Commissioner may -

(a) estimate the respective amounts which, in his

opinion, should have been the amounts estimated by

the taxpayer in pursuance of sub-section (1) in

respect of that year of income;

(b) calculate the amount of provisional tax that would

be payable if the amount so estimated had been

shown in a statement duly furnished by the

taxpayer under sub-section (1); and

(c) serve on the taxpayer notice in writing specifying

the amount of provisional tax so calculated,

and the amount of provisional tax so specified is the

amount of provisional tax payable by the taxpayer."

11. The respondent, purporting to exercise the powers conferred by s.221YDA(4), by letter dated 20th May 1986 informed the applicant that an estimate of provisional tax had been made on the basis that the applicant's taxable income would be increased to $50,755.00. The amount of provisional tax payable on that basis was $21,307.00.

12. The applicant in attacking the decision of the respondent under s.221YDA(4) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, relies on s.5(1) (e) - read with s. 5(2) (a) - and ss. 5(1) (f) and 5(1) (j) of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review Act, which provide:-

"(1) A person who is aggrieved by a decision to which this

Act applies . . . may apply to the Court for an order of

review in respect of the decision on any one or more of

the following grounds:

(e) that the making of the decision was an improper

exercise of the power conferred by the enactment in

pursuance of which it was purported to be made;

(f) that the decision involved an error of law, whether

or not the error appears on the record of the

decision;

(j) that the decision was otherwise contrary to law.

(2) The reference in paragraph (1) (e) to an improper

exercise of a power shall be construed as including a

reference to -

(a) taking an irrelevant consideration into account in

the exercise of a power."

13. S.221YDA(4) is so framed that it requires the Commissioner to have "reason to believe" that the taxable income which will be or has been derived by a taxpayer in a year of income is greater than the estimated taxable income. If the Commissioner has that view, he is then empowered to take the steps listed in the paragraphs of s.221YDA(4). I should note in passing the applicant's contention that two "decisions" in terms of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act are involved when the Commissioner performs the functions referred to in s.221YDA(4)(a). The first such decision, it is suggested, is the "decision" that the Commissioner has "reason to believe" the matters listed in the opening words of s.221YDA(4). The second such decision is the estimation referred to in s.221YDA(4)(a).

14. It is clear, it seems to me, that the opening words of s.221YDA(4) mean that there must in fact be the belief there referred to, and that there must be reasonable grounds for that belief (Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v. Saddler (1983) 14 ATR 616 per Mitchell A.C.J. at 617). The absence of one of those matters would mean that the Commissioner's powers to take the steps referred to in the sub-paragraphs of s.221YDA(4) had not arisen. It does not follow, however, that the formation of the view that there is reason to believe is itself a "decision" for the purposes of s.5 of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act, and I do not think that it is. The "decision" for the purposes of s.5 does not occur until, at the earliest, the Commissioner has made the estimate referred to in s.221YDA(4) (a).

15. It is then necessary to consider the ambit of the Commissioner's power under s.221YDA(4)(a). The applicant contends in this regard that the scheme of the provisions is that it is a matter for the taxpayer under s.221YDA(1)(c) to make such estimate as he chooses of his taxable income for the relevant year and that when the Commissioner is exercising the power under s.221YDA(4), he is required to estimate at what figure the taxpayer might subjectively have estimated his taxable income for the relevant year. To put it more specifically, if the applicant in this case believed that he was entitled to have his taxable income for 1984-1985 reduced to nil on the basis of the claims and deductions shown in his income tax return, as amended by his notice of objection, the Commissioner's power under s.221YDA(4)(a) was limited to making an estimate which gave effect to that belief.

16. I do not accept that proposition and, apart from the fact that it seems inherently unlikely, my reasons for not accepting it are as follows. Firstly, s.221YDA(4) comes into play when the Commissioner has reason to believe that the taxpayer's taxable income in a year has been or will be greater than the "estimated taxable income". The Commissioner then has power to estimate what "should have been" the estimated taxable income. The provision suggests that the Commissioner's belief and estimate may be made on the basis of the information then known to him, and that when s.221YDA(4)(a) speaks of the "amounts which . . . should have been the amounts estimated by the taxpayer in pursuance of subsection (1)" it is speaking of the amount of the actual taxable income for the relevant year (so far as it then can be estimated), subject also to the limitation that it shall not exceed that of the preceding year (s.221YDA(5)). Secondly, the purpose of provisional tax is to enable "the income tax that will be payable" by a taxpayer to be collected during the year for which it is levied (s.221YB(1)). Thirdly, s.221YDB(1) provides in certain circumstances for the imposition of additional tax, by way of penalty, if the estimated taxable income is underestimated by more than ten per cent. These circumstances all suggest that the estimates contemplated by the provisions to which I have referred are intended to approach the actual taxable income for the year as closely as possible and do not support the "subjective" approach contended for by the applicant.

17. The real question which then arises is whether the respondent's officers erred in the approach which they took under s.221YDA(4).

18. In this regard it must be borne in mind that the applicant gave no assistance at all to the respondent in his letter and application for variation of provisional tax. He simply asserted that his objections to the 1984-85 assessment should be allowed, that the taxable income for that year should thus be nil, and that in consequence the provisional tax for the 1985-1986 year should be nil. The validity of that conclusion may perhaps be doubtful (see s.221YC(1)(b)), but it is unnecessary to examine the matter further, because it is manifest that the question with which s.221YDA (4) is concerned is the likely taxable income during the 1985-1986 year, rather than the taxable income during the 1984-1985 year.

19. In the absence of any information provided by the applicant the respondent's officers took the course which I shall now describe.

20. The first step in that approach was to make an estimate of the applicant's gross income for the year ended 30th June 1986. That was done by looking at the deposits which had been made to a joint bank account conducted by the applicant and his trustee in bankruptcy, pursuant to an order in the bankruptcy under which all the applicant's income was to be paid into the account. Deposits to that account for the period 1st July 1985 to 17th April 1986 showed a total of $87,494.00 and, extending that over a full year, an amount of $110,000.00 would have been the likely gross income in the year in question. During the preceding year, as I have said, the applicant received gross fees of at least $89,665.00. The applicant, both in his written submissions and in his oral submissions specifically did not challenge this approach to the estimation of the gross assessable income which he might earn in the year ended 30th June 1986.

21. The applicant's complaint, however, is that after estimating likely gross income for 1985-1986 as "around $110,000.00", the respondent's officers were of the view "that allowable claims for deduction would be of a similar order to the 1985 year".

22. Stated as a general proposition there seems to me no error in the approach to allowable deductions taken by the Commissioner's officers. There was no suggestion that the applicant was not continuing to carry on the business which he had carried on during 1984-1985 and indeed his Notice of Objection (Exhibit "D"), though directed to the 1984-1985 assessment, suggested, particularly in paragraphs 8, 9 and 10, that he was continuing to carry on his business from the same place and in the same way.

23. The applicant's substantial complaint, however, is that having assumed that the claims for deductions for 1985-1986 would be similar to those made in 1984-1985, the respondent's officers assumed wrongly that the fate of his objection to the 1984-1985 assessment would be that it failed in toto, and that any review or appeal would be determined against him.

24. I think that it is correct to say that involved in the decision in question was the view of the Commissioner's officers that the applicant's claims for deductions had been properly disallowed but I am not persuaded that an error of the nature contemplated by the provisions of s.5 (1) of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act to which I have referred was involved in their so doing.

25. The terms of s.221YDA (4) (a), in my view do not require the Commissioner to decide objections made to the previous year's assessment of taxable income. Nor do they require the Commissioner to predict the outcome of a review or appeal, if instituted, from the disallowance of an objection which he has not yet disallowed. I see no reason why, in exercising the power under s.221YDA(4)(a) the Commissioner may not use the "fate", as it appears at that point, of claims to deductions in a previous year, or in previous years, as relevant to the position in the year in respect of which s.221YDA(4)(a) is being applied. Of course, if it is apparent that an objection will be allowed in respect of a previous year (as, for example, if a deduction were disallowed by reason of a misapprehension) it would be appropriate to take it into account for the purpose of s.221YDA(4)(a). That does not mean, however, that the process involved in utilising s.221YDA(4) involves deciding upon the merits of objections to the previous year's assessment. I see no reason why, as a general proposition, and in the absence of particular facts being placed before him, the Commissioner may not rely on the prior year's assessment as an indication of the likely situation in the year in question.

26. I should add in particular that I see no reason why the respondent's officers might not take into account the fact that the applicant's major claim to a deduction in the 1984-1985 year was for $23,188.00, being "Rent paid to Sebel Town House", and that the claim was stated in the Notice of Objection to be similar to that in previous years and the current year, and that when the issue had been taken to a Board of Review in previous years, the issue had been found against the applicant.

27. In this regard the Notice of Objection stated:-

"8. In relation to $23,188 referred to in the adjustment

sheet as "Sebel Town House rent", the facts are as

follows:-

(a) Throughout the year in question, and for many years

previous to that, I have occupied suite 909 at the Sebel

Town House, 23 Elizabeth Bay Rd., Sydney as my office.

(b) Suite 909 has been wholly used as an office, and it

was and is my only office. The fact that I sleep in it is

(in my contention) quite incidental.

(c) I work in it for sixteen hours a day; I work

throughout most weekends; and even during the other eight

hours I often hop up and do some work.

(d) The suite contains my desk, stationery, files and

law library. And Sebels also make available their telex

and phone service, access to hire cars, porters who do my

copying and arrange postage of documents and courier

delivery, and a store room for files (as you know very

well, having searched it recently and justified your

search on the basis that these were business documents).

I pay a high rent, partly because these commercially

valuable services go with the room.

(e) The suite is referred to on my letterheads, even on

this objection, as my Sydney office. (I can't go to what

used to be my Vienna office, because you, and more

recently the trustees won't let me go there])

(f) Sebels is also advertised as my office adress (sic)

whenever I advertise my services; it is part of my

business image; and it is given as my adress (sic) for

clients to contact me in my monthly newsletter, and in

most of my books.

9. In the light of the facts set out in par.8, it is

contended that the rent paid for Suite 909 should be

treated wholly as rent paid for an office and ancillary

commercial services, and should be treated as wholly

related to the production of assessable income, and wholly

deductible."

28. The Notice of Objection clearly created the impression that the situation in 1985-1986 was the same as that obtaining in earlier years and in respect of some of those years the Taxation Board of Review No. 1 had decided the question against the applicant. See 82 ATC 489 at 496-497 per Mr Stevens and at 519-520 per Mr Pape. Mr Stevens said:-

"34. The claim in respect of Sebel Town House $8,565

(para.11) consists of a number of components, e.g. room

rental, telephone, etc., but as the amounts allowed for

telephone and courier/postage/photocopy (para.11) exceed

the amounts set out in exhibit AP the disputed items are:

$

Room rental 4,590

Hire cars and taxis 189

Restaurant (1/2 $1,721) 860

Miscellaneous 201

35. With respect to the room rental (which has increased

from $120 per week in July 1977 to $284 per week in April

1980) it is the taxpayer's submission that 85% should be

allowed. The room in question is basically a normal

motel type room. On entering, the bathroom is on the left

with wardrobes on the right hand wall with drawers and a

desk top continuing on. A bed, which can be curtained

off, is situated in the main room with space for a desk

and chairs (earlier coffee table and chairs). The total

area is approximately 28' x 17'. The taxpayer has no

other accommodation and both works (including interviewing

clients) and lives; i.e. watches television, reads,

showers, sleeps, etc.; in that room. He states he does

"not work union hours" and that the bathroom is both his

office and domestic bathroom.

36. The taxpayer in opening said his basic argument was

that, applying the principle of Forsyth and Handley (81

ATC 4157 and 4165) and inverting it, an office is an

office is an office and is wholly deductible even if the

taxpayer sleeps in it (and presumably has nowhere else to

reside). In address he said the issue was "really a

question of fact" and that it was a question of saying "to

what degree is he using that room for purposes associated

with his business". I do not accept that this is the

proper way of looking at the issue in respect of a person

who has nowhere else to reside. Where it is (as here) his

only place of residence that is determinative of the

character of the cost thereof - the proposition that a

"usage" basis may be adopted having been rejected in

Forsyth and Handley.

37. Even if I were of a different view I do not think I

could accept the evidence as establishing a 85% usage for

office purposes. Apart from actual usage (in respect of

which the details available are rather less than precise)

the fact is that the room is available 100% of the time

for private purposes and is used indiscriminately for such

purposes."

and Mr Pape said:-

"(iv) Expenditure re Sebel Town House - $4,590. This

expenditure was incurred for the purpose of providing

accommodation. The reason for its incurrence is not to be

found as was urged upon the Board by Mr Clyne in the

necessity to have a place to carry on his business but for

a place to live. It was submitted that the room had a

dual role, one for carrying on his business and one for

domestic purposes. I would reject this submission for the

reason that whilst it may be said there was a dual use for

the room its essential use and the reason for the

incurrence of the expenditure was the provision of

accommodation. It was an outgoing incurred independently

of whether he carried on business from his room at the

Sebel Town House. The expenditure had the essential

character in my opinion of being for private and domestic

purposes - see Handley v. F.C. of T. 81 ATC 4165, F.C. of

T. v. Forsyth 81 ATC 4157."

29. In these circumstances and being quite conscious of the fact that the decision on this question in one year does not determine the same question in future years (The Shell Company of Australia Ltd v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1930) 44 CLR 530), I am unable to see why the Commissioner could not treat the claims in respect of the Sebel Town House rent as claims which would be made again in the 1985-1986 year and as claims which were prima facie not likely to have a fate different from those earlier dealt with by the Board of Review.

30. Once that view was taken it was apparent enough that if one were simply to add together three figures, namely:-

(a) the likely increase in gross income from $89,665 to

approximately $110,000.00, an increase of say

$20,000.00;

(b) the 1985 deductions disallowed which the applicant

did not challenge, some $4,140; and

(c) a sum of at least $23,188 in respect of Sebel Town

House rent;

there would be little difficulty in reaching the view that the applicant's taxable income for the year ended 30th June 1986 would probably exceed the taxable income for the preceding year.

31. The applicant has not demonstrated any error on the part of the respondent and the application should fail.
