Crockett and Commissioner of Taxation [1998] AATA 1033; (1998) 41 ATR 1164; 99 ATC 2218 (21 December 1998)

Last Updated: 22 September 2009





Administrative

Appeals

Tribunal



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No QT98/300

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re WILLIAM JAMES CROCKETT

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



Decision No: 13566

DECISION

Tribunal Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date 21 December 1998

Place Brisbane

Decision The Tribunal decides that:

(a) The decision under review is set aside; and

(b) the matter is remitted to the respondent Commissioner with a direction that a Departure Authorisation Certificate issue today authorising the applicant to depart from Australia for United Kingdom and Sarawak not later than seven days after 22 December 1998 and return to Australia not later than 31 January 1999.



Decision No 13566 (Sgd) K L Beddoe

(Senior Member)



CATCHWORDS

TAXATION ADMINISTRATION : Departure Authorisation Certificate - meaning of “on humanitarian grounds”



Taxation Administration Act 1953 ss 14R, 14S, 14U



Re QT95/4 and FC of T (Decision No 9964)

REASONS FOR DECISION

21 December 1998 Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



  1. On 18 March 1997 the applicant was advised that a Departure Prohibition Order (“DPO”) had been made pursuant to s14S of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 (“the Act”). By letter dated 25 August 1998 the applicant applied to the respondent for a “Temporary Departure Certificate” for a short visit to England and the Malaysian State of Sarawak as soon as possible.
  2. By letter dated 9 November 1998 the respondent's officer notified the applicant that the request for a Departure Authorisation Certificate (“DAC”) had been denied. On 6 November 1998 the applicant applied to this Tribunal for review of the decision. The disparity in the dates came about because the applicant was given oral advice of the decision to refuse the DAC on 5 November 1998. It was not suggested that the application was invalid; nor was it said, in particular, that the circumstances were the same as those that arose in Re QT95/4 and FC of T (Decision No 9964).
  3. Section 14R of the Act provides that a person in respect of whom a DPO is in force, and who knows that such an order is in force in respect of him, shall not depart from Australia for a foreign country unless the departure is authorised by a DAC.
  4. The respondent's (and this Tribunal’s) authority to grant a DAC is found in s 14U(1) of the Act which reads as follows:
14U. (1) Where, on application made by a person in respect of whom a departure prohibition order is in force:
(a) the Commissioner is satisfied:
  • (i) that, if a departure authorization certificate is issued in respect of the person, it is likely that:
  • (A) the person will depart from Australia and will return to Australia within such period as the Commissioner considers to be appropriate in relation to the person; and
  • (B) circumstances of the kind referred to in paragraph 14T (1) (a) will come into existence within such period as the Commissioner considers to be appropriate in relation to the person; and
    • (ii) that it is not necessary or desirable for the person to give security under subsection (2) for the person’s return to Australia; or
(b) in a case where the Commissioner is not satisfied with respect to the matters referred to in paragraph (a):
  • (i) the person has given security under subsection (2) to the satisfaction of the Commissioner for the person’s return to Australia; or
  • (ii) if the person is unable to give such security, the Commissioner is satisfied that:
    • (A) a departure authorization certificate should be issued in respect of the person on humanitarian grounds; or
    • (B) a refusal to issue a departure authorization certificate in respect of the person would be detrimental to the interests of Australia:
the Commissioner shall issue a certificate authorizing the person to depart from Australia for a foreign country on or before the seventh day after a day (being a day later than, but not more than 7 days later than, the day on which the certificate is issued) specified in the certificate.


  1. At the hearing the applicant conducted his own case and an officer of the Taxation Office represented the respondent. The documents lodged in the Tribunal pursuant to s 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 were placed before the Tribunal as the T documents and further documents were tendered and marked as exhibits. Oral evidence was given by the applicant. No oral evidence was called for the respondent.
  2. The applicant was audited by the respondent's officers in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1988 to 30 June 1996 inclusive. Assessments made as a consequence of the audit have been the subject of review of the objection decisions in this Tribunal. The Tribunal completed the hearing of those reviews on the day prior to the hearing of this matter. The decisions in the review of the objection decisions and the review of the present application are being published at the same time.
  3. The essence of the applicant's case may be summarised as follows:
    • (a) he believes it is necessary that he is able to visit sick relatives in the United Kingdom;
    • (b) he believes it is essential that he has time with his wife and children in Sarawak;
    • (c) he believes it is necessary that he also spends time with the village in Sarawak in which his family resides and where he is providing informal health care and organising the construction of a church; and
    • (d) he believes that no good purpose is served by keeping him in Australia while he has no funds and relies on social security benefits paid by the Department of Family and Community Services.

  1. The applicant has not offered any form of security and I am satisfied and find that he is unable to give such security. The only asset of consequence held by the applicant is his interest in proceedings against an insurance company. It is not for this Tribunal to try to forecast the applicant's prospects of success in these proceedings; however the applicant made it clear that he needs to be back in Australia at the beginning of February 1999 for the trial in that matter.
  2. The respondent's case is that he has an unsatisfied judgment in favour of a Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in an amount of $377,105.25 plus costs. It should be said immediately that because of concessions subsequently made by the respondent and the decisions of this Tribunal the applicant's debt, when recalculated, will be substantially less than the amount of the judgment. The respondent is concerned that although the applicant is an Australian citizen (since 1993) he has no particular reason to remain in Australia. The respondent is also concerned that there is a history of very substantial dissipation to overseas of investment funds previously held in Australia. It is clear and I find that the funds were sent overseas for family reasons prior to any issue arising between the applicant and the respondent. It is clear to me that the applicant had a cavalier and reckless attitude to his obligations in relation to income tax and it would be easy to infer that the applicant was seeking to avoid payment of income tax when he transferred funds overseas. I am satisfied that is not the case in so far as the funds were transmitted overseas to his sons of his first marriage. The cause of the dissipation of the funds was almost certainly the breakup of the applicant's second marriage and the proceedings in the Family Court. The dissipation of the funds is more readily explained by these circumstances than by any action on behalf of the respondent. In coming to that conclusion I have relied upon evidence given in the review of the objection decisions.
  3. The applicant says that his bona fides are established by the fact that he returned to Australia after he became aware of the issue of the DPO and he asserts that he will also return to Australia in time for the trial of his claim against the insurance company. He sought to show that his need to travel overseas was on humanitarian grounds because of his need to see his family and his voluntary work for the village in Sarawak.
  4. I have come to the view that a Departure Authorisation Certificate should be issued. I am satisfied, on the basis of the applicant's evidence, that it is more likely than not that he will return to Australia prior to 31 January 1999. I am not satisfied that there is any reasonable prospect that the applicant will discharge his liability for unpaid income tax before 1 February 1999. I am satisfied that the applicant cannot offer worthwhile security and it is not desirable therefore that he seek to do so. I find he is unable to give meaningful security for his return to Australia to the respondent.
  5. I am satisfied that the applicant's personal circumstances are such that a DAC should be issued in respect of the applicant on humanitarian grounds. I received little assistance as to what it meant by “humanitarian grounds” in the hearing. Contrary to the submissions of the applicant I am not satisfied that it applies only to those who do good works. Rather it adopts the meaning of “humane” in the sense of compassion so that the test is really whether the applicant's circumstances are such that the DAC should be issued on compassionate grounds.
  6. The applicant's personal circumstances are that he suffered a serious mental breakdown in the early 1980’s, lost his employment as a design engineer and has suffered severe emotional disturbance since that time. He maintained employment as a baggage handler with Air Canada for a number of years and still qualifies for discounted air travel as a former employee of the airline - hence his capacity to purchase international airline tickets with only minimal financial capacity.
  7. I am satisfied it would be humane to issue a DAC in this case. Taking into account the applicant's evidence to this Tribunal the Certificate should be issued on 21 December 1998 for the period to and including 31 January 1999.
  8. The decision under review will be set aside and the matter remitted to the respondent Commissioner with a direction that a Departure Authorisation Certificate issue authorising the applicant to depart from Australia for United Kingdom and Sarawak not later than seven days after 22 December 1998 and return to Australia not later than 31 January 1999.

I certify that this and the five (5) preceding pages are a true copy of the decision and reasons for decision herein of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



Signed: A Brown

Secretary
