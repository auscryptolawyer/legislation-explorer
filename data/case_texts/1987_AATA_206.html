Re Kazimierz Sosnowski and Commissioner of Superannuation [1987] AATA 206 (29 May 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: KAZIMIERZ SOSNOWSKI

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. V86/435

AAT No. 3515

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

I.R. Thompson (Deputy President)

Dr D.M. Sutherland (Member)

Dr C.G. Woodard (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - lumbar disc degeneration and cervical disc degeneration - whether applicant failed to furnish information or furnished false (or false or misleading) information - relevance of state of mind of applicant - withholding information of compensation claim and prior accidents

Superannuation Act 1976 ss 16(11), 45, 66(2), 154(2)

Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act (No 2) 1986 s 10

Fry v. Commissioner for Superannuation [1984] FCA 211; (1984) 6 ALD 229

Re Watts and Commissioner for Superannuation (1981) 3 ALD 267

Re Whiteford and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision No V86/34: 6 February 1987)

HEARING

CANBERRA

29:5:1987

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

This application for review is brought under section 154(6) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the 1976 Act"). It seeks review of a decision of a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation which has been varied upon reconsideration in pursuance of section 154(4) of that Act. At the hearing the applicant was represented by Mr G. Moore, of Counsel, and the respondent by Mr R. C. Whithear, who is the Assistant Commissioner for Superannuation. Oral evidence was given by the applicant only. The documentary evidence tendered included all the documents lodged with the Tribunal pursuant to section 37 of Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, documents relating to injuries suffered by the applicant in the course of his employment in 1969, 1976 and 1977 and to compensation claims made in respect of those injuries, clinical notes made by doctors who examined the applicant in May 1969, November 1969, November 1974 and September 1982, a statement of account in respect of the applicant sent to his employer by a medical clinic which he attended in March 1969 and a report by an orthopaedic surgeon, Mr M. A. Khan, dated 30 September 1986.

2. The documentary evidence and the oral evidence of the applicant establishes his history from his youth, and in particular his work history from 1968 onwards. He is now 58 years old. He was born in Poland but fled from that country when it was overrun by the Germans and the Russians during the Second World War. From 1942 until 1951 he lived in East Africa. He attended a school run for Polish children in Arusha, Tanganyika, where the language of instruction was Polish. At school he was taught carpentry; after leaving school he worked for some time in Kenya as a carpenter. He came to Australia in 1950 at the age of 21.

3. He worked in Australia initially as a labourer, then as a miner and after that as a carpenter. He entered the Commonwealth Public Service in 1967 as a carpenter in the Postmaster-General's Department. He resigned from the Public Service in 1970 and then worked in private industry first as a mechanical draftsman, then as a carpenter and after that again as a mechanical draftsman. In 1974 the company for which he was working retrenched him. He had the opportunity to obtain employment in the Postmaster-General's Department as a driver and did so. He continued in that work, initially with the Postmaster-General's Department and then, when the Australian Telecommunications Commission ("Telecom") was established in 1975, with it. Until 1978 he was a temporary employee and not eligible to contribute for superannuation benefits under the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the 1922 Act") or the 1976 Act.

4. In 1978, however, he became an "eligible employee" and section 45 of the 1976 Act required that he pay fortnightly basic contributions for superannuation benefits. He was required, under section 16 of that Act, to undergo a medical examination. Although the purpose of such an examination is not expressly stated in the Act, it is apparent from other provisions that it is to enable the Commissioner to decide whether a benefit classification certificate should be issued in respect of the employee and, if so, what physical or mental conditions should be specified in the certificate. In 1978 a employee was required to complete a form (Form S6) and take it with him when attending for examination; the particulars he was required to give related to his medical history. In collaboration with his brother-in-law, who made the actual entries, the applicant completed Form S6 and then attended for examination by a Commonwealth Medical Officer, Dr Rutter. After the examination the Commonwealth Medical Officer made certain entries on the form and signed it. As a consequence of those entries the Commissioner decided to issue a benefit classification certificate in respect of the applicant, specifying "spondylosis of cervical spine". In September 1982 the applicant's retirement on grounds of invalidity was recommended by another Commonwealth Medical Officer; as a result he was retired by Telecom.

5. The documentary evidence and the oral evidence of the applicant establishes that on 14 March 1969 he hurt his lower back in the course of his work in the Postmaster-General's Department. Because he was working at Essendon at the time, he went to a clinic in Moonee Ponds rather than to the doctor in Richmond whom he normally attended. The doctor who saw him at the clinic arranged for his lumbo-sacral spine to be X-rayed; apparently after examining the X-ray photograph, the doctor recorded that the applicant had suffered "severe sprained back". He informed the applicant that he had not damaged his spine and that the pain was muscular; he arranged for him to receive physiotherapy. The applicant was away from work for one month; his claim for compensation under the workers' compensation legislation then in force in relation to government employees was accepted; he was paid compensation for his incapacity. Also his medical expenses were paid. At the end of that month he returned to work.

6. In May 1969 he was medically examined for the purposes of the 1922 Act, to decide whether he was entitled to contribute for superannuation benefits and, if so, whether to the Superannuation Fund or only to the Provident Account. The Commonwealth Medical Officer who examined him wrote in his clinical notes that he had viewed the X-ray taken at the clinic and that there was a possibility of early spondylolisthesis. He recorded also that on clinical examination he found the applicant's back to be "quite flexible" with "no abnormal clinical findings in spine or lower extremities". He recommended that a decision on whether the applicant was entitled to contribute to the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account be deferred for six months. On 27 November 1969 another Commonwealth Medical Officer examined him and recorded in his clinical notes that the applicant had been absent from work due to his back on one further day, 13 May 1969, but had built himself a home between May and November "without further back trouble". He wrote "nil abnormal detected in spine or lower extremities". However, he noted that a further X-ray examination had been made of the lumbo-sacral spine and that the intervertebral disc space between LV4-LV5 was "slightly diminished" and its appearance consistent with a lesion of that intervertebral space. He noted also a similar diminution of the disc space between LV2-LV3. He wrote "no other abnormality of structure or relationship was shown". He recommended that the applicant be accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account but not to the Superannuation Fund. In December 1969 a standard form letter was sent to the applicant telling him that he had been accepted as a Provident Account contributor and that "the reports of the medical examination which you have since undergone show that your physical fitness is below the standard set down for admission to the Superannuation Fund". It did not tell him in what respect his physical fitness fell below that standard. He resigned from the Public Service in January 1970.

7. The applicant gave evidence that from the time he left the Postmaster-General's Department in January 1970 until an accident in 1976 he had suffered no injury and made no compensation claim. Furthermore, he said that he had experienced no further problem with his back during that period. In November 1974 a Commonwealth Medical Officer examined him for what is stated on the report form to have been a "special" reason, but the reason is not specified. The Commonwealth Medical Officer wrote in his clinical notes "1969 - lumbar disc lesion - no recurrence of pain or disability for past 5 years; apart from this, minor ailments only". It is likely that the special reason for the examination was that the Postmaster-General's Department was aware that the applicant had suffered the injury to his back in 1969 and wished to ascertain whether there was any continuing incapacity. It is clear that the Commonwealth Medical Officer had access to the previous record. What he recorded was consistent with the evidence which the applicant gave in this proceeding.

8. The documentary evidence and the applicant's oral evidence establish that on 26 October 1976 he was standing beside his motor car holding the door open with his hand and waiting for traffic to pass, when a passing taxi struck the door and jarred the applicant's right shoulder. The applicant made a claim in respect of the injury under the current workers' compensation legislation; a determination was made that he had sustained a personal injury, contusion of the right shoulder. It did not cause him to be absent from work; compensation for incapacity was not paid. $6.90 was paid in respect of the cost of medical treatment. In view of the documentary evidence we have no hesitation in accepting the applicant's evidence that the injury which he suffered was minor, transient and never incapacitating.

9. The documentary evidence and the oral evidence of the applicant establishes that on 19 September 1977, when the applicant was driving a motor car in the course of his employment, it was struck from behind by another motor vehicle; he suffered a whiplash injury to his neck as a result. Again he claimed compensation under the currently applicable workers' compensation legislation; a determination was made that he had sustained a personal injury, namely whiplash injury to the neck, and that the Commonwealth was liable to pay him compensation in respect of it. Compensation was paid for incapacity during the period of one month immediately following the accident and also on one day in February 1981, four days in July 1981, three days in February 1982 and six days in April 1982. In addition, the cost of medical treatment which he received between 1977 and January 1978, between February 1981 and July 1981 and between April 1982 and 1 June 1982 was paid by the Commonwealth. The applicant gave evidence that he had been involved in a similar collision ten days before that in which he suffered the injury for which he claimed compensation; it seems, however, that that had not resulted in incapacity.

10. The documentary evidence and the oral evidence of the applicant establish that on 7 December 1981 in the course of his work the applicant injured his lower back while lifting a box to put it into the boot of his motor car. His sick leave record discloses that he was absent from work because of "back strain" from 8 December 1981 to 24 December 1981 and then because of "lower back strain" from 5 July 1982 to 2 August 1982 and again from 10 August 1982 to 4 October 1982.

11. On 10 September 1982 a Commonwealth Medical Officer, Dr McInnes, examined him and reported both for the purpose of consideration of the Commonwealth's liability under the workers' compensation legislation and also with regard to the applicant's fitness to continue in his employment. Dr McInnes recorded that the applicant suffered from both lumbar and cervical intervertebral disc degeneration, the former having been aggravated by "the incidents of 14th March 1969 and 7th December 1981" and the latter by "the incident of 19th September 1977". He considered the applicant to be totally incapacitated for work and that the incapacity was likely to be permanent. He stated that the prognosis was poor and that he considered that the applicant was unfit for continued employment. He recommended his retirement. He expressed the opinion that both conditions had contributed materially to the necessity for retirement. As required by the form, he estimated the percentage which each condition contributed to the applicant's incapacity, the lumbar disc degeneration 70% and the cervical disc degeneration 30%. As a result of his report the applicant was retired from the Public Service on the ground of invalidity.

12. Section 66 of the 1976 Act contains provision for determining the rate of an employee's invalidity pension. If he has had less than 20 years' contributory service and the Commissioner is of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused or substantially contributed to by a physical or mental condition specified in a benefit classification certificate, or by a physical or mental condition connected with any such condition, the invalidity benefit is less than it would otherwise be. A delegate of the Commissioner formed the opinion that the physical condition, spondylosis of cervical spine, specified in the benefit classification certificate issued in respect of the applicant had caused or substantially contributed to the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement, and decided that he was entitled to receive only the smaller benefit.

13. The applicant was dissatisfied with that decision and wrote to the Commissioner stating that he believed that the medical condition specified in the benefit classification certificate had not caused his retirement and that the medical condition for which he had been superannuated was related to a problem in his lower back caused by the compensable accident suffered at work in December 1981. He enclosed a letter written by Dr A. Skarbek, the general practitioner who had normally looked after him from 1967 onwards. Dr Skarbek expressed the opinion that the neck injury was "not the reason for the applicant's superannuation" and that the reason for it was the low back strain and continuous back aches. The delegate's decision was then reconsidered by Mr Whithear. On the basis of Dr McInnes' estimate of the percentage contribution made by each of the two conditions to the incapacity which had caused the applicant to be retired he decided that the spondylosis of the cervical spine had not caused or substantially contributed to the incapacity. Consequently, having reconsidered the decision, he revoked it.

14. However, the evidence which he had examined for the purposes of reconsidering the original decision caused him to consider the possibility that in July 1978 at the time of the medical examination the applicant might have failed to furnish information required to be furnished or he might have furnished false information. After further investigations, a delegate of the Commissioner concluded that the applicant had failed to furnish information, or had furnished false information, regarding his low back injury and that, if he had not done so, there would have been in force in respect of him a benefit classification certificate specifying both lumbar disc degeneration and spondylosis of cervical spine. Consequently, pursuant to section 16(11) of the 1972 Act on 31 October 1985 he issued a benefit classification certificate in which both of those conditions were specified. By virtue of the provisions of that sub-section, that certificate was then deemed to have been in force in respect of the applicant immediately before his retirement.

15. On the following day another delegate decided that the incapacity which was the ground of the applicant's retirement was caused or substantially contributed to by lumbar disc degeneration and cervical disc degeneration so that the provisions of section 66(2) were applicable to him. The applicant was notified of that decision and requested its reconsideration. Mr Whithear undertook that reconsideration pursuant to section 154(4) of the 1972 Act. Having reconsidered it, he confirmed it. The application to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal was made in respect of Mr Whithear's decision; however, at the commencement of the hearing it was agreed by the parties that the decisions to be reviewed were those made on 31 October 1985 and 1 November 1985.

16. At the time when those decisions were made, and also at the time when they were confirmed, section 16(11) was as follows:-

17. However, late in 1986 Parliament enacted the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act (No. 2) 1986. Section 10, which amended section 16 of the 1976 Act in a number of respects, came into operation on 18 December 1986. The only part of its provisions with which we are concerned in this proceeding is that containing amendments by way of the omission of sub-sections (11) and (11A) from section 16 and the substitution for them of the following new sub-sections (11), (11A), (11B) and (11C):-

18. Although the amendments are extensive, for the purpose of the present proceeding, as both Mr Whithear and Mr Moore agreed, the only one which needs to be taken into account is the inclusion of the words "or misleading" in the expression "false or misleading information". Mr Whithear suggested that the addition of those two words had made no substantive change to the meaning of the expression but had merely clarified it. That may be correct but we express no concluded view on the matter as, in any event, as both Mr Whithear and Mr Moore again agreed, any change which it made is of no significance in relation to the facts which we are required to consider in this proceeding.

19. Both Mr Whithear and Mr Moore submitted that the provisions which we were required to apply in deciding the application in this proceeding were those contained in the 1976 Act before its amendment by the 1986 Act. Rightly, in our view, they submitted that whether the previous provisions or the amended provisions were to be applied depended upon whether or not any accrued rights of the applicant were affected by the amendment. Both Mr Whithear and Mr Moore took the view that at the time when the decision under review was made the applicant had an accrued right to be paid superannuation benefits at the full rate, unless the facts of which the Commissioner was required to be satisfied under section 16(11)(d) existed at that time. We regard the matter as by no means easy; a thorough consideration of the law relating to the applicability and non-applicability of provisions in amending legislation and also a thorough consideration of the provisions of the 1976 Act and the 1986 amending Act would be necessary before we could resolve it properly. As the amendment has effected no change of significance in relation to the facts in the present proceeding, we refrain from embarking upon any such investigation. We understand from Mr Whithear that there is another case now before the Tribunal in another State in which the matter will have to be resolved; as it is immaterial in this proceeding which provisions are applicable, it is better that we do not muddy the water by expressing an opinion on the matter.

20. The failure to furnish information or the furnishing of false information (or the giving of false or misleading information) by the applicant is alleged by the respondent to have occurred when he handed to the Commonwealth Medical Officer on 12 July 1978 the Form S6 completed on his behalf by his brother-in-law and signed by him and did not give Dr Rutter information about the injury to his lower back in 1969. On the first page of the form he was required to write his name and address. Those were entered correctly. On the second page he was required first to state his family medical history. That part was completed and nothing has occurred to cause there to be any doubt as to the accuracy of what he stated.

21. The third part, Part B, was on the second and third pages; it was headed "Applicant's Statement of Personal Medical History". Immediately under the heading and before the questions which were to be answered there was a note which read:-

"Every question in Part B must be answered 'Yes' or

'No' and full details of all 'Yes' answers must be

furnished in Part C on Page 3 before attending for

the medical examination."

There were 68 questions in Part B; all were required to be answered. The first 53 were prefaced by the question "Are you now suffering or have you ever suffered from any of the following disabilities - ". Each question related to one disability or several associated disabilities. For instance, one question related only to "migraine"; another related to "nervous fatigue or neurasthenia"; yet another related to "tuberculosis, excessive sputum or disease of the lungs". We need to be concerned with only two of those 53 questions, one related to "disturbance of sensation of muscular activity" and the other to "lumbago, paralysis or disorder of the limbs or spine". To both those questions the applicant answered "No".

22. After the 53 questions were two questions relating to injuries to the eyes and to conditions requiring the wearing of glasses or contact lenses (to the latter of which the applicant made no reply at all). The remaining eleven questions were of a broad nature. The first was "Have you had any operations, surgical treatment or accidents?". To that the applicant answered "No". The next question was "Have you had any complaints, illness or injury not mentioned above?". He answered "Yes". The following question was "Have you been absent from school or work through sickness over the past two years? (If so please state the duration)". To that also he answered "Yes". The next significant question was "Date you last received medical or surgical treatment and for what condition?" To that he answered "Yes". (As already stated, he was required to answer "Yes" or "No" although neither answer could possibly have been correct in view of the manner in which the question was framed.) There were then a number of questions intended to ascertain whether he had had a bad medical record in terms of non-acceptance for life assurance, superannuation or similar benefits or claims for disability pension or compensation. The first of those questions was "Have you ever been rejected as a first class risk for the life assurance, superannuation (including provident account), D.F.R.B. or D.F.R.D.B. benefits or any other retirement benefit scheme?". The applicant answered "No" to that question. The third question in that group related to application for or receipt of war service pension or retirement on invalidity grounds. The next question was "Have you ever received payments under the Compensation Act?". To that the applicant answered "No". The following two questions asked whether he had had any disabilities accepted as war caused by the Repatriation Department and whether he was in receipt of a Repatriation Pension.

23. Part C of Form S6 required the applicant to furnish details of "all 'Yes' answers" to questions in Part B. Having answered "Yes" to a question among the first 53 which asked whether he had had any tropical or infectious disease, he stated in Part C that he had had malaria while living in Africa. In respect of the question whether he had had any complaints, illness or injury, he gave details of the "whiplash neck injury" in October 1977, stating that it had occurred while he was on duty. He then gave details purportedly in respect of the question whether he had been absent from work through sickness; he wrote that he had worn a neck brace for 4 weeks in November 1977. He repeated the details in respect of the whiplash injury and the neck brace in amplification of his answer to the question asking the date on which he had last received medical or surgical treatment. Finally, in amplification of a question asking whether he had been a contributor to any superannuation scheme prior to his current application, he wrote that he had been employed as a carpenter in the Postmaster-General's Department in 1968, was permanent and "belonged to Superannuation Fund".

24. Mr Whithear submitted that the applicant either failed to furnish information or furnished false information (or gave false or misleading information) when he answered "No" to the two questions among the first 53 in Part B to which we have referred above, when he answered "No" to the question whether he had had any operations, surgical treatment or accidents, when he answered "No" to the question whether he had ever received payments "under the Compensation Act" and when he amplified in Part C his answer to the question about membership of a superannuation scheme and stated that he "belonged to Superannuation Fund".

25. The Form S6 bears notes made by Dr Rutter written adjacent to the questions whether the applicant had had any complaints, illness or injury, whether he had been absent from work through sickness over the previous two years, and when he had last received medical or surgical treatment and for what condition. In respect of each he wrote simply "whiplash injury of neck". In respect of the question relating to the date he added "21.9.77". As the applicant had written in Part C that he had worn a neckbrace for four weeks in November 1977, it is somewhat surprising that Dr Rutter accepted the date 21.9.77 as the last date on which the applicant had received treatment, even if that was what the applicant told him. Although there was a space in Part C of Form S6 for "C.M.O.'s comments" with an instruction "notes are required on all affirmative answers", Dr Rutter made no comment on any of the applicant's affirmative answers other than the brief reference to "whiplash injury of neck" and the date "21.9.77", written not in the space provided for his comments but adjacent to the answers in Part B.

26. On another page he was required to record the results of his clinical examination of the applicant. In answer to the question "Is there any abnormality in use of legs and arms? Any restriction or pain in the movement of the spine?" he wrote "Limitation of movement to R. & L in cervical spine. Tenderness on left over trapezium in occipital region. Limits normal as is that of spine". In a box on the front page of the form headed "Commonwealth Medical Officer's Comment" he stated that he considered that the applicant did not meet the general medical standard due to the physical condition "spondylosis of cervical spine". The applicant admitted at the hearing that he did not tell Dr Rutter about the pain in his lumbar region in 1969 or about the injury to his shoulder in 1976. We are satisfied that nothing was observed by Dr Rutter to cause him to have further investigations made of the condition of the applicant's lumbar spine and that the benefit classification certificate was issued in ignorance of the condition of the lumbar spine at that time, whatever it was.

27. Mr Whithear submitted that the applicant should have answered "Yes" either to the question whether he had ever suffered "lumbago, paralysis or disorder of the limbs or spine" or to the question whether he had ever suffered disturbance of muscular activity, or to both. If either of those questions had been answered "Yes", he submitted, the condition of his back would have been investigated and the degeneration of his lumbar spine discovered and taken into account. The same result would have followed, he submitted, if the applicant had answered "Yes" to the question whether he had had any accidents and had then recorded in Part C details of the accident in 1969, or if, when Dr Rutter asked him about his complaints, illnesses or injury, the applicant had informed him of the injury in 1969, or if he had answered "Yes" to either the question whether he had ever been rejected as a first class risk for life assurance, superannuation etc. or the question whether he had ever received payments under the Compensation Act. Finally, he submitted that, although under the 1922 Act the Provident Account was part of the Superannuation Fund, the statement by the applicant that he belonged to the Superannuation Fund was false (or false and misleading) and that, if he had stated that he had contributed to the Provident Account, the reason for that would have been investigated with the same result.

28. Mr Moore conceded that, if information had been given which had drawn attention to the back problem in 1969, the Tribunal would need to find on a balance of probabilities that the Commissioner would have caused an investigation to be made into the condition of the applicant's lumbar spine and that, as there was some degeneration of it, that would have been specified in the benefit classification certificate. We accept that that is so.

29. There is no doubt that the applicant did not furnish information relating to the 1969 incident, the pain which he suffered in his back as the result of it or the fact that he had been paid compensation for incapacity resulting from it. We have no doubt that the questions whether he had had any complaints, illness or injury and whether he had ever received payments under the Compensation Act required that that information be furnished. However, that is not the end of the matter. It is well established that, for the purposes of section 16(11)(d)(i) a person does not fail to give information if he has no knowledge of the relevant facts (Fry v. Commissioner for Superannuation [1984] FCA 211; (1984) 6 ALD 229; Re Watts and Commissioner for Superannuation (1981) 3 ALD 267). Nor is it required of a person that he seek information as to the relevant facts in order that he may have knowledge of them and be able to give the information (Fry). He fails to furnish information only if it is information of which he has been apprised or told.

30. In the present case the applicant had knowledge that he had suffered pain in his back following an incident when he lifted a heavy object at work in 1969, that he had been incapacitated for 4 weeks by the pain and that he had received compensation for that incapacity. However, Mr Moore has submitted that the evidence establishes that he did not understand the questions and that his failure to furnish the information which was required was the result of his not knowing that it was required. The applicant gave evidence that his brother-in-law, who assisted him to complete the form, was employed in the Public Service as a clerk. Between them they had read the questions. As there was considerable evidence before the Tribunal that the applicant was deaf to a marked degree by 1978 but he answered "No" to the question whether he was suffering from deafness, it is apparent that they did not read with any great care at least the block of 53 questions.

31. However, even if they had done so, we consider it is unlikely that either of them would have understood "disturbance of sensation or muscular activity" to be a reference to pain or the inhibition of movements of the muscles because of pain; indeed we doubt very much whether it was intended to bear that meaning. We consider that, in view of the state of the applicant's knowledge at the time, the question whether he was suffering, or had suffered, from "lumbago, paralysis or disorder of the limbs or spine" was correctly answered "No". The doctor at the clinic had recorded that the injury in 1969 was a severely sprained back; we accept that he had informed the applicant that his spine had not been damaged. Lumbago is said by the Shorter Oxford English Dictionary to mean "a rheumatic affection in the lumbar region of the body". Although spondylosis is generally regarded as a rheumatic condition, there is no evidence that the applicant was aware that he suffered spondylosis of his spine.

32. His answer to the question whether he had had any operations, surgical treatment or accidents was, we think, correct insofar as it related to operations and surgical treatment. The applicant gave evidence that he understood "accidents" to be events such as collisions of motor vehicles. On the balance of probabilities we find that he did not realise that the expression applied to the incident in 1969 when his lifting the heavy weight precipitated the pain in his lower back. In respect of the question whether he had had "any complaints, illness or injury", he said that he did not understand what was meant by "complaints" and that he had not considered the pain in his back in 1969 as having been caused by an injury. He said that he did not understand the question relating to "payment under the Compensation Act" to relate to workers' compensation payments. He pointed out that the question appeared between those relating to war service pensions, retirement on invalidity grounds from the Defence Forces, disabilities accepted as war caused by the Repatriation Department and receipt of Repatriation pension. He said that he thought it referred to service in the armed forces and that that was why he had answered "No".

33. In Fry, Sweeney J., with whom the other two Judges agreed, observed at page 237:-

"There must be a requirement made of the employee to

furnish information before any obligation arises.

The extent of that obligation depends upon the

questions asked of him and the actual state of his

knowledge in relation to them.

. . . . .

An employee complies with the section if he answers

the questions truthfully according to his actual

knowledge and state of mind." (our emphasis)

Mr Whithear asked us to decide the meaning of "state of mind". As we pointed out at the hearing, the phrase is not part of the language of the statute; it was used by Sweeney J. to state his understanding of what the statute required. We think that no useful purpose would be served by exploring its precise meaning. On the other hand we consider that it is significant that His Honour referred not only to "actual knowledge" but to the employee's "state of mind", thereby indicating that something more than mere knowledge of the facts in respect of which information was required had to be taken into account.

34. We are conscious of the fact that a protestation of misunderstanding of a question in Form S, or its current replacement, may be a mere pretext for failure to answer it truthfully or to furnish the information required. We are not to be taken to encourage such deceit. However, where an employee, having given to a question the requisite degree of attention that the completion of the form properly requires, genuinely misunderstands it and that misunderstanding is the reason why the answer to the question is not what it should have been, the employee ought not to be held to have failed to furnish the information or to have furnished false information (or to have given false or misleading information); we consider that this accords with the principles which underlie the decision in Fry's case and Sweeney J.'s reference to "state of mind".

35. We turn, therefore, to consider whether the applicant's incorrect answers to which we have referred were the result of a genuine misunderstanding, itself not due to any failure to give them the attention which he should have given them. In coming to a conclusion on that issue, we consider that the following matters are relevant:-

(1) the applicant's lack of formal education in the

English language and his lack of confidence in

his knowledge of English as a written language,

as evidenced by the fact that all the forms which

he has completed over the years for various

purposes and which were tendered in evidence

before us were completed on his behalf by others;

(2) the fact that, after initially stating in

evidence that he did not consider the problem

which he had with his back in 1969 to be the

result of an injury, he subsequently admitted in

cross-examination that it was an injury;

(3) the fact that he claimed compensation in respect

of the injuries in 1969, 1976 and 1977;

(4) the fact that he disclosed in Part C that he had

suffered the neck injury in the course of his

duty, which should have alerted anyone

scrutinising his answers to perceive that he

might have received payments under the

Compensation Act and that his answer that he had

not done so might be incorrect;

(5) the fact that the form was filled in to take to

the medical examination and was used by the

Commonwealth Medical Officer as the basis of

asking the applicant questions about injuries and

the fact that the Commonwealth Medical Officer,

in spite of being told that the whiplash injury

resulted from a motor vehicle accident, did not

cause the applicant to alter his answer to the

question whether he had had any accidents and

either did not ask him whether he had received

compensation payments or, if he did and the

applicant told him that he had received them, did

not cause him to alter the answer to his question

whether he had received payments under the

Compensation Act.

36. To this point all the members of the Tribunal are in agreement. Unfortunately, we are not all able to agree on the question whether the applicant's failure, by the end of the medical examination, to furnish the information about his injury in 1969 and the workers' compensation payments which he had received in respect of it was the result of a misunderstanding, not resulting from his failure to give the questions proper attention, of the questions which he was required to answer. Mr Woodard's view differs from ours. We are satisfied on a balance of probabilities that the applicant did not misunderstand the questions relating to whether he had suffered injury or received payments under the Compensation Act; certainly he could not have misunderstood them, or what he should have told Dr Rutter, if he had given the questions proper attention. We find that he knew that he should tell Dr Rutter of the 1969 injury and that he should have answered "Yes" to the question about compensation and given details in Part C. In spite of his evidence to the contrary we do not rule out the possibility that, when he completed the form and during the medical examination, he may possibly have decided not to refer to the injuries in 1969 and 1976 as he had suffered no sequelae from either of those injuries after a period of about a month in the first case and a few days in the second. That, however, would not justify his failing to do so; if he knew that the information was being sought, or would have known if he had given the question proper attention, he should have supplied it. We find, therefore, that in connection with his medical examination he failed to furnish information required of him and he furnished false information. Accordingly we affirm the decision under review.

37. It is not necessary for us to consider the question whether spondylosis of the applicant's cervical spine substantially contributed to the incapacity by reason of which he was retired. Mr Whithear conceded that it did not. However, as the Tribunal is required to decide what is the correct or preferable decision on the evidence before it, it cannot accept such a concession unless it is satisfied that it is correctly made. Dr McInnes' estimate of the relative contributions of the two conditions was in terms of percentages; as the Tribunal observed in Re Whiteford and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision No. V86/34: 6 February 1987) assessment in such terms is not satisfactory. It purports to give precision to something which is usually incapable of precise measurement. Having had regard to the reports of Dr Skarbek, Mr Dooley and Mr Khan we accept that the concession made by Mr Whithear may have been correct; however, we have reached no concluded view on the matter.

REASONS FOR DISSENT

Mr C. G. Woodard, Member

1. The majority judgment in this case traverses common

round. The relevant facts in the different view

I have reached are:-

(a) the applicant's literacy and level of education

are not high;

(b) his comprehension of English is limited and he is

more comfortable in it when an interpreter is

used. His comprehension was less in 1969 when

the crucial injury which occurred during his

first period of Commonwealth service was

suffered;

(c) at his medical examination on 12 July 1978 no

interpreter was present, inadequate and

inconsistent answers were allowed to remain on

the medical report Form S6, and contrary to the

assumption of the applicant the Commonwealth

Medical Officer did not have before him the

applicant's full Commonwealth medical history.

2. Notwithstanding all the above, the applicant was given no

opportunity

to explain the circumstances of his medical examination to the

Commissioner for Superannuation before the Commissioner made an

adjudication adverse to him on the new ground (after two and a half

years' consideration of the case) of section 16(11) of the

Superannuation Act.

3. On the information before us, including about the circumstances in

which the applicant had the Form S6 filled in and in which he was

taken

through it by the Commonwealth Medical Officer and signed it, I am

impelled to conclude:-

(a) that, when the applicant answered "No" to

question 12(a) "Have you had any operations,

surgical treatment or accidents?" and answered

"Yes" to the following question 12(b) "Have you

had any complaints, illness or injury not

mentioned above?" and provided details of his

whiplash injury in 1977, his state of

comprehension was such that he did not understand

that he should have mentioned the

accidents/injuries in 1969 (after which he was

cleared for manual labour involving heavy lifting

and then suffered no further pain until 1981) and

in 1976 (minor incident);

(b) that when he answered "No" to question 14(d)

"Have you ever received payments under the

Compensation Act?", which should have been seen

to be inconsistent with his positive answers to

the earlier questions numbers 12(c) and 13(a)

concerning his absence from work and treatment

for whiplash injury in 1977, but inexplicably was

did not understand that he should have answered
