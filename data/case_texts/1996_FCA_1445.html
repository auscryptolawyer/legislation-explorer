Re Australian Company Number 007 764 249 (In Liquidation) Formerly Adelaide Industrial Equipment Pty Ltd Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v Tony Smith [1996] FCA 1445 (6 May 1996)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

IN THE MATTER OF AUSTRALIAN COMPANY NUMBER 007 764 249 (IN

LIQUIDATION)

formerly ADELAIDE INDUSTRIAL EQUIPMENT PTY LTD

DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION v. TONY SMITH

No. SG 3173 of 1995

FED No. 320/96

Number of pages - 10

Corporations

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

SOUTH AUSTRALIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

BRANSON J

CATCHWORDS

Corporations - appeal pursuant to s1321 of the Corporations Law - amount for which proof of debt should be allowed - agreement to compromise original debt - real issue whether the applicant agreed to abandon claim for the original debt in return for a mere promise to pay a lesser sum or whether the agreement was conditional upon actual payment of the lesser sum - whether dates stated in agreement were expressly or by implication an essential term of the agreement having regard to the previous dealings - whether action of company in resolving to appoint an administrator was a repudiation of the agreement.

Corporations Law s 1321, reg 5-6-54

McDermott v Black and Anor [1940] HCA 4; (1940) 63 CLR 161

Scott v English [1947] VicLawRp 67; (1947) VLR 445

Tallerman and Co Pty Ltd v Nathan's Merchandise (Victoria) Pty Ltd (1957) 98
CLR 93

Nissho Iwai (Australia) Ltd v Shrian Oskar (1983) WAR 53

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v Hadidi [1994] FCA 1173; (1994) 123 ALR 48

Alliance Petroleum Australia NL and Others v Australian Gas Light Company
(1985) 39 SASR 84

Tatachilla Winery Pty Ltd v Curtis and Curtis (unreported, Supreme Court of South Australia, Olsson J, 13 October 1995)

Roder Zelt-Und Hallenkonstruktionen GMBH v Rosendown Park Pty Ltd and Another [1995] FCA 1221; (1995) 57 FCR 216

HEARING

ADELAIDE, 5 March 1996

6:5:1996

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr A R Burton

Solicitors for the Applicant: Australian Government Solicitor

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr R D Ross-Smith

Solicitors for the Respondent: Thomsons

DECISION

BRANSON J By an application under s1321 and regulation 5-6-54 of the Corporations Law, the applicant appeals against a decision of the respondent dated 22 August 1995 whereby the respondent allowed the applicant's proof of debt in the liquidation of Australian Company Number 007 764 249 Pty Ltd (in liq) ("the Company") in the amount of $10,253.75. The applicant seeks to have that decision modified so that the proof of debt of the applicant is admitted in the amount of $132,225.04.

BACKGROUND FACTS

2. The following statement of facts has been agreed by the parties:-

"1. AUSTRALIAN COMPANY NUMBER 007 764 249 PTY LTD was incorporated

in the State of South Australia and formerly carried on business

under the company name Adelaide Industrial Equipment Pty Ltd

before changing its name to Australian Company Number 007 764 249

Pty. Ltd. on 17 November 1994 (hereinafter called the "Company").

2. On 5 April 1995 the company's directors resolved that the

Company be placed into voluntary administration pursuant to Part

5.3 of the Corporations Law and on that day the respondent was

appointed as administrator of the Company.

3. On 9 May 1995 the Company's creditors resolved that the Company

be wound up pursuant to the Corporations Law and thereupon the

respondent was appointed liquidator of the Company.

4. At all material times the applicant (herein called "the ATO")

was a creditor of the Company.

5. On the 25th day of October 1994 the Company was indebted to the

ATO in the amount of $300,747.90 ("the Original Debt") being in

respect of unpaid tax instalments, sales tax and additional

tax/amounts for late payments.

6. On the 31st day of October 1994 the Company and the ATO entered

into an agreement to compromise the Original Debt.

PARTICULARS OF AGREEMENT

The Compromise Agreement was made on 31st October 1994 and is

recorded in correspondence passing between the ATO and the

Company's solicitors Messrs Kelly and Co identified below:

6.1 Letter from Kelly and Co to Australian Taxation Office dated

31 October 1994 (Exhibit "ASS1" to Tony Smith affidavit).

6.2 Letter from Australian Taxation Office to Kelly and Co dated

31 October 1994 (Exhibit "DSN11" to Darren Nissen affidavit).

6.3 Letter from Australian Taxation Office to Kelly and Co dated

2 November 1994 (Exhibit "DSN12" to Darren Nissen affidavit).

(hereinafter called "the Compromise Agreement").

7. The total liability of the Company to the ATO under the

Compromise Agreement is in the amount of $236,547.25 ("the

Compromise Debt").

8. On 3 November 1994 the Company's assets and business was sold

and at settlement an amount of $194,000.00 was paid by the

Company to the ATO pursuant to the Compromise Agreement. This

payment was made as part payment of the Compromise Debt in

accordance with the terms of the Compromise Agreement.

9. Payment of the balance of the Compromise Debt

("the Balance") being:

9.1 The amount of $41,000.00 plus;

9.2 The difference, if any, between the sum of $13,500.00 and the

liability of the company to the ATO for October 1994 sales tax

and tax installment deductions ("the Difference") was due to be

paid on 15 December 1994.

10. The amount of the Difference was advised by the Company on

15 February 1995 and was in the amount of $1,547.00.

11. On 31 January 1995 an amount of $41,000.00 was paid by the

Company to the applicant pursuant to the Compromise Agreement

by cheque. The cheque for $41,000.00 was received by the ATO on

6 February 1995.

12. By amended proof of debt dated 12 April 1995 (Exhibit "DSS16"

to Darren Nissen affidavit) the applicant sought to prove in the

liquidation of the Company for the sum of $156,621.31.

13. By letter dated 22 June 1995 the respondent tendered to the

applicant on behalf of the Company the sum of $1,547.25 being the

balance of the taxation debt due by the Company to the applicant

for the month of October 1994.

14. By letter dated 17 July 1995 the applicant by its solicitors

Australian Government Solicitor wrote to the respondent to reject

the liquidator's payment and submitted in the Company's winding

up a proof of debt on behalf of the applicant in the sum of

$166,875.06.

15. By Notice of Rejection of formal proof of debt dated 22 August

1995 the liquidator allowed the applicant's proof of debt in the

sum of $10,253.75 and disallowed the said proof of debt in the sum

of $155,074.05."

ISSUES

3. The principal contention made on behalf of the applicant is that the agreement of 31 October 1994 to compromise the debt owed by the Company as at 25 October 1994 was an accord executory in its terms and therefore the original debt was not discharged until all obligations were performed. The applicant contends that as the obligation to pay the excess of the October 1994 group tax debt ($1,547.25) was not performed as required by the agreement, there was no accord and satisfaction of the debt.

4. Alternatively, it is contended on behalf of the applicant that the dates stated as the due dates for payment of the agreed instalments in the agreement of 31 October 1994 were an essential term of the agreement, either expressly or by implication having regard to the previous dealings between the applicant and the Company, and the failure by the Company to make due payments on those dates gave rise to a right in the applicant to terminate the agreement.

5. In the further alternative it is contended on behalf of the applicant that the action of the Company in resolving on 5 April 1995 to appoint an administrator was a repudiation of the agreement giving rise to a right in the applicant to terminate the agreement.

6. On behalf of the respondent it is principally contended that two separate arrangements were entered into between the applicant and the Company. First, an agreement with respect to known unpaid tax, and secondly, an acceptance by the Company of its liability to pay such additional tax for certain specified months as may be found to be due and payable. In the alternative, it is contended on behalf of the respondent that time was not of the essence under the agreement, and that the Company has paid or tendered the sum due under the agreement at a time when the agreement was still on foot.

THE TERMS OF THE AGREEMENT

7. The agreed statement of facts and issues identifies the agreement between the applicant and the Company as that recorded in three letters. The first is a letter, or more accurately a message designed for facsimile transmission, from Messrs Kelly and Co ("Kelly and Co") to the Australian Taxation Office ("ATO") dated 31 October 1994. The operative part of this letter is in the following terms:-

"We refer to the agreement reached between yourself and the writer

on Friday 28 October 1994 which we set out hereunder.

1. Your office shall accept the total sum of $235,000.00 in full

satisfaction of unpaid tax instalment deductions, sales tax and

penalties which amount shall include any amounts to become

payable in respect of the month of October 1994.

2. The said sum shall be paid as follows:

2.1 $194,000.00 on the date of settlement of the sale of the

business by Adelaide Industrial Equipment Pty. Ltd.;

2.2 as to the sum of $41,000.00 on the 15th day of December 1994.

3.In consideration of the above payments your company will fully

release Adelaide Industrial Equipment Pty. Ltd. and its directors

from any further liability in respect of the said unpaid

instalment deductions, sales tax and penalties.

Please confirm in writing your acceptance and in your confirmation

specify that not only agreement has been reached but that all

threatened action against the company and its directors is

withdrawn. This confirmation needs to be produced at settlement

to the purchasers of the business assets."

8. The second is a letter from the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation to Kelly and Co also dated 31 October 1994. The relevant part of that letter is in the following terms:-

"We refer to your telephone conversation with Darren Nissen of

this office on 28 October 1994, plus your letter dated 31 October

1994 received by this office, by fax today.

In accordance with the above telephone conversation with Mr Darren

Nissen of this office, it was agreed that the Australian Taxation

Office would accept $235,000.00, in full and final satisfaction

of the TID's and ST and LPP debts providing October 1994 TID's

due on 7 October 1994 and October 1994 ST due on 21 October 1994

does not exceed $13,500.00. If October 1994 ST and TID's does

exceed $13,500.00, we will demand to be paid in excess of

$235,000.00 to the extend that October 1994 ST and TID's exceeds

$13,500.00.

It is also the understanding of this office that the company will

cease trading on 31 October 1994 and will therefore not incur any

further taxation liabilities. If further taxation liabilities are

incurred, we will demand payment of those liabilities in addition

(sic) the amounts detailed in the above paragraph.

As advised by Mr Graham Hobbs, it is our understanding that

settlement will occur on 1 November 1994 and on that day, the

Australian Taxation Office will receive at least $194,000.00.

The balance of at least $41,000.00 (depending on the TID's and

ST liability for October 1994) will be paid on or before

15 December 1994. Please advise if this is not correct,

as the details contained in this letter is what the Australian

Taxation Office is agreeing to.

Please contact Mr Darren Nissen of this office on 208 3226 to

advise of the location of settlement on 1 November 1994, so

we can arrange attendance at settlement to collect at least

$194,000.00.

Providing the Australian Taxation Office is paid according to the

contents of this letter, no further action will be taken by this

office in respect to collection of the balance of the unpaid

amounts owed by the company. In addition, if the above amounts

are paid, the penalties incurred by all the company directors

for breaching an agreement made with this office under section

222ALA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 will be

extinguished."

9. The third letter is dated 2 November 1994. It is also a letter from the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation to Kelly and Co. It was apparently intended to correct certain errors in the earlier letter. The only differences between this letter and the second letter appear in the first two paragraphs of the respective letters. The first two paragraphs of the third letter reads as follows:-

"We refer to your telephone conversation with Darren Nissen

of this office on 28 October 1994, plus your letter dated

31 October 1994 received by this office on that day.

In accordance with the above telephone conversation with Mr

Darren Nissen of this office, it was agreed that The

Australian Taxation Office would accept $235,000.00, in

full and final satisfaction of the TID's and ST and LPP

debts providing October 1994 TID's due on 7 November 1994

and October 1994 ST due on 21 November 1994 does not exceed

$13,500.00. If October 1994 ST and TID's does exceed

$13,500.00, we will demand to be paid in excess of

$235,000.00 to the extent that October 1994 ST and TID's

exceed $13,500.00. Could you please advise us of the

amounts of ST and TID's payable for October 1994 as soon

as possible."

ONE AGREEMENT OR TWO?

10. In my view, the exchange of correspondence set out above reveals that there was only one agreement between the applicant and the Company. The letter from Kelly and Co dated 31 October 1994 plainly proposed a single agreement to cover all unpaid tax instalment deductions, sales tax and penalties including any amounts to become payable in respect of the month of October 1994. The response of the applicant of 31 October 1994 made it plain that she did not accept the understanding of the agreement put forward by Kelly and Co. However, the understanding put forward by her, and agreed to have been accepted on behalf of the Company, is, I consider of a single agreement. The agreement so put forward is to accept payment of $235,000 if certain circumstances should prevail and to accept an amount to exceed $235,000 by a readily calculable amount should other circumstances prevail. There is no legal impediment in the way of a person entering into a contract to pay an amount not calculated as at the date of the contract but which is capable of being calculated with precision on a future date. The correspondence provides for a payment schedule under which any payment in excess of $235,000 would not need to be made until a date later than that upon which the precise amount to be paid by the Company under the agreement would become capable of being calculated.

WAS THE AGREEMENT AN ACCORD EXECUTORY?

11. As Dixon J stated in McDermott v Black and Anor [1940] HCA 4; (1940) 63 CLR 161 at 183-184:-

"The essence of accord and satisfaction is the acceptance by the

plaintiff of something in place of his cause of action. What he

takes is a matter depending on his own consent or agreement. It

may be a promise or contract or it may be the act or thing

promised. But, whatever it is, until it is provided and

accepted the cause of action remains alive and unimpaired.

The accord is the agreement or consent to accept the

satisfaction. Until the satisfaction is given the accord

remains executory and cannot bar the claim."

12. The distinction between a mere "accord" and a contract alluded to in the above extract was made plain by Fullagar J in Scott v English [1947] VicLawRp 67; (1947) VLR 445 at 453 in the following passage:-

"The essence of the matter may be said to be that a mere "accord"

is not a contract at all. But, if we find in any particular case

that there is a contract - a promise accepted in "satisfaction"

against a promise - our problem is not necessarily at an end. We

have still, I think, in some cases to construe the contract to

see whether its effect is to discharge the original cause of

action absolutely, so that the plaintiff can never thereafter

sue on it but can only sue on the new contract or whether it

effects only a conditional discharge, merely suspending the

original cause of action, so that, if it is not performed by

the defendant according to its tenor, the plaintiff may still

maintain the original cause of action."

13. See also Tallerman and Co Pty Ltd v Nathan's Merchandise (Victoria) Pty Ltd [1957] HCA 10; (1957) 98 CLR 93 per Dixon CJ and Fullagar J at 94, but see Taylor J at 149; and Nissho Iwai (Australia) Ltd v Shrian Oskar (1983) WAR 53 per Brinsden J at 58-59.

14. In this case it is an agreed fact that the applicant and the Company entered into a compromise agreement on 31 October 1994. The real issue between the parties is not, in my view, whether an accord executory was reached between the applicant and the Company, but rather whether the applicant and the Company agreed that the applicant would abandon the claim for payment of $300,747.90, in respect of unpaid tax instalments, sales tax and additional tax and other amounts for late payment, in return for a mere promise to pay the lesser sum now known to be $236,547.25 or whether the agreement to abandon the claim for payment of $300,747.90 was conditional upon actual payment of the lesser sum.

15. Unlike the case of Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v Hadidi (1994) 123 ALR 48, this is not a case in which judgment had been obtained by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation against the taxpayer. However, it does not appear that the Company at any time disputed its liability to pay to the applicant the sum of $300,747.90. In such circumstances Part 5.4 of the Corporations Law placed the applicant in a strong position so far as the Company was concerned. In Hadidi's Case each of the members of the Court took the view that it was an unlikely intention to impute to the parties that the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation was agreeing to accept in place of judgments totalling about $100,000 a bare promise to pay instalments totalling $75,000 so that upon any default in payment the Deputy Commissioner would have to commence proceedings on a fresh cause of action (see per Beaumont and Heerey JJ at 55, Wilcox J at 62-63). In my view, in this case it is similarly unlikely that the applicant and the Company intended that the applicant was agreeing to accept in place of a cause or causes of action for $300,747.90 a bare promise to pay $236,547.25 so that if any default in the payment of the lesser sum were made the applicant could only seek by legal proceedings to recover the lesser sum. Moreover, in my view, the terms of the correspondence between the applicant and the Company, suggest against any such intention.

16. I find that the agreement between the applicant and the Company was that the applicant agreed to abandon the claim for $300,747.90 in return for actual payment of the amount now calculated as $236,547.25. As in Deputy Commissioner of Taxation v Hadidi I also find a common intention that the Company would receive credit against its liability to the applicant for any amounts actually paid.

HAS THE COMPANY MADE OR TENDERED PAYMENT AS AGREED?

17. The agreement between the applicant and the Company was that the ATO would receive at least $194,000 on 1 November 1994 and the balance on or before 15 December 1994. On 3 November 1994 the applicant received, and accepted, $194,000 from the Company. On 6 February 1995 the applicant received, and accepted, a cheque dated 31 January 1995 for the sum of $41,000. The amount then outstanding was $1,547.00.

18. On 5 April 1995 the Company was placed in voluntary administration pursuant to Part 5.3 of the Corporations Law. On 12 April 1995 the applicant lodged an amended proof of debt with the respondent in the sum of $156,621.31. On 9 May 1995 the Company went into liquidation. The respondent was appointed first the administrator and then the liquidator of the Company. By letter dated 22 June 1995 the respondent tendered to the applicant, on behalf of the Company, the sum of $1,547.25 but by letter dated 17 July 1995 the applicant rejected the tendered payment.

19. It is submitted on behalf of the applicant that the agreement of 31 October 1994 made the time specified for the payments of the essence of the contract. As Cox J pointed out in Alliance Petroleum Australia NL and Others v Australian Gas Light Company (1985) 39 SASR 84 at 105 after an exhaustive review of the authorities:-

"Time will be of the essence wherever the circumstances of the

case indicate that this would fulfil the intention of the

parties. Such an intention will be found "broadly speaking",

in "mercantile" contracts. Sometimes the word "mercantile" is

used to refer to trade and commerce. Sometimes it is used

interchangeably with "commercial". ... However, in all cases

it will be necessary, in my view, to look not merely at the

broad subject matter of the contract ... but also the nature

of the particular power or allegation that is in question."

(see also Tatachilla Winery Pty Ltd v Curtis and Curtis (unreported, Supreme Court of South Australia, Olsson J, 13 October 1995).

20. I am prepared to assume in favour of the applicant, but without so deciding, that the agreement in this case is a "mercantile" contract. Nonetheless, it is necessary to look carefully at the terms of the contract. The correspondence evidencing the agreement between the applicant and the Company indicates that the first payment to be made was intended to come from the proceeds of the sale of the business of the Company. The Company could not alone control the date of settlement of the sale of that business. The agreed facts record that the settlement in fact took place, not on the expected date of 1 November 1994, but on 3 November 1994. It may also be noted that, as at the date of the agreement, it did not appear that any particular disadvantage would be suffered by the applicant if the payments required to be made by the agreement were delayed for a short time.

21. In my view the times specified by the agreement of 31 October 1994 for payments were not of the essence of the agreement.

22. As a consequence, in my view, the agreement of 31 October 1994 was still on foot immediately prior to the Company going into administration on 5 April 1995. Although the applicant, through her officers, had sought to press agents of the Company with respect to the outstanding amount due to be paid by the Company, and had threatened legal action if not paid immediately, no notice of avoidance of the contract had been given. To the contrary, the conduct of officers of the applicant, in my view, was such as to imply that the contract remained on foot although "immediate" payment of the outstanding amount of $1,547.25 was required by the applicant. I interpolate that, in my view, a demand for immediate payment cannot be characterised as the giving of notice requiring payment by a reasonably specified future date.

23. However, by the appointment of an administrator of the Company pursuant to Part 5.3A of the Corporations Law, the directors of the Company acknowledged that the Company was, or was likely to become, insolvent. An effect of the administration was to prevent the applicant from taking steps to wind up the Company or from commencing proceedings against the Company without the written consent of the administrator or the leave of the Court. The administrator did not become liable to make the payment due to the applicant under the agreement of 31 October 1994. Indeed, the administrator's entitlement to be indemnified out of the Company's property for debts for which he was liable and for his remuneration as fixed under s449G of the Corporations Law took priority over the Company's unsecured debts.

24. In my view, the appointment of the respondent as administrator of the Company amounted to a fundamental breach of the agreement of 31 October 1994 giving rise to a right in the applicant to avoid the contract (see Roder Zelt-Und Hallenkonstruktionen GMBH v Rosendown Park Pty Ltd and Another (1995) [1995] FCA 1221; 57 FCR 216 at 234). Although the amount then outstanding for payment was small in comparison with the total amount to be paid under the contract, its apparent significance to the applicant was made clear by the terms of the correspondence which evidenced the agreement. This is not, in my view, a situation in which the de minimis principle, or any similar principle, can be invoked.

25. I further conclude that the applicant gave notice of avoidance of the contract by lodging a proof of debt with the administrator of the Company on 12 April 1995. In the circumstances, the tender by the respondent to the applicant of $1,547.25 by letter dated 22 June 1995, which tender was rejected by the applicant, was without legal significance.

26. The appeal will be allowed and the decision of the respondent of 22 August 1995 modified. Counsel are to bring in minutes of order appropriate to reflect the reasons of the Court. I will hear counsel as to costs.
