Nelson and Commissioner for Superannuation [1979] AATA 201 (20 December 1979)

Last Updated: 12 March 2007

Superannuation - invalidity retirement -application for review - whether "higher duties allowance" to be taken into account in the calculation of salary for superannuation purposes.

Superannuation Act 1976, s5, 168

Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978, s176

Superannuation (Salary) Regulations (1978) Reg.2,4,5,6

Re: BRIAN LESLIE NELSON

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No.18004 of 1978

AAT Decision No 335

Mr KS Edmunds (Senior Member)

Mr VJ Skermer (Member)

Mr RB Hutchison (Member)

20 December 1979

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No.18004 of 1978

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: BRIAN LESLIE NELSON
And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

DECISION

The Tribunal decides to affirm the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation taken on 21 April 1978.

DATED this 20th day of December 1979.

(Sgd) K.S. EDMUNDS

(K.S. Edmunds)

Senior Member

(Sgd) V.J. SKERMER

(V.J. Skermer)

Member

(Sgd) R.B. HUTCHISON

(R.B. Hutchison)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No.18004 of 1978

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: BRIAN LESLIE NELSON

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

The applicant, a clerk class 5 in the Department of Employment and Industrial Relations was formally retired on the grounds of invalidity by the Public Service Inspector, Perth, under a delegation from the Public Service Board, on 3 June 1977 with effect from 31 March 1977. Before the onset of the illness which necessitated his retirement the applicant had been receiving higher duties allowance appropriate to the position of clerk class 6.

This application has been brought to review a decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation made on 21 April 1978 which confirmed an earlier decision made by the Commissioner's delegate that in calculating the salary of the applicant at the time of his retirement the higher duties allowance should not be taken into account. Section 5 (1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 defines salary to include:-.

"5 (1) In this section, "salary" means salary or wages, and includes -

(a) any allowance, or the value of any allowance, or any fee, that is an allowance or fee of a kind that, under the regulations, is to be treated as salary for the purposes of this Act;"

At the time the decisions abovementioned were made no such regulation had been proclaimed. But on 22 December 1978 by S.R. No. 282 of 1978 the Superannuation (Salary) Regulations were proclaimed and those Regulations by the operation of regulation 2 and s168 of the 1976 Act and s76 of the Superannuation Acts Amendment Act 1978 authorised the retrospective operation of the Regulations to 1 July 1976.

On the coming into force of the Superannuation Act 1976 and in anticipation that the regulations to give effect to s5 of the Act would be made, the Commissioner had given delegates appointed by him under the Act an additional authority which was described in a circular accompanying each delegation as follows:-

"3. Approval has also been given for the occupant of the above position or any person for the time being occupying that position to declare, for the purpose of determining salary for superannuation purposes, that an eligible employee has been in receipt of higher duties allowance for a continuous period of not less than 12 months or that an eligible employee is in receipt of higher duties allowance and is likely to continue to receive higher duties allowance for a total continuous period of at least 12 months."

The Commissioner issued detailed instructions to his delegate relating to the carrying out of their functions under the delegation. Paragraph 6 of the instructions read:-

"Where the delegate of the Commissioner makes a declaration in respect of a contributor who has not completed a period of 12 months higher duties allowance to the effect that his higher duties allowance is likely to continue for a total period of not less than 12 months contributions on the higher salary will be payable from the next anniversary day following the date of the declaration. Benefit cover in respect of the higher salary will be effective from the date of the declaration."

The Commissioner in rejecting the claim of the applicant based his decision on the failure of his delegate to comply with the requirements under which the delegate was required to act and also upon the retirement of the applicant by the Public Service Board on 3 June 1977 with retrospective operation from 31 March 1977, a date earlier than the date the delegate had signed his declaration.

This Tribunal is, however, obliged to consider the application in the light of the regulations contained in SR No 282 which were in force when the application was heard by the Tribunal and which were retrospectively in force from 1 July 1976.The relevant regulations read as follows:-

"4. In this Part, a reference to an allowance of a prescribed kind shall be read as a reference to an allowance of one of the following kinds:

(a) an allowance that is payable to an eligible employee by reason that he temporarily performs the whole or a part of the duties of an office of a higher classification than the classification of the office occupied by him;"

"5. Each of the following kinds of allowance is a kind of allowance that is to be treated as salary for the purposes of the Act:

(1) an allowance of a prescribed kind that is payable on a regular basis to an eligible employee."

"6. (1) An allowance of a prescribed kind shall not, for the purposes of paragraph 5(1), be taken to be payable to an eligible employee on a regular basis unless the allowance is payable in respect of duties or work performed by the eligible employee for a continuous period that commences -

(a) immediately after the eligible employee has performed for a continuous period of 12 months duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of the same kind as that allowance has been paid or is payable to the eligible employee; or

(b) where a certificate in respect of the eligible employee has been given under sub-regulation (2) - on the day specified in the certificate.

(2) If a person authorised by the Commissioner for the purposes of this regulation is of the opinion that there is a likelihood that, from and including a particular day -

(a) an eligible employee will, for a continuous period of not less than 12 months, perform duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of a prescribed kind will be payable to him; or

(b) where the eligible employee has, for a continuous period of less than 12 months immediately preceding the particular day, performed duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of a prescribed kind has been paid or is payable to him - the eligible employee will, for a continuous period of less than 12 months that together with the first-mentioned period will not be less than a period of 12 months, perform duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of the same kind as that allowance will be payable to him,the authorised person shall give to the Commissioner a certificate to that effect specifying the day (not being a day earlier than the day on which the certificate is given) on which the period referred to in paragraph (a) or (b), as the case may be, in relation to the eligible employee will commence.

(3) For the purposes of this regulation, where -

(a) for a continuous period immediately preceding a period of leave of absence, an eligible employee performs duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of a prescribed kind is payable to him;

(b) that allowance or an allowance of the same kind as that allowance is not payable to him during the period of leave; and

(c) immediately after the expiration of the period of leave, the eligible employee performs, for a continuous period, duties or work in respect of the performance of which an allowance of the same kind as that allowance is payable to him, the period of leave shall be disregarded and the periods during which he performs those duties or that work, being the period immediately preceding that period of leave and the period immediately following that period of leave, shall be deemed to be a continuous period.

(4) Where an allowance of a prescribed kind is payable to an eligible employee during a period of leave of absence, the eligible employee shall, for the purposes of this regulation, be deemed to perform during the period of leave of absence the duties or work in respect of the performance of which that allowance is payable."

For the purposes of these reasons it can be accepted that delegates of the Commissioner who had been authorised to give a declaration in relation to higher duties allowances were persons authorised by the Commissioner for the purposed of regulation 6. This matter must therefore be resolved by determining whether the applicant was in receipt of an allowance of a prescribed kind within regulation 4, whether an allowance of that kind was by regulation 5 (1) payable on a regular basis within the meaning of regulation 6 and, lastly, whether the requirements of regulation 6 had otherwise been met.

Relevant facts in relation to which there was no conflict between the parties are set out in the following paragraphs.The applicant,

(a) became a contributor to the superannuation fund on 14 June 1971 and prior to 7 October 1976 occupied the office of clerk class 5.

(b) on and from 7 October 1976 received higher duties allowances appropriate to the position of clerk class 6.
(c) on 20 February 1977 was admitted to Sir Charles Gardiner Hospital, Perth suffering from myocardial infarction. The hospital on 29 April 1977 certified that the applicant "will not be fit to renew his normal duties".

(d) on the 18 May 1977, following a request to the CMO by the Department of Employment and Industrial Relations, was examined by the CMO who reported that he "is unfit for continued employment and should be retired on the grounds of invalidity". The CMO subsequently amended the report to add the rider "his retirement would have been justified from 23 March 1977".

(e) on 26 May 1977 gave written agreement "to being retired at the close of business on 31 March 1977" and on the same date completed an application as a contributor for invalidity retirement pension.

(f) received no payment of higher duties allowance after 23 February and did not at any time after 21 February resume active duty.

A document headed "Declaration that Higher Duties Allowance be Regarded as Salary for Superannuation Purposes" was submitted to the Delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation, Mr D A Finlay on 31 May for completion. This document read as follows:-

"Declaration that Higher Duties Allowance be Regarded as Salary for Superannuation Purposes

RECOMMENDATION TO DELEGATE

Approval has been given to the payment of Higher Duties Allowance to NELSON BRIAN LESLIE in respect of his temporary transfer from the position of 3982 (Show Substantive Position) Actual Salary $11764 p.a. to the position of 7571 (Show Acting Position) Actual Salary (including H.D.A.) $13077 p.a. Payment of Higher Duties Allowance commenced on 7/10/76 and is likely to cease on 7/10/77

As the transferee is likely to occupy the position or a position of equal or higher classification, continuously for a period of not less than 12 months, it is recommended that the Higher Duties Allowance be regarded as salary for superannuation purposes.

30/5/.77. ......V..Morey.

(Date) (Recommending Officer)

DECLARATION BY DELEGATE

I declare that the Higher Duties Allowance be regarded as salary for superannuation purposes in accordance with section 5 of the Superannuation Act 1976.

....31/5/77 ..D..A..Finlay..

(Date) (Delegate of Commissioner

for Superannuation) "

At the time of completing the declaration Mr D A Finlay had before him the applicant's personal file which contained, inter alia, the C.M.O.'s report of 18 May abovementioned and other papers relating to the applicant's proposed retirement. A "Notification of Separation Form" completed by Miss V Morey dated 30 May 1977 for despatch to the Public Service Inspector, Perth although separate from the personal file of the applicant was also before Mr Finlay. Item 2 of this document headed "Method of Separation" contained the endorsement "Retirement (s67) invalidity".

A memorandum dated 30 September 1977 sent from the office of the Commissioner for Superannuation to the Assistant Director Management Services, Department of Employment and Industrial Relations, Perth is relevant and reads as follows:-

"Assistant Director, Management Services Department of Employment &Industrial Relations, PO Box R1273, PERTH WA 6001

BRIAN LESLIE NELSON - HIGHER DUTIES ALLOWANCE YOUR MEMORANDUM DATED 9 JULY 1977 REFERS

As the declaration to the effect that Mr Nelson was likely to be in receipt of a higher duties allowance for a period exceeding 12 months was not made prior to his retirement, his pension had been paid on the basis of his substantive salary only.

2. The instructions issued in relation to the acceptance of higher duties allowance as salary for superannuation purposes clearly states that

"Benefit cover in respect of the higher duties will be effective from the date of declaration". Superannuation Board Circular No. 1976/15 paragraph 6 refers.

3. The main purpose of this memorandum is therfore to impress upon you the importance of ensuring, that where it is most probable that a contributor is likely to be in receipt of a higher duties allowance for a period of at least 12 months, that a declaration is made as soon as practicable so that a contributor or dependents may gain the additional benefits applicable in the event of unforseen circumstances causing death or involuntary retirement.

G Findon for R C Davey Commissioner for Superannuation "

The reply of the Assistant Director (Mr D A Finlay) dated 2 November 1977 is also relevant and reads as follows:-

"Commissioner for Superannuation, Australian Government Retirement Benefits

Office, PO Box 22, BELCONNEN. ACT 2616 BRIAN LESLIE NELSON - ACCEPTANCE OF HDA AS SALARY FOR SUPERANNUATION PURPOSES

I refer to your communication 425171 of 30 September 1977 and subsequent discussions with Mr Whyte of your office, concerning the above matter. It is understood that Higher Duties Allowance which had been paid to Mr Nelson prior to retirement can not be accepted in determination of his pension entitlement. However it is thought that a number of points deserve re-consideration.

Firstly, and most importantly, the question of HDA acceptance was raised with your office on two occasions prior to Nelson's retirement, in an effort to determine:the greatest benefit to the employee, with respect to his early retirement or continuing sick leave. On both occasions (the second was to confirm the initial information) the Department was given to understand that a declaration might be made at any time up to the date of the decision being made to retire the employee.

Secondly, it should be stressed that Nelson's retirement was backdated to 31.2.77 by the Public Service Board, in his best interests, since his paid sick leave entitlements had expired at this date. At the date of the declaration for acceptance of HDA (31.5.77) no decision had been made with respect to his retirement, in fact he had only recently attended for examination with the Commonwealth Medical Officer on 18/5/77.

Finally as your delegate in this Department, I must point out that in signing the declaration (form S17), it was my intention that Nelson receive the additional entitlement in question, and that if the declaration is unacceptable on grounds of its "lateness" this has been due to administrative error and should not disadvantage the pensioner.

I hope that you will be able to re-consider this case. Please contact Mr Leed of this Department if you require further information.

D. A. FINLAY Assistant Director Management Services

2 November 1977"

The applicant in support of his claim for the inclusion of the higher duties allowance in his final salary for pension purpose argued that the Commissioner had no power to set aside the declaration of his delegate Mr D A Finlay. Moreover the act of retirement had no legal efficacy until 3 June 1977, the date the Public Service Board by its delegate had in fact retired him and the declaration of the delegate made on 31 May should be given full effect. It was further argued that if the declaration was not accepted the applicant would suffer a substantial disadvantage in comparison with his fellow officers through the negligent administrative actions of his Department. It was pointed out that the applicant was one of approximately 360 officers of his Department in Western Australia performing higher duties indefinitely on higher duties allowances.

It was argued on behalf of the Commissioner that the coming into force of SR No 282 and the retrospective operation to 1 July 1976 of the regulations contained therein obliged the Commissioner to determine the applicant's claim entirely on the provisions of those regulations. The declaration made by Mr Finlay could not on the facts existing at the time it was made now be read as a certificate complying with the requirements of regulation 6 (2) and indeed at 31 May 1977 the delegate could not in the light of the fact as set out above make a certificate or declaration that would be within the requirements of regulation 6(2).

This Tribunal determines that the document headed "Declaration that Higher Duties Allowances be Regarded as Salary for Superannuation Purposes" completed as to the "Recommendation to Delegate" by Miss V. Morey and as to the "Declaration by Delegate" by Mr D.A. Finlay does not meet the requirements a certificate under regulation 6(2) must possess. The delegate signed the abovementioned document on 31 May 1977 but the applicant, irrespective of whether his retirement took effect from 31 March 1977 or 3 June 1977 did not perform or was not deemed to perform any duties for which higher duties allowances were payable after 23 February 1977.

It is clear from the correspondence between the office of the Commissioner and Mr Finlay that when the latter completed his declaration he believed and intended that the document would have the same effect as it would have had had it been signed on 7 October 1976.

The Tribunal holds the view that at the time of signing the document he never directed his mind to the likelihood of the applicant completing from and including 31 May any period on higher duties allowance.

The declaration on the facts as known at the time did not comply with the requirements of a declaration under the instructions then standing. Mr Finlay could not on 31 May 1977 have reasonably formed an opinion that the applicant, not having completed a period of 12 months' higher duties, was likely to continue for a total period of not less than 12 months or indeed for any period in duties on a higher duties allowance. For the same reasons Mr Finlay could not on 31 May 1977 have given a certificate sufficient to satisfy regulation 6(2)(a) or (b) which as we have said are.now deemed to have been in force.

The Tribunal appreciates the concern of the applicant that Departmental omission to comply with instructions as acknowledged by Mr Finlay in the penultimate paragraph of his letter of 2 November 1977 set out above has affected him financially but we must apply the law as it stands.

For the foregoing reasons the Tribunal affirms the decision of the Commissioner.
