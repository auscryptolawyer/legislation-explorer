Lambertus John Hesterman and Commissioner of Superannuation No: A93/19 Aat [1993] AATA 293; (1993) 30 ALD 423 (17 September 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

LAMBERTUS JOHN HESTERMAN v. COMMISSIONER FOR

SUPERANNUATION

No: A93/19

AAT No. 8988

Number of pages - 6

Superannuation

[1993] AATA 293; (1993) 30 ALD 423 (extract)

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

M.D. ALLEN (Senior Member), N.J. ATTWOOD (Member) AND H.G. JULIAN (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - Preservation of contributor's rights. Late election - extension of time. Failure to make further and better enquiries resulted from Applicant's own assumptions. Extension refused.

Superannuation Act 1976 - Ss.137(1), 157(1)

Hunter Valley Developments Pty Ltd and Ors v Cohen [1984] FCA 176; 3 FCR 344

Re Liddle and Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 191; 14 AAR 456

Re Lander and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8553)

Re Millwood and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8941)

Re Chalk and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8954) followed

Re Boardman and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8832) not followed

Re Jeffrey and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8964) distinguished

HEARING

CANBERRA, 2 and 3 September 1993

17:9:1993

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr P. Brazil

Solicitors for the Applicant: MacPhillamy, Cummins and Gibson

Counsel for the Respondent: Ms Tess Munoz (of counsel)

Solicitors for the Respondent: Mr S. Adams

Retirements Benefits Office

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

M.D. ALLEN, N.J. ATTWOOD AND H.G. JULIAN Pursuant to an application lodged with the Tribunal on 24 February 1993 the Applicant sought review of a decision made 6 January 1993 affirming an earlier determination by a Delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation that it was not desirable that a late election by the Applicant to preserve superannuation benefits be recognised.

2. The relevant legislation is Sub-sections 137(1) and 157(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 (as amended). They state:

"137 (1) A person who -

(a) ceases to be an eligible employee in circumstances to which

paragraph (b) does not apply and, upon so ceasing, is not

entitled to pension under this act or invalidity benefit in

accordance with section 69, 72 or 73; or

(b) ceases to be an eligible employee in circumstances by

virtue of which the person is to be deemed, under subsection

58(3), to have retired involuntarily,

may, not later than 21 days after the person so ceases to be an

eligible employee, elect, by notice in writing to the

Commissioner, that this Division apply in relation to the

person."

"157 (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, where

an election under this Act is made by a person after the

expiration of the period allowed by or under this Act for the

making of the election, and the Commissioner is satisfied that

in all the circumstances of the case it is desirable that the

election should be recognised, the Commissioner may direct that

the election be treated as if it had been made within the

period allowed and the election shall have effect accordingly."

3. The interpretation to be placed upon the above sections of the Act has been considered by the Tribunal in several matters. In a recent matter, namely Re Chalk and Commissioner for Superannuation (Tribunal Decision No 8954) the Tribunal presided over by Bulley J expressly adopted and approved the formulation expressed by the Tribunal in Re Liddle and Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 191; 14 AAR 456 and Re Lander and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8553).

4. As pointed out in Re Millwood and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8941) there has been a consistent series of Tribunal decisions in which the principles of Re Liddle supra and Hunter Valley Developments v Cohen [1984] FCA 176; 3 FCR 344, 58 ALR 305, have been applied. The Applicant submitted that this Tribunal should follow the decision in the matter of Re Boardman and Commissioner of Superannuation (Tribunal Decision No 8832). Given the consistent line of authority cumulating in the decision of Bulley J in Re Chalk supra, this Tribunal declines to follow Re Boardman but respectfully adopts the analysis in Re Liddle supra of the principles governing the application of S.157(1).

5. The Applicant originally joined the Commonwealth Public Service on 27 June 1974. On 24 June 1976 he was appointed a Computer Systems Officer Gd II with the Government Printing Office. Shortly after taking up his appointment at the Government Printing Office he transferred from the Superannuation Scheme established by the Superannuation Act 1922 to that created by the 1976 Superannuation Act.

6. The Applicant resigned from the Commonwealth Public Service effective 26 February 1980. Following his letter of resignation dated 6 February 1980 the Applicant received a letter from the Australian Government Publishing Service which set out certain entitlements due and owing to him and which stated inter alia:

"Please find enclosed a form S2A which must be completed and

returned to this office for a refund of Superannuation

contributions.

If you have any queries please phone Mary O'Keefe on extension

218."

7. In evidence the Applicant stated that he read the form S2A and noted that it referred to an election to preserve superannuation rights. He wished to find out more regarding this option so, on Thursday 21 February 1980, he went to the Government Printing Office who referred him to the Australian Government Publishing Service Headquarters. There he was advised that his query would be answered by the Personnel Clerk. As that officer was at lunch the Applicant waited for him to return. When he did see the Personnel Clerk he found him impatient in manner and at pains to explain that he Hesterman was not entitled to a pension. The Personnel Clerk informed the Applicant that the only options which he had were to take his money out of the fund in a lump sum or to leave it in the fund. No other explanation was given.

8. The Applicant was dissatisfied with this explanation and considered making further enquiries from the Retirement Benefits Office. He did not follow through on this as he was busy preparing for his impending move to Sydney and because he assumed that the retirement fund was similar to those he had been in in the past so that the only information he expected to receive was some sort of guarantee of what the future performance of the fund would be like.

9. Notwithstanding his dissatisfaction with the information that had been given to him the Applicant signed the form at home after his visit to the Australian Government Publishing Service Headquarters and elected to withdraw his money from the fund in a lump sum.

10. In cross-examination the Applicant conceded that his resignation from the Commonwealth Public Service was a considered move on his part taken in order that he might gain superior technical experience with another employer. His resignation took effect on 26 February and the date he commenced employment with his new employer in Sydney was also 26 February.

11. Following the receipt of his money the Applicant invested it in order that it might be preserved. The first investment, in gold bullion, was not successful so the funds were realised and transferred into cash management funds.

12. The Applicant rejoined the Public Service on 22 May 1989. Upon his rejoining he did not attend any induction course as by the time such a course was available to him he was too heavily involved in his work to attend. The Applicant conceded, however, that he had access to brochures on superannuation at that time.

13. Notwithstanding the brochures available to him the Applicant remained ignorant of his right to elect to preserve his rights in the Superannuation Fund and of his right to seek to make a late election until he attended lectures regarding the new PSS Superannuation Scheme. Once he became aware of the possibilities of applying for a late election to preserve superannuation rights, he acted.

14. The Applicant has had two employers in the period between 26 February 1980 and rejoining the Public Service on 22 May 1989. With the first of these employers he had no entitlement to superannuation but with the second the funds paid out to him under that company's superannuation scheme were invested in "roll over funds". The Tribunal accepts that the Applicant has, whenever superannuation funds have come into his hands, acted to preserve the capital of those funds so as to eventually provide him benefits for his retirement.

15. As was pointed out in Re Chalk supra at paragraphs 40 and 41:

"40. As Ms Munoz correctly submitted, this Tribunal must be

satisfied that in all the circumstances of the case it is

desirable that the late election should be recognised. It is

for the applicant to satisfy the Tribunal that the discretion

should be exercised in the applicant's favour. In effect the

Tribunal must be satisfied that it is desirable to extend the

time for making the election. The leading authorities on

extensions of time in the context of the administrative law are

discussed in the judgment of Wilcox J in Hunter Valley

Developments Pty Ltd and Ors v Cohen (sic) (1984) 58 ALR 305.

The following principles distilled by his Honour in the cited

authorities are set out in the judgment at pages 310-311 and

can be summarised as follows:

(a) the prescribed period for making an application is not to

be ignored;

(b) the applicant must show an acceptable explanation for the

delay;

(c) the applicant must also show that it is fair and equitable

in the circumstances to grant the extension of time sought;

(d) it is relevant to consider whether the applicant has

'rested on his rights' so as to lead the decision-maker to

believe that the matter was finally concluded;

(e) prejudice to the respondent occasioned by the delay is a

material factor mitigating against an extension of time, but

the absence of prejudice will not determine the matter in the

applicant's favour;

(f) the merits of the substantial application are properly to

be taken into account; and

(g) considerations of fairness as between the applicant and

other persons in the same position are relevant.

41. In the context of an application for late elections under

Section 157 of the Act we must add that this Tribunal must be

satisfied that in all the circumstances of the case it is

desirable that the late election should be recognised. In Re

Liddle and Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 191; (1991) 25 ALD 307

the Tribunal considered the meaning of 'desirable' in

circumstances not all that different in some respects to the

circumstances of this case. In that case the Tribunal took the

view that 'desirable' connotes a 'positive aspiration' which is

'something worthy of achievement' rather than 'merely

advisable' (paragraph 51 of DP Forgie's reasons). We note in

passing that the cited report refers to 'undesirable' but that

word does not appear in the Tribunal's reasons (c.f. [1991] AATA 191; 14 AAR 456

at 470). The majority decided, because of the particular

circumstances in that case, that it was desirable to extend

time by nearly 10 years."

16. In this matter the Applicant is a well educated man having obtained a PhD in mathematics from the Australian National University in 1975. He was sufficiently alert to make further and better enquiries about the options available to him in February 1980 when he received the form S2A. The enquiries which he did make resulted in him being given information that was deficient in that it was not complete. However, at that time he was aware that he could approach the Retirement Benefits Office but made a conscious decision not to do so. That decision was largely bought about by what he assumed was the operation of the superannuation scheme.

17. Whatever the advice given to him at the Australian Government Publishing Service may have been, it was the Applicant who determined not to pursue it further. He was an educated man and knew where further advice could have been obtained. This is not the case of an ill educated or unsophisticated worker in a depot remote from headquarters or diffident in approaching administrative staff. In this regard the matter can be distinguished from a case such as Re Jeffery and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported Tribunal Decision No 8964). Similarly, after rejoining the Public Service he did not make any further enquiries although he admits brochures on superannuation were available to him. He was at that stage of his life content with the decision he had made and obviously any earlier misgivings at the time of his resignation in February 1980 were not so important to him for him to make further enquiries about his rights once he had been reappointed to the Public Service.

18. This case is analogous to Re Chalk supra and what the Tribunal there said at paragraph 47 of its reasons for decision applies with equal force here, namely:

"There is nothing in this case that attracts us to the view

that it is desirable to grant the late election. We must give

weight to the time limit in the Act, that there is no

acceptable explanation for the delay - we do not regard

ignorance of the law per se to be an acceptable explanation and

we are satisfied that the applicant had no real interest in the

matter until later in his life when he realised he had made a

mistake (whether wittingly or unwittingly) with the benefit of

hindsight."
