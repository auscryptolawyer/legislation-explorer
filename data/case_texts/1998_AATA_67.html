Applicant

OFFICIAL TRUSTEE IN

BANKRUPTCY

Respondent

Decision No 12605

DECISION

Tribunal: Deputy President G.L. McDonald

Date: 9 February 1998

Decision: The application by the ASC to join the proceedings is dismissed.

(sgd) G.L. McDonald

Deputy President

BANKRUPTCY - Application to be joined as party - Application by Australian Securities Commission - Application commenced as a result of separate investigation by ASC - Application for joinder made in preference to other enforcement options available to ASC under Corporations Law - Character of proceedings before Tribunal different to other options available to ASC

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ss.27(1), 30(1A), 33(1)(b)(c), 37, 41

Australian Securities Commission Act 1989 s.127(4)

Bankruptcy Act 1975 s.149A(1)(b)

Corporations Law ss.91A, 229, 230, 1330, 1331, 1332, 1335, 1336

Johns v Australian Securities Commission (1993) 116 ALR 567

Re Marine World Victoria Ltd and Minister for Arts, Heritage and Environment

(1986) 10 ALD 262

Re McHatten and Collector of Customs (NSW) (1977) 1 ALD 67

REASONS FOR DECISION

9 February 1998 Deputy President G.L. McDonald

1. This is an application ("the application") by the Australian Securities Commission ("the ASC") to be joined as a party to Administrative Appeals Tribunal ("the AAT") proceedings under the provisions of s.30(1A) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act ("the AAT Act").

2. The ASC application is made against the following background:

(a) Mr Price applied ("the substantive application") to the Tribunal on 19 August 1997 for the review of a decision of the Official Trustee in Bankruptcy ("the trustee"), dated 4 July 1997. In that decision the trustee objected to Mr Price's discharge from bankruptcy alleging a breach of s.149A(1)(b) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 ("the Bankruptcy Act"), i.e. alleging that after the date of bankruptcy (7 July 1994) Mr Price continued to manage a corporation as mentioned in s.91A of the Corporations Law ("CL") without approval of a court pursuant to the provisions of s.229 of that Law. The decision had the effect of extending the expiration of Mr Price's bankruptcy from 7 July 1997 to 7 July 2002;

(b) in relation to the substantive application, in accordance with the usual procedures of the Tribunal, on 8 October 1997 a preliminary conference was held and a second conference was held on 12 November 1997. On 13 November 1997 the Tribunal issued directions that:

" 1. The applicant file and serve proofs of evidence for all witnesses whom the applicant proposes to call at the hearing of the matter and/or all other relevant documents as well as a statement of issues, facts and contentions on or before 4.00 on 5 December 1997;

2. The respondent file and serve proofs of evidence for all witnesses whom the respondent proposes to call at the hearing of the matter and/or all other relevant documents as well as a statement of issues, facts and contentions on or before 4.00 p.m. on 19 December 1997;

3. The matter be listed for hearing in January or February 1998; and

4. There be liberty for either party to apply."

In accordance with the Directions Mr Price has filed a number of affidavits and witness statements. Not any have been filed by the respondent. By notice dated 2 December 1997 Mr Price and the trustee were informed that a hearing of 10 February 1998 had been set;

(c) on 28 January 1998 the ASC filed a Notice of Intervention, issued pursuant to s.1330 of the CL. There was a further telephone directions hearing at which Mr Price represented himself in opposing the Intervention Notice brought by the ASC; Mr Carroll, of counsel, represented the respondent and did not oppose the ASC's intervention; and Mr M. Gething, of counsel, represented the ASC. The Tribunal determined that an application to it for review of a decision under the CL was not a "proceeding" of the type contemplated in s.1330. Since the matter was a telephone directions hearing and the determination was made on the spot, no written decision was prepared. The Tribunal was requested by the ASC in a subsequent directions hearing held on 4 February 1998 to provide reasons for its decision not to accept the Notice of Intervention and these are those reasons. The Tribunal's attention was drawn by counsel for the ASC to the distinction in the CL between "civil proceedings" in s.1331 and "proceedings" in s.1330. It was submitted that the latter provision was, by inference, not constrained purely to court proceedings and that the term extended to any proceedings including those before the AAT. The CL does not define what is meant by the word "proceeding". It is a word of broad import and, for instance, the AAT Act in s.30(1A) refers to appeals to the AAT as being a "proceeding". However the word must be construed in the context in which it is used in the CL. The Tribunal notes reference in ss.1332, 1335 and 1336 of the CL connect "proceedings" to court proceedings. In contrast Part 9.4A of the CL deals with review of decisions made under the CL by the AAT where the review of such decisions are referred to as "applications" and not "proceedings". For that reason, the Tribunal is satisfied that the purported Notice of Intervention has no affect in relation to AAT appeals. In refusing to accept the Notice of Intervention the Tribunal drew to the ASC's attention the provisions of s.30(1A) of the AAT Act and notice of such an application was foreshadowed.

3. On 3 February 1998 the ASC brought an application under s.30(1A) of the AAT Act. The application was supported by an affidavit from Mr M. Gething, who also was the officer with the operational control of an investigation occurring between 23 July 1997 and 23 January 1998 into suspected breaches of the CL in relation to the management of Oscom Communications Incorporated ("OCI") and Object Design Australia Ltd ("ODA"). In paragraph 4 of his affidavit Mr Gething states that Mr Price was involved in the management of both of those companies during the period in which Mr Price was an undischarged bankrupt. Mr Gething then continues in paragraph 5 of his affidavit as follows:

"5. In the light of the conclusion in paragraph 4, the ASC had three enforcement options:

(a) request the Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions to consider commencing criminal proceedings for the contraventions of CL s229;

(b) itself commence civil proceedings against Mr Price pursuant to CL s230 seeking an order that he be prohibited from taking part in the management of a corporation for a specified period of time; or

(c) intervene in the present application."

The affidavit states that civil proceedings were rejected as an option on the basis that it would be fairer to Mr Price for the ASC to have all aspects of the allegations determined in the one hearing. It was for this reason that initially the Notice of Intervention was filed and the subsequent application to be joined as a party to the substantive application resulted.

4. In paragraph 12 of his affidavit Mr Gething asserts that the ASC is charged with the administration of the CL and that "The interests of justice and the efficient administration of the CL suggest that the issue of whether Mr Price took part in the management of ODA and OCI only be litigated once, and then on the basis of all relevant evidence being before the tribunal of fact".

5. Mr Price opposes the joinder application, pointing out that the ASC have had notice of these proceedings for some months and to allow them to join at this stage would, particularly since he is unrepresented and given the voluminous material filed and the proximity of the hearing date, be unfair. He points out that he has complied with the Tribunal's Directions of 13 November 1997 whereas the trustee has not. The trustee has filed no material in response to the Direction. Mr Price also opposed an adjournment of the hearing, asserting that there was collusion between the ASC and the trustee in relation to the extension of his bankruptcy. Mr Price denies that he has participated in the management of any Australian company during his bankruptcy. He claims that, if the ASC has allegations to make, those allegations should be made in circumstances after the AAT has heard his application when he would have a fairer opportunity to defend himself as (if he is successful in his application) he would not then be bankrupt.

6. At the directions hearing of 4 February 1998, convened to take submissions with respect to the ASC's application for joinder, Mr Price represented himself, Mr Carroll represented the trustee and Mr Hiland represented the ASC. Mr Hiland submitted that information given to the ASC in connection with the exercise of its powers, including investigative powers, was given in confidence. He further submitted that following the decision of the High Court in Johns v Australian Securities Commission (1993) 116 ALR 567 such information could not be disclosed to the trustee in the circumstances provided for in s.127(4) of the ASC Act 1989 without the discloser of the information first being given the opportunity to be heard as to its proposed disclosure. He submitted that this "problem" would be overcome by the ASC being permitted to join the proceedings. Attached to Mr Gething's affidavit were two leaver arch files of documents not previously disclosed to Mr Price. It is this material the ASC was not, because of the decision in John's case, able to disclose to the trustee prior to the joinder application.

DETERMINATION

7. For the Tribunal in determining joinder the question is whether the ASC is "affected" by the decision in relation to which review is being sought. In Re McHattan and Collector of Customs (NSW) (1977) 1 ALD 67 at 70, the Tribunal (presided over by Brennan J) said:

" However, a decision which affects interests of one person directly may affect the interest of others indirectly. Across the pool of sundry interest, the ripples of affection may widely extend. The problem which is inherent in the language of the statute is the determination of the point beyond which the affection of interest by decision should be regarded as too remote for the purposes of s.27(1). The character of the decision is relevant, for if the interest relied on are of such a kind that a decision of a given character could not affect them directly, there must be some evidence to show that the interests are in truth affected."

It is clear that the interests affected need not be legal interests. While it may be said that the ASC have an interest in ensuring Mr Price does not manage a corporation for a period of time, it could not be said that the ASC have an interest in him remaining a bankrupt. As was pointed out in Re Marine World Victoria Ltd and Minister for Arts, Heritage and Environment (1986) 10 ALD 262 at 271, the discretion available under s.30(1A) of the AAT Act must be ". . . exercised rationally with regard paid to all the circumstances of the proceedings and the nature of the interest of each of the persons applying to be made a party".

8. The Tribunal must bear in mind that in this proceeding it is deciding whether or not the trustee reached the correct or preferred decision to object to Mr Price's discharge from bankruptcy. That is a decision to be reached in the context of the Bankruptcy Act. Bankruptcy carries with it onerous consequences for the bankrupt. Those consequences from the point of view of the bankrupt are broader than the consequences which may flow, for instance, from the ASC obtaining an order under the provisions of s.230 of the CL (i.e. a court order that a person not manage a corporation). Where there is specific power under the CL and where there is a specific remedy available if a breach has been found to occur, then it is not, in the Tribunal's view, appropriate for the ASC to use the fact of a proceeding before the AAT under unrelated legislation in which there are broader adverse consequences resulting to an AAT applicant if there is a finding against that applicant, as the forum in which it seeks to achieve a result which it could pursue in another forum. In particular the Tribunal cannot be used as a substitute for the enforcement of criminal proceedings. It is neither proper nor fair that the applicant's appeal for a merits review of a decision of the trustee in bankruptcy should be used by the ASC to achieve a result which it could achieve by commencing its own action - in either civil or criminal proceedings - and which could be determined in a court where the rules of evidence apply. The Tribunal is not bound by the rules of evidence (s.33(1)(c) of the AAT Act) and the applicant may, consequently, in proceedings before the Tribunal be deprived of testing the evidence according to those rules. It would not be so precluded if the matter was to be determined in a court. In this case the nature of the proceedings before the Tribunal, the interests of the applicant and the responsibilities of the ASC do not, in the view of the Tribunal, extend to allowing the Tribunal to exercise its discretion for the ASC to be joined as a party.

9. The application for joinder is, accordingly, refused.

10. During the course of the hearing I said that when I gave this decision, in view of the fact that the matter was listed for hearing on 10 February 1998, I would give directions about whether the matter should proceed on that day. While Mr Price forcibly points out that until the filing of Mr Gething's affidavit no information (apart from that contained in the documents filed for the purposes of s.37 of the AAT Act) had been provided by the respondent, i.e. the direction to file witness statements, etc, had not been complied with. No subpoenas have, apparently, been applied for by the respondent to call the witness whom Mr Carroll claims are the same witnesses as those who have provided the evidence contained in the two leaver arch folders, filed as exhibits to Mr Gething's affidavit. Mr Price submits that his application has been lodged with the Tribunal since 20 August 1997; that, like the ASC, the trustee is a "big boy" and ought be prepared for the hearing and, consequently, no adjournment should be given. The Tribunal notes the provisions of s.33(1)(b) of the AAT Act that a proceeding in the Tribunal is to be conducted ". . . with as much expedition, as the requirements of the Act and every other relevant enactment and a proper consideration of the matters before the Tribunal permit". It is particularly desirable in cases such as this where an applicant remains an undischarged bankrupt until the Tribunal has determined his or her case (in the absence of a stay order under the provisions of s.41 of the AAT Act) that the application be heard as soon as practicable. However, the desire to proceed expeditiously must be balanced against the requirement that a proper consideration be given to the matters before the Tribunal. The Tribunal accepts Mr Gething's unchallenged assertion that the investigation carried out by the ASC was not finalised until 23 January 1998. The Tribunal also accepts that the trustee now has before it the information which until the filing of the joinder application was not available to it because of the secrecy provisions of the ASC law and that that information is relevant to the disposition of the proceedings before the Tribunal. In the view of the Tribunal the correct or preferred decision can only be reached by a proper consideration of the evidence gathered by the ASC. That evidence is now available to be called by the trustee. The trustee should be given that opportunity between the giving of this decision (9 February 1998) and a hearing due to commence the next day will not provide sufficient time for the trustee to be properly prepared. Nor, as Mr Price points out, would it be fair to him if the matter was to proceed so soon after the filing of such voluminous amount of material. Further, it is plain from the material, and from the number of witnesses that Mr Price proposes to call, that the earlier estimation of one day for the hearing will be insufficient. It is undesirable, particularly for an unrepresented applicant, that a hearing should be split. The matter should be listed for hearing over a three day period, at the Tribunal's earliest opportunity. The next available dates to the Tribunal are 11, 12 and 13 May 1998. The matter listed for hearing on 10 February 1998 is adjourned to 11, 12 and 13 May 1998. If those dates are not convenient to either of the parties, they have 24 hours in which to notify the District Registrar of the Tribunal setting out other available dates to them in May 1998 so that the matter may be relisted at that time. To ensure fairness between the parties, consequential directions for the hearing of this matter are issued, as attached. The parties have leave to apply for a variation to those directions if it is necessary.
