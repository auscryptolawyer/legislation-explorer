Re Heather Lynette Miller and Commissioner of Superannuation [1985] AATA 23 (4 February 1985)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: HEATHER LYNETTE MILLER

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. N84/122

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.C. Jennings Q.C. (Deputy President)

Dr. A.H. Marsh (Member)

Mrs. J.H. McClintock (Member)

CATCHWORDS

HEARING

CANBERRA

4:2:1985

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the decision under review and in substitution therefore decides that the benefit entitlement of the applicant upon her retirement on the ground of invalidity as from 27 August 1980 is to be determined in accordance with sub-section 66(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976.

DECISION

The applicant, Mrs. Heather Lynette Miller who is 37 was employed by Telecom or its predecessors as a switchboard operator at Wauchope from 1963 until August 1980. On 6 September, 1977, she became an eligible employee for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("The Act") by virtue of the Commissioner's direction under s.11 (1) of the Act. Her retirement in 1980 was on the ground of invalidity. This application is concerned with a decision of a delegate of the respondent (confirming a prior decision) that the incapacity which was the ground for her retirement was caused or substantially contributed to by the condition "Hormonal Mastopathy presenting as Breast Lumps" or by a physical condition connected with that condition.

The Tribunal had before it in addition to the statement and supporting material required by s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, the oral evidence of the applicant, Dr. de Meyrick who was medical adviser to the respondent at the time of the applicant's retirement and Dr. Chong, the applicant's surgeon since December 1977.

The applicant's full medical history can be gleaned from the T Documents and her evidence, which we accept.

In December 1973, a Dr. Morris removed a lump in the applicant's right breast which was reported as "benign mammary dysplasia". Subsequently, after a period without trouble, she became aware of lumps and soreness or pain from time to time in both breasts as a result of which she consulted six doctors in Wauchope, Port Macquarie and Armidale at various times between 1975 and 1977.

During 1975, Dr. Jones of Port Macquarie noted that she gave a history of soreness and lumpiness for about 18 months but following a laparotomy which he performed in September for a bowel obstruction and adhesions, he considered her breast condition to be tolerable in December of that year despite some soreness and lumps. He noted that there were no discrete masses.

Dr. Simpson of Port Macquarie referred the applicant to Dr. Tollis in February 1977. She says that she had pain in both breasts at that time and that Dr. Tollis "wanted to take a section out from underneath the arm". This did not appeal to her so she sought a reference to Dr. Maguire of Armidale. Possibly due to the hormonal tablets prescribed by Dr. Tollis the pains ceased for some time (she could not say precisely when or for how long), but Dr. Maguire sent her to the Royal North Shore Hospital for further investigation and assessment.

Dr. Maguire told the applicant that on the hospital results she was "all clear".

It was not until November 1977 that she again saw Dr. Maguire who this time referred her to Dr. Chong of Sydney because he thought that her condition "might be severe mammary dysplasia short of malignancy". Unfortunately, Dr. Maguire's records cannot be located.

Dr. Chong saw her on 7 December 1977 and advised a subfascial mastectomy of both breasts which he performed in February 1978. A week later he carried out reconstruction using a silicone prosthesis. His report of 31 October 1980, continues:-

"Since that operation she has multiple complications in the form of fibrous reaction and eventually the prosthesis was replaced with a double lumen one. This complication is in certain ways, aggravated by excessive usage of arms. Apparently Mrs. Miller's job necessitates this, with the telephone exchange. I therefore advised that if she continue (sic) working, the possibility of fibrous reaction may recur due to further injury to the capsules around the prosthesis".

The applicant said that she returned to work about six weeks or more after the second operation. At first she did not go back to the switch board, but in a matter of weeks after she did and began the extensive stretching over long hours necessitated by that work, she was obliged to go back to Dr. Khamis who referred her to Dr. Chong. She had six or seven operations between 1978 and 1980.

The "extensive stretching" to which we refer was occasioned by the fact that at the time the applicant was working at Wauchope, the relatively old and very large switchboard frequently required the operator to reach her arms both upwards and sideways to full length. Those constant movements were clearly a most significant factor in causing the subsequent operations. Photographs of the switchboard were tendered in evidence.

Now the applicant was medically examined by Dr. Drummond on 29 September 1977 by reason of a requirement of the respondent pursuant to s.16 (2) of the Act. Despite the fact that the form required the applicant to give affirmative or negative answers to 17 questions, none of them was adequate to require or produce an answer which revealed the trouble which she had experienced earlier that year with her breasts. However, in completing details of her affirmative answer to the question:

"Have you had any operations (or) surgical treatment . . . ?'

Dr. Drummond wrote:

"1973 Hysterectomy . . . 1975 Removal breast lump - hormonal mastopathy".

Someone subsequently corrected the latter entry by noting:

"Should be 1973".

By reason of the fact that the report of 29 September 1977 was never considered by any delegate of the respondent prior to her retirement, it became necessary after that event for the respondent to address his mind to the provisions of s.16 (10) of The Act which provides as follows (so far as is relevant for present purposes):

"(10) Where -

(a) A person who is an eligible employee . . . ceases to be an

eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity -

(i) before he has undergone a medical examination under sub-section (2);

(ii) if he has undergone such an examination or examinations - before the report or reports of the result or results of the examination or examinations has or have been considered by the Commissioner;

. . . and

(b) the Commissioner is not satisfied that . . . the incapacity which

was the ground for his retirement, was not caused, and was not substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions of the person that existed at the time the person became an eligible employee, or by a physical or mental condition or conditions connected with such a condition or such conditions, the Commissioner shall issue in respect of the person a benefit classification certificate in which there is or are specified the physical or mental condition or conditions of the person which, in the opinion of the Commissioner, existed at the time the person became an eligible employee and, for the purposes of this Act, the certificate shall be deemed to have been in force in respect of the person immediately before his . . . retirement."

On 30 September 1980, a delegate of the respondent issued a certificate (BBC) describing the condition which existed at the time she became an eligible employee as "Removal of Breast Lumps". That decision was varied on further consideration pursuant to s.154 (4) of the Act to read "Hormonal mastopathy presenting as breast lumps". Various other descriptions of her condition at that time were suggested during the hearing such as "advanced mammary dysplasia" but we see no reason to substitute any other description of that condition for the purposes of considering the real question which we have to decide.

That question is - whether the incapacity which was the ground of retirement was caused or substantially contributed to by the condition that existed on 6 September 1977 (i.e. the condition described in the BCC as varied) or by a condition connected therewith.

If the Commissioner is not satisfied that incapacity was not caused (etc) by a particular condition it is his obligation to issue a BCC specifying that condition. It then follows that pursuant to s.66 (2) that the Commissioner being of the opinion that the incapacity was caused (etc) by that condition the persons entitlement on retirement is determined according to the other provisions of s.66.

What then was the incapacity which was the ground of retirement? Following medical examinations in January and April 1980, an officer of the General Personnel Services Branch certified on 23 June 1980, that the applicant could not be placed on restricted duties and gave approval for her retirement due to the following medical conditions:-

"Bilateral simple mastectomy with prosthetic replacement and adhesions 100%".

The decision of the delegate of 30 September 1980 under s.66 (2) of the Act, was expressed to have had regard to the conditions as so described, but it was based on the BCC as first issued in which "Removal of breast lumps" was described as the condition.

Having regard to the decision of this Tribunal in:

Burr and Commissioner for Superannuation (30 September 1980)

we regard the incapacity which gave rise to retirement, not so much as

described in the certificate of 23 June 1980 or the decisions of 30 September 1980, but as the pain due to adhesions which occurred as a result of stretching and lifting of the arms. Then was that incapacitating pain caused or contributed to by the physical condition described in the BCC as amended or by a condition connected therewith?

It is apparent, because both breasts have been removed, that the incapacitating pain was neither caused nor substantially contributed to by breast lumps. However, a more difficult question arises when considering whether, as previous decisions of this Tribunal have interpreted the legislation now in question, there is "a real and demonstrable connection on a sustainable medical basis" between the incapacity and the physical condition connected with "hormonal mastopathy presenting as breast lumps".

The physical condition said to be so connected is the adhesions which resulted from the mastectomy which became necessary because of breast lumps.

Dr. Chong expressed the opinion, which was not challenged, that although adhesions may well follow the type of operation which the applicant had for removal of her breasts (e.g. in about 50% of such cases) it is most unusual for a patient to be unable to work as a consequence of such adhesions. Indeed, the applicant was the only such case he could recall in his experience. He explained that the reaction to surgery is described as "capsular contractures", the exact cause of which is not known. Excessive reaction results in fibrous tissue squeezing on the prosthesis which then hardens.

Now there is no doubt from a causal point of view the applicant could not have suffered adhesions of any degree of severity unless she had undergone mastectomy for breast lumps. There is therefore an apparent connection between the condition described in the BCC and the physical condition which resulted in her incapacity for work.

But the difficulty facing the respondent, is the evidence of Dr. Chong as to the wholly unexpected consequence of the operation. Counsel for the applicant argued that although there exists a temporal connection and an incidental medical one, there is no substantial medical connection, nor is there a real and demonstrable one.

The operation which the applicant underwent was both elective and preventitive. It was not essential surgery in the sense that cancer was not diagnosed.

The consequence of the surgery in February 1978 was quite beyond the expectation of the surgeon and could not have reasonably been foreseen. That leads, in our view, to the conclusion that the incapacity which was the ground for retirement was not caused or substantially contributed to by a physical condition connected with the condition referred to in the BCC.

Counsel for the respondent referred to a decision of this Tribunal in Dawes and The Commissioner for Superannuation (11 May 1984) and argued that if paragraph 41 of that decision is its ratio then it was a decision which must be confined to its own particular facts. That paragraph reads as follows:-

"41. Section 81 (2) for present purposes is concerned only with physical conditions which cause or substantially contribute to the cause of death or which have a connection with the physical condition referred to in the Certificate. It is not, therefore, concerned with events which are no more that temporal incidents leading to circumstances which in turn create the physical conditions which cause or substantially contribute to the cause of death. The decision to consult the surgeon, to consider and then accept his advice, to enter hospital and to submit to surgery, are not events which constitute connecting links in a chain of physical conditions."

Counsel further submitted that in the present case there would have been no operation, but for the substantial mammary dysplasia and that it was a condition arising from the operation which caused the retirement.

Counsel for the applicant in reply said that if that were correct Dawes was wrongly decided because Mr. Dawes died as a result of an operation which would never have occurred but for his obesity.

We accept that latter submission although we do not base our decision on Dawes which indeed depended on its own facts as does the present application. We accept the submission that there is no substantial medical connection between the condition which gave rise to retirement and the condition existing in September 1977. There was an incidental connection only and a temporal connection but not the real and substantial connection contemplated by the Act as interpreted in prior decisions of this Tribunal such as:

Vaughan (27 April 1979) Burr (30 September 1980) Brown (29 May 1981) Hegedues (16 April 1982) Dawes (Supra)

The decision under review will be set aside and in substitution therefore the Tribunal decides that the benefit entitlement of the applicant upon her retirement on the ground of invalidity as from 27 August 1980 is to be determined in accordance with sub-section 66(1) of the Act.
