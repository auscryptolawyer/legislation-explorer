ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - carry forward of prior year losses - company -change of business carried on - whether provisions of s80A operable

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, S80A

Acts Interpretation Act 1901, SS15AA, 15AB

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No AT87/92

AAT Decision No 3950

Deputy President RK Todd

27 November 1987

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. AT87/92

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Applicant

Tribunal : Deputy President RK Todd

Date : 27 November 1987

Place : Canberra

Decision : The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

R K TODD

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No AT87/92

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

27 November 1987 Deputy President RK Todd

  1. This reference relates to the carrying forward of prior year losses by a company which changed the business that it carried on and which also was subject to a change of ownership.
  2. The company, which is the applicant in these proceedings, changed its business from that of promoting and selling overseas hunting safaris to that of office machinery wholesaler on 1 April 1985. This change, not surprisingly, also involved a change of name, although that is of no relevance to the case.
  3. Initially the company had issued and paid-up capital of $2, consisting of two one dollar shares. One share each was owned by Mr M and Mr D who were also the directors of the company.
  4. In the year with which I am concerned, the year of income ended 30 June 1985 ("fiscal 1985"), the issued and paid-up capital of the company was increased to 100 shares of one dollar each. Mr M and Mr D both disposed of their shares during this year, and Mr D also ceased to have any direct or indirect shareholding in the company. After the additional issue of shares, the shareholding of the company was held as follows:

P Pty Ltd 25 shares

C Pty Ltd 60 shares

A Family Trust 10 shares

Ms M 5 shares.

Mr M owned 99 per cent of the issued capital in C Pty Ltd.

  1. Losses had been incurred by the applicant company to the extent of $9,116 in fiscal 1982, $126 in fiscal 1983, and $487 in fiscal 1983. Both parties agreed that the applicant was not carrying on the same business at all times during the year of income.
  2. It was submitted by the Commissioner's representative that s80A of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITAA") operated to prevent the applicant carrying forward losses for taxation purposes into fiscal 1985.
  3. Section 80A, as far as is relevant, states:

"80A. (1) ..., a loss incurred by a company in a year before the year of income shall not be taken into account for the purposes of section 80, 80AAA or 80AA unless -

(a) the company satisfies the Commissioner; or

(b) ...

that, at all times during the year of income, shares in the company carrying between them -

(c) the right to exercise more than one-half of the voting power in the company;

(d) the right to receive more than one-half of any dividends that may be paid by the company; and

(e) the right to receive more than one-half of any distribution of capital of the company,

were beneficially owned by persons who, at all times during the year in which the loss was incurred, beneficially owned shares in the company carrying between them rights of those kinds.

(2) Where -

(a) sub-section (1) would, but for this sub-section, apply for the purpose of determining whether a loss incurred by a company in a year before the year of income is to be taken into account for the purposes of section 80, 80AAA or 80AA;

(b) during the whole or any part of the year in which the loss was incurred, or during the whole or any part of the year of income, another company or other companies beneficially owned all or any of the shares in the first-mentioned company or an interest or interests in all or any of those shares; and

(c) the first-mentioned company requests the Commissioner at the time when it furnishes to him a return (or, if more than one return is furnished, the first return) of its income of the year of income, or within such further period as the Commissioner allows, that sub-section (3) should apply for the purpose referred to in paragraph (a) or the Commissioner considers it reasonable that that sub-section should apply for that purpose,

then sub-section (3) applies for that purpose in lieu of sub-section (1).

(3) Where, by virtue of sub-section (2), this sub-section applies for the purpose of determining whether a loss incurred by a company (in this sub-section referred to as the "loss company") in a year before the year of income is to be taken into account for the purposes of section 80, 80AAA or 80AA, then, notwithstanding those sections but subject to sub-section (5) and sections 80B, 80DA and 80E, that loss shall not be taken into account for the purposes of section 80, 80AAA or 80AA unless the Commissioner is satisfied, or considers that it is reasonable to assume, that -

(a) at all times during the year of income the voting power in the loss company was, either directly or through one or more interposed companies, trustees or partnerships, controlled, or capable of being controlled, by a person not being a company, or by 2 or more persons not being companies, who, either directly or through one or more interposed companies, trustees or partnerships, controlled, or was or were capable of controlling, the voting power in the loss company at all times -during the year in which the loss was incurred."

  1. It must be noted here that at no stage prior to the hearing had the applicant made a request in the terms of s80A(2)(c) in respect of Mr M's "beneficial" ownership of 60 shares through his control of C Pty Ltd. At the hearing however Mr Hawketts, for the Commissioner, consented to the request being made at that time.
  2. The question that is before the Tribunal is therefore, taking into consideration the effective control that Mr M has over the 60 shares held by C Pty Ltd, whether the provisions of s80A operate to deny to the applicant the ability to carry forward the losses incurred in prior years.
  3. It was submitted by the applicant's representative that, as Mr M owned half of the shares initially and then controlled 60 per cent of the shares, the provisions of the section did not operate. To support this he argued that the Tribunal should not take a literal interpretation of the term "more than one-half" but should look at the control of the applicant company and consequently accept that the 50 per cent shareholding held by Mr M prior to the rearrangements was sufficient to avoid the operation of this section.
  4. I find it impossible here to apply a purposive test so as effectively to find that the words "more than half" mean "half or more". While the winds of change have in recent years blown over the rules of statutory interpretation, not least by the insertion of S.15AA and S.15AB of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901, the scope for that kind of purposive interpretation is in my opinion probably at its lowest in the ITAA. The ITAA is a highly technical piece of legislation which, particularly in relation to the complex and technical amendments of later years contains provisions chosen seemingly as carefully as the human mind is able. Generally on this topic I refer to what was said in Case U114 at paras 7 to 10.
  5. Mr M only controlled one half of the shares prior to the arrangement and, as Mr D had no shareholding after the rearrangement, the applicant can only show a continuing shareholder who controlled one half of the shares. The consequence of this is that the applicant is unable to show a person or persons who held more than half of the shares in the company at all times during the year of income.
  6. As this was a two man company, the only way in which losses could have been carried forward, considering the change that occurred in the business carried on, would have been for Mr M and Mr D to have both continued to hold shares and to have, between them, held more than half of the shares after the restructuring.
  7. For the above reasons the Commissioner's decision on objection is affirmed.
