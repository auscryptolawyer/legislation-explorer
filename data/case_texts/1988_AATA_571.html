ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - assessable income - profits on sale of land near outskirts of town acquired by estate agent - purpose of acquisition - onus of proof.

Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 ss. 26(a), 190(b).

Pascoe v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation 11 ATD 108.

Applicant COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

No. NT87/1899-1900

AAT Decision No 4486

Mr. J. R. Gibson, Senior Member

Sydney

11 July 1988

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT87/1899-1900

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Mr. J.R. Gibson (Senior Member)

Date : 11 July, 1988

Place : Sydney

Decision : The Tribunal affirms the objection decisions under review.

(sgd.)J.R. Gibson

Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT87/1899-1900

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

11 July, 1988 Mr. J.R. Gibson (Senior Member)

1. The applicant seeks review of decisions of the respondent disallowing his objections to amended assessments to income tax for the years of income ended 30 June 1973 and 30 June 1974, issued on 6 May 1980, adding amounts totalling $191,341 and $71,758 respectively to the amounts of income previously assessed. The amounts referred to comprised amounts of $103,889 and $87,452 in the former year, and $39,997 and $31,761 in the latter year, described in the adjustment sheets which accompanied the assessments as profits on the sale of specified lands of the applicant, in the former year to a family company (which will be called Realty), and in the latter year to the Housing Commission. After the lodgment of the objections to the amended assessments, the assessments were further amended so as to reduce the total profit figures to $171,362 for the year ended 30 June 1973 and $68,861 for the year ended 30 June 1974. At the hearing there was no dispute as to the calculation of the amount of the profits.

  1. The basis of the applicant's objections, maintained at the hearing, was that the lands referred to in the amended assessments had not been acquired for the purpose of profit making by sale nor in carrying on or carrying out a profit making undertaking or scheme within the meaning of s. 26(a) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936; and that the profits were not made in the course of any business carried on by the applicant and so were not assessable under s.25(1) of the Act.
  2. The evidence at the hearing before the Tribunal consisted of the oral evidence of the applicant and the documents tendered. Those documents included the formal documents forwarded to the Tribunal by the Commissioner, photographs, copies of maps, plans, title documents and applications to the local Council relating to lands which had been acquired by the applicant and the area in which they are situated, summaries of land transactions, copies of income tax returns and certificates of title.
  3. On the basis of evidence which is not in dispute, it is possible to construct an outline of events and transactions which are, or may be, relevant. The applicant was born in 1920. He married in 1943 and there are five children of the marriage, whose ages range from 34 years to 45 years. He served in the Army in the second World War and, some years after the War, commenced business as a builder in a large country town. He extended that business to buying, building on or repairing properties and reselling them. Having qualified as a valuer, he then set up in business as an estate agent and valuer, acting on sales and making valuations of both town and rural properties. He became and was for many years a member of the local Council, of the local government association and many community organisations.
  4. In about 1966 the applicant bought some land (which will be called Orange) not very far from the outskirts of the town. In January 1968 he lodged an application for approval of subdivision of that land and the approval was granted that month. After subdivision the land was sold to the Housing Commission in three parcels, the first sale being in March 1969 and the last in December 1971.
  5. In May 1969 the applicant contracted to purchase a property of an area exceeding 200 acres (which will be called Green) part of which adjoined the property Orange and which was on the side of Orange further from the town. The purchase price was $51,000 and 90% of the price was provided by way of a mortgage to the vendor. In the same year the applicant purchased for $40,000 a property of about 140 acres (which will be called Pink) and two other properties with a total area of about 40 acres (which will be called Yellow), all of which adjoined the property Green on the side nearer the town. A substantial part of the purchase price of Pink was provided by way of a mortgage to the Vendor.
  6. On 9 June 1969, apparently in anticipation of the purchase of Yellow, application was made to the Council for approval of a development on part of Yellow for 19 residential lots. The application was approved on 23 July 1969. The first lot was sold by the applicant on 4 December 1969 and the remaining lots were sold over a period of about two years.

8. In 1969 or 1970 the applicant sold 20 acres from the property Green to a man (M) or to him and his wife, who, according to the applicant, wanted to run horses on it. This sale divided Green from Yellow at that point, but the land sold to M was not fenced off. In November 1971, on behalf of M and himself, the applicant offered to sell to the Housing Commission an area of 80 acres, consisting of 60 acres from Green and the 20 acres which had been sold to M, for $1,750 per acre.

9. The applicant's return of income for the year ended 30 June 1970 was lodged in March 1971. The usual certificate on the return by his tax agent was dated 26 February 1971 and the usual declaration as to the correctness of the return was signed by the applicant and dated 11 March 1971. Each of the documents which accompanied the return was also signed by the applicant. On one of those documents it was shown, inter alia, that the taxpayer was deriving rent from the properties which have been referred to in these reasons as Green and Pink. There was also a note that interest paid in connection with land purchases for resale had not been claimed as a deduction, and the document concluded with the following:-

"NOTE. During the year taxpayer purchased a property known as (Yellow) which is being developed for resale purposes. Profits when made will be returned as taxable income.

A block of 1 flats called "Staff Flat" were also purchased. These are for letting purposes.

The land known as (Pink, Green) is being prepared for farming purposes, pending development and resale at a profit which will be returned as taxable income".

  1. On 20 January 1972 the family company, Realty, was incorporated. The shareholders in the company were the applicant, his wife, and their five children. They were also shareholders in another company, (which will be called Investments), to which the applicant transferred his office and home. The applicant is and was the controlling shareholder in both companies.
  2. In 1972 Realty acquired for $250,000 the property, Orange, which adjoined Green and Pink. The purchase was financed by the vendor. On 21 October 1972 Realty lodged with the local council an application "to develop approx. 670 acres as a Suburban Subdivision (1300 lots) with neighbourhood shopping, educational, sporting and recreational facilities including golf course, 250 acre lake, ovals, swimming pools etc." The land included the properties Green, Orange and Pink.
  3. The property Green (excluding only the 60 acres which, with the 20 acres owned by M, had been offered to the Housing Commission) and the property Pink were transferred by the applicant to the company Realty on 17 January 1973 and it is the profits on those sales which were treated by the Commissioner as assessable income of the applicant in the year of income ended 30 June 1973.
  4. On 4 April 1974 the applicant sold to the Housing Commission the 60 acre portion of the property Green which he had offered to it, and M also sold to the Housing Commission the 20 acres which he had acquired in November 1971. The profit on this sale was assessed by the Commissioner as part of the applicant's income for the year ended 30 June 1974.
  5. The decisions of the Commissioner disallowing the applicant's objection to the assessments including the profits referred to in the two preceding paragraphs, are the decisions the subject of this review by the Tribunal.
  6. During 1974 the applicant's wife acquired a parcel of land adjoining the property Yellow. That land was subsequently subdivided and was sold later that year to the Housing Commission. At about that time the applicant had erected a shop on part of the property Green and had sold the land and building thereafter.
  7. Since 1974 Realty has sold three 10 acre lots from the property Pink, following a change of zoning of that property or part of it from rural to urban.
  8. There were many other dealings in land by the applicant, the family companies, and members of the family but those referred to above are dealings which concern or seem relevant to the development of the properties Green and Yellow.
  9. The applicant gave evidence that after leaving school his parents obtained employment for him as a farmhand and that he was later engaged in dairy herd testing. After three years service during the War he purchased a dairy farm in another State and operated it for about eighteen months. He then returned to New South Wales and did a technical college course in carpentry and joinery. Upon completion of the course he became apprenticed to a builder and, after completing his apprenticeship, worked as a builder for about six years. In the course of that occupation he purchased blocks of land, erected houses thereon and then sold the lands and buildings. He later became qualified as a valuer and commenced business as a real estate agent and valuer.
  10. In about 1948, while he was doing his technical college course, the applicant bought a farmlet and he and his family lived on it for about twelve months. While living on that property they kept a cow and grew vegetables. The applicant said that when he became an estate agent he looked around for a rural property close to the town which he could operate as a farm while retaining his estate agency business. A year or two before he bought the property Green he acquired the property Yellow. He ran some stock on it but found that, because of its previous use, it was not suitable land; also, because it was close to town he suffered losses of stock. The applicant later subdivided one part of the land into residential blocks, which were sold and then, over a period, sold the remainder of the property to the Housing Commission.
  11. Eventually, the applicant said, he found that Green was available for sale on favourable terms and, after extensive enquiries, he purchased it. He said that he felt that he needed more acreage to have a viable farm and therefore, when, shortly after the purchase of Green, the property Pink became available, he added it to his holdings. Later, for the same reason, the applicant said, the property Orange was acquired by Realty. The applicant's evidence was that his intention at the time of all of those purchases was to retain the lands and use them for a family farm.
  12. The applicant said that he took over a crop and some very old equipment when he purchased Green. There was no residence on that property or on Pink, and the residence on Orange was occupied by its former owner until the 1980's. The applicant said that he had carried on various farming and grazing operations on that part of the properties retained by him and Realty, but in recent years has only run cattle on the land as it is worn out and needs a long spell, fertilising and turning over. The applicant's return of income in the year ended 30 June 1970 showed rents as having been received from the properties Green and Pink. In the following two years, gross farming incomes of $1,675 and $608 were disclosed. In its return of income for the year ended 30 June 1973 Realty disclosed farm income of $10,729 and farm expenditure of $30,000.
  13. The applicant told the Tribunal that he had been hoping to make $20,000 a year out of the farming and grazing lands. However, because of rising interest rates on moneys which he had borrowed to purchase properties and to build a home, he decided in 1984 to obtain an off-shore loan of $1.1 million to pay out all his debts. Because of the effect of devaluation the amount required to repay the loan commenced to increase alarmingly not long after the money was borrowed and the debt has now risen to about $2.5 million. The applicant said that his financial difficulties had been aggravated by a four year drought and a bad period for real estate. He wished to retain the properties Green, Pink and Orange, which, with treatment, would be suitable for farming. The applicant said that the value of those properties, 53 acres of which had been rezoned residential, was now about $1 million.
  14. The applicant agreed that he had signed the declaration on his return of income for the year ended 30 June 1970 and the documents accompanying the return but said that he had not read the return or the documents and was completely unaware of the statements in the document which was referred to in paragraph 9 above. The return had been prepared by his accountant but the applicant said that neither the accountant nor any member of his staff had been given any information or instructions that there was to be development and resale of the properties Green, Pink and Yellow.
  15. The applicant said that he had regarded the accountant who had prepared the 1970 and other income tax returns as a friend as well as an accountant and that it was on his suggestion that the family companies had been formed and the farm properties were transferred to it. That project had then been discussed with his solicitor and a valuation of the properties was obtained for stamp duty purposes. The applicant said that there had been no discussion with the accountant or solicitor about selling the properties for a profit and that he had told his solicitor at the time that he intended to retain the lands.

25. In relation to the residential subdivision of the property Yellow (referred to in paragraph 7 above) the applicant stated that its purpose was to establish a price which would enable him to obtain a better price from the later sales to the Housing Commission, and he believed that that purpose had been achieved. As to the plans for development of 670 acres which was submitted to the Council in 1972, the applicant said that its purpose was to impress his bank so that he could obtain finance, and that it had never been his intention to implement that proposal. He said that there would have been no prospect of it succeeding because the land was not zoned residential. He conceded that over the years expansion of the town had taken place and the area of land zoned residential was being extended.

26. In giving evidence in cross-examination the applicant said that if not forced to sell the property he would retain it for farming and to be handed over to his family. His bank had requested that the land be sold or that an arrangement be made to repay his debt to the bank. He said that his son had ideas about the future of the land and that it was hoped that the bank would be repaid. But, as cross-examination proceeded, it emerged that a proposal for development of the lands of Realty as a tourist resort, with funds provided by an Asian company, had been made public by the applicant from August 1987 and that the applicant had been a spokesman for the project.

27. At the conclusion of the evidence it was submitted for the applicant that on the basis of his own evidence as to intention, and of the fact that the greater part of the properties Green Pink and Orange had been retained for some 18 years, and of the fact that farming and grazing had been carried on on them, the Tribunal should accept that the dominant purpose of the purchase of Green and Pink was for use for farming and grazing. As to the statements made in the 1970 income tax return, Counsel said it was unlikely that a speculator would want to indicate his intention to speculate and that an accountant knowing a taxpayer's intention to speculate would recommend a transfer of land to a company at a very large profit; it was much more likely that the accountant himself was not aware of the material in the return. Counsel submitted that subsequent events, particularly events at a time when the applicant was in financial difficulties, were not inconsistent with the applicant's original intention of retaining the lands.

28. Counsel for the respondent submitted that the applicant's credibility, which was crucial, had not been established and that the probability of the applicant's evidence as to his intention to purchase the subject lands should be determined by reference to the surrounding circumstances. The applicant, an enterprising and knowledgeable business man, had purchased the lands, strategically placed near an expanding town, and had taken opportunities to develop and re-sell those and other properties. The farming and grazing activities on the land had been minimal and were inconsistent with the claimed intention of developing a viable farming and grazing enterprise. There had been no real explanation of the material in the 1970 income tax return, which had obviously been prepared on the basis that the land was not a farm.

29. At the time of the assessments and the objection decisions the subject of the applications for review s. 26(a) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936, provided:-

"(a) profit arising from the sale by the taxpayer of any property acquired by him for the purpose of profit-making by sale, or from the carrying on or carrying out of any profit-making undertaking or scheme;"

The purpose referred to in the provision is the dominant purpose actuating the acquisition of the property (Pascoe v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation 11 ATD 108 p.112) and statements by a taxpayer as to purpose must be decided closely and received with caution (11 ATD 108 at p.111).

  1. In endeavouring to discharge the burden imposed on him by s.190(b) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936, of showing that the assessments in question were excessive, the applicant was faced with the evidence contained in his return of income for the year ended 30 June 1970, the evidence of his activities as a dealer and developer, particularly of lands in the area where Green and Pink were situated, and the evidence of subsequent sales of parts of those properties and proposals for development of the remainder.
  2. As to the statements in the income tax return, it is not inherently improbable that the applicant signed the return and the accompanying documents without reading them. It is possible that a mistake could have been made by the person preparing the return but, in the absence of explanation, it is difficult to understand how it could have been made and that it was not later detected and corrected. That the applicant was involved in buying and selling land was clear and, if the subject lands were not acquired for re-sale, it should have been obvious to the person preparing the income tax return that it was desirable to indicate that intention.
  3. The activities of a taxpayer as a land dealer and developer would not, of course, preclude that taxpayer from engaging in farming and grazing and acquiring lands for that purpose. On the other hand, it would not be inconsistent with a purpose of eventual re-sale or development that the taxpayer should do some farming and grazing while holding the lands until the time was right for re-sale or development.
  4. In giving his oral evidence the applicant tended not to give considered answers to questions but, rather, to seize upon some words or phrases in the question as a basis for reiterating that his intention had been to have a family farm on the subject lands. This may well have been because of his anxiety to impress the Tribunal on that matter and because of his anxiety about the result of the proceedings. However, making allowance for such difficulties, the evidence of the applicant as to his intention was not convincing. Furthermore, his evidence was not corroborated by evidence from any member of his family or any other person, nor was there any evidence from his former accountant or solicitor.

34. On the evidence, the applicant has failed to satisfy the Tribunal that his dominant purpose when acquiring the properties Green and Pink was not profit-making by sale and so he has not discharged the burden imposed upon him by s.190(b) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 to prove that the assessments were excessive. The decision of the Tribunal, therefore, must be to affirm the objection decisions which are the subject of the applications for review.
