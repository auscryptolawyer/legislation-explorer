ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - rebates - expenses in connection with adoption -whether sufficient connection - whether overseas proceedings included

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, S159X

Acts Interpretation Act 1901, S15AB

Case R110 84 ATC 733

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

AT86/152

AAT Decision No 3846



Deputy President RK Todd

19 October 1986

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. AT86/152

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Deputy President RK Todd

Date : 19 October 1987

Place : Canberra

Decision : The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

(sgd.) RK Todd

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No AT86/152

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

19 October 1987 Deputy President RK Todd

1. The applicant, who represented himself at the hearing of this matter, together with his wife adopted two children from Chile in the year of income ended 30 June 1985. He claimed, pursuant to the now repealed S159X of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITAA"), a rebate of $17,658 for expenses incurred in adopting the children. At the relevant time, S159X read as follows:

"159X(1) An amount equal to the total of the amounts (if any) paid by the taxpayer in the year of income in respect of expenses in connexion with the adoption by the taxpayer, or by the taxpayer and his or her spouse, of a child under the age of 21 years, to the extent to which those amounts have not been repaid to the taxpayer in the year of income shall, for the purposes of section 159N, be treated as a rebatable amount in respect of that year of income.

159X(2) In sub-section (1), 'expenses' means -

(a) expenses for the services of a barrister or solicitor;

(b) expenses of proceedings before a Court; or

(c) fees payable to the Commonwealth, a State, the Administration of a Territory or an organization approved as a private adoption agency under a law of the Commonwealth or of a State or Territory,

but does not include expenses in connexion with the obtaining of an order of a Court dispensing with the consent of a person to the adoption."

2. In his original return the applicant included such items as airfares in his claim. At the hearing however he narrowed his claim to the following expenses:

  1. Solicitors fees relating to the adoption of R. - A$5,302
  2. Solicitors fees relating to the adoption of N. - A$3,880.
  1. To adopt a child from overseas the couple had to follow procedures set down by the relevant State/Territory authority. These procedures had been developed in consultation with the child's country of origin. Having been approved by the authority as being suitable to adopt, the prospective parents would then approach the overseas country to ascertain whether a child was available. To enable a suitable child to leave its country of origin and enter Australia, proceedings were instituted in the country of origin to have the guardianship of the child vested in the Director of Welfare of the State/Territory as delegate of the Minister of Immigration. Custody of the child, as opposed to guardianship which was retained by the Director, was then granted to the couple who brought the child back to Australia and, after further assessment, applied to a court for an adoption order. The cost of this process was borne by the prospective parents.
  2. Of the $17,658 originally claimed, $185 paid to Australian solicitors was allowed by the Commissioner, who consequently disallowed the balance of $17,473.
  3. The expenses still in dispute in this case were incurred in obtaining the guardianship of the child for the Director of Welfare so as to enable the children to be brought to Australia.
  4. It was argued by the applicant, in a well thought-out submission, that first, obtaining a guardianship order in favour of the Director of Welfare was a necessary step in the adoption process and therefore within S159X and, secondly, that the Tribunal should not 'read down' the provisions of S.159X(2) as only applying to court proceedings within Australia.
  5. In support of the assessment the Commissioner's representative relied on Case R110, 84 ATC 733 in which a claim for a rebate for expenses incurred relating to adoption of a child from India were rejected. The facts of that case differ from the facts before the Tribunal as the expenses in dispute in that case consisted of items such as airfares which have all been conceded by the applicant in this case (see para2 above).
  6. The reasoning of the Board of Review in Case R110 is however relevant to the facts before the Tribunal in the present case. Mr HP Stevens (Chairman) at 736 stated:

"12. As at present advised I think travelling costs are not covered and that Australian Court proceedings are the only ones covered. However, in the present case I do not think it is necessary to base a decision thereon for the evidence has made it clear that the expenses were not in relation to any actual adoption proceedings by the taxpayer but were related to the guardianship proceedings and the subsequent bringing of the child to Australia. Admittedly these were a prerequisite to the obtaining of the child in respect of whom adoption proceedings later took place in NSW but I cannot see them as 'expenses of proceedings before a Court' 'in connexion with the adoption by the taxpayer ... of a child'."

9. After applying S15AB of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 and having read the second reading speech relating to this provision, Mr McCarthy in his Reasons in Case R110 relied on the term "normally incurred in adopting a child" to conclude that the word "Court" should be interpreted as meaning "a Court exercising jurisdiction within the Commonwealth in connection with the adoption of children".

  1. The applicant in this case led evidence which, he said, showed that it was now "normal" to adopt from overseas. In 1984/85 39 per cent, and in 1985/86 59 per cent, of adoption orders in his State/Territory were in respect of overseas adoptions. It was argued by the Commissioner that what was "normal" should be determined as at 1971 when the section was introduced - at which time local adoption was "normal". Due to the conclusion to which I have come on the question of the connection of the guardianship proceedings to the adoption, it is unnecessary for me to refer further to this line of argument.
  2. Although this is one of the cases in which obtaining guardianship is a necessary prerequisite to adoption, and in spite of the careful submissions on this point by the applicant, I am unable to find the necessary connection between guardianship and adoption. The obtaining of an adoption order does not necessarily follow from the guardianship proceedings. Evidence was given by Mrs. X, the Director of Child Welfare that:

"...It is possible for a number of reasons that an adoption order might not be made. The most usual reason is that parents having spent a lot of money obtaining a child from overseas do not go [to Court] - and they have the child in Australia, and they know that the child may get citizenship when it is 16, and they also know they can make valid wills to advantage the child."

In addition, it was also indicated that the granting of an adoption order did not automatically follow from the child being placed in a couple's custody when brought to Australia.

  1. Considering all of the factors mentioned I find that there is not a sufficient connection between the guardianship proceedings and the adoption of the children for a rebate to be allowable under S.159X.
  2. As I am also in agreement with the members of the No.l Board of Review in Case R110 that "Court" in S159X refers to Australian courts, this case would fall to be decided adversely to the applicant on that footing alone.

14. For the reasons given above, the Tribunal will affirm the Commissioner's objection decision under review.
