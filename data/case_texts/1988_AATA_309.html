ADDENDUM

26 October 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

The purpose of this addendum is to make good an omission from the preceding text: the failure to explain the conclusion that s.260 of the Act operates to strike down the claim found to have otherwise been allowable pursuant to s.51(1) (pa.48) and another might have been so allowable (pa.46). Those conclusions do not deny the force of the observations in Cecil Bros. Pty. Ltd. v. F.C.T. [(1962-1964) [1964] HCA 82; 111 C.L.R. 430] and, in particular, the observation of Dixon C.J. (supported by Kitto, J. and Taylor, J.) when his Honour said (at p.438):-

"I have great difficulty in seeing how (s.260 of the Act) could apply to defeat or reduce any deduction otherwise truly allowable under s.51."

Those observations were directed to a situation in which the only "arrangement" contended for was one in which a taxpayer freely chose to pay more for its stock-in-trade than was necessary. The Court refused to disallow the payment as a deduction either in whole or in part. Taylor, J. went to the heart of the matter when he said at pp.438-39:-

"I find great difficulty in identifying what, on the facts of the case, is said to constitute the 'contract, agreement or arrangement' falling within s.260."

But, in the circumstances of this case, there is, in my view, unquestionably an "arrangement" of which the obligations to pay interest, whether to Herunda or the Trustees, formed but part. That arrangement, in order to have the efficacy claimed by the Applicant, necessarily also involved the application of the moneys borrowed to income-producing purposes: the contention being that the income-earning application of the moneys made deductible the interest outgoings. It is that "arrangement" in its entirety which is void. That being so, the matter is to be determined on the basis that no income was derived (pa.48). It then follows that the interest outgoings are also "annihilated": they ceased to be directed to the derivation of income. In these circumstances, and for those reasons, s.260 of the Act applies.

REASONS FOR DECISION

24 October 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. The Applicant in these proceedings is a self-employed surburban accountant who, at all material times, has also been a married man and a father of two children. As 30 June 1978 approached he embarked upon two courses of action which, should they be as effective as he hoped, would wholly rid him of liability to income tax in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1978 and substantially reduce, if not eliminate, his liability to income tax in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1979.
  2. Pursuant to the first course of action he invested in a partnership known as the "Vaucluse Partnership No. 20" ("Vaucluse Partnership"). He invested $2,500 on the 27th day of June 1978. Later he was to claim deductions in relation to partnership losses in sums of $19,996 and $29 in the years of income ended 30 June 1978 and 1979 and to acknowledge having derived assessable income in the sum of $48 from the partnership in the years of income ended 30 June 1980 and 1981. By the second course of action he arranged to borrow $100,000 from Herunda Pty. Limited ("Herunda") upon terms which left him exposed to the risk of being called on to pay interest in advance for 65 months of the near forty year term of the loan. Demand for payment was made the day after the loan was entered into and it is said that $74,750 was paid to Herunda by way of interest in June 1978. In consequence the Applicant claimed a deduction in that sum for that year of income.
    1. In the next year of income, in June 1979, he invested $250 in a partnership known as the "Dover Heights Partnership" ("Dover Partnership") and then went on to claim that he was entitled to a deduction in the sum of $25,609 being his share of the partnership loss to 30 June 1979. A further loss of $562 was to be claimed in the next year.
    2. In the same year he also claimed a deduction in the sum of $9,312 said to be moneys paid by way of interest to the trustees of trusts for his two children ("the Trusts"). The claim is that he was entitled to a deduction on interest so paid because the moneys borrowed from the trustees of the children's trusts had been applied the previous year in payment of the interest paid to Herunda. Further claims in relation to interest paid to the trustees of the children's trusts were to be made in the years of income ended 30 June, 1980, 1981 and 1982s each in the sum of $9,312.
    3. As 30 June 1980 approached, the Applicant embarked on a further course of action whereby it is said he borrowed a sum of $150,000 from Frandette Pty. Limited ("Frandette") which sum was to be applied to income-earning purposes by being lent to Astralan Pty. Limited ("Astralan"). It was said that, in consequence of the lending to Astralan, the Applicant was to derive assessable income in the year of income ended 30 June 1980 in the sum of $277 and thereafter in succeeding years in the sum of $20,250. However, it was also said that, in order to fund the lending, it was necessary to borrow and to pay interest on the moneys borrowed; and also to pay a procuration fee. The result was that deductions were claimed for interest paid to Frandette in the sum of $20,451 in the year of income ended 30 June 1980 and thereafter in sums of $20,175. In addition, there was another smaller amount of interest in sums of $37 and $75 in the years of income ended 30 June 1981 and 1984 respectively and a claim to deduct $207 (being one-fifth of a procuration fee of $1,037) in the years of income ended 30 June 1980 to 1983 inclusive. (Due to an oversight, no claim was made to deduct the balance of the procuration fee in the 1984 year). As a result, it was claimed that there was a net loss of $20,381 in the year of income ended 30 June 1980; of $169 in the year of income ended 30 June 1981; and of $132 in each of the years of income ended 30 June 1982 and 1983. Had the procuration fee claim been made in the 1984 year the claimed loss would have been $207.

6. The final course of action to be considered in these proceedings was also undertaken in the month of June 1981. In that month the Applicant advanced $2,000 to Australian Explorers (Petroleum and Minerals) Pty. Limited ("Explorers") and, it is said, borrowed a further $8,000, all subscribed in the purchase of interests in a joint venture with Explorers. It is claimed that, in consequence, he became entitled to a deduction pursuant to S.124AH of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") for the year of income ended 30 June 1981 and that, by reason of the later payment of interest on the moneys so said to have been borrowed, during the year of income ended 30 June 1984, he claimed to be entitled to an income tax deduction in the sum of $889.

The Applicant was also able to produce letters written in the second half of the calendar years 1978 to 1981 inclusive advising him that his losses (or profit, as the case may be) were in the sums later claimed as detailed in his returns. In addition he was able to produce minutes of meetings of Directors of a company Bintara Pty. 13. In my view the evidence before me is insufficient to establish what activities, if any, were undertaken by Vaucluse Partnership. It follows that I am not satisfied of any circumstances having occurred which could possibly give rise to an entitlement in the Applicant to an income tax deduction for any alleged partnership loss. The mere production of documents is not alone enough to establish the occurrence of events and transactions referred to in those documents. In so holding I adopt the reasons for decision expressed in Case V38 88 A.T.C. 325. (I understand that decision to be subject to an appeal to a Full Bench of the Federal Court of Australia). As the Commissioner has not claimed that the alleged income of later years should be assessed, I make no finding in that regard.

HERUNDA AND THE CHILDREN'S TRUSTS;

16. What was acknowledged during the hearing to be colloquially referred to as "an interest prepayment scheme" is essentially limited to the dealing between the Applicant and Herunda. However, that is so closely associated with other dealings involved in the full implementation of the plan that it is appropriate to deal at the one time with both claims – the claim to deduct the interest of $74,750 paid to Herunda in the year of income ended 30 June 1978; and the claims to deduct $9,312 in each of the years of income ended 30 June 1979 to 1982 inclusive - being payments made to the trustees of the children's trusts. The Applicant claims that he is entitled to those deductions on the basis that he suffered "losses and outgoings" by way of interest paid on moneys borrowed and applied to income-producing purposes, with the interest being paid in the course of deriving assessable income and not by way of payments of capital or of a capital nature.

17. The Applicant has satisfied me that a plan of action was arranged with Herunda which was to involve other interests and which was in all respects implemented as envisaged. I find that, on 19 January 1978, the Applicant executed a loan agreement with Herunda. The agreement provided for an advance of $100,000 to be repaid -

"On a day during the calendar year ending on the thirty-first day of December 2017 to be nominated by the lender on 1st January, 2017 and in default of such nomination to be repaid on 31st December 2017."

Interest was to be payable monthly in arrears at the rate of 13.8% per annum to 31 December 2012 and thereafter at the rate of 8 3/4% per annum. The agreement then expressly

provided:-

"4. Notwithstanding any other Provision of this Agreement should the Lender on or prior to 31 January 1978 give notice in writing to the Borrower that it requires the payment of interest for the first 65 months of the term of this Agreement calculated at the rate of thirteen and eight-tenths per centum (13.8%) per annum on the Principal Sum and Provided that the Borrower pays the amount so calculated to the Lender on or before 31 January 1978 then the interest payable under this Agreement shall thereafter be calculated at the rate of eight and three-quarters (8 3/4%) per centum per annum in lieu of the rate of thirteen and eight-tenths (13.8%) per centum per annum in respect of the balance of the term in the manner provided by Clause 3 hereof, upon the Principal Sum outstanding and the next succeeding payment of interest shall be made on the thirtieth day of June 1983 and interest at such rate of eight and three-quarters (8 3/4%) per centum per annum shall be paid monthly until the Principal Sura shall be repaid."

  1. I find that, pursuant to the agreement, $100,000 was deposited to a bank account in the name of and under the control of the Applicant which had been established for the purpose in Canberra. I find that at the time moneys were credited to that account the bank already held an authority from the Applicant directing it to transfer the moneys to a Sydney account under the control of the Applicant; and to do so by telegraphic transfer. I find that the $100,000 so deposited in Canberra on 19 January 1978 was transferred that day as proposed. I find that the Applicant thereon applied to a company, No. 22 Pty. Limited ("No. 22"), for 100,000 $1 redeemable preference shares in that company. I find that No. 22 was a company incorporated on 10 January 1978; that it was controlled by the Applicant; that the Applicant and his wife each held one of the two subscriber $1 ordinary shares; and that the Applicant and his wife were the only Directors of the company.
  2. Despite non-production of the Memorandum and Articles of Association of No. 22; of its Share Register; and of Scrip Certificates; but relying on the production in evidence of Minutes of the proceedings of Directors of No. 22 recording a resolution to effect the issue of the shares applied for and a copy from the company's records of a return of allotment; and also relying on the evidence of the Applicant as a Director of No. 22, I am satisfied that those things were done and that the doing of them was within power. I find that the shares applied for were duly allotted on 19 January 1978 and that $100,000 was received to the credit of the bank account of No. 22 as a result of drawing funds against the account under the control of the Applicant. I find that on 19 January 1978, as evidenced by a further Minute of Directors' Meeting produced from the records of No. 22 and of a loan agreement in writing executed under the company seal of No. 22 and witnessed by the Applicant and his wife, that No. 22 agreed to lend $100,000. I further find that, on that day, $100,000 was drawn against the account of No. 22 and credited to an account in the name of the borrowers.

20. The account to which the moneys were so credited was an account in the joint names of the Applicant and his wife described in the title of the account as "Trustees for" their children. I further find that, much earlier - on 29 October 1974 in one case and on a date unidentified in 1976 in the other - a settlor had declared trusts in favour of the son and the daughter respectively of the Applicant, appointing the parents of the children to be trustees of each trust. Nothing turns upon the terms of either trust. Further, I am satisfied that nothing turns on the circumstance that, except for income tax purposes, the trustees committed the trust estates jointly. I find that, by the last-mentioned agreement for loan of 19 January 1978, No. 22 agreed in writing to lend to the "Trust Fund for J and A K - jointly" $100,000 to be repaid "upon the expiration of five (5) days after the service by the Lender on the Borrower of a demand in writing requiring repayment of the principal sum". The loan was to be advanced "free of interest save as hereinafter provided". Having provided by Clause 2 for repayment in accordance with a demand as previously mentioned, the agreement went on to provide:-

"3. If the borrower shall fail to make due payment of the principal sum or any part thereof after the same fall (sic) due the borrower shall pay interest thereon or on so much thereof as shall from time to time be outstanding at the rate set out in Item D of the said schedule."

The rate of interest so specified was 8% per annum.

21. I am satisfied on the evidence before me that the Applicant and his wife, in their distinct capacities as joint trustees of the separate trusts, formally committed the trusts to the joint borrowing. Minutes of the Trustees of each trust made 19 January 1978 in each case recorded:-

"Resolved that the trust fund apply jointly with the trust fund for ( ) for a loan in the sum of $100,000 on call at an interest rate of 8% per annum from No. 22 Pty. Limited"

and I find that $100,000 was credited that day to the aforementioned account in the name of the two trustees.

22. I further find that $74,500 was on the same day advanced from the last-mentioned account to the Applicant and deposited to his firm account and that that advance was by way of loan. I so find, notwithstanding the absence of any written agreement and notwithstanding the seeming failure of the Applicant and his wife as trustees to have regard to their full responsibilities as trustees [cf. Fouche v. State Superannuation Board [1952] HCA 1; (1952) 88 C.L.R. 609; and Glennon v. F.C.T. (1972) 127 C.L.R. 5033. I find that that loan was made on the terms recorded in the last-mentioned Minutes of Trustees in which it was recorded:-

"Resolved that the trust fund, jointly with the trust fund for ( ) make a loan to (the Applicant) at an interest rate of 12 1/2% per annum in the sum of $74,500."

23. I further find that it was on that day, or the day following, that Herunda made demand of the Applicant in the following terms:-

"Pursuant to Clause 4 of the Deed dated 19th January 1978 we hereby advise that we require the payment of 65 months' interest calculated at the rate of thirteen and eight-tenths per centum per annum on the principal amount advanced to you. This amount should now be paid and, provided that the appropriate interest amount is received by us, the interest rate under the terms of the Deed will reduce to eight and three-quarters (8 3/4%)

per centum per annum in accordance with that Clause." I find that the demand so made was fully expected by the Applicant. Had it not been made the opportunity primarily sought by the transaction would not have eventuated. It was for that reason that the previous day the Applicant had arranged to enter into the agreement with himself and his wife as trustees with a view to borrowing the $74,500 deposited to his firm account. That being so, he was in a position to satisfy, and did satisfy, the demand of Herunda on the same day as it was made.

24. I also find that, completely in accordance with the expectations of all concerned, Herunda, no later than 20 January 1978, made an offer to the Applicant and his wife in their capacity as trustees as follows:-

"To assign to you the Principal Sum of $100,000 all interest to hereafter accrue thereon, the right title and interest of this company in or arising out of a Loan Agreement dated the nineteenth day of January 1978 made between this company of the one part and (the Applicant) of the other part, and the full benefit and advantage for the sum of $25,492. It is noted that a notice has been given pursuant to Clause 4 of the said Loan Agreement. This offer shall only be capable of acceptance by your paying to this company a cheque for $25,492 within seven days of the date of this notice."

25. By the terms of the offer it would seem that the draftsman had assumed that the giving of a Notice of Demand pursuant to Clause 4 of the Loan Agreement would automatically result in Hereunda receiving payment of the moneys so demanded. But whether that assumption in fact was made or not, the trustees did accept the offer. They accepted it with such alacrity that they did not await the passing of any of the 7 days allowed for by Herunda. Instead, on 20 January 1978, $25,492 was paid from the account of the trustees to Herunda. From that point the Applicant was indebted to the trustees only and was liable to pay to them $100,000 in the year 2017 and, pending such payment, to pay interest on $100,000 at the rate of 8 3/4% per annum by monthly instalments commencing on 30 June 1983.

  1. In the remaining days of the financial year ended 30 June 1978, I find that No. 22 received $3,550 by way of interest from the trustees and that No. 22 thereupon immediately declared and distributed to the Applicant as holder of the redeemable preference shares a dividend of $2,000. I find that No. 22 declared its income to the Commissioner consistently with those transactions and was assessed as liable to tax. I further find that each trust declared its income to the Commissioner in accordance with those arrangements and, in doing so, claimed a deduction for $1,775 being interest paid and returned in a calculation of the net income of each trust for the years of income ended 30 June 1978 and 1979 and disclosed as wholly distributed.
  2. I also find that in the year of income ended 30 June 1979 the Applicant paid $9,312 to the trustees in discharge of his obligation to pay interest on the moneys borrowed and that he did so in each of the years of income ended 30 June 1979 to 1982 inclusive. The Commissioner has disallowed the claims of the Applicant for deductions for the moneys so paid. I find that no further claim was made because, following 30 June 1982, the loan was paid out. I note that interest was not paid up to the date on which the principal was repaid. That may be a matter of significance in considering questions of trusteeship and may reflect the attitude of the Applicant generally in relation to the plan, but it is not of any particular tax significance.

28. As to the Herunda claim I accept that the moneys as borrowed initially were applied to an income-producing purpose acquisition of the redeemable preference shares in No. 22. The evidence placed before me indicates that No. 22 paid dividends in June 1978 of $2,000; January 1980 $3,082; April 1981 $2,900; and April 1982 $3,815: all of which were returned as assessable income by the Applicant. The accounts of No. 22 indicate that $2,012 was reserved in the year of income ended 30 June 1982 to enable a dividend of $2,012 to be paid to the holders of redeemable preference shares. I accept that a demand was made in June 1978 in respect of a period of 65 months and I accept that the Applicant paid $74,750 in June 1978 in satisfaction of the liability which existed pursuant to the loan to pay interest on demand. I do not accept that the payment so made was made "in the course of" deriving assessable income. The involvement of the Applicant with Herunda was not something which arose in isolation. The dealing between the Applicant and Herunda was but one element in a complex of arrangements as to which it was necessary either that all elements should play the part which they did or that some other part be played to the same effect as any omitted element. The objective of that complex of arrangements was primarily to eliminate liability in the Applicant to income tax in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1978, with some incidental tax consequences for the other participants in the arrangement by way of profit to Herunda, No. 22 and the Trusts; and with further, albeit lesser advantageous tax consequences for the Applicant in subsequent years.

THE INTEREST DIFFERENTIAL SCHEME

30. On 25 June 1980 the Applicant entered into loan agreements. One agreement provided for him to borrow $150,000 from Frandette at an interest rate of 13.5% per annum: the other provided for him to lend $150,000 to Astralan at a rate of 13.5% per annum to 30 June 1990 and at 13.9% thereafter. To provide some security to Frandette to ensure performance by the Applicant of his obligations, on the same day as the indebtedness of Australan arose the debt was assigned by the Applicant to Frandette. The agreement with Frandette obliged the Applicant to pay interest of $276 on 29 June 1980 and thereafter $20,175 per annum commencing on 30 June 1980 continuing until 30 June 2009. To enable the payment due on 30 June 1980 to be made the Applicant entered into a loan agreement with Bintara on 26 June 1980 whereby he borrowed $20,174 at an interest rate of 13.5% per annum repayable on 1 July 1980. The Bintara moneys were used to pay the interest due to Frandette on 30 June 1980. The loan agreement to Astralan obliged Astralan to pay interest to 30 June 1980 ($277) on 28 June 1980 and thereafter until 30 June 1990 $20,250 annually on 1 July, commencing on 1 July 1980. A cheque from Astralan in the sum of $277 was credited to the account of Applicant on 26 June 1980, providing a sufficient sum to enable the Applicant to draw $276 that day in favour of Frandette. On 30 June 1980 the $20,174 borrowed from Bintara, when added to the $1 remaining in the account, enabled the Applicant to pay the $20,175 due to Frandette that day. Thus, by midnight 30 June 1980 the Applicant is said to have "derived" $277 by way of assessable income in the form of interest from Astralan and to have incurred "losses and outgoings" in the course of deriving that income in the sum of $20,451. To enable the Applicant to fund the additional expenditure he had borrowed $20,174 from Bintara. Next day, on 1 July 1980, there was credited to his account from Astralan $20,250 of which it seems $20,211 (Principal $20,174 plus Interest $37) was immediately paid to Bintara, thereby satisfying all indebtedness to Bintara.

31. As 30 June 1981 approached, the promoters forwarded to the Applicant for execution two cheques drawn on his account. I am satisfied that a similar practice was followed in subsequent years. The result was that in each of the years ended 30 June 1981 to 30 June 1984 the Applicant returned as assessable income interest from Astralan in the sum of $20,250 (being a credit effected on 1 July - day 1 - of the relevant year of income); and claimed a deduction for $20,175 being interest "paid" To Frandette on 30 June - day 365 - of the year of income). After further provision was made for borrowings on incidental loans ($37 - 1981 - seemingly 5 days interest on $20,174 at 13.5% per annum; and $75 - 1984) and claims for the writing off of the procuration fees, the losses claimed in the years ended 30 June 1981 to 1984 (as the overall result of the transaction) were respectively $169, $132, $132 and nil. Had the pattern continued without any further claims to deductions for procuration fees or interest payments other than payments to Frandette under the loan agreement, and had the arrangement run its full course there would have been a net gain in each year of $75 ($20,250 from Astralan less $20,175 to Frandette). That would be until the year of income ended 30 June 2010, in which year the Applicant would have returned interest from Astralan amounting to $20,250, received 1 July 2009, without having incurred any outgoing liability in interest in that financial year. The evidence does not persuade me that in fact that would have happened, or that it was ever intended that that should happen. It might not have happened because the Applicant might not have lived to that date. But, had he lived to that date, I am not persuaded that he would by that time have found an enthusiasm for paying income tax which was lacking in the 1970's and 1980's.

31. Be that as it may, the essence of the interest differential scheme lay simply in this:

(a) that the taxpayer entered into arrangements with two entities, to borrow from the one and to lend to the other, a large sum of money;

(b) that, as the amount to be lent was to be equal to the amount borrowed, the Applicant needed no financial resources of his own to effect those transactions;

(c) that there was some unspecified relationship between the lender and the borrower and all others participating in the production and enactment of the scheme whereby all of the acts in the scenario were co-ordinated;

(d) that the arrangements did not necessitate that either lender or borrower have any capacity, financial or otherwise, other than to ensure that a banker would honour one cheque by presenting it against the other, thereby effecting self-cancelling transactions through a bank account sufficient to be recorded on a bank statement;

(e) that, having regard only to considerations of interest, over the term of the loan it was proposed that the interest receivable from Astralan would slightly exceed the interest payable to Frandette;

(f) that in all years but the first and last of the term (assuming that it ran full-term in accordance with the documents) interest received would slightly exceed interest outlaid; but

(g) that the attraction of the scheme lay in the circumstance that, in the first year interest outlaid would substantially exceed interest received, and do so in an amount approximating that by which, in the last year of the term (should the scheme run the full course reflected by the documents) interest received would exceed interest outlaid by a similar amount;

(h) that, should the scheme be effective, liability to pay income tax could be avoided in substantial degree in the first year of the proposed term until "balanced" by a liability to tax upon a comparable sum in the last year of the term; and

(i) that the magnitude of the advantage to be so had need be in no way reflected by the financial capacity of the Applicant or of the other parties to the borrowings, nor indeed influenced by any other consideration than the desire of the Applicant to avoid tax.

THE LAW - GENERALLY

33. All of the questions arising in these references touch the application of the Act to the affairs of the Applicant. The Act sets out the criteria for the determination by assessment of the taxable income of the applicant. His liability in income tax is determined by that assessment. S.48 of the Act provides that:-

"In calculating the taxable income of a taxpayer, the total assessable income derived by him during the year of income shall be taken as a basis, and from it there shall be deducted all allowable deductions."

The only concept of allowable deductions which arises for consideration in relation to the issues (other than exploration expenditure) is that provided for in s.51(1) which provides:-

"All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income."

That Section at least requires as a condition of deductibility that there be a nexus between the expense claimed as a deduction and the generation of, or the attempt to generate, assessable income. In the circumstances of this Applicant the assertion is that the interest expenses claimed as deductions are sufficiently related to assessable income in that they were related to either the production of assessable income by way of interest receivable on loans or as dividends expected to be received upon shares.

34. As Toohey, J. pointed out in F.C. of T. v. Ilbery (81 A.T.C. 4661) the phrase "for the purpose" in s.51 does not explicitly qualify both of the positive limbs of the section.

As his Honour said (at page 4665):-

"The opening formulation of the section is disjunctive and the reference to 'purpose' is in relation to the preceding words."

However, his Honour immediately went on to say:-

"That is not to say that purpose may not be relevant in considering whether losses or outgoings 'are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income'; it is simply to say that as a matter of language the express reference to 'purpose' in sec. 51 is only in relation to the carrying on of a business."

  1. S.51(1) is so structured that, in order to establish an entitlement to deductibility, a taxpayer must first establish either that the expense was incurred "in gaining or producing the assessable income" - the first positive limb; or was "necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income" - the second positive limb. In the circumstances of the Applicant, there is no suggestion that the activities he undertook in any way consitituted "the carrying on of a business". Each claim to a s.51(1) deduction relates to a transaction isolated in itself. There was no element common to the several claims, such as would constitute the multiplicity of them as a "single business": what was common to them was a desire to avoid liability to income tax, not any business or commercial objective.
  2. As Brennan, J. observed in Ure v. F.C. of T. (81 A.T.C. 4100 at p.4104):-

"The purposes for which money is laid out is an issue of fact, turning upon the objective circumstances which human experience would judge to be relevant to the issue."

In that case, where the taxpayers had on-lent to a related trust at a nominal rate of interest moneys borrowed by the taxpayers at a commercial rate of interest, his Honour said:-

"The disparity in interest rates is itself eloquent to suggest the existence of purposes ulterior to the earning of interest at the rate of 1% per annum, and the evidence confirms the existence of further purposes...."

"... there is an air of unreality about the proposition that the borrowed moneys were laid out wholly for the purpose of earning a return of 1% per annum. Rather is it right to say that the purpose for which the borrowed moneys were laid out included all the purposes earlier mentioned, only one of which was to earn a return of 1% per annum."

  1. But, having satisfied either or both of the positive tests, it is next necessary to avoid the operation of the negative tests. In the circumstances of these cases that requires that the Applicant establish that the losses and outgoings claimed did not constitute outgoings "of capital, or of a capital ... nature".
  2. Even so, having satisfied the requirements of s.51(1) will not necessarily ensure to the Applicant the benefit he seeks, for s.260 provides:-

"Every contract, agreement, or arrangement made or entered into, orally or in writing, whether before or after the commencement of this Act, shall so far as it has or purports to have the purpose or effect of in any way, directly or indirectly-

(a) altering the incidence of any income tax;
  • (b) relieving any person from liability to pay any income tax or make any return;
  • (c) defeating, evading or avoiding any duty or liability imposed on any person by this Act; or
  • (d) preventing the operation of this Act in any respect,

be absolutely void, as against the Commissioner, or in regard to any proceeding under this Act, but without prejudice to such validity as it may have in any other respect or for any other purpose."

39. In considering the application of s.260 to the claims of the Applicant it will suffice to have regard to the decision of the Privy Council in Newton & Ors. v. F.C. of T. C(1958) [1958] HCA 7; 98 C.L.R. 13. In that case, their Lordships advised that:

" (S.260) is not concerned with the motives of individuals. It is not concerned with their desire to avoid tax, but only with the means which they employ to do it. It affects every 'contract, agreement or arrangement' (which their Lordships will henceforward refer to compendiously as 'arrangement') which has the purpose or effect of avoiding tax. In applying the section you must, by the very words of it, look at the arrangement itself and see which is its effect - which it does - irrespective of the motives of the persons who made it. Williams J. put it well when he said "The purpose of a contract, agreement or arrangement must be what it is intended to effect and that intention must be ascertained from its terms. These terms may be oral or written or may have to be inferred from the circumstances but, when they have been ascertained, their purpose must be what they effect'."

As their Lordships said:-

"The word 'purpose' means, not motive, but the effect which it is said to achieve - the end in view. The word 'effect' means the end accomplished or achieved. The whole set of words denotes concerted action to an end - the end of avoiding tax."

40. It is not every arrangement which has the effect of avoiding or reducing tax which is struck down by the section for, before the section will operate, it must be possible, in the words of Lord Denning:-

"you must be able to predicate - by looking at the overt acts by which it (the arrangement) was implemented -that it was implemented in that particular way so as to avoid tax. If you cannot so predicate, but have to acknowledge that the transactions are capable of explanation by reference to ordinary business or family dealing, without necessarily being labelled as a means to avoid tax, then the arrangement does not come within the section."

41. I now turn to apply those principles to the particular claims.

VAUCLUSE PARTNERSHIP

42. For reasons previously indicated I am not persuaded that any expense was incurred in circumstances which satisfy the positive requirements of s.51(1) of the Act.

DOVER HEIGHTS

43. For reasons previously indicated I am not persuaded that any expense was incurred in circumstances which satisfy the positive requirements of s.51(1) of the Act.

$74,500 PAID TO HERUNDA AS INTEREST

  1. I am satisfied that $100,000 was borrowed; that it was applied in the purchase of shares in No. 22; that it was intended that the shares should be productive of assessable income; and that the shares were productive of assessable income. I find that all of those things occurred in the context of a more comprehensive arrangement to which the Applicant was a party whereby, at the time the principal sum was borrowed, it was intended that it should be paid to No. 22; No. 22 should lend the money to the trustees; and that the trustees would lend $74,500 to the Applicant at interest to be applied (as it was applied) in payment to Herunda of interest in accordance with the demand which it made and which had been accepted would be made for payment of interest for 65 months within two days of the making of the initial advance. I also find that, wholly as expected, the remaining $25,492 was paid by the trustees to Herunda whereon the trustees took an assignment of the indebtedness of the Applicant to Herunda for a sum of $100,000 payable in 2017 A.D., together with the right to be paid interest on the amount outstanding at the rate of 8 3/4 percent per annum from 1983.
  2. In those circumstances I am satisfied that the Applicant has fulfilled the requirements of the first positive limb of s.51(1) but I also hold that his action in paying interest in advance for 65 months constituted an affair of capital or of a capital nature. Having regard to the fact that, as between the Applicant and Herunda, he was to have the use of $100,000 for only a few days and thereafter to have the use of only $25,500, in my view the $74,500 paid falls outside the scope of s.51(1). (F.C. of T. v. Ilbery - ante).

46. But, whether the latter conclusion be correct or not, in my view the Applicant faces a further difficulty. The entirety of the arrangement was lacking in any of the profit-making characteristics which distinguish a commercial transaction or a desire upon the part of the Applicant to increase his wealth save one. The exception is that it was the objective and purpose of the transaction to qualify the Applicant for a tax deduction such as would eliminate in that year his liability to income tax and would effect a reduction in his tax liability in the year following. By every reasonable standard, the transaction cannot be otherwise characterized. That being so, even if the requirements of s.51(1) were satisfied, the claim fails by reason of s.260 of the Act.

$9,312 PER ANNUM PAID TO THE TRUSTEES AS INTEREST

47. I accept that the Applicant borrowed $74,500 from the trustees at interest; that the moneys were applied to defray an interest obligation to Herunda in relation to moneys borrowed and applied for the purposes of producing assessable income; and that the moneys borrowed from the trustees were so applied. That being so, I am satisfied that the requirements of s.51(1) are satisfied. In this instance there is no basis for contending that the outgoings are outgoings of capital or of a capital nature.

48. However, as with the payment to Herunda, I am satisfied that s.260 of the Act applies. The dealing with the trustees was a borrowing within the same total arrangement as embraced the payment to Herunda. That being so, s.260 strikes down the claim otherwise allowable pursuant to s.51(1). But there is yet a further dimension to the arrangement to be considered. A third element in the arrangement was the investment of the moneys borrowed from Herunda in the purchase of income-producing assets: the shares in No. 22. They were productive of income $2,000; $3,082; $2,900; and $3,815 in the years of income ended 30 June 1978, 1980, 1981 and 1982 respectively. That income was disclosed as assessable income by the Applicant and was assessed against him. But the effect of s.260 in these circumstances is to strike down the entirety of the arrangement (Newton & Ors. v. F.C. of T. - ante). That being so the Applicant is to be assessed not only as if he had not incurred the outgoings of interest but also as if he had not derived the assessable income by way of dividends. For that reason the taxable income of the Applicant is to be reduced by the dividends received.

FRANDETTE

49. I find, in relation to Frandette, that the $150,000 was borrowed from Frandette at interest in order to be on-lent to Astralan at interest; that the moneys borrowed were so on-lent; that the documented agreements provided for the loans to extend over terms of forty years (less one day in the case of the loan to Astralan); that the total amount payable by Astralan by way of interest was to be $619,777; that the total amount payable by the Applicant to Frandette by way of interest was to be $605,526; and that, upon the evidence, the Applicant was to incur other expenses in relation to the borrowing from Frandette amounting to $1,307 by way of procuration fees and, in two years to date, liability for small amounts of interest payable to third parties. On the face of the transactions, over the full term, the Applicant would probably have a small surplus. However, the transaction is so wanting in commercial reality that I am not persuaded that it was the intention of the Applicant that the transaction should be performed according to the terms of the documents throughout a term of forty years. Further, I am not persuaded that in fact Astralan had, or was every thought to have had, a capacity, throughout thirty years and in particular in the thirtieth year, to have discharged its obligations or to have made satisfaction for its default in discharging them. I am not persuaded upon the evidence that either Frandette or Astralan had any other capacity than to ensure that a bank would enter into its records the deposit of particular sums to be immediately satisfied by the debiting of identical amounts.

50. Nonetheless, the fact remains that, unless s.260 applies, the outgoing by way of interest was incurred in relation to an income-producing undertaking. Furthermore, on one view the effect of the arrangement was merely to take advantage of opportunities seemingly available by paying an outgoing at the close of a fiscal year while only deriving assessable income to which that related in the year following. That being so, had no more been involved than that the Applicant as borrower had opted to pay interest in an earlier year than he was obliged to or had he merely deferred the derivation of interest until a later year, it is probable that that could not have been challenged. In this case the inflow and outflow of moneys is substantially tax-neutral in all years save two: the first year and the last year. But if the transactions be tax-effective the consequence is that the Applicant would be able to substantially reduce his tax liability in the first year by reason of matters which, as a matter of historical fact have occurred, at the price of the possibility, which I am not persuaded would ever have eventuated, of increasing his taxable income by a similar amount in thirty years time.

51. I have no hesitation in saying that, by the criteria which apply in relation to s.260 of the Act, the transaction in its entirety is void for the purposes of the Act. For the purposes of the Act, it is as if the transactions and all of their elements had never happened.

EXPLORATION EXPENDITURE

52. Having explored all the foregoing possibilities as to the exploitation of s.51 of the Act, on 30 June 1981 the Applicant took up a new interest: said to be exploration and prospecting in Australia for the purpose of discovering petroleum. His interest was sparked by s.124AH(1) of the Act which provided:-

"Subject to this section, expenditure incurred by the taxpayer during the year of income on exploration or prospecting in Australia for the purpose of discovering petroleum is an allowable deduction."

53. The Applicant claimed a deduction in the sum of $10,000 based upon an advance from funds under his control on 30 June 1981 of $2,000 and, probably, the execution of some documents. The documents to which he was said to have become a party provided for him to become a joint venturer with Australian Explorers (Petroleum and Minerals) Pty. Limited ("Explorers"); and for the borrowing of $8,000 from an unidentified third party on the security of a Bill of Sale and Possessory Lien, which amount was said to have been advanced on account of the Applicant to Explorers as part of his capital contribution to the joint venture. His claim is that, as a joint venturer he satisfied the requirement of S.124AH(1) that the claim be limited to expenditure "incurred by the taxpayer" and, by reason of the alleged subscription of $10,000 on 30 June 1981, he claims to have satisfied the requirement that the expenditure be "incurred" by him "during the year of income". At the same time he claims that it is appropriate that the Tribunal should be persuaded that the totality of the moneys advanced to the joint venture in his name that day ($10,000) constituted expenditure incurred that day "on exploration or prospecting in Australia for the purpose of discovering petroleum".

54. The evidence to support those propositions is at best woefully inadequate. I am satisfied that on 30 June 1981 the Applicant did pay $2,000 to the solicitor representing Explorers. I have no evidence as to the terms of any joint venture agreement and I have no evidence as to any loan agreement. The only evidence presented comprised an undated letter from the solicitor asserting (inter alia) that:-

"In accordance with your instructions, I did on the 30th day of June (sic) attend to settlement on your behalf and I would now confirm the transfer to you of 61/100,000 in the two claims comprising the Flinders Prospect and your contribution under the terms of the joint venture agreement was made to (Explorers), the operator under the Joint Venture and the Works Contractor, were effected at that time."

The letter referred to the $2,000 paid by the Applicant; and also to $8,000 being –

"Funds from advance pursuant to Bill of Sale and Possessory Lein (sic) paid to Australian Explorers."

56. There was also a letter from Explorers, which was also undated, acknowledging receipt of $10,000 on 30 June 1981 in relation to a 61/100,000 interest in the two claims described as "Coral Seas Mining Area - Flinders Prospect". An Agreement, in the form of a "Sub-contract for the Execution of Works" entered into by Explorers and a third party contractor, was produced. It also bore date 30 June 1981. However, it is of very little assistance to the Applicant. It only identifies the works to be the subject of the contract by reference to a "principal contract" not produced in evidence. It also provided that Explorers would pay to the contractor $50,000 on the signing of the Agreement.

57. But even if those problems did not exist, the principal obstacles to the Applicant's claim arise from the requirements of the section. Such evidence as he has produced by himself and his witness strongly points to the conclusion that the requirements of the section have not been, and could not have been, satisfied in the circumstances. In so far as a sub-contract for the execution of "works" related in some unspecified way to exploration and prospecting for petroleum were concerned, it only purports to have been entered into on 30 June 1981. It is inherently improbable that in what remained of that day any substantial works of exploration or prospecting were undertaken. However, that is not inconsistent with a view that the $50,000 which was to be paid that day was by way of a prepayment for services to be rendered. However, such evidence as was produced indicated that the works of exploration and prospecting had in large measure at least been carried out prior to 30 June 1981. So much appears from an account rendered that day to Explorers in the sum of $44,430. That account made claim for :-

"Geological Consultancy Fees to 26 June 1981

240.80 hours at $60 per hour $14,448.00

Out-of-Pocket Expenses Literature, Research

and Valuation 8,772.30

Boat Charter 6,000.00

Physical Survey Costs 4,615.21

Photographic Survey Costs 218.84

Travel, Fares and Accommodation 9,837.00

Administration 588.65

TOTAL $44,430.00"(sic)

58. Other evidence produced from the same source indicates that the contractor claimed further consultancy fees in the sum of $26,670 (exclusive of disbursements and expenses) in relation to the period 29 June 1981 to 12 February 1982 but no persuasive evidence was forthcoming to explain the relationships between the accounts or identify with any precision the services in relation to which the claims were made. The closing accounts rendered by the contractor at 12 February 1982 made claim in all to $151,828.29 to be offset against moneys which it had held as "deposit received $175,000". That account contained a single item "Legal (clearing) $76,670" but no evidence was offered to establish its relationship to the claim of the Applicant.

59. In summary, although I would be disposed in the interpretation of S.124AH of the Act to take a broad view of what constitutes "expenditure... on exploration or prospecting... for the purpose of discovering petroleum" and, similarly, so as not to deny eligibility for deductions to amounts pre-paid, nonetheless I am not persuaded that any amount was so expended by the Applicant during the year of income so as to satisfy the requirements of the sub-section. Further, I am not persuaded that there was any borrowing. In consequence, I am not persuaded that the expenditure claimable pursuant to the section on the part of the Applicant could have exceeded $2,000. Again, I am certainly of the view that his actions in subscribing capital to the joint venture on 30 June 1981 did not of themselves constitute "expenditure incurred by the taxpayer during the year of income on exploration or prospecting in Australia for the purpose of discovering petroleum. I conclude that the new enterprise was as unproductive as those which preceded it: it produced neither black gold nor tax deductions.

In relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1978, by reason of disallowing claims to deductions in relation to Vaucluse Partnership $19,996; and Herunda $74,750, the Commissioner imposed additional tax for incorrect return pursuant to the provisions of the then s.226 of the Act.
