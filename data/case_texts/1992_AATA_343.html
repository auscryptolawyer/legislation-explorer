Re Jacqueline Maureen Lockwood and Commissioner of Superannuation and Christine Mcmurray No: A91/81 Aat [1992] AATA 343 (6 November 1992)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JACQUELINE MAUREEN LOCKWOOD

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION and CHRISTINE

McMURRAY

No: A91/81

AAT No. 8360

Number of pages - 18

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRAIVE DIVISION

B.H. Burns(1) (Deputy President), H.G. Julian(1) (Member) and Dr D.B. Travers(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - spouse's pension - eligible employee - eligible children - definition of spouse - permanent and bona fide de facto relationship - continuous period of not less than three years - decision affirmed

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 s.37

Social Security Act, 1947

Superannuation Act, 1976 ss.3(1)(c), 82(2), 98, 109

Lambe v Director-General of Social Services [1981] FCA 171; (1981) 38 ALR 405

Re Tang and Director-General of Social Services (1981) 3 ALN N49 (Decision No. 476)

Lynam v Director-General of Social Security (1983) 1 AAR 197

Staunton-Smith v Secretary, Department of Social Security, [1991] FCA 513; (1991) 14 AAR 325

HEARING

CANBERRA

6:11:1992

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision of the delegate of 11 August 1989.

DECISION

This is an application to review a reviewable decision of a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation dated 11 August 1989, in which it was determined that for the purposes of s.98 of the Superannuation Act, 1976, ("the Act") (T3 p.13):

"(a) Kenneth William Lockwood was an "eligible employee" within the

meaning of subsection 3(1) of the Act who died before

attaining his maximum retiring age and who had a period of

prospective service of not less than one year;

(b) Felicity Anne Lockwood and Andrew Kenneth Lockwood are

"eligible children" within the meaning of subsection 3(1) of

the Act; and

(c) Kenneth William Lockwood is survived by Christine McMurray, who

is a "spouse" within the meaning of subsection 3(1) of the

Act and as a consequence orphan's pension is not payable to

Felicity Anne Lockwood and Andrew Kenneth Lockwood."

This decision was confirmed in a reconsideration of 2 May 1991 (T2 p 2). The Tribunal noted and the parties were in agreement that the reconsideration decision incorrectly referred to s.109 of the Act and the correct section was s.98.

2. The parties before the Tribunal consisted of Mrs Jacqueline M. Lockwood ("the applicant"), the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the respondent") and Mrs Christine McMurray who was joined as a party to these proceedings.

3. Mr McKeown of counsel represented the applicant. Ms Munoz of counsel represented the respondent and Mrs McMurray was represented by Mr Wilcox of counsel.

4. The Tribunal had before it the documentation lodged pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975, together with the other documentation tendered during the hearing, namely exhibits A1-A15, P1-P18 and C1. The applicant gave oral evidence and called Mrs Iris Lockwood and a Ms Lachlan to give evidence. Mrs McMurray also gave evidence and called seven witnesses, namely Mrs Skilbeck, Dr Shaw and Messrs Doyle, Mitchell, Dixon, Hamish McMurray and Kalnins. The Tribunal also has had regard to the oral and written submissions of the parties.

5. At the hearing it was conceded by all parties that the applicant's former husband, Kenneth William Lockwood ("Mr Lockwood") was an "eligible employee" within the meaning of s.3(1) of the Act, who died before attaining his maximum retiring age and who had a period of prospective service of not less than one year. It was further conceded that Felicity Anne Lockwood and Andrew Kenneth Lockwood (who are children of Kenneth William Lockwood and the applicant), are "eligible children" within the meaning of s.3(1) of the Act.

6. Section 98 of the Act sets out the criteria for payment of an orphan benefit. Not unnaturally one of the requirements is that the eligible employee "is not survived by a spouse but is survived by a person or persons who is or are his child or children". This requirement necessitates an examination of whether or not Mr Lockwood was survived by a "spouse". "Spouse" is variously defined in s.3 of the Act. Mrs McMurray claims that she is a "spouse" by virtue of paragraph (c) of that definition and that orphan benefit is not payable to the abovementioned children.

7. The relevant definition reads as follows:

"Spouse" in relation to a person who has died and was, at

the time of his death, an eligible employee or a retirement

pensioner means-

(c) a person who was not legally married to the

deceased person at the time of the person's death but

who, for a continuous period of not less than 3 years

immediately preceeding the person's death, had

ordinarily lived with the person as the person's

husband or wife as the case may be, on a permanent and

'bona fide' domestic basis"

It is agreed that at the time of his death, Mr Lockwood was an eligible employee and it is not in dispute that at the same time, Mrs McMurray was not legally married to Mr Lockwood and the Tribunal so finds.

8. The central issue as between the parties is whether or not Mrs McMurray, for a continuous period of not less than 3 years immediately preceeding Mr Lockwood's death, had ordinarily lived with him as his wife on a permanent and "bona fide" domestic basis.

9. The Tribunal indicates that it has had regard to the whole of the evidence before it, to the accuracy of the observations and memory of the respective witnesses and their demeanour and to all of the submissions placed before it. As indicated previously, eleven witnesses gave evidence on oath. Each was subjected to a thorough and searching cross-examination.

10. The Tribunal found Mrs McMurray to be a very impressive witness. She was honest, objective and did not exaggerate in giving her testimony. She was careful and thoughtful with her answers and was in our opinion a sound historian of the events in question. We accept her evidence and reject that which is at variance with it.

11. The Tribunal makes the following findings of fact based on the evidence which it has accepted. The applicant worked in the geography department at the Australian National University. Mr Lockwood was a technical officer in the same department. They married in 1980 and their daughter Felicity was born in that year on 15 June. In 1981 the applicant and Mr Lockwood separated and he commenced a sexual relationship with Mrs McMurray, a demographer in the department, whom he had known for some four years. Their relationship continued somewhat sporadically until the applicant and Mr Lockwood finally separated in April 1982. Felicity lived with the applicant. Mr Lockwood moved in with Mrs McMurray who sold her house and bought a 40 acre property ("Tarcoola") outside Canberra in early 1983. She with her children and Mr Lockwood, moved onto Tarcoola and began to rebuild the house there. Mr Lockwood sold his house.

12. In 1984 the applicant and Mr Lockwood divorced. He agreed to pay $35.00 per week maintenance for Felicity and Andrew, born in May 1983.

13. In October 1986, Mr Lockwood visited his parents in Adelaide. On the return journey to Canberra, he sustained head injuries in a car accident and died in hospital in Adelaide on 28 October 1986. Mrs McMurray applied for a spouse's benefit from the respondent on 3 November 1986 (T5 p 15). The applicant applied for orphan's benefits on behalf of Felicity and Andrew on 13 November 1986 (T6 p 17).

14. The relevant definition of "spouse" in s.3(1) of the Act is essentially similar to that of de facto spouse in the Social Security Act, 1947. The Tribunal in Re Tang and Director-General of Social Services (1981) 3 ALN N49 and the Federal Court in Lambe v Director-General of Social Services [1981] FCA 171; (1981) 38 ALR 405 interpreted the expression "bona fide domestic basis" to require consideration of all aspects of the particular relationship of the people in question. The applicant submitted that the Tribunal should look for factors which suggest that Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood were living separately and apart. To do so, in the opinion of the Tribunal, would place the wrong emphasis upon the task of the Tribunal. The task of the Tribunal is clearly to look for indicators that suggest whether for a continuous period of not less than 3 years immediately preceeding Mr Lockwood's death, Mrs McMurray had ordinarily lived with him as his wife on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis. That task requires the Tribunal to take into account all facets of the inter-personal relationship that existed between Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood in the 3 years immediately preceding the death of Mr Lockwood. The Tribunal acknowledges however, that facts which establish that a man and a woman are living "separately and apart" from each other would also be relevant in ascertaining whether they are living together in a "bona fide domestic basis". However, the above terms are not interchangeable as was pointed out by O'Loughlin J. in Staunton-Smith v Secretary, Department of Social Security [1991] FCA 513; (1991), 14 AAR 325 at 328.

15. In examining the relationship in question, the Tribunal adopts the approach taken in Tang (supra) and Lambe (supra). In so doing, the Tribunal would indicate that it is mindful in characterising the nature of the relationship under examination in these proceedings of the dangers associated with attempting to isolate individual factors and to attribute to them relative degrees of materiality or importance. In this respect, the Tribunal has had regard to the reasoning of Fitzgerald J. in Lynam v Director-General of Social Security (1983) 1 AAR 197 at 200 where in following the approach taken by the Full Court in Lambe (supra), His Honour said:

"Financial arrangements cannot be taken in isolation and

considered of particular importance in determining the

nature of relationship. Their materiality, like each of the

other elements of the relationship, stems from the impact

which they have as part of an overall situation. Each

element of a relationship draws its colour and its

significance from the other elements, some of which may

point in one direction and some in the other. What must be

looked at is the composite picture. Any attempt to isolate

individual factors and to attribute to them relative degrees

of materiality or importance involves a denial of common

experience and will almost inevitably be productive of

error. The endless scope for differences in human attitudes

and activities means that there will be an almost infinite

variety of combinations of circumstances which may fall for

consideration. In any particular case, it will be a

question of fact and degree, a jury question, whether a

relationship between two unrelated persons of the opposite

sex meets the statutory test."

With the above considerations in mind, the Tribunal now turns to a consideration of the material before it.

Permanence of Relationship

16. In examining the permanence or otherwise of the relationship in question, the Tribunal can see no reason to depart from the ordinary everyday meaning of the work "permanent" where it appears in the relevant definition of "spouse" in s.3(1) of the Act. The Tribunal adopts the meaning given in the Macquarie Dictionary, namely "lasting or intended to last indefinitely; remaining unchanged; not temporary; enduring; abiding".

17. The Tribunal makes the following findings of fact. In April 1982, Mr Lockwood moved out of his house in which he had been living with the applicant, and lived with Mrs McMurray and her two boys (then aged 7 and 9) in her house. On the day he moved in, Mrs McMurray gave Mr Lockwood the key to the house (transcript pp 87, 138 and A2 - her 1982 diary). They began to look for a property outside Canberra and at the end of 1982, put both their houses up for sale (transcript p 47). In early 1983 Mrs McMurray's house was sold. She and Mr Lockwood then found a suitable property ("Tarcoola") which she purchased from the proceeds of the sale of her house and her savings.

18. Mrs McMurray, her children and Mr Lockwood then lived at his house prior to moving on to Tarcoola in about May or June 1983. In late 1983 Mr Lockwood sold his house and on 25 November 1983, he and Mrs McMurray executed a Deed of Trust ("the agreement") (P6). In the agreement, Mr Lockwood as beneficiary paid to Mrs McMurray as trustee $40,000 to be spent on the development of Tarcoola which sum was paid into a joint bank account. The agreement also provided that Tarcoola would be occupied by the parties jointly. They held Tarcoola as tenants in common in shares of two thirds for Mrs McMurray and one third for Mr Lockwood. Outgoings, (e.g. rates) were to be shared according to these proportions and Mrs McMurray's obligation to repay the $40,000 to Mr Lockwood did not arise until 10 years after the date of the agreement.

19. Mrs McMurray regarded the agreement as a marriage contract. She and Mr Lockwood had decided together to enter into it "to protect both our interests" as the title to Tarcoola was in Mrs McMurray's name (transcript pp 98-99):

"Q. Was it at his instigation or yours that this deed was

entered into?

A. We regarded it in the nature of marriage contracts that

people sometimes draw up, which allocate separate interests

in property acquired before the date of the marriage.

Q. When you purchased the property, it was your

understanding that Ken would be also involved in the

property?

A. Yes.

Q. Why then do you put a 10 year restriction on the deed?

A. Forever is a very idealistic concept, Mr McKeown. We

knew that relationships needed time to be tested and to

develop. Both of us had been married twice before at that

stage."

20. Together, Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood developed the plans for renovating the house on Tarcoola. Mr Kalnins, a close friend of Mr Lockwood for 25 years drafted the plans for the renovation. He described how Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood worked together on the renovations (transcript p 219). Dr Shaw, an old friend and current colleague of Mrs McMurray in the department, similarly described the planning and renovations to the house. He observed that Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood also spoke of fencing and tree-planting on Tarcoola (transcript p 68).

21. On the permanency of the relationship, Dr Shaw said (transcript pp 67-68):

"Q. In terms of permanency of the relationship, from your

own observations or from anything that was said to you by

others, if need be, can you help us on that?

A. I think the best I can say is that among the friends I

know of Christine, they thought it was a very satisfactory

and outwardly implying that therefore a lasting

relationship. Let me say that among the friends that I

share with Christine there certainly were conversations

about how good the relationship was and 'aren't they a great

couple' and those sorts of conversations and a lot of this

revolved around doing the house or other other things.

Q. Was there any talk from others who knew Christine

McMurray and Mr Lockwood well along these lines, 'I can't

see it lasting' 'they've had other relationships' 'this is

just another one of a fleeting type' shall we say ?

A. No, I can't recall that."

22. Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood's plans for the future were to spend "increasing amounts of time at home on the property together", and they intended to reduce or cease their regular employment, she to write novels and he to spend more time on his hobbies (transcript pp 132-133). They intended to marry "as a statement of our relationship" (transcript pp 132, 135). Mr Kalnins recalled that the intending marriage had been discussed for "sort of two, three years", and Mr Lockwood had proposed to discuss it with his parents shortly before he died (transcript p 224). Mrs McMurray and her son, Hamish, gave evidence that Mr Lockwood had, in fact, telephoned his parents and told them of the plans (transcript pp 212-213).

23. The Tribunal accepts the evidence of Mr Kalnins, Dr Shaw and Hamish as reliable evidence from people who had known both Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood for a considerable time.

24. Mr Lockwood made a will in 1984 and left his considerable collection of tools, three of his cars and his personal effects to Mrs McMurray, and the residue of his estate to his daughter, Felicity (P3). The applicant contended that this evidenced his lack of commitment to the relationship. Further, the applicant argued that the agreement which effectively gave Mrs McMurray 10 years to repay the $40,000 to Mr Lockwood was for a finite period and therefore the parties did not see the relationship as permanent. Counsel for Mrs McMurray submitted that in the total context of the relationship between the parties, Mr Lockwood's will and the agreement were complimentary and evidenced their concern for each other and the relationship. The Tribunal accepts this submission as consistent with Mrs McMurray's evidence.

25. Finally, the applicant submitted that the relationship was, in fact, short-term and had ended after a row between Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood in December 1984. Mrs Iris Lockwood, mother of Mr Lockwood said that she and her husband had been staying at Tarcoola when the row occurred and Mr Lockwood told her that he would never sleep with Mrs McMurray again. Later he telephoned Mrs Iris Lockwood and told her that they had separated and that he was living separately from Mrs McMurray in another part of the house (transcript pp 251-252). She said that Mr Lockwood had been upset after he had signed the agreement and had told them of that in 1984 and 1985. He had also expressed unhappiness to her about his relationship with Mrs McMurray and said that he wanted to leave Tarcoola (transcript p 254). When he left Adelaide in October 1986, he said to her "well, don't you worry, only seven years to go" which she understood to be a reference to the agreement (transcript p 247).

26. The applicant tendered statutory declarations by Mr Lockwood's parents (P8, P10), and from his sister (P2) to support her contentions that Mr Lockwood's relationship with Mrs McMurray was not permanent and that he was unhappy in it.

27. The Tribunal has considered the above evidence advanced by the applicant and does not regard the testimony of Mrs Iris Lockwood as reliable and attaches little weight to the statutory declarations. The Tribunal was of the view that Mrs Iris Lockwood's evidence was coloured by her obvious disapproval of the relationship between her son and Mrs McMurray, and her concern for continuing access to her grandchildren who live with the applicant. In any event, evidence of reported unhappiness by a party to a relationship is not necessarily an indication of the true status of that relationship.

28. Mrs McMurray agreed that there had been a row between herself and Mr Lockwood and said that the cause of the row was disagreement between herself and Mrs Iris Lockwood. Mrs McMurray further agreed that Mr Lockwood had told his mother of a separation, but that this was to keep his parents happy (transcript pp 140, 141). Mrs McMurray said that her defacto relationship with Mr Lockwood did not end after the row - it in fact was improved by it (transcript pp 81, 115) and the Tribunal so finds. Messrs Shaw, Kalnins and Dixon (who had known both Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood since 1980) were of the view that the de facto relationship did not cease in December 1984, and we accept their evidence in preference to that of Mrs Iris Lockwood.

29. The Tribunal finds as a fact that the relationship between Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood was, in every sense, "permanent" within the meaning given to that word earlier in these reasons. It was so, both in the minds of the couple in question and in the opinion of the friends who knew them well. The Tribunal finds that the relationship commenced when Mr Lockwood came to live with Mrs McMurray in April 1982 and continued until his death some four and a half years later.

The legal rights of the parties to enforce obligations in respect of each other

30. The Tribunal finds that the parties clearly enjoyed legal rights to enforce obligations in respect of each other concerning the agreement and their financial arrangements, concerning the renovations to Tarcoola.

Sexual Relationship

31. The Tribunal finds that Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood had sexual relations from time to time in 1981 and 1982, until they began to live together. When they lived together, they slept together and continued to do so until Mr Lockwood's death. Friends of both Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood gave evidence that there had been a sexual relationship between the parties since 1982. Mrs Skilbeck, a friend of Mrs McMurray gave evidence of the sexual relationship in 1982 and its continuation until 1986. Mr Kalnins recalled Mr Lockwood and Dr Shaw recalled Mrs McMurray speaking about their sex life (transcript pp 220, 226-267). On camping trips, the parties had their own tent and shared a double air mattress (transcript pp 197, 208). The Tribunal accepts this evidence and finds that there was a sexual relationship between Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood which had its origins prior to April 1982, and which continued throughout the period they lived together, until Mr Lockwood's death.

Exclusiveness of Living Arrangements and Sexual Activity

32. The Tribunal finds that between the separation in 1982 and July 1986, Mr Lockwood visited the applicant on several occasions, had sexual intercourse with her and fathered Andrew. The applicant contended that behaviour of this nature, indicated a lack of an exclusive quality in the relationship between him and Mrs McMurray which meant they were not in a bona fide de facto relationship. Although Mrs McMurray was unaware of Mr Lockwood's sexual activities with the applicant, she regarded her relationship with him as exclusive (transcript p 82). Dr Shaw regarded the relationship as exclusive to Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood (transcript p 67).

33. The Tribunal in Re Tang did not consider that the sexual relationships between the parties and others affected the primacy of the sexual relationship between the parties, nor do we on the facts before us. The Tribunal finds that Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood lived together and slept together for four and a half years. When all evidence of the sexual relationship between Mr Lockwood and Mrs McMurray is considered, we find that their sexual relationship was the primary sexual relationship for the parties.

Pooling Resources and Sharing Expenses

34. The Tribunal makes the following findings. In 1981 Mrs McMurray deposited $2,000 in Mr Lockwood's building society account to give him a credit rating whereby he could borrow money to finance the property settlement with the applicant (transcript pp 48, 98). In 1983 Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood opened a joint bank account to pay contractors and assist in the renovations (transcript pp 78, 79, 133). Mr Lockwood put $40,000 from the proceeds of the sale of his house into this account.

35. Mr Lockwood and Mrs McMurray worked together on the renovations (transcript p 78):

"Q. Just tell us the sort of thing to begin with that you,

or that you did together?

A. We dug and prepared the footings, we laid the steel in

the footings, we concreted the footings, we set out the

brickwork, we hired bricklayers to do the actual

construction of the piers and the brick skirt around the

house. We worked on the timber floor ourselves, we hired

carpenters to construct the main shell of the house. We

were doing all the site works and labouring for all the

contractors who came on site. The carpenters put the roof

on and from then on we completed the construction ourselves

in all the lining, detailing, interior work and so on."

Mr Lockwood used his own tools to do this work.

36. Shopping was a joint activity and expenses of this and most bills were shared equally (transcript p 133). Mr Lockwood regularly picked up Mrs McMurray from the University and they travelled to Tarcoola together.

37. Mrs Iris Lockwood reported that Mr Lockwood had told her he was paying $30.00 per week board to Mrs McMurray. Mrs Iris Lockwood stated that when Mrs McMurray came to Adelaide, on the occasion of Mr Lockwood's death, Mrs McMurray told her that Mr Lockwood paid her $30.00 per week which was less than the maintenance that he paid for his children (transcript pp 254, 270). The applicant tendered affidavits to this effect from Mr and Mrs Lockwood (P8, P10, P11), from his sister (P7) and others present (P4, P11). Ms Lachlan, a friend of Mr Lockwood's sister agreed that she had been present and this was what was said (transcript p 147).

38. Mrs McMurray denied that she had told Mrs Iris Lockwood or anyone else on that occasion in Adelaide that Mr Lockwood was paying her board (transcript pp.108-108):

"The matter of the children was raised and Ken's

contribution towards their maintenance was raised by some of

the Lockwood relatives, and I volunteered that Ken had

always been very sad that although he'd paid maintenance for

the children, he had extremely limited access to them, and

that he'd said it was ridiculous because he was paying more

for those children than his own food cost.

Q. So he paid you $30 a week? From when?

A. It was just a contribution towards the food, that had to

go out. Sometimes he took it out of his pocket at the

supermarket. We had a ball park figure of $30 a week but he

often bought other things like wine, and various other

things. From when I can't exactly say, but whenever we went

out shopping he made a contribution and we had a ball park

figure that it was around $30 a week."

The Tribunal accepts the evidence of Mrs McMurray rather than that of Mrs Iris Lockwood, Ms Lachlan and the evidence in the affidavits. We find it more probable than not, that Mr Lockwood and Mrs McMurray shared expenses for food, and that Mr Lockwood did not pay board to Mrs McMurray. Further, the reliability of what Mr Lockwood told his mother is questionable, given the evidence that he believed he needed to placate her.

39. The applicant submitted that the agreement between Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood set out unequal shares in Tarcoola and that this was not indicative of an equal partnership, and that expenses were shared on a one third to two thirds basis. The Tribunal accepts the evidence of Mrs McMurray that apart from rates and other like outgoings, the parties shared expenses equally. Furthermore, the Tribunal is of the view that unequal shares in Tarcoola was entirely consistent with the pooling of resources between Mr Lockwood and Mrs McMurray in their relationship.

40. The applicant contended that Tarcoola being in the name of Mrs McMurray only, was not indicative of a bona fide de facto relationship. Further, Mr Wilcox had indicated the reason for maintaining the title in Mrs McMurray's name and entering into the agreement, was to equitably and clearly protect the financial interests of both parties. The Tribunal has considered all the evidence as to the pooling of resources and sharing expenses between the parties and does not believe that an equal sharing of any one thing is a necessity in a bona fide domestic relationship. To accept the applicant's contention would be contrary to the reality of the many variations which exist in bona fide de facto relationships. It is however, a relevant factor to be considered when assessing the nature of the relationship.

The Parties holding themselves out as Married

41. The Tribunal finds that Mrs McMurray was frequently called "Mrs Lockwood" and answered to that name. When Mr Lockwood was in hospital following his car accident it was she who was notified, and was addressed as "Mrs Lockwood". It was she who was in contact with the treating doctors, and she who authorised the life support systems to be turned off when the deceased was pronounced brain dead. Mrs McMurray negotiated with the undertaker about Mr Lockwood's cremation (which he had wanted) and funeral (which he had not wanted).

42. Dr Shaw observed that the parties held themselves out as husband and wife. Mr Lockwood would wait for her in the evenings to travel home from work together. Dr Shaw, his partner and many other people attended meals and dinner parties held by Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood as a couple (transcript p 67). They were, in his eyes, "a couple living together" (transcript p 53). Mrs McMurray also described how she and Mr Lockwood would entertain friends together as a couple, and would visit friends together (transcript pp 53, 95).

43. The department sent classes out to Tarcoola on excursions in 1983 and 1986 (transcript pp 58, 91) and the MG Car Club in 1985 held a rally picnic at "Ken and Chris' beautiful property Tarcoola" (transcript p 95, A10).

44. The applicant submitted that Mrs McMurray did not attend Mr Lockwood's funeral and appeared angry and not tearful when he died, and this showed that she was not his de facto spouse. Mrs McMurray said that she did not attend the funeral because Mr Lockwood had previously expressed a clear wish that she not do so were he to pre-decease her, and that she was respecting his wishes (transcript pp 83, 85, 120). Mrs McMurray said that she was most upset at Mr Lockwood's death, and cut off a lock of his hair as he lay dying which she still wears in a locket (transcript p 85). She said that she could not express her grief because she was "alone with strangers" and felt unsupported in Adelaide. Mr Dixon, Dr Shaw, Mrs Skilbeck and Hamish all reported Mrs McMurray grieving and being very upset on her return to Canberra. The Tribunal accepts Mrs McMurray's evidence and that of her friends and family on this matter and finds that she and Mr Lockwood held themselves out as a married couple from April 1982 until Mr Lockwood's death.

The Parties' Subjective belief that their relationship was like one of Husband and Wife

45. The Tribunal makes the following findings and observations. Tarcoola was where Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood lived. All their possessions were there. Mrs McMurray's son, Hamish, felt that Mr Lockwood was like a father to him (transcript p 210). He described the many activities that he was involved in with Mr Lockwood, including homework, fixing cars and a trip to the Bathurst car races (transcript pp 134, 206).

46. Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood shared activities such as attending car shows, riding horses, flying model aircraft. Although she was not a sailor, Mrs McMurray attended the sailing club as Mr Lockwood's partner (transcript p 133). Mr Lockwood spoke frequently of "Chris and me" to Messrs Dixon and Kalnins. Mrs McMurray often spoke of Mr Lockwood to other people at the department and would frequently have morning tea and lunch with him (transcript pp 53, 67).

47. The friends of both Mr Lockwood and Mrs McMurray who were called as witnesses, observed them expressing physical affection to each other (transcript pp 192, 195-198 and P6). Mr Dixon observed that they would frequently discuss the details of their professional work together (transcript p 198). Hamish described frequent camping trips with his brother, Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood as a family (transcript pp 206, 207). He said that they ate together and shared Christmas and birthdays (transcript pp 204, 209). Mrs McMurray said that she, Mr Lockwood and the children did things as a family (transcript p 134), and that she and he both paid for these activities.

48. Finally, Mrs McMurray grieved at Mr Lockwood's death, and the Tribunal is of the view that she is still deeply moved by it. She said that she has not yet removed his clothes from the wardrobe. Hamish said that Mr Lockwood's trophies were still on shelves in the house (transcript p 210).

49. The Tribunal, in accepting the above evidence, is satisfied and so finds that the subjective belief of Mrs McMurray and Mr Lockwood was that their relationship was like that of husband and wife.

Conclusion

50. The parties to this application are all agreed that there is one issue to be determined by the Tribunal in respect of the application, namely whether Mrs McMurray was the "spouse" of Mr Lockwood within the meaning of that word in s.3(1) of the Act. The Tribunal has taken into account all of the submissions made on behalf of the applicant, the respondent and Mrs McMurray, and has considered all the evidence before it. We find that Mrs McMurray had been living with Mr Lockwood for a continuous period of some four and a half years immediately preceeding his death, and had ordinarily lived with him as his wife on a permanent and bona fide domestic basis throughout this time.

51. The applicant submitted that if Mrs McMurray was found to be a "spouse", then her entitlement to spouse's benefit should date from 11 August 1989, the date of the reviewable decision (T3 p 13). The Tribunal would point out however, that the definition of "spouse" under s.3(1) of the Act is concerned with whether an eligible employee is survived by a spouse "at the time of his death". Mrs McMurray elected for a maximum pension under s.82 of the Act. The basis of a spouse's benefit under s.82(2) is as follows:

"(2) The annual rate of spouse's pension of a spouse to

whom this section applies is 67 per centum of the annual

rate of the invalidity pension to which the deceased

eligible employee would have been entitled under section 67

if he had not died, but had, on the day immediately

following the date of his death become entitled to

invalidity benefit in accordance with that section."

(underlining added)

The entitlement of Mrs McMurray to the spouse's benefit is therefore not affected by the date of the delegate's determination of 11 August 1989.

52. The decision of the Tribunal is that the decision of the delegate of 11 August 1989 (T3 p 13) is affirmed.
