WT86/2728-2729 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 440; (1987) 19 ATR 3073; 88 ATC 214 (9 December 1987)

Last Updated: 3 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax – Allowable deductions – Claim to deduct cost of repairs to third party vehicle damaged in collision after attending social function - Whether accident incurred in producing the assessable income - Contributions by State public servant to superannuation fund - Whether contribution a loss or outgoing in gaining or producing assessable income - Whether of a capital, private or domestic nature - Whether disability insurance component deductible - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936-1981 - sec 51(1)

Lodge v FC of T 72 ATC 4174

Halstead (HM Inspector of Taxes) v Condon (1970) 46 TC 289

Lunney v FC of T (1958) 100 CLR 478

FC of T v DP Smith 81 ATC 4114

APPLICANT

And:

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION Before: Dr P. Gerber

Date: 9 December 1987

Place: Perth

The claim in this case involves two issues.

  1. The applicant was at all relevant times the Deputy Director of a credit society involved in substantial financial dealings. In 1983, he attended a social function at which the guests were, in the main, stockbrokers and financial managers. It was submitted on the applicant's behalf that the opportunity to mix socially with such kindred spirits (no pun intended) enabled the applicant to make more informed commercial judgments. In the process of imbibing the state of the financial market, the applicant acquired a blood alcohol level of .08 and promptly collided with a utility when driving his motor vehicle on his way home. In the circumstances, his comprehensive insurance cover was vitiated and the applicant was compelled to compensate the owner of the utility in the amount of $3439. This amount was claimed as a deduction and disallowed.
  2. It was submitted on behalf of the applicant that the expenditure was incurred "while the taxpayer was in fact carrying out activities which were relevant to the gaining or producing his assessable income" and reliance was placed on Case T22 86 ATC 223.

4. For an expenditure to be allowable, it must be "incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income"; (cf sec 51(1)). However, the word "in" requires something more than a mere temporal connection: what must be looked to is the character of the expenditure; cf Mason J in Lodge v FC of T 72 ATC 4174, 4176 and the joint judgment of Williams, Kitto and Taylor JJ in Lunney v FC of T (1958) 100 CLR 478. I am satisfied that attending a social function, is - save in exceptional circumstances - an affair of a private or domestic nature and does not become part of the income-earning process merely because other guests share the taxpayer's professional interest any more than a butcher attending a butcher's picnic can be said to be involved in the performance of his duties: it has nothing to do with sausages or the price of meat; (cf Halstead (HM Inspector of Taxes) v Condon (1970) 46 TC 289). Case T22 involved a self-employed lawnmower contractor travelling in his van to a service station to refuel, when he was involved in a collision with another vehicle. Taxation Board of Review No. 2 concluded on the evidence that the taxpayer was undertaking activities which were part and parcel of his normal business operations and allowed the claim of the cost of repairing both vehicles.

5. Case T22 is a far cry from the facts of this case. For the reasons outlined above, this aspect of the claim must fail.

6. The other claim involves deductions of $1690, $1949, $2138 and $2739 for the years 1981, 1982, 1983 and 1984 respectively, being premium payments paid pursuant to a State superannuation scheme for the benefit of that State's public servants. The scheme provides benefits, inter alia, in the event of invalidity in service, retirement benefits from age 55 to 65 (when retirement is compulsory), death in service and spouse reversion.

  1. The Tribunal was ably assisted by the evidence of Mr Isaac Barton Griffith, an actuary of impeccable qualifications and a former State Actuary, who was able precisely to dissect the "invalidity in service" component of the annual contributions in the years now under review. His highly complex actuarial calculations are recorded in the transcript and will no doubt prove invaluable to any appeals tribunal if this matter should go further. Suffice it for present purposes that this witness presented me with the cost component of each of the various contingency factors which, collectively, are used in calculating the cost of the contributions to this particular scheme. The cost of the "invalidity in service" component was calculated at 15.36% of contributions. The witness was not shaken despite rigorous cross-examination. No countervailing evidence was called by the respondent. I have therefore no hesitation in accepting the witness' calculations.
  2. I am satisfied that the cost of obtaining cover against the loss or impairment of income earning capacity is deductible under the first limb of sec 51(1); cf FC of T v DP Smith 81 ATC 4114 and I have not been persuaded that the cost of this component is any less deductible because it is "mixed" in a lump sum with other expenditures of a non-deductible kind and/or has become "amalgamated". Provided that the cost can be calculated and identified, it is a loss or outgoing incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income. It would be merely re-discovering the wheel if I were to observe that apportionment has, since time immemorial, been built into section 51(1) of the Tax Act; cf Ronpibon Tin NL & Tonqkah Compound NL v. FC of T [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47. Much reliance was placed by the Crown on Case P130 82 ATC 651, decided by Board of Review No. 1 and involving a public servant who was required to contribute to a statutory superannuation fund which entitled him, depending on the circumstances, to either a pension or lump sum benefits; in the event of death, certain benefits accrued to his widow or dependants (if any). The attempt to claim the total amounts of contribution failed, predominantly on the ground that they were in respect of "a package of statutory rights", and thus of a capital nature; alternatively, even if such payments could be viewed as a contribution to the receipt of assessable income, they nevertheless were "instalments of the price of an annuity", and hence of an affair of capital. Again, because benefits became payable to the widow/dependants in the event of death in service, the expenditure was characterised as being of a private or domestic nature. One Member concluded that, being contributions to a fund which relates to a period after employment had ceased, the outgoing was not a loss or outgoing incurred in deriving present income. Impressive though these conclusions are, I have some difficulty in reconciling some of the observations in that case with the (earlier) decision of the Federal Court in Smith (supra). Be that as it may, I note that no attempt was made in that Case P130 to dissect the "invalidity in service" component. This is sufficient to distinguish that case from the one now before me. Case P130 was in turn followed by Board of Review No. 2 in Case 018 83 ATC 63. Again, the taxpayer in that case claimed the whole of the contribution to a superannuation fund and made no attempt to dissect the invalidity component.
  3. Of more relevance is the (unreported) decision Board of Review No. 2 (identified as Nos. M478-480 of 1984), heard in April 1986 in which the appellant was the present applicant. The transcript of the ex tempore decision of the Board was handed up for my guidance. It is fair to observe that the taxpayer in the earlier claims sought to deduct everything that wasn't nailed to the floor. The decision overflows with references to funny trusts, failures to make full disclosure, arguments on shams, as well as 260 other defences of the Commissioner's decisions on the various objections. It is therefore not surprising that amidst all these claims, the attempt to deduct the contributions to the superannution fund was treated by the Board almost as a jeu d' esprit and dismissed in one short paragraph:-

"On the question of the deductibility in terms of section 51(1) of the Act of the contributions to the superannuation fund, we are of the view that those payments are of a capital nature. It seems to us that the taxpayer, as a member of the fund, acquired a variety of rights and that the amounts contributed represent the price paid for the collection of benefits flowing from those rights. In our opinion the taxpayer was contributing in respect of a package of statutory rights and on that basis we regard those payments as being no different from any other capital investment that he may have chosen to make at that time. Because the payments are of a capital nature, no deduction is permitted in terms of the section."

I have quoted the Board's decision on this aspect of the claim in full because the taxpayer on that occasion tendered a report by Mr Isaac Barton Griffith which had been commissioned by the State. This report, which deals with actuarial valuations as at 30 June 1985, was also made an exhibit in this case. Unlike today, Mr Griffith was not called on the previous occasion. I do not know whether or not it would have been possible to dissect the "invalidity in service" component from the mass of actuarial calculations in the report, but I suspect that this kind of enlightenment is vouchsafed only to the closed brotherhood of the Ancient Guild of Actuaries. What is certain is that it is not the function of a tribunal to attempt its own calculations. It is, of course, trite to point out that the onus of proof is on every applicant who seeks a review of an administrative decision.

10. Undeterred, this applicant has once again claimed the full amount of his contributions to the fund, and it was only as a result of my prompting that evidence emerged from which the deductible component of the premiums could be calculated. For the reasons outlined before, I have concluded that the cost of obtaining what has been referred to as "invalidity insurance" cover is an allowable deduction.

11. In the result, the decision on the objections is varied by applying the 15.36% formula to the claimed contributions to the fund. This has the effect of reducing the applicant's taxable income in the years 1981-1984 inclusive as follows:-

1981 1982 1983 1984

$260 $299 $328 $421

This is to certify that this and the preceding 6 pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons for Decision of Dr P. Gerber, Senior Member in matter Nos WTB6/2728, WT86/2729, WT86/2767, WT86/2800.
