Albadeen and Tax Practitioners Board [2026] ARTA 990 (4 June 2026)

Decision: The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

Statement made on 02 June 2026 at 1:54pm

Statement of Reasons

INTRODUCTOIN

  1. Mr Albadeen (Applicant), applied to the Tax Practitioners Board (Board or Respondent) for registration as a tax agent. Mr Albadeen contended he satisfied the requirements for registration, including those found in clause 203 of Schedule 2 of the Tax Agent Services Regulations 2022 (Cth) (TASR).
  2. The Respondent rejected the application. This was because the Board was not satisfied Mr Albadeen had completed a course in commercial law as required by clause 203(c) of Schedule 2 of the TASR and had not undertaken relevant experience as required by clause 201(d) of Schedule 2 of the TASR. Mr Albadeen seeks a review of the Respondent’s decision.

BACKGROUND

  1. On 9 August 2024, Mr Albadeen applied for registration as a tax agent.[1] In addition to the material accompanying the application for registration, further evidence was submitted by the Applicant in November 2024, at the request of the Respondent.[2]
  2. In December 2024, the Respondent advised the Applicant that all the information submitted would be provided in full to the Conduct Committee for consideration.
  3. On 5 December 2024, the Board found that Mr Albadeen:[3]
    • met the requirements under clause 203(a) in Schedule 2 of the TASR, as he had been awarded a diploma or higher award in accounting from a registered training organisation or an equivalent institution;
    • met the requirements under clause 203(b) of Schedule 2 of the TASR, as he had successfully completed a course in Australian taxation approved by the Board;
    • did not meet the requirements under clause 203(c) of Schedule 2 of the TASR, as he had not successfully completed a course in commercial law approved by the Board;
    • did not meet the requirements under clause 203(d) of Schedule 2 of the TASR, as he had not undertaken at least 2 years of full time relevant experience (or part time equivalent) in the last 5 years; and
    • resolved to reject the Applicant’s registration application pursuant to s. 20-25(1) of the Tax Agent Services Act 2009 (TASA) on the basis that Mr Albadeen failed to satisfy the Board that he met the requirements prescribed by the regulations relating to qualifications and experience, and therefore could not be considered eligible for registration as an individual tax agent pursuant to s. 20-5(1)(b) of the TASA.
  4. The Respondent informed the Applicant of the decision by letter dated 22 January 2025.[4]
  5. On 2 February 2025, Mr Albadeen filed an application with the Tribunal seeking review of the Respondent’s decision.[5]
  6. In October 2025, Mr Albadeen advised the Board that he had successfully completed ‘Commercial Law for Tax Agents Skill Set FN SSS00005’ and provided a certificate and a record of results issued by the National Business Institute of Australia on 24 October 2025.[6] The units completed included a course in commercial law approved by the Respondent as required under clause 203(c) of Schedule 2 of the TASR.
  7. On 20 November 2025, the Tribunal made directions timetabling the application for review to hearing. The Respondent was permitted, in accordance with those directions, to file an Amended Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions. That direction was made specifically to allow the Respondent to strike out the commercial law requirement element of their case.
  8. The Amended Statement of Facts Issues and Contentions was filed by the Respondent on 4 December 2025. Following an extension, the Respondent filed a written outline of submissions on 3 February 2026. Mr Albadeen filed his written outline of submissions on 19 February 2026.
  9. On 25 February 2026, the Respondent made an application to the Tribunal seeking remittal of the application for review back to the decision maker for reconsideration pursuant to s. 85(1) of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth). The Respondent considered the decision, in so far as it related the issue of relevant experience, was unsupported by adequate reasons. Remittal would allow the Applicant to put any new evidence or information to Board and if the application was still to be rejected, provide more fulsome reasons.
  10. The remittal application was made shortly before the matter was due to proceed to hearing. Mr Albadeen opposed the application and expressed a clear preference for the Tribunal to determine his entitlement to registration, as in his view, remittal would cause unnecessary delay and prejudice.
  11. During an interlocutory hearing, the parties respective positions were explored in greater detail. Rather than remitting the decision for reconsideration or refusing the remittal application, the Tribunal vacated the hearing date and relisted it on a later date, with directions providing the Applicant additional opportunity to furnish further information and evidence concerning his relevant experience. The Respondent’s remittal application was withdrawn.
  12. On 30 March 2026, a directions hearing took place so the Tribunal could determine if the proceedings had resolved or would continue. The Tribunal was advised the parties had failed to resolve the matter. In that context, the Applicant was permitted to file any further evidence upon which he sought to rely and he did so on 1 April 2026.

    • The Supplementary Tribunal documents;[8]
    • Applicant’s Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions;

Before the Tribunal, was a signed statement of relevant experience (SRE) declared by Mr McNamara.[11]

The matters contained within the SRE form are those considered relevant to determining the sufficiency of the supervised experience, including, the details of the number and type of returns prepared by the applicant under supervision or control of the registered tax agent.

In circumstances where the Board concedes Mr Albadeen has completed 2,254 hours of relevant experience as defined by clause 212(c) of Schedule 2 of the TASR (being work under the supervision and control of a registered tax agent), it was not necessary for Mr McNamara to provide a witness statement or to give evidence.
  2. Mr Albadeen has been registered as an individual BAS agent since 22 October 2019.[12] In his application, he also relied upon work undertaken in this capacity as relevant experience of work of another kind approved by the Board within the meaning of clause 212 (f) of Schedule 2 of the TASR.

As a registered BAS agent, he was competent to provide BAS services without supervision.
  2. In his oral evidence, Mr Albadeen expanded on his experience as a BAS agent and why it should be considered relevant experience of work of another kind. He advised:

    • In 2021, he decided to start his own business. His office hours are generally 9:30am to 4:30pm five days per week.     • As a registered BAS agent, he has accumulated more than 4000 hours of experience. About 50% of his work relates to BAS agent services.

Further, apart from a client list, Mr Albadeen has not provided any documents evidencing BAS agent services or a statement from any clients detailing the nature and extent of the BAS services supplied.

THE RELEVANT LAW

  1. Subsection 20-5(1) of the TASA prescribes the requirements for a person to be eligible for registration as a tax agent as follows:
    20-5 Eligibility for registration as registered tax agent or BAS agent

    Individuals

    (1) An individual, aged 18 years or more, is eligible for registration as a *registered tax agent or BAS agent if the Board is satisfied that:

    (a) the individual is a fit and proper person; and

    (b) the individual meets the requirements prescribed by the regulations (including, but not limited to, requirements relating to qualifications and experience) in respect of registration as a registered tax agent or BAS agent; and

    (c) in the case of registration which is not a renewal—the individual maintains, or will be able to maintain, professional indemnity insurance that meets the Board’s requirements; and

    ...

  2. Section 20-25 of the TASA states:
    20-25 Registration

    Grant of application for registration

    (1) If you have applied to the Board for a type of registration, the Board must grant your application if you are eligible for registration of that type. Otherwise, the Board must reject your application.

    (2) The Board must decide your application within 4 months of receiving it.

    (3) If the Board does not decide your application within 4 months of receiving it, the Board is taken to have rejected your application. However, this does not apply if your application is for renewal of your registration.

  3. As is evident from s. 20-25(1) of the TASA, the Board must reject the application if the eligibility requirements of the TASA and the TASR are not met.
  4. Schedule 2 of the TASR prescribes requirements applicable to individuals seeking registration. In respect of individuals seeking registration on the basis they hold a diploma or higher award, clause 203 of Schedule 2 of the TASR provides:
    Diploma or higher award

    203 This clause applies if the individual:

    (a) has been awarded a diploma or higher award in accounting from a registered training organisation or an equivalent institution; and

    (b) has successfully completed a course in Australian taxation law that is approved by the Board; and

    (c) has successfully completed a course in commercial law that is approved by the Board; and

    (d) has undertaken at least 2 years of full time relevant experience (or part time equivalent) in the last 5 years

    Note 1: The Board may approve a course by an approval process, an accreditation scheme, or by other means

  5. The expressions full time and part-time equivalent are not defined in the legislation. Relevant experience is defined in clause 212 of Schedule 2 of the TASR as follows:
    relevant experience means work by an individual:

    (a) as a registered tax agent; or

    (b) as a tax agent registered under Part VIIA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 as in force immediately before 1 March 2010; or

    (c) under the supervision and control of a registered tax agent; or

    (d) under the supervision and control of a tax agent registered under Part VIIA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 as in force immediately before 1 March 2010; or

    (e) as an Australian legal practitioner; or

    (f) of another kind approved by the Board;

    that includes substantial involvement in the provision of one or more of the types of tax agent services described in section 90-5 of the Act, or substantial involvement in the practice of a particular area of taxation law to which one or more of those types of tax agent services relate.

    Note: The terms registered tax agent and tax agent service are defined in the Act.

  6. Relevant experience can include work of another kind approved by the Board, which experience must include substantial involvement in the provision of tax agent services described in s. 90-5 of the TASA ,or in a particular area of taxation law to which such services relate.
  7. In this regard, s. 90-5 of the TASA defines tax agent services as follows:
    90-5 Meaning of tax agent service

    (1) A tax agent service is any service:

    (a) that relates to:

    (i) ascertaining liabilities, obligations or entitlements of an entity that arise, or could arise, under a *taxation law; or

    (ii) advising an entity about liabilities, obligations or entitlements of the entity or another entity that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law; or

    (iii) representing an entity in their dealings with the Commissioner; and

    (b) that is provided in circumstances where the entity can reasonably be expected to rely on the service for either or both of the following purposes:

    (i) to satisfy liabilities or obligations that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law;

    (ii) to claim entitlements that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law.

    (2) A service specified in the regulations for the purposes of this subsection is not a tax agent service.

    Note: For specification by class, see subsection 13(3) of the Legislation Act 2003.

THE ISSUES

  1. The Tribunal’s task is to determine whether the Respondent’s decision to reject the Applicant’s application for registration as a tax agent is the correct or preferable decision on the material before the Tribunal.
  2. In undertaking this task, the Tribunal is not limited to evidence available to the Respondent at the time of the reviewable decision but should take into account all relevant evidence available to it at the time of the hearing.[13] By extension, the Tribunal is able to consider new or additional grounds to those identified in the reasons for decision, subject to procedural fairness considerations.
  3. In the circumstances of this case, the Tribunal must determine whether Mr Albadeen meets the requirements under s. 20- 5(1)(b) of the TASA and is therefore eligible for registration as a tax agent pursuant to s. 20-5(1) of the TASA .
  4. In issue is whether the Applicant has undertaken at least two years of full-time relevant experience (or part time equivalent) in the last five years, as required under clause 203(d) of Schedule 2 to the TASR. More specifically, whether Mr Albadeen’s work as a BAS agent totalling 826 hours is relevant experience of another kind that includes substantial involvement in the provision of one or more of the types of tax agent services within the meaning of clause 212(f) of Schedule 2 of the TASR.

CONSIDERATION OF THE ISSUES

  1. In accordance with the legislative scheme, an individual is entitled to registration as a tax agent only if the requirements prescribed by the provisions of the TASA and the TASR are satisfied. Relevantly, it is incumbent upon an applicant to demonstrate the requisite relevant experience for the purposes of clause 203(d) of Schedule 2 of the TASR. If the eligibility requirements are not met, the Board must reject the registration application.
  2. The Respondent accepts Mr Albadeen has undertaken 2254 hours of relevant experience under clause 212(c) of the TASR. The Applicant did not argue that he had subsequently accumulated additional supervised tax agent hours nor was there any updated evidence demonstrating that the shortfall of 826 hours had been overcome through supervised practice. Mr Albadeen’s case was directed entirely to the proposition that his work as a registered BAS agent should be treated as relevant experience of another kind approved by the Board. Accordingly, the outcome of the review turns on whether the Applicant’s work as a BAS agent can be accepted as constituting relevant experience under clause 212(f) of Schedule 2 of the TASR.
  3. Mr Albadeen submitted the Board had effectively accepted that the original decision was not the correct or preferable decision when it brought the remittal application. Mr Albadeen contended that the Tribunal should similarly conclude that the decision was incorrect because of that admission. I do not accept that submission. The Tribunal must determine the review on the evidence now available to it, and the Applicant was afforded an opportunity to provide evidence up until only a few days before the hearing to establish he satisfied the statutory requirements, including the requirement of substantial involvement in the provision of one or more tax agent services as described in the TASA.
  4. As to what type of work constitutes relevant experience, it must be work that includes substantial involvement in the provision of tax agent services or the practice of a particular area of taxation law relating to such services. That is the work must:
    (a) relate to:
    (i) ascertaining liabilities, obligations or entitlements of an entity that arise, or could arise, under a *taxation law; or

    (ii) advising an entity about liabilities, obligations or entitlements of the entity or another entity that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law; or

    (iii) representing an entity in their dealings with the Commissioner; and

    (b) be provided in circumstances where the entity can reasonably be expected to rely on the service for either or both of the following purposes:

    (i) to satisfy liabilities or obligations that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law;

    (ii) to claim entitlements that arise, or could arise, under a taxation law.

  5. Alternatively, the work must be in a particular area of taxation law relating to such work.
  6. In Dennis and Tax Practitioners Board [14] (Dennis), the Tribunal recently considered the interpretation of the expression ‘substantially involved in the provision of one or more types of tax services.’ Senior Member Harrowell opined that the expression must be construed in the context of the statutory scheme and the purposes of the TASA, which regulates those who provide tax agent services and requires that registered tax agents possess the necessary qualifications and experience. In that context, the requirement is not satisfied merely by demonstrating that a significant amount of time is spent undertaking tax agent services. It requires consideration of whether the Applicants involvement in the provision of one of the categories of tax agent services was substantial in a qualitative sense.
  7. I endorse and adopt the approach taken in Dennis. Whether an individual has been substantially involved in the provision of one or more of the types of tax services as described in s. 90-5 of the TASA, requires an assessment of the nature and extent of their involvement in the relevant service, separate from the amount of time spent in the task. This interpretation is consistent with the approach adopted by the court, where the expression substantial, albeit in a different context, has been held to mean more than trivial, minimal or nominal and instead denotes involvement that is real, meaningful or of substance, when viewed in light of the relevant statutory context.[15] Accordingly, the question is whether the Applicant's involvement in the provision of one or more types of tax agent services is substantial having regard to the purpose and operation of the legislative scheme as a whole.
  8. Mr Albadeen submitted that the Respondent’s position was premised upon demonstrating that the tax agent services provided required a degree of complexity. I do not accept that this accurately characterises the Respondent’s case. However, I agree with the Applicant’s submission that the legislative scheme does not impose any condition of complexity in the relevant experience requirement. Rather, the focus is on whether the nature and extent of an applicant’s involvement in the provision of the relevant tax service was substantial.
  9. In the present case there is no dispute that Mr Albadeen has completed at least 826 hours of work as a registered BAS agent within the past five years. The Board’s position was not directed to the quantitative aspect of the Applicant’s relevant experience but to the qualitative.
  10. The Tribunal accepts Mr Albadeen has been registered as a BAS agent since October 2019 and has established his own business. However, that fact does not, of itself, establish the Applicant has demonstrated relevant experience for registration as a tax agent. The legislative regime draws a distinction between registration as a BAS agent and registration as a tax agent. Although there may be some overlap between the services capable of being provided by persons registered in those respective capacities, they are distinct forms of registration subject to different requirements. If the relevant requirements required for registration as a BAS agent were necessarily equivalent to the relevant requirements for registration as a tax agent, there would be little utility in the legislative scheme prescribing separate registration categories.
  11. Accordingly, the fact that Mr Albadeen has lawfully provided BAS agent services or has been recognised as competent to do so through his BAS agent registration, does not resolve the question presently before the Tribunal, being whether the evidence established that he has undertaken work which includes substantial involvement in the provision of a tax agent service within the meaning of Schedule 2 of the TASR.
  12. Whether an individual's work involved substantial involvement in the provision of tax agent services cannot be assessed in the abstract. It requires evidence of the services actually provided, enabling consideration of whether those services correspond with the types of tax agent services described in s. 90- 5 of the TASA and whether the evidence demonstrates meaningful involvement in the provision of those services.
  13. The SRE completed by Mr Albadeen establishes the number of BAS and IAS prepared and lodged by the Applicant over the relevant period. However, it does not identify the tasks performed in preparing those statements, whether Mr Albadeen undertook work involving the ascertainment of liabilities, obligations, or entitlements under a taxation law, the extent to which the Applicant was required to exercise professional judgement or any dealings with the Tax Office. Nor does it demonstrate whether Mr Albadeen provided advice or otherwise engaged in activities which taxpayers could reasonably be expected to rely. In those circumstances, evidence of the preparation of BAS statements, without more, does not establish substantial involvement in the provision of a tax agent service, as opposed to involvement that may have been administrative, clerical, or otherwise peripheral in nature.
  14. Further, Mr Albadeen’s response to question 8 in the SRE, which invited him to provide any other information or comments regarding work of another kind that may be considered relevant experience, was similarly of limited assistance. The response noted that the Applicant had been registered as a BAS agent since 2019 and referred generally to reconciling BAS and lodging statements. However, it was largely broad and conclusory assertions regarding the work undertaken, rather than particulars of the services actually performed. The response did not provide sufficient detail to enable an assessment of whether Mr Albadeen had undertaken services of a kind corresponding to the tax agent services described in the TASA.
  15. Mr Albadeen rejected the suggestion that the work he undertook was routine and administrative in nature, submitting that the preparation and lodgement of BAS and IAS require some understanding of the GST system and is not something that just anyone can do. Although I accept that the Tribunal can infer a degree of knowledge, that is not the issue. The question is whether the evidence positively demonstrates that the Applicant's involvement in the provision of the relevant tax agent services was substantial within the meaning of the statutory scheme.
  16. The Applicant did not file a witness statement[16] setting out the details of the relevant experience upon which he relied. Nevertheless, the Tribunal afforded him an opportunity to give oral evidence. In particular, the Tribunal invited Mr Albadeen to explain in detail the work he performed as a BAS agent, including the nature of the services provided, the types of clients for whom those services were performed, and examples of the tasks he undertook. Mr Aberdeen was also invited to identify any documents in the evidence that may assist in demonstrating the nature and extent of his involvement in the provision of tax services.
  17. Mr Albadeen in his oral evidence stated that he provided advice to clients, ascertained their GST, PAYG, FBT and SGC liabilities and dealt with the Tax Office on their behalf. However, the evidence was brief and did not descend into any meaningful level of detail. Specific examples of client matters, the issues considered, the advice provided, the reasoning applied or the nature of the Applicant’s involvement were absent. The Tribunal does not suggest that the Applicant was required to identify clients by name or disclose confidential information. However, some evidence of the substance of the work undertaken was necessary to enable an assessment of whether the relevant experience satisfied the legislative requirement. The evidence remained largely at the level of broad assertion that certain work had been performed and advice given.
  18. Mr Albadeen was also unable to direct the Tribunal to any documents in the evidence which may corroborate those assertions. He suggested that aspects could be independently verified through records held by the Australian Taxation Office, but no such material was produced. In any event, it was incumbent upon the Applicant to adduce evidence sufficient to establish the factual basis for his claim. The Tribunal’s role is to determine the matter on the evidence before it, not to undertake investigations on behalf of a party.

  1. It is notable that the Respondent did not seek to cross examine the Applicant. This reflects the fact that the evidence was expressed at such a level of generality that there was little of substance which required testing. The difficulty was not that Mr Albadeen’s evidence was disputed. The difficulty was that the evidence taken its highest did not provide a sufficient foundation for the findings the Applicant asked the Tribunal to make.
  2. I am not satisfied that work by Mr Albadeen as a BAS agent included substantial involvement in the provision of one or more types tax agent services as described in s. 90-5 of the TASA. While I accept Mr Albadeen is a registered BAS agent, there is no evidence as to the specific tasks undertaken including the extent to which they involved:
    • ascertaining liabilities, obligations or entitlements under a taxation law;
    • advising an entity about liabilities obligations or entitlements of the entity that could arise under a taxation law ;or
    • representing an entity in their dealings with the Commissioner; and
    • the services were provided in circumstances where an entity can reasonably be expected to rely on the service.
  3. This is principally because apart from Mr Albadeen’s broad assertions, there was no independent evidence capable of substantiating or corroborating the character and extent of the work performed. Evidence capable of addressing that issue might include records of advice provided to clients, file notes, correspondence, emails, or a statement from a client or colleague. The Tribunal does not suggest that any particular form of evidence was required, just that some evidentiary foundation was necessary to complete an assessment of whether the work performed involved the requisite degree of involvement in tax agent services.
  4. The Tribunal accepts that Mr Albadeen was unrepresented and that circumstance informed the manner in which the hearing was conducted. While an unrepresented litigant may require procedural accommodation, the Tribunal cannot relieve a party of the obligation to establish the factual matters upon which their review depends. Nor does procedural fairness require the Respondent of the Tribunal to identify every category of evidence that might be relevant or otherwise assume responsibility for the Applicant’s case.
  5. Mr Albadeen argued during the hearing that the Respondent had not been sufficiently prescriptive in identifying the evidence required to establish his claim and suggested that this hindered his ability to present his case. The Tribunal does not accept that submission. The Board’s outline of written submissions explicitly stated[17] that the Applicant had failed to discharge his onus of demonstrating to the requisite level, his BAS agent experience and how it substantially involved one more types of tax agent services, including that it was unsupported by any secondary evidence. The Applicant was given at least two opportunities to file further evidence with the Tribunal after receiving the Respondent’s submissions. The Tribunal is satisfied that the Applicant has had a fair opportunity to present his case.
  6. The Tribunal notes the Respondent provides public guidance for applicants seeking registration as a tax agent.[18] The Tribunal accepts that their Respondent’s published guidance does not itself prescribe the legal requirements for registration and cannot alter the statutory criteria. However, the guidance is relevant in so far as that it identifies the types of evidence that may assist an applicant in establishing the relevant experience requirement and negates the assertion that the Respondent has never explained to Mr Albadeen the evidence they (and by extension the Tribunal) may have regard to in the review.
  7. Relevantly, the guidance concerning the relevant experience requirement provides:
    If your experience includes 'work of another kind' you must explain the reasons why this should be approved as 'relevant experience' in the relevant section of the application form. You must also provide independent verification of your experience.
  8. Independent verification is said to ‘include a reference from a registered tax agent or 2 separate references from individuals who are able to verify the type and amount of your experience. These individuals may include colleagues or clients. Independent verification must not be provided by family members.’ The purpose of such material is plainly to assist the decision maker in evaluating both the quantitative and qualitative character of the experience relied upon.

CONCLUSION

  1. The Tribunal finds in respect of the 826 hours of BAS agent experience, the evidence does not establish the nature, character and extent of the work undertaken in the provision of those services and therefore the Tribunal is unable to conclude that work of another kind includes substantial involvement in the provision of relevant tax service.
  2. Therefore Mr Albadeen:
    • Does not meet the requirements under clause 212(f) of Schedule 2 of the TASR as he has failed to demonstrate that work undertaken as a BAS agent included substantial involvement in the provision of one or more types of tax agent services as described in s. 90-5 of the TASA; and
    • Does meet the requirements under clause 203(d) of Schedule 2 of the TASR, as he has failed to demonstrate that he has undertaken at least 2 years of full time relevant experience (or part time equivalent) in the last 5 years; and
    • His application for registration as a tax agent must be rejected pursuant to s. 20-25(1) of the TASA on the basis that Mr Albadeen does not meet the requirements prescribed by the regulations relating to relevant experience, and therefore cannot be considered eligible for registration as an individual tax agent pursuant to s. 20-5(1)(b) of the TASA.
    • Accordingly, the decision of the Respondent to refuse the Applicant’s registration as a tax agent is the correct and preferable decision.

DECISION

  1. The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.
