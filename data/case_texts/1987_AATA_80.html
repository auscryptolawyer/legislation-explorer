NT86/9318-19 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 80; (1987) 18 ATR 3291; 87 ATC 367 (6 March 1987)

Last Updated: 14 July 2009

Re: TAXATION APPEALS (No. 102)

No. NT86/9318 -19

AAT Decision No. 3278

Income Tax



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member) (1)



HRNG



CANBERRA

#DATE 6:3:1987



JUDGE1



In these references the Applicant company (TCO) claims deductions pursuant to section 51 of the Income Tax Assessment Act ('the Act') in sums of $29,560 and $1,968 in the years of income ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 respectively. It is common ground that in those years TCO was carrying on business as a caravan-park operator on a coastal site. The monies claimed as deductions were expended on the construction of a toilet block; a substantial staircase upon an embankment; barbeque facilities in an area at the foot of that staircase; and a further staircase leading from that area down to the ocean beach. Those structures ("the facilities") were all erected on land adjoining the site of the caravan-park which had been leased by TCO from the State Minister for Lands. The works were carried out pursuant to a related agreement between the Minister, the Shire and TCO.



2. A caravan-park had been conducted in the area from the 1940's and from time to time doubts had arisen as to the continuance of park operations due to public pressure to have coastal lands open to the public. In 1977, the owner and operator of the business and lessee of the site was a company, ACO. ACO was operated as a family company under the direction of A. T was tax agent to ACO and generally advised A in relation to the business.



3. By 1977 A was willing to retire from the business. In order to make the business more attractive to prospective purchasers A, with the assistance of T, set about negotiating a longer tenancy of the site. At about the same time A caused a new company (TCO) to be incorporated. A and his wife became the only shareholders and T became the secretary and public officer of TCO.



4. Early in 1977 agreement was tentatively reached with the Crown for a new lease. ACO was to surrender its existing lease; TCO was to become the new lessee for a 21-year term; pending the new lease, the rent payable by ACO was to be increased from approximately $2,200 p.a. to $6,880 p.a. - the

expectation being that the new lease would commence from 1 July 1987; that the leased area would be reduced leaving the northern portion of the former site open to the public soas to provide access to the beach; and the new lessee (TCO) would be obliged to construct on public lands the facilities previously mentioned.



5. ACO continued to operate the business under the direction of A. Somewhat about this time T became interested in acquiring control of the business through a company which would be under his control.



6. On 2 November 1977, the Minister for Lands entered into a Special Lease of the foreshortened area to TCO for a term of 21-years commencing 1 July 1977 (sic). The annual rental was to be $21,000 p.a. for the first 7 years and there after to be subject to review. By contemporaneous agreement made between the Minister, the Shire and TCO, TCO assumed obligations:-



a) to construct the facilities aforementioned; and



b) to maintain them to the satisfaction of the Shire

"for a period of 21 years from the date hereof (sic) or such further period in respect of which (the special lease) is extended". If TCO had failed to carry out its obligations, the Shire could have carried out the work and recovered the cost from TCO. A recital to the Deed provided:-



"WHEREAS in consideration of obtaining Special Lease . . . . . . . . . Land District . . . . . . . . being the land outlined in red on the plan attached hereto from the owner, and the reduction in rental attaching thereto, the lessee

has agreed to develop and maintain on behalf of the trustee the area of land outlined in blue on the plan attached hereto."



7. Although the lease was now held by TCO, the business continued to be carried on as previously by ACO. In March 1978 it was arranged - neither the documents nor the terms of the arrangements were evidenced before me - that T and his interests would acquire all the shares in TCO, and thereupon TCO would take possession of the site and commence the business operations carried on up to the date of completion by ACO. On 5 June 1978 those arrangements were brought to fruition: the trading activities of ACO as operator of the park ceased; and TCO commenced carrying out those operations. In the years of income ended 30 June 1982 and 1983 TCO incurred expenses in performing the construction obligations. They are the expenses in issue. Pursuant to the lease from the Minister, TCO also incurred - as it was obliged to - substantial expenses in connecting the buildings on the demised land to the sewerage service provided by the Shire.



8. The site operated by TCO comprised nearly 300 caravan sites. Three complexes providing toilet and other facilities had existed on the site from prior to 1977. Those facilities were only available to patrons of the caravan-park, access being only by use of a key provided by management. Those complexes included sets of male and female toilets, bathrooms and showers; and also laundries and dishwashing facilities. By contrast, the toilet block on public land provided only toilet and cold water facilities which were open for use by the public. Barbeque facilities were provided within the park to a somewhat higher standard to those on the public land. T, who gave evidence, acknowledged that from time to time some park patrons would have used the facilities on public land and that sometimes members of the public would use barbeque facilities - but not other facilities constructed on the site.



9. To explain the rapid increase in rental T produced, in support of his own evidence as to his understanding of the Crown's attitude to the re-negotiated lease, internal Crown memoranda. The first acknowledged a "reappraisal" agreement of March 1977 increasing rent to $6,880 p.a., pending the new lease from 1 July 1977 at $20,000 p.a. The other (of 12 July, 1977) records the Crown's appraisal of what would be involved for it in establishing on public lands the facilities ultimately provided through TCO. The document included drafting instructions for the new Special Lease, and proposed that the rental value of the original area was $33,000 p.a., but that of the lesser area $29,000 p.a. It estimated cost of construction of the facilities at $51,500 (later $44,500) and proposed that those works had a value to the Department of at least $7,400, (later $7,000) per year, and added that "the value of cleaning and maintenance justifies the rental reduction to the agreed sum of $20,000 p.a."



10. It is clear from the foregoing that:-



a) When the facilities were constructed, TCO was

carrying on business as a caravan-park operator.



b) When the obligation to construct those facilities

arose, TCO was not carrying on business as a

caravan-park operator.



c) The obligation to construct those facilities, along

with other obligations undertaken at the same time,

was undertaken in order to secure a long-term tenancy

of the site of the caravan-park so that TCO might at

some future time carry on business there as a

caravan-park operator while occupying those premises

as lessee from the Crown.



d) The obligations so undertaken included not only the

obligation to construct the facilities in question

outside the leased land but also to effect sewerage

connections from existing service complexes within

the leased site; to pay rent for the leased site

during the period of the lease; and to clean and

maintain the facilities so erected outside the leased

site during the period of the lease and of any

extension thereof.



e) The obligation to construct the facilities was not

assumed in substitution for, or in order to reduce

the burden of, any other obligation of TCO, let alone

any subsisting obligation suffered or arising in the

course of any business carried on by TCO (That

finding makes irrelevant the principles applied by

the High Court of Australia in W. Nevill & Co Ltd v

F.C.T. [1937] HCA 9; (1937) 56 CLR 290); and



f) That the erection of the facilities added to the

physical assets of another; not to the physical

assets of TCO.



g) That the construction satisfied an obligation without

which TCO may never have acquired the leasehold

interest which was vital to the conduct of the

business as it was later carried on by TCO. By

undertaking the obligation TCO was able to secure an

"enduring advantage".



11. In my view the essential question is whether the expenses incurred in discharging the obligation so incurred belong to revenue account - as with the recurrent expenses incurred in discharging the obligations to pay rent for the site and to clean and maintain the facilities constructed off-site – or whether they belong to capital account - along with the expenses of effecting sewerage connections for the toilet facilities within the leased site.



12. It was suggested that it was not a matter of capital account because no single outlay was incurred at any particular point of time. I find nothing in that argument. Rather what is significant is that the occasion of the obligation was non-recurrent - once the obligation to construct the facilities was undertaken it played its part in securing the enduring advantage of gaining for TCO the status of being a long-term lessee; and once the facilities were constructed in discharge of that obligation that would be an end of the matter in that regard. Further, in the ordinary course of events the facilities so constructed should still be in sound condition and wholly useful when the 21-year term of the lease has run its course.



13. In determining whether the expenditure belongs to revenue or capital account, it is common ground that the decision in Sun Newspapers Ltd. and Associated Newspapers Ltd. v. F.C.T. be applied and particularly the reasons expounded by Dixon J., as he then was.



14. His Honour spoke of



"the distinction between the business entity,

structure, or organization set-up or established for the

earning of profit and the process by which such an

organization operates to obtain regular returns by means

of regular outlay, the difference between the outlay and

returns representing profit or loss.";



"the business structure or entity or organization may

assume any of an almost infinite variety of shapes and

it may be difficult to comprehend under one description

all the forms in which it may be manifested.";



"the difference between an outlay which is recurrent,

repeated or continual and that which is final or made

"once for all"; and also to the effect that the Courts

relied" to a still greater extent upon the distinction

to be discovered in the nature of the asset or advantage

obtained by the outlay.";



He also identified as capital, expenditure incurred

"to bring into existence or procure some asset or

advantage of a lasting character which will enure for

the benefit of the organization or system or

"profit-earning subject.""



15. In my view, those principles equally apply to a situation in which the expenditure is incurred during a period of operating the business structure if, as here, the obligation to incur the expenditure was assumed in order that a "profit-yielding subject" might come into existence for the taxpayer to be available to the "enduring advantage" of the business it proposed to conduct at a future date.16. I uphold the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection.
