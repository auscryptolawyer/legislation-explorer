Re Taxation Appeals [1988] AATA 218 (8 July 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. NT86/10675

AAT No. 4495

Income Tax

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

P.M. Roach (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Practice and Procedure - Applicant overseas - unable to attend in person or by representative before the Tribunal - application by Commissioner to dismiss for non-appearance - principles applicable.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 - s.42A(2);

Income Tax Rates Act 1982- ss.3(1), 23(1);

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - ss.6, 208;

Income Tax (Individuals) Act 1985 - s.5(1).

HEARING

SYDNEY

8:7:1988

ORDER

The Tribunal directs that:

(a) this application be removed from the list of

matters for hearing by the Tribunal;

(b) leave be reserved to the parties to apply for

reinstatement of this application in the list

of matters for hearing by the Tribunal;

(c) my direction be communicated to the Applicant

and the decisioner-maker.

DECISION

The Applicant claims that the Commissioner has assessed him as liable to pay an excessive amount of income tax. The Commissioner assessed the Applicant as liable to pay $1,586.70 by way of income tax and, after giving credit for an amount already received by the Commissioner under the PAYE system ($1,128.17), gave notice that there remained due and owing to the Commissioner as a debt due and payable to the Crown the sum of $458.53. The Applicant claims that the Commissioner should not have assessed him as being liable to pay more than $173.50; and that he should have received a refund of $954.67.

2. The Applicant objected to the assessment made and in due course the Commissioner disallowed the objection. Thereupon the Applicant applied for an independent review of his claim and the Commissioner complied with that request by referring the matter to this Tribunal for determination.

3. Fortunately for the Applicant at the date on which he requested an independent review of the Commissioner's decision to disallow the taxpayer's objection to the assessment made it was not required of him that he should pay a fee of $240 as the price of securing that independent adjudication. Had it been necessary to pay such a fee he might have found that he was unable to pay it because the Commissioner, having incorrectly assessed him, was withholding from him the very moneys which he might have used to pay the $240.

4. In due course the Tribunal gave notice to both the Applicant and the Commissioner requiring the parties to attend before the Tribunal in Preliminary Conference. The Applicant failed to attend and thereon was liable to suffer his application to be dismissed by reason of non-attendance in person or by his representative, s.42A(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975. That being so, the Commissioner's representative at the conference followed common practice. He applied for the application to be dismissed.

5. But the Applicant had a sound explanation for not attending. He was in New Zealand and seemed likely to remain there for some time. From what is now known of the matter it seems likely that he could neither afford the fare to personally travel to Sydney to attend before the Tribunal, nor afford to retain the services of professional advisers to represent him at the Preliminary Conference.

6. Being aware of similar problems faced by applicants from such places as Indonesia, North America and the United Kingdom, and conscious that there may be many others, the question which arose in face of the Commissioner's application to dismiss was whether that application should be granted; or no action taken; or something done to try to provide for an independent adjudication of the issue.

7. The difference between the parties did not relate to any issue as to the quantum of taxable income. Both the Applicant and Respondent agree that the taxable income for the Applicant for the year of income ended 30 June 1986 was $5,289. The difference between them is that the Commissioner says that it was his obligation to assess the tax payable on that taxable income of the Applicant at a special rate: a rate heavier than the standard rate applied to the taxation of natural persons. He said that by the provisions of the Income Tax Rates Act 1982 the Parliament had prescribed such a special and heavier rate as applicable to "non-residents" such as the Applicant.

8. Thus the question to be resolved is whether the Applicant at all material times was a "resident" of Australia within the special meaning of that term as defined by s.6 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). If he was, he is entitled to succeed in his claim. If he was not such a resident, his claim should fail. However, as matters stand he will not be able to prove his entitlement unless he can attend in Australia before the Tribunal and persuade it, or by some other means achieve the same end, or persuade the Commissioner that his judgment in the matter was erroneous.

9. Conscious that the Commissioner is not always correct when he disallows objections, after some deliberation notice was given to the Commissioner calling on him to attend before the Tribunal in a Directions Hearing and requesting that the Tribunal should

"have the assistance of the Commissioner's submissions

as to the procedures to be followed in order to provide

a just determination for the objections of such persons

as the Applicant and, in particular, as to what might

constitute the presentation to the Tribunal of

sufficient evidence to enable a taxpayer who has in fact

been incorrectly assessed to discharge the burden of

proof which he bears in that regard."

10. To that point the only information made available to the Tribunal by the Commissioner had been by way of the formal documents. They comprised the Notice of Assessment; the Objection; the decision on the Objection; a request for reference to this Tribunal; and a statement which said:

"(C)THE COMMISSIONER'S REASONS FOR DISALLOWING THE

OBJECTIONS

During the year of income ended 30 June 1986 the

taxpayer was a "non-resident" of Australia and a

"non-resident taxpayer" as defined by sub-section

6(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 and

sub-section 3(1) of the Income Tax (Rates) Act 1982

respectively.

Accordingly, under section 17 of the Income Tax

Assessment Act 1936 and sub-section 5(1) of the

Income Tax (Individuals) Act 1985 the taxpayer is

subject, for the year of income ended 30 June 1986,

to the rates of tax declared by sub-section 23(1)

and Part II of Schedule 19 of the Income Tax

(Rates) Act 1982."

11. However, in addition, the Tribunal had on file a letter from the Applicant received in reply to a notice sent to the Applicant enquiring as to his intentions in relation to the review he had sought. The reply was hand-written by a person not likely to be well-versed in the intricacies of tax law. In that letter the Applicant said:

"I am writing to the letter recieved 16/1/88. I would

like my tax rebate to be taken up in front of the board

of review for review. All I would like to say is when I

arrived in Australia in 85 I was intending to stay, a

perminate (sic) resident. I worked hard for 11 weeks

and was layed (sic) off with the completion of work. I

stayed on for four weeks in Sydney looking for work,

then moved on to Melbourne but could not find work. By

then I had run out of money so decided to come back to

N.Z. rather than get the unemployment benefit. My wages

were not great and I worked 7 days a week at times so

would be pleased if I did recieve (sic) some sort of tax

refund."

12. In response to the Tribunal's request for assistance, the Commissioner's representative provided further information, much of which, according to the records of the Commissioner, was in writing and originated with the Applicant. Accordingly, although I have not had an opportunity to hear the Applicant and he has not had an opportunity to test the evidence presented against him I find on the basis of the evidence before me that the facts were as follows.

13. The Applicant is a New Zealander by birth, having been born in that country on 13 July 1957. He arrived in Australia on 1 July 1985 and his entry was authorised pursuant to a temporary work permit. The Commissioner's staff did not record the length of time he was permitted to reside in this country. Having arrived in Australia he sought work as a labourer. After being unemployed for a fortnight he commenced work with a Sydney company and was employed by them from 16 July 1985 to 9 October 1985. His efforts earned him $5,000 plus a travelling allowance of $409. He took out membership in the Builders Labourers Federation at a cost of $120. In due course he was to return a taxable income of $5,289. He was unemployed from 9 October 1985 until his departure for New Zealand in November 1985. On 14 October 1985 he attended upon the offices of the Commissioner in Hunter Street, Sydney requesting the early processing of his return of income with a view to his pending return to New Zealand.

14. I find that, although he dated his income tax return "14/9/85", it was in fact completed and lodged with the Commissioner on 14 October 1985: the same day on which he completed a form provided by the Commissioner for use in such circumstances. In his income tax return he acknowledged 1983 to be the year of his last return. In the other form he identified "1/7/72" (sic) as the date of his first arrival in this country; "November 1982" (sic) as the date he had left Australia; and "1 July 1985" as the date on which he had last returned to Australia. Further, he identified the purpose of his visit to Australia as "contract of employment"; and his reason for departure from Australia as "not saving money".

15. Subsequently a notice of assessment issued against him on 6 November 1985. On 20 November 1985 the Commissioner received an undated letter which he treated as an objection. In that letter the Applicant stated:

"I have been to Australia six times and have always

worked to the lower tax bracket. Before I started work

I bought one union ticket costing me $120. I worked for

11 weeks earning on the average about $330 a week, thats

including overtime and weekend work. I usually get a

very fair and considerable tax return. When lodging my

return I was on the understanding I would recieve (sic)

at least 80% of tax I payd (sic) in back .... I am sorry

about the inconvenience caused but would appreciate a

quick return of the money I'm owed as I have already

spent some of the cheque I was about to recieve (sic)."

16. In a further undated letter received by the Commissioner on 7 February 1988 the Applicant said, inter alia,

"As I am a single man I shall most likely return to

Australia again. I have never had any trouble in

previous years of getting back my rebates (sic)."

17. By a further undated letter addressed to the Commissioner the Applicant complained "I have been waiting for a reply on my last two letters concerning my tax rebate". He went on to say:

"At the moment I am out of work and am seriously

thinking about returning to Australia to live ... In

all the years I've been over there in Australia I have

had my tax cheques posted out to me within 24 days."

18. It is difficult to establish the date of that letter because it does not appear to be responsive to the notice of disallowance which had issued on 26 March 1986, yet it is marked as received in the "Priority Control" section of the Australian Tax Office on 10 April 1986.

19. Upon giving notice of disallowance of the objection on 26 March 1986 the Commissioner took the not altogether common course of seeking to explain to the taxpayer why he had assessed the taxpayer as he had. In that letter it was stated that "the tax-free threshold for non-residents has been withdrawn as from 30 October 1982". But it did not offer any explanation as to how it could have been that the Applicant had not been assessed by that standard in previous years. The apparent inconsistency not having been acknowledged, explained or resolved the Applicant was undeterred in seeking relief. With his next letter, he "put in a postal order for $2" and said that he

"would like you to put this letter forward to your

review board. I like Australia and intend to be back

not far in the future. Please don't discourage me from

returning to Auzzie (sic)."

20. No demand for payment of the outstanding sum of $458.53 plus interest has been made: probably in recognition of the impracticability of recovery unless the Applicant returns to Australia.

21. Having considered all of the foregoing it is appropriate to make the following comments:

(a) it is not for this Tribunal to express any views as

to whether the substantive law setting the special,

high rates of tax alleged to apply in the

assessment of the taxpayer is or is not fair;

(b) the work history of the Applicant would seem to

indicate a factual basis as to why the Parliament

might have thought it appropriate to introduce the

legislation it did;

(c) the available evidence indicates that the

Commissioner had apparently very sound reasons for

forming the judgments he did; although

(d) it is always possible that, if the Applicant were

fully heard on the matters in dispute, a different

conclusion might be reached and the determination

of the Commissioner set aside.

22. I have considered many - hopefully all - of the arguments I have heard in the past two years as to the desirability of matters referred to the Taxation Appeals Division of this Tribunal being brought to "finality" by the making of Orders of dismissal.

23. It has been said that:

(a) The Tribunal needs to finalise its

responsibilities. That is true, but in doing so it

needs to act fairly.

(b) That if the Tribunal dismisses an application the

Tribunal could then dispose of its file record -

thereby relieving the Tribunal of the expense of

storage. I am not persuaded that it is so. As I

understand it, whatever order might be made the

Tribunal would continue to hold the file in its own

storage system for some years to come, and even if

thereafter it parted with possession of its

records, the same problem of storage would arise

through Commonwealth Archives. Expenditure in the

budget of the Tribunal might be reduced, but the

expense to the community would remain.

(c) That the Commissioner wants to finalise his

records. That is not unreasonable and, if

dismissal now would be followed by immediate

destruction of those records and thereby some

saving for the community, that would be a matter

which should be taken into account. However,

whether such destruction of records would take

place immediately I do not know.

(d) That the statistics used in the Commissioner's

Annual Report as presented to the Parliament has no

classification for "de-listed" matters. If

inconvenience in the presentation of statistics is

to be the measure of this Tribunal's performance in

the administration of justice, then the argument

has some merit. However, if fairness and justice

in the administration of the process of the

independent review which this Tribunal exists to

provide is a matter of first priority for this

Tribunal, then the Commissioner should modify the

statistical format he uses to accommodate

"de-listings" to distinguish matters removed from

the lists from those "awaiting hearing".

(e) That recovery proceedings against Applicants cannot

be proceeded with by the Commissioner until

applications are finalised. I am far from

persuaded that that is so. S.208 of the Act

provides that the sum assessed constitutes a debt

immediately due and payable to the Crown. It is

true that the Courts have restrained the

Commissioner from proceeding with recovery action

where the Commissioner has been in default of his

obligation to carry out his statutory duty to refer

matters to the Tribunal and even where, despite

having done so, the Tribunal cannot provide for the

hearing of the review or the appeal. But never, so

far as I am aware, has the Court intervened when

the delay in adjudication has been occasioned by

the Applicant. But, whatever may be the merit of

the argument in some other cases, it has no

application here. No attempt has been made to

recover. Nor is any attempt at recovery likely to

be made, unless the Applicant returns to Australia.

(f) That the Recovery Section in the Commissioner's

office cannot proceed to "write-off" debts such as

that now outstanding until the application to the

Tribunal is finalised. The suggestion seems to be

entirely without merit. Why any such limitation

should be thought to exist has never been

explained.

(g) That the matter ought to be finalised because it is

without merit and because the Applicant has no

prospect of success. I accept that as a relevant

factor. However, it is not the principal issue.

As existing procedures of the Tribunal stand the

question presently at issue is whether the

Applicant should be denied an opportunity to be

heard because at the present time he is in New

Zealand.

(h) That matters such as this are too small to be

troubled about. I doubt that that view is widely

held. The amount at issue may be trifling in the

affairs of the community, but it is substantial in

the affairs of the Applicant. But, if it be true

that the amount is too trifling to be troubled

about collecting it on fair terms, the answer might

be not to claim the tax rather than to deny the

taxpayer an opportunity of challenging the

assessment.

24. For my part I do not accept that the present issue is to be determined by reference to criteria such as the convenience of the Commissioner or the Tribunal in the presentation of statistics as to matters "finalised" in Annual Reports to be tabled before the Parliament. The Tribunal exists to serve people - collectively (as represented by the Commissioner and individually (as Applicants). Statistics are useful in so far as they assist in the provision of that service and in the sound evaluation of the work of the Tribunal. Unfortunately many of the statistics recorded and tabulated are quite misleading.

25. But, as to this there should be no doubt: statistics must be adapted to the service of people - and not people bent to the convenience of statistics. The role of this Tribunal is to adjudicate fairly between the Applicant and the Respondent - the Applicant as an individual: the Respondent as the representative of Government: a Government which exists to serve each and all and to do so justly according to law.

26. In my view the Applicant should not be denied a reasonable opportunity to seek to persuade the Tribunal. Accordingly, I will direct that the matter be removed from the list of matters awaiting hearing before the Tribunal.

27. If the Applicant returns to Australia at a later date he can then seek the hearing which distance now denies him. His return will also provide him with an opportunity to claim the moneys he asserts are due to him. If he does not return or, having returned, does not seek a hearing within, say, the three years from now, it would not be unreasonable for the Commissioner to renew his present application - if he then considers that the advantages to flow from a successful application will warrant the expenditure involved for the community.
