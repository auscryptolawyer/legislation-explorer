VT86/10206 and Commissioner of Taxation [1990] AATA 581 (25 July 1990)

Last Updated: 5 June 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION



INCOME TAX - Additional tax for late lodgement of return - Powers of Tribunal to review.



Income Tax Assessment Act – 1936 S.s 193(2); 222 (1)

AAT Decision No 6126



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION ) No VT 86/10206



Re: Applicant



And: Commissioner of Taxation Respondent



D E C I S I O N



TRIBUNAL: MR PJB BURNS (MEMBER)

DATE: 25 July 1990

PLACE: MELBOURNE

DECISION: The decision of the Tribunal is that it has no power to review the additional tax imposed.



PJB Burns



Signed _____________________

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) NO. VT86/10206

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



RE: Applicant



And: Commissioner of Taxation

Respondent



REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Mr P J B Burns (Member)



Date: 25 July 1990



Place: Melbourne



1. The matter in dispute in this application for reference concerns the imposition of additional tax for late lodgement of a return of income by the applicant in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1935.



2. The applicant's return of income for the year was prepared and lodged on his behalf by a firm of accountants and registered Tax agents on 2 April 1986, under what is known as The Alternative Lodgement Program. This was one of two special programs available to registered t.ax agents enabling them to spread their workloads over a longer period than would otherwise be the case if the normal lodgement date of 31 August applied. The Taxation Office requirements for this program are contained in Tax Agents Circular 2/1985 dated August 1985 (the "Circular").



3. Under the program the registered agents were required to meet certain lodgement deadlines for returns of income of their clients related to their tax levels, ie tax payable.



4. During the year the applicant was an elected member of a State Parliament and for the purposes of the agents' lodgement program fell into a category described as SPECIAL CIRCUMSTANCES TAXPAYERS which was explained in the Circular as follows-



"SPECIAL CIRCUMSTANCE TAXPAYERS



Clients falling into this category are included in the final section of the client list, where applicable, and form part of an agent's normal lodgement program. Lodqements concessions beyond 31 December 1985 in respect of these clients will depend on an agent meetinq his particular proqram requirement at that date.



The actual arrangement date for special circumstance taxpayers will not be indicated on client listings forwarded to achieving agents in January 1986. Nevertheless, the returns need to be lodqed in accordance with the lodqement proqram applicable which requires lOO~o lodqement at ke~ dates for particular tax levels and, in any event, no later than 31 March 1986. Agents s~iould refer to the previous assessment notice to ascertain the tax level of such clients. The notes below the table of tax levels at the centre of the Commissioner's Annual Circular dated June 1985 refers".



5. At the hearing the Commissioner's representative presented an agreed statement of facts and submitted that because the additional tax imposed under section 222 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 193~ (the "Act") did not exceed 20%per annum of the tax properly payable based on the period that the return was lodged late the Tribunal did not have the power to review the matter due to the excluding provisions of section 193(2) of the Act.



6. The applicant agreed with the Comissioner's representative that the additional tax (if payable) had been calculated in accordance with section 222 (supra) and that it did not exceed 20% per annum. He submitted that there was no additional tax payable and therefore, where there is no additional tax payable, the limitation of the Tribunal's power to review the matter contained in section 193(2) did not apply. He based this claim upon what he considered to be ambiguity in the lodgement requirements for Special Circumstance Taxpayers as set out in the Circular. He claimed that the words meant that you have to lodge in accordance with the program but you must lodge no later than 1 March 1986. The agent, he submitted, had followed the correct lodgement program and that the return was lodged on 2 April 198 which is during the one week period of grace allowed for the lodgement of returns after their due date, and complied with the ambiguous wording of the Circular which he believed could be construed as saying, however no later than 31 March 1986.



7. In reply to that submission the Commissioner's representative said that the words were not ambiguous and that before a 31 March date was permissible an agent needed to achieve the "100 per cent lodgement key dates for particular tax levels." As the agent had not met that requirement the return had been correctly considered to have been lodged late. The representative did concede that generally the agent had complied with the program.



8. I cannot a~3ree with the applicant's submission that the Commissioner did not have the authority to impose additional tax based on the claim that the words in the Circular are ambiguous and J accept the interpretation of those words given by the Commissioner's representative.



9. Secondly the applicant submitted that because the matter had been referred to the Tribunal some years previously and that there had been several preliminary conferences involving representatives of the Commissioner, and at those conferences an application had been made to have the matter removed from the Tribunal’s jurisdiction.



10. Although the handling of this matter by the Taxation Office has not been without fault and has caused unnecessary inconvenience to the applicant by requiring his participation in the conferences and the hearing, I do not believe that the referring of the matter by the Commissioner to the Tribunal overrides the provisions of section 193(2).



11. I might also add that it would seem from the evidence presented at the hearing that the additional tax which was imposed was excessive considering the somewhat technical breach by the agent of the lodgement program.



12. The decision of the Tribunal is that it has no power to review the additional tax imposed.
