DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No AT97/6

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

Decision No 13200

DECISION



Tribunal Mr B. H. Pascoe, Senior Member

Date 21 August 1998

Place Canberra

Decision The Tribunal varies the decision under review and remits the matter to the respondent with a direction to amend the applicant's assessment for the year ended 30 June 1995 to further remit additional tax imposed for late lodgement of the return to an amount equal to 20% per annum of the tax payable calculated from 31 December 1995 to 28 June 1996 in accordance with the reasons for this decision.



........(Sgd) B. H. Pascoe..........

Senior Member



CATCHWORDS

INCOME TAX - additional tax for late lodgement of return - whether extension of time for lodgement granted - whether additional tax calculated from 31 October or some later date - whether further remission warranted

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936

Balnaves v Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4592

Case 58 95 ATC 467

REASONS FOR DECISION

August 1998 Mr B. H. Pascoe, Senior Member



  1. This is an application to review a decision of the respondent to disallow an objection lodged by the applicant against a notice of assessment of income tax in respect of the year ended 30 June 1995. The sole issue in dispute was the imposition of additional tax of $5,128.99 for late lodgement of the return of income for that year.
  2. At the hearing the applicant was represented by Mr Livingston of counsel and the respondent by an officer of the respondent. No evidence was given at the hearing.
  3. The facts of this matter were not in dispute. The applicant lodged her income tax return using the services of a tax agent through whom she had lodged prior years returns. Under the lodgement program of the agent, her return for the year ended 30 June 1995 was due for lodgement no later than 15 April 1996. It was not lodged by that date and the respondent issued a final notice to lodge on 10 May 1996. This notice required the applicant to lodge the return by 31 May 1996. The return was lodged on 28 June 1996 and the notice of assessment, issued on 19 July 1996, included the additional tax for late lodgement. The additional tax of $5,128.99 was calculated at the rate of 20% per annum from 31 October 1995 to 28 June 1996. The estimated tax payable, excluding the additional tax, had been paid by the applicant to the respondent at the time of lodging the relevant return.
  4. It was submitted for the applicant that any additional tax should have been calculated from 15 April 1996 being the last day for lodgement of the return under the lodgement program of the tax agent under the respondent's lodgement rules for the 1995 tax year. Alternatively, it was argued that, pursuant to the respondent's Ruling IT2475 the additional tax should have been calculated from 31 December 1995 where the return had been lodged before 30 June 1996 with full payment of estimated tax made with the return. As a third option, it was submitted for the applicant that IT2475 had adopted 31 December as a "key date" which was appropriate when the Ruling was made in May 1988 and more restrictive lodgement rules applied in respect of the year ended 30 June 1987. However, it was said that the 1995 year rules specified 1 April 1996 as "the critical monitoring rate" and it should be that 1 April as the date from which additional tax for late lodgement with accompanying payment should be calculated.
  5. For the respondent, it was submitted that no extension of time for lodgement of the applicant's return had been granted to 15 April 1996. It was said that the tax agent lodgement rules were not an extension of time to individual taxpayers but an administrative arrangement with tax agents in relation to the conduct of the tax agents' business. It was argued further, that the applicant was not entitled to have the additional tax calculated from 31 December 1995 on the basis of having paid the estimated tax with lodgement of the return as that concession did not apply where a final notice had issued. Consequently, it was said, the additional tax was correctly calculated from 31 October 1995 being the date specified in the Commonwealth Gazette in accordance with section 161 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act").
  6. The relevant provisions of the Act are sections 161(1), 222(1) and 227:
"161(1) Every person must, if required by the Commissioner by notice published in the Gazette, give to the Commissioner a return for the year of income within the period specified in the notice or such further period as the Commissioner allows.

...

222(1) Where a taxpayer refuses or fails to furnish, when and as required under or pursuant to this Act or the regulations to do so, a return, or any information, relating to a year of income, being a return relating to or information relating to, or to the affairs of, the taxpayer, the taxpayer is liable to pay, by way of penalty, additional tax equal to double the amount of tax payable by the taxpayer in respect of the year of income.

...

227(1) The Commissioner shall make an assessment of the additional tax payable by a person under a provision of this Part.

227(2) Nothing in this Act shall be taken to preclude notice of an assessment made in respect of a person under subsection (1) from being incorporated in notice of any other assessment made in respect of the person under this Act.

227(3) The Commissioner may, in the Commissioner's discretion, remit the whole or any part of the additional tax payable by a person under a provision of this Part, but, for the purposes of the application of subsection 33(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 to the power of remission conferred by this subsection, nothing in this Act shall be taken to preclude the exercise of the power at a time before an assessment is made under subsection (1) of the additional tax."

In IT2475 issued in May 1988 and effective 1 November 1988, the respondent had stated that the statutory amount of additional tax for late lodgement under section 222 would be remitted to a basic component of 20% per annum of the lesser of tax payable or net amount payable calculated from 31 October to date of lodgement. It was further stated that any further remission would be contemplated only where exceptional or unpredictable circumstances led to the delay in lodgement. In paragraphs 26 to 30 the Ruling provided that, where payment of estimated tax is made at or before lodgement of the return which is lodged after 15 April but before 30 June, additional tax would be levied at 20% per annum calculated from 31 December to date of lodgement. This two month concession was stated as not applying to taxpayers for whom prior year returns were outstanding or who had received demands to lodge by dates earlier than those permitted by their tax agent's lodgement program. In a document entitled 1995 Lodgment Rules which the respondent issued to tax agents, the late lodgement penalty of 20% per annum calculated from 31 October was reiterated. The concession where payment accompanies a late return lodged by 30 June was stated as being calculated from "program end date" to lodgement date. In this case the program end date was 15 April but this document added a further condition which was that "the return must not be the subject of a Final Notice for lodgment".

  1. Much of the argument of both parties was directed at whether or not it could be said that the applicant had been allowed a further period to 15 April 1996 in which to lodge her return pursuant to section 161(1). In Balnaves v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation 85 ATC 4592 the appellant was a registered tax agent who had been advised by the Commissioner that he would not be granted an extension of time for lodging clients' tax returns beyond 31 December because his lodgement performance to that date had not been satisfactory. At first instance Foster J held that this decision was not reviewable under the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977. On appeal, the Full Federal Court held that a decision to grant or withhold an extension of time for the lodgement of a return was a decision under section 161(1) of the Act and reviewable. The Court had no difficulty in deciding that the extension of time granted for lodgement of returns by tax agents was a decision under section 161(1) allowing a further period for lodgement. The Court recognised (at page 4595) that:
"A taxpayer will also be dependent upon his tax agent correctly notifying changes, in due time, to the list of names kept by the Commissioner. The final limit of an extension so far as concerns a particular taxpayer, rests in the hands of the tax agent."

I have no difficulty in finding, as did the Tribunal in Case 58, 95 ATC 467, that the respondent under his lodgement rules had allowed a further period to 15 April 1996 for lodgement of the applicant's return. It is not to the point that the tax agent was required to lodge a particular percentage of returns by an earlier date. Under the 1995 lodgement rules the penalty for failure to meet the program obligations by an agent was the possibility of losing two weeks off the following year's program for the particular tax level groupings. The penalty was not a failure to have an extension of time for the balance of returns granted. The applicant's name was on a list of the tax agent's clients prepared by the respondent and against her name under the column headed "Latest day for lodgment" appeared the date "15 April 1996".

  1. As stated, I agree with the finding of Senior Member Beddoe in Case 58/95 and am also satisfied that, where there is a lodgement program agreed by the respondent with a tax agent, as is the case here, the last day allowed for lodgement of returns is the last day for lodgement provided for in the program and this is the further period allowed under section 161(1). However, this is not the end of the matter. In Case 58/95, the Tribunal was concerned solely with the question of whether the Tribunal had jurisdiction to deal with the application. This depended upon whether it could be said that the additional tax imposed for late lodgement exceeded 20% per annum calculated from the last day allowed for furnishing the return to the day on which the return was lodged. If not, the objection decision is "an ineligible income tax remission decision" under section 14ZS of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 and not reviewable. It is only under that section that the question of what was the last day allowed for lodgement becomes a critical factor. However, where jurisdiction exists as it was found in Case 58/95 and is true in this case, the issue then becomes one of the exercise of the discretion to remit, pursuant to section 227 of the Act, part or all of the additional tax imposed by section 222.
  2. In a subsequent decision on that substantive issue in Case 58/95, the Tribunal considered that the reasons for late lodgement in that matter were such that either section 222(1) did not apply or, if it did, the discretion should be exercised under section 227 to remit the whole of the additional tax (AAT 10532A, 21 June 1996). In this case no reasons were advanced for the failure to lodge the return prior to 28 June 1996. Consequently, it is clear that section 222(1) applies to impose additional tax equal to double the amount of tax payable in respect of the 1994/1995 year of income. In IT2475 and in the 1995 Lodgment Rules the respondent set out the method by which he would exercise the discretion to remit part of such additional tax. This method was to calculate the additional tax on a formula based on the period from 31 October to date of lodgement at 20% per annum and remit the balance. This method has been widely promulgated and well-known by tax agents for many years. Nowhere in these documents has the respondent stated or implied that the use of 31 October as the commencing point was because it was the last date for lodgement of returns. It is simply a method of arriving at a basis for remission pursuant to section 227. It may well have been that the date of 31 October was chosen on the assumption that this method would exclude such additional tax and remission decision from review by the Tribunal. If that is the case then the respondent was incorrect in relation to returns lodged through tax agents. But that does not mean that the method of calculation is an inappropriate use of the discretion under section 227. Given its longstanding and well understood application by tax agents, I have no reason to believe that, where there is no reasonable explanation for delay in lodging a return, the Tribunal should seek to substitute a different method of determining the extent of the discretion to remit additional tax imposed under section 222. A further factor in this case which goes against consideration of any further remission of the additional tax for late lodgement is that the applicant failed to comply with the final notice issued by the respondent on 10 May 1996, again with no explanation.
  3. It then remains to consider the alternative submission that further remission of the additional tax for late lodgement should be made as a consequence of payment of the estimated tax with the return when lodged on 28 June 1996. It was argued that the applicant should be entitled to the concession set out in paragraph 26 to 30 of IT2475 and further that the date of 31 December used therein should be updated to 1 April. Mr Livingston submitted that, if the applicant had lodged her return on 15 April 1996, an assessment would have been likely to have been issued within 14 days resulting in tax payable at the end of May 1996. The estimated tax was paid on 28 June 1996 and the resultant penalty for this actual one month delay but calculated at 20% for about 8 months was said to be excessively penal. It was said further that there was inconsistency between the terms of IT2475 and the 1995 Lodgment Rules and that the Ruling should be given primacy.
  4. It should be said that neither of these documents are Public Rulings within Part IVAAA of the Taxation Administration Act 1953. Relating as they do in this context to the way in which the respondent will calculate additional tax imposed by section 222 of the Act they do not relate to liability for "income tax" as defined. In addition IT2475 was issued prior to 1 July 1992. Neither document is binding on the respondent or the Tribunal. Nevertheless, it is appropriate to apply the terms of these documents in the interests of administrative consistency. The 1995 Lodgment Rules relating to additional tax where payment was made with the return, contained two significant differences from IT2475. Firstly, the 20% per annum was to be calculated from 15 April 1996 not 31 December 1995 and, secondly, the concession of applying that later date was conditional upon the return not being subject to a Final Notice for lodgement. In this case, a Final Notice had issued prior to lodgement and the concession, which should have been well understood by the tax agent, at least, was not available within the terms of the lodgement rules. Given the two changes to the previously announced policy in IT2475, it is reasonable for a taxpayer to believe that, if the lodgement rules did not apply, then the prior terms of IT2475 would apply. Consequently, I am of the view that an appropriate exercise of the discretion in section 227 is that the additional tax for late lodgement should be remitted to an amount equal to 20% per annum of the tax payable calculated from 31 December 1995 to 28 June 1996 being 180 days. Whilst understanding Mr Livingston's innovative submission that 31 December should be replaced by 1 April, it relies on pure conjecture as to why that earlier date was chosen in IT2475. I am not disposed to seek to confirm that conjecture nor accept this submission.
  5. It follows that the decision under review should be varied by remitting that matter to the respondent with a direction to amend the assessment for the year ended 30 June 1995 by a further remission of additional tax for late lodgement to an amount calculated at the rate of 20% per annum from 31 December 1995 to 28 June 1996 of the tax payable.
