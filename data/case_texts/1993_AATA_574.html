Underwood and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland [1993] AATA 574 (27 July 1993)

Last Updated: 26 August 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNALGENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION



TAX AGENT REGISTRATION - Whether applicant held the qualifications as prescribed by Regulation 156 of Income Tax Regulations - "relevant employment" criteria - supervision and control



Income Assessment Act 1936: Ss251BC , 251JA and S251N

Income Tax Regulations: R.156



Tax Agents' Board of Queensland v Seymour (1990) 20 FCR 357

S & T Income Tax Aid Specialists Pty Limited and Christopher Forward v Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales (DP Bannon, Mr Stevens and Mr Taylor) 9 April 1987



AAT Decision No 8878



DECISION AND REASONS



RE: BARRY CLIFTON UNDERWOOD



APPLICANT



AND: TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF QUEENSLAND



RESPONDENT



Q92/356



TRIBUNAL: Mr. D.W. Muller, Senior Member



DATE: 27 July 1993



PLACE: Brisbane



DECISION



The Tribunal affirms the decision of the Tax Agents' Board of Queensland dated 16 June 1992 refusing to register Barry Clifton Underwood as a Tax Agent.



SIGNED

D.W. MULLER

SENIOR MEMBER



Q92/356



RE: BARRY CLIFTON UNDERWOOD



APPLICANT



AND: TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF QUEENSLAND



RESPONDENT



TRIBUNAL: Mr. D.W. Muller, Senior Member



DATE: 27 July 1993



PLACE: Brisbane



REASONS FOR DECISION



  1. This is an application to review a decision of the Tax Agents' Board of Queensland dated 16 June 1992 refusing to register Barry Clifton Underwood, the applicant, as a tax agent.

  2. This matter was heard in Bundaberg on 11 March 1993. Mr. Underwood was represented by his solicitor, Mr. Binnie, and the respondent was represented by Mrs. Wolfe of Counsel.

  3. The question for determination by the Tribunal is whether or not Barry Clifton Underwood, the applicant, satisfies the provisions and regulations relating to the registration of tax agents pursuant to the Income Assessment Act 1936 (the Act) and the Income Tax Regulations (the Regulations).

  4. The following material was placed before the Tribunal:

(a) The Section 37 statement

(b) Two affidavits from Barry James Rienecker, secretary of the Tax Agents' Board of Queensland

(c) A pamphlet distributed by a company called Complete Taxation Services

(d) Oral evidence by the applicant

(e) Oral evidence by Mr. Rienecker.



  1. The requirements for registration as a tax agent are set out in sections 251 BC and 251 JA of the Act. The requirements include certain minimum standards of academic study and practical experience as prescribed from time to time. The prescribed qualifications are contained in Regulation 156. It is common ground that the only contentious matter in Mr. Underwood's case is whether he holds the qualifications as prescribed by regulation 156.

  2. Regulation 156(1) provides four different ways in which a person may qualify for the purposes of sub paragraph 251 BC (1) (b) (ii) of the Act. In each case there must be a combination of specified academic achievement, a course of study in Australian income tax law acceptable to the Board and a period of "relevant employment". The higher the academic level achieved by the applicant the lower the number of years of relevant employment which must have been completed. Mr. Underwood has completed a Bachelor of Economics (Accounting) from La Trobe University and he has completed an accepted course of study in Australian income tax law. The relevant employment criteria which apply to Mr. Underwood are those set out in Regulation 156(1) (a) (ii) (A), (B) and (C).

R. 156(1)(a)(ii)

(A) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding 5 years;

(B) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent that the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in sub-subparagraph (A); or

(C) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as the Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant employment as referred to in sub-subparagraph (A);

"Relevant employment" is defined in Regulation 156(2)

R.156

(2) In this regulation "relevant employment" means employment by a person or a partnership or as a member of a partnership in the course of which there has been substantial involvement in income tax matters including:

(a) the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns;

(b) the preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued in respect of such returns; and

(c) the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns, assessments or objections.



It is Mr. Underwood's claim that he had been engaged in relevant employment for 12 months within the preceding five years or at least in employment equivalent to relevant employment for the required period.



  1. Upon assessing the material placed before the Tribunal, outlined in paragraph 4 above, I make the following findings of fact.



(a) From 3 July 1989 to 26 November 1990 the applicant worked for a number of entities managed by a man called Steve Hart. Mr. Hart's main operation was in Brisbane. Mr. Underwood worked in a branch office in Bundaberg.



(i) From 3 July 1989 to October 1989 the applicant was employed by Complete Taxation Services a division of Hartcorp Australia Ltd.

(ii) From October 1989 to mid-July 1990 the applicant was employed by Steve Hart and Associates Pty Ltd.

(iii) From mid-July 1990 to late November 1990 there is some doubt about the name of Mr. Underwood's employer.

(iv) At all times mentioned in this sub paragraph the employer's name according to the group certificate was Bamilsco Pty Ltd., a service company.



(b) In the period described in sub paragraphs 7(a)(i) and (ii) above (3 July 1989 to mid July 1990), Mr. Underwood worked in an office in Woongarra Street, Bundaberg. He was solely responsible for the taxation work done by that office. He had the assistance of a part-time receptionist. At all times during this particular period Mr. Underwood believed that Mr. Hart was a registered tax agent. The literature put out by Mr. Hart certainly suggested that he and his companies were registered tax agents. Mr. Underwood sent returns to Brisbane where he expected that they were checked, signed and sent on to the Taxation Department. If there were any queries about any of the returns prepared by Mr. Underwood the Brisbane people would contact Mr. Underwood by fax or by phone. Mr. Hart visited the office in Bundaberg about once or twice per month.



(c) In the period described in sub paragraph 7(a)(iii) above (mid July 1990 to late November 1990) Mr. Underwood worked in an office at Bargara Court, Bundaberg. This office had been owned by a company called Drumhella Pty Ltd., practising as Ralph and Royle, when it was bought by, or amalgamated with, one of Steve Hart's companies. Steve Hart, or one of his companies, also took over an accounting firm run by a Stanley Horton. After the amalgamation Mr. Underwood worked at Bargara Court under the supervision of Richard Royle and Stanley Horton.



(d) Richard Royle was registered as nominee of Ralph and Royle on 28 November 1988.



(e) Stanley Horton was registered as a tax agent on 18 October 1988.



(f) Mr. Royle and Mr. Horton were registered as nominees of Steve Hart and Associates Pty. Ltd. on 14 January 1991.



(g) Steve Hart and Associates Pty. Ltd. was registered as a tax agent on 11 November 1981.



(h) Hartcorp Australia Pty. Ltd. is the sole owner of a company now called Harts Pty. Ltd., previously known as Steve Hart and Associates Pty. Ltd. The Tax Agents' Board of Queensland has never been formally notified of the name changes and Hartcorp Australia Pty. Ltd. has not been registered as a tax agent.



(i) Steve Hart has never been registered as a tax agent nor as a nominee of the companies named above.



(j) Between April 1989 and December 1990 the registered nominees of Steve Hart and Associates Pty. Ltd. were Geoffrey Arthur Matthews, John Andrew Coupe and Robin Christopher Whittaker.



(k) Mr. Underwood's employment with the Hart group was terminated in November 1990.



  1. After leaving Mr. Hart and his associated companies, Mr. Underwood performed work for James Fairlie, a solicitor in Maryborough (T4 and T5). He did the accounting, maintaining of trust accounts and maintaining the general ledger. He told the Tribunal that during his time with Mr. Fairlie he prepared income tax returns for persons who had been his clients when he had been working for Steve Hart and associated companies. Mr. Underwood used Mr. Fairlie's tax agent's number from July 1991 to November 1991. Mr. Underwood had assured Mr. Fairlie that he was in the process of applying for registration and that he had cleared this temporary arrangement with the Tax Agent's Board. In December 1991 Mr. Fairlie asked Mr. Underwood how his application was going. Mr. Underwood told Mr. Fairlie that he was concerned about the time it was taking to process. Mr. Fairlie contacted a representative of the Board on 13 January 1992 and was told that applications are usually processed quickly unless there is some problem. Mr. Underwood had not applied for registration as at December 1991 because he had been told in September 1991 that his application would stand little chance of success. Mr. Underwood did not apply until 23 January 1992.

  2. Mr. Fairlie received no remuneration from Mr. Underwood's tax operation. It was run as a separate entity. Mr. Fairlie did not in any way supervise Mr. Underwood's tax "practice". Mr. Fairlie freely admitted in a letter to the Board (T4) that he knew very little about taxation matters. During the time that he was using Mr. Fairlie's number, Mr. Underwood prepared returns for about 78 individuals, 10 trusts, no companies, 10 to 15 partnerships, no superannuation funds and no objections.

  3. Mr. Underwood estimated that from July 1989 to July 1990, he prepared about 160 individual returns and five partnership returns. He estimated that from July 1990 to November 1990 he prepared returns for 180 individuals, 25 partnerships, 70 companies and 20 trusts. The returns covered a range of types of businesses. A form completed by Mr. Underwood at the time of his application for registration (T3) shows that he said that he was involved in the preparation of income tax returns for individual salary and wage earners, various types of small business operations such as hotels, service stations, small crops, grazing, retailers, cane growers and mixed farming and business. That form, headed "EMPLOYER'S STATEMENT AS TO RELEVANT EMPLOYMENT" was completed and signed by Mr. Underwood. His ties with Mr. Hart, Mr. Royle and Mr. Horton had been severed. Any employer signing the form could only do so on the basis that they were a registered tax agent and had known the applicant for twelve months or more. None of the abovementioned persons fitted this description.

  4. Mr. Underwood does not have any experience in superannuation and if successful in obtaining registration intends to place that type of work in the hands of experts in that field. During the five years prior to the hearing Mr. Underwood had dealt with one objection, which was the result of the work of another person. He believes that objections are no longer common because the Taxation Department is now in the habit of reassessing rather than following the objection process through, particularly because of the small amounts of money usually involved. Mr. Underwood told the Tribunal that he had wide managerial experience in a variety of operations and held a tax agency in Victoria in the 1980's. He allowed it to lapse because he was involved in other things at the time.

  5. Mr. Binnie, for the applicant, submitted that the periods 3 July 1989 to October 1989, October 1989 to July 1990, and July 1990 to November 1990 satisfy the requirement of being engaged in relevant employment on a full time basis for not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding five years, or being engaged in other employment which the Board should regard as equivalent. He also referred to experience gained during the time Mr. Underwood was working with Mr. Fairlie as equivalent to relevant employment. Mr. Binnie relied on Mr. Underwood's evidence that he had taken part in the preparation of a broad range of income tax returns, for individuals, partnerships and trusts. He submitted that because of the self-assessment provisions now in force, objections were no longer as common as they used to be.

  6. Mrs. Wolfe, for the respondent, submitted that Mr. Underwood was not engaged in relevant employment because of the lack of supervision and control over his work and that he was not substantially involved in the broad range of matters required by Regulation 156(2).

  7. It is not an explicit requirement of the Regulations dealing with the registration of tax agents that supervision and control be exercised during the period of relevant employment but it is clearly one of the aims of that part of the Act relating to the registration of tax agents that there be supervision during the twelve months of relevant employment. In Tax Agents' Board of Queensland v Seymour (1990) 20 FCR 357, Pincus J relied on the provisions of Section 251N (which refer to supervision and control) to form his definition of relevant employment. He noted that the regulation-making power is limited to the making of regulations not inconsistent with the Act and, if possible, the definition of "relevant employment" in regulation 156(2) should be read so as not to create a conflict between the regulations and the Act. He found that the word "employment" as it was used in the Act excluded those who were self-employed. It was the intention of the Act that "the work of preparing returns and objections for fees is to be done only by registered tax agents, their partners and employees". The combined effect of the Act and the regulations is that for employees to be engaged in "relevant employment", they must be under the type of supervision and control contemplated in Section 251N.

"251N(1) A registered tax agent or a person exempted under 251L shall not allow any person, not being his employee, a registered tax agent or, in the case of a partnership which is registered as a tax agent, a member of that partnership -



(a) to prepare on his behalf, either directly or indirectly, his own or any other income tax return or objection; or



(b) to conduct on his behalf, either directly or indirectly, any business of himself or any other person relating to any income tax return or income tax matter.



251N(2) A partnership or company which is registered as a tax agent shall not allow any person to do anything specified in paragraph (1)(a) or (b) except under the supervision and control of a registered nominee of the partnership or company.



251N(2A) A natural person who is registered as a tax agent shall not allow any person to do anything specified in paragraph (1)(a) or (b) except under the supervision and control of:



(a) the tax agent; or

(b) a registered nominee of the tax agent.



Penalties are imposed for noncompliance."



  1. The Tribunal was referred to S & T Income Tax Aid Specialists Pty Limited and Christopher Forward v Tax Agents' Board, New South Wales, a decision of this Tribunal composed of Deputy President Bannon, Mr. Stevens and Mr. Taylor handed down on 9 April 1987. The Tribunal in that case was concerned with the standard of supervision to be exercised by nominees pursuant to section 251N, which, it said, required:

"much more than that the nominee supervise the preparation of a tax return in the sense of checking a document prepared by an unskilled employee in order to see if the figures appear



reasonable. The evidence established that unqualified employees (termed consultants) interviewed the client, examined the client's expenditure receipts and vouchers and entered the particulars in a tax return form, generally a salaried employee's return. The client's receipts and vouchers were then returned to the person concerned. The next step was for the completed document to be perused and checked by another consultant who was provided with a tape check on the figures therein. If these matched and the return appeared reasonable, it was passed to a manager for further checking before being passed to a nominee for his perusal and checking prior to signing or, if any doubt arose as to its correctness, for the taking of appropriate action. Evidence was given that the nominee would take one to three minutes to deal with an "S" return. Section 251N calls for much more than this. It requires supervision and control of the preparation of the returns and of the business transacted by S. & T. relating to any income tax return or income tax matter. This involves, in our opinion, at least making spot checks on the accuracy of the initial material from which the returns are prepared and supervising the office work. S. & T.'s nominees have in many cases done no more than a few hours work in moonlighting jobs over and above their normal employment, making a rough appraisal of the completed work of unqualified employees of S. & T. When the Act speaks of the nominee being an employee and supervising the work it is really calling for substantial supervision and employment while the agent's office is open to transact tax business."



  1. The evidence discloses that during the period July 1989 to July 1990 Mr. Underwood worked for Mr. Hart's companies out of an office in Woongarra Street, Bundaberg. The registered nominees of that entity which was registered as a tax agent were Messrs Matthews, Coupe and Whittaker. There is no evidence before the Tribunal that any of the registered nominees exercised any of the methods of supervision described in the cases mentioned above. In my view the standard of supervision during this period did not measure up to that required by the Act and the Regulations.

  2. Between July 1990 and November 1990 Mr. Underwood was under the supervision of Messrs Royle and Horton. I find that the supervision was of the standard required. Unfortunately for Mr. Underwood his employment was terminated at a point when only five months of this work had been completed.

  3. I find that Mr. Underwood's work in association with Mr. Fairlie was not of the type contemplated by the Act, the Regulations or the decided cases.

  4. I have therefore come to the conclusion that the applicant had five months of relevant employment in the preceding five years. The regulations require twelve months of relevant employment. Consequently, the decision to refuse to register the applicant will be affirmed.

I hereby certify that this and the preceding 8 pages are a true and correct copy of the decision and reasons for decision of Mr. D.W. Muller, Senior Member in the matter of BARRY CLIFTON UNDERWOOD and TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF QUEENSLAND.



Signed B. Hitchcock (Secretary)



Date 27 July 1993
