Last Updated: 30 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - depreciation – whether articles used during year for the purpose of producing assessable income.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s54

Case N53 (1963) 13 TBRD

Case N5Q (1981) ATC 245

Applicant

REASONS FOR DECISION

16 November, 1987 Mrs H E Hallowes, Senior Member

1. The applicant seeks review of a decision rejecting his claim for deductions for the year of income ended 30 June, 1984 pursuant to section 54 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") which provides:

"(1) Depreciation during the year of income of any property, being plant or articles owned by a taxpayer and used by him during that year for the purpose of producing assessable income, and of any property being plant or articles owned by the taxpayer which has been installed ready for use for that purpose and is during that year held in reserve by him shall, subject to this Act, be an allowable deduction."

2. The applicant was born in 1911 and gave his occupation on his S Form Salary and Wages Income Tax Return dated 28 August, 1984 as "retired teacher". He declared income from the Department of Social Security and the Victorian Government Pensioners State Superannuation Board of Victoria, payment in both instances being for the period 1 July, 1983 to 30 June, 1984. He claimed expenditure incurred for the purpose of producing assessable income of $375. In an attachment to his return he advised that the sura represented 7.5% of the undepreciated value at 30 June, 1984 of his "training aids, text and reference books". He further advised that the deduction claimed had been allowed until 31 December, 1978. However from that date:

"All Victorian Education Department teachers over the age of 65 years were arbitrarily retired and from this date my concession was withdrawn in a demonstrably inequitable manner"

The applicant was advised by the respondent that the depreciation of his professional library was disallowed as there was no "nexus betweeen the derivation of your income and the expenditure incurred". On 4 June, 1985 the applicant was further advised that:

"[S]ince your income is not 'personal exertion' income, there is no expenditure which can be said to have been incurred in the earning thereof."

3. The applicant has led an interesting life from the time he worked on the gold fields of Western Australia, through his enlistment with the RAAF, to his twenty-seven years as a teacher followed by ten years as an honorary education counsellor with the Home Tutor Scheme, Technical And Further Education ("TAFE"). He states that he has been motivated by "pursuit of excellence" and "learning by doing". He has participated in the community through his membership of boards, clubs and unions and he has been elected to represent ratepayers within his local Shire. He was retired from his work as a teacher by the Education Department of Victoria at the end of 1978 when he was aged sixty-seven. He had hoped to continue working as a teacher until he was aged seventy. He is opening a door for other people who are retired by encouraging them to keep their minds active. He is still acting as an education counsellor which is an honorary position. His home is the centre of his counselling activities and he lends books from his library for no fee to those interested in using them. He is a life member of the Community Education Association. During his employment as a teacher with the Victorian Education Department he contributed to a superannuation scheme run by the State Superannuation Board of Victoria. He put to the Tribunal that he would not be receiving superannuation had it not been for his work as a teacher and that the benefit he is now reaping through his superannuation payments is a result of his personal exertion during his employment.

  1. It was put to the Tribunal by the respondent that the applicant gained an entitlement to his superannuation through his employment prior to his retirement and that depreciation of plant or articles owned by a taxpayer are only allowable as a deduction if, "during the year of income", the taxpayer has used the plant or articles during "that year" for the purpose of producing assessable income.
  2. In Case N53 (1963) 13 TBRD a taxpayer purchased a bulldozer to clear an area of land which had been purchased and which was later transferred to a son. At no time did the taxpayer pasture livestock on the land or plant crops upon it although the son later used it for grazing purposes. The Board held that depreciation on the bulldozer was not deductible as it was not used for the purpose of producing assessible income of the taxpayer but was used solely for the purpose of improving the subject of a gift intended for the taxpayer's son.
  3. This application is not dissimilar from Case N5Q (1981) ATC 245. The taxpayer aged seventy-two, a retired architect claimed deductions for expenditures incurred on subscriptions to professional journals, travelling and accommodation expenses and the depreciation of his professional and technical library. His assessable income for the year was his age pension and superannuation receipts. There was no income from the practice of his profession in that year. The Board held that the taxpayer was not carrying on a business and that the words in sub-section 54(1) of the Act "used by him during that year for the purpose of producing assessable income" meant "used by him (in the course of operations carried on) for the purpose of producing assessable income". As there was no operation carried on by the taxpayer during the income year, the use made of the library by the taxpayer was not a use required by the section and depreciation was not allowable.

7. In this application the applicant has not used his library for the purpose of producing assessable income for the relevant year of taxation. The Commissioner's decision on this objection must be affirmed. Whatever admiration the Tribunal may have for the taxpayer for his encouragement to those around him to remain active in their pursuit of knowledge and excellence through the use of his library, no income flowed from the use of the library during the relevant year. His superannuation entitlement did not arise nor was it produced as assessable income during the relevant year.

Mrs HE Hallowes

Senior member

Clerk
