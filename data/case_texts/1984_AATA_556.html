Hills and Commissioner for Superannuation - AAT No 1811 - [1984] AATA 556 (5 October 1984)

Last Updated: 9 January 2008

Superannuation - determination of retirement benefit - service with University of Tasmania before joining Commonwealth Public Service - contributor to University's Superannuation Scheme -election of contributor not to pay transfer value pursuant to Superannuation Act 1922 - number of units of pension restricted -contributor pursuant to Superannuation Act 1976 modified by regulations - "prescribed person" - entitlement calculated under s213(3)(a) - pension reduced due to non-payment of transfer value - Commissioner's decision affirmed

Superannuation Act 1976, ss 66, 67, 119D, 119J, 119K, 183, 212A, 213

MALCOLM CLEMENT BARTON HILLS

COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

NO A82/40

AAT Decision No 1811

Deputy President AN Hall

Dr AH Marsh (Member)

Mrs J McClintock (Member)

5 October 1984

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. A82/40 GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: MALCOLM CLEMENT BARTON HILLS

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal : Deputy President A.N. Hall

Dr A.H. Marsh (Member)

Mrs J. McClintock (Member)

Date : 5 October 1984

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review, namely that the annual rate of pension payable to the applicant as at the date of his retirement was $15890.70.

Allan N. Hall

Presiding Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. A82/40 GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: MALCOLM CLEMENT BARTON HILLS

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR UPERANNUATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

  1. On the 27th February 1981, the applicant in this matter was retired from the Public Service on the grounds of invalidity. On the 23rd April 1981, a letter was sent to the applicant from the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office (AGRBO) advising him that the pension to which he was entitled, under what we shall refer to as the modified Superannuation Act 1976 (the 1976 Act), had been determined at the rate of $15,890.70 per annum.
  2. The applicant claims that, as at the date of his retirement, he was entitled to a pension equal to 70% of his final annual rate of salary under s67(2) of the modified Act -that being the maximum rate of pension payable to a person such as the applicant with a period of 20 years prospective service. The Commissioner contends, in effect, that the 70% rate was required to be discounted, pursuant to s213(3)(a)(i) of the modified Act, by reason of the fact that when the applicant joined the Public Service in 1974, he had elected not to pay into the former Superannuation Fund a transfer value to which he became entitled on termination of his previous employment with the University of Tasmania.
  3. Except in one minor respect, the essential facts are not in dispute. Mr Hills was born on the 29th February 1932. He joined the Commonwealth Public Service on the 25th February 1974 and, shortly thereafter, became a contributor to the Superannuation Fund established pursuant to the Superannuation Act 1922 (the superseded Act). For a period of approximately nine years prior to joining the Public Service Mr Hills had been employed at the University of Tasmania, where he had been a contributor to the University's Staff Superannuation Scheme which was based on a life insurance policy. On his retirement from the University, he became entitled to a lump sum payment of $9,724.70 from that Scheme. Pursuant to the provisions of Part XA of the superseded Act - dealing amongst other things with the preservation of rights from previous employment - this lump sum benefit was deemed to be a transfer value by virtue of the provisions of s119D(1) (a) (iii) and s119D(3) of the superseded Act. As a consequence, Mr Hills was a person to whom the provisions of S119H of that Act applied. Accordingly, the succeeding provisions of Division 2 of Part XA of the Act applied to him.
  4. Pursuant to S119J, Mr Hills had the right to make an election to pay to the former Superannuation Board an amount equal to the amount of the transfer value. If he had made that election, he would have been required to pay that amount to the Board within the limited period determined by or in accordance with that section. By making that election and payment he would have received a number of fully paid up units of pension, as determined by the Board, for the purposes of the superseded Superannuation Fund (see s119J(3)). He declined to make the election and consequential payment of the transfer value to the Board. As a result, the provisions of S119K of the superseded Act applied to him.
  5. Section 119K(3) imposed a restriction on the number of units of pension which a person who has failed to make an election under s119J, could contribute under that Act. We were told that, acting pursuant to s119K(2)(a), the Superannuation Board had determined that the transfer value of $9,724.70 was equivalent to 24 units of pension. Having regard to his salary for superannuation purposes at the time when he joined the Public Service, Mr Hills would have been required to contribute for 100 units of pension (see s20). However, as the provisions of S119K applied to him, he was limited by operation of sub-sections (2) and (3) of that section to contributing for 76 units of pension at that date (see letter from Commissioner for Superannuation dated 24 September 1984). By the date of commencement of the 1976 Act (1 July 1976), the number of units for which Mr Hills was contributing under the superseded Act had increased, by reason of increments in his salary, to 101 units.
  6. From the date of commencement of the 1976 Act, no further contributions were payable by the applicant under the superseded Act (see S.19A of that Act). On 1 July, 1976, Mr Hills became an eligible employee within the meaning of paragraph (a) of the definition of "eligible employee" in s3(1) of the 1976 Act. He was, within the language of that paragraph of the definition, "a person, who immediately before the commencement of this Act, was an employee for the purposes of the superseded Act and has continued to be, and is, such an employee".
  7. As from 1 July, 1976, Mr Hills became liable to pay fortnightly basic contributions in an amount equal to 5% of his fortnightly rate of salary in accordance with ss 45 and 46 of the 1976 Act. He continued to make those contributions until his invalidity retirement on 27 February, 1981.
  8. It was essentially common ground that the benefit payable to Mr Hills following his retirement fell to be determined in accordance with the provisions of the 1976 Act as modified by the Superannuation (Former Contributors for Units of Pension) Regulations (Statutory Rules No. 281 of 1978) and the Superannuation (Period of Contributory Service) Regulations (Statutory Rules No. 105 of 1977). These regulations are deemed to have taken effect from and including 1 July 1976 (see reg. 2 of each of the Regulations). The Regulations were made pursuant to powers conferred in that behalf under s183 of the Act, which provides as follows:-

"183.(1) This section applies to a person who is or has ceased to be an eligible employee, being a person who is, or at any time has been, a person referred to in paragraph (a) of the definition of "eligible employee" in sub-section 3(1).

(2) The regulations may make provision for modifying this Act, or a provision of this Act specified in the regulations, in the application of this Act or that provision to and in relation- to a person to whom this section applies, or to and in relation to a prescribed class of persons to whom this section applies.

(3) The modifications that may be made by regulations in pursuance of sub-section (2) include, but are not limited to, modifications providing for the payment of contributions in addition to, or in substitution for, contributions that would otherwise be payable under this Act and for benefits in addition to, or in substitution for, benefits provided for by this Act."

9. As modified (so far as presently relevant), s66(1) of the Act provides as follows:-

"66.(1) Subject to section 79, where a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity before attaining his maximum retiring age, then, except in a case where sub-section (2) of this section applies, the person is entitled -

(a) if the person does not make an election under section 68 or 69 - to invalidity pension in accordance with section 67 or section 213 (3) or 214 (3) and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, a lump sum benefit in accordance with section 67."

It was common ground that neither s79 nor s66(2) has any relevance in the present case. It was also common ground that the applicant had not made an election under s68 or 69 and that the applicant had not paid supplementary contributions. The only question in issue is whether the invalidity pension payable to the applicant was payable in accordance with s67 or s213(3) or 214(3) of the modified 1976 Act.

10. Mr Mitchell. who appeared on behalf of the applicant, submitted that the modified s66(1)(a) gave no indication as to which of the sections referred to should be applied, and that the Commissioner therefore had a discretion, in the exercise of which he should have selected the method of calculating pension that was most beneficial to the applicant, namely s67(2). In our view, however, whilst s66(1) may not give any guidance on which section is to be applied, the modified s67 places the matter beyond doubt. That section, so far as presently relevant, provides as follows:-

"67.(1) This section applies to a person, not being a prescribed person (within the meaning of Division 8 of Part XII) to whom section 213 or 214 applies, who -

(a) is entitled to invalidity benefit by virtue of sub-section 66(1); and

(b) does not make an election under section 68 or 69.

...

(2) Where a person to whom this section applies is a person who became an employee for the purposes of the superseded Act on or after 14 December 1959, then, subject to sub-section (3) and (4), the annual rate of the pension to which the person is entitled is -

(a) if the period of the prospective service of the person is not less than 20 years - 70 per centum of that person's final rate of salary; or

(b) ..."
  1. As will have been noted, s67 does not apply to a person who is a "prescribed person" within the meaning of Division 8 of Part XII of the Act to whom s213 or 214 applies. The Commissioner formed the view that the applicant was a "prescribed person" to whom s.213 applied. Accordingly, in the Commissioner's opinion, s67 was incapable of applying to the applicant. This contention was disputed by Mr Mitchell.
  2. It will be convenient, at this stage, if we refer to the relevant provisions of Division 8 of Part XII of the modified Act, which are as follows:-

"212A.(1) In this Division, unless the contrary intention appears -

'prescribed person' means a person -

(a) who has ceased to be an eligible employee, being an eligible emp1oyee referred to in paragraph (a) of the definition of ‘eligible employee' in sub-section 3(1);

(b) who has not after so ceasing again become an eligible employee; and

(c) who -

( i) immediately before the commencing day, was, or would, but for sub-section 100J (2), 107K (2), 110 (2) or 119K (2) of the superseded Act, have been a contributor to the existing Fund under Part III of that Act; or

(ii) would, but for section 19A of the superseded Act, have become such a contributor on or after the commencing day; (emphasis added)

...

213.(1) This section applies to a prescribed person who, after becoming an employee for the purposes of the superseded Act, or, where' the prescribed person became such an employee on more than one occasion, after last becoming such an employee -

(a) made an election under section 69 of the superseded Act;
  • (b) by virtue of sub-section 100J(2), 107K(2), 110(2) or 119K(2) of the superseded Act was at any time not required or permitted to contribute to the existing Fund in respect of units of pension; or
  • (c) was at any time required to contribute to the existing Fund in respect of units of pension in accordance with sub-section 100J(3 ) , 107K(3), 110(3) or 119K(3) of the superseded Act, (emphasis added)
    • (2) ...
  • (3) Where a person, being a prescribed person to whom this section applies, is entitled to invalidity benefit by virtue of sub-section 66(1), the annual rate of the pension to which the person is entitled is -

(a) if the person does not make an election under section 68 or 69 - an amount per annum equal to whichever is the greater of -

(i) the amount by which the annual rate of the pension to which, but for this sub-section, the person would have been entitled exceeds an amount in dollars ascertained in accordance with the formula 91 x A; and

(ii) the amount per annum of the invalidity pension that would be payable to him if he were not a prescribed person but had become on the commencing day an eligible employee other than an eligible employee referred to in paragraph (a) of the definition of 'eligible employee1 in sub-section (3)(1); or

......

(4) For the purpose of the application in relation to a prescribed person to whom this section applies of a formula in this section, A is -

(a) in the case of such a person who made an election under s.69 of the superseded Act - .....; or

(b) in the case of any other such person -

(i) ; or

(ii) if sub-section 119K(2) or (3) of the superseded Act applies in relation to the person -the sum of the number of units of pensiqn specified in each of the determinations made by the Board in relation to the person under sub-section 119K( 2) of that Act." (emphasis added)

  1. Although he was obliged to concede that, taken literally, the definition of "prescribed person" was capable of applying to the applicant, Mr Mitchell submitted that the provisions of Division 8 could not have been intended to so apply. It was his submission that s67(2)(a) and, consequently, a pension at the rate of 70% of final salary, should be applied to Mr Hills.
  2. When pressed by the Tribunal for a reason why the definition of "prescribed person" should be read in such a way as to exclude the applicant, Mr Mitchell called in aid the principle that the Act should be construed beneficially. He referred us to Pearce on Statutory Interpretation, 2nd Edn p187.
  3. In our view, however, there is no justification in the present case for the Tribunal to ignore the plain words of the modified 1976 Act. There is no ambiguity in the language of the modified s67(1). The intention of the provision is, we think, quite clear. This is not a case where, faced with competing interpretations of somewhat opaque language, the Tribunal must decide which interpretation should be adopted (cf Re Butterworths Pty Ltd and Minister for Business and Consumer Affairs (1979) 2 ALD 612 at 618; Computer Manufacture and Design Pty Ltd and Comptroller-General of Customs (1983) 5 ALD 218 at 227). Section 67 provides, quite unequivocally, that it is not to apply to a person who is a "prescribed person" within the meaning of Division 8 of Part XII to whom s213 or 214 applies. To ignore those words, and the concession that the applicant comes within the terms of the definition, is not to adopt a beneficial construction of the statute; it is to set the language of the statute at nought. In our view, therefore, the modified s67 cannot be applied to the applicant. His pension entitlement falls to be determined under s213.
  4. It will be seen from s213(3) that, where the person who has retired does not make an election under s68 or s69 (which is the case with Mr Hills) , the annual rate of the pension payable is an amount per annum equal to whichever is the greater of the two amounts calculated in accordance with s 213(3)(a)(i) and (ii). For the purposes of applying the formula in sub-paragraph (3)(a)(i) (i.e. 91 x A), it was not disputed that the Commissioner was correct in concluding that, by application of sub-paragraph (4)(b)(ii), the quantity "A" for the purposes of the formula, was the number 24, that being the number of units of pension specified in the determination made by the former Superannuation Board in relation to the applicant under S119K (2) of the superseded Act. Somewhat indirectly, however, Mr Mitchell did challenge the correctness of the Commissioner's calculation for the purposes of sub-paragraph 3(a)(ii).
  5. The calculations made by the Commissioner for the purposes of s213(3)(a) were as follows:-

(i) Sub-paragraph (3)(a)(i);

It was common ground the amount of the annual rate of the pension to which, but for s213(3)(b), the applicant would have been entitled, was a pension calculated by reference to the modified s67(2). It was also common ground that, having regard to the modified definitions of "period of prospective service" and "period of contributory service" in s3(1) of the Act (see Statutory Rules No. 105 of 1977), the period of prospective service of the applicant for the purposes of the modified s67(2) was not less than 20 years and that, in accordance with the modified s67(2)(a), the annual rate of pension would have been 70% of the applicant's final annual rate of salary, namely $18,074.74. It was further common ground that the amount in dollars ascertained in accordance with the formula 91 x A was $2,184.00 (91 X 24). Thus the amount determined in accordance with sub-paragraph (3)(a)(i) was $18,074.70 less $2,184.00, namely $15,890.70.

(ii) Sub-paragraph (3)(a)(ii);

The Commissioner determined that the amount per annum of the invalidity pension that would be payable to the applicant if he were not a "prescribed person" for the purposes of Division 8, but had become on 1st July 1976 an "eligible employee" other than an eligible employee referred to in paragraph (a) of the definition of "eligible employee" in s3(1)(a), was $14,456.76. This amount was calculated by reference to the unmodified provisions of s67 and by having regard to the period of prospective service of the applicant (20 years according to the Commissioner) on the assumptions required to be made by s213(3)(a)(ii), namely that Mr Hills had become, on the commencing day of the 1976 Act (1 July 1976), an eligible employee other than an eligible employee to whom Division 8 of Part XII of the Act applied. Any employment prior to that date must be disregarded for the purposes of making this calculation. In addition, although the point was not expressly canvassed in argument, we think that it must be assumed, for the purposes of this calculation that the applicant did not pay in a transfer value from his University employment. Thus the applicant can receive no credit, in that calculation, for his University service.

In the present case, therefore, as the annual rate determined by the Commissioner pursuant to sub-paragraph (3)(a)(i) was higher than that calculated under sub-paragraph (3)(a)(ii), it was the former rate ($15,890.70 pa) that applied.

18. In our view, it becomes clear, in the light of these calculations, that the effect of s213(3)(a)(ii) is to ensure that the annual rate of pension payable to a person such as the applicant will be not less than the annual rate of pension to which he would have been entitled if he had never been a contributor under the superseded Act and had first become a contributor under the 1976 Act on 1 July 1976. It equally becomes clear that a person to whom s213 applies cannot expect to achieve the full rate of pension to which he would have been entitled if the provisions of Division 8 had not applied to him.

  1. However, Mr Mitchell submitted on behalf of the applicant that the applicant was, in fact, worse off in terms of the annual rate of pension, as determined by the Commissioner, than he would have been if he had joined the Service on 1 July 1976. According to the figures placed before us by Mr Mitchell, the annual rate of pension that would have been payable to the applicant had he first become an eligible employee on 1 July 1976 was $16,009.02. This figure represents 62% of Mr Hills' final annual rate of salary. The Commissioner's calculation, however, was based on 56% of final salary.
  2. Reference to the unmodified provisions of s67 and Schedule 4 of the Act leaves us in no doubt that the percentage rate adopted by Mr Mitchell was mistaken. Schedule 4 converts the number of complete years of prospective service into a percentage of final salary in order to determine the rate of pension payable. The Schedule discloses that 56% - the percentage applied by the Commissioner - is the percentage equivalent of 20 complete years of prospective service. If it be assumed (as s213(3)(a)(ii) requires) that Mr Hills commenced his service on 1 July 1976, that calculation is undoubtedly correct. He had 4 years 7 months from 1 July 1976 to 27February 1981 - the date of retirement - and 16 years from 28 February 1981 to 20 February 1997 - the date of his 65th birthday (see definitions of "period of prospective service" in s3(1) of the unmodified Act).
  3. 62% on the other hand, which Mr Mitchell used in his calculation in order to achieve a pension of $16,009.02 p.a., has no equivalent number of years in Schedule 4. If it did, it would represent a number of years service in excess of 24 complete years. On no view of the facts could Mr Hills be seen as having that number of years prospective service.
  4. In his opening address Mr Mitchell referred to a percentage of 60.2% (not 62%) as being the applicant's percentage entitlement. Schedule 4 shows that 60.2% is the percentage equivalent of 23 complete years of prospective service, which is the applicant's period of prospective service if it is calculated from 25 February 1974, the date when he joined the Public Service. By a process of transmogrification, 60.2% seems to have become 62% somewhere along the way. We therefore put the 62% figure ($16,009.02) aside as irrelevant.
  5. 60.2% of the applicant's salary, for what that calculation may be worth, equals $15,544.24 - a lesser amount than that calculated by the Commissioner under sub-paragraph (3)(a)(i)($15,890.70). Thus, even if regard could be paid to Mr Hills' service prior to 1 July 1976 for the purpose of making the calculation required under sub-paragraph (3)(a)(ii) (which, as earlier noted it clearly cannot - see para 16(ii)), the applicant's contention that he is worse off under s213 than he would have been, if his pension had been determined under the unmodified provisions of s67 on the assumption that he had joined the Service on 1 July 1976, cannot be sustained. In our view, the Commissioner's calculations were correct. The annual rate of pension payable to the applicant pursuant to s213(3)(a) was properly determined as $15,890.70.
  6. As an argument of last resort Mr Mitchell submitted that, if the Tribunal were to take the view that the provisions of the Act as modified by regulations pursuant to s183 of the 1976 Act had been correctly applied by the Commissioner, those regulations should be found to have exceeded the regulation making powers conferred by the Act. In support of this submission he relied upon the decision of the Supreme Court of Queensland in R. v The Mining Warden at Gladstone: Ex p Midcoast Lands Pty Ltd (1979) Qd R 427 at 433/4. The substance of the submission, as we understood it, was that the regulations which modified the provisions of the principal Act diminished or qualified the benefits conferred by Parliament itself. In legislation intended to have a beneficial operation, it was Mr Mitchell's submission that Parliament could not have intended such a result to follow.
  7. In our view, however, the foundation to this submission has been demonstrated in what we have already said, to be erroneous. There is no evidence that the modified provisions of the Act do operate to the detriment of a person such as the applicant. On the contrary, as we have indicated, the applicant is in our view better off in terms of his annual rate of pension under the modified provisions of the Act than he would have been under the unmodified provisions of s67 (see para 24).
  8. More importantly however, the submission makes too light of the fact that the regulation-making power is conferred in terms that plainly contemplate that the provisions of the Act may be modified by regulation. Section 183 is an example of a provision sometimes referred to as a "Henry VIII" clause (see Pearce on Delegated Legislation, paragraphs 12 to 15). Whilst it may be unusual for Parliament to confer power upon the Executive to modify, by regulations, the provisions of the Act itself, it was not suggested to us that it was beyond the constitutional powers of Parliament so to do.
  9. In our view, no arguments have been placed before us' that provide any basis for concluding that the regulations exceed the regulation-making power conferred by s183 of the Act. Moreover, as we have said, we consider that the applicant would be in a worse position than he presently is, if the regulations were invalid.
  10. For the reasons we have given we propose therefore to affirm the decision under review.

Summary

  1. Because of the complexity of the legislation and the inevitable complexity of our reasons for decision, we think that it may be of assistance to the applicant if we were to set out, in as plain English as possible, our conclusions on the substantial issues raised in these proceedings.
  2. Firstly, we think it is clear that, in respect of persons such as the applicant who were former contributors for a pension under the superseded Act, the modified provisions of the 1976 Act were intended to provide a guaranteed minimum pension based on the person's period of prospective service calculated from 1 July 1976 (see s213(3)( a)( ii)) . Had the applicant paid the transfer value in 1974, he would not have been subject to the restriction that he was as to the number of units of pension for which he was required to contribute (see S119K) and he would not have been a "prescribed person" to whom s.213 applied. He would have been entitled, as we see it, to a pension at the rate of 70% of his final salary in accordance with the modified s67(2).
  3. Because the transfer value was not paid, Mr Hills lost the benefit of that earlier service so far as his future pension entitlements were concerned and his pension fell to be determined in accordance with s213(3) (a). The first of the two calculations required to be made under that provision takes 70% of the applicant's final salary as its starting point and then discounts that figure by an amount calculated by reference to the formula 91 x A referred to in s213(3)(a)(i) . This formula is evidently intended to reflect the reduction to the full rate of pension that is considered appropriate because of the non-payment of the transfer value. As the amount so determined ($18,047.70 - $2,184.00 = $15,890.70) exceeds what we have referred to as the guaranteed minimum calculated in accordance with s213(3)(a)(ii) ($14,456.76), it is the discounted 70% rate of pension that applies.

32. From the correspondence included in the T-documents it is apparent that Mr Hills feels he has been penalised because of the fact that, when he joined the Commonwealth Public Service, his previous contributions to the University of Tasmania Superannuation Scheme gave rise to a transfer value under the superseded Act. The substance of his complaint in this regard is set out in his letter to the respondent dated 3 February 1981 (Ex T4/94). Mr Hills pointed to:-

" ...the apparent inequity arising from the fact that the qualifying service I had with the University of Tasmania seems to reduce the amount of superannuation to which you state I would be entitled. If I had not served with the University but had come straight into the Public Service in 1974 there would have been no reduction. It seems very unfair that with longer service I receive lower benefits."

33. In one sense this complaint may appear to have some substance. By failing to make an election under S119J of the superseded Act, the applicant has incurred a restriction upon his ultimate retirement benefits. It is equally true, as we understand the position, that if, when he joined the Public Service, he had not been entitled to a transfer value from his previous employment, he would not have suffered such a restriction.

  1. However, the point which the applicant appears to overlook is that if he had not had a transfer value, he would have been required to contribute for 100 units of pension, not 76. Accordingly, his contributions between the date when he became a contributor to the Fund established under the superseded Act and 1 July 1976, when the 1976 Act came into operation, were less than they would otherwise have been.
  2. Furthermore, he has had the benefit of the payment from the University Superannuation Scheme since 1974. At the time when that money was received by Mr Hills it would, we think, have been regarded as a substantial amount. For better or worse, Mr Hills accepted a limitation upon his ultimate retirement benefits when he decided to retain the payout from the University Scheme and not to make an election under S119J of the superseded Act. This limitation has been given substance in the modified provisions of the 1976 Act by the provisions of Division 8 of Part XII, particularly s213 so far as the applicant was concerned. In our view those provisions have been correctly applied by the Commissioner and the applicant is better off in terms of his ultimate pension than he would have been if he had joined the service on 1 July 1976.
