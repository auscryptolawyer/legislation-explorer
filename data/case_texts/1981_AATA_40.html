Re Stephen Ronald Watson and Commissioner of Superannuation [1981] AATA 40 (2 June 1981)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: STEPHEN RONALD WATSON

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. Q80/21

Superannuation Act 1922 as amended

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Honourable J.B.K. Williams

Dr M. Glick

Mr I. Prowse

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation Act 1922 as amended - decision that applicant's health and physical fitness were such that he be accepted as a contributor to Provident Account - decision made following medical examination in 1973 - by administrative error applicant informed that he had been accepted as a contributor to Superannuation Fund and deductions made from salary accordingly - error not discovered until 1978 - original decision confirmed by respondent in 1980 - hearing by Tribunal in 1981 when medical evidence of applicant's then condition adduced - respondent's contention that 1981 evidence is evidence irrelevant to the issues raised - decision that 1981 evidence was relevant to issues raised - decision of respondent set aside and decision substituted that applicant was fit to be accepted as a contributor to Superannuation Fund.

HEARING

CANBERRA

2:6:1981

ORDER

The decision of the Tribunal is that the decision of the respondent taken on 18 November 1980 confirming the decision of 3 July 1973 that the health and physical fitness of the applicant are such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account be set aside and that there be substituted for that decision a decision that the health and physical fitness of the applicant are such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund under Part III of the Superannuation Act 1922 (as amended).

DECISION

The applicant in this matter, Mr Stephen Ronald Watson, joined the Commonwealth Public Service as an exempt employee on 14 May, 1970 and in 1973 applied, pursuant to Section 4(5) of the Superannuation Act 1922, for a direction that he be deemed an employee within the meaning of the Section.

2. He was required to undergo a medical examination by an approved legally qualified medical practitioner in order to determine whether his health and physical fitness were such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund under para. III of the Act or to the Provident Account.

3. He was medically examined on 29 June, 1973 by a Commonwealth Medical Officer. That officer, for reasons which will later appear, recommended the applicant's acceptance to the Provident Account and on 3 July, 1973 a certificate was issued under Section 5 of the Superannuation Act 1922 that the applicant's health and physical fitness were such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account. In July 1973 his employing Department was advised to that effect. However, by administrative error the applicant was informed that he had been accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund and contributions to the fund were deducted from his salary. That error was not discovered until 1978. On 21 September, 1978 the Regional Executive Officer of the Department of Primary Industry, Brisbane, wrote to the applicant in the following terms:-

"Further to our discussions on your arrears of contributions, this is to confirm that the situation occured due to an error by this Department. In July 1973, the Department was told that you had been accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account. Due to an oversight, you were informed that you had been accepted for the Superannuation Fund and your contributions were based on that premise.

Until this month, you could not have known that you had been accepted for the Provident Account rather than the Superannuation Scheme."

4. In the result it appears that the contributions which should have been made by the applicant as a contributor to the Provident Account exceeded the contributions actually made by him as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund by an amount of $440.76 and he has now been asked to make good this sum.

5. Upon learning the true position the applicant wrote to the respondent a letter dated 22 September, 1978 seeking a review of the decision to accept him as a contributor to the Provident Account. This letter was treated by the respondent as an application to reconsider the decision under the provisions of Section 154(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976. It is conceded by the respondent (properly, we think) that the decision was a "reviewable decision" within the meaning of Section 154 being a decision made under the earlier Act and that the application for reconsideration was made within the time prescribed by Section 154(2).

6. The respondent reconsidered the matter and on 18 November, 1980 confirmed the decision taken on 3 July, 1973 that the health and physical fitness of the applicant was such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Fund. Being dissatisfied with that decision, the applicant now seeks a review of it by this Tribunal pursuant to Section 154(6) of the Superannuation Act 1976.

7. As has been said, the applicant was medically examined by a Commonwealth Medical Officer on 29 June, 1973. A form SB6 was completed in connection with this examination, partly by the applicant in answer to questions and partly by the examining medical officer. In answer to questions the applicant revealed that he had suffered arthritis due to an infection of "Ross River" Fever. The medical officer's comment on this was "probably a viral arthritis 3 years ago - no recurrence". The applicant also stated he had had an attack of asthma at the age of 6 weeks which had not recurred until the year 1971. The medical officer's comment on this was "minimal broncho-spasms for a period in 1971 - not otherwise". It was also revealed by the applicant that he had been injured in his right eye when poked by a stick. To this the medical officer's comment was "serious eye injury - aged 9 - penetration - resolved and has had no trouble since age of 10". The applicant also stated that he had suffered knee and ankle injuries whilst playing football. To this the medical officer's comment was "sprains to (R) knee and (R) ankle football in 1968 - now asynytomatic". Also recorded on the form SB6 is the medical officer's recommendation - "Provident Account due history of polyarthritis (viral) and eye and ligamentous injuries to knee and ankle. There is also in evidence before us another form in which are recorded the medical officer's clinical notes in the following terms:-

"P.H. Arthritis. "Ross River" Fever. Asthma, M.V. accident. Leg and facial injuries. Eye injury. Glandular Fever. German Measles. Knee and ankle injuries (football). Arthritis probably of viral origin and unlikely to be significant. In view of arthritis, ligamentous injuries. Rec accept Provident Fund."

In summary, therefore, it appears that the medical officer's recommendation that Mr Watson be accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account was based upon the applicant's eye injury, the possibility of the development of arthritis as a consequence of having suffered "Ross River" disease and his ligamentous injuries from football.

8. There was in existence at the time of the medical examination a handbook for the guidance of medical officers when conducting examinations for the purpose of entry to the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account. It is not, we think, necessary to set out the relevant provisions of the handbook in full; suffice it to say that in the case for acceptance to the Superannuation Fund standards to apply were strict standards designed to ensure so far as possible that the person concerned would be capable of performing his or her full duties up to the then normal retiring age (65). In the case of acceptance for the Provident Account the standards were less strict in that they were designed to ensure that the person concerned would be capable of carrying out his or her full duties for a period of 7 years. It should be stated that at the time of the examination of the applicant in 1973 he was then about 22 years old.

9. On 2 May, 1975 the applicant was again medically examined, on this occasion for permanent appointment to the Commonwealth Public Service. On this occasion the applicant was found to be fit and the medical officer recommended the applicant's acceptance for permanent appointment "under Superannuation Fund conditions".

10. In his request for reconsideration by the respondent of the 1973 decision the applicant made the following points:-

(1) That he was not aware until September 1978 that he was not a contributor to the Superannuation Fund;

(2) That he had always enjoyed good health, suffering no serious sickness, disease or accident, and the condition which lead to the recommendation for his placement in the Provident Account could not have been serious;

(3) That he had not had excessive sick leave; and

(4) That in May 1975 he was passed fit for permanent appointment.

11. As has been said earlier, the respondent, following reconsideration of the matter, decided to confirm the decision taken on 3 July, 1973 that the health and physical fitness of the applicant was such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account. His reasons for that decision are set out in the papers before us. In paragraph 5(e) of those reasons it is stated that on 3 July, 1973 the Delegate certified, in pursuant of Section 36 of the superseded Act, that the health and physical fitness of the applicant was such as to justify his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account. In paragraph 5(f) of the reasons it is stated that having regard to the above that the respondent was satisfied in effect that the original decision was the correct one and should be confirmed. It is also stated in the reasons that with respect to the claims made by the applicant that he enjoyed good health and had not taken excessive sick leave, these were matters which would have been considered when determining his acceptance to the Provident Account on 3 July, 1973. As to the point made by the applicant that he had been passed medically fit for permanent appointment to the Public Service in May 1975, the respondent commented that the medical assessments made at that time were of no effect as the applicant was already a contributor to the Provident Account and that the only means by which he could have been transferred to the Superannuation Fund would have been as a result of an application to the Superannuation Board under Section 79(2) of the 1922 Act or to dispute the original decision to place him in the Provident Account under Section 141 of that Act.

12. At the hearing before the Tribunal oral evidence was given by Dr J.R. Meyers, Assistant Director (Medical), Commonwealth Health Department, Brisbane. From his evidence it appears that the medical officer who had conducted the 1973 medical examination has since died. Dr Meyers explained in some detail the standards applied by Commonwealth Medical Officers when conducting medical examinations for the purpose of determining fitness for entry to either the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account. He was shown the clinical notes taken by Dr Pye in 1973 and was asked his opinion as to the most significant conditions when determining fitness for entry to the Superannuation Fund or the Provident Account. He said that he thought there were two, namely, the history of asthma with a recurrence in 1971 and the history of injury to ankle and knee. As to the asthma condition he said that it was well known, that it was a most unpredictable disease, and that a person could suffer from it for many years, be free of it for many years and suddenly redevelop it. As to the history of injury to the ankle and knee he thought this of significance as there may be a subsequent development of osteoarthritis in the affected joints. He added that the relationship between traumatic joint episodes in football and the subsequent development of osteoarthritis in later years was well known. In summary he considered that the conclusions reached by Dr Pye were by no means unreasonable.

13. Cross-examined by the applicant, Dr Meyers agreed that the "Ross River" Fever referred to in the clinical notes was not of prognostic consequence from the point of view of subsequent development of arthritis. He also agreed that there did not appear to be "any serious sequelae" to the applicant's eye injury.

14. The applicant tendered in evidence two documents, the first a letter from his own doctor, Dr J.R. Rothnie, dated 20 March, 1981 and the second a report dated 12 March, 1981 by Dr David Clowes, a Radiologist, made following x-rays taken of the applicant's knees and ankles. These documents were admitted in evidence following objection by Mr Wilde who appeared for the respondent that evidence of the applicant's present condition was irrelevant to the issues involved in the present case.

15. Dr Rothnie's letter is in the following terms:-

"Mr Watson has been a patient of mine for ten years and in such excellent physical health that I could not believe he was medically failed for the superannuation fund.

I believe the grounds were "arthritis and ligamentous injuries." Mr Watson has never had arthritis but arthralgia from "Ross River" Fever, and this is never followed by joint damage (arthritis) as recent x-rays confirm beyond doubt. Ligamentous injuries in the past were transient and have left no residual weakness or damage of any nature, so much so that I would defy anyone to nominate what ligaments were damaged on a physical examination.

Mr Watson has said to have had asthma once as an infant, but as this never recurred since I very much doubt the accuracy of the original diagnosis.

In summary I can say without hesitation that I rarely see a patient who is as physically fit as Mr Watson."

Dr Clowes' report is as follows:-

"Knees: The cortical margins of the femoral condyles and tibial plateaus are normal with no erosion or joint space narrowing shown. Soft tissues also show no abnormality. A small osteophyte is shown on the proximal left tibia. An osteochondroma is not entirely excluded.

Ankles: No bone or joint abnormality shown. There is a small osteophyte shown at the insertion of the achillaes tendon in the left heel. The subtalar joints also appear normal.

Conclusions: No arthritic changes seen in the knees or ankles."

16. Dr Meyers was cross-examined by the applicant concerning the contents of these documents and was invited to examine the x-ray photographs that had been taken. He said that, having regard to the location of the osteophyte on the proximal left tibia and the osteophyte shown at the insertion of the achillaes tendon in the left heel that they would have much less significance than if associated directly with a joint and agreed that it was quite possible that the applicant would suffer no ill-effect from them.

17. It should also be noted that with relation to the asthma condition referred to in the clinical notes the following question and answer appears in the evidence of Dr Meyers:-

In relation to his asthma all these years, and an episode of shortness of breath in a child of 6 weeks is not uncommon with an upper respiratory tract infection which could be misdiagnosed as asthma, and Mr Watson is denying that the episode in 1971 was asthma, rather more bronchitis. I just wondered if you feel, as I said before, that the history of asthma would have been of such significance as to warrant consideration for refusing him entry to the superannuation scheme? ---Without examining him at the time, I could not answer that categorically yes or no. Over the years I have found asthma, as I say, to be entirely unpredictable and to lead to considerable invalidity and considerable sick leave. It is something which I have always given the greatest attention to, particularly in the first instance, for establishing the diagnosis with the greatest degree of certainty as possible.

18. It is apparent that the relevance of the medical evidence adduced by the applicant concerning his health and fitness in 1981 becomes of considerable importance in the determination of this case. As has been stated, it is the contention of the respondent that this evidence is irrelevant and that consideration can only be given to evidence of the applicant's condition at the time of the examination in 1973.

19. At the conclusion of the hearing, Mr Wilde who appeared for the respondent was asked to furnish written submissions in support of the respondent's contention. This has now been done.

20. In the submissions it is stated that the respondent accepts as a general proposition that the Tribunal is not restricted to the material or evidence before the original decision-maker and that additional material or evidence may be adduced at the hearing. It is asserted, however, that this must be relevant to the decision under review. It is then said that the decision under review is a 1973 decision confirmed by the respondent in 1980 and that admission of "post decision" evidence in this case does not assist in the performance of the decision-maker's function because it does not affect the assessment of risk at the time when the applicant sought to enter the Superannuation Scheme; at best it is relevant to the applicant's separate and severable right to review under Section 16(6) and Section 16(8) of the Superannuation Act 1976.

21. It is necessary to consider the question posed for decision by the original decision-maker in 1973. That question was whether "the health and physical fitness of the person are such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the fund under Part III of this Act or to the Provident Account". This question was required to be answered after consideration of a report of an approved legally qualified medical practitioner. He was provided with a handbook containing guidelines upon which to base his report. The handbook has no statutory authority but was an administrative document purely. However, when regard is had to the Act as a whole it is evident that the legislative intent behind requiring the medical examination was to ascertain whether or not a prospective contributor would be likely to become a premature drain on the scheme without contributing to it over the years until his normal time of retirement. It thus might be said that the handbook for the guidance of medical officers reflected the evident legislative policy.

22. Considered in this light, the task before the original decision-maker was to make, after consideration of a medical opinion, a prospective assessment of the capacity of the person from the stand point of health and fitness to perform the duties of his office during the expected currency of his engagement as an officer of the Commonwealth Public Service i.e. - in the case of a person admitted to the Superannuation Scheme and until normal retirement at age 65 and in the case of a person admitted to the Provident Account for a period of 7 years.

23. It is properly conceded by the respondent that this Tribunal is now standing in the shoes of the original decision-maker and it is now for the Tribunal to make this assessment.

24. The argument advanced for the respondent is that the Tribunal must approach its task as if it were considering the matter in 1973 unaided by any information concerning the health and fitness of the applicant which may have been since acquired. This argument seems to us necessarily to involve the proposition that the Tribunal's task is to say whether or not, on the evidence available in 1973, the decision-maker was right or wrong in his decision.

25. This proposition is completely acceptable if the principles of judicial review are applied to the functions of the Tribunal. But there is judicial authority to the contrary. It was said by Brennan J. in Bryan Lawlor Automotive Pty. Ltd. and the Collector of Customs (N.S.W.) (1978) 1 ALD 167 at page 177:-

"The questions which are relevant in a review by the Tribunal include questions of the kind to which a court directs its attention when exercising a supervisory jurisdiction but the Tribunal's questions range over a wider subject matter."

Again it was said by Smithers J. in Collector of Customs and Bryan Lawlor Automotive Pty. Ltd. 2 ALD (1) at page 23:-

"It is important to observe that the Tribunal is not constituted as a body to review decisions according to the principles applicable to judicial review."

26. The nature of the Tribunal's functions was also considered by the Federal Court of Australia in Drake -v- Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs 2 ALD (60). In the joint judgement of Bowen C.J. and Deane J. the following passage occurs at page 68:-

"The function of the Tribunal is, as we have said, an administrative one. It is to review the administrative decision that is under attack before it. In that review, the Tribunal is not restricted to consideration of the questions which are relevant to a judicial determination of whether a discretionary power allowed by statute has been validly exercised. Except in a case where only one decision can lawfully be made, it is not ordinarily part of the function of a court either to determine what decision should be made in the exercise of an administrative discretion in a given case or, where a decision has been lawfully made in pursuance of a permissible policy, to adjudicate upon the merits of the decision or the propriety of the policy. That is primarily an administrative rather than a judicial function. It is the function which has been entrusted to the Tribunal.

The question for the determination of the Tribunal is not whether the decision which the decision-maker made was the correct or preferable one on the material before him. The question for the determination of the Tribunal is whether that decision was the correct or preferable one on the material before the Tribunal."

Reference might also here be made to the decision of the Tribunal in A.J. Hanlon -v- Commissioner for Superannuation (decision of 7 December, 1979).

27. Reference might here be made to relevant provisions of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act. Under Section 43(1) of that Act the Tribunal may exercise all the power and discretions that are conferred by any relevant enactment on the person who made the decision and shall make a decision in writing affirming the decision under review varying that decision or setting aside that decision and may make a decision in substitution for the decision so set aside or may remit the matter for reconsideration in accordance with directions or recommendations. Under Sub-Section 6 of Section 43 it is provided that a decision of a person as varied by the Tribunal or a decision made by the Tribunal in substitution for the decision of a person shall, with exceptions not relevant or present considerations, be deemed to be a decision of that person and unless otherwise ordered has effect on and from the day on which the decision under review has or had effect. Under Section 40 of the Act the Tribunal has power to summons witnesses to give evidence before it.

28. As has been said, the task before the original decision-maker was to make a prospective assessment of the capacity of the applicant from the stand point of health and fitness to perform the duties of his office during the currency of his expected engagement as an officer of the Commonwealth Public Service. The Tribunal is now called upon to perform the same task and it is called upon to do so in the year 1981 and so doing it is, we think, the plain duty of the Tribunal to consider all relevant evidence.

29. It is said by the respondent that evidence of the applicant's physical state in 1981 is irrelevant to the Tribunal's consideration. We fail to see why this should be so. This may well have been the case if the original question was the formation of an opinion as to the applicant's physical condition on a specified date, that is the date of the original decision but clearly this is not the question which was posed; rather the question was the formation of a view as to the likelihood or otherwise that the applicant's health and physical fitness would, at some future time, become so impaired as to cause inability to carry out his assigned tasks or to bring about his premature retirement from the Public Service. He is now about 30 years old and has a prospective working career in the Public Service of about another 35 years. We cannot agree that evidence of his health and physical fitness presented to us at the hearing is irrelevant to the question in issue and we therefore propose to have regard to it in deciding this case.

30. As related earlier in these reasons, the original decision was based upon the report of the Commonwealth Medical Officer of his examination in 1973. The matters which apparently gave rise to the Commonwealth Medical Officer's recommendation related to arthritis, ligamentous injuries and eye injury. The arthritis mentioned was apparently allied with "Ross River" Fever stated by the applicant to have been suffered by him. However, Dr Meyers, in his evidence, agreed that any condition of arthritis following "Ross River" Fever would be of a merely transitory nature and one unlikely to have any long-term effect upon the applicant. As to the ligamentous injuries, the Commonwealth Medical Officer's concern in this respect apparently flowed from the football injuries said by the applicant to have been suffered by him. It is clear from the report of Dr Clowes that recent x-ray examinations have revealed no degenerative changes in the knees or ankles. Dr Meyers, after examination of the x-ray photographs, agreed with this. Further, Dr Meyers attached no significance to the eye injury.

31. Dr Meyers did express concern with regard to the applicant's stated history of having suffered from asthma in his early childhood. As earlier related however, he stated that he could express no firm view on this aspect of the matter as he had not examined the applicant. It is perhaps significant that the Commonwealth Medical Officer who did examine the applicant, namely Dr Pye, apparently attached no weight to this. Dr Rothnie who has been the medical adviser to the applicant for some 10 years stated that the asthmatic condition, the diagnosis of which he very much doubted, had never recurred.

32. Upon this state of the evidence we are satisfied that the applicant's health and physical fitness are such as to warrant his admission to the Superannuation Fund. In so saying we accept that on the information available to the Commonwealth Medical Officer in 1973 grounds for doubt may have appeared; but whatever doubt may have been felt by him it appears to us that this has now been resolved by the recent medical evidence and the evidence concerning his acceptance in 1975 as a permanent officer of the Commonwealth Public Service.

33. For these reasons we would set aside the original decision and substitute for it a decision that the applicant's health and physical fitness are such as to justify his being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund under Part III of the Act.

34. It has been submitted on behalf of the respondent that there are other avenues open to the applicant under the Superannuation Act 1976 and that it is appropriate for the Tribunal to enable the applicant to explore these avenues rather than to accede to his present application. Assuming that these avenues are open it is, nevertheless, the fact that the Tribunal has before it a "reviewable decision" and the applicant is entitled to an adjudication upon that decision.
