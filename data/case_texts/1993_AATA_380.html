Re Taxation Appeals [1993] AATA 380; (1993) 27 ATR 1190 (22 December 1993)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. VT93/139

AAT No. 9198

Number of pages - 20

Taxation

[1993] AATA 380; (1993) 27 ATR 1190

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

JUSTICE OLNEY (Presidential Member)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - applicant and wife directors and only shareholders of company trustee of their family trust - applicant's services as a media personality provided to radio station pursuant to contract with trustee company - trustee company had management and control of applicant's performing services - termination of agreement with radio station to enable applicant's services to be provided to a rival radio station - payment by second station to trustee company - whether payment was a capital sum or income derived by trustee company - whether payment was attributed to the applicant.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, s 6(1), Part IVA ss 189(1), (2), 190, 260

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, ss 37, 43

Taxation Administration Act 1953, s 142K

Taxation Laws Amendment (No. 3) Act 1991, s 114

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Smith [1981] HCA 10; (1981) 147 CLR 578

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Inkster (1989) 89 ATR 5142

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Elton (1990) 90 ACT 4078

HEARING

MELBOURNE, 3 and 4 November 1993

22:12:1993

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr P.K. Searle

Solicitors for the Applicant: Mills Oakley McKay

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr G.J. Davies

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government

Solicitors

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the objection decision under review.

DECISION

JUSTICE OLNEY This is an application for review of a decision of the respondent (the Deputy Commissioner) disallowing an objection by the applicant (the taxpayer) to the inclusion in the assessment of the taxpayer's assessable income for the year ending 30 June 1986 of an amount of $125,000. The objection decision was referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (the Tribunal) pursuant to the now repealed provisions of s 189(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Tax Act). The referral of the decision was deemed by the provisions of s 189(2) to constitute the making of an application to the Tribunal by the taxpayer for review of the decision. Despite the subsequent repeal of the relevant provisions of the Tax Act and of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 the repealed provisions continue to apply to the hearing and determination of the application (Taxation Laws Amendment (No 3) Act 1991, s 114).

2. In order to comply with the provisions of ss (2C) which was at the relevant time taken to be added to s 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (the AAT Act) for the purposes of proceedings such as these, it is necessary that the Tribunal ensure, so far as it is practical to do so, that the Tribunal's reasons for its decision are framed so as not to disclose the identity of the person who requested the review of the objection decision (Taxation Administration Act 1953, s 14ZK now repealed).

3. By reason of the nature of the taxpayer's employment and his very high public profile it is unlikely that any device that the Tribunal may resort to will ensure that his identity will not be disclosed. Further, as the proceedings have already involved two appeals to the Federal Court of Australia, which are reported under the taxpayer's name, there is no practical way in which the policy of s 14ZK can be given any real effect.

4. Notwithstanding the observations in the preceding paragraph, neither the taxpayer, members of his family nor companies directly associated with him will be referred to by name. To some extent proper names will be used but they will be of significance only to those who are already sufficiently familiar with the taxpayer's affairs as to be able to identify him in any event.

BACKGROUND

5. In his income tax return for the year ending 30 June 1986 the taxpayer disclosed a taxable income of $242,763. Accompanying the return was a detailed statement in which he disclosed the receipt on 9 May 1986 of the sum of $125,000 by a company (T Co) of which he and his wife were the directors and which was the trustee of a discretionary family trust, (the trust) of which the taxpayer, his wife and his daughter were beneficiaries. The taxpayer's statement set out in detail reasons why it was said that the sum received was not part of his taxable income.

6. On 15 April 1987 the Deputy Commissioner issued a notice of assessment of income tax based upon a taxable income of $367,763. The assessment was accompanied by an adjustment sheet showing that the taxable income as returned had been "Adjusted to include trust income - $125,000" which sum was added to the amount returned to give a total taxable income of $367,763.

7. By letter dated 12 June 1987 the taxpayer's accountants, on his behalf, gave notice to the Deputy Commissioner of objection to the assessment claiming that the $125,000 should not have been included in the taxpayer's assessable income. The notice of objection set out comprehensive reasons in support of the objection.

8. On 6 April 1988 the Deputy Commissioner advised the taxpayer that the objection had been disallowed. The taxpayer was dissatisfied with the decision on the objection and on 15 April 1988 requested that the matter be referred to the Tribunal.

9. The Tribunal, constituted by Deputy President Thompson (the first Tribunal) heard evidence on 17 and 18 December 1990 and on 18 January 1991 gave its decision whereby it set aside the Deputy Commissioner's decision and allowed the objection in full.

10. The Deputy Commissioner appealed to the Federal Court against the decision of the first Tribunal and on 3 February 1992 Jenkinson J allowed the appeal, set aside the Tribunal's decision and ordered, inter alia, that the matter be remitted to the Tribunal to be determined according to law.

11. The matter came before the Tribunal, constituted by Senior Member Balmford, (the second Tribunal) for further hearing on 25 and 26 August 1992 and on 28 October 1992 gave its decision whereby it affirmed the decision of the Deputy Commissioner.

12. A further appeal to the Federal Court was instituted, on this occasion by the taxpayer, and this was heard by a Full Court (Sweeney, Keely and Gray JJ). The Full Court allowed the appeal on 23 July 1993 and in so doing set aside the second Tribunal's decision and remitted the matter to the Tribunal to be heard and decided again.

13. The matter came before the Tribunal as presently constituted for hearing on 3 and 4 November 1993.

14. It is unnecessary to examine the reasoning which formed the bases of the two earlier Tribunal decisions and the two successful appeals. The proceedings now before the Tribunal require that the Tribunal consider the material placed before it and to decide the matter free of any earlier conclusions of fact or law expressed by other members of the Tribunal. By consent of the parties, the Tribunal has before it all of the evidence and documents that were adduced at the two previous Tribunal hearings. Oral evidence was given by the taxpayer before the first Tribunal and I have available a transcript of same. Whilst I did not have the advantage of observing the taxpayer give evidence (as was the case with the first Tribunal but not the second) I am not in a position to make any assessment of his credibility except to the extent that other objective evidence leads to a particular conclusion. No additional evidence was adduced at the hearing on 3 and 4 November 1993.

THE FACTS OF THE CASE

15. To a very large extent the facts of the case are not in dispute. There are however some areas of crucial importance as to which a detailed analysis of the evidence will be made but to the extent there is no contest between the parties I find the matters set out in the following paragraphs numbered 16 to 28 (inclusive) to be fact.

16. At all material times since 1969 the taxpayer has followed the occupation of a radio and television announcer and can fairly be described as a well known media personality.

17. T Co was incorporated on 9 November 1976 and at all material times the taxpayer and his wife have been its directors and shareholders.

18. The trust was settled on 1 July 1980 and at all material times T Co has been, and has carried on business as, the trustee of the trust. The taxpayer, his wife and child are, and have at all times been, the beneficiaries of the trust.

19. On 2 February 1981 T Co and the proprietor of a Melbourne radio station (Radio X) executed an agreement under which T Co agreed to provide the services of the taxpayer as a radio broadcaster for the Radio X. The taxpayer guaranteed the due performance and observance by T Co of its obligations under the agreement.

20. On 23 March 1983 and again in August 1985, T Co and Radio X entered into further similar agreements. The latter agreement is hereafter referred to as the August 1985 agreement.

21. On 21 March 1986, the Deputy Commissioner issued assessments and notices of assessment for the years ended 30 June 1983 to 30 June 1985 (inclusive) deeming the whole of the income derived by the trust from the personal exertion of the taxpayer in those years to be the assessable income of the taxpayer by reason of the provisions of s 260 and Part IVA of the Tax Act. After some negotiation between the Deputy Commissioner and the taxpayer's accountants, the taxpayer accepted the correctness of the amended assessments.

22. On 14 April 1986 the taxpayer had a discussion with one White who represented a national radio network (Radio Z). It was agreed that the taxpayer would cease acting as a radio broadcaster for Radio X and would broadcast for Radio Z.

23. On 30 April 1986 the taxpayer ceased performing broadcasting services for Radio X.

24. On 9 May 1986 Radio Z paid the sum of $125,000 to T Co.

25. On 16 May 1986 Radio X and the taxpayer executed a deed which recited that Radio X, T Co and the taxpayer had agreed to terminate the August 1985 agreement as from 1 May 1986 upon terms and conditions set out in the deed. Those terms included an agreement by T Co to pay Radio X a sum of money and a covenant having the effect of ensuring that T Co would not until 30 June 1986 permit the taxpayer to appear or allow his voice to be used in certain capacities on any other radio station without the consent of Radio X.

26. On 28 June 1986 the directors of T Co resolved that any income of the trust deemed to be personal exertion income attributed to the taxpayer be distributed to him but otherwise distributed the accounting profit and net profit for the year ending 30 June 1986 to the taxpayer's wife and his daughter.

27. On 1 July 1986 the taxpayer commenced to broadcast for Radio Z.

28. On 5 March 1987 T Co and Radio Z executed a written agreement under which T Co agreed that the broadcasting services of the taxpayer would be provided to Radio Z. The taxpayer guaranteed the due performance by T Co of its obligations under the agreement.

THE 1986 TAX RETURNS

29. The statements of fact set out in the following paragraphs numbered 30 to 33 (inclusive) are based upon information contained in copies of the income tax returns for the year ending 30 June 1986 of the taxpayer, the trust, the taxpayer's wife and the taxpayer's daughter.

33. The return of the taxpayer's daughter discloses a distribution of $35,000 from the trust and is accompanied by a copy of the "note re capital receipt".

34. Reference has already been made to the adjustment sheet in respect of the taxpayer's return whereby the Deputy Commissioner treated the whole of the $125,000 referred to in the "note re capital receipt" as income earned by the taxpayer. It is common cause that the Deputy Commissioner assessed both the taxpayer's wife and his daughter as if they had no entitlement to any part of the $125,000.

THE TAXPAYER'S CASE

36. It is unnecessary to rehearse in any detail the case submitted on the taxpayer's behalf in opposition to the Deputy Commissioner's amended assessment and in support of the objection. The substance of the case was set out in the "note re capital receipt" which accompanied all four tax returns.

37. The taxpayer's notice of objection also sets out the specific grounds relied upon. A number of the grounds address the question of capital gains and Part IVA of the Tax Act. In view of the conclusions which I have reached it is unnecessary to canvass those grounds in these reasons. The primary issue raised was stated in paragraph 6 of the grounds of objection as:

THAT the sum of $125,000 derived by (T Co) was in the nature of

a capital sum received in consideration of the company entering

into a contract whereby it undertook not to contract with any

other entity the services of (the taxpayer),media personality,

without prior approval of the payer of the sum during the

period of the currency of the contract between (T Co) and

(Radio 2) to provide the services of (the taxpayer).

This ground raised the exact issue raised in the note which accompanied the tax returns.

THE DEPUTY COMMISSIONER'S CASE

38. In opening the case for the Deputy Commissioner, counsel identified five issues which the Tribunal should address, namely:

a) Whether the $125,000 should be characterised as income

rather than capital.

b) Was the payment derived by T Co in its own right or merely

as an agent for the taxpayer.

c) If the payment was derived by T Co in its own right, did the

resolution of 28 June 1986 have the effect of distributing the

amount paid by Radio Z to the taxpayer.

d) If the answer (c) is in the negative, should the payment be

treated as having been derived by the taxpayer and not the

trust by reason of the provisions of s 260 and/or Part IVA of

the Tax Act.

e) If the payment was a capital receipt, was it a capital gain

under Part IIIA of the Tax Act, and if so, was it an assessable

gain of the taxpayer.

TAXPAYER'S DEALINGS WITH RADIO X

39. In the following paragraphs numbered 40 to 52 (inclusive) detailed reference is made to the various contractual arrangements, about which there is evidence, relating to the provision of the taxpayer's services to Radio X. The statements contained in these paragraphs are my findings of fact based upon the transcript of the taxpayer's evidence and the documents tendered at the first and second tribunal hearings.

40. Prior to the formation of T Co in 1976 the taxpayer had contracted his services as a radio announcer in his own name. Subsequently, a number of contracts were entered into by T Co which contracted to provide his services both for radio and for television stations.

41. The first agreement between T Co and a radio station of which there is evidence was entered into on 2 February 1981 when Radio X agreed with T Co that until 31 December 1992 T Co would ensure that the taxpayer would perform certain duties including acting as a radio broadcaster on weekdays between 5am and 7am. The agreement recites that the taxpayer is subject to the management and control of T Co and contains an express representation and warranty on the part of T Co that it has entered into a valid and presently subsisting service agreement with the taxpayer under which it is able to contract for the provision of the taxpayer's services. The contract provides for the payment to T Co of a weekly sum which increased progressively over time and a further sum payable weekly upon the taxpayer's program obtaining No 1 rating. The agreement was executed under the common seal of T Co. Annexed to the agreement, and of even date therewith, is a deed of guarantee executed by the taxpayer whereby he guaranteed the performance of the obligations imposed on T Co under the contract.

42. On 23 March 1983, Radio X and T Co entered into a further agreement in substantially similar terms to the agreement of 2 February 1981 under which T Co agreed to provide the taxpayer's services for a three year period commencing on 1 January 1983. The agreement was signed on behalf of Radio X by one White (described as general manager) and on behalf of T Co by the taxpayer. There is no guarantee annexed to this agreement as was the case with the earlier agreement. By paragraph 7 of the agreement, Radio X agreed to indemnify T Co and the taxpayer against claims arising out of the performance of the taxpayer's functions and further, Radio X agreed that it would not seek any indemnity or contribution in respect of claims made against it. Radio X agreed to enter into a separate agreement with the taxpayer to the same effect.

43. Pursuant to its obligation under paragraph 7, on 23 March 1983 Radio X entered into an agreement with the taxpayer binding itself in accordance with that paragraph. This latter agreement was also executed by White on behalf of Radio X. On the same day T Co entered into an agreement with the taxpayer whereby it bound itself to him to enforce its rights against Radio X as provided for in paragraph 7.

44. In August 1985, in anticipation of the expiration of the agreement of 23 March 1983, Radio X entered into a fresh agreement for the provision of the taxpayer's services. The new agreement was for a term of 3 years commencing on 1 January 1986. It took the form of a letter dated 21 August 1985 on Radio X letterhead addressed to one Disney followed by written acceptance dated 22 August 1985. The letter set out the terms and conditions under which T Co was to be employed by Radio X, and contained, inter alia, an agreement that T Co would provide the taxpayer's services to conduct the breakfast program on Radio X on weekdays between 5.30am and 8.30am. The letter is signed on behalf of Radio X by White as general manager. The following memorandum is attached to the letter:

We have read the terms and conditions expressed in this

Agreement on the preceding pages and accept same.

SIGNED the 22nd day of August 1985

(Signed) (Taxpayer)

In the presence of:

(Signed) (Disney)

45. For most of the taxpayer's career in radio and television Disney has acted as his personal agent. Disney's role as agent for the taxpayer extended to acting as agent for T Co in relation to the provision of the taxpayer's services and was a function he performed in an individual capacity rather than on behalf of the company of which he was managing director.

46. The August 1985 agreement provided that in each of the three 12 monthly periods of the term of the agreement T Co would be paid a fixed sum which increased progressively each year. In the first year (1986) the agreed amount was to be paid by a first instalment of an amount equal to about 53% of the annual sum payable on 8 January 1986 followed by 51 equal weekly instalments of an amount equal to one fifty-first part of the balance of the annual sum.

47. The taxpayer commenced performing the services contracted for by T Co on 1 January 1986, and Radio X performed its part of the agreement by making the payments agreed to be made.

48. In about mid-March 1986 White, who the taxpayer described as his mentor, left Radio X to join Radio Z. At the time of White's departure the taxpayer told him that he would be happy to join Radio Z and White had said that he would be happy to have the taxpayer along and would contact him after he (White) had become entrenched at Radio Z.

49. On 14 April 1986, following a telephone call from White, the taxpayer met White at the Regent Hotel in Melbourne where White was staying. The exact details of what was said at this meeting have not been proved with any degree of certainty and the matter is dealt with below as a separate issue. However, it is sufficient to say at this stage that as a result of the meeting it was agreed that the taxpayer would, at a time indicated by White, cease to provide services for Radio X and would at some then unspecified time thereafter provide similar services for Radio Z.

50. Following a telephone call from Disney on about 13 April 1986, the taxpayer ceased to broadcast for Radio X. His last appearance was on 30 April 1986. He was well aware that some financial settlement would have to be reached with Radio X. The August 1985 agreement did not contain any provisions that would apply in the event of T Co terminating the agreement. On 1 May 1986 a meeting took place at the Regency Hotel in Melbourne between representatives of Radio X, their legal advisers, the taxpayer and his legal advisers. An agreement was reached and an informal deed of release was drawn up. This document was not put in evidence and only passing reference was made to it in the taxpayer's evidence. However, a formal agreement was later drawn up and executed by Radio X, T Co and the taxpayer. This document which is dated 16 May 1986 provides in general terms, as follows:

1. As from 1 May 1986 T Co and the taxpayer were released from

all commitments under the August 1985 agreement.

2. T Co would repay to Radio X on or before 10 May 1986 a

specified sum said to represent the amount received over and

above the pro-rata weekly entitlement to the annual sum

referred to in the agreement.

3. The clause in the August 1985 agreement which prevented the

taxpayer appearing on other radio stations without Radio X's

consent would continue to operate until 30 June 1986.

(It is a matter of mere arithmetic that the sum agreed to be paid under the deed of release did in fact represent the difference between the amount actually paid by Radio X after 1 January 1986 and the amount that would have been paid if the annual sum had been paid by equal weekly instalments for the period from 1 January 1986 to 30 April 1986).

51. On 9 May 1986 following negotiations involving Disney and White, Radio Z paid $125,000 to the credit of T Co's bank account. There is no evidence as to when the amount agreed to be paid to Radio X was paid, but I infer from the following facts, namely that an informal agreement was reached with Radio X on 1 May 1986, that the deed of release provided for payment on 10 May 1986 and that the deed was executed on 16 May 1986, that the payment to Radio X had in fact been made by the time the formal deed was executed.

52. I infer from the course of dealings between Radio X and T Co over a period of several years when White was general manager of Radio X, from the actual terms of the agreements between Radio X and T Co made in 1981, 1983 and 1985, and from the fact that Radio Z paid the $125,000 to T Co, that at all times White's dealings with the taxpayer, including his discussion with him on 14 April 1986, were conducted in the knowledge and on the understanding that the taxpayer was acting in his capacity as director and spokesman for T Co and that insofar as White had dealings with Disney in relation to the provision of the taxpayer's services by T Co, he understood that Disney was acting as agent for T Co.

THE AGREEMENT WITH RADIO Z

53. The taxpayer said he was not surprised to hear from White in April 1986 and he had a fair idea of what their meeting at the Regent Hotel was to be about. Unfortunately, White died before the matter first came before the first Tribunal which meant that the only evidence that could be given of what took place at the meeting on 14 April 1986, at which only he and the taxpayer were present, was that of the taxpayer. It might therefore be thought that the evidence of what was said and agreed would be concise and consistent but this was not the case. Obviously, the passage of time between April 1986 and December 1990 (when the taxpayer gave evidence) was considerable. It is understandable that the taxpayer's recollection would not be precise and no adverse inference can be drawn from his inability to repeat the exact conversation which took place. It is therefore necessary to examine in detail the various pieces of evidence relating to the meeting.

54. The following is an abbreviated statement of part of the evidence-in-chief of the taxpayer from pages 22 - 23 of the transcript of 17 December 1990 in which the taxpayer described the conversation he had with White on 14 April 1986:

White: We would like you to come over to the Radio Z.

Taxpayer: I would love to but you realise my company is under

contract.

White: We can handle all that; I have been instructed to get

the best in Australia and that is why you are here today.

Taxpayer: You realise this will cause an awful fracas; Radio X

are not going to be happy.

White: That is OK; we will look after all that.

Taxpayer: So long as you pay my company's legal expenses in

relation to this.

White: That is not a problem.

Taxpayer: Well fine; OK; when do you want me to jump.

White: At the moment can we just shake hands and call that our

agreement.

Taxpayer: Fine

White: Welcome aboard Radio Z. I will be in touch. I will let

you know when the time is right.

55. The taxpayer said that it was a short meeting. Nothing was said about money other than that it was tacitly accepted T Co would get the same fees as it was receiving from Radio X. The taxpayer said that he had no further conversation with White concerning the matter in the following weeks and that all negotiations were carried on between Disney and White. Indeed White's instruction to cease appearing on Radio X was conveyed to the taxpayer by Disney and not by White.

56. It was the taxpayer's evidence that it was around the time when he was instructed to terminate the Radio X contract that it was suggested that although he did not have a contract with Radio Z, Radio Z would be prepared to pay a capital sum to prevent T Co entering into negotiations with another radio station. Disney conducted the negotiations with Radio Z. The sum of $125,000 was mentioned but the taxpayer could not recall who his first discussions were with. He said that on 30 April 1986 he had a discussion with Disney and that Disney did mention $125,000, although it is not clear from the transcript if this was on 30 April 1986 or some other occasion. In any event, the taxpayer agreed that the amount should be $125,000 and that sum was paid by Radio Z on 9 May 1986. The taxpayer started appearing on Radio Z on 1 July 1986, although no written contract was entered into until March 1987.

57. When cross-examined, the taxpayer confirmed that all discussions concerning the $125,000 were between Disney and White in late April or early May and that he was not involved. There had been no discussion with White on 14 April 1986 concerning the $125,000. He said that Disney told him that Radio Z had agreed to pay the money to prevent him or T Co contracting elsewhere during the period he was without a contract.

58. Having given the foregoing responses to questions in cross-examination the taxpayer was questioned concerning certain further and better particulars which had been supplied to the Deputy Commissioner by the taxpayer's solicitors on 4 September 1990. In those particulars the taxpayer said of the agreement to transfer his services to Radio Z:

The contract was oral and was made on the 14th April 1986 at

the Regent Hotel, Melbourne. The conversation in the oral

contract took place between (the taxpayer) as director of (T

Co) and Brian White (now deceased) on behalf of (Radio Z). The

material substance of the conversation was that, from 30th June

1986 or otherwise agreed T Co would provide the services of

(the taxpayer) to (Radio Z) in return for the sum of $(X) per

week.

and concerning the agreement for Radio Z to pay $125,000:

The contract was oral and was made on the 14th April 1986 at

the Regent Hotel, Melbourne. The conversation in the oral

contract took place between (the taxpayer) as director of (T

Co) and Brian White (now deceased) on behalf of (Radio Z). The

material substance of the conversation was that, in

consideration of the sum of $125,000 T Co agreed not to

negotiate or to provide the services of (the taxpayer) to any

other radio station in Australia during the currency of the

contract.

59. When the foregoing particulars were put to the taxpayer he asserted that the account given in the particulars was inaccurate but he agreed that the figure of $125,000 may have been discussed on 14 April 1986. He said that he had had a discussion with Disney in late April 1986 but could not recall what was said although they did "kick around" some figures but he was not sure of what figures. He agreed that it was possible that the figure of $125,000 was settled with White on 14 April 1986.

60. The taxpayer explained the difference between the particulars given in September 1990 and the evidence he gave in-chief. He said that at the time of the event he had told his wife what had occurred and that subsequently she conveyed to him her recollection of what he had told her which the taxpayer accepted as an accurate statement of what had in fact occurred.

61. Neither Disney nor the taxpayer's wife was called and accordingly neither their memories nor their credibility could be tested. The state of the taxpayer's evidence on the question of what was agreed on 14 April 1986 and when and by whom the agreement to pay $125,000 was reached is of insufficient probative value to justify me relying upon it to make any positive finding of fact. Accordingly, I propose to look to the objective facts as disclosed by contemporaneous writings and the conduct of the persons involved to determine if any relevant inferences can be drawn.

62. An intriguing aspect of the evidence which was not made a feature of by either party has to do with the date from which the taxpayer was to commence appearing on Radio Z. In his evidence-in-chief the taxpayer said nothing as to a commencing date. On his evidence even the date of his termination with Radio X was left open until he received the message from Disney on 29 April 1986. In his particulars given in September 1990 he said that it was agreed on 14 April 1986 that his services would be provided to Radio Z from 30 June 1986 or otherwise as agreed. However, in cross-examination he said that the starting date with Radio Z was arranged at the meeting at the Regency. He emphasised that it was at the Regency not the Regent. The point being made was that he was saying that it was at the meeting he and his lawyer had with Radio X and their lawyer on 1 May 1986 that agreement as to this date was reached. It is difficult to understand how any agreement between the taxpayer or T Co on the one part and Radio X on the other could affect the date on which the new arrangement with Radio Z would commence.

63. However, it may well be that Radio X did have a say in that they were being asked to forego the benefit of the restriction against the taxpayer appearing on rival radio stations and possibly it was at their insistence that the date 30 June 1986 was arrived at. Be that as it may, it is clear that it was firmly established as of 1 May 1986 that Radio X would receive payment of an agreed sum from T Co and that the taxpayer would not be on air again until after 30 June 1986, a period of some eight weeks or so from 1 May 1986.

64. Without going into the exact details, it is common cause, and I find it to be fact, that the total of the amount T Co agreed to "refund" to Radio X and an amount equal to eight fifty-second parts of the annual fee payable for 1986 under the August 1985 agreement, amounts almost exactly to $125,000.

65. The evidence is clearly capable of supporting inferences that White agreed that Radio Z would compensate T Co for the cost to it of breaking its agreement with Radio X; that until it was known what terms could be negotiated with Radio X the amount of that compensation could not be ascertained; that upon an agreement being reached with Radio X on 1 May 1986 the extent of the compensation was capable of being calculated; and that on 9 May 1986 Radio Z paid T Co $125,000, as a sum which in round figures compensated T Co in accordance with the agreement between White and the taxpayer made on 14 April 1986. I am assisted in reaching these conclusions by the facts that the agreement with Radio X was to pay "the refund" on 10 May 1986 and that the payment by Radio Z was made on 9 May 1986. I infer that Radio Z put T Co in funds in time for it to meet its obligation to Radio X. Furthermore, there is evidence, which I accept, that Radio Z paid the legal costs incurred by T Co in relation to the settlement with Radio X, which is consistent with my finding concerning the agreement reached on 14 April 1986, and with the taxpayer's statement under cross-examination that it was implicit in the arrangement with White that he would not be worse off by moving over to Radio Z.

66. I reject the taxpayer's evidence that Radio Z paid the money in order to ensure that the taxpayer's services were not contracted elsewhere. It is fundamental to the taxpayer's case that on 14 April 1986 an agreement has been reached. He said that upon shaking hands with White on 14 April 1986 his word was his bond, as was White's. He never expressed any doubt as to that. If White had any doubts he did not convey them to the taxpayer. And if White expressed doubts to Disney, the only way to establish that fact (in view of White's death) was to call Disney to give evidence. No explanation was given for the failure to call Disney and I am, by that fact, more able to draw the inference that his evidence would not have supported the taxpayer's case.

67. From the totality of the material before me and the inferences that can be drawn, and in the absence of any objective proof that the parties contemplated that a payment would be made to prevent T Co from contracting to supply the taxpayer's services to another radio station, I conclude that that was not the purpose of the payment.

68. I find as fact that the payment of $125,000 was made by Radio Z to ensure that T Co would not be worse off by reason of the termination of its contract with Radio X and its agreement with Radio Z. There were two components in the figure finally arrived at: first, an amount to compensate T Co for the amount of the "refund" payable to Radio X; second, an amount to compensate T Co for the loss of fees for the period 1 May 1986 to 30 June 1986.

CHARACTERISATION OF THE PAYMENT

69. By reason of the findings of fact contained in paragraphs 64 and 68 (inclusive) I am of the opinion that the sum of $125,000 paid by Radio Z on 9 May 1986 represented compensation for loss of earnings and/or reimbursement of outgoings of a revenue nature. The amount paid is properly to be characterised as being in the nature of income (Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Smith [1981] HCA 10; (1981) 147 CLR 578; Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Inkster (1989) 89 ATR 5142).

THE CAPACITY IN WHICH THE PAYMENT WAS RECEIVED

70. The evidence suggests very strongly, and I so find, that T Co was not merely a "front" for the taxpayer. It was engaged in a range of business activities, some not associated with the personal activities of the taxpayer, from which it derived significant income. Further, there is evidence that it also provides the services of the taxpayer's wife to a television network.

71. T Co was carrying on a legitimate commercial operation which included the provision of the taxpayer's services to radio and television stations. The payment of $125,000 was made to compensate T Co for a loss of income it would otherwise have sustained by reason of the termination of the August 1985 agreement with Radio X.

72. The taxpayer was under no personal liability to Radio X by reason of the termination of the August 1985 agreement. There had been a long history of dealings between Radio X and T Co in which White had participated as general manager of Radio X. White would obviously have carried his knowledge of the relationships between Radio X, T Co and the taxpayer with him when he moved to Radio Z. He was the person who negotiated first with the taxpayer and later with Disney on behalf of Radio Z. For all of these reasons, and the reasons set out in paragraph 52, I find that when White dealt with the taxpayer he did so upon the understanding that the taxpayer was acting as agent for T Co, and that when he dealt with Disney, he did so on the basis that Disney was acting as an agent for T Co. I find that the payment of $125,000 was made to T Co in its own right and not as an agent for the taxpayer.

THE RESOLUTION OF 28 JUNE 1986

73. Counsel for the taxpayer argues that the resolution of the directors of T Co passed on 28 June 1986 had the effect of distributing the money paid by Radio Z on 9 May 1986 to the taxpayer's wife and child and that as a result the taxpayer at no stage became presently entitled to receive any part thereof.

74. The effect of the resolution will depend upon the meaning to be attributed to the proviso and in particular the words:

"any income deemed to be personal exertion income attributed to

(the taxpayer)".

75. The terms "income from personal exertion" and "income derived from personal exertion" are defined in s 6(1) of the Tax Act but they are so defined to include money derived from a great variety of sources including the proceeds of any business carried on by the taxpayer.

76. On the basis of my finding that the payment of $125,000 was made by Radio Z to compensate T Co for losses of a revenue nature, which losses were directly associated with the performance (or non-performance) by the taxpayer of his normal occupation as a radio broadcaster, it could fairly be said that the amount paid to T Co was part of the proceeds of the business carried on by T Co and those proceeds were attributable to the taxpayer.

77. If the general concept of personal exertion income as used in the resolution was intended to be directly related to the Tax Act definition of "income from personal exertion" I have no difficulty in construing the proviso of the resolution as having the effect of distributing to the taxpayer the amount of $125,000 paid by Radio Z.

78. Another approach is to consider the resolution in the context of the accounts of T Co for the year ending 30 June 1986 as they appear in the trust's income tax return for that year.

79. As indicated earlier in these reasons, the accounts disclose income from two main sources. The major item of income, "fees received", is shown under the heading of "personal exertion income of (the taxpayer)". The only other item under that heading is profit on the sale of a leased vehicle. The other source of income is described as "trust income" and under that heading is included the profits from the sale of tapes and t-shirts, interest and a small profit on the sale of shares. The "extraordinary item - capital receipt" of $125,000 is also shown as "trust income". The two major items of expense debited against the personal exertion income of the taxpayer are "Salaries" and "commission (Including contract negotiation)". The salary paid to the taxpayer's wife is debited as an expense against "trust income" and that is the only salary so debited.

80. There can be no doubt that the form of the resolution was drawn in language consistent with that used in the trust accounts and tax return and was in a form designed to avoid any difficulty, such as occurred in earlier years, that may have been occasioned by the diversion to the taxpayer's wife and child of income which was properly to be regarded as being derived by the taxpayer and taxed accordingly.

81. Once it is established, as it has been, that the payment from Radio Z was of a revenue nature and not a capital sum, and that its receipt was "attributed to" the taxpayer, then by operation of the resolution of 28 June 1986 the whole of that sum was distributed to the taxpayer. It follows that the statement of distribution of net income set out in the trust's income tax return did not properly reflect the true effect of the resolution.

THE ONUS OF PROOF

82. Section 190 of the Tax Act (which continues to apply to this proceeding despite its repeal) provided that on a review before the Tribunal the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive shall lie upon the taxpayer. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Elton (1990) 90 ACT 4078 (at 4081) Davies J approved the proposition that it is only when the Tribunal is not satisfied one way or the other that the statutory direction as to the burden of proof comes into play, with the result that, when neither party has persuaded the Tribunal that its contentions are more probably correct, then the applicant fails.

83. In this proceeding I am satisfied on the evidence (and so find) that the contention put on the applicant's behalf that the payment in question was a capital sum paid in consideration for the granting of a restrictive covenant is not correct, and that the contention put on the Deputy Commissioner's behalf that the amount paid represented compensation for loss of earning and reimbursement of an outgoing of a revenue nature is correct. Furthermore, I find that by reason of the resolution of 28 June 1986 the taxpayer became presently entitled to the whole of the amount in question. There is therefore no occasion for the provisions of s 190 to have any operation.

CONCLUSION

84. For the foregoing reasons, I find that the Deputy Commissioner properly assessed the payment of $125,000 as income taxable in the hands of the taxpayer. It is unnecessary to consider what effect, if any, the provisions of s 260 and/or Part IV A of the Tax Act would have in relation to the taxpayer's liability for tax in the 1986 year of income.

85. The objection decision will be affirmed.
