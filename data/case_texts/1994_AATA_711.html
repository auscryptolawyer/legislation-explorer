Alcorn and Commissioner for Superannuation [1994] AATA 711 (21 December 1994)

Last Updated: 23 September 2008



SUPERANNUATION - Preservation of superannuation benefits by a resigning eligible employee - election outside of prescribed period - discretion to recognise late election.



Superannuation Act 1976 Ss 137(1), Ss 157(1)



Re Chalk v Commissioner for Superannuation [1994] FCA 1063; 19 AAR 450

Re Chalk and Commissioner for Superannuation (Tribunal Decision No 8954)

Commissioner for Superannuation v Boardman [1994] FCA 1159; 123 ALR 239

Liddle v Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 191; 14 AAR 456



AAT Decision No 9921



Re: GARY ALLAN ALCORN



Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION



Respondent



Tribunal: Miss WJF Purcell (Senior Member)

Miss A Brennan (Member)

Professor EK Christie (Member)



Date: 21 December 1994



Place: Brisbane



No: Q93/198

DECISION



The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.



WJF Purcell

Senior Member



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. Q93/198

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )



Re: GARY ALLAN ALCORN



Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION



Respondent



REASONS FOR DECISION



1. This is an application for review of decision of a delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation (the Commissioner) of 23 January 1993 affirming the decision of a delegate of 12 December 1991, not to direct that the election made by the applicant on 7 January 1991, be treated as if it had been made within the period allowed by subsection 137(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 (the Act).



2. The evidence before the Tribunal comprised the documents filed pursuant to s 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, (the "documents") together with exhibits tendered by the parties. The applicant gave oral evidence, and was represented by Mr Pavusa who called Mr Logan, a former colleague of the applicant. Mr Adams appeared on behalf of the respondent ("the Commissioner").



3. The applicant who is now 53 years of age, joined the then Postmaster-General's Department (now Telecom) on 13 January 1958 when he was 16. On 20 August 1958 he became a contributor to the Superannuation Fund established by the Superannuation Act 1922. He was still a contributor at the commencement of the Act (on 1 July 1976), and hence became an "eligible employee" for the purposes of the Act.



4. The applicant was a "technician in training" during the first year of his employment; and joined the radio section, where he completed a five year traineeship. He rose to the level of Senior Technical Officer, Grade 1, remaining in the radio section in the Divisional Office until his resignation from the public service on 13 September 1978. He had more than 20 years service and has not returned to public sector employment. The applicant received a refund of accumulated contributions and would have completed a Form 2A. In accordance with Telecom's usual archival practice the applicant's personal file has now been destroyed. The sample Form 2A is headed :



"APPLICATION FOR REFUND OF ACCUMULATED CONTRIBUTIONS/

LUMP SUM BENEFIT OR

ELECTION FOR PRESERVATION OF SUPERANNUATION RIGHTS.



Note: This application form should be completed by a contributor who resigns or a contributor who retires and who elects to exercise an option to take a Lump Sum Benefit in lieu of a pension entitlement.



This form should not be completed by a contributor who is applying for a pension and a refund of accumulated contributions or supplementary contributions."



5. The eligible employee is required to apply for one of these benefits by deleting the other two inapplicable benefits. The applicant does not recall reading or completing the form, but assumes that it was witnessed by the Radio Section Acting Staff Clerk, as the permanent occupant of the position was on leave at the

time. Subsequently, the applicant's accumulated contributions in the sum of $5777.40 were refunded. Some 12 years later, in late 1990, the applicant was advised by a former Telecom colleague, that preservation of benefits was an alternative to a refund of contributions. On 7 January 1991 the applicant wrote to the Retirement Benefits Office (the "RBO") in the following terms:

"I have recently become aware that at the time of my resignation from TELECOM AUSTRALIA on 13th September, 1978 that it was possible to preserve my CSS benefits instead of resigning from the scheme with a lump sum payout. Also, that it is possible for me to apply for readmission to the scheme and a late election to defer benefits.



I believe that I meet the requirements necessary to make me eligible for re-admission and hereby seek direction to achieve my previous status of CSS membership.



Employment History:

...



At the time of my resignation, even though I was located in the Divisional Office, there was no suggestion or advice from any Clerical Officer regarding preservation of CSS benefits as an alternative to taking a lump sum refund.



My records show that an initial refund of $4418.64 was made in October 1978. I subsequently received a second "surplus" payment of $1358.76 in March 1979. I believe that it will be necessary for me to return the amounts received to enable my full reinstatement.



To this end, could you please confirm that re-admission to the fund is possible, and forward any forms necessary for completion to the above address.”



6. The applicant's letter was treated by the RBO as an election under subsection 137(1) of the Act, which provides:



(1) A person who



(a) ceases to be an eligible employee in circumstances to which paragraph (b) does not apply and, upon so ceasing, is not entitled to pension under this Act or invalidity benefit in accordance with section 69, 72 or 73; or



(b) ceases to be an eligible employee in circumstances by virtue of which the person is to be deemed, under sub-section 58(3), to have retired involuntarily,



may, not later than 21 days after the person so ceases to be an eligible employee, elect, by notice in writing to the Commissioner, that this division apply in relation to the person.



(2) If a person makes an election for the purposes of this section within 1 month before he ceases to be an eligible employee, the election has effect as if it had been made on the day after he ceased to be an eligible employee.



(3) Subject to sub-section (4) an election under this section is of no effect unless the person who made the election gives notice in writing to the Commissioner within 21 days, or within such further period as the Commissioner allows, after the expiration of the period that is the prescribed period in relation to him -



(a) stating whether he was employed at the expiration of the prescribed period and, if so, the name and address of his employer; and



(b) stating whether he was at the expiration of the prescribed period a member of the superannuation scheme applicable in relation to that employment and, if so, specifying the scheme concerned.



7. Subsection 157(1) of the Act confers upon the Commissioner, a discretion to treat a person's late election as if it had been made within the period allowed. The subsection provides:



157. (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, where an election under this Act is made by a person after the expiration of the period allowed by or under this Act for the making of the election, and the Commissioner is satisfied that in all the circumstances of the case it is desirable that the election should be recognised, the Commissioner may direct that the election be treated as if it had been made within the period allowed and the election shall have effect accordingly.



8. On 12 December 1991 a delegate of the Commissioner decided that in all the circumstances it was not desirable that the applicant's election under subsection 137(1) of the Act should be recognised. On 23 January 1993 this decision was confirmed by the decision under review.



9. The applicant submits that as a meticulous, dedicated "workaholic" in a specialist area he and his colleagues cared little for unions, working conditions or the details of superannuation. The general "folklore" of the time was that the only benefit available was a refund of contributions. The Form 2A is ambiguous and the eligibility for deferral of benefit can be interpreted as applying only to an officer who retires. The Commissioner had an obligation to inform and to counsel the applicant about the options available to him and had he known of the options he would have considered them carefully and he believes, now, that he would have preserved his benefits. He had no need of the money at the time, he has suffered prejudice; and in all the circumstances it is "desirable" that the discretion provided in s 157(1) of the Act be exercised in his favour.



10. The Commissioner argues that the applicant was sufficiently put on notice by the contents of the Form 2A which he completed. He had every opportunity to make enquiries about his entitlements had he not understood the implications of the Form 2A; and in signing the Form 2A without making such enquiries, he did so "careless of his entitlements". In the Commissioner's submission it is only with the benefit of hindsight that the applicant can maintain that he did not need the money at the time and that he would probably have preserved his benefits. The applicant, on the basis of objective criteria, has not satisfied the practical onus or evidentiary burden to satisfy the Commissioner (and hence the Tribunal) that in all the circumstances it is desirable that his late election to preserve superannuation rights be recognised.



11. We have summarised the parties' submission in a brief and general way. What we have said is by no means a complete account of the submission, all of which we have taken into consideration.



12. The applicant gave oral evidence of the general lack of interest exhibited by the officers of Telecom in any aspects of superannuation benefits. We accept his evidence of the prevailing folklore that the only benefit available on resignation was a refund of contributions. The "Conditions of Employment" booklet, published in June 1977, under the heading "Superannuation" reads at page 27 under the sub-heading "Benefits":



"6. Resignation

On resignation a contributor is refunded all his or her contributions, plus interest."



The applicant said that he located the booklet among papers he took with him when he resigned, but does not assert that he had read the booklet prior to resignation nor that he relied upon the information provided in the booklet. He proffers it as the only document available to him as a concrete example of his and his colleagues' understanding of the situation. At the commencement of the section on Superannuation the booklet reads, at page 23:



Superannuation Act 1976



Contributors to the superannuation scheme receive certain benefits, by way of a pension or, in certain circumstances, a lump sum payment or both, at the end of their service with the Commission.



The simple description given here is intended mainly for new contributors to the new scheme introduced on 1 July 1976. If you were a contributor to the superseded scheme you should refer to more detailed documents for carry-over conditions.



The applicant drew our attention to the contrast between this inaccurate information, and the detailed "Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme" booklet of some 83 pages published by the RBO in July 1992.



13. Although the "Conditions of Employment" booklet may be the only written information the applicant has retained, it is clear on the evidence, that the introduction of the Act was accompanied by a number of information circulars published by the RBO. Superannuation Board Circular 1976/8 of March 1976 "New Superannuation Scheme for Commonwealth Employees Effective from 1 July 1976", attached paper one of a series of seven. Entitled "General scope of the scheme for contributors to the superseded scheme", it reads at paragraph 15:



15. A resignation benefit, available upon resignation prior to attaining a retiring age under the relevant conditions of employment, consists of:



(a) a lump sum benefit equal to accumulated contributions; or



(b) if the contributor is eligible and so elects;



(i) a preservation pension falling due on death or invalidity or at retiring age, or



(ii) the transfer of a Transfer Value to a scheme approved under the preservation provisions of the new scheme.



Paragraph 25 reads:



INFORMATION PAPERS

25. A series of papers on the new scheme is being prepared for the information of contributors who transfer from the superseded scheme to the new scheme on 1 July 1976, as well as contributors commencing to contribute to the new scheme on or after 1 July 1976. The following papers are being prepared in the order listed:



Part 2 Age Retirement and Early Retirement Benefits

Part 3 Invalidity Retirement Benefits

Part 4 Former Provident Account Contributors Benefits

Part 5 Spouses and Eligible Children's Benefits

Part 6 Contributions and Benefits for New Contributors Under

New Superannuation Scheme

Part 7 Preservation Benefits



The paper (Part 7 of the series) "Preservation of Superannuation Rights" was distributed by the RBO on 2 August 1978.



14. The applicant said in evidence that he did not recall reading Part 7, which he considered had probably not reached his Branch by the time of his resignation. In relation to the other RBO circulars he does not recall receiving any written information regarding the Act, nor did he attend any information sessions or seminars on the topic.



15. On the applicant's evidence he regarded the Form 2A as a routine form to be completed to recover his benefits. He does not recall reading the form, nor did he make any enquiries of Telecom's personnel and staff clerks. These officers' desks adjoined the applicant's work station, but he says he had no reason to make enquiries; there was nothing to suggest to him that he should have made enquiries. It was surely their responsibility to inform him, but none of these staff members drew any literature or information to his attention.



16. The applicant said in evidence that in the prevailing culture in 1978 there was little importance attached to superannuation. In today's climate, however, he would have taken a different course, if information such as that contained in the 1992 booklet had been available. The applicant said in evidence: "Well, had I known of the options I certainly would have considered it and I believe I would have preserved." (transcript p.28). He would have undertaken the mathematical calculations as to the possible value of the preserved benefit, and "had I made the calculation, realised that amount of money might have made me a pension equal to what I was earning at the time, probably would have persuaded me to preserve". (transcript p.29)



17. The applicant gave evidence that he had no immediate need for the lump sum of $5777.40, the equivalent of approximately 40% of his gross annual salary of $14,450. When he received the first payment of $4418.64 in October 1978 he deposited it in his ANZ bank account, and it, along with other moneys, was used to make a $10,000 deposit in an interest bearing deposit in his wife's name. The second payment of $1358.76, in March 1979, was deposited for a short period in the ANZ account and, with other moneys, a total of $2000 was placed with ESANDA in his wife's name. The moneys were expended in 1982 in the purchase of a block of flats. The applicant, however, has no recollection of the lump sum of more than 50% of his annual income, in lieu of long service leave. He has quite understandably, with the benefit of hindsight, focussed solely upon the superannuation payment.



18. The Federal Court has examined recently the exercise of the discretion provided by s 157 of the Act. In Chalk v Commissioner for Superannuation [1994] FCA 1063; 19 AAR 450 Davies J, with whom Black CJ and Cooper J agreed, said at p.455:



"If a relevant provision confers a discretion, as does s 157 of the Act, the discretion should be exercised by reference to the words of the statute and the context within which the discretion is conferred. Section 157 uses simple words and the issues which arise under it are invariably issues of fact, the ultimate issue being whether it is desirable that a late election should be recognised."



And at p. 456:



"Many such provisions, including s 157, are not concerned with rights. If an Act is not performed within the prescribed time, there is no right to have it performed. But there is a discretion to permit to be extended time should it be appropriate in the circumstances to do so. In the exercise of this discretion, it may be more important to consider the consequences of extending or refusing to extend time than to debate the reasons why the act was not done in time. Section 157 merely requires that the Commissioner 'be satisfied that in all the circumstances of the case it is desirable that the election should be recognised'. 'All the circumstances' include all relevant circumstances existing at the time the discretion is to be exercised."



See also Cooper J at pp. 458/9



"However, s 157 does not require that the Commissioner determine whether there exists attributable fault on the part of the appellant or any other person giving rise to the need to extend time; nor that the discretion be exercised on the basis of the existence or absence of attributable fault. Rather, as explained by Davies J in his reasons, the section merely requires that the Commissioner 'be satisfied that in all the circumstances it is desirable that the election should be recognised'. Those circumstances are all the relevant circumstances existing at the time the exercise of the discretion falls for consideration and will include circumstances of the type identified by Davies J as relevant to the statutory purpose for which the discretionary power in s 157 to treat an election as within time was enacted."



In Commissioner for Superannuation v Boardman [1994] FCA 1159; 123 ALR 239 at p.247 Von Doussa J said:



"In s157(1) 'desirable' is not referable to a state of mind - whether of the elector or of the Commissioner. Whether a late election is 'desirable' is to be judged objectively having regard to all the circumstances of case. The suggested meaning 'something worthy of achievement' does not imply other than an objective evaluation of the circumstances of the case; but in the end, whether the recognition of a late election is 'something worthy of achievement' must depend on what is fair in order to do justice between the person who seeks to make the late election and the requirements of the administration of the funds."



19. The applicant at the time of his resignation was a 37 year old married man with two children preparing for High School. He was entering a new field of endeavour, and it is difficult to accept that he would have been prepared to leave his money in the Commonwealth scheme for another 25 or so years. In any event this is but one of the factors to be taken into account. He made no inquiries as to his rights. He was not an officer stationed in a remote area, nor with any education or intellectual disabilities. On his own evidence, he was third in command of the Section, with a substantial number of subordinates. His desk adjoined the personnel section; these officers were well known to him, he chose not to seek their advice but to rely on the folklore of the time.



20. When the applicant completed the Form 2A he deleted the two inappropriate benefits. Had he read the form carefully he would have been alerted to the availability of preservation; and we adopt, with respect, Deputy President Forgie's reasons in re Liddle v Commissioner for Superannuation [1991] AATA 191; 14 AAR 456 at p.473:



"The form (Form 2A) is not as comprehensive in its explanations as the form now used (Ex A). So, for example, it does not set out what is meant by 'preserving a benefit' or when a person should make an election. However, what the form does do is to alert the reader to the fact that he or she must choose one of three things: a refund of accumulated contributions, a lump sum benefit or preservation of superannuation rights. Certainly, no information can be gleaned from the form itself about these options but the form does, in my view, put the reader on notice that he or she should make further enquiries as to whether these options apply to his or her circumstances and generally find out more information about them."



21. Mr Logan gave evidence, which we accept, that when he resigned in September 1979, (one year later than the applicant), he noted that Form 2A provided for an alternative described as "preservation of benefits". He questioned the Section administrative officer, who had made enquiries of Branch Office, and advised on the following day, that the provision applied to special cases only. Mr Logan and the administrative officer thought that such persons could be Vietnam War conscripts who returned to the Public Service. Mr Logan concluded that a refund of accumulated benefits was the only choice available to him, and elected accordingly.



22. The applicant, unlike his colleague Mr Logan, made no further enquiries nor on his own evidence did he rely upon incorrect information in the "Conditions of Employment" booklet. He simply took no real interest in the matter. As the Tribunal said in the matter of re Chalk and Commissioner for Superannuation in Decision No 8954 at paragraph 47:



"There is nothing in this case that attracts us to the view that it is desirable to grant the late election. You must give weight to the time limit in the Act, that there is no acceptable explanation for the delay - we do not regard ignorance of the law per se to be an acceptable explanation and we are satisfied that the applicant had no real interest in the matter until later in his life when he realised he had made a mistake (whether wittingly or unwittingly) with the benefit of hindsight."



24. We have taken into account the whole of the evidence and Counsels' submissions, and we are satisfied that in all the circumstances of this matter it is not appropriate that the discretion provided in s 157 of the Act should be exercised in the applicant's favour.



25. For these reasons the Tribunal affirms the decision under review.



I certify that this and the eleven (11) preceding pages are a true copy of the decision and reasons for decision herein of Miss WJF Purcell (Senior Member).
