Sibrava and Commissioner for Superannuation [1978] AATA 33 (19 April 1978)

Last Updated: 14 March 2007

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No.117/77

Re: SHIRLEY EDITH SIBRAVA

And: ACTING COMMISSIONER FOR

SUPERANNUATION



AAT Decision No 73

DECISION

The Tribunal sets aside the decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation and remits the matter for reconsideration by the Commissioner with a recommendation that he consider the application of Section 66 of the Superannuation Act 1976 to the facts of this matter.

DATED this nineteenth day of April 1978

K.S. Edmunds

Senior Member

W.J. Smith

Member

Dr M Glick

Member

REASONS FOR DECISION

The Applicant, Shirley Edith Sibrava, is the widow of Jerome Charles Sibrava, who was retired from his employment with the National Capital Development Commission on the grounds of invalidity on 14 January, 1977. Mr Sibrava died in August 1977. Mrs Sibrava has requested the Tribunal to review a decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation made following a request by Mr Sibrava under section 154 of the Superannuation Act for a pension under the Act following his retirement on medical grounds.

The Acting Commissioner's decision was notified to Mr Sibrava by letter dated 5 August, 1977, and the reasons for the decision were stated in that letter to be as follows:-

"I refer to your letter which was received on 18 April 1977, in which you requested reconsideration pursuant to section 154 of the Superannuation Act 1976, of the decision conveyed to you by this Office's letter of 10 March 1977 that a Benefit Classification Certificate applied in respect of the benefit payable to you on retirement.

Your case was reconsidered by the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation on 28 July 1977. After examining the circumstances of your case against the terms of the superannuation legislation, the Acting Commissioner has confirmed the previous decision."

In reaching his decision, the Acting Commissioner had regard to the medical standards for acceptance to the Superannuation Fund and Provident Account in 1974, and the application of those standards to your case following the medical reports of 5 January 1973 and 17 January 1974. Regard was also had to the medical report of 8 December 1976. The Acting Commissioner has stated that:

'In my opinion the incapacity which was the ground for Mr JC Sibrava's retirement was caused or was substantially contributed to by the condition "history of nervous depression" as specified in the Benefit Classification Certificate deemed to be in force on 1 July 1976 and still in force on 14 January 1977 in respect of Mr Sibrava."'

The grounds upon which the Applicant relied in her request to the Tribunal were given by her Counsel on the hearing of her request by the Tribunal to be that the decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation was firstly wrong in law and secondly cannot be supported on the facts.

Mr Sibrava had commenced employment with the National Capital Development Commission on 31 October, 1972, as an architect. On 29 November, 1972, he completed a form SB6 which was required for the purposes of the then Superannuation Act ("the superseded Act") and on 5 January, 1973, a Commonwealth Medical Officer who examined Mr Sibrava stated that in his opinion Mr Sibrava was unfit to contribute to the Superannuation fund and recommended deferment for eleven months and gave as his reasons for that recommendation the following:

"Because of the depression in June 1972 deferment is recommended. This nervous depression was a single episode caused by marital discord. If the sick leave record is satisfactory regarding this, and provided being fit at the recheck medical examination,

it is anticipated the Provident Account acceptance will be recommended because of the weakness in the left chest wall caused by the rib resection. It is a potential weak spot because of the unprotected lung and heart underneath".

The reference in the reasons referred to of a "depression in June 1972" was a notation in the medical examination report in the Details of Yes Answers and Medical Officers Comments when Mr Sibrava had stated:

"Relatively mild case of depression as a side effect of family crisis and of resulting divorce proceedings. Physician prescribed 5 mg of Triptinol a day for a period of approx. 6-7 weeks",

and the medical officer's comments which were:

"self explanatory history - Finds he is improving now that all is settled - taking a Mandrax for sleep and slowly cutting it down".

There was a further reference in the form to Mr Sibrava's insomnia and his statement that it was rapidly improving and that he was taking 1/2 Mandrax. In the form also in a section for completion by the examining medical officer appeared the question: "Is there any history suggestive of mental or nervous troubles including anxiety state?" and against this question the medical officer stated: "Yes, he is still taking sleeping tablets after the depression of June 1972".

The medical officer made a further report on 17 January, 1974, which read as follows:

"The abovenamed person had been deferred after the examination of 29.11.72 because of the mild depression he had suffered from in June 1972.

Since then he has apparently got over the depression as evidenced by his sick leave record and his general health.

His sick leave record shows he had an attack of bronchitis in November 1973 and this necessitated taking a week off work.
On re-examination his lungs are quite clear now.
Recommendation: Because of the deformity in the left chest wall (caused by the rib resection), the heart and left lung are unprotected, and as this is a potential weak spot liable to injury and herniation, acceptance for the Provident Account only is recommended. This recommendation is made subject to his job not being of a strenuous nature".

The form SB6 which we have referred to had a section headed:

"This section for official use only." then followed "Superannuation Board Decision" and words indicating acceptance for Provident Account, and under the words "approved delegate of the Board" was the signature "G.D. Thompson" and the date 11 March 1974.

On 1 July, 1976, the Superannuation Act 1976, the new Act, came into force. Mr Sibrava, having been an employee for the purposes of the superseded Act became for the purposes of the new Act an "existing contributor". The old distinction which had existed under the superseded Act between Superannuation Fund and Provident Fund Contributions no longer applied under the new Act.

In the case of a person becoming an eligible employee after the commencement of the new Act and who under the superseded Act because of a physical or mental condition would have become a contributor to the Provident Account the new Act provided in Section 16 for the issue of a benefit classification certificate by the Commissioner appointed under that Act. This certificate set out any physical or mental condition the Commissioner considered likely would prevent the eligible employee continuing in employment until he reached maximum retiring age. To meet the case of persons such as Mr Sibrava who had been contributing to the Provident Account the Act provided in section 184 that a benefit classification certificate in respect of them was deemed:

(a) to have been in force on the day the Act came into operation;

(b) to be issued by the Commissioner under sub-section (4) of section 16; and

(c) to have specified the physical or mental conditions by reason of which the Superannuation Board did not accept the person as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund established under the superseded Act.

Mr Sibrava was medically examined on 4 November, 1976, by a Commonwealth Medical Officer. The reason for the examination was stated to be "retirement on invalidity grounds". The Medical Officer's report was that Mr Sibrava was suffering from anxiety state and depression and estimated the percentage of each incapacity at 50%. He reported the brief history of the condition as:

"pressures of work and unsatisfactory relationships at work have exacerbated the conditions. His marital relationship has improved. His psychiatrist reports the condition will deteriorate if he his not retired".

The Medical Officer considered Mr Sibrava unfit for continued employment and he should be retired on the ground of invalidity.

Dr DEH Lawrence, a psychiatrist, issued a report dated 8 December, 1976, and addressed to "To whom it may concern". He referred to a previous letter he had written stating Mr Sibrava had been through a large number of emotional crises in his life and that he had reached a point where he was unable to ride through these storms as easily as he did before. Dr Lawrence described the emotional crises and that the superannuation of Mr Sibrava was most appropriate and gave his reasons for that conclusion.

On 18 February, 1977, the Australian Government Retirement Benefits Office was advised by the National Capital Development Commission that Mr Sibrava was retired on invalidity grounds on 14 January, 1977.

Evidence was given on the hearing before the Tribunal by Mr Gary Douglas Thompson, the officer who, under delegation, determined in 1974 that Mr Sibrava should be a contributor to the Provident Account and not the Superannuation Fund. Mr Thompson said that from a consideration of the medical reports and extracts from a publication "Handbook for the Guidance of Medical Officers" issued for medical practitioners examining persons for employment with the Commonwealth, he would have considered Mr Sibrava could not be accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation pension scheme because of:

(a)History of nervous depression;
(b)Weakness in left chest wall caused by rib resection; and
(c)Mild varicose veins (R) left-calf and anterior leg.

Mr Thompson in evidence said he did not formally record his reasons when he decided Mr Sibrava should be a contributor to the Provident Account. He did not have any independent recollection of making a determination in this case as he exercised the same power of delegation 20,000 times a year. He did prepare a note on 13 December, 1977, setting out the material which he would have considered when reaching his decision in Mr Sibrava's case.

Mr Thompson's delegation was under the superseded Act. There is no evidence that he held a delegation from the Commissioner in relation to the Commissioner's functions under section 184. Another matter for comment is that Mr Thompson placed reliance on the Handbook for the Guidance of Medical Officer. Mr Thompson was not acting as a medical officer and was not medically qualified. His function under section 5(1)(c) of the superseded Act was to consider the report of the medical practitioner and then make his decision. There was adequate justification for his decision to accept Mr Sibrava for the Provident Account because of his chest condition alone but it is not clear if he was empowered under section 5 to override a medical practitioner's findings that Mr Sibrava had apparently got over the depression from which he had been suffering.

Although section 184 deems the benefit classification certificate to have been in force on the day the Act came into force, no formal recording of physical or mental conditions was apparently made by a delegate of the Commissioner until 17 March, 1977, and it is of note that he did not fully agree with Mr Thompson's reconstituted conclusions as on his consideration of the medical reports he decided that Mr Sibrava's physical and mental conditions were:

(1) History of Nervous depression; and
(2)Weakness on left chest wall caused by rib resection; but he made no finding in relation to varicose veins. The formal recording was entered on a form s14 provided under the new Act and was not appropriate for use in the case of a person who had already retired.

Another difficulty arises in connexion with the application of section 184 in that it applies the tests to be applied by the Commissioner under section.16(4), namely physical or mental condition or conditions. In fact, the Superannuation Board was required under section 5(1)(c) (referred to above) to consider the "health and physical fitness" of a person. Counsel for the applicant submitted the 'test of "health and physical fitness" was different from the test of "physical or mental condition or conditions".

The Tribunal has considered Counsel's submission and if the tests are, as he contended, based on different standards, the Act in its present form in so far as it relates to persons transferring from the Provident Account under the superseded Act would be unworkable. The expression "physical fitness" would not, we think, include conditions pertaining to the mind but the word "health" when employed with the words physical fitness would include a mental condition. Also, one should bear in mind the purpose of the test which was to enable a decision to be made whether a person would be able to be employed until he reached his maximum retiring age. This expectation of length of service is also the purpose of the test of physical or mental condition or conditions in the new Act. There is, therefore, really no effective distinction between the purpose of the tests in the new Act and the superseded Act and a test based on "physical or mental condition or conditions" includes a test of "health and physical fitness".

As Mr Sibrava retired on the grounds of invalidity before attaining his maximum retiring age, and as a classification certificate was deemed to have been in existence in relation to him, the provisions of section 66 of the Act applied. Sub-section (2) of that section reads:

"(2) Subject to sub-sections (3) and (4) of this section and section 79, where-

(a) a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity before attaining his maximum retiring age;

(b) there was in force in respect of the person, immediately before the person's retirement, a benefit classification certificate; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the certificate or by a physical or mental condition connected with such a condition or such conditions,

the person is entitled-

(d) where the period of contributory service of the

person is not less than 8 years-

(i) if the person does not make an election under section 71 or 72 - to invalidity pension in accordance with section 70 and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, a lump sum benefit in accordance with that section; (ii) if the person makes an election under section 71-to invalidity pension, and a lump sum benefit, in accordance with that section; or (iii) if the person is entitled to make an election under section 72 and makes such an election -to a lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 72(2) and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, an additional lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 72(3); or (e) where the period of contributory service of the person is less than 8 years-to a lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 73(2) and, where the person has paid supplementary contributions, an additional lump sum benefit in accordance with sub-section 73(3)."

The consequences of a finding by the Commissioner under that sub-section would have the result that as Mr Sibrava had not been a contributor for a period of 8 years, he would become entitled to a lump sum payment and not a pension.

On 21 December, 1976, a minute paper was prepared recommending the application of section 66(2). This paper read as follows:

"21 Dec 1976 PA590044

RETIREMENT - INVALIDITY

JEROME CHARLES SIBRAVA - ARCHITECT - N.C.D.C. CANBERRA

D.O.B. - 8 July 1920

Admitted PA - 11 March 1974 (Following Deferment)

Retired - 6 January 1977

B.C.C. Conditions: History of nervous depression

Weakness in left chest wall,

caused by rib resection.

RETIREMENT DISABILITY: Anxiety State 50%

Depression 50%

COMMENT

In view of Mr Sibrava's history of nervous depression and his original deferment it is considered that s66(2) should be applied.

RECOMMENDATION: s66(2) applies.

(signed) F.H. Marryat 23/12/76

DECISION Section 66(2) applies

(signed)

J.I. Downes

Delegate of the Commissioner

10.1.77"

The decision of the delegate of the Commissioner was taken, as will be seen, on 10 January, 1977, that is, four days before Mr Sibrava's retirement. It is not apparent how the delegate knew what was specified in the deemed benefit classification certificate as it was not formally recorded until 3 months later. A letter dated 10 March, 1977, from the Authority to Mr Sibrava also referred to a benefit classification which had issued. That matter is not of importance as a decision applying section 66(2) cannot be taken prospectively. The sub-section makes it quite clear that it is after the retirement of an employee that the decision must be taken. It was possible, for example, in the instant case for Mr Sibrava to have died from some cause quite unconnected with the specified conditions, for example, in a road accident between the 10th and 14th January.

It was from the decision of the delegate taken on 10 January, 1977, that Mr Sibrava appealed to the Commissioner and the Commissioner's decision denying the request for reconsideration was based on the validity of the application of the benefit classification certificate. As we have found, the benefit classification certificate was never lawfully

applied under sub-section (2) of section 66 by the delegate of the Commissioner, and it follows the Commissioner's decision supporting the delegate's decision is of no effect as it cannot give support to what is a nullity.

The Tribunal therefore sets aside the decision under review. The Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act provides that if the Tribunal sets aside a decision under review it may either make a decision in substitution or remit the matter for reconsideration in accordance with any directions or recommendations of the Tribunal. In the circumstances of this case the Tribunal remits the matter for reconsideration by the Commissioner with a recommendation that he consider the matter under section 66 of the Act.

Although it is not necessary for the Tribunal to decide the question, we are of the opinion that there is sufficient evidence to support a decision that a deemed benefit classification certificate would contain the physical and mental condition specified in the certificate on 17 March, 1977. We express this opinion in the hope that it may be of some assistance to the Applicant in considering any action she may take if the Commissioner does apply sub-section (2) of section 66 in the manner his delegate attempted to apply it. The views we have expressed in this regard we must point out have no binding effect on any other Tribunal constituted for the purpose of considering any later request.
