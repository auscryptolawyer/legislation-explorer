Re Independent Order of Odd Fellows of Victoria v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1990] FCA 467; 91 Atc 4001/21 Atr 1083 27 FCR 132 (26 November 1990)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: INDEPENDENT ORDER OF ODD FELLOWS OF VICTORIA

And: FEDERAL COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. V G106 OF 1990

FED No. 677

Income Tax

91 ATC 4001/21 ATR 1083

27 FCR 132

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Sweeney A.C.J.(1), Northrop(2) and Wilcox(1) JJ.

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Friendly society carrying on "eligible insurance business" - Taxability of income derived from the investment of moneys in a Fund separate from the Funds through which the society carried on its eligible insurance businesses.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss.23(g), 116E, 116F, 116G.

Independent Order of Odd Fellows of Victoria v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation

HEARING

MELBOURNE

26:11:1990

Counsel for the Applicant: F.G.A. Beaumont, QC and J.G. Judd

Solicitors for the Applicant: Higgins Teale

Counsel for the Respondent: J.I. Fajgenbaum, QC and Dr S.C. Kenny

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The appeal be dismissed.

The applicant pay to the respondent his costs of the appeal.

NOTE: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

We have had the advantage of reading in draft form the reasons for judgment of Northrop J. We need not repeat the facts and relevant provisions set out by his Honour.

2. Regrettably, we are unable to agree with the conclusion expressed by Northrop J. It seems to us that the relevant income was derived from "eligible insurance business" of the applicant and that it is, therefore, taxable.

3. As Northrop J points out, the applicant is a friendly society and accordingly has the benefit of the underlying principle enshrined in s.23(g)(i) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936; namely, that its income is exempt from income tax. However, since 1984, that principle has been subject to the exception embodied in s.116G of the Act whereby a friendly society is obliged to pay income tax on so much of its total income (other than premiums) "as is derived from eligible insurance business of the society". The only question which arises in this appeal is whether the sum of $954,439, received by the society by way of return on the money held in a Fund known as "Life Insurance Management Fund No.2" ("the No.2 Fund"), fell within this description.

4. It is common ground that the money paid into the No.2 Fund, whose investment generated the subject income, was money which had been earned by the society in the conduct of various eligible insurance businesses. A total of $7,051,724 was transferred during the course of the 1984-1985 financial year from eight other Funds; which were used by the society to carry on an activity on its behalf which fell within the s.116E definition of "eligible insurance business". The greater part of this money ($6,600,000) was transferred on 1 July 1984.

5. According to the unchallenged evidence of Dr Allen Truslove, who was then an officer of the Government Statist and Actuary and who was responsible for setting up the No.2 Fund, the amount which was transferred from each Fund comprised two elements: "a part which would meet the precisely calculated tax liability" and "a pure transfer of free surplus". It appears from the 30 June 1985 Balance Sheet of the No.2 Fund that, by that day, the whole of the amount transferred to the Fund had been disbursed, leaving only the contentious $954,439 less bank changes of $2,260.

6. Speaking of the concept of "eligible insurance business", the learned senior member who constituted the Administrative Appeals Tribunal said:

"This means in my view that, all matters and things associated with such

business, one of which is the investment of monies (so as to ensure

sufficient monies or assets to meet claims) and the other is the payment

of tax.

Premium income being distinguished, all other income derived from the

business of or in relation to the issuing of policies or undertaking of

liability is taxable. The income of the No.2 fund is income of the

society. It is not premium income and it is taxable."

7. The applicant challenges this approach, contending that each of its Funds is kept completely separate, so that the income of each Fund takes its character from the Fund itself. It is immaterial, say counsel, that the moneys paid into the No.2 Fund emanated from income earned by the applicant in its eligible insurance business; the No.2 Fund was established for its own specific purposes; it is freestanding and independent of all other Funds of the applicant.

8. Subject to one matter, we agree that the source of the moneys paid into the No.2 Fund does not necessarily result in a conclusion that the investment income of those moneys is itself income from an eligible insurance business. It might have been possible for the applicant to so order its affairs that moneys paid into the No.2 Fund were thereafter rendered unavailable to any of the eligible insurance businesses, so that it could not thereafter be said that they were held "in relation to" the business of undertaking liability under eligible insurance policies. But, as it seems to us, the problem in the applicant's case is that it failed to take this course. The rules governing the No.2 Fund include rule 6.12.6. That sub-rule permits all or part of the surplus generated by the Fund (that is any surplus moneys after the payment of income tax) to be transferred to any other Fund; including, of course, any of the eight Funds whereby the society carries on eligible insurance businesses. In other words, the moneys held in the No.2 Fund are available for the purposes of the eligible insurance businesses at any time. It is true that the sub-rule requires the approval of the applicant's Board of Management and the Government Statist and Actuary for the making of any transfer. But that requirement does not deny their availability. It might be expected that approval would be readily granted if any of the eight insurance Funds encountered liquidity problems.

9. If it matters, the fact that transfer was more than a theoretical possibility is demonstrated by evidence that a number of transfers have in fact been made out of the No.2 Fund to other Funds of the applicant; although, admittedly, not to an "eligible insurance business" Fund.

10. In the course of their submissions counsel for the applicant referred to two decisions in this Court relating to the tax liability of insurance companies: Chamber of Manufacturers Insurance Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation (1984) 2 FCR 455 and CMI Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation (1989) ATC 4847. The problem addressed in each of those cases was whether particular capital profits should be regarded as taxable income of the taxpayer insurance company, that question depending upon whether the relevant investments were held by the taxpayer for the purpose of conducting its insurance business or in connection with a separate business. In each case it was held to be insufficient to enquire whether the relevant assets were treated separately in the taxpayer's accounts; the critical question was whether they were rendered unavailable to the insurance business.

11. The present problem arises out of a specific statutory context. It is, therefore, different from that which arose in Chamber of Manufacturers Insurance and in CMI Services. But, to the extent that there is an analogy between the two issues, those decisions support, rather than detract from, the decision of the Tribunal.

12. It will be recalled that we indicated one reservation on our acceptance of the applicant's argument that the source of the moneys paid to the No.2 Fund did not necessarily render their investment proceeds income of an eligible insurance business. That reservation arises out of an alternative submission made by counsel for the respondent, to the effect that business in relation to the undertaking of liability under eligible insurance policies continues until all the liabilities of the business - including liability for tax - are discharged. Counsel referred to Theophile v Solicitor-General (1950) AC 186 and Re Bird (1962) 1 WLR 686. It follows, say counsel, that income derived by the No.2 Fund on moneys held for the purpose of paying the tax liability which the applicant generated by the conduct of its eligible insurance businesses would be income derived from the conduct of that business, even if those moneys had been irrevocably rendered unavailable to the Funds through which the society carried on those businesses.

13. Having regard to the terms of rule 6.12.6, we do not find it necessary to reach a concluded view about this argument, although it seems to be correct.

14. In our opinion the appeal ought to be dismissed with costs.

This appeal raises for consideration the question of whether interest received by a friendly society on investments held in one of its funds, is income of the Society liable to tax under Division 8A of Part III of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Assessment Act"). The appeal is brought under s.44 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and is within the original jurisdiction of this Court. It is an appeal, on a question of law, from a decision of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal constituted by a Senior Member. It is being heard and determined by a Full Court pursuant to a direction given by the Chief Justice.

2. The applicant ("the Society") is a friendly society under the Friendly Societies Act 1958 (Vic) and its affairs are conducted in conformity with the requirements of that Act. Under paragraph 23(g)(i) of the Assessment Act its income is exempt income and thus its income is exempt from income tax under the Assessment Act.

3. Division 8A of Part III, comprising sections 116E to 116J, was inserted into the Assessment Act by Act No. 47 of 1984 and came into operation with respect to income received for the tax year ending 30 June 1985. The effect of sections 116F and s.116G as so inserted in the Assessment Act, having regard to the definitions contained in s.116E, was that notwithstanding paragraph 23(g)(i):-

"The assessable income of the year of income of ... (the Society) . ..

shall include so much of the total income (other than premiums) of the

society of the year of income as is derived from eligible insurance

business of the society".

4. Pursuant to s.116E of the Assessment Act, in section 116G(1), the phrase "eligible insurance business" means "business of, or in relation to, the issuing of, or the undertaking of liability under, eligible insurance policies."

5. It is not necessary to refer to the other definitions contained in s.116E but it is sufficient to state that some of the activities carried on by the Society during the year of income ended 30 June 1985 came within the meaning of eligible insurance business. It is not disputed that the Society is liable to tax on the total income (other than premiums) of the Society as was derived from those activities. The dispute arises, in fact, from an activity introduced by the Society whereby it set up a fund into which monies and investments representing the amount of the liability for tax for each eligible insurance business of the Society was transferred to be invested until the tax was to be paid. The Society paid the tax, when assessed, from assets held in this activity, which is described as the Life Insurance Management Fund No. 2 ("the No. 2 Fund"). The question is whether interest received by the Society in the year of income ended 30 June 1985 (gross $954,439) on investments in the No. 2 Fund was income derived from the business of or in relation to the issuing of, or the undertaking of liability under, eligible insurance policies.

6. The Friendly Societies Act contains provisions relating to the registration and control of friendly societies within Victoria. In order to be registered, societies must comply with the requirements of that Act. The Act provides that the affairs of a society are to be regulated by rules which must include rules making provisions for the investment of the funds, the keeping of the accounts and the audit of the same once a year at least. In practice, societies are required to maintain separate funds with respect to each of the activities of the society. By s.9, the Government Statist, with the approval of the Minister, is required to prepare model forms of accounts, balance sheets and valuations to be used by societies. Section 14 imposes obligations on societies and these obligations are based on the concept of funds representing the different activities of societies. Thus under paragraph 14(1)(da) a society is required annually to submit to the Government Statist an annual return, in the prescribed form, containing a revenue account in respect of each of the several funds of the society. Further, under sub-section 14(3) all moneys received on account of any particular fund provided for by the rules of the society are to be entered into a separate account and kept separate from moneys received on account of any other fund.

7. In conformity with the requirements of the Friendly Societies Act, the affairs of the Society are regulated by rules. The rules make provision for a large number of separate and distinct funds. The Society maintains some 37 separate funds including some 21 funds which are described as "Benefit Funds". Eight of the Benefit Funds are funds which come within the definition of an eligible insurance business as used in Division 8A of Part III of the Assessment Act. Thus the Society is liable to tax on so much of its income as is derived from each of those eight funds. Rule 7 of the rules of the Society provides for "Benefit Funds". Part of Rule 7.1, which applies to all Benefit Funds, is as follows:-

"PART 7 - BENEFIT FUNDS

7.1 (a) The Society may, for the purpose of providing for the

welfare of its members and their dependents, maintain benefit
funds

whose nature and circumstances shall be decided by the Grand Lodge

in Session. Each Benefit Fund shall be kept clear and distinct from

other fund. The assets of any Benefit Fund may be combined with the

assets of another fund, for investment purposes, only with the speci

written consent of the Government Statist".

8. The Flexible Insurance Fund No. 1 is one of the eligible insurance businesses conducted by the Society under Rule 7. This fund is managed in conformity with Rule 7.4 of the rules of the Society. Rule 7.4 enables persons to make application to contribute to the fund and on the payment of contributions, (premiums) those persons become members of the Society and are entitled to the benefits provided for by that rule. It is interesting to note the form of the rule setting up this fund:-

"7.4 THE FLEXIBLE INSURANCE FUND NO. 1

PART A - GENERAL

7.4.1 There shall be established a Flexible Insurance Fund No. 1

(hereinafter called the Fund) under the control of the Grand Lodge.

7.4.2 The Fund shall be kept separate and distinct from all other

Funds. The assets of the Fund shall be invested in such securities
as

approved by the Government Statist. All contributions received under

this Rule and all interest derived from the investment of moneys shall

paid to the credit of the Fund. Payments shall be made from the Fund a

provided by this Rule."

9. Income of the Society includes income (other than premiums) derived from this fund as well as income derived from the other eligible insurance businesses of the Society. In addition, the Society derives income from other funds managed by it which funds are not eligible insurance businesses. The Society, relying on paragraph 23(g)(i) of the Assessment Act is not liable for tax on the income derived from these funds.

10. Rule 6 is headed "Part 6 - Grand Lodge Funds" and a number of funds have been established under this Rule. Rule 6.1 has general application and provides:-

"6.1 In addition to the various Benefit Funds identified in Part 7 of

these rules, the Society may, for the purposes of administering its
affairs

and of preserving and furthering its objects, maintain Grand Lodge

Funds to be used for such purposes as the Grand Lodge sees fit. Each

Grand Lodge Fund shall be kept clear and distinct from each other fund

but the assets of Grand Lodge Funds may, with the consent of the

Government Statist, be combined for investment purposes."

11. The No. 2 Fund, being the fund giving rise to this appeal, comes within Part 6 of the rules of the Society and is managed in conformity with Rule 6.12. Rule 6.12 is in a form approved by the Government Statist and Actuary and is common to a number of friendly societies. It was formulated and introduced to take account of the proposed introduction of Division 8A into Part III of the Assessment Act. During the relevant period, the only moneys and investments transferred into the No. 2 Fund represented the anticipated tax liabilities of the Society with respect to income derived from its eligible insurance businesses. Rule 6.12 is set out in full:-

"6.12 LIFE INSURANCE MANAGEMENT FUND NO. 2

6.12.1 There shall be established a Life Assurance Management

Fund No. 2 under the control of the Board of Management.

6.12.2 The assets of the fund shall be kept separate and distinct from

all other assets of the Society.

6.12.3 The fund shall be invested according to guidelines laid down

by the Government Statist and Actuary.

6.12.4 Into this fund shall be paid the provision for taxation as

calculated for each financial year, and also any interest earned
on

this amount.

6.12.5 The find shall be used for the purposes of paying income tax

assessments as advised by the Australian Taxation Office from year
to

year.

6.12.6 All or part of the surplus generated by this fund may be

transferred to any other fund subject to approval of the Board of

Management and the Government Statist and Actuary."

12. As stated earlier in these reasons, the question for decision is whether interest received by the Society from investments held in the No. 2 Fund is income of the Society derived from an eligible insurance business of the Society being "business of or in relation to" the funds of the Society which constitute its eligible insurance businesses.

13. To some extent, the question becomes confused from the fact that one taxpayer only, the Society, is involved. Subject to the provisions of Division 8A of Part III of the Assessment Act its income is exempt from income tax. Tax is charged on so much of the total income (other than premiums) of the Society of the year of income as is derived from the eligible insurance business of the Society. The word "business" is not defined for this purpose, but the parties concede, correctly, that each of the eight funds constituted under Part 7 of the rules of the Society which have been accepted by them as being an eligible insurance business, is a separate business of the Society. Each of these funds constitutes directly a business of the issuing of or the undertaking of liability under eligible insurance policies. The relevant income derived by the Society from investments in those funds is liable to tax and the Society must pay that tax. The No. 2 Fund is not directly such a fund, and therefore is not directly an eligible insurance business. The moneys and investments transferred to the No. 2 Fund came from the eligible insurance businesses of the Society. It would seem to be clear that if the Society, in making provision to pay its liability for tax, had paid the amount of tax by way of transfer from each eligible insurance business to a stranger as an interest free loan repayable on demand, that stranger would be liable to tax on income received from the amount so lent but that the Society would not be liable to tax on that income since it would not be income derived by the Society. If the income of the Society was not exempt from tax, the Society would be liable to pay tax on the income derived from the investments in the No. 2 Fund. If rates of tax differed, questions would arise in these circumstances as to the quantum of tax to be levied.

14. The essence of the contentions made on behalf of the Commissioner is that the eight eligible insurance businesses involved are not separate taxpayers, that the Society is the only taxpayer, and that the character of the interest earned on the investments in the No. 2 Fund is not altered by being derived from that Fund; the interest received remains income from the eligible insurance businesses and the nexus between income and eligible insurance business is not broken merely because the capital upon which it is earned is kept in a fund which, as a matter of accounting, does not have life assurance, accident and disability policy holders. Reference was made to Rule 6.12 which enables moneys and investments held in the No. 2 Fund to be transferred to other funds (businesses). It was contended further that in any event the words "in relation to" as used in the definition of eligible insurance business, are words of very wide import and should be construed in such a way that the income received from investments in the No. 2 Fund were received in relation to the business of the issuing of, or the undertaking of liability, under the eight eligible insurance businesses.

15. These contentions are rejected. For present purposes, it must be accepted that the Society, by transferring moneys and investments from the eight eligible insurance funds, was acting within powers conferred upon it by its rules. The transfer constitutes the breaking of the nexus between each of the eligible insurance businesses on the one hand and the other activities of the Society on the other. It is the Society which is liable to pay tax and it is free to make its own provisions to enable the tax to be paid. The liability to pay tax is imposed on the Society and not on an eligible insurance business being conducted by the Society. In the absence of paragraph 23(g)(i) of the Assessment Act, the Society would be liable to pay tax on all its income. Liability for tax cannot be imposed on the general activities of the Society under the guise of treating the income as coming within Division 8A of Part III.

16. It is accepted that the phrase "in relation to" has a very wide import, but those words cannot prevent the nexus being broken. The fact that the Society may transfer surplus moneys and investments from the No. 2 Fund to other funds does not alter the position. No suggestion has been made that the procedure adopted by the Society was improper. During the course of submissions, counsel for the Commission conceded that if the surplus funds in the No. 2 Fund were transferred sufficiently by a series of transfers to funds other than any of the eight eligible insurance businesses, the nexus with those businesses could be broken. It is, so it was contended, a matter of degree. Once it is accepted that the nexus is broken when the provision for tax is paid into the No. 2 Fund, the essential relationship between the income on the moneys and investments in the No. 2 Fund and the liability of the Society to members who have contributed to the eligible insurances business ceases to apply. That income does not form part of nor is it available directly for the purposes of or in relation to the business of the issuing of or the undertaking of liability arising out of each of those eight funds respectively. The income forms part of the general income of the Society to be dealt with in accordance with its rules.

17. Accordingly, the appeal should be allowed and the decision of the Tribunal should be set aside. Normally, the matter should be referred back to the Tribunal to be heard and determined according to law.

18. In the present case, counsel for both parties are agreed that the only issue relates to the liability to tax on the sum of $954,439 and that if the appeal is allowed, the Court should order also that the objection by the Society should be allowed and that the Commissioner should issue an assessment in conformity with these reasons. In the special circumstances of this case, that order should be made. The Commissioner should pay the costs of this appeal.
