ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - applicant received profit on sale of shares -whether the profit derived from sale of shares liable to income tax - section 26(a) - section 26AAA - consideration of when sale of shares took place.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.) ss.26(a), 26AAA, 260

REASONS FOR DECISION

6 May 1987 Deputy President C J Bannon PC

In order to protect the identity of the applicant it is necessary to set out a list of pseudonymns for the persons concerned. The applicant is a proprietary company ("the applicant") and its accountant will be referred to as "Land". Let the manufacturing business, later described, be known as Artifact Producers Pty. Limited ("Artifact"). Let the principal shareholders in Artifact be known as "Westend" & "Smith" and the smaller shareholder in that company be called "Artifact's accountant". Let Smith's family company be called "Corporate Veil". Let the takeover company referred to later be called "Skyline Pty Ltd."

In this application the applicant seeks a review of an assessment of income tax based on income derived by it during the year of income ended 30th June 1978 and issued to it by Notice of Assessment dated 14th May 1981 (T11). It specifically claims that the item shown on the adjustment sheet accompanying the assessment as "profit or sale of shares included in assessable income add $199,877.00 should be deleted and excluded from the assessable income of the taxpayer and the assessment amended accordingly. While a number of grounds of objection are taken, the principal ground is that the said sum of $199,877 being profit on the sale of shares in Artifact is not subject to income tax. The Commissioner of Taxation ("the respondent") on the other hand claims the said sum was correctly included in the applicant taxpayer's assessable income in accordance with section 26(a) or section 26AAA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.) ("the Act").

Artifact carried on a manufacturing business founded by a relative of Westend. Artifact at all relevant times has been a proprietary company of which the principal and roughly equal shareholders were Westend and Smith. In addition Artifact's accountant held a small shareholding. Smith was the active manager of Artifact. Artifact had accumulated substantial tax paid reserves as well as carrying on its manufacturing business. According to the evidence Artifact's accountant approached Land to ascertain if Westend would have any objection to Smith substituting a family company known as Corporate Veil for himself as a shareholder in Artifact. The transfer of shares in Artifact was restricted and subject to first offer to other shareholders. According to Land he had been urging Westend for some time to transfer Westend's shareholding to a family company for family reasons (transcript p. 127).

Land and Artifact's accountant discussed the setting up of Smith's family company, Corporate Veil, around April 1976 (transcript p. 139). Artifact's accountant had prepared a share valuation for Smith's shares in Artifact, which Land considered would be useful when Westend transferred his shares to a family company (transcript p. 141). A file note made by Land refers to the valuation of Artifact. It forms part of Exhibit R and bears the date 1st May 1976, but Land said it was a composite document, with notes made at various times and the date was added later. The note refers to the name of an officer in the Victorian Stamp Duties Office and the reference number in that office to the transfer (transcript p. 143). Further evidence about the note appears at transcript pp. 179, 180, 269 to 271.

The evidence of Land continued that his partner in his accounting business had clients who were shareholders in a dormant company (the applicant). They were anxious to dispose of their shares in the applicant and to avoid the necessity of a winding-up of the dormant company. Land said he arranged for Westend to acquire the applicant and to sell his shares in Artifact to it. He was corroborated in this by Westend. To that end he typed an agreement for the sale of Westend's shares in Artifact to the applicant on a typewriter at his office in St Kilda Road, Melbourne (transcript pp. 144, 199). The document in question became Exhibit 0. It was signed on 29th April 1976 (transcript pp. 198, 143) according to Land, corroborated by Westend. It was also signed on behalf of the applicant by its former directors under the common seal of the applicant.

A form of transfer of Westend's shares in Artifact to the applicant was typed according to Land by him on his typewriter in Sydney, where he was living (transcript p. 145). However, the date of the transfer was inserted on the typewriter at St. Kilda when he arrived in Melbourne (transcript pp. 145, 146). The transfer purports to be accepted by the applicant under its common seal applied by Land (transcript p. 147) and to be signed on 25th February 1977 by Westend and his wife as directors of the applicant. Westend signed as transferor on 29th April 1976 according to Land (transcript p. 146). The transfer is Exhibit L which is also reproduced as part of Exhibit R. Land explained that after Westend signed as transferor he kept possession of the incomplete document (transcript p. 146).

Turning to the transfers of the shares in the applicant from the previous shareholders, Land deposed that this was at no cost to Westend (transcript p. 148). He said his partner arranged for the execution of those transfers to Westend and his wife in April 1976 and that they were then executed by Westend (transcript p. 148a). The transfer journal of the applicant contained in Exhibit N shows all shares but one transferred to Westend and the remaining share to his wife on 29th April 1976. Mrs Westend executed a declaration of trust in favour of her husband in relation to that share (Exhibit G). The former shareholders in the applicant remained as its directors until February 1977 when they were replaced by Mr and Mrs Westend. The documentation is in Exhibit 5.

Land explained that Westend, once he had control of the applicant, was in no hurry to disclose to the Board of Artifact that he had sold his shareholding to the applicant. He remained on the register of Artifact as a shareholder and as a director, together with Smith and Artifact's accountant (transcript p. 73). Westend said in evidence that he wished to keep his movements as private as possible (transcript p. 30). It was explained that in Victoria, Stamp Duty is not leviable upon an agreement to transfer shares but only on the transfer (transcript p. 32). It was not until 24th February 1977 that he notified the Board of Artifact that his shares in Artifact had belonged to the applicant beneficially since 29th April 1976 (Exhibit K). Westend proferred no valid reason for the delay (transcript p. 75).

The income tax return of the applicant for the year of income ended 30th June 1976 (Exhibit 6) does not reveal the beneficial ownership of the shares in Artifact said to be acquired in April 1976. The applicant's tax return for the year of income ended 30th June 1977, although it reveals the acquisition, does not reveal the date thereof (Exhibit 7). Westend's tax return for the year of income ended 30th June 1976 (Exhibit 1) reveals the receipt of dividends from Artifact. His return for the year of income ended 30th June 1977 (Exhibit 2) shows him as still receiving dividends from Artifact and makes no mention of a sale of Artifact shares in April 1976.

Artifact's tax return for the year of income ended 30th June 1978 (Exhibit 8) records advice of the sale of Westend's shares and their transfer in February 1977. It also includes a copy of the minutes of a director's meeting of Artifact held on 28th February 1977, which said a transfer of shares dated and signed by Westend "as Transferor on 29th April 1976 such date being shown as the date of sale transferring the whole of his holding namely 1900 shares to "the applicant" was produced to the meeting duly stamped". The return also included a copy of Westend's letter dated 24th February 1977 to Artifact advising of a disposition on 29th April 1976. Exhibit 5 is a copy of the share certificate in Artifact issued to the applicant on 28th February 1977.

Artifact's tax return for the year of income ended 30th June 1976 (Exhibit 4) was prepared by its accountant. It does not mention any sale by Westend on 29th April 1976, but shows the crediting of a dividend, presumably to Westend on 29th April 1976 and a proposed dividend for the year of income ended 30th June 1976.

It is interesting to note that prior to his death Artifact's accountant made a written statement as to his knowledge of Westend's dealings with the applicant. This was admitted into evidence and is item 6 in Exhibit R. While his statement recites discussions with Westend's accountant, Land, as to the transfer of Smith's shares to Corporate Veil and as to their value, he states that he does not recollect being informed of Westend's sale to the applicant during 1976. He does refer to the desire of Smith to sell out his interest in Artifact and Westend's desire to continue the existing business which had been in his family for a number of years.

Strange features of Westend's dealings with the applicant appear from his evidence (transcript p. 96) and Land's evidence (transcript pp. 127b to 138). The loose leaf records of the applicant produced, became Exhibit K. A journal entry on the page marked 23 records the purchase of the shares in Artifact on 29th April 1976. It is out of calendar sequence, following entries on 30th June 1976. Land explained that his partner had made the latter entries while he inserted the former, the partner being unaware of the precise date of acquisition. Westend used the company to pay moneys to bookmakers, treating its accounts as his own. No bank account was opened by the new owners until March 1977 (transcript p. 129). The contract dated 29th April 1976 imposed no immediate requirement on the applicant to pay for the Artifact shares purchased, but that is understandable in the light of the applicant becoming Westend's family company.

In February 1977, Artifact was a cashed up company ripe for dividend stripping. Smith wanted to sell out. Westend wanted to take over the manufacturing business. It was put to me that in February 1977, Westend and Land had no motive to fabricate an agreement date of 29th April 1976. Land had a client and personal friend I will call "Skyline" who controlled a company which was in a position to write off considerable depreciation claims as against its profits. This company will be referred to as Skyline Pty Ltd. Skyline was introduced to Westend and Smith by Land in June 1977 according to Land (transcript pp. 161, 162). Skyline Pty Ltd. purchased all the shares in Artifact from Corporate Veil and the applicant and also the interest of Artifact's accountant. The control of Artifact was transferred to Skyline Pty. Ltd. on 4th July 1977 (transcript p. 165). The transaction was handled on behalf of the applicant by a Melbourne firm of solicitors, Messrs. Barker Harty & Company. Documents dealing with the sale were received into evidence (Exhibit D) together with the affidavit of Mr S.D. Wilkins (Exhibit C) to which they were exhibited (See also Exhibit M).

The applicant company made slightly more on the sale than did Corporate Veil. The sale agreement is Exhibit X. Prior to the sale, Artifact sold the stock and plant of its business to a company which Westend had purchased under an agreement of 1st July 1977 (Exhibit V). That company changed its names to a name I shall call Westend Artifact. Westend Artifact carried on the business under Westend's control. Skyline Pty. Ltd. in effect purchased the accrued money reserves of Artifact, on which tax had been paid. Skyline Pty. Ltd. later sold the shares it acquired in Artifact to two persons under an agreement made on 27th June 1978 (Exhibit U). Before selling Artifact, Artifact paid $613,050 in dividends to its new shareholder Skyline. These were disclosed to the respondent. Artifact duly paid its income tax as assessed.

Skyline Pty. Ltd. disclosed its receipts and the profit it made on the resale of its shares in Artifact in its Income Tax Return for the year of income ended 30th June 1978 (Confidential Exhibit 13). It was assessed to tax on the profit disclosed in that return and tax was paid (transcript pp. 284-287). The Taxation Department has never suggested Skyline Pty. Ltd. was engaged in tax avoidance. At one stage in these proceedings the respondent was given leave to raise a ground in support of its assessment under section 260 of the Act, but this ground was subsequently abandoned and it seemed to me properly as there was a paucity of evidence concerning "purpose" in relation to the transaction between the applicant and Skyline Pty. Ltd. (See Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Gulland [1985] HCA 83; (1985) 62 ALR 545, 552.)

The chief thrust of the respondent's case was that no agreement was made between Westend and the applicant in April 1976 and indeed there was no sale of shares until February 1977.

Westend's accountant, Land, was seriously upset in 1976. His accountancy partnership was dissolved (transcript p. 293) and he was suffering serious personal illness (transcript p. 306). One possibility which cannot be discarded is that he intended to put in train steps to transfer Westend's shares in Artifact in 1976 but did not manage to do so because of his problems. Another possibility is that when the advantages of selling Artifact for its accumulated reserves became apparent in 1977, Westend and Land thought it expedient to create a contract dated April 1976 to avoid the effects of section 26(a) and section 26AAA of the Act. However, it has to be observed that no motive was proved for Land, a professional man, to lend himself to a deception, apart from an eagerness to serve a client, whose principal colleague as a shareholder in Artifact wanted to sell out. The accountant also had an available buyer in the offing in the first half of 1977. Apart from those considerations, Artifact's accumulated reserves were an obvious temptation to Land's client to make a realisation without paying tax on dividends if that could be achieved.

A tax assessor with the respondent noticed a discrepancy in returns lodged by the applicant and by Artifact as to the ownership of shares in Artifact in 1976. He sent out questionnaires about these matters, one to Westend and his answers are in Exhibit 11. Westend now had another solicitor, a Mr Walter, acting for him. Mr Walter had a telephone conversation with a tax investigator on 10th October 1981. The investigator made a note of the conversation (Exhibit EE). He also made notes prior to the conference (Exhibit DD) concerning matters for investigation. Amongst other things Mr Walter told the investigator that he had interviewed Westend and was satisfied he was genuine. He asked what evidence would be accepted to establish that the applicant's purchase of shares took place on 29th April 1976. After discussion he said he would contact the investigator again. Mr Walter also made notes of this conversation (Exhibit HH).

On 22nd December 1981, the Tax Assessor, Mr M.R. Anderson, the Tax Investigator, Mr R. Humphries, the accountant, Land and Mr Walter, conferred at the Tax Office in Melbourne. Mr Humphries in evidence stated that he could not remember the particular interview (transcript p. 309) but that he made notes in his own handwriting which he signed and finished either that same day or the next, a fact he inferred from internal evidence in the notes (transcript p. 310) . He said the red ink notes on the page numbered 142 were not part of his original notes. The notes were admitted as Exhibit 18. Omitting names of persons, the following is stated:

The Agreement of 29.4.76

Prepared by the Accountant, - , during a time of personal stress.

No diary notes as to when it was prepared, but was not 29 April 1976.

From the type face used "(the Accountant)" thinks it was prepared in October/November 1976 on one of his trips to Melbourne.

Probably signed by (Westend) October/November 1976 but not sure.

No idea when the "(previous shareholders in the applicant)" signed it as they were (Accountant's partner) clients.

Why date of 29.4.76 - see below.

General

(Accountant) stated that he had engineered the transaction after a phone call from "(Westend)" on 29/4/76 - no evidence produced of such a phone call.

(Accountant) had recommended to (Westend) that for ease of administering his estate, shares in ("Artifact") should be transferred to a company. (Westend) had agreed. (Accountant) knew that his ex-partner ( ) had an inactive shelf company (the applicant) available and he decided that he would use it for the share transfer. He advised (Accountant's partner) of his intention but did nothing further until February 1977. He claims however that (Westend) signed the share transfer in May 1976, but has no proof. It was unclear when the share transfer was signed by the (applicant's) shareholders, but this was probably not until after February 1977.

....

Books of Account Minute Book (Applicant)

(Accountant) only has them from Feb. 77 onwards - all written from that date.

1976 Tax Return/Annual Accts

Prepared/lodged by (Accountant's partner) who ignored share purchase. (Accountant) claims amended documents were prepared but they were not available.

Statement by (Accountant)

(Accountant) has prepared a statement which John Walters will have typed and submit to us.

Other matters of fact and opinion are also noted.

Mr Humphries was skilfully cross-examined by counsel but remained unshaken. The assessor Mr M.R. Anderson was called at my insistence. He supported Mr Humphries but the value of his evidence was seriously diminished by the fact that he had refreshed his memory from a copy of Mr Humphries' notes and had very little personal recollection of the conversation, if any.

The solicitor, Mr John Walter, made an affidavit (Exhibit FF) and gave evidence. He also remembered very little about the matter, which was not surprising. He had been through various changes of firms since then. His notes were admitted into evidence (Exhibit HH) as explained, being the notes of his telephone conversation with Mr Humphries and Exhibit KK being notes of the conference at the Tax Office. The latter note reads:-

"Conference attended -at the Office of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, 270 King Street Melbourne at 3:30 p.m. on December 22, 1981.

At that conference submissions were made to the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation based upon the material presented to me by (Accountant) earlier in the afternoon of December 22, 1981.

There was an examination of company registers and records and I read out the material which had been prepared by (Accountant) in written form as to his notes.

The conference lasted approximately 1 hour."

Also admitted into evidence, but only on a limited basis, was a copy letter Mr Walter sent to Land dated 18th December 1981 (Exhibit JJ). The letter does not prove the facts but it does show Mr Walter's state of mind. It seeks Land's help to establish that the agreement for sale of shares in Artifact to the applicant was in fact prepared and executed on 29th April 1976. Its importance lies in the fact that it shows the matters noted by Mr Humphries as raised and answered were understood to be in issue before the conference.

A further document, being Land's notes became Exhibit Y. There is an inference from those notes that the share transfer agreement was not signed in April 1976. Those notes were not sent to the Respondent. Instead Mr Walter said a memorandum, Exhibit 19, was sent in to the Respondent by his office. Paragraph 5.3 of that memorandum shows Mr Walter had some doubts. It reads:-

"5.3 An agreement is in existence dated April 29, 1976 with respect to the transfer of shares from Mr (Westend) to (the applicant) but there is no evidence this agreement was executed prior to February 1977."

An affidavit by a former director of the applicant became Exhibit W. It does not throw any real light upon the date of execution of the agreement for sale of shares in the company Artifact by Westend to the applicant. Paragraph 4 of that affidavit was not read (transcript p. 224).

In this matter I have carefully listened to all the evidence. With some regret I have formed the conclusion that the evidence of Mr Humphries is to be preferred whenever there is a conflict between him and other evidence. I say with some regret, because I believe the accountant, Land, did have in mind making some arrangement for Westend in the first half of 1976 but for various reasons, did not get around to it. It was urged that I should infer an oral agreement at that time. However, the terms of such an agreement were not considered in April 1976 and it cannot be said that the terms embodied in the document (Exhibit 0) were ever adverted to in substance or in detail at that time in such a way that it could be given commercial effect. While admissions made by the accountant, Land, to the Taxation officers do not appear to me to be evidence against his principal, see Fraser Henleins Pty. Ltd. v Cody [1945] HCA 49; (1945) 70 CLR 100, 113, they leave me in the position that I am unable to rely on his evidence. The inconsistencies in Westend's actions and conduct detailed previously leave me in doubt that any agreement was made on or about 29th April 1976 as he alleges or that a transfer of Westend's shares in Artifact was signed by Westend when alleged.

The onus of proof of upsetting the assessment lies on the taxpayer. I am satisfied it has not been discharged and that the profits derived by the applicant taxpayer from its sale of shares in Artifact on or about 4th July 1977, after an acquisition in around February 1977 are liable to income tax pursuant to section 26(a) and section 26AAA of the Act.

The assessment under review is affirmed.
