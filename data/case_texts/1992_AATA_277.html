The Tribunal decides that:

allowed; and

(b) the objection decisions in respect of the years of income

Matter No. QT89/283-291

The Tribunal decides that the respondent Commissioner's decisions on the objections in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1973 to 30 June 1981 inclusive be set aside and the objections allowed in full.

DECISION

The applicants, who seek review of various objection decisions, elected to have their applications heard together. They are husband and wife and I will refer to them as H and W respectively. These applications arise out of the making of default assessments by the respondent in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1973 to 30 June 1981 inclusive.

2. Section 190 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") provides that in proceedings before this Tribunal under Part V of the Act the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive shall be upon the taxpayer. This means that the burden of establishing that the assessed taxable income exceeds the taxable income actually derived during the year of income lies upon the taxpayer (George v FCT [1952] HCA 21; (1952) 86 CLR 183 AT 201).

3. In McCormack Pty Ltd v FCT (1979) 79 ATC 411 at 4121, 4123 and 4132, Gibbs, Stephen and Murphy JJ. approved dicta of Mason J. (dissenting) in Gauci v FCT 1975 75 ATC 4257 at 4261 as follows:

"Section 190(b) of the Act imposed on the appellants the burden of

proving that the assessments were excessive. The appellants relied on

their evidence and that of Graham in order to show that the

assessments were excessive. Once that evidence was rejected, the

appellants' case necessarily failed.

The Act does not place any onus on the Commissioner to show that the

assessments were correctly made. Nor is there any statutory

requirements that the assessments should be sustained or supported by

evidence. The implication of such a requirement would be inconsistent

with sec.190(b) for it is a consequence of that provision that unless

the appellant shows by evidence that the assessment is incorrect, it

will prevail."

The applicant will discharge the onus by proving factual material which shows that it is more likely than not that the assessment is excessive. It is the applicant who is presumed to know the facts relevant to ascertainment of the applicant's taxable income and it is therefore for the applicant to put that evidence before the Tribunal. If the applicant chooses not to put the evidence before the Tribunal then it is open to the Tribunal to infer that the applicant's evidence would not assist the case unless an adequate explanation is given.

4. The applicant will not succeed merely by establishing that the Commissioner made an error. This is especially the case where assessments are made pursuant to the powers conferred on the Commissioner by section 167 of the Act and is made clear by decision of the High Court in FCT v Dalco (1990) 90 ATC 4088. At page 4090 Brennan J. posed the question to be answered in an appeal against an assessment made pursuant to paragraph 167(b) of the Act in these words:

" . . . does the taxpayer discharge the burden of proving that the

assessment is excessive where

(a) he does not prove that the amount assessed as his taxable income

in fact exceeds his taxable income, but

(b) he shows that the Commissioner formed a judgment as to the amount

of his taxable income on a wrong basis?"

5. That question was answered by Brennan J. (with whom Mason C.J. Gaudron and McHugh JJ. agreed) at pages 4093-4 as follows:

"But where, as here, the taxpayer has not proved that his actual

taxable income is less than the amount assessed, the Court does not

know all the material facts and it cannot find that the amount

assessed is wrong. A taxpayer who shows on the facts that are known a

mere error by the Commissioner in assessing the amount of the

taxpayer's taxable income does not show that his objection should have

been allowed. . . . Unless the amount of the assessment is found

to be excessive in the sense of being greater than the taxable income

on which tax ought to have been levied, the taxpayer fails on his
appeal."

6. I turn now to the facts. By notices of amended assessment dated 29 February 1984 the Commissioner notified amended assessments in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1973 to 30 June 1981 inclusive to H. On 29 March 1984 the respondent took the same action in relation to W except that in respect of some years the assessments were probably not amended assessments. Nothing turns on this.

7. The basis for the amended assessments is to be found in Exhibit 15 which consists of a statement of assets and liabilities and a statement entitled "Calculation of Taxable Income". These statements are sometimes euphemistically described as betterment statements, a description unlikely to be accepted by the object of the statement.

8. The statements were prepared on the basis that H and W were in effect in partnership on an equal share basis. As will appear in the course of these reasons that has been shown to be an invalid assumption on the respondent's part.

12. While there are other adjustments also having the effect, so it is said, of disclosing understatements of taxable income those amounts are the major amounts in dispute.

13. Probably, on the evidence, W became aware during 1976 that H was engaged in the importation of prohibited imports. As to how H's activities relate to the joint venture fishing business conducted by H and W I do not know. It is clear, however, that some part of the income derived by the illegal operation was disclosed as income from fishing in the partnership returns.

14. Some time after she became aware of the illegal activities W consulted a solicitor who eventually referred H and W to senior counsel. As to what transpired with senior counsel I do not know, but thereafter an amount of at least $150,000 was deposited in the solicitor's trust account, H and W moved home and H gave up, so I was told, any further connection with the importation of prohibited imports. In December 1981 H and W were arrested and charged with offences relevant to the illegal importations. H was subsequently convicted and W was acquitted.

15. In relation to the year ended 30 June 1973, W identified certain unexplained withdrawals from bank accounts totalling $3,654. She was, however, unable to give any frank explanation in relation to these sums because of the effluxion of time. $2,000 seems to have come from a personal loan obtained from the Bank of New South Wales. That loan was not taken into account by the respondent's statement of assets and liabilities and the taxable incomes assessed are thereby probably overstated by $2,000 if I accept the evidence of W.

16. A further personal loan of $2,000 was obtained from the St George Building Society and once again failure to take this into account has the effect of overstating the taxable income.

17. Both personal loans were obtained for the purpose of financing the purchase of a home. The statement of assets and liabilities shows a loan obtained for $2,178 in the 1973 year and the purchase of freehold property in the 1974 year.

18. A further adjustment is necessary in respect of an amount of $3,500 shown as a savings bank deposit. That amount should be reduced to $2,748 and was, on W's explanation, used as a deposit on a home. Whether the $2,748 was actually n a bank account at 30 June 1973 I do not know. However, I am satisfied on W's evidence that the amount is overstated by $752.

19. If the amended assessments are to be maintained an adjustment must be made by a reduction of $2,752 in the combined taxable income for the year ended 30 June 1973.

20. In respect to the year ended 30 June 1974 I accept W's evidence that the cost of their home acquired in that year has been overstated by approximately $2,500. Her explanation was rational and I have no reason to doubt the truth of the explanation given at pages 25 and 97 of the transcript. The combined taxable income will need to be reduced by $2,500.

21. The private expenditure in the 1974 year is overstated by an amount of $22 per week said to be rent paid during the period November 1973 to June 1974. That adjustment is $760 reduction in the combined taxable income calculated.

22. A further adjustment is necessary in relation to a bond of $840 lodged with the local council in connection with the removal of a house. The bond was financed by a loan from the Bank of New south Wales financed by his father. The inclusion of the bond as an asset is appropriate but there should be an offsetting liability in respect of the loan from the Bank. The combined taxable income must be reduced by $840.

23. It is relevant to note that a family trip to Bali in December 1974 at $1,000 for plane fares plus accommodation for one month plus spending money was estimated at $400. The trip to Bali is inconsistent with the evidence that the family was having a struggle financially at that time. Although those amounts were probably incurred in the 1975 year of income the undertaking of the trip at that time is inconsistent with the claim of financial hardship during the 1974 year of income.

24. As will appear later these adjustments have led me to the conclusion that the amended assessments are not authorised by section 170 of the Act.

25. During the year ended 30 June 1975, and in addition to the family trip to Bali, H also travelled overseas without the family in both February and April 1975. Both trips were apparently to Singapore and/or Malaysia. The amount spent on these trips is uncertain.

26. An adjustment was claimed in respect of the purchase of a television set for $895. On the evidence of W that purchase has been counted twice in calculating the value of household furniture. I accept the explanation given by W at page 31 of the transcript which would justify a reduction of the combined taxable income by an amount of $895 in the 1975 year.

27. Although the private expenditure levels for the 1975 year have increased markedly on previous years I was left to speculate as to whether the amounts adopted by the respondent were in fact excessive. As already indicated, relatively substantial amounts were being spent on overseas travel. As to exactly what occurred on those trips was not established before me but W clearly now believes that H engaged in the illegal activities while on these and later trips. I am entitled to assume that H was deriving income from the importation of prohibited goods by this time and that there was an additional income flow to the family. I am not prepared to accept the evidence about the family's private expenditure given by W. Although she may not have known it seems to me that it is highly probable that the family income was being augmented from the illegal activities in the year of income ended 30 June 1975. However, as appears later, I do not need to consider the adjustments necessary for this year of income.

28. In respect of the year ended 30 June 1976, as with the earlier years, W gave evidence of detailed calculations designed to show that the assets betterment statement was incorrect. Unlike the earlier years W was prepared to concede that the adjustments made for the 1976 year resulted in an understatement of income but not her income.

29. In her evidence W sought to explain a number of adjustments which needed to be made to in effect exclude double counting. She identified several amounts namely $960 for housing loan repayments, purchase of a typewriter at $165, and $2,522 as private expenditure already included in the assets betterment statement. While it might be true, as W says, that there has been some double counting, so that the combined taxable income is excessive, I am not prepared to make any adjustments for the 1976 year to the combined taxable income. The only issue to be decided for the 1976 year is whether W derived any of the undisclosed income. That illegal activities were being carried out in the 1976 year is evidenced by the expenditure on overseas air travel.

30. The same situation continued into the year ended 30 June 1977 because H continued his illegal activities during this year of income. The only adjustment which needs to be made for this year is that the capital loss of $4,000 said to have been incurred in respect of a motor vehicle should be deleted and a profit of $2,000 on the sale of the car being a profit of a capital nature should also be excluded from the taxable income calculation. That means a reduction of $6,000 in the calculated combined taxable income.

31. Exhibit H is a photocopy of pages said to be from a solicitor's trust account. W said in evidence that the trust account was kept in two parts in a false name and was apparently used to transfer funds to false name building society accounts. The copy documents do little to explain the transactions recorded but do show considerable amounts of money passing between the trust accounts and also building society accounts. An amount of $70,000 was deposited in the "Financial Affairs" account in either May or June 1977 according to the record. There is, of course, nothing that I can discern on the exhibit that identifies the account as belonging to H or W. W denies most emphatically that she had any interest in the trust account and that tends to be confirmed by the false name used for the account in that it purports to be the name of a male person.

32. There is no room for doubt in the light of W's evidence that at least $150,000 was paid into the false name trust accounts before July 1977. It is the subsequent transactions which have quite frankly left me totally bemused. It seems the solicitors adopted a practice of moving funds between the trust account and various building society accounts. Those transactions have not been satisfactorily explained before me although I suspect the respondent has fallen into error in relation to these transfers of funds between the trust account and the building societies.

33. It is because I am unable to effectively analyse the transactions in the trust account that I cannot be satisfied in respect of the years ended 30 June 1978, 30 June 1979, and 30 June 1980 that the combined taxable income calculated by the respondent is excessive. W's evidence as to detailed transactions during these years of income does not overcome the basic problem of the cash flow in those years as evidenced in the trust accounts and the flow to and from the false name building society accounts also operated by the solicitors. As might be expected the solicitors were not called to explain the operation of these accounts. I am left to speculate as to whether the income derived from the trust account through the building society accounts was derived by the so-called trustees, one or both of the applicants or someone else (c.f. Harmer and Others v FCT 91 ATC 5000). Notwithstanding the lack of evidence I am prepared to accept that it is more likely than not that W did not derive any part of the $150,000 originally deposited in the trust accounts. To the extent that a share of those amounts has been included in her assessed taxable income the assessments are excessive. The reason for this finding is that I have accepted W's evidence that she did not in any sense participate in the illegal importations. It follows that she did not derive the income derived from those operations.

34. In respect of the year ended 30 June 1979 W gave evidence about various specific items which she said explained in part the unexplained withdrawals of $67,463. I accept W's evidence that an amount of $4,582 was incurred in purchasing a house and should be added to that item in the statement of assets. That adjustment will not, however, affect the calculation of the net assets. An amount of$2,000 shown as an asset was in fact consideration allowed on transfer of a boat in part consideration for purchase of real estate (Exhibit C).

35. I also accept W's evidence that funds held in the false name accounts were transferred to the fishing account from time to time in what seems to have been an unsophisticated system for laundering money.

36. I do not accept W's evidence in relation to adjustments necessary in the year ended 30 June 1980 in respect of the purchase of a fishing boat and the proposed purchase of another boat. Those transactions appear to relate to a company which, so far as I can see, has not been taken into account in the asset betterment statement. The loan of $8,000 to H and W from H's father and $6,000 from W's parents should, however, be taken into account so as to include the liability of $14,000. I am satisfied that these funds were advanced by the respective parents and were not funds of either applicant "laundered" by passing amounts through the accounts of the applicants. In this regard the evidence of the respective fathers was clear and to the point. I have no reason to doubt the truth of their evidence.

37. Although W conceded in cross-examination that she had operated false name bank accounts to launder money it does not follow, on the evidence, that income generated by these accounts and income passing into these accounts was her income. Clearly for some period of time commencing in 1976 W was concerned to conceal and conserve funds initially derived from the illegal imports. From that time on she became irretrievably involved in the movements of the funds which emanated from the solicitor's office. I am not satisfied, however, that she ever became involved in the importing of the prohibited goods. The moneys derived from the sale of the prohibited goods was not derived in whole or in part by W.

38. The evidence makes it clear that the amount of at least $150,000 deposited with the solicitors was income derived from the illegal operations but there is no evidence before me to suggest that income from these operations was limited to $150,000. It seems to me to be entirely open as to whether other income was also derived from this source.

39. During the 1980 year of income an amount of $42,000 was deposited with another firm of solicitors under false names. In the notices of objection for the year ended 30 June 1980 signed, so it seems, by H and W the taxpayers admitted that their respective taxable incomes had been understated by an amount of $21,000. W did not deny the statement but claimed to have not been directly involved. In view of her very direct involvement in the conduct of the false name building society accounts it may be thought surprising that she was not also involved in the false name deposit with the solicitors.

40. H was frank as to his convictions and his failure to disclose the income from the prohibited imports. He said that the total amount of that income was $160,000 plus travelling expenses. That seems to me to approximate $200,000. He acknowledged that $150,000 was deposited with solicitors in June 1977 who on lent the money on short term contracts secured by mortgages.

41. However, H was, in my view, less than frank in relation to other parts of his evidence and notwithstanding the existence of an order under sub-section 35(2) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 he refused to answer questions in cross-examination on the ground that his answers may incriminate him; as he was entitled to do. When this objection is taken during cross-examination the Tribunal must consider what weight should be given to the evidence as a whole. In this case I would not give any weight to H's uncorroborated evidence, except as indicated, because I formed the opinion that he was deliberately vague and dissembling. I accept, however, that he did seek to keep secret his importing of prohibited substances from W so that she was not initially aware of those activities and did not participate in the importations.

42. It is likely, however, that W was aware of H's illegal activities by the time he made the overseas trip in March 1977. There is no dispute between the parties that the illegal importations took place in July 1976 and March 1977.

43. Evidence was given by an officer of the Australian Federal Police involved in the investigation and prosecution of H and W in 1982. I have re-read the transcript of that evidence but found it of little assistance in deciding this matter.

44. The respondent called one of his officers who described himself as an auditor but who is not an accountant and apparently does not have any relevant tertiary qualifications. The officer is the author of Exhibit 15 which is the "Statement of Assets and Liabilities" and the "Calculation of Taxable Income". I accept that the methodology adopted does not depart, as a general proposition, from the standard basis adopted in preparing such statements. The problems in this case arise from the details rather than the methodology with resulting errors in the methodology.

45. The item Private Expenditure seems to have been based on an analysis of the applicant's banking transactions and an estimate of private expenditure prepared by the applicants in March 1979 in respect of the year ended 30 June 1978 (Exhibit 17). That document shows total private expenditure of $7,707.75 for that year. The officer did not accept this statement but calculated private expenditure by cash as $3,524 and private expenditure by cheque as $5,841. There is no explanation for this apparent discrepancy. I am satisfied that Exhibit 17 is a reasonable attempt to estimate the private expenditure for H and W and their two children for the year ended 30 June 1978. I have therefore come to the conclusion that the estimate of private cash expenditure for that year used by the respondent is likely to be correct. In the circumstances I think I should accept that the estimates of private expenditure both by cheque and cash adopted by the respondent are probably close to the mark for the years ended 30 June 1978 through to 30 June 1981. The estimates adopted certainly increase over the later years but those increases are consistent, in my view, with inflationary trends and the increased economic resources of H and W from 1976 on. In relation to the earlier years no adjustment has been made for the fact that the children of H and W were born in 1972 and 1975 respectively. To adopt the 1978 estimate of personal expenditure on the basis for the estimates in the years ended 30 June 1973, 30 June 1974 and 30 June 1975 is to ignore totally the change in the size of the family and the fact, which is commonly accepted, that children tend to cost more to maintain as they mature. I have therefore come to the conclusion that the estimates of personal expenditure for the early years are probably overstated by a significant amount. I cannot and do not need to quantify the overstatement on any rational basis but I think the figures adopted should be reduced by one-third calculated as follows:

46. I should add that during the 1973 year of income, H and W were residing with H's parents for part of the year.

47. In relation to the trust account transactions the auditor relied upon his analysis of ledger sheets held by the solicitors. Those ledger sheets referred to the false name accounts in respect of the deposit of $150,000. There is no reason to doubt the auditor's evidence in relation to the analysis and I would adopt those figures subject to a fundamental problem to which I will return. The analysis by the auditor is Exhibit 20.

48. I accept the evidence of W in relation to the alleged purchase of a boat in 1979. In fact this was given as part consideration for the purchase of a property at Caringbah from H's father for a total consideration of $34,880. The boat was given as part of the consideration at a value of $2,000 - this is made clear by Exhibit L. It seems to me to be of no consequence that the family company purchased the property. There is simply no basis for continuing to include the boat as an asset of H and W.

49. The capital loss of $4,000 shown in the calculation for the year ended 30 June 1977 was said by the auditor to be a loss arising from the sale of a Range Rover motor vehicle originally purchased for $16,000 and sold for $12,000. No explanation was provided by the auditor as to how he arrived at these figures.

50. In her evidence W said (page 36) that the Range Rover was purchased for $10,000 and sold for $12,000. She said that $16,000 was the new vehicle price. Exhibit G corroborates the evidence as to the sale price. Somewhat strangely there is no reference to the vehicle in the partnership return of income for the year ended 30 June 1977.

51. As I have formed the view that W is a witness of truth I am prepared to accept her evidence. That means that the capital loss of $4,000 should be deleted. Whether the $2,000 profit is an assessable profit was ignored by the parties. I should not speculate as to the circumstances of the purchase and sale but I did form the impression that the vehicle was purchased for use in the fishing business. In these circumstances the profit is probably not taxable and an adjustment would need to be made to delete the $4,000.

52. The trust account expenditure was analysed by the auditor in a document which is now Exhibit 21. Although the exhibit shows the movement of funds out of the trust account it does not describe the nature of the transactions except in relation to one entry. The auditor made allowances for outgoings from the trust account which he decided were tax deductible. Those amounts are not included in the amounts shown in the assets betterment statement.

53. The withdrawals from the trust accounts were, as already noted, part of the process of laundering the moneys through loans and false name building societies accounts which in turn have unexplained withdrawals.

54. The problem is that these withdrawals, although unexplained, do not represent assessable income. The exception to this is any amounts of interest derived in the false name accounts. If the original sums deposited in the solicitor's trust account are brought to account as assessable income the mere transfer of those funds, although unexplained, through various building society accounts does not thereby generate further income.

55. In the ordinary course of preparing an asset betterment statement and a calculation of taxable income, funds which are merely passing from one account to another account do not thereby affect the calculation of the taxable income because they do not affect the overall balance of the net assets.

56. That principle has been lost sight of in the present case because of the complex arrangements entered into by H and W to camouflage the $150,000 originally deposited with the solicitors. It has not been established that there was any significant further amount derived from the illegal activities and equally it has not been established that H and W declared all their assessable income.

57. But that is not to say that amounts which cannot be traced on a money trail thereby become assessable income. Such a course may be tenable if there is uncertainty as to undisclosed income. Where, however, the undisclosed income has already been exposed and in the main quantified, to then add unexplained withdrawals from those funds because the money chain cannot be followed, is to admit to double counting of income. It is the income derived from the false name accounts which has to be ascertained. It is not to the point to adopt a basis which is patently incorrect on its face. To illustrate, it might be assumed that the moneys in the false name accounts derived interest income at an average of 10% per annum. The amount of undisclosed income in the first year would be $15,000 and in the second year $16,500. The withdrawals from the accounts and deposits in other accounts may affect this figure but do not form a basis for calculating the income.

into the account?

A. I was, to a certain extent.

59. Although these matters were heard together the evidence has established and the submissions assert a very different position for H and W. H concedes through his counsel that during the years ended 30 June 1976 and 30 June 1977 he derived an amount approximating $170,000 to $200,000 from importation of illegal substances. No such concession is made in respect of W.

60. Given the nature of the transactions involving the illegal importations, evidence about them is likely to be less than satisfactory and that is the case here. However, I was impressed by the frank and forthright manner in which W gave her evidence before this Tribunal. That is not to ignore the evidence that she participated actively in the laundering of the proceeds of the illegal imports and also that she attempted to cover up her husband's involvement.

61. I have come to the conclusion that W gave her evidence before me truthfully and I am prepared to accept her evidence as the best evidence of what in fact happened in relation to the income not disclosed to the respondent. It was she who convinced her husband to take the money to the solicitors. There the money was maintained in various false name accounts for four years until H and W were arrested and charged by the police in December 1981 in respect of the illegal importations. There is absolutely no basis in the material before me that would justify a finding that W participated in the illegal importations. It is true that she developed suspicions about her husband's illegal activities, that she eventually became aware of those activities and that she then took action to protect him from the consequences having satisfied herself, it seems, that the illegal importations ceased. I am satisfied that W did not participate as a joint venturer or a partner with H in the deriving of the income. It follows that the income from the illegal importations should be accepted as having been derived by H and not by W. That view is consistent with H's evidence that he tried to keep W out of the illegal importations. As already indicated I am not prepared to give any weight to H's evidence other than to note the consistency of his evidence on this aspect.

62. It might be thought that W's active involvement in getting her husband to deliver the illegally obtained money into the hands of the solicitors and her subsequent actions in relation to the false name banking accounts at the direction of the solicitors and H suggests a significant level of complicity. I think that view is open on the evidence but only in relation to events in time after illegal funds were derived by H. It seems to be the case that W set out to protect H from the consequences of his actions and certainly became an accessory in the laundering of the money. But that is not to say that she derived the income. She assisted H in deriving the interest income including the use of joint name accounts. However, it was his money invested at his direction. I can find no relationship between this money and the fishing business except of course that the income of the fishing business was inflated in an attempt to launder some of the illegal funds. That does not thereby make those illegal funds income derived by the partnership. In fact the business of the fishing partnership appears to have been entirely divorced from the illegal importations.

63. In relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1973 to 30 June 1975 inclusive I am satisfied that the asset betterment statement reveals nothing more than unexplained transactions which, due the passage of time have mostly been forgotten. Examples of specific errors in the statement are the overstatement of the value of property by $6,000 as at 30 June 1974 which accounts for most of the alleged understatement for that year. The balance of assets for the year ended 30 June 1973 is also overstated by at least $752 and may be overstated in total. All of this points to unexplained transactions rather than providing a basis for a finding of avoidance of tax due to fraud or evasion. Case X61 90 ATC 448 is an example of fraudulent avoidance of tax. The present case may be one of negligence but there is nothing before me to justify a finding that the applicants made false statements knowing them to be false in respect of the years ended 30 June 1973 and 30 June 1974. I am therefore satisfied that the precondition for the operation of paragraph 170(2)(a) of the act has not been established. While it is clear enough that H probably made fraudulent statements in relation to his income tax returns for the years ended 30 June 1976 et seq, I cannot see any basis for such a finding in relation to the earlier years.

64. It follows that because the conditions of sub-section 170(2) have not been satisfied in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1973 and 30 June 1974, the objection decisions must be set aside and the objections allowed on the basis clearly raised in the objections that the amended assessments were not authorised by section 170.

65. From the year of income ended 30 June 1975 et seq it is likely that income commenced to flow from the illegal activities of H. There is no basis in fact before me for a conclusion that the assessments made in respect of H for the year ended 30 June 1975 et seq are excessive. Rather some of those assessments may be understated because undisclosed income has been attributed incorrectly, in my view, to W. As already discussed, there have also been overstatements of income in Exhibit 15 due to the error in methodology.

66. On the other hand W's assessments for the year ended 30 June 1975 et seq have been shown to be excessive because they include a share of the undisclosed income derived by H. I have not overlooked the concession made by W in her notices of objection. However, as the respondent failed to acknowledge the concession I do not think I should take it into account in view of the evidence before the Tribunal.

67. I was invited to concern myself with the methodology of an asset betterment statement. A fundamental prerequisite to preparation of an asset betterment statement is to determine the persons who derived the assessable income. If the income disclosed by a correctly prepared betterment statement attributes that income to a person who is shown not to have derived the income then the asset betterment statement is flawed. That is the position with the present case where W has established that it is more likely than not that the income derived from the importation of illegal substances was not derived by W.

68. There is, however, the question of an admission by W that she deposited $42,000 with a firm of solicitors in a false name but on her own account and also an admission in relation to this amount in the objection for the year of income ended 30 June 1980. I am unable to be certain as to what the truth of the matter is in relation to this money except that I am satisfied that it is more likely than not that the $42,000 was part of the proceeds from H's illegal activities and was therefore income derived by him. It follows that no part of the $42,000 deposited with the Melbourne solicitors represented income derived by W. As I have concluded that W operated the false name banking accounts for the benefit of H and W has denied in evidence, which I accept, that she had any part in the deposit of the $42,000 with the Melbourne solicitors, I am also satisfied that any income derived from that deposit was derived by H solely. In coming to that conclusion I have not overlooked the concession in the notice of objection in relation to $21,000. During the hearing I refused an application to delete this concession from the grounds of objection. However, I have come to the conclusion that I am not bound by such a concession. The question of whether the income from the illegal imports and consequential interest earned thereon was fully argued before me - indeed as is obvious it was the main issue. My decision to refuse the amendment of the objection was probably wrong but more importantly to bind the Tribunal to such a concession is to bind the Tribunal to make an incorrect decision. (Lighthouse Philatelics Pty Ltd v FCT (1991) 22 ATR 707).

69. I have therefore come to the conclusion that each of the objection decisions in respect of W under review should be set aside and the objections allowed. In this regard I am satisfied after reading each objection carefully that they can be allowed so as to reduce W's taxable income to the amounts assessed prior to the investigation.

70. In respect of H the objection decisions in relation to the years of income ended 30 June 1973 and 30 June 1974 will be set aside and the objections allowed on the basis that the amended assessments are not authorised by section 170 of the Act.

71. As to the balance of the objection decisions under review in respect of H, I cannot be satisfied, on the material before me, that the amended assessments (original assessment for 1981 year) are excessive. The applicant H has failed to satisfy me on the balance of probabilities that those assessments are excessive. Indeed, those assessments understate rather than overstate the taxable income and tax payable by H. I find it necessary in terms of section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 to affirm those objection decisions because this Tribunal does not have power to make amended assessments. The material before the Tribunal indicates that some of the assessments, the subject of the objection decisions under review, are probably understated. Counsel for H admitted as much in the course of submissions. The Tribunal does not have the power to remedy the position either directly, or, it seems. by setting aside the objection decisions and remitting the matters back to the respondent. In these circumstances I understand that I can only affirm the subject objection decisions which disallowed the objections by H. Whether the respondent is authorised to make further amended assessments is not before me and is a matter for the respondent to consider.

72. In relation to the additional tax assessed in respect of H, I can see no basis for interfering with the respondent's decisions on the remissions for the years of income ended 30 June 1975 to 30 June 1981 inclusive notwithstanding that the tax assessed and additional tax assessed total an amount in excess of the taxable income in some years. In Case Y12 91 ATC 186 at 191 I said that it should be a very rare case where the tax and additional tax assessed exceed in total the taxable income. This is such a case. The evasion of tax by H was about as blatant as the respondent is ever likely to encounter.

73. For these reasons the objection decisions in respect of both applicants for the years of income ended 30 June 1973 and 30 June 1974 will be set aside and the objections allowed. The objection decisions for the years of income ended 30 June 1975 to 30 June 1981 in respect of the husband will be affirmed. The objection decisions for the years of income ended 30 June 1975 to 1981 in respect of the wife will be set aside and the objections allowed.
