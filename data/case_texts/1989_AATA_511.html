NT89/95 and Commissioner of Taxation [1989] AATA 511; (1989) 20 ATR 3910; 89 ATC 716 (11 August 1989)

Last Updated: 16 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

SALES TAX - Practice and Procedure - Two objections of different date to single assessment - Notice expressly-disallowing only first objection - Application to extend time for requesting independent review - Whether necessary.

Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) 1930 - S.29

Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 5) 1930

Sales Tax Procedure Act 1934 - S.12B

Applicant

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

No. NT89/95

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

10 August 1989

Sydney

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT89/95 TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

10 August 1989 Mr Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. The issue presented by the representatives of the parties as the issue for determination upon this reference is whether the Applicant Company should have the benefit of having the time extended within which to request reference of a decision of the Respondent disallowing an objection for review to this Tribunal. On that basis the application is for further time to do what should have been done within 60 days (sic) of the Applicant being served with a Notice of Assessment of Sales Tax. The assessment under challenge was an assessment which issued over 12 years (sic) after the date upon which liability to payment of sales tax was alleged to have arisen.
  2. The presentation of the issue was confused and the evidence imprecise for, notwithstanding the circumstance that the Applicant's representative was a solicitor, it seems that he was too close to the problem and too personally involved to have responded with the degree of precision which was called for. Not only did he represent the Applicant, but he was the Son of its principal officer ("Father") and through much of the period was a solicitor working in the legal practice of his Father - a practice later taken over by others. On the other hand the Commissioner's representative seems to have been the officer who at all material times over recent years has had the carriage of the file. He too was in some respects overly concerned in the matter, being troubled to defend the propriety and fairness of his conduct in representing the Commissioner in dealings with the Applicant: conduct which was in no way criticised by the Applicant. But one thing which followed from the approaches adopted by both representatives to the presentation of the issues was that both overlooked one all important factor.

3. Early in 1975 the Applicant imported a boat to Australia. It claimed that it was exempt from liability to sales tax by reason of the vessel being a licenced fishing vessel (cf. S.26(A)(1) First Schedule Sales of the Tax (Exemptions & Classifications) Act. On 26 May 1975 the Australian Customs Service established the value for duty of the vessel at $106,125. No assessment of sales tax was made and no dispute came into existence between the Applicant and the Commissioner. In consequence, in the ordinary course of events and in the absence of any contention that there had been fraud or evasion on the part of the Applicant, any claim on the part of the Commonwealth to sales tax would have passed forever into history during 1978. But in the event the matter did not become a dead issue for in August 1981 the sales tax status of the transaction was under consideration before Mr. Keith Beddoe, then Senior Assistant Commissioner of Taxation, but now a Senior Member of this Tribunal.

  1. Then in 1983 a second vessel was imported by the Applicant Company. That resulted in an assessment and in an action being brought by the Respondent against the Applicant in the Supreme Court of New South Wales claiming payment of sales tax in the sum of $150,341.52, plus additional tax pursuant to S.39 of the Sales Tax Assessment Act. That action was heard and a judgment in favour of the Commissioner in relation to the second vessel was handed down in favour of the Commissioner on 19 May 1987. The Applicant instituted an appeal against that decision.
  2. Then by notice in writing of 1 July 1987 the Commissioner gave notice to the Applicant that it was liable to pay $59,216.73 in sales tax forthwith in relation to the first-mentioned vessel ("the 1975 vessel"). The amount so claimed was made up as follows:

ASSESSMENT UNDER SUB-SECTION 10(1)

OF SALES TAX ASSESSMENT ACT (NO. 5) 1930, AS AMENDED

In respect of goods imported into Australia and entered for home consumption during the period 1 June 1975 and 30 June 1975 (both dates inclusive):-

RATES OF TAX TOTAL SALE VALUE AMOUNTS OF TAX

THEREON

15% $160,461.06 $24,069.16

Total $24,069.16

Less tax previously paid Nil

$24,069.16

Additional tax under section 8 of Sales Tax Procedure Act 1934, as amended and section 29 of Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) 1930, as amended, as incorporated in Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 5) 1930, as amended $35,147.57

Total amount $59,216.73

  1. In addition the Applicant was notified that, should the amount not be paid forthwith, it would be liable to additional tax which, pursuant to S.29 of Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) 1930, as amended, as incorporated in Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 5) 1930, as amended, would accrue at the rate of 20% per annum on the amount unpaid. On 4 August 1987 the principal officer of the company ("Father") was examined under oath by the Commissioner.
  2. By a notice in writing of 20 August 1987, prepared by a solicitor in the employ of the Father's firm, objection was taken to the assessment. It was in the following terms:

. "NOTICE OF OBJECTION

I hereby lodge notice of objection against the amount and value upon which (this company) has been required by notice of assessment dated 1st July, 1987 (copy attached), to pay tax as stated hereunder in respect of the following goods.

Description Sale Value Notified Tax

Motor Vessel $160,461.06 $24,069.16

Additional tax under Section 8 of Sales Tax Procedure Act, and Sales Tax

Assessment Acts No. 1 & 5 $35,147.57

TOTAL 559,216.73

The grounds for the objection are as follows:

  1. The sale value exceeds the correct amount as defined in the legislation.
  2. The additional tax exceeds the correct amount as defined in the legislation.

DATED 20th August 1987

(Signed)

Director - (Applicant Company)

Some dialogue followed between representatives of the Applicant and the Commissioner's representative upon the hearing and that resulted in a letter of 1 September 1987 being delivered to the Commissioner on the following day enclosing:

"a further Notice of Objection which is additional to the one lodged on 20th August, 1987."

It was contended for the Applicant that the objection was within time but that, in the alternative, an extension of time should be allowed. It is not disputed that the Commissioner granted any extension of time which might have been necessary.

8. The objection was in the following terms:

"ADDITIONAL NOTICE OF OBJECTION

I hereby lodge an additional notice of objection against the amount and value upon which (this company) has been required by notice of assessment dated 1st July, 1987 to pay tax as stated hereunder in respect of the following goods.

Description Sale Value Notified Tax

Motor Vessel $160,461.06 $24,069,16

Additional tax under Section 8 of Sales Tax Procedure Act, and Sales

Tax Assessment Acts $35,147.57

TOTAL $59,216.73

The grounds for the objection are as follows:

1. The vessel was exempt from sales tax under Item 119(1) of the Sales Tax (Exemptions & Classifications) Act

Alternatively;

2. The vessel was exempt from sales tax under Item 20A of the Sales Tax (Exemptions & Classifications) Act.

DATED: 1st September 1987

(Signed)

Director - (Applicant Company)

9. It is to be noted that no ground of objection was based upon any contention that it was a prerequisite to the authority of the Commissioner to make any assessment that he should have been satisfied that the payment of the tax was avoided by fraud or evasion.

10. By letter of 17 September 1987 the Commissioner responded acknowledging:



"Receipt of your notice of objection dated 20 August 1987 and further letter of 1 September 1987 enclosing an additional notice of objection ..."

Extensive particulars were sought by the Commissioner and, according to my understanding, it was common ground that the particulars were supplied, albeit not with alacrity and indeed only after notice had been served requiring answers in writing as provided for by paragraph 23(1)(a) of the Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) (1930). That notice was delivered on 26 November 1987.

11. By letter of 17 December 1987, written in response to the Commissioner's notice of the previous month, the Applicant requested certain information. It was provided under cover of a letter of 22 January 1988. It advised that:

"The departmental sale value of $160,461.06 has been calculated as follows:

$

Value for duty 106,125.04

Duty 27,502.51

133,717.55 Plus 20% Sales Tax Assessment Act (No.5) 26,743.51

160,461.06"

The Applicant was asked to advise whether it would now withdraw its letter of objection of 20 August 1987 and whether it wished to proceed with:

"the additional grounds of objection contained in your letter of 1 September 1987 which was lodged out of time and if so, you should promptly provide the information requested in questions 3, 4 and 5 of our letter of 17 September 1987."

The Applicant by its solicitors responded on 28 January 1988. In face of the information provided it acknowledged the accuracy of the calculation of tax payable but contended (inter alia):

"... as the vessel was imported in 1975 and the assessment was not assessed (sic) until 1987 (some 12 years later) we believe that the calculation of interest should only run from the date of the assessment.

We also believe that the Commissioner should, under Section 12B of the Sales Tax Procedure Act remit the whole of the tax alleged to be payable as the three year period noted in that section had expired four times over."

The letter went on to advise:

"In relation to the additional grounds of objection contained in our letter of 1st September 1987 we do intend to proceed with it (if it is necessary in light of our request above) ..."

12. Then by notice of 9 March 1988 the Commissioner gave notice to the Applicant:

"Your objection dated 20 August 1987 to the assessment, notice of which was issued on 1 July 1987, in respect of goods sold (sic) during the period 1 June 1975 to 30 June 1975 has been considered and has been disallowed." The letter went on to give notice of the right of the Applicant to request reference of the Commissioner's decision to this Tribunal or to a specified Federal Court and also gave notice of the obligation to pay the fee then required of $240.

13. No application for review or by way of appeal was made within the time allowed. Why that was so was quite unclear, but I am satisfied that it was the wish of the company as known to and expressed by its executives that there should have been a reference to this Tribunal. Whether the omission to request such a reference within the time allowed was occasioned by confusion on the part of the representative of the Company appearing before me, or whether it was occasioned by default on the part of a solicitor to implement instructions given him, I am unable to say. However, I do accept that the confusion was attributable in substantial measure to the rearrangements which followed the transfer of the business of a solicitor conducted by Father with the assistance of his Son and others to some of those others one of whom was the solicitor who was intended to have requested the reference.

14. The matter having been overlooked, it remained so until attention was somehow drawn to the matter - probably by the Commissioner's officer. It resulted in a letter being prepared on Friday, 23 December 1988 and delivered to the Commissioner that day. The letter requested that the application be referred to the Tribunal out of time. It stated:

"The reasons for the request being made outside the required period are as follows:

* We were acting under the mistaken belief that no further action would be taken on this matter until the determination by the Courts of the proceedings involving the second motor vessel.

* Not to grant the extension would result in undue hardship.

* The facts and circumstances relating to the importation and use of the (1975) motor vessel although similar are substantially different from those relating to the (1983 vessel).

* The assessment has only relatively recently been issued in respect of an importation which was made some 14 years ago."

15. On 30 December 1988 the Commissioner, by his officer, replied acknowledging receipt of the letter but not of the cheque said to have been enclosed with it and stating that the receptionist to the solicitors to the Applicant had been advised at 11:40 a.m. that day of the omission. The letter stated that by that hour the Applicant's representative had already departed on holidays, as had the Company's accountant. By reason of the omission of the required fee the Applicant was advised that:

"Your letter of 23 December 1988 must be treated as an invalid request for extension of time to lodge an out of time request for reference to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal."

It was not until 17 February 1989 that the fee "omitted due to clerical error" was delivered to the Commissioner.

16. On 2 May 1989, the eve of the hearing of the appeal against the decision of the Supreme Court of New South Wales relating to the 1983 vessel, the Applicant withdrew its appeal. Next day the Commissioner referred the application for extension of time to the Tribunal, requesting that the hearing be expedited

because:

"As a result of the Taxpayer's withdrawal of that appeal the Commissioner has issued a summons seeking an order from the Court that the Company be wound up under the Companies (New South Wales) Code. This hearing is set down for 30 May 1989."

The letter recorded the view of the Commissioner that the issues in relation to the two vessels were "considered to be identical". That letter did not form part of the formal documents submitted to the Tribunal and its terms did not become known to the Applicant until the hearing. However, there was also a further omission from the formal documents: the second letter of objection was also omitted.

17. However this litigation of ill-starred fortune did not even then progress smoothly. The Tribunal gave notice of a telephone directions hearing for 11 May 1989 to be followed by a hearing of the application later that month. The notice was not responded to and on the appointed date for the telephone directions hearing contact could not be established with the Applicant's representative. It is now known why. In the preceding few weeks the premises from which the Company had long carried on business closed. But that did not offer any reasonable explanation for the failure of the Applicant to be represented when the hearing was scheduled to commence on 25 May 1989. However, as no contact had been made by any officer of the Commissioner with the Applicant, no explanation was available in the lead-up to the hearing as to the non-appearance. It was therefore not known whether the Applicant had ever been made aware of the time appointed for the hearing. On this occasion contact was successfully made by telephone. The explanation was then offered that the notice of the telephone directions hearing and hearing having been received only after 11 May 1989, it had been assumed - rashly - that another date for hearing would have been appointed. The simple courtesy of a phone call to the Tribunal might have dispelled that misunderstanding. A fresh hearing date was then appointed. The case was heard on that date but even then, as I indicated earlier, it was ill presented. Inter alia no steps had been taken to arrange for the attendance of the solicitor said to probably have been responsible for the omission to effect a request for reference in time.

  1. If skill and diligence on the part of an Applicant or its representative - particularly its legal representative - was a prerequisite to the granting of such an application as was made, the application should probably have been refused. But once the confusion was dispelled the problem proved to be not so simple.
  2. In favour of the Applicant's cause the overwhelming circumstance is that the objection of 1 September 1987 has not been disallowed as required by the Act, whatever might have been the intention of the Commissioner's officers. On my calculations it was not out of time and it was certainly not contended before me that, in the absence of a grant of leave from the Tribunal, it would be out of time. Although referred to on one occasion by the Commissioner in documentation as only stating "additional grounds", on the whole it was clearly presented as an objection, even if as a further objection. If it were to be argued that it was not an objection; or that it could not be an objection because any right of objection had been exhausted with the first objection, I would still consider those circumstances as favouring an extension of time. On the other hand, if the first objection has been abandoned and the second objection remains undetermined, there is in fact no need for any exercise of power by this Tribunal. The appropriate course would simply be for the Commissioner to consider the second objection and then to disallow it, if he considered that course appropriate. Then the Applicant could exercise its rights to seek a review before this -Tribunal, or to proceed by way of appeal to the Federal Court should it wish to do so. In that event it may be that there is no such "fraud or evasion" as would have allowed the assessment to be raised: a question which may put in issue the interpretation of provisions such as S.12B of the Sales Tax Procedure Act and which may present difficulties not ordinarily addressed in relation to the Income Tax Assessment Act, 1936 ("the Act"). But if that is to become an issue the Applicant will still need to seek relief on grounds not presently referred to in either objection.

20. The conclusions I have reached are that, despite the fact that there is much to be criticised in the approach of the applicant in this problem, the applicant should not be denied an independent review of any relevant determination of the Commissioner. That can be achieved by granting an extension of time for requesting a reference for independent review of the Commissioner's decision upon the first objection and, should it be considered as only part of the first objection, of the second objection. At the other end of the scale, it is unnecessary that this Tribunal should do anything at all. In those circumstances I will authorise any necessary extension of time.

21. In the interests of expedition, efficiency and economy I propose to reserve Monday 22 November 1989 as the date for the hearing of all such disputes between the Applicant and the Commissioner as shall then be properly brought before the Tribunal. If, prior to that date, it pleases the Commissioner to give formal notice disallowing the second objection and the Applicant then requests an independent review before this Tribunal and pays another fee (now $300), and in turn the Commissioner transmits all necessary documents to enable the matter to be properly brought forward and determined, the determination so sought shall then be made.

22. With a view to ensuring the due progress of the matter and, in particular, the definition of the issues to be argued, I shall cause to issue with these reasons for decision a notice of directions hearing.
