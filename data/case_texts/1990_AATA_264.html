Re Taxation Appeals [1990] AATA 264; 21 ATR 3786 (2 November 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. VT88/177

AAT No. 6341

Taxation - Practice and Procedure

[1990] AATA 264; 21 ATR 3786

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

I.R. Thompson(1) (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - taxpayer a body corporate - income received by taxpayer as trustee - taxpayer assessed under section 99 of Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - objection disallowed - reference to AAT - taxpayer then dissolved - new trustee appointed on day of hearing - whether the deemed application can be continued by new trustee - liability of taxpayer as trustee not passing to new trustee - new trustee cannot continue application

Practice and Procedure - after application applicant company dissolved - cannot continue application - whether application can be dismissed under section 42A(2) of Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 - no failure by party to attend as applicant - no longer a party - Case U78 not followed - application removed from hearing list

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 95, 99, 189, 254

Hardoon v Belilios (1901) AC 118

Re Johnson (1880) 15 Ch D 548

Octavo Investments Proprietary Limited v Knight [1979] HCA 61; (1979) 144 CLR 360

Xebec Pty Ltd (in liq) v Enthe Pty Ltd (1987) 18 ATR 893 Case U78 87 ATC 464

HEARING

MELBOURNE

2:11:1990

ORDER

Whereas the taxpayer in these proceedings was a body corporate and whereas since the respondent's objection decisions were referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal at the request of the taxpayer, the taxpayer has been dissolved;

It is directed that the said referrals, being deemed to be applications to the Tribunal for review of the objection decisions, are to be removed from the hearing list.

DECISION

The taxpayer at whose request the objection decisions which are the subject of these proceedings were referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal was a company originally incorporated under the relevant provisions of the Companies Act 1961 (Victoria). It was the trustee of the X Trust. In August 1987 notices of assessment were issued in respect of its income tax liability as such trustee for each of the 1980, 1981, 1982 and 1983 taxation years. In September 1987 it lodged a notice of objection pursuant to section 185 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") in respect of all those assessments. In December 1987 it was notified by the respondent that all the objections had been rejected. In January 1988 it lodged with the respondent a written request in respect of each of the objection decisions for each to be referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal. In March 1988 the respondent referred them to the Tribunal in accordance with section 189 of the Act. At that time the taxpayer was still in existence. Subsequently, however, it was deregistered pursuant to section 459(4) of the Companies (Victoria) Code and thereby was dissolved.

2. The deed of trust of the X Trust authorized the appointor to appoint a new trustee. He did not do so immediately but on the day on which the review of the referred objection decisions was listed to commence he appointed another company to be trustee. The respondent says that the referral of the four objection decisions, deemed by virtue of section 189(2) of the Act to be applications to the Tribunal for review of those objection decisions, lapsed when the taxpayer ceased to exist. The new trustee, on the other hand, says that the application has not lapsed and that the new trustee has standing to pursue the deemed applications and to have them heard and determined by the Tribunal. The Tribunal decided that it would hear argument on that question as a preliminary issue and the hearing was adjourned for that purpose. When it was resumed Mr J. W. de Wijn, of Counsel, represented the new trustee; the respondent was represented by Mr R. Kendall, of Counsel. No evidence was given orally but some documentary evidence was presented, including the deed of trust, a certificate of incorporation of the taxpayer on the change of its name and a copy of the minutes of an extraordinary general meeting of shareholders of the taxpayer held on 31 May 1985. Also tendered was an affidavit by the directors of the new trustee, who are shown in the minutes of the extraordinary general meeting of the taxpayer as having been its shareholders also. Exhibited to the affidavit was a copy of a judgment entered against the taxpayer by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in May 1989.

3. After the hearing was completed the Australian Government Solicitor, as the respondent's solicitor, sent to the Tribunal a letter accompanied by an affidavit sworn by an officer of the Australian Taxation Office. The new trustee's solicitors informed the Tribunal that they had not been informed of the Australian Government Solicitor's intention to write that letter and informed the Tribunal in writing that they did not propose to respond to the substance of the affidavit as it had become clear that the preliminary point was one of principle without reference to the precise facts of the case. The affidavit related to the steps taken by the Corporate Affairs Commission of Victoria which resulted in the taxpayer being deregistered pursuant to section 459(4) of the Companies (Victoria) Code. Its contents add nothing which is of significance at this stage of the proceedings to the facts which were agreed at the hearing and which were supported by the documentary evidence lodged at the hearing. I instructed, therefore, that the affidavit be returned to the respondent. I have not taken it into account in reaching my decision on the question which was argued as a preliminary issue.

4. The assessments which are the subject of the objection decisions were made pursuant to section 99 of the Act. Sub-sections (2), (3), (4) and (5) all provide that in the circumstances referred to in them "the trustee shall be assessed and is liable to pay tax on . . . the net income of the trust estate as if it were the income of an individual . . .". The section expressly imposes upon the trustee the liability to pay tax assessed under it. However, as Mr de Wijn pointed out, the trustee is entitled under the general law relating to trusts to be indemnified by the beneficiaries against obligations attaching to the performance of his office as trustee (Hardoon v Belilios (1901( AC 118). Such a right to indemnity can be expressly excluded but in the case of the X Trust the deed of trust expressly provides for such indemnity. Mr de Wijn drew attention also to the definition of "net income" in section 95(1) of the Act; it is stated to mean "the total assessable income of the trust estate calculated under this Act as if the trustee were a taxpayer in respect of that income and . . . ". He submitted, therefore, that, although section 99 required the trustee to be assessed and imposed on him liability to pay the tax so assessed, it was really the trust estate which bore that liability. Consequently, he submitted, the new trustee could take the place of the taxpayer in pursuing the deemed applications for review of the objection decisions.

5. Mr Kendall referred the Tribunal to section 96 of the Act which provides that a trustee is not liable as such to pay income tax on the income of the trust estate except as provided in the Act and referred also to the express provisions of section 99 which made the taxpayer in this case liable. He referred also to paragraphs (d) and (e) of section 254(1) which require a trustee to retain out of trust moneys so much as is sufficient to pay the tax which will become due in respect of the trust income and make him personally liable to pay the amount that he has retained or should have retained for that purpose. That, however, does not affect the right of the trustee to be indemnified by the beneficiaries.

6. In the case of Re Johnson (1880) 15 Ch D 548 at 552 Jessel M.R. said of the right of creditors to proceed against a trust estate:-

". . . I understand the doctrine to be this,

that where a trustee is authorized by a

testator, or by a settlor - for it makes

no difference - to carry on a business

with certain funds which he gives to the

trustee for that purpose, the creditor

who trusts the executor has a right to

say, 'I had the personal liability of

the man I trusted, and I have also a

right to be put in his place against the

assets; that is, I have a right to the

benefit of indemnity or lien which he

has against the assets devoted to the

purposes of the trade'. The first right

is his general right by contract,

because he trusted the trustee or

executor: he has a personal right to

sue him and to get judgment and make him

a bankrupt. The second right is a mere

corollary to those numerous cases in

Equity in which persons are allowed to

follow trust assets. The trust assets

having been devoted to carrying on the

trade, it would not be right that the

cestui que trust should get the benefit

of the trade without paying the

liabilities; therefore the Court says to

him, You shall not set up a trustee who

may be a man of straw, and make him a

bankrupt to avoid the responsibility of

the assets for carrying on the trade:

the Court puts the creditor, so to

speak, as I understand it, in the place

of the trustee."

7. Similarly, in Octavo Investments Proprietary Limited v Knight [1979] HCA 61; (1979) 144 CLR 360 at 371 Stephen, Mason, Aickin and Wilson JJ. said:-

". . . Once it is recognized that a trustee

may enjoy a right of indemnity over

trust property in respect of liabilities

incurred by him in the administration of

the trust, it follows that the creditors

of a trust business may have resort to

the assets of the trust to the extent of

the liabilities incurred by the

trustee."

8. However, those were cases in which the trustee remained in existence; so that his right of indemnity against the trust assets also remained in existence and the creditors could enforce that right by subrogation. Certainly the right to indemnity has not passed to the new trustee (Xebec Pty Ltd (in liq) v Enthe Pty Ltd (1987) 18 ATR 893 at 898). Xebec was concerned with the liability of a trustee to tax under section 98 of the Act, which like section 99 provides for the trustee to be assessed and to be liable to pay tax. It was held that the trustee's right to indemnity arose at the time at which the liability to tax was incurred, that is to say when the trustee was assessed. In the present case the taxpayer was in existence at that time and was trustee of X Trust; the new trustee was not appointed until this year and is not entitled to the indemnity. The respondent cannot, therefore, have resort to the assets of the trust by way of subrogation to any right of the new trustee.

9. The respondent obtained judgment against the taxpayer in May 1989. There is no evidence that he took any steps at any time to have the taxpayer enforce his right of indemnity against the trust property or to do so himself by subrogation. Neither party has applied for reinstatement of the taxpayer to the register under the provisions of the Companies (Victoria) Code.

At present, therefore, there is no person, as far as I can ascertain, to whom the right of indemnity attaches and by subrogation to whose rights the respondent can proceed against the trust property which is now vested in the new trustee. The new trustee undoubtedly has no personal liability to pay the tax. If the individual beneficiaries have any liability to do so, that does not give the respondent a right to proceed directly against the trust property. In those circumstances I have come to the conclusion that the fact that the taxpayer incurred its liability to pay the tax as trustee of the X Trust has not prevented its right of indemnity from having become unenforceable, in the same way as any other rights it had have become unenforceable, because of its dissolution, and I find accordingly.

10. The Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 does not contain any express provisions for dealing with an application for review where the applicant has ceased to exist. However, in various sections, e.g. sections 32 and 34 there are references to "a party" or "the parties" and to their taking part in conferences and the hearing of the application. Section 39 requires the Tribunal to ensure that every party is given a reasonable opportunity to present his case. Section 40(1)(b) empowers the Tribunal to proceed "in the absence of a party" in the circumstances specified there. Where the applicant has ceased to exist, there is no longer an applicant. He is no longer a party to the proceedings. Although in Case U78 87 ATC 464 the Tribunal, constituted by a Senior Member, a Member and myself, dismissed an application under section 42A of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 where the applicant had ceased to exist, that decision was made without the Tribunal having had the benefit of any argument as to its power to make it. I am now of the view that, once an applicant has ceased to be a party to a proceeding, it cannot be said that "a party" has failed to appear at a preliminary conference or a hearing so as to enliven the Tribunal's power to dismiss the application under section 42A(2). All the Tribunal can properly do is to remove the application (or in this case the deemed applications) from the hearing list on the basis that there is no longer any applicant to pursue it. I am satisfied that no other person can pursue it in place of the applicant and also that, as the applicant no longer exists, no person can represent the applicant and continue the proceedings on the applicant's behalf.

11. Mr de Wijn informed the Tribunal that, if the application could not be proceeded with by the new trustee, the applicant might apply to have the taxpayer reinstated to the register. If the taxpayer is reinstated, a new situation will exist and it will be necessary for the parties and the Tribunal to consider it.
