Anil Gupta and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales [1994] AATA 261; (1994) 94 ATC 2113 (1994) 29 ATR 1092 (1 September 1994)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

ANIL GUPTA v TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF NEW SOUTH WALES

No. A94/38

AAT No. 9710

Number of pages - 14

Tax Agent

(1994) 94 ATC 2113

(1994) 29 ATR 1092

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

G ETTINGER (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Tax Agent- application for tax agent registration - academically qualified - whether applicant engaged in relevant employment for requisite period - whether substantial involvement in a broad range of income tax returns

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 251JA(1), 251QA, 251BC(1)

Income Tax Regulations 1936 Regulation 156

Re Webb and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland (1992) 92 ATC 2101

Re Ellis and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland (1991) 92 ATC 2001

Re Egulian and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales (1991) 91 ATC 2060

Re Civiti and Tax Agents' Board of Victoria (1990) 90 ATC 2039

King and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales (Decision No. 9408,
unreported, March 1994)

Re D'Alessandro and Tax Agents' Board (1993) 93 ATC 2028

Re Chenouda and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales (1991) 91 ATC 2027

HEARING

CANBERRA, 14 July 1994

1:9:1994

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr D Sloan (Solicitor)

Solicitor for the Applicant: Meyer Boettcher and Clapham

Counsel for the Respondent: Ms L McCallum

Solicitor for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

G ETTINGER This is an application under Section 251QA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") for review of a decision of the Tax Agents Board of New South Wales ("the Board") dated 28 February 1994 (T19), refusing the applicant registration as a tax agent. The decision was made pursuant to Section 251JA of the Act and Regulation 156 of the Income Tax Regulations 1936 ("the Regulations"). The Board refused to grant the applicant registration as a tax agent because it was not satisfied that her relevant work experience qualified as "relevant employment" in that she had not demonstrated a substantial involvement in a broad range of income tax returns as required by Income Tax Regulation 156.

2. The applicant was represented by Mr Damian Sloan of Meyer Boettcher and Clapham and the respondent by Ms Lucy McCallum of counsel.

3. The Tribunal accepted into evidence documents and supplementary documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Act 1975 (T1-T19 and ST1-ST5) as Exhibit R1 and a statement of Dieter Slavik dated 12 July 1994 as Exhibit A1. Oral evidence was given by the applicant.

ISSUES BEFORE THE TRIBUNAL

4. The parties having agreed that the applicant's formal education satisfied the requirements for registration, the issue before this Tribunal was whether the applicant's employment experience satisfied the requirements of the prescribed qualifications under Regulation 156 of the Regulations. The issue could therefore be described as follows:

(a) whether the applicant's part-time employment satisfies the

requirements of Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii) of the Regulations; and

(b) whether the applicant's employment constituted "relevant

employment" as prescribed under Regulation 156(2) of the

Regulations.

EVIDENCE BEFORE THE TRIBUNAL

5. The applicant told the Tribunal that she graduated from the University of Manchester with a Bachelor of Arts in Economics and Social Studies on 7 July 1966 (T13). She was then admitted as an Associate to the following institutions:

(a) The London Chartered Institute of Cost and Management Accountants

on 20 January 1984 (T12)

(b) The Institute of Data Processing Management (T6)

(c) The Australian Society of Certified Practising Accountants on 14

December 1988 (T10)

6. The applicant told the Tribunal that she worked as a lecturer in the following tertiary institutions:

(a) Lecturer in commerce at the University of Malawi from 1973 to

November 1975 (T6)

(b) Part-time lecturer in accounting at the University of Papua New

Guinea (PNG) from July 1977 to November 1977 (T6)

(c) Lecturer in commerce at the University of PNG from September 1985

to August 1986 (T6)

(d) The applicant also worked part-time as a tutor in accounting at

the College of Advanced Education in Canberra from March 1989 to

November 1989 (T6)

(e) The applicant also worked as assistant accountant and budgets

officer at the University of PNG from November 1977 to November

1983 (T6)

7. From July 1990, to the date of this hearing, the applicant has been employed part-time by H and R Block as a tax preparer (T17). However, on the evidence given by the applicant to the Tribunal, during the annual tax season of July to October the applicant worked on a full time basis. The applicant estimated the total amount of time worked over the remaining eight months of each year would amount to the equivalent of two to three months of full time work.

8. The major component of the applicant's tax work at H and R Block consisted of the preparation of individual tax returns. The applicant's work can be summarised in the following table. This table is based on the figures listed in Mrs Gupta's application to the Board (T17). In the table below, the reference to a year refers to the financial year ending 30 June, except for the year 1993 when the figures are to 31 October (T1 and T17).

Trust Returns

9. Mrs Gupta estimated in her testimony that of the 1757 individual returns, approximately 25% were for small business' and involved the preparation of manufacturing accounts, trading accounts and balance sheets. They required specialist knowledge. Mrs Gupta submitted that at least three of the company returns were not straight forward.

10. The applicant told the Tribunal that the advice she gave to the small business clients was limited to advice on the preparation of returns, the benefits of incorporation and the valuation of stock under tax legislation. She worked as a tax preparer, not a tax adviser.

11. Financial advice of a similar nature was given by the applicant in relation to the forty-five partnership returns prepared by her. The applicant told the Tribunal that the advice she gave in regard to individual and partnership returns was not in writing; it was discussed at client conferences.

12. The eight company returns prepared by the applicant, concerned private companies. The applicant told the Tribunal that each of the eight companies were shelf companies with two share holders. The applicant contended that three of the eight company returns were not straight forward returns. They involved the preparation of the client's accounts from manufacturing, trading accounts and balance sheets. They could not have been prepared using a "tax pack". However the applicant agreed with counsel for the respondent that the complexities associated with these company returns were not outside the usual realm of complexity for company returns.

13. The five trust returns prepared by the applicant consisted of three single family unit trusts and two deceased estate trusts. The preparation of the family trusts involved the preparation of trading and profit and loss accounts. Upon cross-examination the applicant agreed that the two deceased estate trust returns involved matters of little complexity.

14. The applicant told the Tribunal that she had not prepared any superannuation or fringe benefit returns. Mrs Gupta said the clients of H and R Block had not required the preparation of such returns (T2). Mrs Gupta submitted that she had successfully completed the tax unit of the Certified Practising Accountants Programme which included the preparation of superannuation and fringe benefit returns and that she has kept her knowledge in this area up to date.

15. The applicant told the Tribunal that she had not prepared any objections to assessments issued in respect of income tax returns as no clients in the practice where she was employed required assistance in that area. THE LEGISLATION

16. Part VIIA of the Act deals with the registration of tax agents. The requirements for registration are found in section 251JA(1) of the Act. In relation to this matter, Section 251JA(1)(a)(i) provides as relevant:

Section 251JA(1):

The Board shall register the applicant as a tax agent if the applicant

satisfies the Board that:

(a) if the applicant is a natural person:

(i) the applicant is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax

returns and transact business on behalf of tax payers in

income tax matters;...

17. The meaning of "a fit and proper person" is found in section 251BC of the Act which provides as relevant:

Section 251BC(1):

Without limiting the generality of an expression used in this Part, but

subject to this section, a person is not a fit and proper person to

prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of

taxpayers in income tax matters, as at a particular time, if:

(a) ...

(b) both of the following conditions are satisfied:

(i) the person was not registered as a tax agent, or as a nominee,

for the purposes of this Part immediately before the

commencement of section 39 of the Taxation Laws Amendment Act

(No. 2) 1988;

(ii) the person does not hold such qualifications (whether

academic, by way of experience or otherwise) as are

prescribed;

(c)... (f)

18. The "qualifications" referred to in section 251BC(1)(b)(ii) are prescribed under Regulation 156 of the Regulations as follows:

Regulation 156(1):

For the purposes of subparagraph 251BC(1)(b)(ii) of the Act, the

following qualifications are prescribed:

(a) the person:

(i) shall have completed the academic requirements for the award

of a degree, diploma or other qualification from an Australian

university, college of advanced education or other tertiary

institution of an equivalent standard, and have passed

examinations in such subjects, under whatever name, which an

appropriate authority of the university, college or advanced

education or other tertiary institution certifies to the Board

to represent a course of study in accountancy of not less than

3 years' duration and in commercial law of not less than 18

months' duration or shall possess such other qualifications as

the Board regards as equivalent to those qualifications;

(ii) shall have:

(A) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for

not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding 5 years;

(B) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent that

the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in

sub-subparagraph (A); or

(C) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as the

Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant

employment as referred to in sub-subparagraph (A);and

(i) shall have, by written examination, successfully completed

a course of study in Australian income tax law acceptable

to the Board;

(b) ... (d)

19. The meaning of "relevant employment" is found in Regulation 156(2) of the Regulations which provides that:

Regulation 156(2)

"...relevant employment" means employment by a person or a

partnership or as a member of a partnership in the course of which

there has been substantial involvement in income tax matters

including:

(a) the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax
returns;

(b) the preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued in re

of such returns; and

(c) the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns, assessments
o

objections.

SUBMISSIONS AND CONCLUSIONS

20. The Tribunal's consideration of the legislative requirements outlined above in relation to the applicant follow.

Academic Qualifications

21. The applicants academic qualifications were not in issue before the Tribunal (T4). The Tax Agents Board of NSW states that the applicant "...holds a degree in Economics from Manchester University and is an associate member of the ASCPA...the Board considers that Mrs Gupta possesses the academic requirements for registration..." (T3 and T4) The Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant meets the academic requirements as specified in Regulation 156(1)(a)(i). The Tribunal is also satisfied that the applicant meets the academic requirements of Regulation 156(1)(a)(iii), (T6).

Employment

22. The Tribunal will address the required period of employment under the legislation, before examining the definition of "relevant employment". Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii) is divided into three sub-paragraphs (A), (B) and (C). There were no submissions made by Mr Sloan as to which sub-paragraph applied to Mrs Gupta. The respondent submitted that the applicant had been unable to refer to any employment, prior to the preceding five years, which could be regarded as equivalent employment under Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii)(B) or (C).

23. The Tribunal notes that prior to her employment at H and R Block, Mrs Gupta's employed work experience did not include the preparation of tax returns or the giving of advice in relation to tax returns. The Tribunal has therefore decided to assess the applicant's "relevant employment" according to Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii)(A). Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii)(A) requires the applicant to be engaged in "relevant employment" on a full-time basis for not less than twelve months in total over the preceding five years.

24. The applicant told the Tribunal that she began as an employee at H and R Block in July 1990 and remained employed by this firm at the date of the application and was still employed at the date of the hearing. During the annual four month tax season of July to October the applicant said she worked on a full-time basis with H and R Block. For the remaining eight months of each year the applicant was employed part-time. The applicant estimated that the total amount of time worked over this eight month period would approximate to two to three months full time work. The respondent did not dispute this estimation.

25. In order to calculate the preceding relevant number of years, the Tribunal must first consider whether the relevant date from which to calculate is the date of the application or the date of the hearing. This question has been previously considered by the Tribunal in the cases of Re Webb and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland (1992) 92 ATC 2101, Re Ellis and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland (1991) 92 ATC 2001, Re Egulian and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales (1991) 91 ATC 2060.

26. In the applicant's case it appears that the requisite twelve month period of relevant full-time employment is satisfied whether the date of the application or the date of the hearing is chosen as the date from which to calculate. In either calculation Mrs Gupta appears to have been employed in excess of twelve months over the previous five years. The Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant meets the requirements of Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii)(A).

"Relevant Employment"

27. "Relevant employment" as defined by Regulation 156(2) of the Regulations, requires a "...substantial involvement in income tax matters...". These matters are then outlined in three sub-paragraphs (a), (b) and (c). Mr Sloan submitted that the words "substantial involvement in income tax matters including:..." suggested factors other than those of paragraphs (a) to (c) could be taken into account. Ms McCallum submitted that the correct approach "...was simply a question of running down the checklist of Regulation 156(2)....".

28. The Tribunal concurs with Deputy President Forrest in Re Civiti and Tax Agent's Board of Victoria (1990) 90 ATC 2039 at page 2042, where he stated that the requirements of the sub-paragraphs in Regulation 156(2) are cumulative. The Tribunal adopts Deputy President Forrest's view that all sub- paragraphs must be satisfied, that is, sub-paragraph (a) must be satisfied before looking to sub-paragraphs (b), then (c).

Regulation 156(2)(a)

29. Regulation 156(2)(a) requires a "substantial involvement" in "the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns". Mr Sloan submitted that the respondent had applied a strict and literal interpretation to this Regulation in respect of income tax returns prepared by the applicant. The respondent had, he argued, confined itself to a numerical assessment of the returns prepared by Mrs Gupta. According to Mr Sloan the respondent had equated the words "substantial" and "broad" with the term "all encompassing." Mr Sloan submitted that the applicant's lack of involvement in the preparation or examination of superannuation or fringe benefit returns should not be used as a primary reason for refusing her application. Mr Sloan referred the Tribunal to the case of Re Civiti (supra) at page 2043 where the meaning of the word "broad" in Regulation 156(2) was described by Deputy President Forrest as meaning:

"...a word of plain meaning and common understanding. It has a similar

meaning to 'wide'."

30. Mr Sloan also referred the Tribunal to the case of King and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales (Decision No 9408, unreported, 16 March 1994) at page 6 where Senior Member Allen stated:

"The sub-regulation does, however, call for substantial involvement

i income tax matters and involvement in a broad range. Note: does

not call for involvement in all categories of returns.">

31. Ms McCallum, counsel for the respondent, rejected Mr Sloan's submission that the Board had interpreted Regulation 156(2) as requiring experience in all categories of returns. Ms McCallum submitted that the Board required experience in a broad, as opposed to a narrow, range of income tax returns. According to the respondent, the range of Mrs Gupta's experience in the preparation of tax returns was narrow, consisting predominantly of individual and partnership returns. Ms McCallum submitted that the eight company, and five trust returns prepared by the applicant, were not outside the usual realm of complexity for such returns. The applicant's experience in preparing these returns was therefore to be regarded as "quite minimal". In support of this contention, the Tribunal was referred by the respondent to the cases of Re Webb (supra) and Re D'Alessandro and Tax Agent's Board (1993) 93 ATC 2028. The applicants in these cases had experience predominantly in the areas of individual and partnership returns, she submitted. This Tribunal notes that in both those cases the findings had been that the applicants had insufficient involvement in the preparation or examination of a "broad range" of income tax returns.

32. The meaning of "substantial involvement" and "broad", referred to in Regulation 156(2), has been considered by the Tribunal in several cases. In Re Civiti (supra) at page 2043, Deputy President Forrest stated:

"The word 'broad' is not one of similar meaning to 'substantial'.

In the context in which they are used, both terms are used as

qualifying adjectives. 'Broad range does not mean the same

thing as 'substantial involvement' and vice versa. There may

be one without the other. The respondent has to be satisfied the

applicant has acquired both in order to have been engaged in relevant

employment within the meaning of reg. 156.

Sub-regulation 156(2) cannot, in my opinion, be construed as

applying to a particular category of returns. It is not

the experience within a category of returns but rather the scope

of the returns which is the test to be applied.

It is not a relevant consideration that reg. 156 does not

specify the categories of returns in defining 'relevant

employment'. It is explicit in the language used, that

wide-ranging experience is required. A lack of experience in the

areas of returns referred to, in my opinion, precludes

satisfaction of the test of relevant employment."

33. The "..lack of experience in the areas of returns referred to..." in Civiti's case (supra) were company, trust and superannuation returns. In cross-examination, the applicant told the Tribunal that the eight company and five trust returns she had prepared were not outside the usual realm of complexity for such returns. These areas have been considered, not by Deputy President Forrest alone, as forming part of the broad range of returns required by Regulation 156(2) of the Regulations. In the case of Re Egulian (supra) Deputy President McMahon stated at page 2064:

"...superannuation, fringe benefits or trust returns...form part of

the broad range referred to in the regulation."

34. The Tribunal notes that Mrs Gupta has had no involvement in the preparation or examination of superannuation or fringe benefit returns. These returns have been recognised by the Tribunal as forming part of the broad range of income tax returns required by the regulation (Re Egulian (supra), Re Civiti (supra) ). The Tribunal agrees with Deputy President McMahon's explanation of "substantial involvement" in Re Egulian (supra):

"Substantial involvement necessarily implies involvement to a

substantial extent. To be substantially involved in one,

or two or three returns, is not what is required by the

regulation. What is required is involvement in a substantial

number of returns which represent a broad range."

35. The Tribunal is satisfied that the "broad range" referred to in Regulation 156(2), refers to both the range and the number of returns prepared or examined. It notes that the applicant prepared a substantial number of individual and partnership returns. However the Tribunal is not satisfied that Mrs Gupta's involvement in company, trusts, superannuation, and fringe benefits returns comprised the "substantial involvement" required by Regulation 156(2) of the Regulations.

36. The Tribunal acknowledges and commends Mrs Gupta's academic achievements, her regular updating of her tax studies, and her theoretical knowledge of problems that might arise in relation to returns where she has had no "substantial involvement". However these qualities do not satisfy the requirements of Regulation 156(2)(a). The Tribunal is not satisfied that the applicant had substantial involvement in a broad range of income tax returns. She therefore does not meet the requirements of Regulation 156(2)(a).

37. The Tribunal will now consider whether the applicant satisfies the requirements of Regulation 156(2) paragraphs (b) and (c).

Regulation 156(2)(b)

38. Regulation 156(2)(b) requires a "substantial involvement" in "the preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued in respect of a broad range of income tax returns." The applicant told the Tribunal that she had not prepared any objections to income tax assessments. However, Mrs Gupta said she had "seen" objections prepared by other staff officers.

39. Ms McCallum submitted that this did not amount to an "examination" as referred to in Regulation 156(2)(b). The Tribunal agrees with the reasoning of Ms McCallum that the term "examination" is a specific term:

"...referring to examination in the sense in which the tax office examines

objections to determine them, but merely to look at them and read

assessments prepared by other people would not...meet that

requirement..."

40. The Tribunal finds that the applicant fails to fulfil the requirements of Regulation 156(2)(b).

Regulation 156(2)(c)

41. Regulation 156(2)(c) requires a "substantial involvement" in "the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns, assessments or objections." Mrs Gupta told the Tribunal that she had given advice in relation to business and partnership returns. This advice had not been prepared in writing and appeared to be advice confined to matters ancillary to the preparation of a tax return.

42. Ms McCallum acknowledged that in the course of preparing income tax returns, Mrs Gupta had provided advice to clients in relation to their returns. However, Ms McCallum submitted that the applicant had no involvement in the provision of advice in relation to assessments or objections.

43. The Tribunal finds no evidence to suggest the applicant provided advice in relation to objections or assessments to income tax returns. The Tribunal finds that Mrs Gupta had no "substantial involvement" in the provision of advice required by Regulation 156(2) of the Regulations.

44. Mr Sloan referred the Tribunal to a passage from the case of King (supra) at page 7 where Senior Member Allen stated:

"I concede the applicant's involvement in the latter aspects

(the preparation of objections to assessments and the

giving of advice) is not of itself substantial but that

is not (what) the regulation requires. What I am satisfied

of is that he has sufficient experience now in Australian

tax law and those aspects that with his background he can

be entrusted to undertake same."

45. The Tribunal notes that the above finding appears to contradict the decisions of Re Chenouda and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales (1991) 91 ATC 2027 and Civiti (supra) which both discussed the requirements of "substantial" under Regulation 156(2). This Tribunal would seek to distinguish the facts in King's case (supra). Senior Member Allen was satisfied that Mr King's experience prior to the five years preceding the date of his hearing or the date of his application to the Board, was equivalent to being engaged in "relevant employment" as referred to in Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii)(C). In other words, Senior Member Allen was judging Mr King's employment experience against the criteria of Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii)(C) rather than 156(1)(a)(ii)(A). The Tribunal notes that Mrs Gupta had been unable to refer to any employment, prior to the preceding five years before her application to the Board or the date of hearing, which could be regarded as equivalent employment under Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii)(B) or (C).

46. The Tribunal acknowledges the academic and vocational competency and ability of the applicant. Mrs Gupta affirmed a question asked by the Tribunal that she would be considered a senior staff member at H and R Block. The Tribunal notes the statement of D Slavik, District Manager of H and R Block, (A1), in which he stated that "... I believe that with her experience and knowledge, Anil (Gupta) is able to carry out the most complicated tax work in any category of return." However, the legislation requires specific employment experience. Deputy President Forrest discussed this at pages 2042 and 2043 in the case of Re Civiti (supra):

"The substantial amendments to the Act introduced by the Taxation

Laws Amendment Act (No.2) No. 78 of 1988, which came into force

from 1 November 1988, reflect the objective that persons who

prepare income tax returns or transact business in income tax

matters for reward have appropriate knowledge of income tax law and

practice. The respondent (the Tax Agent's Board of Victoria) has

the responsibility of assuring that objective. In the present

circumstanstances it must be satified that the applicant

has had substantial involvement in income tax matters, including

the preparation or examination of a broad range of returns...

...once registration is granted, the agent is not limited in

the taxation work he or she may undertake... The respondent

has to ensure as best it can, that the person entrusted with

any type of return or income tax business is fit to undertake

the work."

47. The Tribunal finds that the applicant fails to fulfill the requirements of Regulation 156(2)(c).

48. In conclusion, the Tribunal is not satisfied that the applicant meets all the relevant requirements of the legislation. There is no disagreement between the parties, and the Tribunal accepts that the applicant satisfies the academic requirements for a tax agent. The Tribunal also finds that Mrs Gupta has been employed for the requisite twelve month period in the five years preceding her application, satisfying Regulation 156(1)(a)(ii) of the Regulations.

49. However, the Tribunal finds that the applicant fails to satisfy the requirements of Regulation 156(2) of the Regulations for the following reasons:

(a) The Tribunal finds that the applicant has not had "substantial

involvement" in "the preparation or examination of a broad range

of income tax returns." Mrs Gupta has not had experience in the

preparation or examination of superannuation or fringe benefit

returns. Nor has her involvement in the preparation of company

and trust returns satisfied the Tribunal as being the

"substantial involvement" required by Regulation 156(2) of the

Regulations.

(b) The applicant has had no "substantial involvement" in the

preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued

in respect of a broad range of income tax returns.

(c) The applicant has had no "substantial involvement" in "the

provision of advice in relation to income tax returns,

assessments or objections."

50. For these reasons the Tribunal affirms the decision under review.
