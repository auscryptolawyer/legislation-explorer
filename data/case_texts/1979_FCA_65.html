Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Smith (No 2) [1979] FCA 65; (1979) 38 FLR 115 (17 October 1979)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

FEDERAL COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION v. SMITH (No. 2) [1979] FCA 65; (1979) 38 FLR 115

Income Tax

COURT

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

GENERAL DIVISION

Bowen C.J.(1), Franki(2) and Toohey(3) JJ.

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Assessable income - Insurance moneys received on disability - Object and effect of payments - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.), ss. 25, 26(j).

Income Tax - Deductions - Premiums on policy of disability insurance - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth.), s. 51(1). Held, that payments to an insured under a policy of insurance constitute income according to ordinary concepts where the payments have the object and effect of providing a substitute or a replacement for earnings lost by the insured. Such payments would also form part of the assessable income of the insured under s. 26(j) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. Conversely, premiums paid in respect of such a policy are a deductible outgoing under s. 51 (1) of the Act.

Carapark Holdings Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1967] HCA 5; (1967), 115 CLR 653, and Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Dixon [1952] HCA 65; (1952), 86 CLR 540, considered.

Decision of the Supreme Court of Western Australia (Wickham J.) Smith v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1978), 9 ATR 389, reversed.

HEARING

Perth, 1979, May 8, 9; October 17. 17:10:1979

APPEAL

Appeal from the Supreme Court of Western Australia (Wickham J.).

The facts appear from the judgment of Toohey J.

E.M. Franklyn Q.C. and G.M. Calder, for the appellant.

B.W. Rowland Q.C. and R.K. O'Connor, for the respondent.

Cur. adv. vult.

Solicitor for the appellant: B. J. O'Donovan (Acting Commonwealth Crown

Solicitor).

Solicitors for the respondent: Stone James & Co.

J. H. KARKAR

DECISION

October 17.

The following written judgments were delivered.

BOWEN C.J. I have had the advantage of reading the reasons for judgment
prepared by Toohey J. I agree with those reasons. Accordingly, I would allow the appeal and the cross appeal. (at p114)

2. The question of costs before us and in the proceedings below raises some difficulty. The main costs before us would relate to the appeal. The commissioner has succeeded in his appeal. The taxpayer was awarded his costs in the Supreme Court but our allowance of the appeal and cross appeal negates the result in that court, which arose from the fact that the grounds relied upon by the taxpayer differed from the position put forward in his return. Nevertheless, the result of our allowance of the appeal and cross appeal will be to require an amendment of the assessment, so that it will accord with the taxpayer's return. In the circumstances, it might seem somewhat harsh if orders were made requiring the taxpayer to bear the main burden of costs. (at p116)

3. I would feel inclined to leave each party to bear its own costs of the proceedings before us and the Supreme Court. However, I would be reluctant to make orders to this effect if either party wished to advance argument before us for a different result on the question of costs. (at p116)

4. I propose the court now makes orders allowing the appeal and cross appeal, setting aside the judgment of the Supreme Court and remitting the matter to the commissioner to re-assess in accordance with the reasons for judgment of this Court. I further propose that the matter be stood over generally with liberty to either party to apply within twenty-one days from this date if he wishes to make any application for costs of the proceedings before the Supreme Court or before this Court. If any such application is made the matter will be re-listed for further consideration of the question of costs either at the next sittings of the court in Perth or, if the parties agree, the court will decide any application upon written submissions. (at p116)

FRANKI J. I agree with the orders proposed by the Chief Judge. I have had the benefit of reading the reasons for judgment prepared by Toohey J. I agree with those reasons but I wish to say something in addition. It is unnecessary for me to set out the facts. (at p116)

2. I have carefully considered what was said in Atlas Tiles Ltd. v. Briers [1978] HCA 37; (1978) 52 ALJR 707 and by Gibbs J. in Groves v. United Pacific Transport Pty. Ltd. (1965) Qd R 62 , but I do not find anything there which requires me to take any different view to that expressed by Toohey J. (at p116)

3. In relation to the cross appeal I note that the statement in Ronpibon Tin N.L. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47 , to which Toohey J. refers and which includes the word "expected", follows after a passage in the joint judgment which reads: "The words 'incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income' mean in the course of gaining or producing such income" (1949) 78 CLR, at pp 56-57 . This passage has been referred to on many occasions by the High Court. See for example Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Green [1950] HCA 20; (1950) 81 CLR 313, at p 317 and Charles Moore & Co. (W.A.) Pty. Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1956] HCA 77; (1956) 95 CLR 344, at p 349 . In this last-mentioned case the court was considering whether a taxpayer who carried on the business of a department store was entitled under s. 51(1) to deduct an amount representing money stolen when employees of it were carrying takings to the bank. The court held that this loss was one incurred in gaining or producing assessable income and was an allowable deduction. The members of the court, by way of dicta in a joint judgment, said: "But unfortunately it is still a familiar and recognised hazard and there could be little doubt that if it had been insured against the premium would have formed an allowable deduction" (1956) 95 CLR, at p 351 . Although the taxpayer in that case was carrying on a business that passage appears to indicate that the court did not give the word "expected" the same significance as did Wickham J. when he adopted the reasons he had given in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Chapkhana (1977) 77 ATC 4412 . In my opinion the relevant premium was paid in the course of gaining or producing assessable income. It was incidental and relevant to the derivation of income. (at p117)

4. I do not consider that the premium was an outgoing of a capital, private or domestic nature. The essential character of the premium was not of any such nature. (at p117)

5. As at present advised, had I come to the conclusion that the payments under the policy were not income within s. 25(1), I would have held that they were income within s. 26(j) which, so far as is relevant, provided that:

"The assessable income of a taxpayer shall include -

(j) any amount received by way of insurance or indemnity for

or in respect of any loss -

(ii) of . . . income which would have been assessable income,

if the loss had not occurred, and any amount so received for or in

respect of any loss or outgoing which is an allowable deduction." (at

p117)

6. I consider that the words "for or in respect of" coupled with the words "by way of" indicate that the words "insurance or indemnity" are not to be given a restricted meaning: see generally Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Wade [1951] HCA 66; (1951) 84 CLR 105, at pp 112, 115-116 . I consider that the payments under the policy were received by way of insurance or indemnity in respect of a loss of income which would have been assessable income if the loss had not occurred. (at p117)

TOOHEY J. The court heard an appeal by the commissioner against a decision of the Supreme Court of Western Australia (Wickham J.) and a cross appeal by the taxpayer against that decision. As will appear, the cross appeal only arises if the appeal itself is allowed. (at p117)

2. The taxpayer is a medical practitioner. On 19th October, 1977, while a senior resident medical officer at Sir Charles Gairdner Hospital, Perth, he was injured in a motor vehicle accident. He sustained several injuries including a comminuted fracture of the left leg, as a result of which he was unable to resume work until 16th February, 1978. (at p118)

3. Early in 1975, while a junior resident medical officer at the same hospital, the taxpayer had taken out an insurance policy with Australian Casualty Company Ltd. The period of insurance began on 26th February, 1975; the policy was renewable on 1st June, 1975. It was renewed on that date and annually thereafter and was current at the time of the taxpayer's accident. (at p118)

4. By the terms of the policy the insurer promised "to pay indemnity for disability covered by this policy and sustained by the insured resulting from injury or sickness". The monthly indemnity was expressed to be $600, payable when, as the result of injury or sickness, the insured sustained "total disability". That expression was given a particular definition in the policy which it is as well to set out in full: "'Total disability' wherever used in this policy means the inability of the insured by reason of injury or sickness to perform each and every gainful occupation for which he is reasonably suited by education, training or experience except that during the first two years of any such period of disability the insured shall be deemed to be totally disabled while he is both: (1) unable to perform each and every duty of his occupation; and (2) not engaged in any occupation or employment for wage or profit." (at p118)

5. It will be seen that the concept of total disability is geared to occupations for which the insured is reasonably suited, but that during the first two years of disability there must exist the additional factor that the insured is not engaged in any occupation or employment for wage or profit. (at p118)

6. Thus, after two years, the insured may continue to receive the monthly indemnity even though in some occupation or employment, so long as it is not one capable of description as a gainful occupation for which the insured "is reasonably suited by education, training or experience". (at p118)

7. The insurer accepted that by reason of the accident the taxpayer sustained total disability and it paid him in respect of the period from 19th October, 1977, until 16th February, 1978, save for the first thirty days, the policy prescribing a waiting period of that duration during which no indemnity was payable. (at p118)

8. In his income tax return for the year ended 30th June, 1978, the taxpayer listed as income in the category "Fees, commissions" the sum of $2,112, described as "A.C.C. disability insurance benefits". At the same time he claimed as a deduction the premium of $91 paid in that year, described as "loss of income insurances". Although the indemnity specified was $600 a month, the taxpayer was actually paid at the rate of $720 a month. In a letter written by the insurer to the taxpayer it was explained that his monthly benefit had been increased by twenty per cent "due to the favourable experience enjoyed by clients of Bongiorno & Company". The taxpayer had insured as part of a group of clients of Bongiorno & Company, a firm of accountants. (at p119)

9. In assessing the taxpayer, the commissioner accepted the inclusion in his assessable income of the sum of $2,112 but rejected the deduction of $91. An objection to the rejection was disallowed and the matter then came before the Supreme Court of Western Australia where Wickham J. upheld the disallowance but also upheld an argument the taxpayer was allowed to raise without objection that the sum of $2,112 was wrongly included as part of his assessable income. The commissioner then appealed to this Court against the exclusion of the monthly indemnity from the taxpayer's assessable income and the taxpayer countered with a cross appeal that if it be held that the indemnity payments constituted assessable income, it should also be held that the premium payment was a deduction. (at p119)

10. The commissioner's notice of appeal challenged, on a number of grounds, the conclusion reached by Wickham J. In argument, counsel for the commissioner developed two main propositions. The first was that the payments by way of monthly indemnity were in the nature of income according to ordinary concepts and so assessable under s. 25 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. Alternatively, they constituted amounts received by way of insurance or indemnity for or in respect of a loss of income which hence assessable under s. 26(j) of the Act. (at p119)

11. Wickham J. reached his conclusion in this way. The policy of insurance was not a contract of indemnity. It was a contract to pay specific sums upon the happening of specified events and was not related to actual pecuniary loss of revenue but only to disability or incapacity. Earning ability or capacity is a capital asset. The policy of insurance was an insurance against loss of an ability, the only apparent connexion between it and other income being the provision that benefits under the policy were to be reduced by any amounts paid under workers' compensation or similar legislation. The payments did not fall within s. 26(j); they were received, not in place or instead of loss of income, but for loss of an ability. (at p119)

12. I turn first to the question whether the money received by the taxpayer fell within s. 25 of the Act, that is whether it constituted income according to ordinary concepts. (at p119)

13. A useful starting point is a passage from the judgment of Kitto, Taylor and Owen JJ. in Carapark Holdings Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation: "In general, insurance moneys are to be considered as received on revenue account where the purpose of the insurance was to fill the place of a revenue receipt which the event insured against has prevented from arising, or of any outgoing which has been incurred on revenue account in consequence of the event insured against, whether as a legal liability or as a gratuitous payment actuated only by considerations of morality or expediency" [1967] HCA 5; (1967) 115 CLR 653, at p 663 . (at p120)

14. The inquiry as to purpose of which this judgment speaks is not, I think, the same as that undertaken in relation to s. 26(a) of the Act. The latter speaks of the purpose of acquisition and for that reason it becomes vital to focus upon the mind of the taxpayer. The terms of any document under which property was acquired may throw little, if any, light on that purpose. In my view, the purpose spoken of in Carapark Holdings Ltd.'s case is a more objective one in which the terms of any document by which the taxpayer receives money (in the present case, an insurance policy) may assume great significance: "The purpose which has to be considered in determining the nature of the receipt, however, is the purpose which in actual fact the insurance serves" (1967) 115 CLR, at p 664 . (at p120)

15. That is not to say that other circumstances may not be relevant. In the present case, for instance, the position of the taxpayer as an employed medical practitioner, his conditions of employment including provision for sick leave, and the sort of financial loss he might be expected to suffer as a result of a disabling accident, may all prove material to the inquiry whether the moneys paid constituted income. In that respect I differ from the view expressed by Wickham J. in Smith v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation that "this . . . is not a case where the characterization of the receipt requires consideration of the purpose for which the taxpayer entered into the contract of insurance which produced the amount" (1978) 9 ATR 389, at p 390 . (at p120)

16. But if by "purpose" his Honour meant rather the motives that led to the taking out of the insurance policy, I am in substantial agreement with him. The taxpayer's own evidence threw no great light on that matter. In evidence in chief he explained that he took out the policy because in the event of sickness he might well be left without a source of money to live on. In cross-examination he agreed that he saw the need to replace income he might lose because of a disability, adding: "When I take out a policy like this I am covering myself to provide money if the situation should occur whereby I cannot earn that income, or that amount of money." (at p120)

17. Wickham J. took the view that the insurance policy, notwithstanding its description, was not truly a contract of indemnity but rather one to pay specific sums upon the happening of specified events, the insurer's liability arising not by reason of actual pecuniary loss but only because of disability or incapacity to work. (at p120)

18. In the main I agree with what his Honour said regarding the insurance policy. But, with respect, that does not dispose of the matter. The taxpayer stood to lose financially in the event of injury or sickness because he might then be precluded from earning his salary as an employed medical practitioner in circumstances where his entitlement to sick leave would not provide adequate replacement and where no other source was available to compensate him for the earnings he lost. The purpose served by the insurance policy was to fill the place, if only in part, of those lost earnings and to do so by monthly payments. (at p121)

19. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Dixon [1952] HCA 65; (1952) 86 CLR 540 the court was concerned with the character of a sum provided by an employer to make up, during the war, the difference between the military pay of an employee who had enlisted and the pay he would have received in his civilian occupation. In the course of his judgment, Fullagar J. said: What is paid is not salary or remuneration, and it is not paid in respect of or in relation to any employment of the recipient. But it is intended to be, and is in fact, a substitute for - the equivalent pro tanto of - the salary or wages which would have been earned and paid if the enlistment had not taken place. As such, it must be income, even though it is paid voluntarily and there is not even a moral obligation to continue making the payments. It acquires the character of that for which it is substituted and that to which it is added" (1952) 86 CLR, at p 568 . (at p121)

20. In the present case the position cannot be put so strongly. Although it was clear enough from the taxpayer's evidence, including the proposal which led to the making of the contract of insurance, that the taxpayer sought to cover himself in respect of about two-thirds of his salary ("The sum insured for I thought would be sufficient to cover my commitments at the time"), the amount of monthly indemnity bore no necessary relation to the taxpayer's earnings. And since payments under the policy might continue for many years in the event of an accident producing serious disability, after two years the taxpayer would continue to receive payments even though he was earning an income, so long as it were not from work to which he was "reasonably suited" in terms of the policy. (at p121)

21. Nevertheless, a consideration of the terms of the policy and the circumstances in which it was taken out shows that what is to be paid is substantially to replace earnings lost by the taxpayer. The entitlement to regular monthly payments is important although not decisive (Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Dixon (1952) 86 CLR, at p 568 ). Borrowing and adopting the language of Fullagar J. in that case, what is decisive is that the object and effect of payments under the policy are to provide something in substitution for earnings lost by the insured. (at p121)

22. For the first two years of total disability it is necessary that the insured be not engaged in any occupation or employment for wage or profit. It is true that on the face of the policy the monthly indemnity need have no particular relationship to the earnings of the insured. But that does not detract from the general object and effect of the payments. And it is not without significance that one of the questions asked in the proposal for the policy was: "What portion of your average earnings does the disability indemnity under all policies you have or are applying for represent?" The proposal provided for an answer "under fifty per cent", "fifty per cent to seventy-five per cent" or "over seventy-five per cent". The taxpayer answered "fifty per cent to seventy-five per cent". (at p122)

23. It may be said that once a total disability period of two years has passed an insured may still continue to receive monthly payments (at least, under the present policy, in the case of accident) even though he may be earning as much as he would in his pre-accident occupation. That may be so but it would be wrong to see the insurance as "a mere gamble, having no purpose but the gaining of a windfall" (Carapark Holdings Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1967] HCA 5; (1967) 115 CLR 653 ). The probabilities are that a gainful occupation for which an insured is not reasonably suited by education, training or experience will not produce earnings as great as one for which he is so suited. (at p122)

24. In placing the emphasis on loss of earning capacity or ability, his Honour referred to Atlas Tiles Ltd. v. Briers [1978] HCA 37; (1978) 52 ALJR 707 to support the proposition that the earning capacity is a capital asset. That decision presents difficulties of analysis, partly because of the different views taken by the members of the court, and also because what was an action for damages in contract for wrongful dismissal led the court into a discussion of the concept of loss of earnings in actions in tort. But in any event, the judgments in that case do not throw much light upon the question which this Court has to resolve. To characterize earning capacity as a capital asset does not resolve the dilemma whether money has been received to fill the place of a revenue receipt. (at p122)

25. In Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Hatchett [1971] HCA 47; (1971) 125 CLR 494 Menzies J. was concerned with the deductibility under s. 51 of the Act of items of expenditure by an employed teacher. In the course of his judgment his Honour said: "There has been some suggestion that to equip the taxpayer's mind in order that he may have higher earning capacity is an affair of capital . . . This is, I think, a misunderstanding. It seems to me that it would be wrong to consider any of the expenditure here under consideration as an outgoing of capital or of a capital nature. An outlay is of that character when it is expended to obtain what can properly be described as capital in the economic sense. . . . In the field of taxation, as in the field of business, 'capital' is used in contrast with 'revenue'; it has no reference to a man's body, mind, or capacity" (1971) 125 CLR, at p 497 . (at p123)

26. In that sense, the monthly indemnity paid to the taxpayer in the present case was received on account of revenue. It was intended to compensate him (not necessarily in full) for earnings he might be expected to lose by reason of his injuries, those payments to continue so long as he was unable to carry out any occupation for which he was reasonably suited by education, training or experience. (at p123)

27. I do not think that any relevant distinction can be drawn in regard to the $120 a month the taxpayer received as a "bonus". Although a voluntary payment by the insurer, it arose from the policy and was intended to serve the same purpose as the money payable as a matter of contract under the policy. It was expressed by the insurer to be and was calculated as a twenty per cent increase on the "monthly benefit". It too was received on revenue account. (at p123)

28. These conclusions make it unnecessary for me to consider the arguments addressed in relation to s. 26(j) of the Act. But the view I have taken of the object and effect of payments under the policy seems to me to lead inevitably to the result that those payments were received by way of insurance or indemnity for or at any rate in respect of a loss of income which would have been assessable income. (at p123)

29. Having decided that the appeal by the commissioner should be upheld, the taxpayer's cross appeal arises for consideration. Since the taxpayer is an employed medical practitioner, the premium is an allowable deduction under s. 51(1) of the Act only if it comes within the description: "All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income . . . except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature." (at p123)

30. Wickham J. upheld the commissioner in this respect and he did so on two grounds. The first was that the payment of the premium was not incurred in gaining or producing assessable income because it was paid for insurance against a future risk. The second was that it was not incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income because the payments under the policy did not constitute income. In view of the conclusion I have reached regarding the assessability of the payments, the second objection no longer stands. (at p123)

31. There is one factual matter to be mentioned. The relevant tax year is that ended 30th June, 1978. The taxpayer's entitlement to be paid the monthly sum of $600 prescribed by the policy arose thirty days after his accident, that is thirty days after 19th October, 1977. The policy fell due for renewal on 1st June each year so that the payments made to the taxpayer were by reason of the renewal effected on 1st June, 1977. The premium claimed as a deduction was that paid on 1st June, 1978. However, the lack of a direct connexion between the particular premium claimed and the assessable income of the taxpayer is not fatal to the taxpayer's claim: "The better view, however, is that s. 51 as now drawn does not in either limb require a rigid restriction to the gaining or production of assessable income of the current year." (Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Finn per Dixon C.J. [1961] HCA 61; (1961) 106 CLR 60, at p 68 ). See also A.G.C. (Advances) Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation per Mason J. [1975] HCA 7; (1975) 132 CLR 175, at pp 196-197 . (at p124)

32. In words that have been referred to in so many cases: "To come within the initial part of the sub-section it is both sufficient and necessary that the occasion of the loss or outgoing should be found in whatever is productive of the assessable income or, if none be produced, would be expected to produce assessable income." (Ronpibon Tin N.L. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1949) 78 CLR, at p 57 ). (at p124)

33. In rejecting the taxpayer's claim to a deduction, Wickham J. adopted the reasons he expressed in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Chapkhana (1977) 77 ATC 4412 . That case concerned premiums paid on a personal disability policy by a legal practitioner in the employ of the Crown. His Honour's approach was that in taking out such an insurance policy, the taxpayer recognized the possibility of loss if he did not insure but did not expect to produce income from that source. (at p124)

34. With respect, that approach seems to me in error. The deductibility of the premium falls to be assessed in the context that any payments made under the policy constitute assessable income. It is true, as his Honour suggested, that money payable under the policy is only forthcoming in the event that certain contingencies are satisfied. But that is not inconsistent with the expectation of assessable income. There is nothing in the language used by the court in the Ronpibon Tin N.L. case [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47 or in other cases to like effect that requires the production of income to be free from contingencies. I agree with the dilemma posed by Mr. Todd, a member of the board that heard the objection of Mr. Chapkhana, when he said in Chapkhana v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation: "I find the greatest difficulty in concluding that a periodical payment outlaid, as previously stated, to no other end and for no other purpose than the production of periodical payments, themselves constituting assessable income upon the happening of events specified in the contract of insurance, constitutes an outgoing of a private nature" (1977) 77 ATC 186, at p 189 . (at p124)

35. In Amalgamated Zinc (de Bavay's) Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1935] HCA 81; (1935) 54 CLR 295, at pp 309-310 Dixon J. stressed the need for a connexion between the assessable income and the expenditure. In Ronpibon Tin N.L. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation the court said: "For expenditure to form an allowable deduction as an outgoing incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income it must be incidental and relevant to that end" (1949) 78 CLR, at p 56 . In Charles Moore & Co. (W.A.) Pty. Ltd. v. Federal Commissioner of Taxation the court explained that: " . . . the phrase 'incidental and relevant' . . . (does) not refer to the frequency, expectedness or likelihood of their occurrence or the antecedent risk of their being incurred, but to their nature or character. What matters is their connection with the operations which more directly gain or produce the assessable income" [1956] HCA 77; (1956) 95 CLR 344, at p 351 . (at p125)

36. Now it is true that in the case of an insurance policy premiums may be paid year after year without ever producing assessable income. But to say that is not to say that there is lacking a connexion between the two. Once the events postulated in the policy have occurred, money will be paid and that money will constitute assessable income. In my view that represents a sufficient connexion between the payment of premiums and the production of income. (at p125)

37. For reasons already given, I do not regard the premium in question as an outgoing of a private nature. Nor was it of a capital nature. The advantage which the expenditure was intended to gain was monthly payments in the event of total disability, payments which would serve to replace income the taxpayer stood to lose by reason of that disability. It was an advantage of a revenue character. See Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. South Australian Battery Makers Pty. Ltd. especially per Gibbs A.C.J. [1978] HCA 32; (1978) 52 ALJR 640 . (at p125)

38. In my view the cross appeal likewise should be allowed. I agree with the orders proposed by the Chief Judge. (at p125)

ORDER

Appeal and cross appeal allowed. Judgment of Supreme Court set aside. Matter remitted to the commissioner to re-assess in accordance with reasons for judgment.
