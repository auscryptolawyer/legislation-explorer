N94/78 and Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner [1995] AATA 461 (8 March 1995)

Last Updated: 23 September 2008



Administrative Appeals Tribunal



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

TAXATION - Trustee of fund entered into mortgage secured over property purchased by fund. Breach of regulation preventing other than borrowing to secure temporary finance. Whether special circumstances existed to exercise discretion to treat fund as satisfying superannuation standards. Wrong advice by Accountants not special circumstances.



Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 - Ss.13(1)

Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations - Reg 16(1)(b)



Re Beadle and Director-General of Social Security 6 ALD 1

REASONS FOR DECISION



8 March 1995 Senior Member M.D. Allen



  1. The Applicant Company is the Trustee of a Superannuation Fund of whom C was the only member. He was also a Director of the Applicant.
  2. On 31 May 1993 a Delegate of the Respondent and, on review on 17 June 1993, the Respondent determined that the Applicant Fund should not be treated as if it had satisfied the Superannuation Fund conditions set forth in the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 (the OSS Act) for the 1987/88 and 1988/89 years of income of the fund.
  3. Relief against a breach of the standards can be granted pursuant to Ss.13(1) of the OSS Act which reads:

“(1) Where, in relation to a fund in relation to a year of income of the fund:



(a) the trustees of the fund have not given the Commissioner the return and certificates referred to in subsection 12 (1), or the trustees of the fund have given the Commissioner the return and certificates referred to in that subsection but the Commissioner is not satisfied that the fund satisfied the superannuation fund conditions; and



(b) the trustees of the fund satisfy the Commissioner that, because of special circumstances that existed in relation to the fund during the year of income, it would be reasonable for the fund to be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions; and



(c) ...



The Commissioner shall give notice in writing to the trustees of the fund stating that the Commissioner is satisfied that the fund should be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in relation to the year of income.”

  1. The term “special circumstances” has been referred to in many decisions of this Tribunal. I need only refer to Re Beadle and Director-General of Social Security 6 ALD 1, on appeal Beadle v Director-General of Social Security [1984] AATA 176; 7 ALD 670. See also Secretary, Department of Social Security v Hulls 13 AAR 414 and Re Hajar and Secretary, Department of Social Security 16 ALD 716. As was stated by the High Court in Ex parte Bucknell [1936] HCA 67; 56 CLR 221 at 224:

“It is not necessary or desirable to attempt to enumerate all the classes or circumstances which may be regarded as special in this connection. Indeed, the very use of the work ‘special’ is designed to make it possible for the court to deal with each case individually in relation to its own particular circumstances.”

  1. It is common ground between the parties that the fund breached the conditions by buying an investment property subject to a mortgage raised against that property.
  2. Before the Trustee, that is to say the Applicant Company, entered into that arrangement C, as a Director of the company, sought advice from G C & Co, a respected firm of Accountants with whom he had entrusted the management of the fund’s affairs and with whom he also had considerable other business.
  3. Conscious as I am of not having had the matter fully canvassed before me, I can see no reason to doubt the evidence from the Director of the Applicant Company that he was advised by the Accountants that the investment would be in order. The advice was, however, totally wrong.
  4. An internal memorandum subpoenaed from the Accountants shows that their current attitude to the allegation that they gave wrong advice is simply one of “if that’s what he alleges let him prove it”.
  5. Unfortunately neither C nor, it would appear, the officers of the Accounting firm rendering the advice, made any notes at the time as to the advice received or given.
  6. As stated above, the investment property was purchased subject to a mortgage, settlement occurring on 30 June 1987. The said property was then sold on 14 September 1988 and the mortgage discharged.
  7. There is no dispute that at all times the funds were secure and the fund benefited from the transaction with no loss being incurred. The transaction was, however, in breach of paragraph 16(1)(b) of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations which states:

”the trustees of a superannuation fund shall not borrow, or maintain an existing borrowing of, money, whether by way of a secured or unsecured loan, otherwise than to secure temporary finance.”

  1. The Directors of the Trustee Company only became aware of the breach of regulations in January 1993 when informed by the firm of Accountants. It would appear that prior to that advice taxation returns which ought to have been prepared in respect of the fund by the Accountants had not been prepared and that, upon becoming aware of this, the Accountants had had discussions with the Respondent and officers of the Commissioner of Taxation before advising the Directors of the Applicant Company of the true state of affairs.
  2. The Applicant now seeks to have the Tribunal set aside the decision under review and exercise the discretion in Ss.13(1) of the OSS Act in its favour.
  3. On behalf of the Applicant it was argued that it was understandable that a professional man such as C, busy in running his own practice, would hand over the management of his Superannuation Fund to a firm of professional managers such as that set up by the Accountants and rely upon the advice given by them. I totally agree with this submission.
  4. Unfortunately, there is a consistent line of authority in this Tribunal that wrong legal advice of itself will not constitute a “special circumstance”. See, for example, Re Hajar and Secretary, Department of Social Security 16 ALD 716 at 720 and Case 34/94 94 ATC 306 at 314.
  5. Other matters were pointed to on behalf of the Applicant to show that special circumstances existed. But, as pointed out by Purvis J in Case 34/94 supra at p313, s.13(1) requires that the special circumstances exist during the relevant years of income. See also Case Z27 92 ATC 255 at 258.
  6. The only special circumstance which could be said to exist during the year(s) of income was that no detriment or risk was occasioned to the fund or its member, indeed it would seem a profit was made. As was pointed out by Deputy President McMahon in Case 17/94 94 ATC 198, this cannot amount to a special circumstance.
  7. Thus, while I consider that the breach occasioned by the Applicant was totally inadvertent and made no difference whatsoever or at all to the operation of the fund or its security, I find that I am constrained as a matter of comity with previous Tribunal decisions, including two decisions of Presidential Members, to hold that the reliance by the Applicant Company upon professional advice which proved wrong is not a special circumstance.
  8. Apart from the matter raised in paragraph 16 above, this was the only ground upon which relief could have been given, indeed all other matters advanced on behalf of the Applicant either flowed from this or did not occur during the year of income. I find therefore that special circumstances do not exist and affirm the decision under review.
