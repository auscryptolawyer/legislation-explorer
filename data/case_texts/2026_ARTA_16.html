Statement of Reasons

  1. Mr Shuo (Simon) Zhou (the Applicant) seeks a review of the Commissioner’s objection decision dated 12 December 2023 (“Objection Decision”).
  1. The issues for determination in these proceedings are broadly:
  1. Whether the Applicant discharged or was able to discharge the onus of proving the Amended Assessments for the income tax years ending 30 June 2013, 30 June 2015, 30 June 2025, 30 June 2016 and 30 June 2017 (Relevant Years) were excessive and, what the correct amount of the Assessments should have been in each of the Relevant Years.
  2. Whether the Applicant discharged the onus to prove the absence of “fraud or evasion” when the Respondent, pursuant to s 170 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the 1936 Act), issued Amended Assessments having found that there was avoidance of tax and formed the opinion it was “due to fraud or evasion”.
  3. Whether the Applicant has discharged or is able to discharge the onus of proving the decisions in respect to Administrative Penalties at the rate of 50% for recklessly making a false or misleading statement should not have been made or should have been made differently.
  4. Whether the Respondent should have exercised his discretion to remit all or part of the penalty imposed on the Applicant for making false or misleading statements for the Relevant Years.
  1. For the reasons that follow, Mr Zhou’s application for review must fail because he made no attempt to demonstrate his actual taxable income for the Relevant Years. The Applicant
cannot succeed if he does not prove his actual income. That of itself is sufficient to dispose of the review and ordinarily, that would be the end of the matter. However, in view of the history of the matter and the fact the Applicant was self-represented at the hearing the Tribunal has considered the Applicant’s evidence.

ISSUE 1 – WHETHER THE APPLICANT DISCHARGED OR IS ABLE TO DISCHARGE THE ONUS OF PROVING THAT THE AMENDED ASSESSMENTS ARE EXCESSIVE AND WHAT THE CORRECT AMOUNT OF THE ASSESSMENTS SHOULD BE

  1. The respondent issued the Amended Assessments for the Relevant Years under section 167 of the 1936 Act. In McPartland v Commissioner of Taxation,6 the Full Court explained the requirement in the following terms:

A taxpayer’s burden in challenging an assessment made pursuant to s 167 is to establish on the balance of probabilities their “actual taxable income” and, in so doing, show that the amount of money for which tax was levied exceeded their actual substantive liability: Gashi at [63], citing Commissioner of Taxation v Dalco (1990) 168 CLR 614 at 621, 623-625 (Dalco).

The taxpayer cannot discharge their burden only by showing that the Commissioner erred in the formation of his assessment for the purposes of s 167 of the ITAA: Dalco at 621; Gashi at [62]; Bosanac at [35], [47]-[48].

6 [2025] FCAFC 23 at [13] – [15].

The taxpayer bears the onus on all issues, save where the Commissioner and taxpayer agree to confine the issues to a particular point of law or fact, in which case the taxpayer bears the onus in respect of those: Dalco at 624. No particular method by which the taxpayer must discharge their burden is defined or specified. The method will vary according to the circumstances of the case: Gashi at [63], citing Dalco at 624It is well-settled that an assessment under section 167 places a heavy burden on the Applicant to prove on the balance of probabilities to the civil standard that the Amended Assessments were excessive and what the Amended Assessment should have been in each of the relevant years.

  1. The Uncle’s statement was “So the total amount of the loan was RMB 32.2 Million and part of the loan was initiated by Shou Zhou in the goal of helping some of his friends who were

ISSUE 2 ONUS OF PROOF – FRAUD OR EVASION

  1. Under s.170 of the 1936 Act the Respondent may amend an assessment at any time if he is of the opinion that there has been “fraud or evasion”.
  1. In this case the Respondent exercised this power and issued Notices of Amended Assessments for the Relevant Years having formed the opinion that there had been evasion of taxation.
  1. It is well settled that under the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 the Tribunal is placed in the same position as the administrative decision-maker at first instance under s 54.
  1. Section 14ZZK of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 relates to grounds of objections and burden of proof. It provides that on an application in the Tribunal for review of a reviewable objection decision, the applicant is, unless the Tribunal otherwise orders, limited to the grounds stated in the taxation objection to which the decision relates and has the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive.
  1. Under s 175 of the 1936 Act and Schedule 1 TAA s.350-10(1) item 2 the production of a notice of assessment is considered evidence of the due making and correctness of the assessment.
  1. In Rawson Finances Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation [2013] FCAFC 26; (2013) 133 ALD 39 at [90] this question was addressed by Jagot J (as her Honour then was) as follows:

Section 14ZZK is a modification of the AAT Act because, but for that provision, an applicant would not have the “burden of proving that ... the assessment is excessive”. There are no equivalent provisions in the AAT Act and it is well-established that as the tribunal may “exercise all the powers and discretions that are conferred by any relevant enactment on the person who made the decision” ..., an applicant for review is not subject to any burden of proof, the tribunal’s obligation being whether the decision under review is the correct or preferable decision... (Emphasis added.)

  1. It follows that the onus of proof lies on the Applicant to demonstrate he made full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for his assessment and there had not been an avoidance of tax due to evasion. Taking the Applicant’s evidence at its highest the Applicant failed to prove there had been no evasion within the meaning of section 170(1) 1936 Act; that is culpable misconduct (see: Denver Chemical Manufacturing Co v Commissioner of Taxation [1949] HCA 25; 79 CLR 296 at 301 per McTiernan and Web J and 313 per Dixon J).

ISSUE 3 – WHETHER THE APPLICANTS HAVE DISCHARGED OR ARE ABLE TO DISCHARGE THE ONUS OF PROVING THAT THE DECISIONS IN RESPECT TO PENALTIES SHOULD NOT HAVE BEEN MADE OR SHOULD HAVE BEEN MADE DIFFERENTLY

  1. On the evidence and in the absence of any testimony from the Applicant, the Tribunal cannot be satisfied that the Applicant has discharged the onus or adduced sufficient evidence to make out a compelling case for setting aside or varying the liability or quantum of the penalty amounts or for remitting the penalty in respect of the Relevant Years.

CONCLUSION

  1. There is no onus on the Respondent to prove evasion. In the present case the evidence before the Tribunal was sufficient for the Tribunal to be satisfied the Respondent correctly excised the power given to the Respondent to amend the assessments in the Relevant years under s 170(1) )item 5 1936 Act.
  1. The relevant onus on the Applicant is to establish that in each case the Assessments are excessive and what the correct amount of the assessments should be.
  1. The relevant Amended Assessments were made under s 167 of the Act.
  1. On the evidence before the Tribunal, the Applicant did not establish the Assessments were excessive and what was the actual taxable income.
  1. The Applicant has not discharged the onus of proving the decisions in respect to liability to pay penalties and not to remit the penalties should not have been made or should have be differently.
