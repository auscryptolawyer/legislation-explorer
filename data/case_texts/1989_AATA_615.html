NT89/195 and Commissioner of Taxation [1989] AATA 615 (18 October 1989)

Last Updated: 11 April 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

INCOME TAX - Assessable Income – Acquisition of partly-paid shares under employee share scheme - Shares without right to dividend, with restricted voting rights and non-transferable until fully paid - Whether value of such shares greater than 90% of market value of freely transferable fully paid ordinary shares

INCOME TAX - Assessable Income - Acquisition of partly-paid shares under employee share scheme - Shares without right to dividend, with restricted voting rights and non-transferable until fully paid - Whether acquisition deemed deferred until freed of restrictions or disposed of

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) s26AAC

ADMINISTRATIVE APFEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT89/195

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

AAT Decision No 5441

Tribunal : Mr Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

Date : 18 October 1989

Place : Sydney

Direction : That the decision of the Commissioner under review be varied and the objections wholly allowed.

(Sgd) Peter M. Roach

Senior Member

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT89/195

TAXATION APPELS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

18 October 1989 Mr Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. In December 1984 the Applicant - a Technical Services Officer by calling - joined the staff of a company (Cubco) which was a wholly owned subsidiary of a publicly listed holding company (Scoutco). In the event, his service with Cubco was not to be longlasting but, while in the service of Cubco, he became entitled to participate in a "Directors and Employee Share Plan" promoted by Scoutco. To demonstrate his confidence in the employer-organisation he agreed to buy 10,000 $1 shares paid to 2.5 cents per share. That was in August 1985. At the time, the market value of publicly listed fully paid shares in Scoutco was less than the price which he agreed to pay.
  2. Then in January 1986 he was again offered an opportunity to purchase 10,000 shares in Scoutco. He accepted the offer. The offer was to purchase them at $1.26 being 90% of the current market value of the publicly and listed freely transferable fully paid shares in the company. The Commissioner claims that the Applicant thereby became liable to pay income tax on 14c per share ($1400) representing the difference between the price payable by the Applicant and the current market value for the publicly listed and fully paid shares.
  3. The Applicant had a different view of the matter. He saw no advantage to himself in the acquisition of the shares. In his mind they were merely an expression of confidence on his part in the employer organisation. The shares he held did not confer any dividend entitlement. Voting rights in relation to the shares were restricted. He believed that he had no right to transfer them until they become fully paid-up. He was liable to calls should the company default, and he would be liable to payment of the unpaid portion of the purchase price if he ceased to be in the service of the company - but with the right in Scoutco to claim being deferred for two years in the event that he died or retired on grounds of age (55 years) or incapacity. In those circumstances in presenting his income tax return he did not disclose the transactions. That course was followed on the advice of his tax agent.
  4. His income tax return was assessed as returned in August 1986 and the tax assessed thereby was due and payable on 31 October 1986. Even before that date passed, the Commissioner had carried out certain investigations which resulted in the Melbourne office advising the Sydney office in September 1986 that the Applicant was a person who had derived assessable income by reason of accepting benefits under the share scheme. Shortly thereafter, in November 1986, the Applicant left the service of Cubco. Upon doing so, he did the only thing which he believed was open to be done in the circumstances: he transferred the shares at cost to another employee at the direction of his employers. He derived no profit. But, it was correctly contended by the Commissioner's advocate he might have taken a profit earlier. However, as the Applicant argued, that possibility could only have been realised in his circumstances by making the shares fully-paid: something only possible for him by selling the shares.

5. Then in November 1987 the Commissioner issued an amended assessment: the assessment which is the subject of challenge before the Tribunal. By that assessment the Commissioner increased the taxable income of the Applicant by $1,400 thereby increasing primary tax by $840 and health care levy by $14. The Commissioner considered that assessable income had been derived by reason of S.26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act"). Secondly, the Commissioner assessed the Applicant as liable to additional tax for incorrect return. Instead of assessing that liability in the sum of $1,680 (200% of primary tax avoided) the Commissioner exercised his discretion to effect remissions. In doing so he calculated a per annum component at the rate of 20% per annum and a "culpability factor" at 40%. The burden of the per annum component was eased for the Applicant in that the penalty was only calculated to 2 February 1987, a date some nine months before the issue of the amended assessment.

  1. As to quantum, the Commissioner gave effect to a very-simple view. It was that, since fully paid, freely transferable and publicly listed shares had a market value at date of allotment of $1.40, shares such as were allotted to the Applicant were of the same value at the time.
  2. In order to understand precisely what had eventuated, it is necessary to have regard to the actual "Terms and Conditions under which the shares held by the Applicant were issued. At the hearing, two versions of those terms and conditions were presented. Clearly enough one document was a revised draft of the other but more than that it was not possible to say. No witness was available to explain the difference. However, that difficulty was resolved when it was agreed by the Applicant and the Commissioner's representative that a particular set of terms and conditions presented by the Applicant should be accepted as the definitive statement of all relevant terms and conditions. refer to Exhibit F. Neither the Memorandum of Association nor the Articles of Association of Scoutco were presented in evidence.
  3. I find that on 24 January 1986 the Applicant was invited "to subscribe for" 10,000 shares "at the issue price of $1.26 (being at a 10% discount off the average sale price over the last five business days - $1.40). Subscription price is 2.5c". The offer was attended by Explanatory Notes and "a copy of the Terms and Conditions for your detailed information": the former being designed to assist the Applicant to understand the latter.

The Explanatory Notes indicated

that, to participate in the plan, the Applicant would have to pay 2.5c per share;

that you may pay the balance of the issue price at any time. However, any unpaid balance will be called if you cease to be a Director or employee other than by retirement after reaching the age of 55 or due to illness or incapacity. It will otherwise be called at the expiration of two years after retirement or death, unless previously paid...;

that the shares would not carry any dividend entitlements until fully paid;

that rights to participate in new issues would be on the same basis as the company's other ordinary shares;

that the shares would only carry voting rights in proportion to the amount paid up;

that there was no open market to give a precise indication of the value of the shares;

that he would receive and have access to all the usual reports required to be submitted by the company;

that "there would be a call for further payment if the company entered into liquidation or receivership. However, the Directors believe this to be a most unlikely event; and that he could only sell his shares "after they become fully paid".

9. Those Explanatory Notes provided a reasonably accurate but necessarily imprecise summary of the Terms and Conditions. Only one of those conditions needs to be quoted. It was Condition 7 which stated:

"Notwithstanding anything contained in these Terms and Conditions the shares issued and allotted under the Plan shall not be subject in any manner whatsoever to any condition or restriction (other than a condition or restriction applying to all shares in the capital of the company) by which an Executive's or his associate's right to dispose of any share or of any interest therein is restricted in any manner or an Executive or his associate is liable to be divested of ownership of or right to any such share".

10. I find no inconsistency between that statement in the Terms and Conditions and the representation in the Explanatory Notes that shares can only be sold after becoming fully paid. I am satisfied that, just as shares would not carry any dividend entitlements until they were fully paid, so too there was no right of transfer until they were fully paid.

11. That being so, I am satisfied that:

"by reason of... conditions or restrictions applicable only to shares in the company acquired under (the) scheme...the right of the taxpayer to dispose of the share (was) restricted

That being so, it is provided by Section 25AAC (15) of the Act that:

"the acquisition of the share by the taxpayer shall be deemed for the purposes of this section to have taken place..." only after 30 June 1986.

12. Having so concluded, the objection should be upheld and it is not necessary to deal with two other questions although some comment on them is appropriate. First, I am satisfied that the assessment is excessive in as much as I am well satisfied that it was not appropriate to consider the market value of these partly paid; non-dividend bearing; limited voting right shares, subject to restrictions on transfer, as being equal to that of fully paid, freely transferable shares with full rights to dividend and in voting. Secondly, it is not necessary to determine whether there is power in the Tribunal to grant relief by allowing further remissions of additional tax. It was contended that clearly, as the Commissioner's representative recognized, the impositions based upon a compensatory factor of 20% per annum and a culpability factor of 40% were excessive. What the Commissioner's representative proposed as more reasonable, namely a compensatory factor of 14.026% per annum and a "culpability factor" not exceeding 5% would be much more reasonable. I would merely observe that it would have been unfortunate if, by reason of the deficiencies in the drafting of the objection, it had been necessary to uphold an excessive and unjust imposition in as much as no express ground of objection expressly asserted that the Commissioner had failed in any way in exercising his discretion to remit additional tax for incorrect return.
