QT86/855 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 1; 87 ATC 188 (2 January 1987)

Last Updated: 8 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Allowable Deductions, election expenses, expenses incurred in contesting municipal elections.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss51 (1), 74

Case 510 85 ATC 160

F C of T v Maddalena (1971) 45 ALJR 426, 71 ATC 4161

DECISION AND REASONS

Re: QT86/855

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No: QT86/855

AAT Decision No 3145

Place: Brisbane

Date: 2 January 1987

Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) QT86/855 TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re: QT86/855

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

The question at issue in this application is whether the applicant is entitled to a deduction in respect of expenses incurred in contesting an election for alderman of a municipal authority.

2. The question arises under sub-section 51(1) and s74 of the Income Tax Assessment Act. S74 reads:

"74.(1) Expenditure incurred in the year of income by the taxpayer in being elected as a member, or in contesting an election for membership, of the Parliament, of the Parliament of a State or of the Legislative Assembly of the Northern Territory of Australia shall be an allowable deduction.

(2) When a deduction has been allowed or is allowable under sub-section (1) in respect of any expenditure and that expenditure or any part of it is reimbursed to the taxpayer or paid for him by any other person or by any organization the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year in which the amount is so reimbursed or paid shall include that amount."

Sub-section 51(1) reads:-

"51(1). All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income."

3. There was no dispute before the Tribunal as to the quantum of the deduction claimed by the applicant, the respondent's representative conceding that the amount of $5861 claimed in respect of election expenses had been incurred by the applicant. In the return of income for the year ended 30 June 1985 the applicant set out in considerable detail the amounts going to make up the total figure of $5861 and having reviewed that schedule of the return I am satisfied that the respondent was correct in accepting the quantum of the claims in dispute.

4. The applicant is employed in normal day to day work which results in derivation of assessable income. The applicant is employed in a professional position not relevant to these proceedings.

5. In essence the applicant's case was very straight forward. The applicant argued that if the claim had arisen out of contesting an election for a State Parliament, or for that matter the Federal Parliament, the Commissioner would have allowed a deduction in respect of the election expenses (vide s74). The applicant submitted that the particular municipal authority in question in this application was little different in standing to a State Parliament and therefore the same rules should apply. While it would be wrong to suggest that persons elected to the municipal authority enjoyed the same conditions as to remuneration and status etc enjoyed by members of parliament it is sufficient for the purposes of these reasons to say that there is no material difference between a member of parliament and an alderman of the municipal authority in question relevant to the income tax law.

6. The applicant also claimed and the respondent's representative conceded that the election expenses claimed would have been allowed as a deduction under sub-section 51(1) by the Commissioner if the applicant had been successful at the election. The applicant submitted that there should not be any difference as to deductibility of the election expenses between a candidate who was successful (who is allowed a deduction for the election expenses) and a candidate who was not successful. The applicant submitted that the test was to be applied at the time the expenditure was incurred not in the light of events which occurred up to several months after the applicant had incurred the expenditure.

7. It is somewhat surprising that the Commissioner takes the view that success or otherwise in the municipal elections determines whether a candidate is entitled to a deduction for the election expenses. It is surprising for a number of reasons which include the general principles applicable to sub-section 51(1) as enumerated by the Courts in the leading cases; the decision of Taxation Board of Review No 3 in Case S10 85 ATC 160 where the Board held that expenses incurred by an alderman in being re-elected to a City Council were not allowable deductions thereby confirming the Commissioner's assessment and the recent amendment of the Act by the Parliament to allow certain deductions for election expenses incurred by candidates at municipal elections as from 1 July 1985.

8. Notwithstanding the attitude taken by the Commissioner, this Tribunal must apply the law as it is found in the Income Tax Assessment Act and the decisions of the Courts which interpret that Act.

9. It is clear enough that section 74 does not apply to persons contesting municipal elections. The applicant sought to argue by analogy with the section but the Parliament has made the scope of the section's operation quite clear. The applicant cannot succeed under section 74.

10. Turning to sub-section 51(1) I have no doubt that Board of Review No 3 was correct in its decision in Case S10 and that decision applies equally to the facts of this case. The election expenses were incurred by the applicant with the object of being elected to the municipal authority. If elected the applicant would derive assessable income in respect of salary paid to an alderman. The decision of the High court in FC of T v Maddalena (1971) 45 ALJR 426, 71 ATC 4161 deals with the same issue in the sense that in Maddalena's case the taxpayer incurred expenses in seeking and obtaining a contract of employment as a professional footballer. The High Court held that such expenditure incurred in securing new employment is neither incurred in gaining or producing the taxpayer's assessable income nor necessarily incurred in carrying on a business within the terms of the second limb of sub-section 51(1). The expenditure was incurred at a point too soon to be regarded as incurred in derivation of assessable income.

10. While the factual situation in this application is different there is no material distinction which would allow this Tribunal to depart from the decision of the High Court, and the Tribunal is therefore bound to affirm the respondent's decision on the objection.

This and the preceding five (5) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT86/455.
