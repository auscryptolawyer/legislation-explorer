REASONS FOR DECISION

24 February 1988 Mrs. R.A. Balmford, Senior Member

  1. This is an application for review of two decisions of the respondent disallowing objections by the applicant to the inclusion in the respondent's assessment of his taxable income for each of the years ended 30 June 1983 and 1984, of amounts claimed as deductions in respect of wages paid to his wife.
  2. The applicant requested that each of the objections be referred to a Board of Review, and these requests were complied with by the respondent. By virtue of Section 223(1) of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986 ("the Transfer of Jurisdiction Act") and sub-section 189(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Assessment Act") that referral of the decisions on the applicant's objections is deemed to constitute the making by the applicant of applications to the Tribunal for review of the decisions. As the issue was the same in respect of each year of income, both matters were heard together. Evidence was given at the hearing by the applicant who was unrepresented, and a number of documents were before the Tribunal.
  3. The applicant said that he commenced practice in approximately 1981 as a tax agent and financial adviser. At that time, and at all relevant times, he was employed full-time as a school teacher. He conducted the practice from the spare bedroom of his house. He gave the following information as to his income from the practice and the wages paid to his wife for her work in the practice.

He said that in 1987 the practice was conducted by a company which paid him a salary: in that year the tax agent's fees received were of the order of $16,000.

  1. The amounts of $3357 in respect of the year ended 30 June 1983 and $3126 in respect of the year ended 30 June 1984 are the amounts in issue in this application for review.
  2. The relevant provisions of the Act are sub-sections 51(1) and 65(1), which read as follows:

51(1) All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income.

65(1) Subject to this section, the amount, or a part of the amount, of any payment made or liability incurred in the year of income by a taxpayer to an associated person that would, but for this sub-section, be an allowable deduction is allowable as a deduction only to the extent to which, in the opinion of the Commissioner, it is reasonable.

By virtue of sub-paragraph 65(1D)(a)(i) of the Act and the definition of "relative" in Section 6, the applicant's wife is "an associated person" in relation to him for the purposes of sub-section 65(1).

  1. The officer of the Australian Taxation Office who represented the respondent at the hearing said that the Commissioner had formed the opinion in 1983, pursuant to sub-section 65(1), that it would be reasonable to allow a deduction of thirty per cent of gross fees earned, and in 1984, that it would be reasonable to allow a deduction of twenty five per cent of gross fees earned.
  2. In Case No. C80, (1971) 71 ATC 353, Mr Todd, with whom the other members of the Board of Review agreed, said, at pages 356-357:

"Section 190(b) of the Act provides that the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive shall lie upon the taxpayer'. This means, in the context of section 65, that a decision favourable to the taxpayer can only be reached by furnishing the mind of the Board with evidence of such particularity that the Board can make a sensible and just substitution of its opinion for that of the Commissioner: See the reasons for decision of Mr Davies and Mr Thompson (Members) in Case C6, 71 ATC 24 at page 26. The problem is thus not one of determining what the Board would have considered a reasonable payment if the decision had been one for the Board in the first place. Previous decisions of Boards of Review have noted the difficulty of determining what is reasonable remuneration for the purposes of section 65. To obtain opinions from different persons as to what is a reasonable sum in the circumstances is likely to produce as many different results as there are persons asked.

In such a situation as this, therefore, a positive case must be made out by the taxpayer if he is to succeed."

  1. The applicant said that his wife's duties included typing of taxation returns and other materials; filing; distribution to letterboxes of promotional leaflets numbering five to ten thousand in each year; mailing and banking; collection and delivery of documents to clients; receptionist duties; and cleaning the spare bedroom which was used as an office. He emphasised the importance of the promotional material, and of his wife's role, in that context, in the increase in the business of the practice which is demonstrated by the figures set out in paragraph 3 supra.
  2. He said that there was no written agreement between himself and his wife as to her duties as an employee in the practice, but there was an implied obligation that if the work was there to be done she would do it. Payment was at the Department of Labour hourly rate for secretarial work, the cleaning to count as half-time. His wife would cash his teaching salary cheque, and take her wages out of that and use them for herself and for housekeeping. He produced a list of hours worked, which he said was prepared monthly from a weekly diary. However the list, which was handwritten in ink, did not appear to have been prepared at monthly intervals. The weekly hours were all in multiples of five, and the applicant agreed that they were in part an estimate.
  3. At the close of the hearing it was agreed that the applicant would be permitted to lodge with the Tribunal, within a stated period, further documentary material in support of this application, including the weekly diary, the respondent to be given a stated period within which to inspect that material and make further submissions thereon. However, the applicant did not avail himself of this opportunity.
  4. I note that, although the taxpayer's wife's duties included the typing of taxation returns, she had not typed the taxpayer's return in either of the years in question, or the answers to a questionnaire sent to him by the respondent, all of which documents were handwritten. I note that the list showed that the taxpayer's wife had worked 25 hours in the week ending 9 July 1982, although her second child was born on 6 July 1982. The taxpayer's return for each of the years in question included the statement "wife is asthmatic and requires constant medical attention", and in the 1983 return a similar statement was made with regard to his son. The taxpayer did not call his wife to give evidence.
  5. Having considered the evidence in the light of the statement of principle cited above from Case No. C80, I find that the taxpayer has not discharged the burden of proving that either of the two assessments before me is excessive. The objection decisions under review will be affirmed.
