NT86/10557 and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 241; (1988) 19 ATR 3743; 88 ATC 758 (29 July 1988)

Last Updated: 1 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

INCOME TAX - interest withholding tax - loan from foreign corporation - borrowing guaranteed by Australian corporation - loan moneys advanced by deposit to credit of borrower with Australian guarantor - moneys drawn upon Australian guarantor by installments - whether one loan or several.

INCOME TAX - interest withholding tax - loan from foreign corporation - borrowing guaranteed by Australian corporation - some moneys used to pay out mortgages on properties owned by others - properties mortgaged by those others to provide security for the borrowings - whether moneys borrowed used only for qualifying purposes.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - ss. 128A, 128G, 128H.

Lilydale Pastoral Co. Pty. Limited v. F.C. of T. 87 A.T.C. 4235;

Case T15 86 A.T.C. 200.

REASONS FOR DECISION

29 July 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. The question for determination in these proceedings is whether the Commissioner acted correctly in refusing to issue a certificate of exemption from withholding tax under S.128H of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). The Commissioner gave notice to the Applicant of his decision to refuse the application made for an exemption certificate on 8 September 1983. Objection was taken on behalf of the Applicant and the objection was disallowed on 24 October 1984 resulting in a request for reference for independent review on 10 December 1984. On 23 June 1986 the Commissioner referred the matter for determination.
  2. Sadly, on 21 August 1986, the principal officer of the Applicant company (whom I shall refer to as Stephen) suffered serious and permanent injury in a motor vehicle accident. Current medical assessment is that Stephen will never be fit to return to his pre-accident career as a builder and developer. As a result of his condition, Stephen was unable to attend before the Tribunal to give evidence. However, that circumstance has not denied the Applicant the opportunity to establish all material facts. The fact that some matters were not presented in evidence (e.g. the Trading Accounts of the company; and the Principal Loan Agreement) did not depend upon the availability of Stephen to give evidence.
  3. The Applicant company is one of three companies which had been controlled by Stephen. Despite the absence of any evidence as to its Trading Accounts or Balance Sheets, I am satisfied that it was engaged from time to time in building developments and that it was the owner of substantial properties. In particular, I find that, in the period September 1982 to September 1984, it was engaged in investigating the possible development of, and, as appropriate, negotiating with the local Council for development and building approval in relation to a suburban site (Erewhon). I also find that in that period those negotiations, which had commenced in relation to a single lot of the order of "50 feet by 150 feet", were extended to embrace adjoining lots, and resulted in proposals for development consolidation of some six lots of the size of the original lot.
  4. I find that during 1983 the company negotiated with Citibank N.A. ("Citibank") - a non-resident - to borrow on a term loan facility with U.S. Dollar, Deutsche Mark, Swiss Franc, Yen and Sterling currency options. The borrowing was to be for a term of three years from the first advance and was to be guaranteed by Citicorp Australia Limited ("Citicorp"). The declared purpose expressed by Citibank in communicating its offer was "to provide funds to enable the borrower to repay existing mortgages over thirteen townhouses at ............, twenty-nine units at ........, one unit at ........ , and three units at .........To that there was added among the Special Conditions:

"The balance from the initial advance of $A2,500,000 after repaying existing mortgages over the properties of the borrower and/or indemnifiers will be deposited with Citicorp and will be used for the development at Erewhon or any other suitable project should the situation arise". The offer was said to be "Conditional upon the preparation, execution and delivery of documentation in form and substance satisfactory to us and to our solicitors incorporating substantially the terms and conditions set forth herein."

"Nothing in the foregoing contians (sic) any obligation on behalf of Citibank to make available this facility...."

The offer, which was nominated to expire on 30 April 1983, was accepted under the seal of the Applicant on 28 April 1983. No other evidence was tendered to establish the precise terms of any covenant or agreement between Citibank and the Applicant.

5. However, on 25 July 1983 the Applicant entered into a precise and detailed agreement with Citicorp. (I shall refer to it as "the Citicorp Agreement"). Other parties to the Citicorp Agreement who assumed responsibility as guarantors of performance of the obligations of the Applicant were three other companies; Stephen; and his wife. That agreement contemplated the possibility of loans by Citicorp to the Applicant but I find that no such loans ever eventuated. That agreement also obliged the Applicant to indemnify Citicorp against any liability of Citicorp "including payment of any withholding tax" arising as a consequence of the giving of the Citicorp guarantee to Citibank. The latter guarantee was one whereby Citicorp guaranteed payment to Citibank and agreed to indemnify Citibank against "all liabilities with respect thereto payable to any government in Australia or elsewhere (including withholding tax)".

  1. The Citicorp agreement also nominated the properties which were to be made available by way of first mortgage security in favour of Citicorp. They were to comprise thirteen townhouses owned by the Applicant; twenty-nine home units owned by a second company; three home units owned by a third company; three other properties owned by Stephen; and one property owned by his wife. Two properties only are of any particular significance. Those properties comprised the family residence of Mr. and Mrs. Stephen and another residence owned by Stephen and occupied by other members of his family.
  2. On 22 July 1983 a sum of $2,390,000 was advanced by Citibank and deposited in a "Cash Collateral Deposit Account" to the credit of the Applicant with Citicorp. It was to remain there at interest pending its disbursement in accordance with the Citicorp agreement. In anticipation of that loan being made, on 21 April 1983 the Applicant applied to the Reserve Bank of Australia for authority under the Banking (Foreign Exchange) Regulations for the company to enter into and borrow from Citibank? and for Citicorp to issue a guarantee in favour of Citibank to secure the borrowing by the Applicant. That application was approved in writing on 21 April 1983. Then, on 10 May 1983, the Applicant applied to the Commissioner for a certificate pursuant to S.128H of the Act in relation to the proposed borrowing. That application was refused by notice dated 8 September 1983.

8. On 25 July 1983 Citicorp advanced $1,584,731 in accordance with the instructions of the Applicant and, two days later, a further sum of $314,479 was so advanced, leaving a credit at that date of $490,789. Those transactions as such do not give rise to any dispute between the parties. However, on 28 July 1983, further sums of $15,920 and $28,055 were drawn by the Applicant and the funds were used to effect payment to the Commonwealth Savings Bank and to the Commonwealth Trading Bank respectively of amounts due to those banks under mortgages on the two residences registered in the name of Stephen, but subject to mortgages to the banks. That left a sum of $446,815 in the "cash collateral deposit account" with Citicorp. That sum remained there until 24 January 1984 when the amount on deposit in that account was increased by the deposit of a sum of $265,000 lodged by the Applicant to the credit of that account. In the months following there were debits of $60,000 (drawn on the borrower's instructions on 22 February 1984) and $40,744 (by way of interest debit on the loan 24 April 1984); an interest credit $48,307 (4 May 1984) and a further interest debit on the loan of $47,569 (25 July 1984). At that point the balance to the credit of the Applicant stood at $611,808. On 10 September 1984 $556,682 was drawn to enable the Applicant to complete the purchase of the balance of the real estate to which the suburban development project related. That left a balance uncalled of $55,126. An additional loan draw down on Citibank followed ($509,000) and the first - but not the last - settlement of foreign exchange trading losses occurred in the following month ($41,548).

8. The Applicant contends that, in light of the circumstances so stated, it is entitled to an exemption certificate in relation to all payments of interest to be made to Citibank because all that was borrowed from Citibank was applied to qualifying purposes. Alternatively, the Applicant claims to be entitled to exemption certificates in relation to each one of several borrowings in relation to which it has established that the moneys were wholly applied to qualifying purposes. I first address the question whether, in the relevant sense, there was a single borrowing from Citibank or several. The Applicant points to the decision of the Federal Court of Australia in Lilydale Pastoral Co. Pty. Limited v. F.C. of T. (87 A.T.C. 4235) as a decision supportive of its cause. In that case there was but one loan agreement. The loan moneys were advanced by the lender to its solicitor. As I understand the decision, at the point of receipt of moneys by the solicitor, he held the moneys so advanced to him in trust for the lender. If any contractual relationship existed at that time between the borrower and that solicitor, it did not arise by reason of the advance being made by the lender to him as its solicitor. On 23 July 1981 the lender's solicitor applied the moneys to the use of Lilydale as

follows:-

Australian Mutual Provident Society $432,195.30

AGC (Advances) Limited $94,896.44

Tricontinental Corporation Limited $1,244,346.26

To General Account $13,921.06

TOTAL $1,785,359.06

A fortnight later the lender paid the solicitor a sum equivalent to $19,986.72 which he paid out on the same day to Lilydale. No doubt no advances were made until the available security in favour of the lender was perfected; and as advances were made, additional security coming available by reason of the advance, was taken by the lender. It is unlikely that the borrower was entitled viz a viz the lender to have any moneys advanced to its use until the security requirements of the lender - as distinct from the lender's solicitor - were satisfied. Pincus, J. in those circumstances concluded that there were three loans; and being satisfied that the moneys advanced to the AMP Society and Tricontinental were applied to qualified purposes his Honour granted the exemption certificate sought in relation to those loans, but refused certificates in relation to the other advances. The question which arises in this case is whether the role of Citicorp in these arrangements was comparable to that of the solicitor in the Lilydale Case.

9. The first thing to be observed in that regard is that the terms of the loan agreement between the Applicant and Citibank were not established in evidence. That being so, the information which is available is confined to what appears in the letter of acceptance of Citibank's offer of 14 April 1983 (pa. 4) and of the agreement with Citicorp of 25 July 1983 (pa. 5 & ff.). The former document contemplated a loan of moneys for a term of three years from Citibank to the Applicant, being a loan in respect of which all obligations of the Applicant were to be guaranteed by Citicorp.

10. The Citicorp Agreement recited that:-

"(The Applicant) has requested Citicorp to lend to the company and to provide a guarantee of the company's indebtedness to Citibank NA for a sum not exceeding in aggregate two million seven hundred and eighty-five thousand dollars ($2,785,000) or its foreign currency equivalent for the purposes hereinafter referred to."

It went on to provide that, subject to specified conditions, Citicorp would lend, but it would not be obliged to make any advance in excess of the defined "uncalled facility limit" (Clause 2). Then, by Clause 3, it provided:-

"Citicorp agrees with the company and with the guarantors to provide the guarantee sought by Citibank as something prerequisite to it making the promised loan."

11. Having provided for Citicorp to assume risks as a lender and as a guarantor, the agreement was largely given over to the making of provision to protect Citicorp against risk. Prominent among those clauses was a provision which said:-

"7.05. The company shall establish with Citicorp a deposit account upon Citicorp's usual terms and conditions for such accounts. The company shall invest in the deposit account:

(a) any moneys to be deposited pursuant to Clause 2.04 (sic); and

(b) all funds which from time to time are surplus funds of (the Applicant's group).

Provided the company is not in default hereunder, the company may, with the prior written consent of Citicorp, withdraw moneys from the deposit account but only for such purposes as Citicorp may in its absolute discretion approve.

Provided that

And provided further that "

12. In face of those findings I am satisfied that not only was there but only one relevant agreement to borrow from Citibank; there was also only one borrowing from Citibank and but one advance by Citibank to effect that lending. In so far as Citicorp was only prepared to advance moneys upon being satisfied that they would be applied for approved purposes, I am not persuaded that it was merely acting as agent for Citibank. I find that it was acting in protection of its own interest as guarantor of the Citibank transaction and as a prospective lender. There is nothing before me which persuades me that Citicorp would have been in breach of any obligation to Citibank had it permitted the Applicant to draw down moneys in excess of any figure to which Citicorp might previously have committed itself.

13. The Applicant will not be entitled to the certificate it seeks unless (inter alia) it satisfies the requirements of S.128H of the Act. It provides:-

"(1) An entity that has raised a loan the interest on which could, subject to the issue of a certificate under sub-section 2 in respect of the loan, be interest referred to in S.128G may apply to the Commissioner in writing for the issue of such a certificate;

(2) Where an application is made in accordance with sub-section (1) and the Commissioner is satisfied that

(a)

(b) the loan moneys have been employed, and are intended to be employed, only for a qualifying use or for making moneys available for a qualifying use;

(c)

(d)

the Commissioner shall issue to the applicant a certificate containing particulars of the loan and stating that the loan complies with this sub-section, but otherwise he shall refuse the application.

(3) For the purposes of paragraph 2(b) the Commissioner may disregard a deposit or investment of any loan moneys during the period of a temporary delay that was reasonably involved in the making of arrangements for a qualifying use of those moneys."

14. In order to determine whether or not all of the moneys were applied to a qualifying use, it is necessary to have regard to S.128A in so far as it provides:-

"(7). A reference in this Division to the use of moneys for the purposes of an enterprise shall be read as not including use of those moneys in the course of carrying on an enterprise -

(a) by way of providing capital for another enterprise; or

(b) by way of the making of loans."

15. Some of the moneys borrowed by the Applicant from Citibank were used to pay out mortgages which existed on properties held by subsidiaries of the Applicant. I find that that was done in the interests of the Applicant. By arrangement with its subsidiaries, the Applicant gained increased borrowing capacity: an increased capacity which would improve its prospects of successfully embarking upon the developmental project it had under consideration. It achieved that borrowing capacity because its subsidiaries were willing to mortgage their property by way of providing security which would make possible the borrowings of the Applicant. However, as the properties of the subsidiaries were already mortgaged, two things followed. Firstly, without more the value of the security which could be offered by the subsidiaries was limited to their equity interest in that realty. Secondly, so that they might give unlimited first mortgage security over those properties, it was necessary that the subsisting mortgages over those properties be first discharged. To achieve that, the Applicant had to make funds available which would be sufficient to discharge those mortgages. I find that, in the circumstances of this case, it achieved that end by using the moneys borrowed from Citibank. By advancing the moneys as it did, I find that it either made a gift of those moneys to the respective subsidiaries, in each case sufficient to enable the subsidiary to pay out its subsisting mortgage; or it advanced the moneys to the subsidiaries on loan. If the transaction is to be fairly categorized as falling into the former category, it is probable that the Applicant was providing "capital for another enterprise". More probably, it was not so much doing that but rather making a loan, whether at interest or not: the terms of the loan involving at least this, that if the subsidiary were to request redemption of its mortgage, the Applicant would be entitled to recoup the money advanced by it in paying out the original mortgage.

  1. Having reached that conclusion it follows that I am not persuaded that the one loan resulted in a situation in which "the loan moneys have been employed, and are intended to be employed, only for a qualifying use or for making moneys available for a qualifying use". As has been held in Lilydale (ante) and Case T15 (86 A.T.C. 200) where there is but one loan and the requirements of s.128H(2)(b) are not wholly satisfied in relation to that loan, there can be no apportionment.
  2. In those circumstances I find it unnecessary to consider whether or not the holding of the moneys on deposit with Citicorp was not or not sufficiently "temporary" for me to be satisfied in relation to s.l28H (3).

18. Accordingly, the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection under review will be affirmed.
