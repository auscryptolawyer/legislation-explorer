Re Guiseppe Perusco and Tax Agents' Board (NSW) [1982] AATA 272 (3 December 1982)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: GUISEPPE PERUSCO

And: TAX AGENTS' BOARD (N.S.W.)

No. N82/265

Tax Agent

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

McGregor J.

CATCHWORDS

Tax Agent - deregistration by Tax Agents' Board - misconduct - attention to one aspect drawn in show cause letter - additional ground assigned later for deregistration - submissions already recently made in respect of additional ground - no criticism of tax agent's preparation of returns or otherwise of his work with Taxation Department - good character of applicant. Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s.251N, 251K(2)(b)(i) and (ii)

HEARING

CANBERRA

3:12:1982

ORDER

The Tribunal decides to set aside the decision of the Tax Agents' Board (N.S.W.) to cancel the registration of the applicant as a tax agent.

DECISION

GIUSEPPE PERUSCO (applicant) has made application pursuant to s.251K(5) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 for a review of a cancellation of the registration of the applicant as a tax agent on or about 8 July 1982 by the TAX AGENTS' BOARD (N.S.W.) (the Board) established pursuant to the said Act s.251C. The reasons for it are set out in his application dated 28 July 1982 thus -

"(a) The Applicant is and was at all material times a fit and proper person to be and remain registered as a Tax Agent.

(b) The Applicant depends for his livelihood on his remaining registered

as a Tax Agent.

(c) The Applicant was not afforded a proper opportunity, by the Tax Agents' Board, to answer complaints against him.

(d) Neither Mr. nor Mrs. Serafini was at any material time a principal of the Applicant.

(e) The relationship of agency between the Applicant and Mr/Mrs Serafini was dissolved by consent, without the need for notice (or, alternatively, without the need for any notice further than that which was given).

(f) The Applicant did not neglect the business of either Mr. or Mrs. Serafini.

(g) Mr. & Mrs. Serafini were aware, at the time the subject cheque was delivered to the Applicant, that the Applicant would do no further work for them unless his outstanding fees were paid.

(h) The Applicant has not been guilty of misconduct as a Tax Agent.

(i) The Tax Agents' Board (by letter dated 3 June 1982) required the Applicant to show cause why his registration should not be cancelled pursuant to sub-paragraph (i), but purported to cancel his registration pursuant to both sub-paragraphs (i) and (ii), of Section 251K(2)(b).

(j) The Tax Agents' Board was not entitled to have (or, alternatively, ought not to have had) regard to matters "which were subject to consideration at the Board's meeting on 22 April 1982"."

Before considering the matters more immediately relevant to this review,

certain background has to be noted. Thirty summonses had been issued against the applicant for alleged breaches by him of s.251(N)(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 related to his business at a Warrawong branch office. The section makes it an offence for a Tax Agent to allow any person not being e.g. an employee to prepare on behalf of the Tax Agent any income tax return; or to conduct on his behalf business relating to any income tax return. In a hearing at the Wollongong Court of Petty Sessions on 8 May 1981 of one of these charges, the applicant was convicted. An appeal to the District Court of New South Wales against this conviction was heard by his Honour Judge Gee, Q.C. on 28 October 1981. In his reasoned judgment delivered on 25 November 1981, his Honour rejected the appeal and confirmed the conviction. His Honour's reasons for judgment indicate that he was not able to accept the evidence of the applicant in one respect; and that there was an element of neglect on the part of the applicant in certain activities related to the branch office. Following the rejection of this appeal, the applicant, on 1 March 1982, pleaded guilty to the remaining twenty nine charges. What are described as nominal fines were imposed on each of the thirty charges. On 24 March 1982, the Board gave consideration to the applicant's continued registration in the light of these convictions. In a letter dated 25 March 1982 applicant was advised that the Board was of the opinion his registration should be cancelled pursuant to the provisions of s.251K(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936; but before doing so invited him to show cause why this should not be done. Applicant personally left some documents with the Secretary of the Board. Also, there followed correspondence with applicant's solicitors. Submissions made on his behalf in a letter dated 13 April 1982 from applicant's solicitor included a surprising suggestion that the Taxation Office must accept some responsibility for the events which had occurred. A similar claim together with other submissions by the applicant was put forward by him in a letter of the same date. But the Board held its hand. In a letter dated 23 April 1982 the applicant was advised that although it was considered that in terms of s.251K(2)(b)(ii) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 he had been guilty of misconduct as a tax agent, on this occasion discretion had been exercised in his favour and the Board did not cancel his registration.

With this background in mind, the immediately relevant matter can be considered. It arises following a complaint on 2 June 1982 by Mrs. Serafini as to his handling of her tax affairs. The applicant had for some years acted for Mr. Claudio Serafini as his tax agent and also in some arrangement of insurance for him. In 1980 and 1981, the Wollongong office of the Australian Tax Office conducted an investigation into the affairs of this gentleman in respect of income for the years ended June 1977, 1978 and 1979. Following this investigation, a Notice of Amended Assessment was issued requiring him to pay additional tax. The applicant stated in his affidavit filed in support of this application that when Mr. Serafini received his Amended Notice of Assessment in respect of the year 1979 he complained to the applicant of his having to pay additional tax; and that at this time his relationship with Mr. Serafini seemed to deteriorate. In July 1981 Mrs. Serafini advised the applicant that she and her husband would like to have their 1980 tax returns prepared by somebody other than the applicant. He told them that he had already commenced work on their 1980 returns and that he would charge for any work that he had already done; she said that he might as well finish those returns. In or about late July 1981 the applicant prepared and lodged a tax return for Mrs. Serafini, being the first and only tax return that he had prepared for her; although the instructions for its preparation came principally from Mr. Serafini. On 23 September 1981 the Notice of Assessment issued in respect of her 1980 return; and on or about 30 September 1981 he lodged an application for variation of the provisional tax payable by her. On 9 October 1981 and following his application an Amended Notice of Assessment was issued to her. On about 13 October 1981 he wrote advising her of the amount of tax said to be payable and the date by which it should be paid, viz. 26 October 1981. Enclosed with his letter was a copy of his memo of fees for $325.00 dated 22 September 1981. Though addressed to Mr. Serafini, it included reference to work that he had done for her also. In evidence he said that more than half of the work covered by it had been for Mr. Serafini. Applicant was concerned that his fees might not be paid. He said to Mr. Serafini words to the effect "No pay; no work. I can't be expected to do any work for you unless you pay my fees in full."

On a date not precisely identified but it seems to have been 27 October 1981, Mrs. Serafini attended at his office and left two cheques both drawn on St. George Building Society Ltd., the first being a sum for $160.00 in part payment of the said account for his fees; and the second a cheque made payable to the Taxation Department in the sum of $1,832.45. Applicant did not speak to either Mr. or Mrs. Serafini. He said in his affidavit -

"I received no express instructions as to what I should do with the cheque drawn payable to the Taxation Department; however, I did become aware of its existence at my office on or soon after the day on which it was left at my office."

He agreed in evidence this would have been the 27 or 28 October 1981. His affidavit continues -

"(c) I did not deliver the cheque to the Australian Tax Office, or return

it to Mr. or Mrs. Serafini, because:

(i) Both Mr. & Mrs. Serafini had told me that they did not want me to do any work for them after completing their 1980 Tax Returns; and

(ii) I had told Mr. Serafini that I would do no further work for him unless my fees were paid in full."

He said later in the affidavit referring to the cheque for $1832.45 -

"I assumed that they would collect their cheque from me, or make other arrangements for the payment of Mrs. Serafini's tax before the Tax Office commenced recovery proceedings."

He banked the cheque for $160.00 and retained the one payable to the Taxation Department.

Final notice in respect of Mrs. Serafini's assessment was issued on 11 November 1981 and received in the office of the applicant. The practice in such circumstances was to photocopy the notice and send the original or a copy to the client; and to note on the office copy that the notice had been sent to the client. The applicant said that the copy of the original final notice which was annexed to his affidavit did not bear a note of a copy having been sent to Mr. or Mrs. Serafini. It was suggested in argument that no inference could be drawn as to whether or not a copy had been sent or not sent to Mr. or Mrs. Serafini of this final notice. I consider that the inference to be drawn is that no such copy was sent. This would also be consistent with applicant's attitude at that time. Thus the file now included a cheque given him for the Taxation Department by Mrs. Serafini and a final notice for payment of her tax.

The applicant continued to retain the cheque and, as I find, did not advise Mrs. Serafini of the existence of the final notice; a finding to which I adhere despite the applicant having said in his affidavit that on or about 13 November 1981 he sent an account rendered to Mr. Serafini; and that it was possible that a copy of the final notice was enclosed. The matter reached a more acute stage in January 1982 when Mr. Serafini came to the office of the applicant in an excited state. The conversation which ensued is referred to in applicant's affidavit, part of which I quote -

"(Mr. Serafini) The girls forgot to pay the

cheque to the Tax Department.

I have been to the Department

and the cheque has not been

paid.

Applicant I did not forget the cheque."

From information in the s.37 documents it further appears a small debt

summons was issued and was served on her on 6 January 1982. By letter from the Board dated 3 June 1982, the applicant was advised of the complaint by Mrs. Serafini and of the summons. He was asked to furnish reasons as to why the Board should not cancel his registration pursuant to s.251K(2)(b)(i) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. No reference was made to the previous thirty convictions, s.251K(2)(b)(ii) or s.251(N) of that Act. Applicant's solicitors replied on 22 June 1982 conveying his opinion that he was being "persecuted" by the Board and that a "concerted attempt was being made by persons of whose identity he was unaware to prevent him from continuing to act as a registered tax agent."

For reasons mentioned in argument, I find this criticism completely unacceptable; no attempt has been made to support or justify it; and, in fact, counsel very fairly said he had no evidence which would support it. It is not necessary to refer to the rest of the letter which, in my opinion, failed to come to grips with the essence of this problem and provided no reason or excuse for the applicant's failure either to send the cheque to the Department or return it to Mrs. Serafini or to advise her of his inaction or of the receipt of the final notice.

The Board, at a meeting held on 8 July 1982 or thereabouts, as I have said, cancelled his registration and by letter dated 12 July 1982, advised the applicant thereof. In my opinion, on the material before them, which included reference to two abberations by the applicant, the decision of the Board was, with respect, justifiable. The letter said, inter alia -

"I have been directed by the Board to advise you that, after considering the contents of your solicitor's reply to that letter and bearing in mind the matters which were subject to consideration at the Board's meeting on 22 April 1982, your registration as a tax agent has been cancelled pursuant to the provisions of section 251K(2)(b)(1) and (11) of the Income

Tax Assessment Act.

It is explained that whilst the Board accepts your arguments that it is your right to terminate your agency at any time, provided proper notice is given to the principal, the Board was satisfied that in Mr. Serafini's case, proper notice was not given.

The Board is also of the view that by accepting the cheque, it was only reasonable for Mr. Serafini to assume that it would have been dealt with in the proper manner.

On the other hand, if, as your solicitors contend, you were not Mr. Serafini's agent at the time the cheque was handed over, then it should not have been accepted.

It is also explained that the complaint by Mrs. Serafini was received at the Board on 2 June 1982, thus it was not current when your conduct was examined by the Board on 22 April 1982.

Finally, the Board does not accept the view that their decision not to cancel your registration on 22 April 1982, despite having found you guilty of misconduct as a Tax Agent, is evidence that they consider you a fit and proper person to remain registered. The question of your fitness and propriety were not under consideration at that stage."

(Perhaps the reference in paragraph 3 should be a reference to Mrs. Serafini).

It will be noted that the registration was cancelled pursuant to s.251K(2)(b)(i) and (ii); whereas the letter of 3 June 1982 referred only to s.251K(2)(b)(i).

In the documents furnished to this Tribunal pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the Board stated that they were satisfied that as -

"(i) Mr. Perusco failed to properly deal with the sum of $1,832.45 in respect of Mr. Serafini, as the result of which a Small Debt summons

issued against him; and

(ii) he attempted, by the improper retention of these funds, to induce Mr. Serafini to pay his account in respect of professional services rendered; and

(iii) Mr. Perusco did not properly advise Mr. Serafini that he (Mr. Perusco) was no longer his agent, and further, that had he properly terminated his agency, he should not have either accepted or retained the funds;

(iv) he had been convicted of 30 offences against section 251N of the Income Tax Assessment Act and had already, on a previous occasion, been found by the Board, to be guilty in terms of section 251K(2)(b)(ii) of the Income Tax Assessment Act;

he had neglected the business of a principal and was guilty of misconduct as a Tax Agent and accordingly, decided to cancel his registration pursuant to section 251K(2)(b)(i) and (ii) of the Income Tax Assessment Act."

(I assume that in paragraph (i) above it is intended to refer to Mrs. Serafini).

There may be room to criticise, though perhaps only as a matter of expression, the inclusion of paragraph (iv) in this portion of their reasons since the applicant had not been asked to show cause on matters which are referred to in para.(iv) which had been the subject of earlier consideration. This is not to say that the Board, in consideration of the material in (i) to (iii) could not take account of the applicant's previous history. Having regard to the view which ultimately I take, it is not necessary finally to express an opinion on this aspect of the matter. But if the Board was of the view that the applicant's past history as to the 30 summonses was a matter to which some weight should be given in its deliberations on the Serafini case, as in my opinion it was entitled to be, I suggest it would have been more prudent, and certainly fairer, to have given some indication of this in the letter of 3 June 1982; i.e. to have drawn the applicant's attention to the existence of the recently formed view by the Board, expressed in its letter of 23 April 1982. Then if he saw fit he might, in showing cause, add anything thought to be appropriate additional to his previous submissions and what he stated as to the Serafini matter. I do not suggest that, in the final result, the applicant will have been disadvantaged; if there had been some new light to be thrown upon the indiscretions at Warrawong, he would have referred to it in this hearing. That nothing in that regard has been raised shows as it turns out, the reminder in the "show cause" letter of 3 June 1982 would have been unnecessary; but cf. Leary v. National Union of Vehicle Builders (1971) 1 Ch. 34 at e.g. p.48. In a somewhat analogous situation after an invitation to show cause where certain statutory powers were purportedly exercised not only on a ground referred to in the notice but also on another ground under a s.6(k), not specified in the notice, it was said by their Lordships in Board of Trustees of the Maradana Mosque v. Badiuddin Mahmud (1967) 1 A.C. 13 at p.24 -

"If, therefore, an imputed failure under section 6(k) can be shown to have played a material part in the Minister's decision, the appellants were not

fairly treated.

. . . . .

Whether it could be a valid answer to say that the appellants had in truth no defence, even if they had been given an opportunity of presenting it, need not be considered. That point was left open in Ridge v. Baldwin [1963] UKHL 2; (1964) A.C. 40."

cf. Lau Liat Meng v. Disciplinary Committee (1968) A.C. 391. However, these comments do not, as I have stated, represent any final view.

It is necessary to bear in mind that the function of this Tribunal is different from that of the Board. This is most conveniently referred to in Drake v. The Minister for Immigration & Ethnic Affairs (1979) 2 ALD 60 and, in particular, in the judgment of Bowen C.J. and Deane J. where it is said at p.68 -

"The question for the determination of the Tribunal is not whether the decision which the decision-maker made was the correct or preferable one on the material before him. The question for the determination of the Tribunal is whether that decision was the correct or preferable one on the material before the Tribunal."

The decision of the Tribunal is, of course, later in point of time than was the decision of the Board; and inevitably it is assisted with more evidence and information than was available to the Board. Further, I have had the advantage of seeing the applicant in person and hearing him give viva voce evidence and offer undertakings.

It is beyond doubt that if the deregistration is to stand, the hardship to the applicant and his family will be great, not the least portion of which is the loss of status in a community where he has obviously worked hard and has participated to the advantage of the community in its affairs quite beyond his own personal activity or advancement. Also, it would appear that he has a large clientele, built up over numbers of years. His present obligation would be to prepare some 1600 income tax returns. Such persons who would otherwise continue to seek his assistance will have to make other arrangements, involving for them considerable inconvenience, not to say, regret, that the person who has served them in a particular way over years may no longer be permitted to do so. The knowledge he has gained of their affairs would facilitate his continuing to prepare returns and this will have to be transferred to someone else.

The deterrent effect of such deregistration would, in my view, be considerable both on applicant and any Tax Agent who might be tempted to ignore the provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. There would, in my view, be not much chance, anyway, of a repetition of what undoubtedly, has been reprehensible conduct. There is little likelihood of a reoccurrence of the chain of events which occurred here; and I would expect by now the applicant has a much keener appreciation of his duty to the community, to his clients and to the Department. The officers of that Department, as Mr. Osborne's affidavit establishes, place great reliance on the work of tax agents including the applicant. It is pertinent also to bear in mind that there has been no adverse criticism of the applicant's ability and work as an agent except in so far as that may appear in the judgment of the learned judge of the District Court. And his practice has apparently been carried on for years, involving the preparation of many returns.

On the whole of the evidence, I have come to the conclusion that the decision to cancel the applicant's registration should be set aside.
