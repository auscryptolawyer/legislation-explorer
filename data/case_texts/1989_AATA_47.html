ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION - INCOME TAXATION – SUPERANNUATION FUNDS - SOLE PURPOSE OF SUPERANNUATION BENEFITS UPON RETIREMENT OR DEATH - power in trust deed to make interest free loans to members - whether a non-superannuation purpose of the fund

Words and Phrases : Superannuation benefits

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Commonwealth) ss 82AAC, 82AAR, 121DA, 121DAB

REASONS FOR DECISION

1 March 1989 Mr B J McMahon (Senior Member)

1. These 4 applications were heard together. The principal one relates to a fund established by a deed and entitled [Parent] Staff Provident Fund. Objections against the rate of taxation levied on the income of that fund for the years ended 30 June 1985 and 30 June 1986 were disallowed. The other 3 applications deal with claims made by related companies for deductions for contributions by them to the fund which were also disallowed. The outcome of the claims by the related companies will depend upon the outcome of the claims by the fund.

2. The claims by the related companies referred to the year ended 30 June 1986. The claims of the fund relate to the 2 years ended 30 June 1985 and 30 June 1986. During those years, the relevant sections of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act") that were applicable to the income of the fund and to deductions allowable to contributing companies were in the following terms -

"s121DAB The trustee of a superannuation fund that -

(a) is an indefinitely continuing fund established and maintained solely for either or both of the following purposes -

(i) the provision of superannuation benefits for each of the members of the fund in the event of the retirement of the member from any business, trade, profession, vocation, calling, occupation or employment in which he is engaged; and

(ii) the provision of superannuation benefits for dependants of each of the members of the fund in the event of the death of the member,

or for either or both of those purposes and for such incidental and ancillary purposes as the Commissioner approves; and

(b) is not a superannuation fund to which section 121CA, 121CB or 121CC applies in relation to the year of income,

shall be assessed and is liable to pay tax, at the rate declared by the Parliament for the purposes of this section, upon the amount remaining after deducting from the assessable income of the fund of the year of income, other than contributions to the fund, calculated as if the trustee were a taxpayer in respect of that income, all allowable deductions other than concessional deductions and deductions in respect of benefits.

........

s 121DA The trustee of a superannuation fund, other than a superannuation fund to which section 121CA, 121CB, 121CC or 121DAB applies in relation to the year of income, shall be assessed and is liable to pay tax, at the rate declared by the Parliament.

s 82AAC(1) Where a taxpayer, for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, an eligible employee, sets apart or pays in the year of income an amount or amounts as or to a fund or funds from which the benefits are to be provided, and the right of the employee or dependants to receive the benefits is fully secured, the amount or the sum of the amounts, as the case may be, so set apart or paid is, subject to the succeeding provisions of this Subdivision, an allowable deduction.

(2) A deduction is not allowable under this Subdivision in respect of an amount or amounts paid to a fund in a year of income, being the year of income commencing on 1 July 1984 or a subsequent year of income, if section 121DA applies to the fund in relation to the year of income.

........

s 82AAR A deduction is not allowable under any provision of this Act other than this Subdivision in respect of an amount set apart or paid by a taxpayer as or to a fund for the purpose of making provision for superannuation benefits for, or for dependants of, an employee or employees."

3. The relevant legislation was repealed at the end of the period in question and was replaced by a legislative scheme pursuant to which the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations were promulgated on 21 December 1987. Although these regulations have no bearing on the present matters for consideration, it is interesting to note that by Regulation 16, an investment standard is prescribed prohibiting loans by a fund to its members in exercise of a general power of investment.

  1. In the years in question, tax was payable at the rate of 46 per cent per annum on the income of a fund that complied with the requirements of s 121DAB. If it did not comply with those requirements, then the income was taxed under s 121DA at a rate of 60 per cent per annum. The principal question to be decided in this application, therefore, is whether the fund complied with the requirements of s 121DAB, thus entitling it to have its income taxed at the lower rate.
  2. The fund was established by deed dated 31 May 1960. I was informed that it was intended for "shop floor" employees and was not intended to benefit the more highly paid executives of the group. In 1985, the fund had 1,532 members. In 1986, there were 1,434.
  3. The deed and associated rules have been amended from time to time. During the time with which I am concerned, they were in the form of a document tendered as Exhibit C.
  4. The deed recites that "the company desires to establish a provident benefit or superannuation fund to be known as the [Parent] Staff Provident Fund for the purpose of providing fully secured individual personal benefits and retiring allowances for employees of the company and its associated companies having not less than 10 years' service and their dependants". The operative part of the deed then proceeds to establish a fund and to provide for contributions to the trustees by participating companies. No contributions were payable by members. Provision was made for the adoption of rules and for their legal effect. A copy of the then current rules is attached to Exhibit C.

8. Rule 7 provides for payment of benefits on retirement or retrenchment, Rule 8 provides for payment of benefits on death and Rule 9 provides for payment of benefits upon a member ceasing to be employed by any of the group of companies. None of these rules is controversial from a taxation point of view. Rule 10 provides that a member with certain length of service qualifications is entitled to withdraw sums of money from amounts standing to his credit with the fund, up to the limits set out in that rule. I was informed that because of certain decisions, particularly Constable v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1952] HCA 64; 86 CLR 402 and a decision of a Board of Review reported as Case S62 17 TBRD 357, Rule 10 had been inoperative since 1979. Prior to that time, figures shown in Exhibit E indicate that a small number of withdrawals ranging between $200 and $400 were periodically made by members up to 1979. It is not clear to me however, why the Rule can continue to be regarded as "inoperative" in its present form. It seems to me to give an entitlement to members provided they comply with the conditions precedent to that entitlement. The withdrawals (or advance payments) are payable during service. Being withdrawals, they are not repayable at all. They merely have the effect of reducing the credit balance of the applicant's account with the fund. There was no evidence that any such withdrawal was made during the 2 years with which I am presently concerned.

9. The main rule that affects the taxable status of the fund is Rule 11, which provides for loans during service. Its terms are as follows -

(ii) Each loan to a member who became a member prior to July 1, 1984 -

10. The final Rule that is relevant to these proceedings is Rule 20, which deals with the powers of investment and is in the following terms -

"Rule 20 Any moneys available for investment may be invested by the Trustees in their own names or in the names of any two or more of them or in the name of any Trustee or Nominee or other Company approved by the Board of Directors of the Company in or out of the Australian Capital Territory in any of the following investments -

(a) any investment authorised by the law of the Australian Capital Territory or of any State for the investment of trust-funds;

(b) any investment of any kind quoted on any Australian Stock Exchange;

(c) any investment of the kind generally known as a "Unit Trust" or in a share therein;

(d) on loan to any of the companies contributing to the fund.

In determining whether to make or dispose of any investment the Trustees shall have regard to the desirability of enabling the members to participate in the profitability and growth of the Company and [Parent] Limited and the Trustees shall at all times have regard to the desirability of ensuring the continuity of management of the Company and [Parent] Limited and the security of employment of members by the Company. The Trustees shall not make or dispose of any investment unless they are satisfied that the making or disposition of such investment does not in any way prejudice the profitability and growth of the Company and [Parent] Limited the continuity of management of the Company and [Parent] Limited or the employment of members by the Company."

11. Accounts for the fund were tendered in evidence as Exhibit D. In the relevant years, the fund's assets consisted almost entirely of shares in the parent company and loans to members together with current funds on call. In 1985, the market value of the net assets was $7,198,165 which included an amount of loans made to members, pursuant to Rule 11, of $798,270. In 1986, the corresponding figures were $8,887,184 and $930,361. Expressed as a percentage in each of those years of the amount of loans to the market value of the net assets, the figures were 11.09 per cent and 10.47 per cent respectively. Strictly speaking, interest free loans can not be regarded as investments. They yield nothing for the benefit of members generally. They must, however, be counted among the assets of the fund. The balance sheet shows them as a separate item.

12. Of the 1,532 members in 1985, 794 were entitled to loans under Rule 11 because of their length of service and 738 were not. Of the 794 members who were entitled, some 526 members had taken up their loans. In 1986, 572 members out of 819 entitled had taken up their loans. In other words, of those entitled, some 66.25% had taken up their loans in 1985 and 69.84 per cent in 1986. Because the loans are interest free and are therefore non-productive of income to the fund, it follows that those members who did not take up their loan entitlement would be at a disadvantage.

13. Since its inception, the fund has not sought exemption from taxation under s 23F or any other section of the Act. Accordingly, it has been subject to tax at rates which have varied with changes in the legislation over the years. There was evidence before me that in cases where funds seek exempt status under s 23F, they must comply with certain guidelines of the Commissioner concerning advances to members. Normally tax exempt status is preserved only if the rules, and the application of the rules, submit unsecured loans to members to two restrictions. Firstly they must be made only when the member is in difficult financial circumstances and is unable to obtain funds from normal commercial lending sources. Secondly they must be limited in amount to the particular member's contribution account balance. Evidence was also given that in those circumstances, the Commissioner was also prepared to countenance secured loans, such as housing loans, provided that they are made at commercial interest rates.

14. There is no definition in the Act of a superannuation fund. In Scott v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1966) 10 AITR 290 at 312 Windeyer J said -

"Considering the question in this way, and without my attention having been directed by counsel to any commonly accepted definition or to any standard form of constating document, I have come to the conclusion that there is no essential single attribute of a superannuation fund established for the benefit of employees except that it must be a fund bona fide devoted as its sole purpose to providing for employees who are participants money benefits (or benefits having a monetary value) upon their reaching a prescribed age. In this connection "fund", I take it, ordinarily means money (or investments) set aside and invested, the surplus income therefrom being capitalised. I do not put this forward as a definition but rather as a general description."

  1. Although the general observations of His Honour are still of use, the requirements of a fund enjoying the lower rate of taxation are now established by statute and it is to the terms of s 121DAB that one must look in order to establish whether those requirements have been met. In my view, the applicant has not established that the fund is established and maintained solely for the provision of superannuation benefits for each of the members. I have come to the conclusion that loans made under Rule 11 are not "superannuation benefits", and that the provision of these loans is one of the purposes for which the fund was established and is maintained.
  2. The fact that benefits other than superannuation benefits can be provided for in a bona fide fund was recognised in Mahony v Commissioner of Taxation (1967) 41 ALJR 232. Kitto J said in that case at 232 -

"There was no definition in the Act of "a provident, benefit or superannuation fund", and the meaning of the several expressions must therefore be arrived at in the light of ordinary usage and with only one piece of assistance to be gathered from the immediate context. Since a fund, if its income was to be exempt under the provision, was separately required to be one established for the benefit of employees, each of the three descriptive words "provident", "benefit" and "superannuation" must be taken to have connoted a purpose narrower than the purpose of conferring benefits, in a completely general sense, upon employees. Precise definition may be difficult, and in any case is unnecessary for present purposes. All that need be recognized is that just as "provident" and "superannuation" both referred to the provision of a particular kind of benefit - in the one case a provision against contemplated contingencies, and in the other case a provision, to arise on an employee's retirement or death or other cessation of employment, of a subvention for him or his estate or persons towards whom he may have stood in some kind of relation commonly giving rise to a legal or moral responsibility - so "benefit" must have meant a benefit, not in a general sense, but characterized by some specific future purpose. A funeral benefit is a familiar example."

In my view the loans to members are benefits other than superannuation benefits and the fund is maintained (and was probably established) partly for the purpose of providing those loans. The mere provision of benefits "in a completely general sense" is not sufficient to meet the sole statutory purpose test.

  1. "Purpose" is not here used in the trust law sense and is not to be confused with the object of any powers of investment. "Purpose" here refers to the design, plan or intendment of those who established and those who maintain the fund. Such purpose is established both from the initiating document and from the manner of maintenance.
  2. It was submitted by counsel for the applicant that in the context of the administration of this scheme, superannuation benefits should be regarded only as those payable upon the contingencies referred to in Rules 7, 8 and 9. They should be restricted to those payments made upon the termination of the association between the employer company and the member, for whatever reason. Loans could not be so regarded. Although benefits are payable irrespective of whether loans are repaid, I was informed that in fact any outstanding loans are deducted from any entitlement moneys when matters are finally settled between the fund and the employee. Rule 11 itself, of course, precipitates a liability to repay in certain circumstances, whether or not the member actually tenders repayment. One would expect that, for convenience of bookkeeping, both parties would prefer that the liability be satisfied by deduction. However, this is an argument, in my view, that is contrary to the applicant's interest. If in fact the provision of loans on beneficial terms is a benefit separate and distinct from superannuation benefits, and it can be said that the provision of these loans is one of the purposes of the fund then the applicant fund fails to meet the "solely" test. To draw a distinction between moneys paid under Rule 11 and moneys paid under Rules 7, 8 and 9, simply illustrates this.

18. The fund must be not only established, but also must be maintained solely for the provision of superannuation benefits. As an alternative argument, the applicant submitted that the loans could be looked upon as interim payments on account of benefits because of the fact that in practice they were repaid on termination of the association between the fund and the member. Having regard to the terms of the rule, the circumstances of entitlement, and the widespread practice of taking down loans whilst still remaining in the employment of the contributing company, I do not agree that this is a correct characterisation of the payments. The moneys are clearly described as loans repayable in certain circumstances. The fact that repayment is effected through deduction from benefits does not alter the character of these moneys from loans to benefits.

19. Three aspects are relevant when considering whether the fund is maintained solely for the statutory purposes. The requirement to have regard to maintenance means that it is proper here to take into account matters outside the terms of the deed and rules. In the decision at first instance of Owen J in Mahoney (so spelt in the report) 39 ALJR 62 at 63 His Honour said

"There remains a further matter which the appellants must establish affirmatively. Was the fund, during the years in question, being applied for the purpose for which it was established, that is to say for the purpose of benefiting employees? In considering that question I think it is permissible to look at the circumstances leading up to the establishment of the fund, the terms of the deed under which it was managed and controlled, the use made of the powers and discretions conferred by the deed, the extent to which employees received benefits from the fund and the extent to which it was used to benefit persons who were not employees. Nor is the inquiry limited, in my opinion, to an investigation of the manner in which the fund was applied during the particular years of income with which those appeals are concerned since the purpose for which it was used in other years may throw light on the question whether during the years ending 30th June, 1957 and 1958, the fund was in a real sense being applied for the purpose of benefiting employees."

20. A consideration of the facts supports the view that the provision of other benefits "in a completely general sense" is a purpose of the fund. Firstly, some 2 thirds of the members have taken out their loan entitlement. Indeed the entitlement is so designed that the remaining one third continue to be disadvantaged. One can only speculate that their failure to take up their entitlement must be due to ignorance of the consequences. Widespread though they may be, therefore, the full extent of the loans do not coincide with the potential superannuation benefits. Not all those entitled to the latter have taken up the former. For this reason alone, loans could not be regarded as advance payments of superannuation benefits.

  1. Secondly, members are entitled as of right to the loans provided they meet the length of service qualifications and provided they have not overdrawn their credit limits. No discretion is vested in the trustees. This is a right that is clearly conferred by the rules and must be an attractive feature for members. The entitlement is in no way related to retirement or even to achieving any particular age. Almost by definition, a benefit must be related to the passing above a certain number of years before it can be properly described as a superannuation benefit. The etymological derivation of superannuation is in the phrase "supra annos" (literally translated from the Latin as "beyond years"). These loans, for that reason can not be regarded as superannuation benefits. Their supposed attractiveness leads, on the probabilities, to an inference of a non statutory purpose.
  2. Thirdly, the provision of loans, free of interest, on an unsecured basis to members who have not demonstrated an inability to raise money from orthodox financial sources distinguishes this fund from other funds and would thus make it again more attractive to members. Combined with the continued existence of Rule 10 (although inoperative) it provides a range of benefits which must be regarded (despite some of the expert evidence tendered) as unusual in normal industrial superannuation funds. The attraction of the fund for members explains why the fund is maintained partly to provide the loan programme.

23. In any event, I am not satisfied that the fund is established and maintained solely for the provision of benefits for members, however the benefits may be classified. There seem to me to be extrinsic purposes. There may not be a direct benefit to the company from the operation of the fund, as there was in Driclad Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1968] HCA 91; (1968) 121 CLR 45 where funds were loaned back to the company, or in Compton v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1966] HCA 1; (1966) 116 CLR 233, where the company's future obligations to pay long service leave could be recouped out of the fund. It appears to me however, that there is a benefit to the "continuity of management", arising out of the way in which the fund is maintained. It appears from Rule 20 that the Trustees must have regard to "continuity of management" of the company when exercising their powers of investment or (probably more importantly) when exercising their powers of disposition of investments. One may speculate that the intention of this rule is to preserve the position of existing executives within the company and to ensure that they have an ally in the trustees of the fund in the event of any unsolicited takeover offer. Certainly the trustees have to have regard to the rule in exercising their powers of investment. As previously noted, almost all of the investments of the fund consist of shares in the parent company. The fact that this injunction to the trustees appears in the rules indicates to me that it was one of the purposes for which the fund was established. It may be an incidental purpose, or a purpose not of great importance compared with the main purpose of establishing a fund for the benefit of members. Nevertheless it is a purpose, and therefore its existence precludes a finding that the only purpose of establishment and maintenance of the fund is the sole purpose stipulated by the statute.

24. As an alternative submission, counsel for the applicant invited me to find that purposes related to the loans were "incidental and ancillary" and that I, standing in the shoes of the Commissioner, should approve them. In my view, the argument is not open to the applicant. The provisions of the additional sentence in sub-paragraph 121DAB(a)(ii) are available only if it is established that the requirements of either sub-paragraph (i) or sub-paragraph (ii) have been established. The additional words do not detract from the opening words of the paragraph that the fund must be "established and maintained solely for either or both of the following purposes". The use of the word "and" in the additional words makes this clear. I am not free to find that if the fund is not established and maintained for either or both of the statutory purposes, then another related purpose that may be regarded as incidental and ancillary would suffice. The applicant must first establish that the fund complies with the "solely" test. It is only then that additional incidental or ancillary purposes may be considered.

  1. Counsel for the applicant made a number of submissions on the question whether the loans could be regarded as incidental and ancillary to superannuation benefits. He submitted that the loans could be regarded as an interim payment of benefits (with which, as I have indicated, I disagree), that they were not uncommon in other similar superannuation funds and that they were made only to members of the Fund. In my view, none of these arguments can be maintained. Unless the fund meets the "solely" test, the incidental and ancillary aspect can not arise for consideration. The incidental and ancillary purposes must be subservient or subordinate to the only statutory purposes for which the fund is established or maintained.
  2. I have concluded that as the fund does not comply with the provisions of s 121DAB it must be taxed at the rate provided for under s 121DA. It follows that because of s 82AAC (2), a deduction for contributions is not allowable to the contributing companies, the other 3 applicants. Accordingly, the objection decisions under review are affirmed in all 5 applications.
