Dundovich and Commissioner of Taxation (Practice and procedure) [2026] ARTA 846 (19 May 2026)

Last Updated: 21 May 2026

Dundovich and Commissioner of Taxation (Practice and procedure) [2026] ARTA 846 (19 May 2026)

Applicant/s: Kaine Dundovich

Respondent: Commissioner of Taxation

Tribunal Number: 2025/3746

Tribunal: General Member Rebecca Smith

Place: Melbourne

Date: 19 May 2026

Decision: The Tribunal dismisses the reinstatement application

Statement made on 19 May 2026 at 3:44pm

........................................................................

General Member Rebecca Smith

Catchwords

PRACTICE AND PROCEDURE – taxation – proceedings dismissed under ss. 99 and 100 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act – application for reinstatement – failure to comply with direction – failure to attend case event – application dismissed.

Legislation

Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) ss. 9, 56, 99,100, 102

Statement of Reasons

INTRODUCTION

  1. On 14 May 2025, Mr Kaine Dundovich (Mr Dundovich or Applicant) filed an application for review of the Commissioner of Taxation’s (Commissioner or Respondent) decision to disallow in part an objection to an amended assessment for the 2023 income year.
  2. On 30 January 2026, Deputy President Lazanas dismissed the application for review pursuant to s. 100 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) (ART Act), having been satisfied the Applicant had failed within a reasonable time to proceed with the application and comply with an order of the Tribunal in relation to the application.
  3. In this regard, the Applicant had failed to comply with the Tribunal’s directions dated 5 December 2025 which required Mr Dundovich to file and serve a Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions and any evidence upon which he sought to rely by 23 January 2026. Further, the Applicant had failed to attend the case event scheduled for 30 January 2026, which was a conciliation conference.
  4. A copy of the Tribunal's decision to dismiss the application was provided to the parties on 30 January 2026.

REINSTATEMENT APPLICATION

  1. On 26 February 2026 the Applicant wrote to the Tribunal seeking a reinstatement of his application. The communication explained:
    A report was filed due to a phishing threat to personal information. The case could not proceed until the report was investigated for privacy and personal online safety. As stated previously multiple times.

    The report number and Release of information have been provided to the art review Tribunal submission page for specific case reference.

    in which case, has been wrongfully handled, dismissed and is in the best interest to be rectified.

  2. The Tribunal listed the reinstatement application for an interlocutory hearing for 17 April 2026. A listing notice was sent to the parties on 26 March 2026.
  3. On 26 March 2026 and in in response to the listing notice sent to the parties, Mr Dundovich sent an email to the Tribunal and the Respondent in the following terms:
    you're joking right? I have not received the investigation report or the commissioners documented reasonings opposing a reinstatement.
  4. On 31 March 26, the Applicant sent a further email which stated:
    A report had already been filed for investigation. I am still waiting to receive the report. I'm also still waiting to receive the documented reasoning from the commissioners party as to why they do not wish to proceed with a reinstatement application, which is roughly two weeks overdue.

    They have organised a tribunal date without providing any of this documentation or asking about my availability, which is highly unprofessional, and, frankly impossible for me to attend without having these documents.

    Formal complaints have been filed, and an online form has been processed. Do not expect me to attend until all concerns have been addressed.

  5. On 8 April 2026 the Tribunal sent an email to the parties in the following terms:
    we refer to the Applicants email regarding the listing on 17 April 2026. We confirm that the matter was listed by the Tribunal following receipt of the Applicants reinstatement application. We note that the application is opposed however the question of whether reinstatement is appropriate in all the circumstances is a matter for the Tribunal to determine. If either party has any concerns regarding their availability to attend the hearing, those concerns should be raised with the Tribunal as a matter of priority. We confirm the parties will be provided with sufficient opportunity to advance their respective positions prior to the Tribunal determining the application.
  6. On 13 April 2026, the Applicant email the Tribunal and the Respondent. Included in the email was an exert relating to a freedom of information request with the Victorian Police and an email from the Tribunal complaints team. The Applicant also stated:

you're misinterpreting the situation and implied severity.

I provided correspondence regarding this matter below - You will be contacted by me once I have all relevant information.

  1. On 17 April 2026, the interlocutory hearing took place. When the Applicant was not in the lobby of the MS Teams hearing at the commencement time, the Tribunal Officer tried to contact the Applicant on the mobile phone number provided to the Tribunal. The call went to a message service. The hearing proceeded in the absence of the Applicant. Although the Respondent sought dismissal of the reinstatement application, the Tribunal declined to make that order and instead put in place some directions to allow Mr Dundovich one further opportunity to file any material in support of the reinstatement application. The hearing was adjourned.
  2. As a result of an administrative oversight, the directions were not distributed to the parties in a timely manner. Once the error was identified, the Tribunal reviewed and amended the directions to ensure all parties had a proper opportunity to comply with the filing requirements before the next case event. The Tribunal direction are repeated below:
    The Tribunal DIRECTS:
  3. On or before 8 May 2026, the Applicant must give to the Tribunal and the Respondent any evidence and submissions upon which they intend to rely in respect of the reinstatement application.
  4. On or before 13 May 2026, the Respondent may give to the Tribunal and the Applicant any further evidence and submissions upon which they intend to rely in respect of the reinstatement application.

    The Tribunal NOTES:

    The Applicant has made an application seeking reinstatement of his application for review.

    The Applicant failed to join the interlocutory hearing on 17 April 2026 (which was conducted by MS Teams) at the scheduled time. The Tribunal called the Applicant on the mobile phone number provided to the Tribunal but the call went to a message service. The hearing proceeded in the absence of the Applicant.

    The Respondent requested the Tribunal dismiss the reinstatement application. The Tribunal declined to dismiss the reinstatement application and decided instead to give one final opportunity to the Applicant to provide any evidence and submissions in support of his application. The directions made by the Tribunal reflect this.

    Should the Applicant fail to comply with the directions of the Tribunal or fail to attend the next case event, the reinstatement application may be dismissed without further notice.

    The proceeding is listed for an interlocutory hearing to be conducted by MS Teams on 15 May 2026 at 11:30am AEST.

  5. The listing notice and the directions were sent to the parties on 28 April 2026. In the covering email, the Tribunal noted:
    Should there be any difficulty with the next hearing date, namely 15 May 2026 at 11:30am AEST, the parties should contact the Tribunal urgently. Parties are reminded that if a party fails to attend the next listed case event, the matter may be dismissed without further notice.
  6. In response to the communication from the Tribunal, the Applicant replied ‘No, some free advice is remain in contact with your own departments before contacting me and stop disrupting an ongoing investigation’. Included in the response was an email from the Tribunal related to a complaints investigation.
  7. On 17 April 2026, the interlocutory hearing took place. When the Applicant was not in the lobby of the MS Teams hearing at the commencement time, the Tribunal Officer tried to contact the Applicant on the mobile phone number provided to the Tribunal. The call went to a message service. The hearing proceeded in the absence of the Applicant.
  8. The Tribunal decided to dismiss the application for reinstatement, and confirmed short written reasons would follow.

CONSIDERATION

  1. Section 9 of the ART Act sets out the Tribunal’s objectives which includes that applications to the Tribunal are resolved as quickly, and with as little formality and expense, as the proper consideration of the matters before the Tribunal permits. To achieve this objective, it is important for the Tribunal to actively manage the progress of cases.
  2. Section 56 of the ART Act requires that parties and their representatives conduct proceedings before the Tribunal in a manner that supports the Tribunal to achieve its objectives.
  3. Section 99 of the ART Act provides Tribunal may dismiss application if Applicant does not appear and the Tribunal is satisfied that the Applicant received appropriate notice of the date, time and place of the Tribunal case.
  4. Section 100 of the ART Act allows the Tribunal to dismiss an application if the Applicant fails to either proceed with an application or comply with the Act or an order of the Tribunal in relation to the proceeding, in a reasonable time.
  5. Section 102 of the ART Act sets out the circumstances in which an application may be reinstated. Relevantly, Subsection (2) permits the Tribunal to reinstate an application on its own initiative if satisfied the application was dismissed in error. Subsection (7) also provides reinstatement for an application that has been dismissed under section 100.
  6. There is nothing before the Tribunal to suggest, nor any evidence establishing, that the application was dismissed in error. Further, the basis up on which the Applicant seeks reinstatement remains unclear. Mr Dundovich has not provided any substantive material in support of the reinstatement application, despite a direction to do so, nor has the Applicant attended two interlocutory hearings to provide any further context or explanation.
  7. The communications received from Mr Dundovich suggest that he does not intend to engage further with the Tribunalprocess until a separate complaint has been finalised. However, it is not apparent on the material before me how that complaint is connected to the dismissal decision or the application for reinstatement. In those circumstances, the Tribunal is not in a position to meaningfully engage with, or assess the merits of the reinstatement application.
  8. The Tribunal did consider whether there would be any utility in granting a further adjournment to allow one final opportunity for Mr Dundovich to engage with the reinstatement application. However, having regard to the Applicants conduct and communications following the reinstatement application, I was not satisfied that if further time was provided, or another case event scheduled, that Mr Dundovich would file any material or explain the basis of the reinstatement application in any event.
  9. The Applicant has been afforded a reasonable opportunity to present his case and to provide submissions and evidence in support of the reinstatement application, but has declined to do so. In those circumstances, granting a further adjournment would be inconsistent with the objects in s. 9 of the ART Act.
  10. I am satisfied it is appropriate for the reinstatement application to be dismissed. In the circumstances of this matter, I am empowered to dismiss the application pursuant to:
    • s. 99 of the ART Act, on account of the failure of Mr Dundovich to attend the interlocutory hearing on 15 May 2026, after being given appropriate notice of the case event; and
    • s. 100 of the ART Act on account of the failure of Mr Dundovich to comply with the Tribunal’s directions dated 29 April 2026, to file any material in support of the reinstatement application by 8 May 2026, together with the Applicant’s failure to progress the reinstatement application since it was filed on 26 February 2026.

DECISION

  1. The application for reinstatement is dismissed.
