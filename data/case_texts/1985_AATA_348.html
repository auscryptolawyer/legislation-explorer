Re Arnold Bloch Leibler and Company and Australian Taxation Office (No2) [1985] AATA 348 (10 December 1985)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: ARNOLD BLOCH LEIBLER AND COMPANY

And: AUSTRALIAN TAXATION OFFICE (No.2)

No. V83/198

Freedom of Information

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.K Todd (Deputy President)

Sir Ernest Coates (Member)

Mrs H.E. Hallowes (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information - tax office investigation manuals

- disclosure would disclose investigative methods - effect of voluntary release of exempt documents

Freedom of Information Act 1982, ss.9, 14, 34, 36, 37, 38, 40, 45, 58

Re Downie and Department of Territories (No.A85/3, 13 November 1985)

Re Mann and Australian Taxation Office (No.A84/76, 14 June 1985)

Re Swiss Aluminium and Commissioner of Taxation & Ors (No.N84/539, 10 September 1985)

HEARING

CANBERRA

10:12:1985

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

This application seeks review of a decision of the respondent agency refusing to the applicants access to a number of documents, or granting such access only with deletions. The request was in the following terms:

"1. Manuals, Guides and other like documents used

by Officers in the examination of returns and

assessments of income tax.

2. Manuals, Guides and other like documents used

by Officers in considering and treating

Objections and Applications for Appeal and

Review by Taxpayers.

3. Manuals, Guides and other like documents used

by Officers in the investigation of Taxpayers'

affairs.

4. Manuals, Guides and other like documents used

by Officers in the auditing of Taxpayers'

records."

2. After some exchanges of correspondence, the position adopted was that the applicants were informed that copies of Handbooks for use by Taxation Officers in the examination and assessment of income tax returns and in the consideration and treatment of objections and appeals relating thereto were in the course of preparation and would be made available for inspection and purchase in accordance with the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the FOI Act") as soon as possible. In relation to manuals and guides used in the investigation of taxpayers' affairs, access to which had at first been refused in toto, it was ultimately decided to grant access to a manual entitled "Investigations - Training and Reference Text" (including some other documentss referred to below) subject to deletion. Reliance was placed for this purpose on ss.37(1)(a), 37(1)(b), 37(2)(b), 38, 40(1)(c), (d) and (e), and 45. As to the fourth subject of the request, it was stated that officers engaged on audit duties rely on techniques and procedures set down in the investigation manual and appropriate assessing handbooks, that some preliminary work had been undertaken with a view to producing a separate handbook for use by such officers, but that such a document would not be available for some time.

3. As well as the "Investigation - Training and Reference Text", which we shall call "the Text", the respondent also treated a number of "Investigation Information Bulletins", referred to as "IIBs", as being the subject of the request. It is with the Text and the IIBs that this review is concerned. The Text is amended periodically. However, pending publication of amendments, officers of the Branch are kept informed of changes in procedures etc by means of IIBs. Instructions contained in IIBs up to and including IIB 7/1981 had been incorporated in the original issue. IIBs issuing since that date have been incorporated at the front of the Text. Three of these were claimed to be wholly exempt, the other seven have been released with deletions. Access to much of the Text has been granted. It was stated that 90 percent of the material overall had been released.

4. The application for review was heard a considerable time ago. The Tribunal was requested to hear it in conjunction with another application for review (No.V84/47) lodged by the same applicants and likewise seeking review of a decision to refuse access to a number of documents. These latter documents related to what has come to be known as "negative gearing". In that application for review a certificate was issued by the Treasurer under s.34(2) and s.36(3) of the FOI Act in relation to certain of the documents.

5. After the conclusion of the hearing, though no hint that we detected had been given of this intention at the hearing itself, the applicants indicated that "requests (were) being made for reasons for the decisions to issue and not to issue Certificates under Sub-sections 36(3) and 34(2) of the Freedom of Information Act, 1982. These requests are being made pursuant to Sub-section 13(1) of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act, 1977". The Tribunal put the matters aside at this point. No more has been heard of this by the Tribunal. Perhaps the proceedings in V84/47 have, like "negative gearing" itself, begun to fade into the mists of history. At all events the applicants eventually requested the Tribunal to proceed to determine the present application on its own.

6. The fact is that this application relates to a wholly distinct dispute from that in the "negative gearing" case and to that extent the request was a reasonable one. But it is equally true that the two applications were heard together, with submissions and evidence alternating and being intermingled as if both cases were one. In the result, when combined with what occurred in application V84/47 and with the request to deal separately with the present application for review, the procedure that evolved could hardly have been more confusing. It has contributed to the delay that has occurred, and the Tribunal regrets its agreement with the proposal that the applications be heard together. As presently constituted, it would not again concur in such a proposal, in FOI Act matters at least.

7. Turning now to the present application for review, the documents in question, which were provided to the Tribunal for inspection by it, may be referred to briefly as follows:

(a) The Training Text

The subjects covered by this were as follows:

The subjects covered by this were as follows:

As stated above, reliance was placed by the respondent for the many claims of exemption made in respect of the Text upon ss.37(1)(a), 37(1)(b), 37(2)(b), 38, 40(1)(c), (d) and (e), and 45.

(c) The IIBs

(i) IIB 9/1983 (claimed to be wholly exempt under

ss.37(1)(a) and 37(2)(b)). This dealt with a system

containing records of cases being investigated or

about to be investigated for breach or suspected

breach of the taxation law.

(ii) IIB 8/1983 (partial exemption claimed under

ss.37(2)(b), 40(1)(c), (d) and (e), and 45). This

related to the topic "Access to Books, Records etc -

Bank Enquiries".

(iii) IIB 7/1983 (exemption claimed for names of officers

only under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e)). This related to

the topic "Tax Evasion - Detection and Reference of

Cases for Potential Prosecution Action".

(iv) IIB 6/1983 (partial exemption claimed under ss.38,

40(1)(c), (d) and (e)). This was headed "s.264

Notices - Repeated Failure to Attend - Injunctive

Relief".

(v) IIB 5/1983 (exemption claimed for name of officer

only under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e)). This was headed

"Daily Work Report - Four Weekly Summary".

(vi) IIB 4/1982 (claimed to be wholly exempt under

ss.37(1)(b), 37(2)(b), 38 and 45). This was a

document the sensitivity of which was such that even

evasion had reached sufficiently sophisticated

proportions to involve overseas transactions and that

the Commissioner needed some methods to deal with the

problem of evidence relating to overseas matters.

This is set out here not as proof of the facts

alleged by counsel, if proof were needed, but as an

acceptable indication of the scope of the document.

(vii) IIB 3/1982 (exemption claimed for names of officers

only under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e)). This document

dealt with "Manpower Usage Monitoring System", given

the engaging acronym "MUMS".

(viii) IIB 2/1982 (exemption claimed for names of officers

only under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e)). This was a

document with attachments constituted by memoranda in

relation to administration of the Crimes (Taxation

Offences) Act 1980.

(ix) IIB 1/1982 (claimed to be wholly exempt under

ss.37(1)(b), 37(2)(b), 38, 40(1)(c), (d) and (e), and

45, and possibly also s.37(1)(a)). This was a summary

of some sources of information available to the

Commissioner for the purpose of investigating

suspected breaches of the law and was claimed to be

wholly exempt.

(x) IIB 8/1981 (exemption claimed for names of officers

only under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e)). This document

related to Computer Audit Courses and other matters.

8. Evidence was given on affidavit and orally by Mr N.W. Hunt, Acting Assistant Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (Compliance Branch), Income Tax, of the Australian Taxation Office in Victoria. Mr Hunt's duties in that position involve the exercise of executive control over the Compliance Branch of the Australian Taxation Office in Victoria (hereinafter called "the Branch") and the determination of questions of law and policy at the State level in relation to the work of the Branch. The function of the Branch as stated by Mr Hunt in his affidavit is as follows:

"a. To undertake audits and examinations of the

financial affairs of taxpayers in cases of

known or suspected failure to comply with the

taxation laws; and in relation to cases

involving international issues; determine

liability and obtain evidence to raise and

support assessments and amended assessments

before a Court or Board of Review or to

substantiate charges against persons for

breaches of the law. Where appropriate extend

these investigations for the purpose of

recovering outstanding taxes.

b. To conduct research to determine the

appropriate fields of investigation activity

and greatest economical coverage of the

taxpayer population; and research in relation

to the operation of known schemes for the

avoidance of tax and research into new schemes

or arrangements disclosed in the course of

other activities.

c. To undertake particular audits in cases of tax-

payers known or suspected to be involved in tax

avoidance schemes and arrangements; prepare

appropriate reports and recommendations; liaise

with other Branch Offices in respect of these

cases and to prepare guidelines for other areas

of the office to assist in the identification

and effective handling of schemes cases.

d. To deal with tax clearance applications for

Reserve Bank Exchange Control approval."

9. Mr Hunt stated that the Text explained methods and procedures employed in carrying out routine functions of the Branch, and also contained information on associated matters to assist officers in their duties. The training section was, he said, designed to provide a guide to the basic methods involved in conducting an examination of a taxpayer's affairs from commencement to finalization, while the reference section dealt with particular aspects of examinations and ancillary Branch functions. Each officer to whom a copy of the Text is issued is responsible for its safe custody. The Text is, and at all times remains, the property of the Australian Taxation Office, and an officer is not permitted to remove a copy from the Branch without the express approval of a Director. On leaving the Branch on transfer, promotion, retirement, resignation, etc, an officer must return his copy of the Text to his Supervisor. There are thus restrictions on the availability of, and thus on access to, the Text even within the Australian Taxation Office.

10. The Tribunal has had great difficulty with the basis of the case put by the Commissioner. So far as we are aware, the respondent has not proposed the preparation of a discrete manual on the topic of investigations for inspection and purchase pursuant to the provisions of s.9 of the FOI Act, the course proposed in relation to the matters referred to in items 1, 2 and 4 of the request for access. What has thus occurred is the formation on the part of the respondent of a preparedness to release much of the Text, a document prepared for internal purposes in relation to investigations, and parts of a number of IIBs.

11. The document as a whole, including the IIBs was, in our opinion, entitled to be regarded as exempt within s.37(2)(b) of the FOI Act:

"37(2) A document is an exempt document if its

disclosure under this Act, would, or could

reasonably be expected to -

(a) ....

(b) disclose lawful methods or procedures for

preventing, detecting, investigating, or

dealing with matters arising out of, breaches

or evasions of the law the disclosure of

which, would, or would be reasonably likely

to, prejudice the effectiveness of those

methods or procedures;"

12. The conduct of investigations, in a climate of persistent evasion of income tax and noncompliance with the income tax law at almost every level of the income earning community, must be regarded as a crucial weapon against such evasion and noncompliance. In this connection we refer to the evidence of Mr Hunt, in particular to that given by him in connection with the incidence of tax evasion. Mr Hunt contended in his affidavit that enforcement of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 embraces detection, identification and quantification of omissions of income, overstated deductions and other matters, and that it is inherent in the necessary procedures that the credibility of a taxpayer will be required to be tested and that tests be conducted outside the ambit of a taxpayer's records. Mr Hunt said, and perusal of the document confirms, that material in the Text claimed to be exempt explains methods and procedures employed in conducting an examination and contains extensive information concerning methods, procedures, techniques and guidelines to be employed by compliance officers in the performance of their duties. It also contained, he said, material relating to methods and practices adopted to avoid income tax or to prevent detection of breaches of the taxation law and material relating to sources of information which may assist in detecting evasion or breaches of the law. It was claimed, by Mr Hunt, that disclosure or publication of material of the above nature would "benefit those attempting to violate the law and avoid detection, and would enable less ethical advisers to develop systems aimed at evasion or avoidance of tax and the detection thereof". These statements were made in Mr Hunt's affidavit in relation to the material claimed to be exempt. As appears below we are unable, for reasons there stated, to see how they do not apply likewise to the whole of the documents the subject of the relevant request.

13. Mr Hunt also referred to the 62nd Report of the Commissioner of Taxation, 1982/1983, Parliamentary Paper No.43/1983 and stated that, putting on one side tax avoidance schemes of an artificial nature and trust stripping operations in the nature of actual evasion, in the year ended 30 June 1983 examined in the field of general audits 72 percent of cases resulted in adjustments, and 78 percent of special examination cases likewise. We could be pardoned for thinking that there has in recent years been something like a state of war between the Commissioner (who represents not his interests but the interests of taxpayers) and those who would set the taxation system at nought or at least at very little.

14. As counsel for the respondent observed, much of what is in the Text is probably known to, or is suspected by, many persons, who might be familiar with the techniques that are elaborated in the documents. But it is not to be assumed that every person who might be minded to offend against the taxation laws is so sophisticated. To the extent that persons are not aware of the techniques used, then investigations will be that much more effective and the rearrangement of affairs, or the reshaping in advance of answers to questions that may be asked, that much less effective.

15. In our view the entire Text, including the IIBs, was entitled to be regarded as exempt under s.37(2)(b). It runs the gamut of all of the activities and procedures that investigation officers must carry out or follow for the purpose of conducting investigations. It includes reference to the preparatory steps that need to be taken, to the preparation for the conduct of interviews and other modes of enquiry, to the keeping of records and to the making of reports. The respondent would in our view have been perfectly entitled to take the high ground and to contend that the whole document was exempt. This indeed was the stance originally adopted. It was only later decided to release what was described as some 90 percent of the documents.

16. From this it follows that those portions of the Text and of the IIBs in respect of which the respondent maintains its claim of exemption ought themselves prima facie to be regarded as exempt. But the situation is more complicated than that. The fact that so much of the Manual has been disclosed calls into question the basis of the prima facie position that the entire Manual is exempt. The grant of access by an agency does not however necessarily involve a conclusion that there should be any lack of confidence on the part of the Tribunal that a ground of exemption should be supported. It may often equally be concluded that, to the extent that disclosure has occurred, it has so occurred under the aegis of the encouragement offered by s.14. Yet we were assured by counsel for the respondent in this case that this was not the position here. We were told that the release that eventually occurred was based on a decision that the released portions were not exempt.

17. In Re Downie and Department of Territories (A85/3, 13 November 1985) the Tribunal said:

"20. It is common in reviews by the Tribunal of

decisions under the Act to find that out of a

series of related documents some have been released

upon request, while some have been made the subject

of claims of exemption, in whole or in part, either

at the point of an initial decision to refuse

access or upon internal review. By the time of the

hearing, or even during the hearing, further

releases, again either in whole or in part, may

have been agreed to be made. Laudable as this may

be, the difficulty that arises is that whereas the

totality of the documents may arguably have been

made the subject of a persuasive claim for

exemption, notably where s.36 is involved, the

release of some or all of the documents, in whole

or in part, may in some circumstances tend to

modify the integrity of that claim. In some

instances, as for instance where the document is

one discrete, albeit substantial, document in

respect of which a persuasive claim of exemption

might have been made, it will be possible to say

that release by the agency of greater or lesser

portions of it occurred pursuant to the avenue of

encouragement offered by s.14 of the Act rather

than pursuant to the formation by the agency of a

conclusion that the material is not after all

exempt. Where this occurs, the whole document

retains, for the purpose of analysis, its exempt

character, and that which has not been released

remains exempt, notwithstanding release of the

remainder."

18. The document in issue in this case is in our opinion in the latter class as described in this citation. The difficulty that arises here is different from that in Re Downie, where broad considerations of public interest under s.36(1)(b) of the FOI Act were in issue in relation to documents constituting deliberative processes of government. The present document by contrast is a document representing the armory of a Department of State apparently involved in a near combative role as described previously. A problem nevertheless arises, in that while some of the information claimed to be exempt is extremely sensitive material which, in our view, would have to be regarded as exempt under s.37(2)(b) on the most restrictive interpretation of the ambit of the exemption there conferred, other material not released is, to a greater or less extent, close to the margin of the delineation of its exempt character if that margin is to be set in terms dictated by what has been released. The release of so much material that, taken on its own, is seemingly innocuous, tends to create a suspicion that at least some additional material left by the respondent on the other side of the margin must also be innocuous. If the delineation arrived at in that manner be accepted, minute inspection sentence by sentence, if not line by line, of a mass of material would be required in order to decide whether the information in question was, as compared with that released, properly to be regarded as exempt. This exercise has been attempted by us. The more the attempt has been pursued the less satisfactory has it appeared, and in particular the less has it seemed to represent the function of review reposed in the Tribunal by s.58(1) of the FOI Act, under which section the Tribunal "has power ... to review any decision that has been made ... in respect of the request and to decide any matter in relation to the request that, under this Act, could have been or could be decided by an Agency or Minister ...".

19. It seems clear to us that the Tribunal's function is not necessarily adequately, or even properly, exercised by its adopting a piecemeal approach whereby it simply picks up for inspection what remains after what are really bits and pieces of documents have been released by the relevant agency in what we can only identify as a demonstration of a spirit of generosity. Well intentioned as that may seem, it can and has in this case in our view led to a patchwork and possibly distorted release. It would have been much preferable for the agency to have released, under s.9, a manual constructed for the purpose. The document here in issue could then have remained what it was, and for what it was surely prepared to be, namely an internal manual of instruction to be used in the highly complicated and difficult area of the investigation of tax evasion and noncompliance with the income tax law. It is no less such because much of the material is, as the released portions show, quite innocuous. Great resources are apparently devoted, amidst much diminished standards of conscience, to tax evasion and noncompliance, and we see no ground for the application of any restrictive interpretation of s.37(2)(b) in the context. Our primary conclusion is therefore that the whole of the documents the subject of the request, notwithstanding the releases, should be regarded as exempt, with the necessary consequence following that the documents and portions of documents still the subject of claims of exemption are in our opinion exempt under s.37(2)(b).

20. In case we are wrong in this conclusion, and since we have in fact attempted to perform the piecemeal exercise which we were invited to perform, we should express our opinion on the matter on that basis. Our conclusion may in the circumstances be expressed briefly. It is that all of the claims of exemption ultimately adhered to should be upheld either under s.37(2)(b) or under the other provisions of the FOI Act as claimed, namely ss.37(1)(a), 37(1)(b), 40(1)(c), (d) and (e), and 45, save that no reliance on s.38 should be supported. The only document in respect of which reliance was placed solely (save for a "name" claim under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e)), on s.38 was IIB 6/1983, and in this case we consider that the material claimed to be exempt is so exempt under s.41(1) and the document as a whole is exempt under s.37(2)(b). In all other cases where s.38 is claimed, it is so claimed in association with one or more other "basic" claims, that is to say a claim or claims based on grounds of exemption other than "name" claims made under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e).

21. We note that since the hearing of this matter the Tribunal has handed down a number of decisions relating to the exempt character of names of officers of the ATO under s.40(1)(c), (d) and (e), and that it has also said much about s.38: See the summary of these cases in Re Mann and Australian Taxation Office (A84/76, 14 June 1985); and see now also Re Swiss Aluminium and Commissioner of Taxation & Ors (N84/539, 10 September 1985) in respect of which an appeal has been lodged.

22. In all the circumstances the Tribunal will affirm the decision under review.
