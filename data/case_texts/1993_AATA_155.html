Re: TAXATION APPEALS

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

P W Johnston(1) (Deputy President), S D Hotop(1) (Senior Member) and R D Fayle(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Taxation - Unpaid Company Tax - whether Tribunal can review validity of notices of assessment by reason of respondent's failure to form certain opinions and other grounds.

ORDER

The Tribunal rules that, in the conduct of proceedings before it and in accordance with the provisions of s.190 of the ITAA, the applicants shall be limited to the grounds of their objections contained in the T documents and in the Amplified Reasons insofar as they are consistent with those grounds.

DECISION

P W JOHNSTON, S D HOTOP AND R D FAYLE This matter involves eight applications seeking review of four notices of assessment issued to each of Mr and Mrs A ("the applicants") respectively by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (WA) ("the respondent"). The applications were heard together because they concern the same issues. The applications were made in respect of two assessments for each applicant for each of the two relevant years of income.

2. The eight notices of assessment, the subject of these applications, were as follows:

3. The "Seaside Homes" assessments for "vendors' taxable amounts" were issued pursuant to a finding under s.6(1) or s.6(2) of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("TUCT Assessment Act") that "secondary taxable amounts" existed. The "Quid" assessments for "vendors' taxable amounts" were issued pursuant to a finding under s.5(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act that "primary taxable amounts" existed.

4. At the hearing, where the applicants were represented by Mr R Sceales, solicitor, and the respondent, by Mr W Martin of counsel, it was agreed that submissions would be made, and a ruling sought, only in respect of a preliminary issue raised by the respondent. Thus, no evidence was taken. It was that evidence would be led, if appropriate, after the Tribunal had determined this preliminary issue.

5. Documents ("the T documents) filed by the respondent pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("AAT Act"), though not formally taken into evidence, will be referred to where necessary in relation to submissions put to the Tribunal.

The Preliminary Issue

6. The preliminary issue is whether assessments issued for "vendors' taxable amounts" pursuant to the TUCT Assessment Act stand impregnable from review except in proceedings under Part V of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("ITAA") as set out in s.177 of that Act (as the respondent would have it) or whether (as the applicants submit) the Tribunal, before proceeding to review the assessments, must first be satisfied with various aspects of the due making of the assessments, and in particular, that conditions precedent to the issue of assessments under the TUCT Assessment Act have been complied with. Put another way, the applicant seeks to have the Tribunal review certain alleged defects in the process of assessment itself.

Background

7. In each of the notices of objection which give rise to these proceedings the grounds are twofold, namely that:

(1) the Commissioner failed to comply with the requirements

specified in s.5(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act, and

(2) the Commissioner failed to comply with the requirements of

s.6(2) of that Act.

8. In each instance these two grounds are embellished with the following statement:

"4. The grounds set out in this Notice of Objection are submissions

as to the proper operation, application and interpretation of the

Act (the ITAA). The submissions are made as claims in order to

satisfy the requirements of s.185 of the Act. The facts in support

of these claims will emerge at a hearing before the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal or the Federal Court if these objections are

disallowed by the Commissioner."

The objections were disallowed by the Commissioner and those decisions were duly notified to the applicants. They then sought, on 15 May 1992 or thereabouts (see T1), pursuant to s.187 of the ITAA, to have those objection decisions reviewed by this Tribunal.

9. Section 4 of the TUCT Assessment Act adopts certain provisions of the ITAA, unless the contrary intention appears, in relation to the operation of the TUCT Assessment Act. One of these is paragraph 190(a) of the ITAA which provides that on review before the Tribunal the taxpayer shall, unless the Tribunal otherwise orders, be limited to the grounds stated in his or her objection. The Tribunal would not otherwise order unless an appropriate application were made, and, none having been made, the applicants are at this point of time confined to the grounds as presently defined.

10. Subsequent to the Commissioner's determination of the objections based on the grounds stated therein, and to these matters coming before this Tribunal, the applicants filed an "Amplified Summary of Reasons for Objection to Recoupment Assessment" ("Amplified Reasons"). This was filed following a directions hearing before the Tribunal. The Amplified Reasons, which relate to all eight notices of assessment contain eleven paragraphs. Those eleven paragraphs are as follows:

"The Respondent failed to comply with the requirements specified

in s.5(1) of Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ("the

TUCT Assessment Act"), in that -

1. The Respondent did not, alternatively, failed to form the series

of opinions required under the TUCT Assessment Act, as more fully

set out below.

2. The Respondent failed to comply with the requirements of s.5(1)

of the TUCT Assessment Act, by proceeding on the basis of a mistake

of fact and of law in identifying the alleged stripping transaction.

3. Alternatively to 2, the officer who is alleged by the Respondent

to have formed the necessary opinions for the purposes of s.5 of

the TUCT Assessment Act did not direct his mind to one or more of

the statutory tests set out in s.5(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act

and, in particular, the matters referred to in ss.5(1)(e), (f), (g)

and (h) of the TUCT Assessment Act.

4. The Respondent failed to form the necessary opinions required

by s.15, alternatively, s.18 of the TUCT Assessment Act, but relied

merely on a change in shareholding in the company in question.

5. The Respondent failed to comply with the requirements of the

TUCT Assessment Act in regard to service of notices of assessment

and related documentation and, accordingly, failed to comply with

the requirements of s.15, alternatively, s.18 of the TUCT Assessment

Act.

6. The Respondent has failed to provide any, alternatively,

sufficient grounds to show what he relied upon to form the opinions

required under the TUCT Assessment Act.

7. At no material time did the Respondent fulfil the requirements of

the TUCT Assessment Act in relation to the formation of necessary

opinions and the establishment of necessary facts from which such

opinions could be drawn to permit the Respondent to raise a

recoupment tax assessment on the Applicant; alternatively, if any

such opinion was formed, which is not admitted, such opinions were

subsequently abandoned; further or alternatively, any such opinion,

being erroneous in law, results in such opinion being a nullity for

all purposes.

8. In purporting to form opinions under s.15 or s.18 of the TUCT

Assessment Act, the Respondent took into account incorrect facts in

relation to s.5(1), and, accordingly, in forming such an opinion,

vitiated any opinions formed under s.15 or s.18, either in failing

to reach an opinion, or in purporting to reach an opinion upon the

basis of such facts.

9. Such persons as purported to act in relation to the purported

issue of notices of assessment and other documentation to the

Applicant did not at the material time have the requisite authority,

alternatively, were not authorised under the Income Tax Assessment

Act, the Taxation Administration Act or the TUCT Assessment Act at

any material time and did not at the material time have any

authorisation from the Respondent to take the steps which they

purported to take.

10. If the Respondent formed the necessary opinions required by

the TUCT Assessment Act, which is denied, the Applicant contends

that the Respondent has vitiated such opinions by -

10.1 taking into account irrelevant considerations;

10.2 failing to take into account relevant considerations; and

10.3 mistake.

11. The Respondent failed to effect service of any assessment upon

the company in accordance with the requirements of the Income Tax

Assessment Act, the Regulations to that Act, the TUCT Assessment Act

or otherwise."

11. So far as the Tribunal is concerned, to the extent that those Amplified Reasons address provisions other than those contained in the relevant formal objections, they may be read as amplification of the respective objection itself.

12. Mr Martin submitted generally that none of these Amplified Reasons involved a proper ground of review because they went to the making of the assessments and, in the case of Reason 11, to the service of the notices of assessment, by the respondent rather than to the substantive tax liability of the applicants. Mr Martin's submission in relation to each of the 11 Amplified Reasons may be summarised as follows.

13. Reason 1: this reason is misconceived since s.5(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act does not require the formation of any opinions; and even if it did, the formation of such opinions would be part of the process of assessment of which the notices of assessment are conclusive evidence.

14. Reason 2: this reason is superfluous since the question for the Tribunal to decide is: what is the substantive tax liability of the applicants? In deciding that question it is for the Tribunal to make the correct findings of fact and flow.

15. Reason 3: this reason is misconceived in the same way as Reason 1; and, furthermore, it is for the Tribunal to decide, on the evidence before it, whether the relevant matters in s.5(1) are satisfied.

16. Reason 4: this reason is irrelevant to the present applications; it raises an entirely new ground not raised in the notices of objection and cannot be agitated in the present proceedings.

17. Reason 5: to the extent that this reason raises the same new ground as Reason 4, it is also irrelevant to the present proceedings; furthermore, it constitutes no more than a general attack on the service of the notices of assessment which, by virtue of s.177(1) of the ITAA, are deemed to have been duly made.

18. Reason 6: this reason is misconceived in the same way as Reason 1 since it is based on the same false premise - namely, that s.5(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act requires the respondent to form opinions.

19. Reason 7: this reason adds nothing to Reasons 1 and 6 and is similarly flawed.

20. Reason 8: this reason is irrelevant and cannot be agitated in the present proceedings for the same reasons as expressed in relation to Reasons 4 and 5.

21. Reason 9: this reason raises issues of the validity of the exercise of delegated authority and, by reason of s.177(1) of ITAA, the applicants are precluded for agitating it in the present proceedings - see Case X37 90 ATC 330.

22. Reason 10: this reason raises grounds of judicial review which cannot be entertained by the Tribunal in the present proceedings; furthermore, in relation to s.5(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act, it is based on the false premise that the respondent is required to form opinions (see submissions in relation to Reasons 1 and 6) and is therefore misconceived.

23. Reason 11: this reason has no substance since the very fact that the applicants lodged notices of objection and subsequently instituted the present proceedings implies that notices of assessment were properly served on them in accordance with s.18(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act.

24. Mr Martin, relying on s.177(1) of the ITAA, submitted that the production of the notices of assessment against which objections have been lodged, determined and referred, is evidence of the due making of those assessments and that this Tribunal may only review those objection decisions, in terms of whether the amounts assessed as taxable are excessive but may not review the due making of the assessments giving rise to the notices.

25. On the other hand Mr Sceales, for the applicants, submitted that it is incumbent on the Tribunal to be satisfied that the objection decisions which it is now called upon to review were competently and validly made. He argued that the Tribunal may review the regularity of the assessment process itself in respect of certain alleged defects. This requires the Tribunal, so the argument proceeded, to refer the matter back to the original decision-maker (presumably in terms of s.43(1)(c)(ii) of the AAT Act) should the Tribunal decide that the original decisions were defective because a condition precedent to their validity had not been satisfied.

26. It is relevant to note that all eight notices of assessment in issue relate to liabilities for "vendors taxable amounts" under s.8 of the TUCT Assessment Act and imposed by s.4 of the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax - Vendors) Act 1982. Such tax is described as "vendors recoupment tax" in s.3(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act. "Vendors taxable amount" is defined in s.3(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act as follows:

"'vendors taxable amount', in relation to a person, means an

amount that is a primary taxable amount or a secondary taxable

amount in relation to the person."

Subsection 5(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act which was applied by the respondent in this instance to the "Quid" assessments, sets out nine matters contained in paragraphs (a) to (j) (there is no paragraph "(i)") inclusive which, if satisfied, result in a "primary taxable amount". Similarly, where a "vendors taxable amount" exists in relation to a company and the conditions set out in sub-paragraphs 6(2)(a), (b) and (c) of the TUCT Assessment Act are satisfied, then a "secondary taxable amount" arises. It was on the basis of these provisions of sub-section 6(2) that the respondent issued the "Seaside Homes" assessments of "vendors taxable amounts".

27. Before a primary taxable amount can arise under s.5(1), apart from any other matter, an assessment of "ordinary company tax" or "undistributed profits tax" (defined in s.3(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act) under the ITAA must have been made, the period for objecting against those assessments must have expired, and any objection against the assessments finalised (s.5(1)(e) and (f)). Subsection 18(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act empowers the Commissioner to serve a notice of assessment of income tax or undistributed profits tax of a company which still exists, upon a person or persons who, in the opinion of the Commissioner, are likely to become liable for vendors recoupment tax. There are no express statutory prescriptions or criteria for the formation of an opinion under s.18(1). Subsection 18(3) reserves to the person or persons so served the same rights of objection, review and appeal under the ITAA as the company has.

28. In the case of the two "Quid" assessments, one for ordinary company tax and one for undistributed profits tax, both in relation to the year ended 30 June 1981, objections pursuant to s.18(3) were duly lodged and when objection decisions unfavourable to the company issued the matters were referred to the Tribunal for review. Subsequently the requests for review were all dismissed by the Tribunal, pursuant to s.42A of the AAT Act, by consent of the parties to these applications.

29. Insofar as the four "Quid" assessments for vendors taxable amounts are concerned, the matters contained in s.5(1)(e) and (f) TUCT Assessment Act set out below are satisfied:

Section 5(1)(e) and (f) read:......

"5(1)..Where -

(a) ...

(b) ...

(c) ...

(d) ...

(e) an assessment or ordinary company tax or undistributed profits

tax has been made under the Assessment Act, whether before or after

the commencement of this Act, in relation to the company in relation

to a year of income (in this sub-section is referred to as the

"relevant year of income"), being the year of income in which the

last sale time occurred or a preceding year of income;

(f) the period for objecting against the assessment has expired and

any objection against the assessment has been finalised;

(g) ...

(h) ...

(j) ...

the following provisions have effect:

(k) if all the shares referred to in paragraph (a) were sold under

the scheme by one person - a primary taxable amount equal to the

overdue company tax shall be taken to exist at the relevant time in

relation to that person.

30. In the case of the "Seaside Homes" assessments for vendors taxable amounts on the applicants, the respondent applied s.6(2) of the TUCT Assessment Act to determine secondary taxable amounts. The basis for this was that the respondent determined that "Quid" was an intermediary shareholder in "Seaside Homes" and the applicants were the shareholders of "Quid". The applicants disposed of their "Quid" shares, the action which ultimately gave rise to these references since, in the case of the "Quid" assessments, the respondent applied s.5(1) and, in the case of the "Seaside Homes" assessments, s.6(2), to give rise to the respective primary and secondary taxable amounts mentioned above. It may be noted in passing that primary taxable amounts arising to the applicants in relation to the sale of their one share each in "Seaside Homes" were not taken to exist in accordance with s.5(4)(b) TUCT Assessment Act since the respective primary taxable amounts were less than $100.

31. Before a secondary taxable amount can be taken to exist under s.6(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act a vendors taxable amount must exist and an assessment of the recoupment tax payable on the vendors taxable amount must not have been made before the company ceased to exist. In the case of s.6(2), where a vendors taxable amount exists and the Commissioner is of the opinion that, having regard to the sale of shares in the holding company, the holding company would be liable to pay the vendors taxable amount, a secondary taxable amount then exists in relation to the beneficial shareholders of the holding company. In the present instance, those shareholders are the applicants. In the case of either s.6(1) or (2), the provisions of s.18(3) allow the vendors the opportunity to contest the assessments for ordinary company tax and for undistributed profits tax levied on the company pursuant to s.18.

32. As was the case with the "Quid" company assessments, objections, pursuant to s.18(3), were also duly lodged against the "Seaside Homes" assessments for ordinary company tax and for undistributed profits tax in relation to the year ended 30 June 1977. When objection decisions unfavourable to the company issued, applications were made to the Tribunal for review. Subsequently the applications were all dismissed by the Tribunal by consent of the parties pursuant to s.42A of the AAT Act. Therefore, so far as the four "Seaside Homes" assessments for vendors taxable amounts are concerned, and so far as it is relevant, the matters contained in s.5(1)(e) and (f) set out above are satisfied.

33. Mr Sceales acknowledged that the appeal or review process relating to the assessments of both companies, "Quid" and "Seaside Homes", under s.18 (and s.15) had been effectively terminated by the orders of dismissal made by the Tribunal.

34. Notwithstanding the above acknowledgment, a contention basic to the submission of the applicants is that the assessments in question are nullities and in consequence there was nothing against which to formally object in terms of s.185 of the ITAA. If so, it would follow, any determination made by this Tribunal would itself be a nullity. The Tribunal finds that this contention cannot be sustained because in fact there are objection decisions before it to review and further, that there is nothing in the process of getting the matters to this stage that puts in question the status of those assessments as assessments, such as if they were expressed to be notional, provisional or tentative. By reason that these matters have reached this stage, the notices of assessment should be taken to have been duly served; c.f. DFC of T v Briggs 86 ATC 4020. Formal objections were lodged by the applicants, which were duly considered by the respondent and disallowed in terms of Division 2 of Part V of the ITAA. All necessary formalities required to bring the objection decisions on to this Tribunal for review bear no apparent defect. The attendant documentation on its face is in all formal respects consistent with the requirements of the ITAA. Neither party has seen fit to seek any order for judicial review from a competent court to contest the validity of the assessment notices nor to restrain this Tribunal from carrying out its appointed function to review the assessment decisions referred to it with a view to determining whether the liability amounts disclosed in them are "excessive".

35. These references are distinguishable from those to this Tribunal in Case T68 86 ATC 1012, which decision was overturned on appeal to the Federal Court, heard at first instance by Beaumont J as FCT v Cripps and Jones Holdings Pty Ltd 87 ATC 4352 and upheld by the Full Court (87 ATC 4977). There the relevant matter of concern was whether the Commissioner had erred in meeting the requirements of s.15 of the TUCT Assessment Act in forming the opinion required as a precondition for the making of a notional assessment. The issue was not whether the Commissioner conformed with the exactitudes of s.18 in relation to companies which existed. As was observed in that case:

"...s.18 does not empower the Commissioner to issue any kind of

assessment. It is concerned only to provide for notification to

persons likely to be assessed to recoupment tax, and for the

exercise by them of rights of appeal." (p 4985)

36. In the case of these applications, the opportunity for a review of the merits of the relevant determinations in relation to the taxable amounts pursuant to s.18 has expired upon the dismissal of the applications for their review and a logical conclusion is that the applicants having abandoned their rights of review at that stage, this Tribunal is precluded from examining the merits of those determinations as, in this respect, the Tribunal is now functus officio. This is not to deny the applicants the opportunity to satisfy the Tribunal that they are not persons in relation to whom the primary taxable amount exists in the case of the "Quid" assessments and, in relation to the "Seaside Homes" assessments, that they are not persons in relation to whom secondary taxable amounts exist. Should the applicants succeed on these issues then the objection decisions would be set aside as being vitiated by error. As was said in Cripps and Jones Holdings Pty Ltd by the Full Court:

"In cases arising under Div 2 of Pt V of the Income Tax Assessment

Act, (of which this is one) a taxpayer has the right 'to challenge

the existence of the conditions giving rise to the exercise of a

power which imposes on him a new substantive liability to tax'

(F J Bloemen Pty Ltd v FCT 81 ATC 4280 at p 4287 per Mason and

Wilson JJ)." (at pp 4,984-4,985)

37. Both counsel addressed the Tribunal on the relevance of s.177 of the ITAA to the eight notices of assessment in question, raised pursuant to the TUCT Assessment Act. Whilst the application of that provision to assessments of recoupment tax following the quantification of primary and secondary taxable amounts pursuant to s.5 and s.6 respectively of the TUCT Assessment Act was not specifically dealt with by the Full Federal Court in Cripps and Jones Holdings, their Honours concluded that s.175 and s.177 do not apply to notional assessments under s.15 of the TUCT Assessment Act (see p 4985). However, s.4(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act applies Part V of the ITAA mutatis mutandis for purposes of the assessment and collection of recoupment tax, that being the consequence of an initial finding by the respondent pursuant to s.5 and s.6 that primary and secondary taxable amounts exist. Since s.177 is within Part V of ITAA then by operation of s.4(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act, that section has application to assessments of recoupment tax under the TUCT Assessment Act as distinct from notional assessments of company tax under the Act.

38. Section 177 of the ITAA provides, in effect, that a notice of assessment is conclusive evidence of the due making of an assessment except in proceedings under Part V on a review or appeal relating to the assessment. In F J Bloemen Pty Ltd v FCT 81 ATC 4280 at p 4288, Mason and Wilson JJ said in this regard:

"... once the Commissioner takes advantage of sec.177(1) by

producing an appropriate document (the notice of assessment), the

taxpayer is precluded from contesting that the Commissioner has made

an assessment or that in making the assessment he has complied with

the statutory formalities. The taxpayer is entitled to dispute his

substantive liability to tax in proceedings under Part V."

39. The applicants, by these proceedings under Part V of the ITAA, are entitled to dispute the substantive liability to tax evidenced by the notices of assessment in question (see Brennan J in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Dalco [1990] HCA 3; (1990) 168 CLR 614, at 620-623). The assessments each relate to a new and substantive liability to tax imposed, not because the applicants derived income as one might expect, but because the Commissioner, in applying the relevant statutory provisions, assessed them as proper recipients of the assessments in question. The applicants may, in the proceedings before the Tribunal, challenge the existence of the conditions giving rise to the exercise of the power which imposed the new substantive liabilities to tax; c.f. Bloemen at 4287. If the formation of an opinion forms a step in the conclusion of whether or not the taxpayers are liable as assessed, the Tribunal can decide for itself whether such an opinion should be formed or not, having regard to the facts. Such a process, though, is circumscribed by the provisions of s.190 of the ITAA concerning the grounds of objection before the Tribunal and the onus of proof.

40. Primary and secondary taxable amounts arise under circumstances which include a consequence of ordinary company tax and undistributed profits tax assessments having been made and finalised (s.5(1)(e) and (f) of the TUCT Assessment Act). The other factual conditions which would validate an assessment of primary taxable amounts are set out in the other paragraphs of subsection 5(1), up to (j). In respect of the "Seaside Homes" assessments, the s.5(1) factual matters are the only matters which are open to dispute under the relevant objections now before the Tribunal.

41. With regard to the "Quid" assessments it should be borne in mind that, by s.3(1) of the TUCT Assessment Act, "vendors taxable amount" is defined as "in relation to a person, means an amount that is a primary taxable amount or secondary taxable amount in relation to the person". Therefore, in addition to disputing the relevant s.5(1) factual matters mentioned, it is open to the applicants to contest the factual matters referred to in s.6(2)(a) and (b) as well as the proper formation of opinion under paragraph 6(2)(c). As it happens, such an opinion is bounded by fact in any event.

42. The applicants also sought to challenge the notices of assessment on the ground that the assessments may not have been made by officers properly authorised to do so. The Tribunal would make the brief comment that it is not a function of this Tribunal to go behind the assessments to establish whether they were made by a duly authorised officer. Section 177 of the ITAA precludes that course (see Purvis J in Case X 37 (1990) 90 ATC 330, at 322; see also WT87/746; unreported decision, 9 May 1990). For the same reasons, this Tribunal is not prepared to enquire, as a threshold aspect of the question of whether the "Quid" assessments were validly made, whether the requisite notices were served upon the shareholders or representatives of "Seaside Homes". In any case, in the light of the fact that applications for review were lodged on behalf of "Quid", though later dismissed, there would seem to be substantial difficulties in pursuing such a challenge.

43. Therefore, having regard to the above scope of matters open to review, the Tribunal rules in relation to the preliminary issue that, in the conduct of proceedings before it and in accordance with the provisions of s.190 of the ITAA, the applicants shall be limited to the grounds of their objections contained in the T documents and in the Amplified Reasons insofar as they are consistent with those grounds, as indicated above.
