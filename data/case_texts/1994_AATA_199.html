WT93/66 and Commissioner of Taxation [1994] AATA 199; (1994) 28 ATR 1289; 94 ATC 334 (19 July 1994)

WT93/66 and Commissioner of Taxation [1994] AATA 199 (19 July 1994); (1994) 28 ATR 1289; 94 ATC 334

Last Updated: 15 May 2009



INCOME TAX : Employee share scheme - acquisition date - beneficial or legal ownership

INCOME TAX : Notification of election - under sub-section 26AAC(15A) - extension of time - no prejudice to Commissioner - sufficient factual basis

INCOME TAX : Value of shares - market value at date of acquisition



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 : 26AAC

Case X43 90 ATC 354

Case U3 87 ATC 118

Fraunschiel and Ors v FCT 89 ATC 4616

KLDE Pty Limited (in liquidation) v Commissioner of Stamp Duties (QLD) 84 ATC 4793

Palmarc Investments Pty Ltd v FC of T 85 ATC 4410

Patcorp Investments Ltd v FC of T [1976] HCA 67; (1976) 10 ALR 407

R v. Australian Broadcasting Tribunal; ex parte Hardiman [1980] HCA 13; (1980) 144 C.L.R. 13

Windshuttle v DFC of T 93 ATC 4992



AAT No 9613



DECISION AND REASONS



Re: APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No : WT93/66

Date : 19 July 1994

Place : Perth

Tribunal : Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



DECISION



The Tribunal decides that:

(a) the objection decision be set aside; and

(b) the matter be remitted back to the respondent with a direction that the assessment of income tax of the applicant for the year ended 30 June 1992 be amended by reducing the assessed taxable income in accordance with these reasons.



(Sgd) K L Beddoe (SENIOR MEMBER)



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO WT93/66

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:

APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT



REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 19 July 1994

Place: Perth



The applicant seeks a review of an objection decision dated 31 August 1993 which disallowed an objection against assessment of income tax for the year of income ended 30 June 1992. The return for that year had been lodged by the applicant in accordance with a ruling issued by the Commissioner in answer to a request from the applicant for the respondent’s interpretation of the law, confined to the application of section 26AAC of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (“the Act”) to the acquisition of shares under an employees’ share scheme. That section of the Act relevantly provides for the inclusion in the assessable income of a taxpayer, where the taxpayer had acquired shares pursuant to an employees’ share acquisition scheme within the meaning of sub-section 26AAC(1), particular amounts as calculated in accordance with the provisions of the section.



2. Sub-section 26AAC(5) is the charging provision which provides that:



“Where a taxpayer has acquired during the year of income a share in a company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees, the assessable income of the taxpayer of the year of income includes the value of that share at the time when it was acquired by the taxpayer less the sum of -

(a) the amount, if any, paid or payable by the taxpayer as consideration for the share; and



(b) if the taxpayer acquired the share as a result of the exercise or operation of a right (whether that right was unconditional or subject to conditions) to acquire the share - the amount, if any, paid or payable by the taxpayer as consideration for the right.”



3. Sub-section 26AAC(6) deals with the situation where a right to acquire a share in a company has been disposed of to an associate of the taxpayer. The sub-section deems the taxpayer to have acquired the share under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees and to have so acquired the share at the time when it was acquired by the associate.



4. Sub-section 26AAC(14) defines an associate of a taxpayer as including:



(a) . . .;



(b) a trustee of a trust estate, where the taxpayer or any relative of the taxpayer benefits or is capable of benefiting under the trust;”



5. Sub-sections 26AAC(15), (15A) and (15B) deal with a deemed date of acquisition for the purpose of sub-section 26AAC(5).



“26AAC(15) Where -

(a) a taxpayer acquires a share in a company under a scheme for the acquisition of shares by employees; and



(b) by reason of any conditions or restrictions (being conditions or restrictions applicable only to shares in the company acquired under such a scheme) attached to, or to the issue of, the share (including conditions or restrictions in relation to the payment of moneys in respect of the share) the right of the taxpayer to dispose of the share is restricted or the taxpayer is liable to be divested of his ownership of the share,



the acquisition of the share by the taxpayer shall be deemed for the purposes of this section (other than subsections (4A), (4B), (4C), (4D) and (4F)) to have taken place at the time when the right of the taxpayer to dispose of the share ceases to be so restricted, the time when the taxpayer ceases to be so liable to be divested of his ownership of the share or the time immediately before the taxpayer disposes of the share, whichever first happens.



26AAC(15A) A taxpayer may elect that subsection (15) is not to apply in relation to a share being a share issued to the taxpayer after 19 September 1985.



26AAC(15B) An election under subsection (15A) in relation to a share must be made on or before the date of lodgement of the return of income of the taxpayer for the year of income in which the share was issued, or within such further period as the Commissioner allows.”



6. The applicant was represented by Mr Moltoni of Duesburys Accountants and an officer of the Australian Taxation Office represented for the respondent. No oral evidence was heard by the Tribunal, the parties putting an agreed statement of facts before the Tribunal (Exhibit A). The Tribunal also had before it documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and certain further documentation consisting of correspondence and notes of conversations between the applicant, his employer and the respondent, the bundle of which was marked as Exhibit B. Exhibit B was admitted by consent, with an objection, as raised for the respondent, that any legal assertions in those documents were not accepted by the respondent.



7. A copy of the deed governing the Trust and the share scheme in question was included in the T Documents (T4) but appeared as an unexecuted document. This was accepted by consent as being a true copy of the executed document.



8. The following findings of fact are based on the agreed statement of facts (Exhibit A).



9. The applicant was at the relevant time, and continues to be, an employee of B.I. Ltd (“the Company”). The Company, at some time before 26 November 1984 established the “B Employees Share Scheme” (“the Scheme”) which made available to nominated employees of B.I. Ltd the right to acquire shares in the company on favourable terms. The Scheme was a scheme within the terms of sub-section 26AAC(1) of the Act, and I so find. The Scheme was administered through B Custodians Pty Ltd (“the Trustee”) and is evidenced by a deed between the Company and the Trustee (T4).



10. The applicant was duly nominated by the Company as eligible to participate in the Scheme pursuant to the terms of the deed. In participation in the Scheme, the applicant directed the Trustee to apply for 5,000 shares which were acquired by the Trustee for a “subscription amount” of $10,750.



11. The shares were allotted to the Trustee on 26 November 1984 and were held in the Trustee’s name on the share register of the Company. The market value of the 5000 shares was $17,250 on 26 November 1984.



12. Subsequently the applicant was again nominated by the Company as eligible to participate in the scheme and, in participation in the scheme, directed the Trustee to apply for 1,800 shares which were acquired by the Trustee for a “subscription amount” of $9,889.



13. The shares were allotted to the Trustee on 4 March 1987 and were held solely in the Trustee’s name on the share register of the Company. The market value of the 1,800 shares was $14,040 on 4 March 1987.

14. Under the terms of the scheme, the applicant paid 10% of the subscription amount to the Trustee at the time he directed the Trustee to apply for the shares. The Company then made an interest free loan to the Trustee of the balance of the subscription amount, with the Trustee paying an amount equivalent to the 10% subscription amount received from the applicant, directly to the company.



15. During the period the shares were held by the Trustee the applicant paid 10% of the subscription amount to the Trustee annually. The Trustee would then pay an amount equivalent to that received from the applicant to the Company in repayment of the loan. When the Trustee received dividends from the Company, an equivalent amount would be passed on to the applicant. In 1987 the Company made a “one for ten” bonus share issue. Following this issue, 500 bonus shares and 180 bonus shares respectively were allotted to the Trustee on 29 June 1987.



16. The scrip for both parcels of shares was retained by the Trustee until the applicant repaid the “debt” (as defined in clause 1(1)(c) of the deed (T4)) on 12 July 1991. The market value of the two parcels of shares on that date was $85,500 and $30,780 respectively.



17. Both parcels of shares were transferred to the applicant on the share register of the Company on 1 August 1991. The market value of the two parcels of shares on that date was $88,250 and $31,770 respectively. These valuations of the shares were based on the price of the shares on the day on the Sydney Stock Exchange.



18. On 1 October 1991 the applicant sold 800 of the Company shares acquired under the scheme for $14,200. The costs of disposal, being stamp duty and brokerage, were $43 and $142 respectively.



19. On 2 October 1991 the applicant sold 6,000 of the Company shares acquired under the scheme for $106,500. The costs of disposal, being stamp duty and brokerage, were $320 and $1,065 respectively.



20. On 30 March 1992, the applicant’s tax agents, who also represented the applicant before the Tribunal, requested a ruling of the respondent’s interpretation of the law in relation to the taxation consequences of the smaller parcel of 1,800 shares. This request was confined to the application of section 26AAC of the Act to the acquisition of shares.



21. The respondent made a ruling on this request on 24 July 1992 (T5) and the applicant taxpayer lodged his income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1992 in accordance with the ruling (T6).



22. A notice of assessment for the year ended 30 June 1992 issued to the applicant on 3 November 1992 (T7). The applicant’s agents lodged an objection against this notice of assessment on 24 December 1992 (T8) and in July 1993 sent additional information in support of the notice of objection (T9). By letter dated 31 August 1993 the respondent disallowed that objection (T11). The reasons for disallowing the objection were given in that letter as follows:



“The arrangement between [the Company], [the Trustee] and [the applicant] constitutes a ‘scheme’ for the purposes of subsection 26AAC. [The applicant] acquired both lots of shares for the purposes of section 26AAC on 12 July 1991.

Subsection 26AAC(15) did not apply to the acquisition of the shares.

Subsection 26AAC(6) did not apply to the acquisition of a right to acquire shares by [the applicant].”



23. It is that objection decision which is under review in this Tribunal.



24. The Trustee holds a “sub-register” with details of names and addresses, numbers of shares, along with other details of the employees who had requested it to apply for shares. On the Company share register the shares were held solely in the Trustee’s name, and the Tribunal so finds. Clause 4(2) of the trust deed (T4) indicates that:



“Each Selected Employee or his legal personal representative will be entitled to and will be paid by the Trustee all dividends declared and paid on the Shares on and after the date of allotment promptly upon the payment thereof by [the Company] to the Trustee and also will be entitled to receive all and any other benefits which may accrue after the date of allotment of the Shares immediately upon the accrual thereof including any Bonus Shares provided always that any Bonus Shares be held by the Trustee in trust for the Selected Employee who shall be the beneficial owner thereof subject always to the terms of this Deed as amended from time to time and in particular Clauses 5, 6 and 7 hereof unless the Trustee and the Selected Employee agree that the Bonus Shares should be transferred to the Selected Employee at any earlier time and that [the Company] consents thereafter that the said bonus shares be so transferred.”



25. Documents which are part of Exhibit B before the Tribunal indicate that dividends were paid to the Trustee in relation to the undivided parcel of shares and the Trustee then paid individuals a proportion of that amount according to the number of shares they had asked the Trustee to apply for. Other benefits accruing, such as rights issues and bonus issues were dealt with in the same manner by the Trustee. Once the debt had been paid in full, the shares were transferred in the usual manner of lodging share transfer forms.

26. The applicant’s representative argued that sub-section 26AAC(6) would apply, in conjunction with sub-section 26AAC(14), to deem the date of acquisition under sub-section 26AAC(5) by the taxpayer as being the date the shares were acquired by the trustee as an associate of the taxpayer. That provision deals with the situation where the right to acquire shares is disposed of by the taxpayer to an associate of the taxpayer. The Tribunal’s attention was drawn to the decision of Lee J in Fraunschiel and Ors v FCT 89 ATC 4616 where his Honour made a distinction between the right to acquire a share and the right to accept an offer. The latter had no enforceable quality to it whereas the former did.



27. Here it is clear, in partial adoption of the applicant’s submissions, that until the taxpayer directed the trustee to apply for the shares in question, there existed nothing more than a right to direct the trustee to accept an offer. The taxpayer did so direct the trustee to accept the offer. A right to acquire shares arose only when the acceptance was communicated to the company by the trustee in accordance with the applicant’s direction to apply for the issue of shares. Thus, following the reasoning of Lee J, sub-section 26AAC(6) does not apply to the current situation, and I so find. Further, in the present situation, the right of the applicant is only to acquire the shares through the trust mechanism, there is no evidence of any provision in the share acquisition scheme for an employee to purchase the shares outright without the intervention of the trustee. Carrying this line of reasoning on, once the trustee acquires the shares, that is, acquires legal ownership of the shares, the taxpayer acquires beneficial ownership of those shares in accordance with clause 4 of the Trust Deed.



28. Turning to the definition of “acquired” pursuant to sub-section 26AAC(5). There is no definition in the Act to define “acquire” or “acquired” for the purposes of section 26AAC.



29. The Tribunal’s attention was directed to the Federal Court decision in Windshuttle v DFC of T 93 ATC 4992. There von Doussa J considered the application of section 26AAA to the circumstances of the transactions concerning the purchase and then the sale of a block of land. The key term in that section is “purchased”. It was submitted by Mr Moltoni that the word “acquire”, although not being ascribed an expanded definition by the section itself, obviously has a meaning which is something wider than purchase. That is, the ordinary meaning of “purchase” is narrower than that of “acquisition”. The Court, in Windshuttle’s case, emphasised the “distinction between a legal owner who holds property as a trustee, and the person beneficially entitled under that trust . . .” at 5000 and reiterated the principle that income is not derived by a taxpayer unless it is derived beneficially. His Honour referred to Palmarc Investments Pty Ltd v FC of T 85 ATC 4410 where the taxpayer applied for the shares on behalf of another party and held them as trustee. In that case the Court said at 4414:



“There was no purchase by the taxpayer in its own right of any interest in the Carbon shares when they were allotted to it; nor was there a sale by the taxpayer to Sewell of the equitable interest in those shares. The shares were allotted to the taxpayer only (and at all times) as trustee for Sewell, and the taxpayer held those shares only (and at all times) as trustee for Sewell. The taxpayer acted in exactly the same way as any nominee company which was a Magnum shareholder - such as, for example, ANZ Nominees Ltd, which held shares in Magnum as trustee for the taxpayer. As between Magnum and that company, ANZ Nominees was the shareholder and was entitled to payment of a dividend and to vote at meetings and it was liable for calls, etc. Similarly, when it no doubt accepted the offer of the directors of Carbon and took up shares and options in Carbon it was, so far as Carbon was concerned, the shareholder but it acquired those shares and held them as trustee for the taxpayer.”



30. The respondent’s representative’s submission with respect to Windshuttle (supra) was that it was simply not relevant in that it involved a change in the bare legal title to a piece of property and the Court was in effect saying that this did not constitute a change in the beneficial ownership of the land for the purposes of section 26AAA of the Act.



31. The respondent relied on Case X43 90 ATC 354, a decision of Deputy President McDonald of this tribunal, as authority for the proposition that acquisition of a share under section 26AAC means acquisition of the legal interest in the shares. This decision was relied upon heavily by the Commissioner, the issues being raised in that matter being strikingly similar to those raised here, and the factual circumstances surrounding the scheme in question being the same. The Tribunal in this matter was told by Mr Moltoni for the applicant that Case X43 in fact related to the same employee share scheme as in the present case.



32. The decision in Case X43 depends upon the meaning given to “acquire” in the context of section 26AAC. As I have already discussed it has a wider meaning than purchase. The learned Deputy President seems to have adopted a meaning of acquisition of shares as a shareholder in the sense adopted by the High Court in Patcorp Investments Ltd and ors v FCT [1976] HCA 67; (1976) 10 ALR 407. In that case the Court affirmed the general principle that entry on the share register (including entitlement to be so entered) is necessary to constitute membership of a company and that beneficial ownership of shares without registration does not make a person a shareholder for the purposes of the Act.



33. The Deputy President said at page 358 “It seems to me the precondition for the operation of Subsec. (15) (sic) must be a finding that the taxpayer has acquired a legal interest in the shares in question.” In coming to that conclusion the Deputy President looked to sub-section 26AAC(5) which makes the distinction between the situation where the shares have been acquired and where merely a right to acquire the shares exists and went on to say that the same reasoning would apply to sub-section 26AAC(15). The Tribunal thought that a right to acquire the shares arises upon allocation of the shares to the trustee. “From the time of that allocation there is nothing stopping the full vesting of the shares other than the satisfaction of the conditions imposed by the trust deed. The trustee has no discretion to refuse to vest the shares upon the applicant meeting the conditions.” (at page 358). It seems to me that the Deputy President is describing here a right of specific performance, vested in the taxpayer, as against the trustee. In Case X43 (supra), the decision in KLDE Pty Limited (in liquidation) v Commissioner of Stamp Duties (QLD) 84 ATC 4793 was distinguished on the facts in that, that case revolved around the imposition of stamp duty and there was a contract capable of specific performance. The reasoning was that, in the situation before the Tribunal, there was no right as against the company, only against the trustee. With respect, I am unable to agree. It is the obligations imposed on the trustee vis-à-vis the applicant which are of prime importance. Those obligations create the beneficial interest in the shares on the facts of this case. It is clear on the material before me that the trustee held the shares for the benefit of the applicant, and I so find.



34. In KLDE Pty Ltd (in liquidation) v Commissioner for Stamp Duties (Qld) (supra), the Full Court of the High Court clearly explained the concept of beneficial ownership. The Court relied on the basic equitable principle that equity regards as done that which ought to be done. It reiterated that Court’s view as expressed in R v. Australian Broadcasting Tribunal; ex parte Hardiman [1980] HCA 13; (1980) 144 C.L.R. 13 (at p.31 per Gibbs, Stephen, Mason, Aickin and Wilson JJ) that:



“a purchaser who can by way of specific performance compel a transfer of shares under a contract is a beneficial owner of the shares.”



35. Case U3 87 ATC 118 also involved an almost identical factual situation, including the terms of the trust deed. The applicant taxpayer’s representative pointed out that in that matter the Tribunal at page 121 considered that in the circumstances “. . . the trustee acts on behalf of the selected employees and holds the shares on trust for the employees who are the beneficial owners thereof.” This finding is not at odds with the operation of sub-section 26AAC(15). That sub-section merely imposes a “deemed” acquisition date. The wording of the provision contemplates that some form of “acquisition“ or ownership has already occurred (sub-section 26AAC(15)(a)). The sub-section then provides that where there is a restriction on the disposal of the shares (including conditions or restrictions in relation to the payment of moneys in respect of the share, the exact circumstance here), the acquisition of the share, for the purposes of this section (emphasis added) is deemed to have taken place when the right of the taxpayer to dispose of the share ceases to be so restricted, that is, at the time when the debt is repaid. This result does not depend on a characterisation of the acquisition to be acquisition of the legal interest (sub-section 26AAC(5)).



36. The difficulty experienced by the Tribunals in Case X43 and Case U3 was different on the facts to this matter in that in those cases the taxpayers had not paid the debt but had defaulted, thus leading to the disposal of the shares by the respective trustees under the terms of the deed. Therefore there was perceived to be a difficulty in saying the taxpayers had “acquired the shares” because sub-section 26AAC(15) operated to set back the “acquisition date” and because the taxpayers in the reported cases never ceased to be liable to be divested of the ownership of the shares.



37. “Acquires”, as referred to in sub-section 26AAC(15), must refer to acquisition of the beneficial ownership of the shares in question. Beneficial ownership is acquired because the applicant has acquired the rights vis-à-vis the trustee. The terms of the deed governing the scheme make it clear that the applicant’s right to call for transfer of the shares to himself is limited until “the fifth anniversary of the date of allotment” upon payment of the debt in full (Clause 3, document T4). I take “date of allotment” to refer to the date on which the Trustee is allotted shares in the company upon application for those shares as directed by the employee. A restriction of this kind is specifically contemplated by the wording of sub-section 26AAC(15). After the fifth anniversary and payment of the debt in full there is no restriction on the taxpayer requiring the transfer of the shares. The acquisition date for each parcel of shares as deemed by sub-section 26AAC(15) is therefore 12 July 1991. In respect of the parcel of 1800 shares this is a date prior to the fifth anniversary of the date of allotment. Clause 3 of the Trust Deed provides for the shortening of the five years provision in certain circumstances. The circumstances justifying this shortening of time are not before me but I infer that the Trustee exercised its discretion to do so.



38. Mr Moltoni, in submissions, argued that even should sub-section 26AAC(15) apply to alter the acquisition date, the applicant has lodged an election pursuant to sub-section 26AAC(15A) that sub-section 26AAC(15) is not to apply in relation to the shares in question (document T9). Further a request for an extension of time to lodge such an election pursuant to sub-section 26AAC(15B) has been made (document T9). This extension of time is required because that sub-section requires that the election be made on or before the date of lodgment of the return of income of the taxpayer for the year of income in which the shares were issued to the taxpayer unless the respondent Commissioner allows some further period. Mr Moltoni argued that the Commissioner, through various tax rulings, particularly IT2662, has made a global decision with respect to the extension of time in which to lodge such an election. It is, of course, well established that such a Tax Ruling does not have the force of law and that the Commissioner must make a decision properly based on the merits of each individual case.



39. The respondent’s representative made it clear that the respondent did not consider that any valid election had been made. According to her submission, not only would the elections as made be out of time, but no extension of time had been granted by the respondent Commissioner with respect to late lodgment. Further, the respondent’s representative asserted that the applicant’s reliance on IT2662 was misplaced in that it only applies to returns lodged for years of income following the year of income ended 30 June 1992. That submission is in direct conflict with the ruling itself which is dated 5 December 1991 and the date of effect of the ruling which is stated as “immediate”. The ruling was therefore published and in operation before the applicant lodged the relevant income tax return. He was entitled to rely on the ruling vis-à-vis the respondent. Mr Moltoni contended that the phrase “the year of income in which the share was issued” in sub-section 26AAC(15B) should be read as meaning the year of income in which the share was issued to the taxpayer, that is, the 1992 financial year. Nevertheless, the Tribunal, pursuant to section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, is now able to exercise that same discretion standing in the shoes of the respondent. It has not been suggested that the respondent will suffer any prejudice if the time for lodging the declaration is extended. Indeed, the Commissioner’s own ruling indicates that the time should be extended. I so decide.



38. The material before the Tribunal provides a sufficient factual basis for the exercise of the discretion. I do know that the shares were issued to the applicant taxpayer on or about 1 August 1991 when the applicant was entered on the share register as the shareholder. That means that the elections under sub-section 26AAC(15A) were required to be made on or before the date of lodgment of the return of income for the year of income ended 30 June 1992.



39. The elections were in fact lodged with the respondent on 8 July 1993 as attachments to the notice of objection against the assessment of income tax for the year ended 30 June 1992. Given that the applicant’s 1992 income tax return was lodged by tax agents, I take the view it is reasonable to infer that the return was lodged in the Taxation Office on or about 19 October 1992 which is the date it was signed by Mr Moltoni on behalf of Duesburys as tax agents.



40. I am therefore asked to extend time from 19 October 1992 to 8 July 1993 for the lodgment of the declarations. In the circumstances I am satisfied that I should extend time for the making of the elections to 8 July 1993.



41. Allowing the extension of time for notification of such an election means that the date of acquisition for the shares in question under sub-section 26AAC(5) will be the date of allotment to the trustee. That is, 26 November 1984 and 4 March 1987 respectively. It is on these dates that the taxpayer becomes beneficial owner of the shares in the company.



42. Therefore in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1985 and 30 June 1987 the assessed taxable income will have to be amended to take into account assessable income of the taxpayer calculated in accordance with sub-section 26AAC(5).



43. As to the value of the shares. It was submitted that the value ascribed to the shares should be the contractual sum, as paid by the taxpayer. The respondent’s representative, however, argued that sub-section 26AAC(18) applies to make it clear that the value to be given to the shares is simply the market value as at the date of acquisition, without the imposition of any conditions or restrictions. With respect, I think this submission is correct. Should Mr Moltoni’s submission be accepted, then sub-section 26AAC(5) would be meaningless in that the value of the share when acquired and the consideration payable by the taxpayer would be equal and therefore no amount would be included in the assessable income of the taxpayer. The value to be ascribed to the shares for the purposes of sub-section 26AAC(5) is the market value of the shares as at the date of acquisition, i.e. the date of issue of the shares to the trustee, and I so find.



44. It follows that, in the year of income ended 30 June 1985, an amount of $6,500 and in the year of income ended 30 June 1987, an amount of $4,151, should have been included in the assessable income of the applicant.



45. However, in relation to the year of income ended 30 June 1992, the amount included in the assessable income of the taxpayer must be based on the capital gains provisions in Part IIIA of the Act.



46. As the parcel of 5,000 shares was beneficially acquired by the applicant prior to 20 September 1985, Part IIIA does not apply to the disposal of those shares (section 160L).



47. In accordance with Part IIIA, an amount of $12,973 should be included in the taxpayer’s assessable income for the year of income ended 30 June 1992. This amount is calculated by the Tribunal as set out in the following paragraphs.



48. The capital gain to be included in T’s assessable income will be the excess of the consideration received in respect of disposal over the indexed cost base to the taxpayer (section 160Z).



49. The amount of consideration received by the applicant in respect of the disposal of the parcel of 1,800 shares is $31,950. This amount was calculated by apportioning the total consideration received by the applicant on the disposal of the shares in question.



50. The indexed cost base of the shares would be:



Acquisition cost (s.160ZYI)

x indexation factor (s.160ZJ)

14,040 x 1.322 18,561

Plus:

Incidental cost 416

Indexed Cost Base $18,977



The capital gain in question would therefore be $12,973.



51. This amount should have been included in the assessable income of the applicant for the year of income ended 30 June 1992.



52. For the reasons set out above, the Tribunal will set aside the objection decision under review and remit the matter back to the respondent with a direction that the assessment of income tax for the year ended 30 June 1992 be amended by reducing the assessed taxable income in accordance with these reasons.



I hereby certify that this and the preceding fourteen (14) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of WT93/66 and COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION.
