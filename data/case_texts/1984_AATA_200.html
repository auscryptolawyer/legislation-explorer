Re Antonia Scrivanich and Australian Taxation Office (No 2) [1984] AATA 200 (12 June 1984)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: ANTONIA SCRIVANICH

And: AUSTRALIAN TAXATION OFFICE (No. 2)

Nos. S83/47 and S83/53

Freedom of Information

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

J.O. Ballard (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information - documents concerning applicant's complaint to Grievance and Appeals Bureau and appeals to the Promotions Appeal Committee - whether exempt documents - competing public interests - whether unreasonable disclosure of information relating to the personal affairs of another person - documents given in confidence

Freedom of Information Act 1982, ss. 22, 36, 40(1), 41, 45

Scrivanich and the Public Service Board, unreported, No. S83/96 and cases listed thereunder

Science Research Council v Nasse [1979] UKHL 9; (1980) AC 1028

HEARING

CANBERRA

12:6:1984

ORDER

(1) to set aside the decision under review in so far as it is inconsistent with paragraph (2) hereof; and the same is so set aside;

(2) in substitution therefor, to give access to the applicant to the documents listed in the Appendix to the Reasons for Decision hereof and with such deletions as are indicated in that Appendix; and the same is so substituted.

DECISION

The applicant, Mrs Antonia Scrivanich, has made four separate requests under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the Act"). One, number S83/54, which concerned rectification of records, has been completed. Another, number S83/96, is the subject of separate decision as the respondent is the Public Service Board (the Public Service Board case); the evidence was heard in tandem with the evidence in this matter and addresses were taken on that matter together with this. Except for the last day the applicant was not legally represented and, although an attempt was made by the Tribunal and the court reporters to keep the matter concerning the Public Service Board separate from that concerning the Australian Taxation Office, that was not really successful.

2. This decision concerns two applications by the applicant against the Australian Taxation Office - No. S83/47 and S83/53 - which were consolidated. The application under the Act in respect of No. S83/47 reads:

Ron. Kelton

Taxation Office which mention me to the

3. Before this Tribunal evidence was given by Mr Dodson, Senior Assistant Director Accounting in the Australian Taxation Office in Adelaide and Mr Pledge, Assistant Principal Advising Officer in the Taxation Office. It is also necessary to take into account in this matter the evidence of Mr X., in respect of which an order was made under section 35 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the A.A.T. Act"), the applicant herself and a number of the applicant's workmates and generally of the proceedings in the Public Service Board case.

4. At the various hearings in 1983 the applicant represented herself. At the last of these hearings the matter was adjourned to March 1984 for the applicant to produce further evidence and for addresses. The parties were invited to make submissions in writing. On resumption in March 1984 the applicant was legally represented and called one further witness, an official of her union. The applicant was then represented by counsel who made submissions orally, counsel for the respondent presented a written submission and counsel for the applicant addressed in reply.

I express my appreciation for this classification. Without it the Tribunal would have faced a daunting task in reaching conclusions on each of the documents involved.

Category 1

6. Exemption is claimed under sections 40 and 41. These read:

"40.(1) Subject to sub-section (2), a document is

an exempt document if its disclosure under this Act

would, or could reasonably be expected to-

(a) prejudice the effectiveness of procedures or

methods for the conduct of tests, examinations

or audits by an agency;

(b) prejudice the attainment of the objects of

particular tests, examinastions or audits

conducted or to be conducted by an agency;

(c) have a substantial adverse effect on the

management or assessment of personnel by the

Commonwealth or by an agency;

(d) have a substantial adverse effect on the

proper and efficient conduct of the operations

of an agency; or

(e) have a substantial adverse effect on the

conduct by or on behalf of the Commonwealth or

an agency of industrial relations.

(2) This section does not apply to a document in

respect of matter in the document the disclosure of

which under this Act would, on balance, be in the public

interest."

41.(1) A document is an exempt document if its

disclosure under this Act would involve the unreasonable

disclosure of information relating to the personal

affairs of any person (including a deceased person).

(2) Subject to sub-section (3), the provisions of

sub-section (1) do not have effect in relation to a

request by a person for access to a document by reason

only of the inclusion in the document of matter relating

to that person.

(3) Where a request is made to an agency or

Minister for access to a document of the agency, or an

official document of the Minister, that contains

information of a medical or psychiatric nature

concerning the person making the request and it appears

to the principal officer of the agency, or to the

Minister, as the case may be, that the disclosure of the

information to that person might be prejudicial to the

physicical or mental health or well-being of that

person, the principal officer or Minister may direct

that access to the document, so far as it contains that

information, that would otherwise be given to that

person is not to be given to him but is to be given

instead to a medical practitioner to be nominated by

him."

7. In the Public Service Board case I have attempted to set out my appreciation of the operation of the Act on the applicant's claims. I see no purpose in repeating what I there said and will only discuss the law where different issues arise. It will be apparent that I have drawn much of my thinking from the House of Lords decision in Science Research Council v. Nasse, [1979] UKHL 9; (1980) AC 1028, which also concerns the balance to be drawn between the public interest of confidentiality in matters concerning promotions and the competing public interest in securing fairness between applicants for promotion.

8. T3, 1-224 (S83/53) contains Selection Committee reports and other performance appraisals of officers of the Taxation Office. Some, originally in manuscript, are illegible in the photocopies which have been provided; liberty will be given to apply in respect of these. The applicant is mentioned and/or commented on in a large number but by no means the majority of these reports. The generality of these concern assessments and/or comments on others for which exemption is claimed under both sections. Whatever may be the case under section 40 exemption is properly claimed under section 41. To disclose such information would involve unreasonable disclosure of the affairs of other persons. I do not however think it would be unreasonable to merely disclose another officer's name (or designation or seniority date) in the context of the Selection Committee reports or to disclose the name of the officer who was recommended for the particular position. In my view it is in the public interest to disclose such information to the applicant and the same applies to matters in the Committee reports regarding selection criteria and other formal matters. Plainly, exemption does not apply to assessments of the applicant and any such documents are also not exempt. The same principles apply to the documents listed under category 1 in T6, T6A and T6AA. I have indicated in the Appendix folios not exempt, the respondent is directed to grant access of these to the applicant after deleting the exempt material as indicated in the Appendix and pursuant to section 22 of the Act.

Category 2

9. In this category exemption is similarly claimed under sections 40 and 41. Folios 176 and 177 (T3, S83/53), are a letter to the Promotions Appeal Committee forwarding a selection report. Paragraph 3 deals with the applicant's claim and folio 175 contains an assessment of the applicant. The only other officer referred to is so referred as being on leave at the time of the interviews. Exemption cannot be claimed under section 41 as against the applicant, nor do I think this document qualifies for exemption under section 40. I find that this document is not an exempt document. On the other hand T3, folios 181-210 relate only to assessments of other officers which are, in my opinion, quite properly claimed as exempt under section 41 and, probably, section 40 of the Act.

10. As to folios 1-18 in T7 exemption has been properly claimed, for the same reasons, for folios 9-18 but I am unable to agree with this view as to folios 1-6 and 8. Both contain letters from the respondent concerning an appeal by the applicant; apart from paragraphs 4 and 5 in folio 2, there is no unreasonable disclosure of material concerning other persons; in my view exemption cannot be claimed under section 41; further, in my view, the public interest favours disclosure. Neither may exemption be claimed for folio 16 which is a report by Mr X. on the applicant. For the same reasons I am of the view that exemption does not attach to folios 19-20 and 35-38. However, folios 49-55 concern the private affairs of others and are plainly exempt. Folios 77-80 concern appeals against the selection of officers to perform temporarily the duties of two positions; they are exempt in so far as they concern the assessments of officers other than the applicant. The public interest favours disclosure of folios 81-82; mention is made of officers other than the applicant but not such as to bring the documents within the terms of section 41. As to folios 84-87 they should be released after deleting names of persons other than the applicant. Folio 116 contains a record of an interview with the applicant; the material purports to be a factual recording; the balance of public interest is that it be released to the applicant under section 40; no issue arises under section 41. Folios 191-192 are a referee's report on the applicant; this should be released; I take the same view of folios 211-212. In my view folios 211-212, 225-227 and 228-229 (a letter from the applicant to the Selection Committee) are not exempted. Folios 215-219 should have the names of and comments on other officers deleted; the remaining folios are exempt. Similarly out of folios 231-301 folios 231-232, 240-244 (with deletions), 259 and 291-292 are not exempted, the other folios are. Similarly in T7A folios 1-4 (with deletions of matters concerning other officers) and 9 are not exempt.

Category 3

11. Exemption is claimed under sections 40 and 41. This category deals with communication from the Promotions Appeal Committee. They are all in T3. I cannot see how the folios numbered 212-216 can possibly come under section 41. Nor can I see that publication of this very routine correspondence can be exempted under paragraph 40(1)(c). With regard to folio 225 I think the fact of an unsuccessful appeal is sufficient for an exemption under section 41 in relation to the privacy of the person thus appealing. Accordingly on these documents I find that folio 225 as well as folios 178-180 and 211 are exempt under section 41.

Category 4

12. Exemption is claimed under sections 40 and 41. The principles applied in the first 3 categories also apply here. The folios to be released are noted in the Appendix. Deletion of matters exempt under section 41 are indicated.

Category 5

13. Here exemption is claimed under sections 36 and 45 as well as sections 40 and 41. Sub-section 36(1) reads:

"36.(1) Subject to this section, a document is an

exempt document if it is a document the disclosure of

which under this Act-

(a) would disclose matter in the nature of, or

relating to, opinion, advice or recommendation

obtained, prepared or recorded, or

consultation or deliberation that has taken

place, in the course of, or for the purposes

of, the deliberative processes involved in the

functions of an agency or Minister or of the

Government of the Commonwealth; and

(b) would be contrary to the public interest."

Sub-sections (1) and (2) of section 45 read:

45.(1) A document is an exempt document if its

disclosure under this Act would constitute a breach of

confidence.

(2) Sub-section (1) does not apply to any document

to the disclosure of which paragraph 36(1) applies or

would apply, but for the operation of sub-section 36(2),

(5) or (6), being a document prepared by a Minister, a

member of the staff of a Minister, or an officer or

employee of an agency, in the course of his duties, or

by a prescribed authority in the performance of its

functions, for purposes relating to the affairs of an

agency or a Department of State."

14. This category and category 6 are different in character from the rest. They turn particularly on an alleged personal relationship which has gone sour. This cannot in my view be ignored because in the applicant's view it is central to the applicant's grievances about lack of promotion by the respondent. The reports in this category are reports on the applicant by Mr X. who, she claims, was the other party to the personal relationship. They are in the nature of reports of harrassment of Mr X. by the applicant. They are not assessments of her for promotion to more senior positions in the course of management of the agency and in some cases took place away from the work place.

15. As a matter of comment the documents are also claimed to be exempt under section 36; if that be valid it seems they cannot be exempt under section 45 by virtue of sub-section (2) of that section.

16. It seems to me that the claim under section 41 is without foundation as on a reading of the reports they really refer to the personal affairs of the applicant, not that of Mr X. I do not accept the claim under that section.

17. Both sections 36 and 40 are subject to a public interest test. I have discussed the cases on this in the Public Service Board case. It will be clear that courts and tribunals in recent years have accepted the argument for the need for confidence to encourage candour less and less. Here where the allegation is that a personal relationship has affected the applicant's prospects of promotion it seems apparent that the public interest in fairness of promotion tips more sharply in favour of release of the documents. I have no doubt that the balance of public interest is against the exemption of these documents. They therefore are not exempt under section 36 or 40.

18. The only claim therefore to remain is that under section 45 which can only arise if the documents are not documents to which paragraph 36(1) applies or would apply. Similar considerations seem to arise in regard to the report of Mr X. to a Mr Cameron in the Public Service Board case and in that matter a claim for exemption under section 45 was dropped in view of sub-section 45(2). It seems to me that similar considerations apply to Mr X's reports to his own superiors. Further, exemption cannot be obtained under section 45 by simply handing a document over and saying it is confidential. There has to be a real intention that they be kept confidential. Here the intention was to use the documents against the applicant if the need arose. I do not accept the claim for exemption under section 45 on both grounds.

19. In the result none of the documents in this category are exempt.

Category 6

20. Exemption is claimed under sections 40, 41 and 45 in respect of a bundle of private correspondence from the applicant to Mr X... It is also claimed that these documents are not in the possession of the Australian Taxation Office. I am astonished at this last submission which is directly contrary to the evidence of Mr Pledge and Mr X.; Mr Dodson even put it "they are our property at the moment". It is clear from that evidence that Mr X. handed the applicant's personal correspondence to the respondent's officer and that he did so on instructions from his supervisors at the agency. Furthermore, a claim for exemption under section 40 is plainly misconceived. The applicant's personal correspondence is not a matter to which paragraph 40(1)(c) relates nor can I see any aspect of public interest affected. As to section 41 the only person whose privacy is affected is that of the applicant. I do not think this claim for exemption should have been made.

21. The only reasonable ground upon which exemption might be claimed is section 45 as Mr X. does claim that he gave the letters to the respondent in confidence. For the applicant it was argued that these documents were not given to the respondent in confidence; it was said that if a problem arose at a later date the documents would be there and they would be evidence; it is not enough to say that you give documents in confidence as a cloak for a secondary reason for giving the documents. I accept that argument and find that the personal correspondence from the applicant to Mr X. is not exempted from disclosure.

Category 7

22. Exemption is claimed under sections 40 and 41 of the Act. The documents need to be dealt with severally. They are all in T5 of S83/53. As to folios 12 and 13, the balance of public interest under sub-section 40(2) favours release to the applicant of the assessment of herself. Clearly the assessments of others are exempted under section 41 of the Act. Section 22 provides for deletion of exempt matters. The document should be deleted of the exempt matter and the balance released.

23. The exemption under section 40 is, I think, properly claimed in respect of folios 52-57. I see a clear balance of public interest in allowing an employing department to make its own comments to the Public Service Board. This is also so for the reasons I gave for exempting a Minute of Mr Cameron in the applicant's matter against the Public Service Board.

Category 8

24. I see no public interest favouring disclosure of such documents to the applicant. Concerned as they are with the reasons for an unfavourable decision against another person, they have no bearing whatsoever on the applicant's complaint of lack of promotion or discrimination. In addition, the documents are plainly exempt under section 41.

Category 9

25. Exemption is again claimed under sections 40 and 41 of the Act. Section 41 cannot operate to exempt these documents - they concern the personal affairs of the applicant and no other person. Furthermore, I do not think a routine report largely setting out matters of a factual nature comes within the scope of either paragraph (c) or (d) of section 40. In any case, I see a public interest in disclosing to the applicant material purporting to be a factual account of matters relating to her lack of promotion.

Category 10 Now settled.

Category 11

26. This category is claimed under section 36 of the Act. I have already dealt with the claim under section 36 with respect to folios 269-271 in T3 in S83/53 under category 5. As to folios 298-302 these were dealt with in category 9 in respect of claims under sections 40 and 41; I see a public interest in disclosure which is of application to both sections 36 and 40.

27. I cannot see how exemption can be claimed on folio 231 in T3 under section 36 which is, in point of time, earlier than the deliberations relating to an appeal by the applicant and I find it not exempted. The question then arises as to whether it can be claimed as part of some ongoing deliberative process regarding management. Management is dealt with in section 40 of the Act and exemption is not claimed under this provision. I do not think this particular document can be treated as being prepared as part of a deliberative process. In my view this document is not exempt.

28. Folios 276-278 are of a factual nature; they are thus not exempt under section 36. The same applies to folios 293-302; in particular, folios 295-297 purport to be factual accounts of what was said at interviews with the applicant. In my view the public interest also favours disclosure of these documents.

29. The same applies to folios 2-4 in T4 in S83/53. This seems to be a straight report of a visit by the applicant. However, I see folios 83-84 as exempt under section 36 of the Act.

30. Folios 12-13 in T5 in S83/53 have already been dealt with in category 7. I see no public interest why the applicant should not be made aware of the comments concerning herself. The document as amended is not exempt.

Category 12

31. Exemption is again claimed under section 36. The first document is folio 283-287 in T3 in S83/53; it is an examination by the Deputy Commissioner of the applicant's Regulation 33 appeal; it is within the class of documents to which paragraph 36(1)(a) of the Act relates; the public interest criteria is very much the same as Mr Cameron's report in the Public Service Board case; the document is exempt as claimed. The same considerations apply to folios 80-82 in T4. I have already found folios 52-53 in T5 exempt in category 7; it is unnecessary to consider it again. Folios 59 and 60 in T5 also come within section 36(1)(a) and, in my opinion satisfy the public interest criteria and are exempt.

Category 13

32. Exemption is again claimed under section 36. I have already found folios 80-82 in T4 of S83/53 and folios 52-53 and 59-60 in T5 exempt and it is unnecessary to consider them again. I do not, however, consider that folios 55 and 56 satisfy the public interest criteria in paragraph 36(1)(b); they are not therefore exempt.

33. Some documents are listed more than once under different categories. It will, of course, be plain that if a document is found to qualify for exemption under any exemption provision that it is exempt.

34. I have tried to consider the considerable quantity of documents through Appendix A to the respondent's submission. Some, however, of the documents have been illegible in the photocopy. I may, moreover, have misapprehended that appendix or it may have errors (understandable in such a complex document). Liberty will be given to apply in case I have failed to make a decision in respect of any document, misapprehended any document or generally.
