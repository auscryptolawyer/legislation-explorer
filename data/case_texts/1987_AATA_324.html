Re Lance Adam Ogston and Secretary To the Department of Social Security [1987] AATA 324 (11 September 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: LANCE ADAM OGSTON

And: SECRETARY TO THE DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL SECURITY

No. N86/893

AAT No. 3772

Social Security

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Dr R.A. Hayes (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Social Security - amnesty provisions - false statement - knowledge at time of statement.

Social Security Legislation Amendment Act 1986, s.45

HEARING

SYDNEY

11:9:1987

ORDER

The decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

The Review

This is an application for review of a decision by a delegate of the Secretary to the Department of Social Security that the applicant was not a person to whom s.45 of the Social Security Legislation Amendment Act 1986 ("the Act") could be applied, and therefore amnesty in relation to the applicant's liabilities under the Social Security Act 1947 could not be granted.

2. The amnesty provisions, as set out in the Act, were introduced following extensive publicity on 3 June 1986. This was after the period of amnesty, being 12 February 1986 (the date of its official announcement) to 31 May 1986. During the period of amnesty, decisions by Departmental officers were governed by guidelines issued to regional offices.

The Facts

3. On 20 February 1986 the applicant applied to the Department for amnesty in respect of overpayment of unemployment benefit. This application revealed that the applicant had concealed his wife's earnings.

4. During the early part of 1983, the applicant was in receipt of unemployment benefit. His wife was not working. He had two children, a daughter aged 5 years, and a son aged 2 years. The applicant had been unemployed for a significant period of time, and was finding it difficult to cope, financially, on the unemployment benefit.

5. Then in August 1983, the applicant was successful in obtaining a position through the "red scheme" or work pause programme. This was with the Hastings Shire Council and lasted for 16 weeks. The applicant had expectations that this could turn into a permanent position. This did not eventuate. The job ended on 23 December 1983.

6. In this period of employment, the applicant's wife obtained a casual waitressing job at the local RSL Club. This was in the lead up to Christmas, with a greater demand for casual staff. Her employment, at this stage, was regarded as only temporary. There was no guarantee that it would continue after the holiday season.

7. On 29 December 1983, the applicant lodged a claim for unemployment benefit. As part of that claim he stated that neither he nor his wife were employed (including casual employment). In addition the applicant signed a statement, which was also signed by his wife, in which he reiterated that neither he nor his wife had any other income than that disclosed in the statement or claim form.

8. Despite this, the applicant's wife continued in employment, on a casual basis, through to 1986. Although the applicant continued to complete Applications for Continuation of Unemployment Benefit forms on a regular basis, no disclosure was made of his wife's employment until 20 February 1986.

9. The applicant gave evidence in which he said that at the time of his claim for benefit in December 1983, he was told by a Departmental officer he would have to wait 6 weeks before he would receive his payment of benefit. While this did not eventuate, in that the applicant was granted benefit from 2 January 1984, the applicant's evidence was that on being told that he would have to wait, he did not see how he could manage financially.

10. His fear was that his wife's employment would not continue beyond this 6 week period, and that disclosure of his wife's employment would jeopardise their financial security. The applicant did not expect that he would be on unemployment benefit for any extended period, and this influenced his attitude.

11. All these events occurred against a background of financial stress, in which the applicant and his wife were concerned about their ability to meet their commitments. The applicant's daughter was due to commence school, and there were the normal expenses associated with this. In addition, while the applicant had a period of 16 weeks employment, this was preceded by a substantial period of unemployment, during which the applicant and his wife accumulated a number of debts.

12. When the amnesty was announced in February 1986, the applicant and his wife saw it as a way of extricating themselves from the deception in which they had become involved. The applicant approached the Department and completed the application for amnesty. Unbeknown to him, a few days earlier, an anonymous caller had contacted the Department and advised that the applicant's wife was employed.

The Legislation

13. The application for amnesty is to be considered pursuant to Part III of the Act. The substantive eligibility provisions are set out in s.45.

14. "45. (1) Where -

(a) a person has received a relevant pension in respect

of a period that included 12 February 1986;

(b) due to the occurrence of an event or a change of

circumstances during the relevant period -

(i) the person ceased to be entitled to receive

the relevant pension or a related relevant

pension; or

(ii) the rate at which the person was entitled to

receive the relevant pension or a related

relevant pension decreased;

(c) the person failed to comply with an obligation

imposed by the Act in relation to notifying the

Department of the occurrence of the event or the

change in circumstances;

(d) during the period commencing on 12 February 1986

and ending on the expiration of 31 May 1986, the

person has voluntarily informed the Department of

the occurrence of the event or the change of

circumstances; and

(e) the person has not, before so informing the

Department, been -

(i) charged with an offence in respect of the

failure to notify referred to in paragraph

(c); or

(ii) informed in writing by an officer that

proceedings have been or will be instituted in

respect of such an offence or that an amount

of money paid as a result of the failure to

notify is recoverable by the Commonwealth,

the person -

(f) is not guilty of an offence in respect of the

failure to notify; and

(g) is not indebted to the Commonwealth in respect of

any amount of the relevant pension or any related

relevant pension paid to the person, being an

amount that would not have been paid had there not

been the failure to notify.

(2) Where -

(a) a person has received a payment of a relevant

pension in respect of a period that included 12

February 1986;

(b) the person has made a false statement to an

officer, being a statement the making of which

has -

(i) resulted in the relevant pension or a related

relevant pension being paid to the person

during the relevant period -

(A) at a time when the person was not

entitled to be paid that relevant pension

or related relevant pension; or

(B) at a higher rate than the rate at which

the person was entitled to be paid that

relevant pension or related relevant

pension; or

(ii) prevented the cancellation of, or a reduction

of the rate of, the relevant pension or a

related relevant pension, being paid to the

person during the relevant period;

(c) in the case of a statement of a kind referred to in

sub-paragraph (b)(i) - at the time of making the

statement, the person did not know that the

statement was false;

(d) during the period commencing on 12 February 1986

and endidng on the expiration of 31 May 1986, the

person has voluntarily informed the Department of

the making of the false statement; and

(e) the person has not, before so informing the

Department, been -

(i) charged with an offence in respect of the

making of the false statement; or

(ii) informed in writing by an officer that

proceedings have been or will be instituted in

respect of such an offence or that an amount

of money paid as a result of the making of the

false statement is recoverable by the

Commonwealth,

the person -

(f) is not guilty of any offence in respect of the

making of the false statement; and

(g) is not indebted to the Commonwealth in respect of

any amount of the relevant pension or any related

relevant pension paid to the person, being an

amount that would not have been paid had the false

statement not been made."

15. Two situations are covered. The first is where there has been an overpayment due to failure to notify the Department of a change in circumstances. The second is a person has made a false statement to an officer, resulting in the receipt of a higher rate of benefit than that to which he or she was entitled. This is the situation in this matter.

Findings

16. Section 45(2)(c) requires that where an applicant has made a false statement, at the time the statement was made eligibility can only be made out if he or she did not know that the statement was false. It is clear from the applicant's evidence that he cannot satisfy this requirement. Whatever his motivation, it is clear that in filling out the claim form, he deliberately concealed the fact of his wife's employment. Accordingly I have no option but to find that the applicant did not satisfy the amnesty criteria.

Comments about Recovery

17. At the hearing of this matter, the question of recovery of any overpayment was raised. This was not an issue before me. The amount is substantial. On what I have read, heard and seen, of the applicant and his family, they are deserving of sympathy and tolerance from the Department. The applicant acted out of a combination of desparation and stupidity. His motive was concern for his family. He erred. But I cannot regard he and his wife as cynical defrauders of the system. Far from it. The applicant's evidence regarding his financial circumstances makes it clear that he does not have the resources to repay the sum at the present time. Any action by the Department would have to take this into account. It should also take into account the impact of moves for recovery on a young, growing family. I hope that these comments will be taken into account. Mr McDougall, for the respondent, assured me that they would.

18. In light of my finding as set out above, I have no alternative but to affirm the decision under review.
