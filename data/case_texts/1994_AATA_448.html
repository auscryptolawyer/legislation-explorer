Barlow and Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales [1994] AATA 448 (23 February 1994)

Last Updated: 18 September 2008



Administrative Appeals Tribunal



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION



TAX AGENT - Application for registration. Accountant employed in private practice preparing tax returns. Whether a substantial involvement in income tax matters and whether involved in a broad range of income tax returns.



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - Sections 251BC, 251JA

Income Tax Regulations - Regulation 156



Re Civiti and Tax Agents' Board of Victoria 90 ATC 2039



AAT Decision No 9323



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL ) N93/622

)

Applicant

Respondent

Tribunal : Senior Member M.D. Allen

Place : Sydney



Decision : The decision under review is affirmed.



(Sgd) M.D. ALLEN

REASONS FOR DECISION



23 February 1994 Senior Member M.D. Allen



1. By notice lodged 17 September 1993 the Applicant sought further review by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal of a decision of the Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales made 21 May 1993, refusing his application to be registered as a Tax Agent.



2. The requirements for registration as a Tax Agent are set out in Sections 251JA and 251BC of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") and Regulation 156 of the Income Tax Regulations ("the Regulations"). So far as is applicable to the Applicant Section 251JA states inter alia:



"(1) The Board shall register the applicant as a tax agent if the applicant satisfies the Board that:



(a) if the applicant is a natural person:



(i) the applicant is a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters; "



What is meant by the expression "a fit and proper person" is dealt with in Sub-Section 251BC(1) which states inter alia:

"Without limiting the generality of an expression used in this Part, but subject to this section, a person is not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers in income tax matters, as at a particular time, if:



(a) the person is not a natural person;



(b) both of the following conditions are satisfied:



(i) the person was not registered as a tax agent, or as a nominee, for the purposes of this Part immediately before the commencement of section 39 of the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No. 2) 1988;



(ii) the person does not hold such qualifications (whether academic, by way of experience or otherwise) as are prescribed;



(c) the person has not attained the age of 18 years;



(d) the person is not of good fame, integrity and character;



(e) the person has been convicted of a serious taxation offence during the previous 5 years; or



(f) the person is under sentence of imprisonment for a serious taxation offence."



3. Regulation 156 reads inter alia:

"(1) For the purposes of subparagraph 251BC(1)(b)(ii) of the Act, the following qualifications are prescribed:



(a) the person:



(i) shall have completed the academic requirements for the award of a degree, diploma or other qualification from an Australian university, college of advanced education or other tertiary institution of an equivalent standard, and have passed examinations in such subjects, under whatever name, which an appropriate authority of the university, college of advanced education or other tertiary institution certifies to the Board to represent a course of study in accountancy of not less than 3 years' duration and in commercial law of not less than 18 months' duration or shall possess such other qualifications as the Board regards as equivalent to those qualifications;



(ii) shall have:



(A) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for not less than a total of 12 months in the preceding 5 years;



(B) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent that the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in sub-subparagraph (A); or



(C) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as the Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant employment as referred to in sub-subparagraph (A); and



(iii) shall have, by written examination, successfully completed a course of study in Australian income tax law acceptable to the Board:



... "



and



"(2) In this regulation "relevant employment" means employment by a person or a partnership or as a member of a partnership in the course of which there has been substantial involvement in income tax matters including:



(a) the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns;



(b) the preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued in respect of such returns; and



(c) the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns, assessments or objections."



4. It was conceded by Mr Elliott of counsel, who appeared for the Respondent, that the Applicant was, except for paragraph 251BC(1)(b)(ii) of the Act, in all respects a fit and proper person to be registered and had the requisite academic qualifications. However, it was argued for the Respondent that the Applicant's experience was such that he had not been engaged in "relevant employment" as that term is defined is Sub Regulation 156(2).



5. Having completed his degree the Applicant gained employment with Warren Jack & Associates. That firm specialised in preparing taxation returns. The Applicant gave evidence that at that firm he prepared 10 individual returns, 2 superannuation returns, 5 partnership returns, and 85 company returns. He did not prepare or lodge any objections to an income tax assessment.



6. The company returns prepared at Warren Jack & Associates were predominantly for small exempt proprietary companies. A large number were for small tradesmen or sub-contractors who carried on their trades under the umbrella of a proprietary company.



7. Tax Agents' Board Form TAB130 is to be completed by an Applicant's employer or former employer. This was not done by Warren Jack & Associates and I accept the Applicant's evidence that at present the New South Wales Department of Industrial Relations is prosecuting the former employer for breaches of an award regarding the Applicant. In these circumstances the reason for non completion of the form is understandable, although reprehensible. Further, I accept the Applicant's evidence that he did not subpoena Warren Jack, the proprietor of the firm, as he would have been a hostile witness and, as a layman, the Applicant did not consider he had the skills to cope with such a witness.



8. Following his employment with Warren Jack & Associates the Applicant was employed by I.T.E. Pty Ltd from July 1991 to September 1991. That company operates from Shopping Centres and prepares individual returns. Some of those returns involved small traders but, predominantly, were PAYE returns. No other type of return apart from individual returns were undertaken by the Applicant whilst employed by this company.



9. Having ceased employment with I.T.E. Pty Ltd, the Applicant was employed as an accountant by Keith J. Anderson & Associates from September 1991 to August 1992.



10. The Applicant prepared a Form TAB130 and sent it to the principal of the firm, one Keith James Anderson, for completion. Anderson then amended the figures in that form and the Applicant disputes the figures nominated by Anderson. On the Applicant's figures he compiled 110 individual returns, 2 trust returns, 14 partnership returns and 15 company returns whilst employed by Keith J. Anderson & Associates.



11. In addition to his work as an Accountant the Applicant also prepared a newsletter for clients of that firm, outlining various legislative changes regarding Superannuation and the Training Guarantee Levy. He did not, however, whilst in Keith J. Anderson & Associates' employ, prepare any superannuation fund returns, fringe benefit tax returns, although he did prepare one objection to an assessment.



12. The Applicant left the employ of Keith J. Anderson & Associates in August 1992 and commenced employment with Sunbeam Corporation Ltd as an Accountant. His job entailed the preparation of monthly sales tax returns, fringe benefit tax returns, assisting in the preparation of company tax returns for subsidiary companies and other accounting duties.



13. Since March 1993 to date the Applicant has been employed by Stephen Lee and Co of York Street, Sydney, as an Accountant. He estimated that 85% of his time is spent on income tax matters and the balance on other aspects of an accountant's practice.



14. The Applicant stated that in this practice he prepared mainly company returns. A large number of the companies and individuals involved are importers or engaged in joint venture agreements with mainland Chinese. In evidence in chief the Applicant stated unequivocally that he had prepared 5 trust returns and 5 fringe benefit tax returns whilst employed by this practice but, in cross-examination, resiled from this stating he had become confused and these were returns which would be undertaken in 1994.



15. I also have doubts regarding the Applicant's evidence as to the nature and extent of the work undertaken by him at Keith J. Anderson & Associates. As noted above, the proprietor of the firm corrected the Applicant's figures on the Form TAB130 and, in cross-examination, the Applicant was shown to be prone to exaggerate his experience.



16. Further cross-examination revealed that the Applicant has little to no experience in preparing returns for primary producers or professionals and his experience in Superannuation and Trust returns is confined to the less complex examples of the genus.



17. The Applicant's experience in objections to assessment is also limited. He has prepared two objections, one of which related to a return prepared by him and he has sought two rulings from the Commissioner.



18. In re Civiti and Tax Agents' Board of Victoria 90 ATC 2039 at 2043 Deputy President Forrest said in relation to Sub-regulation 156 (2):

"The qualifying adjective 'broad', is a word of plain meaning and common understanding. It has a similar meaning to 'wide'. ... I do not accept the 'ambiguity' argument, or that it is open to put on the word 'broad' the construction urged by the applicant. The word 'broad' is not one of similar meaning to 'substantial'. In the context in which they are used, both terms are used as qualifying adjectives. 'Broad range' does not mean the same thing as 'substantial involvement' and vice versa. There may be one without the other. The Respondent has to be satisfied the applicant has acquired both in order to have been engaged in relevant employment within the meaning of reg. 156.



Subregulation 156(2) cannot, in my opinion, be construed as applying to a particular category of returns. It is not the experience within a category of returns but rather the scope of the returns which is the test to be applied.



It is not a relevant consideration that reg. 156 does not specify the categories of returns in defining 'relevant employment'. It is explicit in the language used, that wide-ranging experience is required. A lack of experience in the areas of returns referred to, in my opinion, precludes satisfaction of the test of relevant employment. ..."



19. Further, as was pointed out by Deputy President Forrest in Civiti supra, registration as a Tax Agent is registration for all purposes and a limited right of practice cannot be granted. Whilst there is much force in the Applicant's argument that the Board cannot, in requiring experience, make the spectrum too wide otherwise no one would be registered, there are certain statutory criteria to be met. On the other hand, this Tribunal must be alert to any tendency in the Tax Agents' Board to become a body dedicated to preserving the status quo and the income of those persons who are already registered as Tax Agents.



20. The Applicant further submitted that on a percentage basis the returns prepared by him reflected the type of returns lodged throughout Australia, for example, across Australia Superannuation returns comprised 1% of all returns lodged and of the returns prepared by him Superannuation returns comprised 1% of the total. Similarly, partnership returns were 5% of the Australian total and 5% of the Applicant's total.



21. Superficially attractive, this argument confuses percentage of work done with experience. I do not find it incongruous that a qualification for practice should require a greater exposure to a particular facet of practice than is undertaken by the profession generally.



22. A similar answer can be made to the Applicant's contention that he had had enough experience and knowledge to know how to use reference materials to assist him in those areas where he lacked practical experience. This argument is undoubtedly true but, unfortunately, the regulations specifically require practical experience (ie preparation or examination) on top of academic qualifications.



23. In particular, the regulations require substantial involvement in the preparation or examination of objections to assessments. The Applicant has been involved in two such exercises and I do not regard this as substantial involvement.



24. In addition, I do not regard the Applicant as having prepared or examined a broad range of income tax returns at present. However, given his evidence as to his present employment, I consider that he will meet this objection in the near future, particularly once the trust and fringe benefit tax returns, referred to in evidence, have been completed.



25. For the reasons above I therefore affirm the decision under review.
