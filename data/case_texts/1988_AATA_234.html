Re Barry Saunders and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 234 (26 July 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: BARRY SAUNDERS

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. V87/584

AAT No. 4508

Freedom of Information

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.J. Jennings QC (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information - Taxation records - basis for amended assessment - whether disclosure could prejudice conduct of investigation - whether disclosure of internal working documents contrary to public interest.

Freedom of Information Act 1982 - s.36(1), 37(1).

Murtagh v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1984] AATA 249; 54 ALR 313

HEARING

HOBART

26:7:1988

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review in so far as it relates to the following documents 1A, 1, 2A, 2, 5A, 6, 8, 9 and 10.

The Tribunal varies the decision under review in so far as it relates to the following documents 3, 5, 7, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 by deciding that the documents are not exempt from disclosure under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 subject to deletion therefrom of the material referred to in these reasons.

The Tribunal makes no decision in relation to the following documents 0, 4 and 16 for the reasons referred to.

There will be liberty to apply.

DECISION

This is an application to review a decision by the respondent to refuse the applicant access to certain documents pursuant to a request made under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the Act").

2. In relation to a Notice of Amended Assessment for the income year ended 30th June 1977 the applicant requested access "to copies of all records and documents relating to the abovementioned amended assessment and all decisions taken about that amended assessment including departmental memoranda, reports, submissions, recommendations and general information relating thereto to the extent to which such information is not exempted under the Act."

3. Access was originally refused to all documents and this refusal was repeated following the internal review required by s.54 of the Act.

4. The applicant's counsel, Mr. de Wijn, made it clear to the Tribunal at the outset of the hearing that he was not seeking all documents relating to the nature of the investigation or to names of tax officers. He was seeking the elements giving rise to the assessment and in particular the final step in that process.

5. The Tribunal began the hearing by receiving the documents lodged pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 and admitting affidavits sworn by taxation officers Messrs. Buckley and Toner. Following submissions by both counsel it received 18 documents for inspection pursuant to s.64 of the Act and made directions pursuant to s.35 of the 1975 Act restricting the publication of evidence and argument to the respondent, his servants or agents. (Transcript p 30)

6. During the hearing two additional documents were produced and portions of documents 3, 11, 12, 14 and 15 were released to the applicant.

7. Mr. Buckley gave evidence in closed hearing, but later repeated certain parts of it in the presence of counsel for the applicant whose efforts to cross examine the witness were the subject of repeated objections most of which were upheld upon the ground that answers would tend to reveal the contents of a document for which exemption was claimed. Mr. Toner gave all his evidence in open hearing.

8. At the conclusion of the evidence the parties agreed to make written submissions. On 26 May 1988 a sanitized copy of the respondent's submission was delivered to the applicant's counsel who made submissions both before and after receiving those of the respondent.

9. The respondent's submission contains an amended schedule of documents claimed to be exempt and indicating the particular provisions of the Act on which he relies in respect of each. This schedule makes it unnecessary to refer to any previous lists relied on.

10. In May 1979 the applicant was issued with a Notice of Income Tax Assessment in respect of the year ended 30 June 1977. He was required to pay tax amounting to $56,945.00. He immediately objected against that assessment, but it was not until January 1984 that his objection was disallowed and referred at his request to the Board of Review whose jurisdiction is now vested in this Tribunal.

11. On 5 December 1986 the respondent issued an amended assessment in respect of the 1977 tax year seeking payment of an additional sum of $708,680.00 in tax and $700,015.08 in penalties imposed under s.226 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1932 ("the Tax Act"). A tax adjustment sheet issued at the time of the amended assessment explained the basic ground for its issue in the following terms:-

"Inclusion of additional income, including income

derived directly or indirectly from activities

associated with or involved in, tax minimization

and/or tax avoidance planning, operation, promotion

or advice, trading or dealing in shares or other

securities etc, taxation and accountancy work, or

activities or operations of a similar nature."

12. An objection against that amended assessment was disallowed on 22 December 1987 and the applicant has requested that it be referred to the Taxation Division of this Tribunal.

13. Neither reference to this Tribunal has been heard. They will be referred to as "The Tax Appeals".

14. In his affidavit dated 2 May 1988 Mr. Buckley said:-

"The Respondent ("the Commissioner") has undertaken

an investigation of the affairs of the Applicant

and entities controlled by him ("the

investigation") in order to ascertain the extent of

the Applicant's income from promotion of tax

avoidance schemes and from other sources, for the

purpose of ascertaining the taxable income of the

Applicant and determining whether there has been a

breach of, or a failure to comply with, the tax

laws. Various senior officers of the

Commissioner, including myself, have formed the

view that the Applicant has been involved in

large-scale tax avoidance and evasion. In

particular it appears that he has been the promoter

of a number of tax avoidance schemes involving

large amounts of tax and for which he has received

substantial remuneration. The structures of

companies and trusts set up by the Applicant for

this purpose are elaborate and complex, and the

process of investigation is long and complicated.

The investigation is not yet complete."

15. Because the 1977 amended assessment was made more than six years after the date upon which tax became due under the original assessment (June 1979) the respondent was required to form an opinion under s.170(2)(a) of the Tax Act that there had been an avoidance of tax by the applicant due to fraud or evasion. This opinion is required to enable an amended assessment to issue where it is alleged that there has not been a full and true disclosure of all the material facts necessary for assessment.

16. It became apparent early in the hearing before the Tribunal, and indeed so much was expressly conceded, that one of the applicant's reasons for pursuing his claim under the Act was to procure documents revealing facts which formed the basis of the opinion formed under s.170(2)(a) of the Tax Act.

17. The final submission made to the Tribunal on behalf of the applicant refers to the administrative steps in the process of making an amended assessment as involving the formation of that statutory opinion, the application of the Tax Act to a particular state of relevant facts and the exercise of directions with respect to penalty. That there must be documents which evidence that procedure was conceded, he said, in paragraph 5 of Mr. Toner's affidavit which is in the following terms:-

"Certain of the documents listed in the schedule

are the subject of a claim for exemption under

sub-section 36(1) of the FOI Act. These documents

contain material of a deliberative nature relating

to the investigation of various aspects of the tax

avoidance arrangements in which the Applicant was

involved. They discuss the applicability of

certain provisions of the tax laws to those

arrangements and contain recommendations as to

future action. These documents form part of the

decision-making process of the ATO. The hallmark

of the deliberative processes involved in the

functions of the ATO is the expression and

subsequent consideration of views, based on

evidence gathered by investigation, as to the

appropriate basis for making and, if necessary,

defending assessments."

18. Much of the argument eventually presented related to the question of public interest referred to s.36(1) of the Act. Mr. de Wijn was also conscious of the importance in this review of exemptions claimed under s.37(1)(a) of the Act.

19. It is convenient therefore to state two provisions which were primarily relied on by the respondent to claim exemption in respect of all documents save that s.36 was not advanced in the case of documents 6 and 10 (which I find are exempt by reason of s.37(1)(b)).

"36.(1) Subject to this section, a document is an exempt

document if it is a document the disclosure of which

under this Act -

(a) would disclose matter in the nature of, or

relating to, opinion, advice or recommendation

obtained, prepared or recorded, or

consultation or deliberation that has taken

place, in the course of, or for the purposes

of, the deliberative processes involved in the

functions of an agency or Minister or of the

Government of the Commonwealth; and

(b) would be contrary to the public interest."

"37.(1) A document is an exempt document if its

disclosure under this Act would, or could reasonably be

expected to -

(a) prejudice the conduct of an investigation of a

breach, or possible breach, of the law, or a

failure, or possible failure, to comply with a

law relating to taxation or prejudice the

enforcement or proper administration of the

law in a particular instance;

(b) disclose, or enable a person to ascertain, the

existence or identity of a confidential source

of information in relation to the enforcement

or administration of the law; or

20. The finding in respect of Documents 6 and 10 requires no further elaboration because Mr. de Wijn did not dispute that documents which were obtained from other sources were exempt, because their disclosure would reveal the existence or identity of a confidential source of information.

Section 37(1)(a)

21. The evidence given in confidence by Mr. Buckley supported the respondent's case for exemption of a number of documents under s.37(1)(a) (see transcript pages 40-115, especially 40-43, 50-56, 80-82, 93-95, 100-101).

22. Mr. de Wijn argued that the only offence relevant to the documents sought "in this case" was failure to disclose income in his 1977 tax return, that the issue of the 1977 amended assessments and penalties imposed was a discrete issue and that there could not be any prejudice in respect of the relevant investigation into this alleged non-disclosure.

23. That argument ignores the terms of s.37(1)(a). It is a sufficient ground for exemption if disclosure could reasonably be expected to prejudice the conduct of any investigation of the type referred to or prejudice the enforcement or proper administration of the law in any particular instance.

24. It is not sufficient for the applicant to say there can be no prejudice to the pending tax appeals. The respondent does not rely on an investigation into that assessment alone and furthermore he also claims prejudice of the type which does not even depend on the existence of an investigation.

25. In my opinion the claims for exemption in respect of a number of documents in the Schedule succeed because they are directly concerned with matters arising under s.37(1)(a). They are 1A, 1, 2A, 2, 5A, 8, 9 and 11.

26. Paragraphs 22 - 25 of the respondent's written submission specify in detail the matter which would be revealed by disclosure of documents 1, 1A, 2 and 2A. I will not repeat it here as the arguments were presented in confidence. Paragraph 28 deals with Document 5A in a similar manner.

27. The submission contains passages in paragraphs 31, 32 and 34 which have also been deleted from the sanitized version given to the applicant's counsel. Those deletions explain that the matter which would be revealed if documents 8, 9 and 11 were disclosed would be matter entitled to exemption under s.37(1)(a), even although the specific provisions of the Act are not expressly mentioned. I accept the submission as valid.

28. However, in my opinion the following documents do not qualify for exemption under s.37: 4, 5, 7, 14, 15 and 16. It is convenient therefore to consider next whether they are entitled to the protection of s.36.

Section 36(1)

29. In each case there is clearly material of the kind referred to in s.36(1)(a). The issue therefore is whether or not their disclosure would be contrary to the public interest by virtue of s.36(1)(b).

30. There is some material in Documents 7 and 14 which is no more than a restatement of the material which I have found to be protected because its disclosure would reveal a confidential source of information of the kind protected by s.37(1)(b). They contain reference to material in Document 6. It is therefore exempt in any event and I accordingly exclude it from present consideration.

31. The nature of the relevant material in all of these documents relates to questions for determination of the respondent prior to issue of the amended assessment.

32. In Document 4 there is discussion about the required finding under s.170(1)(a) of the Tax Act and proposed penalties. Procedures to be adopted in arriving at these decisions are discussed.

33. Document 5 is a telex message confirming a decision for action, and specifying the basis of assessment and indicating a further requirement.

34. Documents 7 and 14 (already partly released up to the first 3 paragraphs) detail:-

(a) Legal authority in support of amending the

assessment after the 6 year limit referred to in

s.170 of the Tax Act.

(b) Four paragraphs which reveal the confidential

source of information mentioned above.

(c) The finding pursuant to s.170(1)(a).

(d) The recommendation which has been released in

Document 15, but omitting the finding in the

previous paragraph (i.e. (c)).

35. The only distinction between the recommendation in Document 7 (April 1986) and that in Document 14 (November 1986) lies in the figures for the proposed assessment. There is no reason why the figures contained in Document 7 should not be similarly released.

36. The release of Document 15 would automatically follow a decision to release those parts of Documents 7 and 14 which are in question. It contains no more information than they do excluding of course the signature of the officer who issued it. Document 15 is already partly released. Its release will only inform the applicant whether the avoidance claimed is said to be due to fraud, evasion or both.

37. Document 16 is entitled "file notes by Auditor re procedures for issue of amended assessments." It consists of nine notes made on the day the amended assessment was issued i.e. 5 December 1986. They refer to a conversation concerning the pending tax appeal and procedures for serving the amended assessment.

38. There are thus two broad classes of material which the respondent claims it would be contrary to the public interest to reveal to the applicant.

39. First, is the applicant entitled to know whether the amended assessment is based on fraud or evasion or both?

40. Second, is the applicant entitled to know upon what basis, including any particular provision of the Tax Act, the respondent relies in arriving at the necessary opinion to proceed after expiry of the six year period? Is he also entitled to know upon what legal authority the respondent relies in arriving at the same opinion?

41. Counsel for the applicant relied on a prior decision of this Tribunal, Murtagh v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1984] AATA 249; 54 ALR 313 (Davies J, Sir Ernest Coates and R.A. Sinclair) in which the respondent failed to satisfy the Tribunal that any of the documents before it (including some which would be relevant to a pending hearing before the Taxation Board of Review) should be treated as exempt from disclosure because that would be contrary to the public interest.

42. The applicant's counsel urged that it could not be contrary to the public interest for him to learn the basis for formation of the necessary opinion under s.170 of the Tax Act, or of the provisions of the Act upon which the respondent proposed to rely.

43. The respondent cannot be bound to limit any argument he presents at the tax appeals to those provisions of the Act or legal principles which are stated in internal documents prior to the hearing. The Tax Act and the manner in which it has been or should be interpreted are matters of public knowledge and public interest. There is nothing confidential about the law.

44. The Tribunal said in Murtagh at page 331:-

"It was put for the respondent that the disclosure of

the information would prejudice the respondent's case

before the Taxation Board of Review. We think that, in

principle, the questions under s.36 must be whether or

not the disclosure of the information is likely to

enhance or inhibit the quality of the decision of the

Taxation Board of Review. But we need not deal with

this aspect of the matter further. In the present

case, there is no aspect of the documents which are in

issue the disclosure of which would be likely to

prejudice the respondent's case before the Taxation

Board of Review."

45. In so far as the material under discussion in documents 5, 7 and 14 is concerned, disclosure of the legal material referred to would not be likely to prejudice the respondent's case at the pending Tax Appeals let alone inhibit the quality of the Tribunal's decision.

46. Likewise it can only be for some tactical reason that the respondent does not wish to reveal the decision to which he came under s.170 of the Tax Act. The public interest requires that a person who is proceeded against in respect of income earnt more than six years after original assessment is entitled to be told which statutory basis has been chosen to enable such a claim to be made.

47. I therefore conclude that the questions posed in paragraphs 39 and 40 above should be answered in the affirmative. The claim for exemption under s.36(1) does not therefore succeed in respect of Documents 5, 7, 14 and 15. In each case however names of tax officers should be deleted.

48. In his written submission Mr. Maxwell counsel for the respondent said:-

"Documents canvassing legal arguments which may be

relied on to support the application of particular

sections in assessing the Applicant (e.g. in documents

14 and 7) should be treated as analogous to documents

covered by legal professional privilege, although no

such claim is here made. It is contrary to the public

interest for the applicant to know in advance the legal

arguments upon which the Respondent would rely in

defending the assessment."

Mr. de Wijn replied:-

"The Applicant repeats that he does not seek documents

canvassing legal arguments. What the Applicant seeks is

documents evidencing the way in which the assessment was

in fact made and the basis upon which it was made, not

arguments upon which the assessment will ultimately be

defended."

49. I have taken the view that the legal arguments mentioned in documents 7 and 14 do evidence the way in which the amended assessment was in fact made, and the basis upon which it was made, just as document 5 reveals a basis for the decision.

50. I also find for the same reasons that in respect of those parts of the same documents which I have just considered they are not exempt by reason of s.37(1)(a) of the Act. There could be no prejudice to the enforcement or proper administration of the law if disclosure produces no impediment to the decision making process on the hearing of the Tax Appeals.

51. Documents 4 and 16 would appear to be covered by the following submission on behalf of the applicant (at page 8):-

"The applicant has made it plain already that he doesn't

seek documents disclosing procedures and if any of the

documents in the schedule do in fact disclose

procedures, as distinct from the results of the

application of procedures, then reference as to those

procedures can be deleted."

52. Document O was produced for the first time during the hearing and released to the applicant without a handwritten footnote on the second page. That footnote does not relate to the 1977 amended assessment and I do not therefore regard it as a document in respect of which a decision is required.

53. Document 3 was released during the hearing except as to the words in a footnote which followed the phrase "make no allowance for ...." The words omitted are entitled to exemption under s.41(1) because they relate to the personal affairs of an individual other than Mr. Sheehan. The applicant's submission does not seek the release of such information.

54. Document 12 was partly released during the hearing. Since the hearing the respondent has agreed to release the remainder subject to the production of a consent by the party whose taxation affairs are there referred to.

55. Document 13 has also been released in part and no objection has been received to its total release.
