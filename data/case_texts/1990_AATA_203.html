Re John Kirby and Commissioner of Superannuation [1990] AATA 203 (4 September 1990)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JOHN KIRBY

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. V88/863

AAT No. 6173

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

B.M. Forrest(1) (Deputy President), Woodard(1) (Member) and

Brewer(1) (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - Benefit Classification Certificate for conditions "History of polyarthritis" and "minimal change glomerulonephritis", whether a "real risk", decision varied.

Superannuation Act 1976 ss 16(4)(5)(6)(8), 154(6)

Re Bergin and Commissioner for Superannuation 19 ALD 279

Re Nicholson and Commissioner of Superannuation 14 ALD 217

Gelsthorpe and Commissioner for Superannuation (unreported decision No. 4724 of 4 November 1988)

HEARING

MELBOURNE

4:9:1990

ORDER

The decision under review is varied and the matter remitted to the respondent with a direction that for the condition "history of polyarthritis" there be substituted the condition "history of back pain" and for the condition "minimal change glomerulonephritis" there be substituted the condition "minimal nonspecific glomerulonephritis and tubular changes".

DECISION

On 11 September 1986 a delegate of the respondent issued a benefit classification certificate ("BCC") pursuant to sub-s 16(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") in respect of the applicant Mr Kirby for the conditions, "history of polyarthritis", "minimal change glomerulonephritis" and "history of anxiety".

2. On 20 October 1988, following reconsideration pursuant to sub-s 154(6) of the Act, a delegate varied the BCC by deleting the condition "history of anxiety". The applicant applied to review that decision contending that the benefit classification certificate should be revoked.

3. The applicant gave evidence and conducted his own application. Mr Browne who appeared for the respondent called Dr Mathew, consultant physician and Dr Niall, a specialist in renal and general medicine, who in addition to giving oral evidence provided written reports. Other medical reports were contained in the documents lodged pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

4. Mr Kirby joined the Australian Taxation Office in October 1985 and became an eligible employee for the purposes of the Act on 7 October 1985. He was then aged 30 years. On 28 August 1985 he was medically examined by a Commonwealth Medical Officer for the purpose of permanent employment. In a questionnaire concerning his health the applicant answered that from 1981-1985 he had suffered "spondylitis and polyarthritis treated by many doctors without success. Disabled 3 1\2 years". To the question, any abnormality of urine, the applicant answered "protein and albumen present". He also disclosed being, "absent from work past 3 1\2 years because of back injury".

5. The background to the applicant's back injury was described to the Tribunal by Mr Kirby as follows:

In early 1981 Mr Kirby first experienced hip pain which he said

was "quite severe". He had three days off work. At the time he

was working as a truck driver, but could not attribute his hip

pain to any specific incident. During the next few months his

work became more demanding and the hours longer, loading and

unloading freight which he carried on interstate trips. Later in

1981 he began to suffer further back pain particularly after a

long working day. In 1982 he was placed on heavier work, loading

and delivering meat, again accompanied by long hours. He had

further back pain and sought medical advice from rheumatologists,

Dr Fraser and later Dr Webb. Dr Fraser first raised the

possibility the applicant was suffering from Reiters syndrome and

recommended physiotherapy and an anti-inflamatory analgesic

phenylbutazone. Mr Kirby described the pain as being in "mid back

to left side". He saw other medical practitioners without success

and in May 1982 he ceased work. For the next three years he

received sickness benefit. In about May 1984 he began working

again assisting friends in the growing of a garlic crop of some

10 acres, helping with the ground cultivation, the watering and

in late 1984 harvesting of the crop. He said he has been free of

back pain since "sometime in 1985". In 1986 the applicant

"settled" for $17,500.00 an accident compensation claim for

"chronic back strain" suffered during the course of his

employment as a truck driver in 1982.

6. When examined by Dr Sheringham in August 1985, the applicant provided a medical report from Dr Heath (T9) in which Dr Heath wrote:

"The main features are the development of musculo-skeletal

symptoms which have persisted since 1981. Back,

heels and temporo-submandibular joints have been

involved. There has been some exacerbation of symptoms

by the performance of heavy physical work. There have

been no eye problems, but some urethral discharge and

evidence of albuminuria ++++ on this occasion. The

microurine shows excessive white cells.

Diagnosis in this case is obviously difficult, but I

believe the most likely diagnosis is Reiter's syndrome

with sacroiliac involvement, pain in the heels, positive

B27 antigen and often limited response to the non-steroidal

anti-inflammatory agents."

7. Dr Sheringham concluded the applicant unlikely to reach maximum retiring age for reasons stated as "Reiter's syndrome - recurrent bouts and evidence of persistent symptoms if treatment ceased. May in fact have Ankylosing Spondylitis as well. Severe albuminuria".

8. Dr Hackett, a medical adviser to the respondent, agreed with Dr Sheringham's conclusion and suggested a review nine months later. He also added the condition "anxiety" which for present purposes is no longer relevant.

9. Dr Fensling, who conducted a re-examination of the applicant on 2 June 1986 for the respondent, was of the opinion the applicant did not meet the general medical standard and recommended the issue of a BCC specifying those conditions which originally were contained in the BCC issued on 11 September 1986.

10. A report dated 23 May 1986 to Dr Fensling from St Vincent's Hospital (T10) contained the following pathology report:

"the pathologist report on the renal biopsy (17 October

1985) states `minimal non-specific glomerular and

tulular changes. Both the light microscope appearances

and the results of immunoflourescence are consistent

with "minimal change" glomerulonephritis. Electron

microscopy will be needed for positive confirmation of

this diagnoses.'"

11. Subsequent to the issue of the BCC the respondent had the applicant examined by Drs Mathew and Niall.

12. Sub-s 16 (8) of the Act relevantly reads:

16(8) The Commissioner shall consider the report

or reports (if any), and such other matters as he

considers relevant, and-

(a) ...

(b) where there is more than 1 physical or mental

condition specified in the benefit classification

certificate-

(i) if the Commissioner is of the opinion that there

is not a real risk that the person, by reason of or

for a reason connected with those conditions, will not

continue to be an eligible employee until the person

attains his maximum retiring age the Commissioner

shall revoke the certificate; or

(ii) if the Commissioner is of the opinion that there

is a real risk that the person, by reason of or for a

reason connected with 1 or some only of the conditions

specified in the certificate, will not continue to be

an eligible employee until he attains his maximum

retiring age the Commissioner shall revoke the

certificate and issue in substitution for the

certificate a new benefit classification certificate

specifying only the condition or conditions in respect

of which the Commissioner is of the opinion referred to

in this sub-paragraph; and

(c) in any other case- the Commissioner shall refuse

the request to revoke the benefit classification

certificate or to revoke the benefit classification

certificate and to issue in substitution for the

certificate a new benefit classification certificate."

13. The issue before the Tribunal is whether there is a "real risk" of premature retirement by reasons of or for a reason connected with the conditions "history of polyarthritis" and "minimal change glomerulonephritis".

HISTORY OF POLYARTHRITIS

14. The applicant denied any "history of polyarthritis" but acknowledged having used that description of his back problem in his application for membership to the Commonwealth Superannuation Scheme. At the hearing he withdrew that description contending he does not and never has suffered from a history of polyarthritis and that it was an incorrect diagnosis of his back injury. He said he provided that description on the questionnaire simply because that is what he was told he had. The applicant, though invited to do so, did not provide any medical evidence to contradict the diagnosis prefering to rely on his own opinion and claiming that it would be fruitless to try and get medical opinion in relation to a back injury he suffered nine years ago. It is noted however the applicant complained to Dr Mathew of the inaccuracy of the diagnosis.

15. Dr Mathew, who examined the applicant on 24 February 1987, said he saw no evidence of arthritis as a result of his examination. The history of back pain and the medical reports referred to earlier suggested to Dr Mathew either Reiter's syndrome or ankylosing spondylitis. Xrays of the lumbosacral spine showed no evidence of ankylosing spondylitis and blood tests were normal.

16. In his written reports Dr Mathews mentioned a 5 per cent risk of premature retirement from the work force because of Reiter's syndrome. In evidence before the Tribunal he said he thought it unlikely that the applicant will be troubled by this condition again. He went on to say that the 5 per cent estimate was too high.

"It is very significantly lower than that now

particularly as he has been well and I saw him myself

and there seems no doubt that he is well from the point

of view of his joints."

17. In a report ("Exh B"), Dr Mathew confirmed the applicant has not got ankylosing spondylitis but he estimated he was at a 5 per cent risk of developing it. Although he conceded the presence of the antigen HLA-B27 may have no relevance to the applicant's condition, there was an increase likelihood because of that antigen of developing ankylosing spondylitis. That antigen is not a cause of ankylosing spondylitis but a high percentage (90%) of patients with ankylosing spondylitis have this antigen, he said.

18. Dr Mathew went on to say that if the applicant has been free of back pain since his examination then there is little risk of ankylosing spondylitis and with Reiter's syndrome the risk is remote - very significantly lower than the 5 per cent referred to in his report (Exh D). Finally, Dr Mathew came to the conclusion the only condition for which the applicant is at risk is minimal change glomerulonephritis.

MINIMAL CHANGE GLOMERULONEPHRITIS

19. Dr Mathew described glomerulonephritis as an inflammatory disease of the glomeruli (microscopic filtration units) of the kidney. It is symptom free he said if damage is sufficiently mild. The lesions seen on biopsy of the kidney are reported as minimal hence the term minimal lesion glomerulonephritis which is a recognised diagnosis in kidney disease. It does not require treatment. Urinalysis in March 1989 revealed elevated protein content which is a sign of a defect in the kidney filtration system consistent with continuance of minimal risk glomerulonephritis (see Exh B). As to the risk of the condition getting worse Dr Mathew said "it is hard to give a figure in my view less than 15 per cent at present with time going by and appropriate follow up it might be quite easy to revise that figure".

20. There was evidence of albuminuria (protein) when examined by Dr Heath on 17 July 1985 and Dr Sheringham on 28 August 1985 noted "severe albuminuria". The results of the renal biopsy at St Vincent's Hospital on 16 October 1985 were consistent with the diagnosis of minimal change glomerulonephritis.

21. Dr Niall, a specialist in renal and general medicine, first saw the applicant in September 1985 when he treated him for an unrelated problem.

22. He said that minimal change glomerulonephritis is "not quite" the correct description of the applicant's condition which is "minimal non-specific glomerulonephritis and tubular changes" and like Dr Mathew described the condition as not requiring ongoing treatment but regular monitoring to check for any evidence of progression. His most recent examination was in December 1989 when urinalysis revealed protein in increased amounts. He regarded the possibility of serious complications developing in the applicant's kidney condition as very remote, only a 5 per cent chance in his opinion, quantified on the basis that less than one per cent generally connotes a risk of retirement that is negligible and 5 - 10 per cent a small but identifiable risk.

23. The expression "real risk" was discussed in detail in Re Bergin and Commissioner for Superannuation 19 ALD 279. The Tribunal in Re Bergin referred to the earlier decisions of Re Nicholson and Commissioner for Superannuation 14 ALD 217 and Gelsthorpe and Commissioner for Superannuation (Unreported decision No. 4724 of 4 November 1988). In Gelsthorpe the Tribunal expressed difficulty in applying only the test in Re Nicholson that "there is a `real risk' where a factual situation exist which has inherent in it a particular potential to result in that detrimental occurrence" and said it is preferable to view the words "real risk" as a matter of common parlance and went on to say "a `real risk' would be a chance which is more than nominal, imaginary, fictitious or fanciful but less than a probability". In our view either view does not impose a different standard. We agree with the view expressed by the Tribunal in Re Bergin that the test in Gelsthorpe is not more favourable to an applicant than in Re Nicholson.

24. The choice of the word "real" is perhaps unfortunate, as viewed in isolation it is imprecise. At one end any risk which is logically possible is real but when "real" is used in ordinary parlance as an adjective it has a connotation of something more than simply "a" or a "remote" risk. On one view any risk may be said to be a "real risk" but it may also be true that a slight risk is not a real risk in common parlance. Nor is it necessarily an answer to look at the purpose of the legislation. The interest of the applicant that he be accepted as a full member of the superannuation scheme is not necessarily in harmony with the interests of existing members of the scheme who may be best served by a narrower interpretation of the term "real risk". Real is not a question of degree and the Tribunal has to be cautious in looking at the risk expressed in terms of degrees. The answer to the meaning of "real risk" does not depend on some percentage of risk. Expressing a percentage is no more than an indicator of whether there is a likelihood of premature retirement.

25. Other factors which we have considered in assessing "real risk" are the applicant's employment. He has a sedentary occupation and his non-work activities are not physically demanding.

26. If the applicants contention is correct, that there is no real risk of premature retirement because of the specified conditions, then the BCC will not cause him any difficulty as in the event of 20 years contributory service the BCC will no longer have any effect. (see sub-s 16(5 of the Act).

27. Mr Browne informed the Tribunal that if the applicant elects to join the Public Sector Superannuation Scheme in operation from 1 July 1990, (for which he has until 30 June 1991 to decide), the BCC is no longer of any force or effect. However, if the applicant elects to remain within the Act scheme then the provisions of the Act remain in force.

28. Furthermore if, as the applicant contends, he continues to remain symptom free of the condition "history of polyarthritis", then with the passage of time it is open to him pursuant to sub-s 16(6) of the Act to request revocation of the certificate insofar as it relates to that condition. The general medical standard used for the purpose of the scheme suggests a five year symptom free period. On medical evidence the applicant is at this time short of the five year period when a further medical examination and re-consideration would be appropriate.

29. So far as, "history of polyarthritis" is concerned we are satisfied on the evidence of Dr Mathew that condition does not present a real risk. The applicant at the outset sought to attack the integrity of Dr Mathew. In our view Dr Mathew was scrupulously fair in his approach and we accept his evidence. In his opinion the applicant does not have ankylosing spondylitis, there is no evidence of arthritic changes and the possibility of Reiters syndrome is remote. However the applicant has had a history of back pain for which he was receiving sickness benefits from 1982-85. In a report dated 14 July 1986 (Exh F) to the applicant's solicitors, Dr Kennedy wrote:

"Present position

1. Persistent lower back aching and stiffness"

and in a report (17 July 1986) Mr Trevor Jones said:

"At present his only symptoms relate to his lower back.

He complains of discomfort in the mid and lower back at

all times".

These reports followed examination of the applicant by Dr Kennedy on 10 July 1986 and Mr Jones on 11 July 1986.

30. We have concluded that the description "History of polyarthritis" is inappropriate and should be replaced by "history of back pain". Whether that condition continues to present a real risk of premature retirement within the meaning of sub-s 16(4) may well be the subject of a further application to the Commissioner at a later date.

31. As to the condition "minimal change glomerulonephritis" we accept the evidence of Dr Niall that the correct description of the kidney complaint is "minimal nonspecific glomerulonephritis and tubular changes". On the evidence of both Dr Niall and Dr Mathew that condition represents a small but identifiable risk. Tests performed in 1989 confirm the ongoing presence of the condition.

32. In the result the Tribunal decides that the decision under view be varied in that for the condition "history of polyarthritis" there be substituted, "history of back pain" and for the condition "minimal change glomerulonephritis" there be substituted the condition "minimal non-specific glomerulonephritis and tubular changes".
