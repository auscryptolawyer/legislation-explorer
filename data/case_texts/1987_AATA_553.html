QT86/1830 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 553; 87 ATC 435 (11 March 1987)

Last Updated: 16 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income tax - allowable deductions - contribution to local council towards sealing of road adjacent to factory - whether expenditure of a capital nature.

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) s51

Vallambrosa Rubber Co Ltd v Farmer (1910) 5 TC 529

Pitt(HM Inspector of Taxes) v Castle Hill Warehousing Co Ltd (1974) 49 TC 638

British Insulated and Helsby Cables Ltd v. Atherton [1926] AC 205

Sun Newspapers Ltd v FC of T [1938] HCA 72; (1939) 61 CLR 337

Case R122 (1984) 84 ATC 787

DECISIONS AND REASONS

Re:

APPLICANT And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

No: QT86/1830

AAT Decision No 3307

Date: 11 March 1987

Place: BRISBANE

Dr P Gerber (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

The facts in this application have been agreed between the parties and are set out below:

"1. Food Products Pty Ltd Is situated adjacent to Prussia Avenue, Smithfield. Blind Alley runs perpendicular to Prussia Avenue.

  1. Prussia Avenue and Blind Alley were unsealed roadways.
    1. For approximately 10 years the nine ratepayers in Blind Alley and Prussia Avenue lobbied the Council to have the two streets sealed; the response was always that there were insufficient funds and other works had higher priority.
    2. Food Products Pty Ltd was particularly concerned to have the roads sealed. Prussia Avenue runs only some four metres from the factory premises and dust created by traffic was causing serious difficulties for the food production process. Furthermore, health regulations required a high standard of cleanliness in the factory at all times.
    3. Mr Jones, a Director of Food Products Pty Ltd at all relevant times, finally personally approached the then Lord Mayor to have the roads sealed.
    4. The Mayor indicated that maybe something could be done if the ratepayers concerned contributed $35,000 towards the cost of same.
    5. Jones thereupon calculated, on a street frontage basis, appropriate proportions of contributions for each ratepayer to the $35,000.
    6. Unfortunately, two ratepayers were not able to contribute anything with the result that there was a shortfall of $10,200 in contributions; in other words, only $24,800 could be raised.
      1. By letter dated 28 April 1981 the Council notified Mr Jones of estimates taken by the Council Works Department for full sealing of Prussia Avenue,

Smithfield. The estimates were as follows:-

3040m of totally unsealed road

surfaces at $16 per m 48,640

500m of lightly sealed road

surface at $9 per m 4,500

$53,140

  1. By letter dated 29 May 1981, Mr Jones of Food Products Pty Ltd, notified the Council of an offer to contribute $18,000 towards the sealing of Prussia Avenue and $6,500 contribution towards the sealing of Blind Alley.
  2. The offer was made on the condition that the two roadways must be fully sealed at the same time or the contribution would not be paid for either street.
  3. By letter dated 9 June 1981, the then Lord Mayor accepted this offer and the conditions made by Mr Jones on behalf of the company. He advised that construction would take place early in the new financial year.
  4. On 9 July 1981, a payment of $10,500 was made by Food Products Pty Ltd to the Town Clerk and construction commenced about six weeks after that date.

The amount was an instalment made for the contribution towards the construction of Prussia Avenue and Blind Alley, Smithfield.

  1. On 20 September 1981 a further payment of $14,300 was made by the other ratepayers direct to the Council towards the construction of Prussia Avenue and Blind Alley.
  2. Contributions made were as follows -

A Pty Ltd 1,800

B Pty Ltd 1,500

C 500

D 3,625

E Pty Ltd 1,625

F Pty Ltd 5,250

TOTAL $14,300

  1. The works section of the Council has advised that such monies would have been paid into an "Ex-Works Trust Account". This account is, in fact, a general trust account that is not confined to funds for road building; other funds held therein are bonds for sub-division work, construction work, etc.
  2. Any roadwork costs are paid for by the Council out of a general working account and the Ex-Works Trust Account is then debited with those costs. When the monies in the Trust Account are exhausted the Council has to pay for the difference out of its general consolidated revenue account.
  3. The work carried out by the Council was the full sealing of Prussia Avenue and Blind Alley to heavy transport standard.
  4. This work was carried out by the Council on a public roadway."

2. Against the above background, the applicant submits that the contribution to the cost of road sealing constitutes an allowable deduction. The respondent, on the other hand, submits that the expenditure is of a capital nature.

3. Mr Jones supplemented the agreed facts by oral evidence which amply supported the need to have the relevant roads sealed. It constituted an improvement to working conditions, providing greater comfort for staff, reduced cleaning costs in the factory and enabled windows to be opened during working hours. It also gave the factory a more attractive appearance when interstate and overseas customers visited the shop floor. In any event, I can take notice of the fact that the absence of dust is a desirable feature in the food manufacturing process.

4. However, none of these features suffice, in my opinion, to remove the "capital" taint attached to this outlay. The expenditure on the road sealing process formed a necessary outlay to obtain an enduring benefit. It was a "once and for all" outlay (cf Vallambrosa Rubber Co Ltd v Farmer 5 TC 529). It matters not that the applicant does not enjoy the exclusive use of the roadway or that it did not obtain an improvement to its assets. The relevant test is the nature of the benefit shown to be derived. That benefit is the enjoyment of the sealed road with its enduring qualities, intended to be used indefinitely in the manner of a fixed asset of a capital nature (cf Pitt (HM Inspector of Taxes) v Castle Hill Warehousing Co Ltd 49 TC 638).

  1. In the circumstances, I see no need to re-examine such well-worn decisions as British Insulated and Helsby Cables Ltd v. Atherton C19263 AC 205, Sun Newspapers Ltd v FC of T [1938] HCA 72; (1939) 61 CLR 337 and Case R122 84 ATC 787 on which much relevance was placed by the respondent. The answer is clear, if unfortunate for this applicant, the expenditure incurred on sealing the roads in question is of a capital nature and thus not allowable as a deduction.
  2. For the above reasons, the Tribunal affirms the decision of the respondent upon the applicant's objection to its assessment for the year ended 30 June 1982.

This and the preceding four (4) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Dr P Gerber (Senior Member) in the matter of QT86/1830.
