Nicholas and Commissioner for Superannuation [1986] AATA 611 (17 November 1986)

Last Updated: 22 January 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Superannuation - period of prospective service - applicant retired on invalidity grounds - date when eligible service commenced - when applicant became liable to contribute to the fund - whether initial appointment on probation - initial letter of appointment not referring to probation - probation mandatory under legislation applicable at the time - no discretion to set earlier date

Superannuation Act 1922 ss 193, 194, 19(1A)(a) (as in force December 1964)

Superannuation Act 1976 ss 3, 66, 183(3)

Superannuation (Period of Contributory Service) Regulations r 4

Public Service Act 1922 (as in force December 1964) ss 47(1) & (7), 47B, 47C

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. At the conclusion of the hearing of this application for review, we stated orally the terms of the decision intended to be made and the reasons therefor. Subsequently, pursuant to section 43(2A) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the applicant requested the Tribunal to furnish a statement in writing of the reasons of the Tribunal for its decision. Those reasons are now restated accordingly and will be furnished to the applicant and the respondent.

2. The applicant retired due to invalidity and became entitled to be paid a pension under section 66 of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the 1976 Act"). As he had less than 30 years' prospective service, section 67(4) of that Act applied to the calculation of the rate of his pension. That read:-

"67. (4) Where the period of prospective service of a person to whom this section applies is less than 30 years, the annual rate of the pension to which the person is entitled is such percentage of the person's final annual rate of salary as, having regard to the number of complete years included in the person's period of prospective service, is applicable in accordance with columns 1 and 2 of Schedule 4."

3. In making the calculation required by section 67(4) it is necessary to have regard to the number of years of his prospective service. "Period of prospective service" is defined in section 3 of the 1976 Act; it means the aggregate of the employee's period of contributory service and the period commencing on the day immediately following the last day of service and ending on the day on which he will attain the age of 65 years or his maximum retiring age, whichever is the earlier.

4. It is, therefore, necessary to ascertain what was Mr Nicholas' period of contributory service. "Period of contributory service" is also defined in section 3 of the 1976 Act. It means a period equal to the period commencing on the employee's first day of service and ending on his last day of service. A delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation decided that the applicant's "contributory service" commenced on 21 June 1965. The Commissioner confirmed the decision. It is now the subject of the review in these proceedings.

5. "First day of service" is defined in section 3 of the 1976 Act as meaning, in the case of an employee who has been an eligible employee only once - that is has had only one period of service - the day on which he became an eligible employee. "Eligible employee" is defined in the same section as including a person who immediately before the commencement of that Act was an employee for the purpose of the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the 1922 Act") and has continued to be and is such an employee.

6. Because the first day of service is defined as being the day on which the employee became an eligible employee, special provision has been made with respect to persons who were contributors under the 1922 Act. Otherwise the day on which they became eligible under the 1976 Act would be the day when the 1976 Act came into force. So regulations have been made under section 183(3) of the 1976 Act to modify the provisions of that Act. They are the Superannuation (Period of Contributory Service) Regulations ("the Regulations"). By virtue of regulation 4 of those Regulations, the provisions in the Schedule to them apply as modifications to the 1976 Act.

7. As a result of modification of section 3(1), for employees in the applicant's circumstances, the period of contributory service is the aggregate of:-

(1) the period from 1 July 1976 (when the 1976 Act came into force) to the last day of his service; and

(2) the period of prescribed service under section 194 (which is added to the 1976 Act by the Regulations).

8. Section 194(1)(a) is applicable in the applicant's case; so far as is relevant in these proceedings it reads:-

"194. (1) Subject to sub-section (2), each of the following periods is a prescribed period of service in relation to a person who has ceased to be an eligible employee, being an eligible employee referred to in paragraph (a) of the definition of 'eligible employee' in sub-section 3(1), and who has not after so ceasing again become an eligible employee:

(a) ... the period that commenced on the date of commencement of his contributions and ended on 30 June 1976 ..."

So the applicant's period of prescribed service was the period "that commenced on the date of commencement of his contributions" and ended on 30 June 1976.

9. In order to discover what is meant by the date of commencement of his contributions, it is necessary to have recourse to section 193(2)(a), which is also added to the 1976 Act by the Regulations. It provides that, in relation to a person who was an employee under the 1922 Act and has ceased to be an eligible employee, a reference to the date of the commencement of his contributions has to be read as a reference to the date on which he became liable to make contributions to the Superannuation Fund under the 1922 Act.

10. We have, therefore, to ascertain when the applicant became liable to make contributions to that fund. That is governed by the provisions of the 1922 Act. At the relevant time section 19(1) of that Act required an employee to contribute to the fund from "the prescribed date". In December 1964, when the applicant joined the Public Service as a permanent employee, the effect of section 19(1A)(a) was that, for a person who was appointed on probation, the prescribed date was that on which his appointment was confirmed. Section 19(1A)(b) provided that, in the case of a person appointed not on probation, the prescribed date was that on which he became an employee. For the purpose of section 194(1)(a), therefore, the date of commencement of the applicant's contributions depends on whether he was appointed on probation or not on probation. In passing it should be noted that in fact he began to contribute to the Fund in June 1965. However, for the purpose of section 194(1)(a) it is the date on which he was required by the 1922 Act to begin to contribute which is relevant, not that on which he actually began to do so.

11. In order to ascertain whether the applicant was appointed on probation or not on probation it is necessary to examine the provisions of the Public Service Act 1922 as they were in force in December 1964. At that time section 47(1) and (7) of that Act were as follows:-

"47. (1) Except as otherwise provided by this Act, the appointment of a person to the Commonwealth Service as an officer of the Second, Third or Fourth Division shall, in the first instance, be an appointment on probation.

(7) As soon as practicable after the expiration of the period of six months from the date on which a probationer commenced duties in pursuance of his appointment, the Board shall either -

(a) confirm the appointment of the probationer;

(b) annul his appointment; or

(c) direct that he continue on probation for such further period as the Board thinks fit, but so that the total period of his probation does not exceed one year."

Sections 47B and 47C provided for the reappointment of certain persons as permanent officers without probation. But those provisions applied only to retired officers and officers who had resigned in order to stand as candidates at an election and had failed to be elected. There was also provision for persons appointed to the Public Service on transfer from other Public Services; it is not entirely clear whether such persons were required to be appointed on probation or not. However, it is certain that all other appointments, except the reappointments provided for in sections 47B and 47C, had to be made on probation.

12. The applicant has provided us with a copy of the letter of appointment which was sent to him. It does not refer to any requirement of probation. He was a temporary employee before he was appointed as a permanent officer. However, the Public Service Act, as in force in December 1964, contained no provision exempting, or authorising the exemption of, temporary employees from the general requirement that persons appointed as permanent officers serve a period of probation after appointment as permanent officers.

13. There can be no doubt that section 47(1) was applicable to the applicant. Although the letter that was sent to Mr Nicholas by the Appointments Officer of the Public Service Board did not refer to the fact that he was appointed on probation, section 47(1) required that his appointment be made on probation. What occurred subsequently is consistent with his having been so appointed. In June 1965 a recommendation was made by the Chief Officer of the part of the Department in which the applicant was serving that his appointment be confirmed. It certified that his conduct, diligence and efficiency had been satisfactory during the period of probation.

14. When the applicant was appointed, the terms of the letter of appointment may well have led him to believe that he was appointed not on probation. However, the Public Service Act, as in force at the time of his appointment, did not permit of his being appointed otherwise than on probation. We are satisfied that he was in fact appointed on probation and that his appointment was confirmed in June 1985 in normal course, as was provided for by section 47(7).

15. That being so, section 19(1A)(a) of the 1922 Act, as in force in December 1964, had the effect that the prescribed date for the purposes of section 19(1) was that on which his appointment was confirmed, that is to say 21 June 1965. Consequently, we have come to the conclusion that the decision under review that that was the date on which his contributory service commenced was the correct decision, that the Commissioner for Superannuation had no discretion to come to any other decision and that this Tribunal similarly has no discretion to do so.
