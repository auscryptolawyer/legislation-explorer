CIRCUMSTANCES IN WHICH THE ISSUES ARISE

There can be few matters that have come before the Tribunal or a Court where the issues raise questions in the same proceedings as to whether:

- business activities over a period of years can be regarded as those of a religious institution within the meaning of the Income Tax Assessment Act; and whether

- a company carrying on business under the name of an insurance company is a bona fide insurance company, purported transactions between it and the religious institution being then at arms length and pursuant to real contracts, loans and interest on loans, together with insurance premiums, then being proper transactions; and whether

- a body carrying on activities under the name of a union is a bona fide union, purported transactions between it and the religious institution being then at arms length, and pursuant to real contracts, loans and interest on loans then being proper transactions; and whether

- in the event of the activities not being those of a religious institution, the entity liable to pay tax on the profits arising from such activities has been properly described as the taxpayer.

These questions all arise in the context of the Respondent seeking to bring to tax the net earnings resulting from the carrying on of the business of a caravan ski park and entertainment enterprise in a country leisure area nearby to Sydney. The respondent also seeks to assess the husband and wife managers and caretakers of the business and park on benefits received by them.

It was said on behalf of the taxpayers that the business activities were carried on as a part of the activities of a church, the Church of S.J., which church had, in November 1972, been structured by the Reverend Saint John (hereinafter called the Reverend John) and other designated persons. The activities were, subject to what appears elsewhere in these reasons, such as did result in a profit.

The Reverend John, had in about 1971/1972, been instrumental in causing the incorporation of a company under the name of R. Insurances Pty Limited, which company thence issued purported policies of annuity and endowment to a limited number of people all said to be employees of the insurance company and, incidentally, members of the Church of S.J. In circumstances which will be detailed later in these reasons, the Church of S.J. allegedly became indebted to the insurance company, such debt attracting Interest Premiums on the policies also fell due.

The Reverend John had also in about November 1972 caused the formation of a union, the A.E.E. Trade Union Association, the members of which were said to be the church, the insurance company and the union as employers, together with persons "employed by" the church, the insurance company and the union. The union was at one time registered pursuant to the provisions of the Trade Union Act 1881 (NSW). The church, in circumstances to be detailed later, purportedly became indebted to the union, and being, it was said, unable to meet the quarterly instalments of interest on such debt, borrowed the same from its creditor, causing the debt and the interest to become larger each quarter day.

If it should transpire that the business activities of the Church of S.J. were those of a religious institution within the meaning of Section 23(e) of the Income Tax Assessment Act, then any profit earned would be exempt from income tax. If it should transpire that the business was not carried on by or on behalf of a religious institution, then in the event of the payments on account of interest not being made pursuant to a real contract and not being bona fide genuine transactions, taxable profits were derived by the business and tax is payable. If the interest payments were payments made in satisfaction of obligations duly incurred in the gaining of assessable income, no tax would be payable as the quantum thereof would reduce any business profit to a loss.

The identification in such circumstances of the taxpayer is also a matter that arises for consideration.

The overall situation is thus easy to state. The facts and circumstances relevant to a determination of the issues and questions that arise are much more detailed and expansive.

Allied with the above issues referable to the Church of S.J. are objections pertaining to the assessability or otherwise of benefits derived by the daughter and son-in-law of the Reverend John, they having been employed as managers and caretakers of the caravan ski park and entertainment area, thereby receiving rent-free accommodation and deriving other benefits from their employment said by the Respondent to be subject to tax. The daughter and son-in-law were also directors of the insurance company and members of the union.

The hearing of this matter extended over 23 days. There were 1809 exhibits tendered on behalf of the taxpayer. Six exhibits were tendered on behalf of the Respondent. The Reverend John, now 74 years of age, appeared on behalf of the Church of S.J., his daughter and son-in-law. In effect, he appeared under protest for he contended that the taxpayer, as designated by the Respondent, the Church of S.J. Trust, had no connection with the church of S.J., and that he himself was not in any way associated with such trust. The Reverend John tendered the appropriate evidence and argued the taxpayers cases.

The procedure adopted at the hearing was unusual in that the applicants cases were substantially dependent on the contents, and the weight to be given to the contents, of documents tendered. Limited evidence on oath was available. The Reverend John, in arguing the case for the applicants, proffered explanations and supplementary oral material of his own from the bar table. The course so adopted was with the consent of the Respondent Commissioner, and indeed espoused by Counsel appearing on the Respondent's behalf. The proceedings warranted this procedure being adopted, no point being taken on the basis that the said explanations and oral material raised by the Reverend John during the course of the proceedings were not given formally by way of sworn evidence. It was not sought to cross-examine the Reverend John.

The Tribunal is thus to give the evidence both oral and documentary, the explanations and material before it such weight as it considers proper in all the circumstances (Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 Section 33). It was as I have said a course convenient and appropriate to be adopted.

Presenting as he did submissions on the evidence and explanations of some of it from the bar table, without objection by the Respondent, and in the interest of having before the Tribunal all relevant material, be it not necessarily on oath, the Reverend John was in effect counsel in his own cause and subjective in the presentation of his arguments and submissions. It is inevitable in such a lengthy matter that any assessment of the issues and the matters relied upon in determining the same where they relate to aspects not covered by documents, will involve some assessment of the presenter himself. Largely, however, the Reverend John relied upon documentary evidence to establish the position maintained in opposition to the assessments. It is to be noted, however, that all documents purporting to evidence resolutions of meetings, gifts, transfers and assignments of property and assumption of liabilities, statements of account and balance sheets, income tax returns, letters and schedules of property, were drafted and drawn up by the Reverend John, and in all cases signed by him and/or a member or members of his family. He is not a qualified lawyer or accountant, and on the evidence did not at any time retain relevant professional advice.

AVAILABLE SCENARIOS

Assuming the establishment of a body in 1972 then and since carrying on business activities under the name of the church of S.J., were such activities those of a religious institution?

There are a number of possible situations that suggest themselves in this matter. One is that under the direction of the Reverend John, the Church of S.J. continued up until and beyond the relevant years to carry on its religious activities, including the commercial venture at the caravan ski park and a holding of investments. Another is that the Church of S.J. was established and continued thereafter with its activities. At the same time, the Reverend John, his daughter and son-in-law carried on the commercial venture and acquired investments, such venture and investments not being activities of the church nor in any way carried on in such a manner that funds were appropriated to or used on behalf of the objects or activities of the Church of S.J.

Still a further situation is that of a church, the structure of which was caused by the Reverend John to have been established in 1972, but which since that time has not engaged in any religious activities other than those of the Reverend John himself. A one man church. The commercial venture is and has always been separate and apart from the church, even though directed by the Reverend John.

In the case of the latter two situations, it would be said by the Respondent that it is not sufficient to point to the religious beliefs of a person or persons. The Tribunal must look to the relevant activities carried on and determine whether the same were intended to be and in fact were in aid of the furtherance of such beliefs. It does not follow that the common religion of a group stamps a religious character on an institution founded, maintained or staffed by members of that group or that the purpose or activity of such an institution is religious. (see Church of the New Faith v Commissioner of Pay-Roil Tax (Vict) [1983] HCA 40; 154 CLR 120 and 128)

A further situation is that there was at no relevant time in existence a religious institution or a body, be it comprised of one or more than one person, carrying on religious activities.

The first situation is simply illustrated by a mission society carrying on retail activities, selling books, clothes and artifacts, the profits arising therefrom being used to directly further the objects of the mission. The second by two or more adherents to a religious belief deciding to go into business together. They retain their faith which might sustain them in abiding by a moral code and in conducting their commercial venture, but the profit arising is distributed amongst them and utilised by them as they each so decide. The third situation is illustrated by that of a person with strongly held religious beliefs. Occasionally others display an interest in what he is about. It is not apparent that there is a group or that there are any activities of a group of like-minded religious people. Simultaneously with the maintenance and practice of his beliefs, he engages in commercial ventures, his religious personality inevitably pervading the same. There is not a utilisation of the resources or funds of the ventures in furtherance of the beliefs.

By looking then at the activities of the Reverend John, of the alleged religious institution, the Church of S.J., and the activities of the business, the nexus, if any, between them should become apparent. There is no doubt, on the evidence, that the business at Anglers Reach was conducted as a commercial venture, the income and expenditure other than that applicable to the living and domestic expenses of the daughter and son-in-law being solely attributable to the trading activities.

The issues are the existence, and the extent, of the religious activities and the nexus, if any, between the same and the commercial venture and investments, the income and expenditure of and from which were not, except for the alleged interest, at the conclusion of the evidence and submissions, themselves in issue.

THE CHURCH OF S. J., ITS FORMATION, OBJECTS AND CONSTITUTION

It was in November 1972 that the Reverend John, as "the appointed trustee" and thirteen others, being friends and relatives of the Reverend John, "by deed" declared themselves "desirous of dedicating themselves to the work of 'Jesus Christ Our Lord'" and so as to better perform that work, agreed to the formation of a "church" to be known as the Church of S.J. It was determined that the church be so formed and designated persons be known as the "founding members". It was by the deed "declared to all sundry that the 'Church of S.J.' has been founded in New South Wales for the glorification of God and the spreading of his word as taught by Saint John throughout the land". It was further declared that "the church is hereby established and the 'appointed trustee', upon affixing his signature hereto, agrees to accept full responsibility for and as to the safe keeping, investment and accumulation of all of the funds, moneys or properties (real or personal) of the church, and all of the duties associated with such trust, and further agrees to hand over to his successor trustee when required all documents and things belonging to the church". The draft Constitution and Rules of the Church of S.J. were recited in the deed and annexed to it. As such, they were approved. The objects of the church were detailed and they included:-

(a) The propagation of the "Holy Scriptures" of the Bible and in particular the teachings of Saint John;

(b) To minister to all of God's children as to their needs both for "body and soul" in a joint combination of the spiritual needs of the soul and the future kingdom of God and man's place in it, and the present physical needs of the body here on Earth now and in the immediate future, treating and ministering to both needs on an equal basis, body and soul, physical and spiritual;

(c) The spiritual side to include all of the usual church facilities and Sunday School and charitable segments associated with church activities, including provision for elderly people and children in special circumstances; and

(d) On the bodily physical side, the propagation on the principles of vegetarianism in its entirety, the teaching of and propagation of the benefits of fasting as taught in the Scriptures and practiced by Our Lord, the teaching and propagation of the benefits to all mankind of the eating of only natural foods, the proper and regular use of physical exercise, and how the combining of all of these things, both physical and spiritual, can create a heaven here and now on Earth in preparation for the eventual Kingdom of Heaven of Our Lord, as promised in our Scriptures;

......

(f) To provide suitable places of worship for members of the church, together with suitable facilities for the health and physical needs of the members in each locality as required.

The Trustee was given control of "all funds and income and outgoings" and was required to "keep all bank accounts and sign all cheques". A power to invest moneys was given to the Executive Council, namely:-

"Any moneys surplus to the immediate needs of the church may, at the direction of the Executive Council, be invested at call with any bank or building society or company controlled by a member of the church, and preference will be given to such members' companies, provided all such surplus funds are at call."

There was not given by the Constitution a power otherwise to operate a business or engage in a commercial enterprise. On dissolution and the church not being "amalgamated with some other church or organization having similar aims or activities", the net assets were available for distribution amongst members in proportion to their contributions. At the first meeting of "Founding Members", the Reverend John was appointed Trustee and permanent Chairman, and an Executive Council of five persons was structured.

About a month after the church was so established, and on 18 December 1972, a meeting of the Executive Council was held at which it was resolved that a special fund be created, to be known as the Public Health and Research Benefit Memorial Fund of the Church of S.J.:

The Church of [S.J.] as a public religious institution or order in furtherance of its aims of ministering to the needs of the body as well as the soul, and wishing to engage in research into the "causes, prevention and cure of disease in human beings", hereby sets out the aims and objectives in creating this fund and the uses to which it may be put, accumulated or used.

The objects of the fund commenced by stipulating that the purpose for which the fund was to be used was:-

"1. To provide the facilities and opportunity and the equipment needed to practice and to research into all aspects of health, sickness and disease and the direct relationship of these results to the primary causes in what is eaten and how people live, including the acquiring of suitable land for growing all kinds of organic fruits and vegetables without any kind of sprays, fertilizers, or artificial additives of any kind whatever, using only organic humus and animal manures as naturally provided by God and instructed in his word.

......

  1. The practice of and the spreading of the benefits of vegetarianism throughout the world. The research into the benefits from the eating only of organically grown food and the direct relationship between health and food.
  2. The practice of and the spreading of the benefits of regular fasting in relation to health and its effectiveness in treating or avoiding sickness or disease."

The Reverend John was appointed Trustee of the fund. All income was to be used only for the purposes of the fund; the Trustee nevertheless was authorised to invest "the moneys of the fund in, or to conduct any business or activity which may produce Additional funds or profits to this fund, provided always that the Executive Council of the church shall consent to such activity being commenced, and that control and ownership always remained with the church, and that all moneys derived be transferred to the fund and used only for the purposes of the fund

The objects of the church and of the fund anticipate the coming into being of a religious institution which, if it carried on activities consistent with the objects and in furtherance of them, would be entitled to the benefit of exemption granted to such a body pursuant to the provisions of Section 23(e) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. The Tribunal is satisfied that if the beliefs, practices and observances envisaged by the objects of the church and the fund were accepted by a group of adherents who asserted such beliefs, practices and observances, then such group could be described as a religious organization, and its activities religious for the purposes of the legislation.

The criteria of religion was said by Mason A.C.J. and Brennan J. in Church of New Faith v Commissioner of Payroll Tax (Vict) [1983] HCA 40; 154 CLR 120 at 136 to be:

".... first, belief in a supernatural Being, Thing or Principle; and second, the acceptance of canons of conduct in order to give effect to that belief, though canons of conduct which offend against the ordinary laws are outside the area of any immunity, privilege or right conferred on the grounds of religion. Those criteria may vary in their comparative importance, and there may be a different intensity of belief or of acceptance of canons of conduct among religious or among the adherents to a religion. The tenets of a religion may give primacy to one particular belief or to one particular canon of conduct. Variations in emphasis may distinguish one religion from other religions, but they are irrelevant to the determination of an individual's or a group's freedom to profess and exercise the religion of his, or their, choice."

At 174 in their joint reasons for decision, Wilson J. and Deane J. said:

"One of the more important indicia of "a religion" is that the particular collection of ideas and/or practices involves belief in the supernatural, that is to say, belief that reality extends beyond that which is capable of perception by the senses. If that be absent, it is unlikely that one has "a religion". Another is that the ideas relate to man's nature and place in the universe and his relation to things supernatural. A third is that the ideas are accepted by adherents as requiring or encouraging them to observe particular standards or codes of conduct or to participate in specific practices having supernatural significance. A fourth is that, however loosely knit and varying in beliefs and practices adherents may be, they constitute an identifiable group or identifiable groups. A fifth, and perhaps more controversial, indicium (cf Malnak v Yogi (1979) 572 F. (2d) 197) is that adherents themselves see the collection of ideas and/or practices as constituting a religion.

As has been said, no one of the above indicia is necessarily determinative of the question whether a particular collection of ideas and/or practices should be objectively characterized as "a religion". They are no more than aids in determining that question and the assistance to be derived from them will vary according to the context in which the question arises. All of those indicia are, however, satisfied by most of all leading religions. It is unlikely that a collection of ideas and/or practices would properly be characterized as a religion if it lacked all or most of them or that, if all were plainly satisfied, what was claimed to be a religion could properly be denied that description. Ultimately, however, that question will fall to be resolved as a matter of judgment on the basis of what the evidence establishes about the claimed religion. Putting to one side the case of the parody or sham, it is important that care be taken, in the exercise of that judgment, to ensure that the question is approached and determined as one of arid characterization not involving any element of assessment of the utility, the intellectual quality, or the essential "Truth" or "worth" of tenets of the claimed religion."

The issue here arising is not as to whether the Reverend John himself or he with his wife, daughter and son-in-law, and perhaps others, comprised a religious institution, or carried on religious activities, but whether the business institution and/or the business activities at Anglers Reach, and by way of investment, were those of the body structured in 1972 and in furtherance of the objects of such body or otherwise.

CHURCH OF S.J. - APPLICATION FOR TAX EXEMPTION

The day following the meeting at which the fund was established, the Reverend John, purportedly on behalf of the Church of S.J., applied under Section 23(e) of the Income Tax Assessment Act for an exemption certificate from taxation for the church. Application was also then made for donations to the fund to be tax deductible. It was stated in the letter supporting the application that the income of the fund was expected to come from gifts made by members of the church.

Exemption was granted in respect of the 1973 and 1974 financial years. The exemption granted in respect of the 1974 year was as against an assessed income of $12 and was only so granted following upon an objection being lodged by the Reverend John in which he stated that:-

"The amounts assessed are moneys received by the Trustee of the fund "Public Health and Research Benefit Memorial Fund" for and on behalf only of that fund to be used only for the purposes of that fund and for no other purposes whatsoever .....

The purposes of the fund jointly include educational, charitable and scientific research and clearly come with Section 23A (e) of the Act.

The income of this fund should not be treated as ordinary income of the church as it is not usable in the ordinary affairs of the church for the special purposes listed in the formation instrument and for no other purposes whatsoever......

The church as a public institution wishing to engage in research of the cause, prevention and cure of disease in human beings, has established this fund to carry these objections into effect."

The Constitution of the Church was amended on 31 May 1976 to restrict the distribution of surplus assets on a winding up or dissolution.

In August 1976, the Reverend John was notified by the Respondent of an exemption thereafter applying, and there being no necessity on the part of the church or the fund to lodge income tax returns unless requested so to do. The application for deductibility was not granted.

It was on 5 September 1983 that the Reverend John was advised by the Respondent that "as a result of an investigation into the affairs of the Church of S.J., the exemption from income tax of the church and the fund was withdrawn".

CHURCH OF S.J. - THE "GIFTS" AND THE "LOAN"

On 18 October 1976, a meeting of the Executive Council of the Church of S.J. was purportedly held. The minutes of the meeting recited a situation, namely:-

"The Chairman informed the Council that he had received an offer from two companies (L. & D. Pty Limited and M.S. Pty Limited) who jointly own a property of approximately 34 acres at [Anglers Reach].......

The companies have offered to give to the church the whole of this property, together with all improvements thereon including all the plant and equipment associated with the park and property as used at this date, and will transfer all the title deeds to the church on condition that the church will accept and take all responsibility for all outstanding debts and liabilities owed by the companies as at the date of the gift incurred by the companies in relation to this property, and further, that the property and any proceeds from the property be used by the church in furtherance of its aims and objectives set out in the special fund sponsored by the church namely the "Public Health and Research Benefit Memorial Fund" to assist in the research into the "cause. Prevention and elimination of sickness and disease in human beings".

The Chairman also pointed out to the Council that this property, together with the improvements and the plant would be eminently suitable for the purposes of the fund, and would greatly assist the church in providing the facilities, opportunity and equipment needed to carry out the research into many aspects of "health, sickness and disease" as planned by the fund".

Although it is a matter that will be dealt with elsewhere further in these reasons, it may be here noted that the Reverend John is said by the minutes to have informed the meeting that:-

"The main liability of the companies is an at call loan from the [A.E.E.] Trade Union Association of about $251,000 bearing interest of 10% per annum.

The Chairman informed the Council that the union would defer any call up of this loan for a period of five years, provided the church guaranteed to continue the payment of interest on the due dates and the full payment of the principle sum in five years or upon the sale or disposal of the property, whichever comes first.

The companies are in default on the loan as at this date as the union has called in the loan."

It was purportedly resolved by the Council to unanimously accept the "offer or gift" and authority was given to the Reverend John to implement the same. Consequential thereon and at the same meeting, it was resolved "to appoint [the son-in-law] and [the daughter] [the directors and beneficial shareholders of the donor companies] as joint trustees of this property and project with full authority to open and operate a bank account with the National Bank and instructing them to keep separate accounting of all income and outgoings relating to this project and moneys expended in the research programme". It was further resolved to empower the fund with full authority to operate the property and businesses at Anglers Reach to produce income for the fund to be used for the purposes of the fund in accordance with its stated objects.

On the basis of the evidence before the Tribunal, the income derived from the operation of the property and business at Anglers Reach was not at any time, and especially during the subject relevant years, used for the purposes of the fund and in accordance with the stated objects of the fund.

There was not any evidence before the Tribunal as to the real estate being in fact transferred into the names of the designated intended trustees, this despite a form of instrument of transfer and gift duty return being lodged with the revenue authorities. No gift duty was in fact paid.

A document entitled "Acknowledgement of a Gift" was executed on 18 October 1976. The persons signing the document on behalf of L. & D. Pty Limited, M.S. Pty Limited, the union and the church were the Reverend John, his daughter and son-in-law in their capacities as trustee, secretary and vice-president of the union, the daughter and son-in-law as secretary and director of the companies, the Reverend John, his daughter and son-in-law as trustee and executive councillors of the church. It may be here noted that on the evidence before the Tribunal, the property was not thereafter used for the stipulated public benefit, nor were there facilities provided for research or normal church activities. On the basis of the case put on behalf of the taxpayers, the companies had not been able, nor was the church in 1976 or thereafter able, "to continue to meet the interest payments on the due dates".

The land owned by the donor companies as joint tenants had been acquired by them in September 1973 for $150,000 with a borrowing sufficient to cover the purchase price and more from the Bank of New South Wales. Later alternate funding was arranged with Telford Property Trust. The property was used as a tourist park and ski gardens and for grazing. It occupied an area of 34 acres. In respect of the years 1974 to 1976 interest was paid on the borrowings from the bank and the trust company. A form of memorandum of mortgage dated 17 June 1975 for a period of four years evidencing a consideration of $167,723 has proposed by the Reverend John and purportedly entered into between the two companies and the union. What then, one may ask, of the Deed of First Charge? Had its existence been forgotten, or was it never intended to be effective? It was stated by the Reverend John in the course of his submissions that the Deed of First Charge was entered into in order that the real estate might be released from the mortgage encumbrance in the event of its being sold. The later purported extension of the purported unregistered mortgage is inconsistent with this submission.

As has been earlier mentioned, the Reverend John had been instrumental in the incorporation some years prior to 1977 of a number of companies, in addition to the two previously mentioned, the issued shares of which were held by various members of his family, namely, he and his wife, two daughters, their husbands and four grandchildren. THE ACTIVITIES AT ANGLERS REACH AND THE "CENTRAL ADMINISTRATION"

Subsequent to October 1976, the business of the tourist park and ski grounds was carried on under the name of the Church of S.J. Vehicles were registered and trading licences issued in this name. The shop attached to the tourist park was licensed to sell consumer goods and tobacco. Petrol was sold. The ski gardens and retail business were open seven days a week. As is apparent from the figures previously set forth in these reasons, there was a large turnover in the relevant years from the carrying on of the commercial activities.

Such of the financial Information as was before the Tribunal evidenced a notation of all moneys paid out on account of expenditure of the daughter and son-in-law and income of and expenditure on account of the business. Yearly statements of moneys so spent and received were available, signed by the daughter and son-in-law, but prepared by the Reverend John. The statements did not identify any church or research expenditure. There was a notation to accounts in March 1978 saying that the "research work and programme into sickness and disease in human beings" had been considerably delayed, and that the growing of "all our fruit and vegetables had been put back". In 1979, 1980, 1981, 1982 and 1983 the statements recited that "the research work has continued on a satisfactory level" and that the "research fund" was "funded by" the Church of S.J. The research work and programme here referred to was, on the evidence, the fasting being undertaken by the Reverend John as herein elsewhere detailed, and not otherwise. It did not entail any expenditure by or on behalf of the business at Anglers Reach. There was not any evidence of a funding of research work by the church. The growing of the fruit and vegetables was for the benefit of the daughter and son-in-law and their family and not otherwise.

Particulars of the financial details of the business activities at Anglers Reach in respect of the relevant subject years as structured by the respondent are set forth elsewhere in these reasons. Like particulars were not available for other years. Suffice here to say that assessments had issued in respect of income earned by the Church of S.J. from the business activities for years prior to 1982, the same based on the result perceived by the Respondent of such activities.

In the 1984 yearly statement and following the sale of the park property (efforts to sell the same had originated in September 1982) it was noted "health research project temporarily discontinued". Other than as above, there was not any evidence as to a "health research project" having been in existence. That is, other than the personal life preserving and fasting project of the Reverend John.

The proceeds of sale of the land and business, namely $415,000, were as to $215,000, left on mortgage with the purchaser, and as to $150,000 placed at interest with the Queensland Government Development Authority. Other than for action taken by the Respondent appropriating investments so held in part satisfaction of amounts due under the assessments, there would have been moneys at interest with a finance company and the authority, in 1984, of no less than $327,000, this at a time when the church allegedly could not afford to pay interest and had to borrow from the union to this end. At 30 June 1983, $265,000 was invested with finance companies, authorities and in unit trusts, the interest allegedly due to the union and the insurance company being purportedly unable to be paid by the church from its own funds.

In respect of the years the subject of review, as with other years prior to 1982, the Reverend John caused to be prepared "accounts" for what was described as the "Central Administration" of the Church of S.J. and a "report and statement of accounts" in respect of Anglers Reach "Research Health Project" conducted by the "Public Health and Research Benefit Memorial Fund". It was said by the Reverend John that the figures were never collated for income tax purposes, but only to narrate a summary of all receipts and payments, he contending that the activities being those of a church, the latter being exempt from lodging returns with appropriate accounts. He says the accounts were prepared for "internal purposes". There was not a notation of any payment in respect of an activity allied with the religious objects and intent herein before set forth.

It is accepted by the Reverend John that in the event of the "Church of S.J. Trust" being the proper taxpayer and there being a liability to pay tax on the net income derived from the carrying on of activities at Anglers Reach and the investments that, subject to the inclusion of "interest" as an expense items, the calculations affected by the Respondent are appropriate and correct. Such calculations, without the interest deduction, as herein before mentioned, show a taxable income derived for the 1982, 1983 and 1984 financial years of $56,843, $ 40,602 and $43,309 respectively.

Other than for a deduction allowed for the private Section 23(e) benefit of the daughter of the Reverend John and his son-in-law (the assessment of which is also the subject of this reference), there is not included in the Respondent's calculations any item of income or of expense not already in the figures contained in the "accounts" of the Reverend John. There are items of expenditure in the "accounts" that were not expenses incurred in the gaining of assessable income and that were of a capital, private or domestic nature. By reference to the "accounts", one gleans an appreciation of the outgoings and thence activities of the business at Anglers Reach. Can it truly be said from such an appreciation of the nature of the business activities, and as obtained from records maintained by the Reverend John, that such business activities were those of the Church of S.J. operating under its Constitution and Rules, and in furtherance of the objects therein specified of the church? On the evidence before the Tribunal, the answer is no. Albeit that a church exists and activities that are religious in nature are performed and carried out by the Reverend John, are they then activities separate and apart from those of the business at Anglers Reach or allied to and interwoven with it? The answer can only be in favour of the former. The gross income of the business is that from the activities of the tourist park. The expenditure as shown by the Reverend John in his "statement of accounts" does not contain any item referable to the carrying on of an activity pursuant to or in aid of implementing the Constitution and Rules of the Church of S.J.

The "expenditure and outgoings" listed by the Reverend John under "Central Administration" in respect of the 1982, 1983 and 1984 years do not include any payment referable to an operation carried out pursuant to the Constitution and Rules of the church. There is not a religion related item of expenditure. Outgoings said to have been associated with a property at Campbelltown are described as "establishment costs and charges" and would not have been deductible in any event. The activities of the "Central Administration" were those of an investment organization and of a family company and family partnership debtor and creditor. There was not any religious element of a financial nature or vice versa present in any item of income or expenditure or any asset or liability appearing in these "accounts". It is noted that it is the income from the third party investments that comprises the major portion of the income of the trust sought to be brought to tax.

THE "HEALTH RESEARCH PROJECT" - FASTING AND THE REVEREND JOHN

Starting no later than February 1973, the Reverend John engaged in periods of fasting. A document entitled "Extracts from the Records of the 'Public Health and Research Benefit Memorial Fund'" in relation to fasting by a "one hundred percent volunteer", the Reverend John, was in evidence. It had, of course, been written and prepared by the Reverend John. The records were not themselves produced. The extract document indicated occasions of fasting extending to seven days, and on one occasion in 1984, a fasting for a period of 21 days. The fasting was sometimes supervised or monitored by relatives of the Reverend John. He also placed himself on a vegetarian diet. In a footnote to the "extracts" document, it was stated that "the fund has supplied the facilities and most of the supervision and the church has assisted by supplying some of the special foods. Considerable amounts of fresh fruits and vegetables have been grown on the farm for use in the research programme. The Church of S.J. has assisted in the research programme by providing the additional finance for the purpose of the special foods and distilled water required, and supplied to the volunteers and in many other ways to assist in the research programme. It will be necessary in the future to employ people and pay them wages and provide accommodation and full supervision to carry out the research required to fully prove the positive benefits that we believe are possible, and make them available to all mankind. The proof will have to be fully and very thoroughly documented to be accepted. The results to date have been astounding, and where the programme has been faithfully carried into effect, it has resulted in the complete elimination of all kinds of sickness and disability over the full period of the testing period to this date".

There was not, other than by this statement, adduced in * evidence any material showing the provision of facilities and supervision by the fund, of the supply of special foods by the church, other than that the daughter and son-in-law grew vegetables on the land purportedly held by them as trustees for the church, of the provision of additional finance or the existence of any "volunteer", other than the Reverend John. Further there was not any material showing that people had been employed, paid wages and/or provided with accommodation and supervision to carry out the research. The "results to date" can only be referable to the continuing good health and vigour of the Reverend John, even be it that for some years he has been in receipt of a partial invalid pension. His wife, after a divorce, died in about 1982. The daughter and son-in-law were at the time of the hearings, which extended over 15 months, unable to attend on account of ill health.

No part of the above is to say other than that the Reverend John is an embodiment of all he professes. He is, as has already been mentioned, 7 4 years of age, and although awarded an invalid pension in January 1978 and an aged pension in January 1979, is seemingly in good physical health and had the stamina and strength of mind and soul to prosecute and conduct these proceedings on behalf of the church and his daughter and son-in-law.

OTHER ALLEGED RELIGIOUS BELIEFS, ACTIVITIES AMD PRACTICES

In a letter written on 6 April 1978 by the Reverend John to the Respondent in reply to a request for information concerning gifts made to the Church of S.J., and especially as to the "times and places where regular church and other services were conducted", it was stated "regular church services and associated church activities are conducted at [Anglers Reach] and the council has sanctioned the use of the picnic shelter and club house for church activities, pending the erection of permanent facilities. The Reverend S.M.C. [the son-in-law], is the local minister for Anglers Reach. The Reverend S.A.F.R. la brother of the Reverend John] is the resident minister for the ...... Parish, and regular services are conducted at his residence in ...... I am the minister for the Parish of ...... but I am not now resident in ....... I have been resident in ...... for the past 10 years, but the property was sold recently and services are now held in one of the member's premises in ...... "

No part of the material placed before the Tribunal -evidenced the holding of services and "associated church activities" by any person at any place at any relevant time.

The wife of the Reverend John had been one of the 14 "founding members" of the Church of S.J. By affidavits sworn in 1984, each of the other 13 "founding members", including the Reverend John, said that:-

" 1. I believe in God Almighty.

  1. I am a Member of the "Church of [S.J.]" in New South Wales.
  2. I believe that the practices and beliefs of the "Church of [S.J.]" are in conformity with God's Word as stated in the "Holy Bible".
  3. I believe in the "basic tenant" of the "Church of [S.J.]" that "the care of the body is equally as important as the care of the soul".
  4. I believe that "God's" wonderful gift to all of the peoples of this world is "our own wonderful bodies and the life we live and enjoy in our bodies on this earth".
  5. I believe it is our duty and God's purpose for us to preserve and care for our bodies here on earth and our deeds and actions here on earth will be judged by God, and our rewards through eternity will be in proportion to our faith in God and our actions here on earth."

The Tribunal does not have any reason to doubt the sincerity of each of the "founding members" in their espousing their beliefs as set forth above. They may well be individually and/or collectively members of a religion and of a religious organization. This is not, however, the issue in these proceedings. The issue to be determined is as to whether the commercial and business activities carried on at Anglers Reach, under the name of the Central Administration, and/or under the name of the fund, were themselves the activities of a religious organization. As has been already stated, the answer to this question is to be gleaned from an analysis of the activities of the business organization, and the end to which the funds derived by it and the investments were put.

The Tribunal is of the opinion and so finds that no part of the moneys received by the Church of S.J. consequential on the operation of the business venture at Anglers Reach was at any relevant time used in furtherance of the tenets said to be held by these 13 deponents. The Reverend John, his wife, his daughter and son-in-law, four of the 14 "founding members", were on the evidence the only deponents who played any part in or derived any benefit from the commercial activities.

The Tribunal is of the opinion and so finds that no one of the expenses incurred by the Church of S.J., and as detailed in the accounts elsewhere referred to in these reasons, was at any relevant time applicable to the carrying out of any one or more of the objects set forth in the Constitution of the church. No expenditure was incurred in relation to research "into the causes of sickness and disease". There were not any moneys spent in connection with the work of the foundation. The Reverend John alone continued with his "part of it", that is, fasting and eating selectively.

In support of an application made in June 1982 to the Attorney-General in and for the State of New South Wales seeking the enactment of an Act of Parliament creating "a body corporate" in the name of the Church of S.J. , the Reverend John stated that:-

"...... it is appropriate that the twin roles of the church be defined. Firstly, there is the care of the earthly body we all have, and secondly comes the spiritual side and life hereafter.

The Church of [S.J.] and all of its members believe that it is useless to ask a person who is drowning to pray to God for help, or a person who is hungry and is watching his family starving to death to preach to him of God's love and the glories of the next life, or the person who is in pain or sick and unhealthy to exhort him to be happy. The church considers that it is its first duty to render whatever aid and assistance is possible immediately to rescue the drowning man, to feed the starving persons and to show to all persons how it is possible to be healthy all the time.

We believe the natural state is to be healthy and happy. God created us that way and only man has changed it.

(1) A healthy body comes first (and we believe it is obtainable).

(2) If you have a healthy body then you will be a happy person, and

(3) If you have health and happiness, you will always be thankful and grateful to the wonderful God who has made it all possible.

So it follows that the first and most important role of the church is not to build churches and preach -this will come after, but to do all things possible to show that a healthy body can be achieved by everyone here and now on earth by following God's teachings.

The way to health is simple but very hard to put into practice. It requires great willpower but the rewards are health and happiness. All foods eaten must be natural; all fresh fruits and vegetables preferably eaten raw or nuts, seeds and grains. No drugs or pills, no alcohol or tobacco. Regular exercise and regular fasting to allow the body the opportunity to clean itself inside.

This is the first role of the church. The second role will follow automatically as each healthy person enjoys each day of life and is grateful for each day to his Creator."

The cause espoused by the Reverend John is worthy indeed. No other person, on the evidence, had sought to himself or herself implement the first "role". There was not any evidence that the business enterprise or investment activities had been utilized to achieve either of the "twin roles".

In January 1982, another daughter of the Reverend John and this daughter's husband offered to the church by way of a gift a property seemingly owned by them at Campbelltown. The offer extended to "all of their possessions and equity or interest ...... provided that the church also take over and assume responsibility for all of their debts and commitments as of the date of the gift". Included amongst such "debts and commitments" was a mortgage liability on the property seemingly owned by them. The real estate, it was said, was to be declared a sanctuary and reserve and "seminary and college of learning", the same to be constructed as soon as possible. By resolution of the Executive Council of the church, the gift was accepted. The church at this time did not display by its accounts as prepared by the Reverend John a capacity to discharge such debts and commitments. The donors were to be appointed "joint resident ministers of the church, caretakers and cleaners of the rectory residence previously and thereafter occupied by them". The real estate was to remain registered in the names of the daughter and son-in-law.

The "Acknowledgement of Gift" recited as a party not only the church and the donors, but for a reason not apparent on the evidence other than as put by the Reverend John "on account of the mortgage and/or first charge", the union. The "gift" did not in any way concern the union. The daughter and son-in-law were recited, again for no apparent reason, as being members of the union and the union "agreed to the transfer referred to above".

By August 1982 the church had resolved to defer the project indefinitely and indeed to dispose of the land and invest the proceeds. On 18 May 1984 the Reverend John and the members of his family, meeting as the Executive Council of the church, resolved to rescind the resolution accepting the gift.

On 6 January 1985 the "Executive Council" resolved to finally refuse the offer of the gift of the property to the church. On 19 January 1985 the Reverend John, in a letter to the local shire council, said:-

"Due to circumstances beyond the control of the church, the Church of [S.J.] has not been able to proceed with the proposed development of this property.

Consequently the property has been disposed of.

As from the sixth day of January 1985 the Church of [S.J.] has no further interest in this property.

The registered owners are now [the son-in-law and daughter of the Reverend John] as joint owners."

On the evidence before the Tribunal, it is apparent that the property had not been "disposed of", the church had never had an "interest in this property", and the "registered owners" had not changed.

RELIGIOUS BELIEFS AND COMMERCIAL ACTIVITIES

Without in any way seeking to deprecate the accepted beliefs of the Reverend John, the Tribunal does not see them extending beyond a coterie of his relatives. The Tribunal does not take issue with the formation of the Church of S.J. in 1972, or the dedication of the 14 foundation members. There was not any evidence of the involvement of persons other than the Reverend John, his daughter, his son-in-law, and to a very limited extent, his brother (supervision of fasting) in the activities of the church since that time. I am not able, however, to divorce an awareness of the exemption from lodging returns of income, and hence paying income tax, attaching to a religious institution, and the Reverend John's desire to associate his "church" with the activities at Anglers Reach. It was an irresistible opportunity, if not a deliberate stratagem.

There is no issue that the Reverend John, prior to 1972, had by reason of accumulation of capital and benefits derived therefrom, been returning income and paying the appropriate tax. From 1942 to 1967, the Reverend John, at first on his own behalf, then in partnership with his wife, and later in partnership with their two daughters, carried on business as proprietors of residentials. When the residentials were sold, the proceeds were invested in units and shares listed on the stock exchange. Real estate was bought and sold. Over a period of years, no less than 14 properties were bought and sold realizing according to the Reverend John, a "non-taxable capital increase" of $329,050. In addition to the real estate, investment in land trust units, L.J. Hooker Investment Corporation Limited shares, and superannuation funds had resulted, according to the Reverend John, in "non-taxable capital increases received by us over the many years" of $316,000 in all a "total of non-taxable amounts received by us over 40 odd years, $645,050". It was said by the Reverend John that he and his wife lived on the tax paid earnings and the investments "were allowed to accumulate over the years in assets".

The Reverend John said that in about 1967, he "became interested in my health and became a health nut and started investigating the possibility of extending the average life span of first myself and the family, and later of all people". In due course, the partnerships were dissolved, and by 1971 a restructuring had been effected to the end that the Reverend John and his wife were the beneficial shareholders in ten private companies, and each daughter, with her husband, beneficial shareholders in another ten companies. The incorporation in 1971 of R. Insurances Pty. Limited, and the issue by it of insurance policies was, according to the Reverend John, "to ensure a secure income for life for both of us and which would be controlled by the group families indefinitely". The capital accounts of the Reverend John and his wife in their partnership, prior to dissolution, showed balances in their favour of 5235,000.

It was, however, in 1971, and shortly thereafter, and at a time when he said he had "retired from business", that the Reverend John caused the insurance company, the church and the union to be structured and/or incorporated. Whether the structuring was in aid of minimizing or eliminating the need to return income and pay tax or not is immaterial to these reasons. The important aspect is the practical effect emanating from the implementation of the design.

As has been elsewhere shown in these reasons, the development was not immediate, although proximate. The insurance company was incorporated in June 1971. The church was formed in November 1972, and the "Public Health and Research Benefit Memorial Fund" in December 1972 with the avowed object to "engage in and carry out research into all aspects of health, sickness and disease in human beings and the causes, prevention and possible elimination of sickness and disease in all human beings". The union was established in November 1972 and registered as a trade union in May 1973 with the object of having "...... one body have a voice for all the small companies and their employees and to act as negotiator for all parties on an equal basis". It would seem that it was thought desirable, if not necessary, to have the union as a tax free entity and the insurance company as a repository of the eventual assets. No doubt at this time, the Reverend John anticipated substantial profits being generated by the activities at Anglers Reach. Profits were indeed derived as is shown by the admitted accounts. In the years prior to 1982, such profits were sufficient to enable the surplus funds to be invested in the various activities herein elsewhere described.

The repository, the insurance company, was ideally situated. Policies taken out, an inflow of funds and a tax-free capital income for many years guaranteed. The union as a debtor of the insurance company was likewise an ideal entity for the placing of resources.

Other than by reason of the activities at Anglers Reach and the interest earned on moneys invested with third parties, no one of the three organizations would have, during the relevant years, earned anything like sufficient, if at all, to support the Reverend John, his daughter and son-in-law, and other members of his family.

The partnership of the Reverend John and his wife was dissolved in 1977, and in March of that year "all of our assets and debts and liabilities" were transferred purportedly to the Church of S.J., since which time he said, "we have dedicated our lives to the works of God". It was in October 1976 that the property at Anglers Reach was said to have been transferred by M.S. Pty Limited and L. & D. Pty Limited to the church.

The Reverend John, by 1978, saw Anglers Reach as a very valuable investment. In a document prepared late in that year, he placed a value of $1,244,218 on the unimproved capital value of the real estate, and put a sale price of $1 million on it.

In a financial report dealing with "future development potential and lists of improvements made and approximate costs of making those improvements based on today's costs" prepared in 197 8, the Reverend John detailed the "several businesses carried on at Anglers Reach as:-

(1) Citrus orchard about 800 trees, about 7 acres

(2) Farm producing fruits and vegetables, 3 acres

(3) Grazing for about 10 head of cattle, 16 acres

(4) Tourist and caravan park 80 sites, 7 acres

(5) Museum of old farming machinery

(6) Ski gardens with boat ramp

(7) Petrol pumps for boats, motors and farm machinery
  • (8) Hire caravans, tent sites with appropriate amenities


(9) Private beaches for hire or rent daily, monthly or annually

(10) Ice-making and sales
  • (11) Kiosk and general store, take-away hot foods etcetera"

In the same document, it was stated that the Church of S.J., was interested in a proposal to convert an area of land into a retirement village sufficient to accommodate some 400 units. It was further stated "this project would not effect in any way the further expansion and development of the tourist park and the many acres of lawns and gardens could be used and enjoyed by both parts."

In the list of items making up the "cost and values of assets and improvements made to the Anglers Reach property", part of the above financial report, and containing in all some 4 8 ingredients, no mention is made of the church or of any religious activities. Suffice here to say that one of the "items" recites as an integral part of the cost structure:

"Business goodwill created over the last 8 years and attached to the many businesses operate:-

Orchard, museum, petrol sales, leases, beaches, rented caravans, ice-making and sales, caravan park sites etcetera, kiosk, shop and store business, take-away food etcetera, and farm grazing etcetera, based on $70,000-S100,000 turnover per year estimated at $300,000."

A further "item" recites:-

"During the past five years, the [daughter and son-in-law] and family have worked on the development of the park and the creation of the goodwill and the good name and reputation of the park and have been solely responsible for the entire development of the park to its present high state of excellence. Many thousands of people have enjoyed the facilities and amenities provided in the park and which have been praised by all and sundry as being far superior to any other park on The ...... During all this period, only minimal wages have been paid to them, and it is estimated that an amount of $80,000 per year have been earned by each [son-in-law and daughter] and has not been remunerated to them. This money has been retained in the business and was used in the development of the park, $100,000."

The "business" so referred to and detailed in 1978 did not change in any relevant sense since up until the time the property was sold in the 1984 financial year. the operations continued as they then had been.

It may well be that the Reverend John, his daughters and sons-in-law and the other deponents or founding members are, by reason of their beliefs and the practices of the Reverend John, members of a religious institution. That institution was not the organization that carried on commercial activities at Anglers Reach or invested moneys in securities. Except for the use of the name, the Church of S.J., there was not any relevant association or nexus between the religious beliefs and practices of these persons and the business and investment income that resulted in the profits sought to be taxed.

THE UNION AND THE PAYMENT OF INTEREST TO IT BY THE CHURCH OF S.J.

It was by deed made 3 November 1972 that the Reverend John, his wife and six members of his family, being the only employees at that time of 17 of the family companies and three family partnerships, agreed to, with the said companies and partnerships, establish a trade union to be known as the A.E.E. Trade Union Association. The deed recited that:-

"This union does not hold itself out, or purport to be (solely) a trade union of employees. It does claim to be a trade union of both employees and employers (amalgamated) and appears to be the first and only organization of its kind in existence in New South Wales."

The Constitution and Rules of the union, as agreed to by the parties to the deed, inter alia, set forth as its objects:-

"The basic purpose of the union being to amalgamate the usually opposing sides of labour, management and ownership into undivided whole, and to encourage all members to work together and co-operate for the common good of all.

The ideal concept being to abandon completely conflict between the boss and the worker and to encourage the idea of all parties working together in partnership for the benefit of all."

And so far as it related to investments of funds provided:-

"All moneys surplus to the immediate needs of the union may, at the direction of the executive committee, be invested at call with any member, company, firm or partnership (in addition to any other investment allowed by law) and preference will be given to members, but all moneys so loaned or invested must be at call."

Under the same date, 3 November 1972, the Reverend John was appointed "trustee" of all assets of the union.

The union was registered as a trade union under the provisions of the Trade Union Act 1881 (NSW) on 29 May 1973. At the time of the hearing, it had been deregistered.

Under date 11 July 1973, the secretary of the union, the son-in-law of the Reverend John, made application to the Respondent for "consideration to be given to the issue of a certificate of exemption from the requirement to lodge annual income tax returns by this union". In due course, the application was granted, the effect of which was to exempt the income of the union from taxation. The Commissioner requested the furnishing of a financial statement and balance sheet for the year ended 30 June 1973. On 31 August 1973, the Reverend John wrote to the Respondent enclosing "the required financial statement and balance sheet" showing "income members' dues" of $1176, disbursements of $4 and "investments at call with member companies" $1172. On 23 April 1974 the Respondent advised the union that it would not be necessary to lodge income tax returns unless requested so to do by the Commissioner of Taxation. Financial reports and balance sheets were to be provided for determination of deductibility of subscriptions pursuant to Section 73 of the Income Tax Assessment Act. The financial statements prepared as at 31 December 1973 disclosed assets of $2462, mostly said to be "on call" with family companies.

By 31 December 1974 the financial position of the union had somewhat altered. The total asset position was stated at $272,571, the same being substantially acquired as a result of "gifts" and comprising land trust units, real estate and amounts owing by family companies. The then liabilities of the union approximated the values ascribed to its assets and were themselves comprised of debts stated to be due to family companies, and amounts representing encumbrances on assets purportedly acquired. Included amongst the debts so assumed was a liability to R. Insurances Pty Limited of $60,332.

There appeared as a notation at the foot of the balance sheet of the union as at 31 December 1974 the following:-

"Property at ...... was acquired by way of gift from an association member (gift duty returns have been submitted) and the property title duly transferred to the union. All debts and liabilities owing by the previous owner have been taken over by the union and these liabilities approximate the value of the property.

Property at ...... Can additional property to the one above] was acquired by purchase from an association member and again the union has accepted the liabilities under the first mortgage and a loan which in total approximates the value of the property.

The union also purchased 220,520 units of 50 cents each in Australian Land Trusts from a member and borrowed the full amount of money at call to make the purchase.

All returns from these assets in excess of the costs of acquiring and conducting them will be used by the union solely for the benefit of the members of the union."

A surplus of assets over liabilities was not apparent. No commercial justification for these "gifts" was, on the evidence before the Tribunal, shown to have existed at the time that they were made or at any other relevant time.

Under date 17 June 1975, the companies M.S. Pty Limited and L. & D. Pty Limited purported to encumber the property at Anglers Reach in favour of the union to the extent of $167,723. There was not any evidence of such moneys having become available to the union for the purpose of a loan by way of trading activities or realization of assets previously held by it or from any external source. The union did not, on the face of its December 1974 accounts, have any asset or assets that could have provided security for a further borrowing. The loan funds could only have come, if they did come at all, by way of inter family company unsecured borrowing.

It was during the 1975 financial year of the union that this purported debt of the two companies first appeared in its accounts described as "at call loans to [Anglers Reach] first mortgage $176,213". Pursuant to a purported memorandum of mortgage dated 17 June 1975, the two companies were said to have been lent by the union $167,723, the same being allegedly used to pay out the debt to Telford Property Trust and other borrowings. When in 1977 the property at Anglers Reach was "given" to the Church of S.J., the debt to the union purportedly went with it.

No books of account of the companies, the union, the partnership or the church were made available to the Tribunal. It is more likely than not that no such books were kept. From an examination of the balance sheets of the union, as prepared by the Reverend John, for the 1974 and 1975 years, it is apparent that it was from R. Insurances Pty Limited and with an increase in borrowing of about $220,000 in the latter year that the funds were derived. From what source had the insurance company obtained the funds? The only money then available to it was the initial $235,000 paid on account of the two annuity policies by the Reverend John and his wife, and the $19,000 paid year by year on account of the six endowment policies, the latter being substantially funded by the annuities.

Thus the Reverend John and his wife had indirectly made the money available to pay out the debt to the finance company, such debt being owed by the two companies, the beneficial owners of which were the family members. If the money had been lent to the two companies by the Reverend John and his wife in order that the third party indebtedness could have been paid out, and security by way of a mortgage obtained in favour of the lenders, then amounts of interest due or paid to them would have been taxable in their hands. To make the creditor of the companies appear to be the union, which itself owed moneys to the insurance company, enabled the interest moneys to be received by an entity or entities that were themselves not required to pay income tax on these amounts.

When the debt was purportedly transferred to the Church of S.J., the church sought to step into the shoes of the companies and claimed deductibility for the interest, the recipient being exempt from tax on it.

The purported increase in the size of the debt from $176,213 in 1975 to $574,739 in 1982 was, but for approximately $117,000, solely attributable to the round robin of purported interest payments. No evidence was available to the Tribunal as to the beneficial recipient of the $117,000, or as to the use to which this money was put.

A form of mortgage was entered into purportedly executed on behalf of the union by the Reverend John and on behalf of the companies by his daughter and son-in-law. The term of the mortgage was four years, the interest 10% per annum, and on default, 12% per annum.

As at 31 December 1975, the total asset position of the union had changed whereby it was then stated to be in an amount of $443,552, the increase largely occurring as a result of the purported assumption of the mortgage debt on the Anglers Reach property, which at 31 December 1975, stood at $176,213. The liabilities correspondingly became larger, here by reason of an increased indebtedness to R. Insurances Pty Limited. Interest was charged by the union on the amount said to be owing by the two family companies to it. Money was purportedly lent to the two companies with which they could then pay their interest to the union.

I have elsewhere in these reasons made mention of the "gift instrument" of 18 October 1976, whereby the two companies purportedly gave to the Church of S.J. the property at Anglers Reach, "upon condition that the church take over all of the debts and liabilities of the 'companies'". At 31 December 1976 liabilities of the two companies to the union so said to have been assumed by the church were stated to be in the amount of $254,505 - this being the figure appearing in the balance sheet of the union as at 31 December 1976 "[Anglers Reach] First Mortgage". The amount appearing in the loan register of the union at 17/12/1976 was $194,505 - "secured" against a property for which the amount of $150,000 had been paid by the two companies in 1973.

It was on this alleged liability and further to the obligations that were said to have been taken over on the happening of the gift that interest was purportedly payable. The church allegedly being without funds sufficient to pay, borrowed and satisfied its obligation, if any, by means of a "round robin" type transaction.

It might here be noted that not one of the items of expenditure by the union, as detailed in its accounts as in evidence, related to resolution, consideration or arbitration of an industrial or employee/employer dispute. The items of expenditure rather related to purported inter company or partnership movements of money. The income derived, or said to have been derived, by it was largely attributable to the interest receipt resulting from the "round robin" series of transactions. On the evidence available to the Tribunal, the union at all relevant times engaged in the business primarily of borrowing and lending moneys from and to family companies or enterprises, one of the latter allegedly being the Church of S.J.

On 31 December 1977 the church "debt" had risen to $340,931 (the balance sheet and loan register now being in accord, the one with the other), interest as aforesaid being paid by the church out of loan moneys, the effect of the same being to increase the indebtedness, the church to the union. The purported liability of the union to the insurance company then stood at a stated figure of $304,990.

By 31 December 1978, the mortgage debt on the Anglers Reach property had risen to $376,325; by 31 December 1979 to $415,392; by 31 December 1980 to $458,515; by 31 December 1981 to $531,379 and by 31 December 1982 to $621,638. The increase in the "debt" of the church to the union, other than by reason of the $117,000 above referred to, arose from the purportedly assumed obligation to pay interest. On the face of the various balance sheets of the union, there were movements on balances reflecting amounts said to be owing to or by family companies. No books of account were however tendered in evidence, produced or indeed it would seem kept, so the transactions between balance dates could not be otherwise examined. There was not any way of certaining details of movements between year end balances, other than those appearing on the statement of the Church/Union Loan Account. In 1983 the relevant debt to the union was expressed at $727,229, this prior to the sale of the Anglers Reach property.

Transactions narrated on a balance sheets were not supported by movements in property of the union. As a notation to the balance sheet of the union as at 31 December 1983 it was stated "S [sold] and D [disposed of] Properties and Trusts matured" realised $220,815. Between 31 December 1982 and 31 December 1983, according to the balance sheets at those dates, there had not been a property sold or disposed of, and no trust had matured. The Tribunal is of the opinion that the accounts of the union tendered in evidence cannot be relied upon for the purpose of these proceedings as reflecting real transactions resulting from contractual obligations, in contrast to anticipated or contrived activities of the union. The accounts were constructed by the Reverend John to show a state of affairs sought after by him and not to reflect a true state of affairs. They were not at any time, nor did they have to be, subject to audit.

The amount outstanding by the church to the union as at 17 June 1984 was said to have been $785,407. On 20 [171 June 1984 the church "in part settlement of the moneys owing under the Deed of First Charge which is in default", transferred and handed over "money together with properties, investments and assets" to the union totalling in all, according to a "full list" prepared and signed by the Reverend John under that date, $421,567.

The assets so purportedly transferred in 1984 by the church to the union comprised all of the properties and investments of the church at that time, the union said to then be "the sole owner of the ...... assets ...... [and] the church shall have no further interest whatsoever". The assets so "transferred" included the amount outstanding under the vendor's mortgage on the sale of Anglers Reach of $215,000 and the 'benefit' of securities appropriated by the respondent in part satisfaction of amounts due under the assessments, namely $187,929. The balance then owing to the union appeared in the "full list" at the sum of $363,840. Yet in a document under date 25 April 1986, the Reverend John detailed "items allocated and transferred" at $161,000, and the balance owing as $624,347, not $363,840 as it had been shown in the "full list" at 20 June 1984. Other items "allocated and transferred" were identified as "interests in investment trusts" and "loan moneys on deposit with third parties", the same substantially funded by the moneys arising from the sale of Anglers Reach, and the benefit of the "first mortgage on [Anglers Reach] ", all said to have been transferred to R. Insurances Pty Limited "to cover the liabilities of the life insurance policies in force".

In 1986, a further transfer of assets was said to have taken place then to the insurance company "Statutory Insurance Fund No. 1" of "listed investments and loans", valued at $496,416. The investments and loans so purportedly transferred were comprised of:

First mortgage on Anglers Reach $215,000

Queensland Government Development

Authority $150,000

Queensland Government Development

Authority $ 46,200

State Energy Commission of

Western Australia $ 14,000

Northern Territory of Australia loan $ 11,000

State Electricity Commission of

Queensland $ 9,500

Telford Trust Units $ 13,086

Land Trusts Units $ 37,630

As with other purported passages of property, no consideration was evidenced and the sole participants in the transactions and meetings were Reverend John and members of his family. The transactions were not real in the sense of their being effective to transfer or assign interest in the property or responsibility for the debts. What was sought to be done by the transfer to the insurance company was to ensure that all of the assets, once said to be the property of the family companies, the church and the union, became the property of the insurance company, the fund and thence the holders of the annuity and endowment policies.

It was stated by the Reverend John in a memorandum of 25 April 1986, "all other investments received by this union have been transferred to cover the liabilities of the life insurance policies in force and controlled by [R.] Insurances Pty Limited, and are not the property of this union". In the balance sheets of the union prepared after that date, no amount was shown as being owed to it by the church.

The Reverend John placed before the Tribunal evidence as to the financial position of himself and his wife and members of his family, together with that of the family companies, the church, the union and the insurance company prior to the 1978 financial year. It might be noted that assessments were issued by the Respondent against the Church of S.J. Trust for the years 1978 to 1981 totalling in all not less than $76,728. Due and proper objection was not taken to these assessments. The interest was not there allowed as a deduction.

The prime purpose sought to be served by the placing of the evidence referable to prior years before the Tribunal was to show that the Respondent, over a period of time, had allowed as a deduction interest when paid on money that had been borrowed from third parties. So when the property at Anglers Reach was said to have been owned by the two family companies and money lent by the Bank of New South Wales and later Telford Property Trust, the Respondent allowed the interest on the borrowing as a deduction in arriving at the Section 80 losses sustained by and allowed to the companies.

It is said that as with the bank and finance company, so with the union. The interest is an allowable deduction. But one may ask, how did the church become indebted to the union? Was there an effective passing over of the liability? Did the church assume responsibility for the debt and interest falling due, or was there merely the appearance of such assumption manifested by the "round robin" each quarter day? The Tribunal has concluded that the true situation was the latter. The deductibility of the interest, it being allowed as an expense to the companies, is thus not germane to the position of the church.

In the view of the Tribunal, the "loan" transactions are not sustainable as being real transactions, i.e. transactions having the meaning and significance sought to be ascribed to them by the taxpayer. In effect, the Reverend John and his wife paid moneys to an insurance company controlled by the Reverend John, which company in turn paid the money to a union controlled by the Reverend John, which union either paid the financier on behalf of the two family companies, or paid the money to the two family companies to be used by them to discharge the third party indebtedness.

It was sought to characterize the money merely by reason of its having purportedly passed through a company or entity. The mere payment of money on behalf of another, a voluntary assumption of a liability on behalf of another, does not enable this payment or assumed liability to be thereby designated as it would have been in accord with the nature of the initial third party transaction.

The movements of the subject assets were not consequent upon the exercise of rights given to the union or insurance company under any valid security documents. Indeed, they were at variance even with such documents as were in evidence. The transactions were reflected in the accounts much as the Reverend John wished that they should be so reflected, regardless of the legal rights, if any, of the union and the insurance company.

The payments of amounts by the church said to have been on account of interest were not made pursuant to a legal obligation assumed by the church consequent upon a legally binding transfer or assumption of liability. There was not any evidence of any real contract underlying the documentation or of any intention that the church should genuinely so assume the liability. The acts were done and documents executed by the Reverend John and members of his family intended by them to give to third parties, including the respondent and the Tribunal, "the appearance of creating between the parties legal rights and obligations different from the actual legal rights and obligations (if any) which the parties intended to create". The transaction was a sham. (See Snook -v- West Riding Investments Limited [1967] 2 QB 786 802; Glenfield Estates Pty Limited -v-Federal Commissioner of Taxation 80 ALR 671 685). The evidence and the findings in relation to it as above and as elsewhere set forth in these reasons do not establish that the alleged interest payments were expenses incurred by the Church of S.J. in the gaining of its assessable income.

THE INSURANCE COMPANY, ITS FORMATION, ACTIVITIES AMD THE PAYMENT OF INTEREST AND PREMIUMS

The R. Insurances Company Pty Limited was incorporated on 18 [21] June 1971, the subscribers to its Memorandum of Association being 13 of the 18 family companies.

The Memorandum of Association set forth as one of the objects of the company:

" To provide all of the facilities of insurance which are available by other companies in Australia to the members of this company on a non-profit basis, all surplus funds available after provision of adequate reserves are to be returned to the members by:-

(a) a reduction of premiums payable; or

(b) no claim bonuses; or

(c) participating bonuses as allowed by law; or
  • (d) in any other equitable manner provided by the Directors and allowed under the law."

On 9 July 1981 a Director's meeting of the company was held attended by the Reverend John, a daughter and son-in-law. It was resolved at such meeting that:-

"In order to qualify for exemption from the Life Insurance Act in respect of the issue of life insurance policies by the company, it is hereby agreed and determined that all or any life insurance business undertaken by this company will be, and is hereby, limited to employees of [R.] Insurances Pty Limited and no other persons in Australia will be eligible for such policies. This resolution is made to enable to company to comply with the requirements of clause (c) of section 4(1) of the definition of "Life Insurance Business" in the Act."

On 21 July 1971 the Reverend John, as a director and secretary of the company, wrote to the Insurance Commissioner informing him of the passing of the above resolution and seeking confirmation that thereafter the company would not need to seek registration under the Life Insurance Act 1945-1965. The Reverend John, at the request of the Insurance Commissioner, confirmed that the company policy would be to only issue life policies to employees, being the "actual staff employed by" the company and "the directors". On 16 August 1971, the Commissioner advised that the company would not be required to register under the Act.

It is noted that the company was thus relieved of obligations under the Life Insurance Act 1932-1966 and could carry on life insurance business, the same restricted to its employees. The company was thereby exempted from the obligation to place moneys on deposit, otherwise required of insurance companies.

On 27 August 1971 the Respondent was asked by letter penned by the Reverend John as to whether there were "any special requirements or provisions under the Act [Income Tax Assessment Act] which would require my attention". In such letter, it was stated that "the company will not deal with the general public at all. As a non-profit organization, it will not pay any dividends to its shareholders. At this stage the company only proposes to operate the 'life insurance business' to its employees. There are only four employees. Proposed policies to be issued in the future total six only, four endowment and two rather large annuity policies to the two ageing directors, $150,000 approximately. As no profits will be made or dividends declared by the company, I would be pleased to know how the Commissioner treats this type of company and what are the obligations of its officers in this regard. The company at this stage will not be complying with the 30/20 rule regarding investments and will not be entering any other field of insurance".

The company lodged an income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1972 under date 1 July 1972, in which it disclosed an issued capital of $13 and "total trust funds" then being administered under "Life Fund No. 1" of $24 8,084. The company at the same time applied to the Respondent for a "certificate exempting this company from the requirement to make annual returns of all income and expenditure". It was said in such application that "all premiums or annuity purchase payments, together with all returns or interests received by the company from investments of the fund is received for and on behalf of the fund and immediately lodged to the credit of the fund "Life Fund No. 1" at National Bank, ...... a total of eight policies have been issued by the company to its directors and/or employees ...... policies 1 and 2 are purchased annuities paid in full and 3 to 8 are pure endowment of ten years duration ......".

The Reverend John is the "trustee" of "Life Fund No. 1". In the return that was lodged in the name of the "Life Fund No. 1", it was shown that "two annuity policies had been bought for cash" and "six pure endowment policies of ten year duration paid to first year only". The funds received were said to total $254,000 being in respect of:-

Trust Fund Received Annuities Purchased

18.9.1971 Policy No. 1 to Wife of Reverend John $ 70,000.15

18.9.1971 Policy No. 2 to Reverend John $165,000.15

Pure Endowment Policies

18.10.1971 Policy No. 3 to Daughter $ 1,200.15

18.10.1971 Policy No. 4 to Son-in-law $ 1,200.15

20.12.1971 Policy No. 5 to Reverend John $ 12,000.15

24. 4.1972 Policy No. 6 to Daughter $ 1,200.15

24. 4.1972 Policy No. 7 to Son-in-law $ 1,200.15

24. 4.1972 Policy No. 8 to Wife of Reverend John $ 1,200.15

The funds were said to have been invested at call with five of the family corporate shareholders. Income derived from the invested funds was allegedly utilized to meet payments on the annuity policies issued to the Reverend John and his wife.

By 1976 the total investments of the "Life Fund No. 1" were said to be $304,061. Interest was purportedly received on the "trust funds invested (at call)" and dispersed in paying the annuities to the Reverend John and his wife. At 30 June 1976, $274,900 of the "investments" of the fund was said to have been loaned to or invested with the union. An examination of the balance sheet of the union as at 31 December 1976 shows that its major debtor was the Church of S.J., and its major creditor in a like amount, the insurance company.

Consequent upon the gift instrument of 1976/1977, herein elsewhere referred to, annuities were paid to the Church of S.J., and out of interest received on funds then invested, such interest having been "paid" by the church on its borrowings. In 1980 annuities of $18,138 were paid and the investment on which interest was allegedly due included an amount owing by the union to the company of $333,522. Premiums of $19,000 were received, $16,600 of which was paid by the church. So, in the years 1981 to 1984, were premiums received approximating the annuities paid, the interest received on investments, substantially from the church, being on lent to the union and thence to the church.

The above was the sole "business" of the insurance company. On 31 December 1982 the Reverend John was notified by the Respondent that in respect of the financial years 1972 to 1982 "it is not considered that the trust is carrying on a business". This view of the Respondent was maintained thereafter.

By 30 June 1986, the total funds held by the insurance company in trust were said to be $503,916, the same being substantially "invested" with the union and the Church of S.J. Interest purportedly received and paid by the union and the church totalled $46,884.73. At the time of the hearing, and accepting the documentation prepared by the Reverend John at its face value, the beneficial recipients of the net assets, once said to have been held by the church, the union and the insurance company, were the holders of the endowment and annuity policies said to have been issued by R. Insurances Pty Limited.

The payments of amounts by the church said to have been on account of interest and funded by the annuities were not made pursuant to a legal obligation assumed by the church. There was not any evidence of any real contract underlying the documentation or of any intention that the church should genuinely assume a liability. The transaction was a sham. The purported interest payments were not expenses incurred by the Church of S.J. in the gaining of its assessable income.

THE PROPER TAXPAYER - THE CHURCH OF S.J. TRUST

The three assessments each issued against the "Trustee for Church of S.J.". Documentation identifies the "Church of S.J. Trust". As has been set forth earlier in these reasons, no objection was taken in the Notices of Objection to this identification of the proper taxpayer. It was not a ground there relied upon.

Section 190 of the Income Tax Assessment Act and prior to the amendment made by Act No. 48 of 1986 provided "upon every such reference or appeal -

(a) The taxpayer shall be limited to the grounds stated in his objection.

........................”

Consequent upon the amendment to the Act, effective 1 July 1986, an objector, provided the Tribunal otherwise orders, can rely upon an additional ground or grounds. In the subject matter, application was not made, even if it be permissible, for such an order to be made.

Nevertheless, the question as to the proper taxpayer was raised by the Reverend John and answered by the respondent. Two bases were relied upon. The first is that the assessment itself asserts a trust, and there is not any evidence to the contrary. It is clear, according to the Respondent, from the material in evidence that the Reverend John, his daughter and son-in-law all purported to act as trustees in respect of the relevant property and activities. They did not purport to act at all as beneficial owners, and the assessments asserting as they do a trust stand unless the contrary is shown. The contrary has not been shown.

Further, the Respondent says that even putting the assessment to one side, the evidence shows a pooling of resources, sometimes in the hands of the Reverend John, sometimes in the hands of the daughter and son-in-law, but a pooling of resources pursuant to which these individuals benefited. Indeed, at the end of the day, and as a consequence of the purported transfers of property that took place as late as 1986, all of the assets of the church, the union and the insurance company were purportedly held for the benefit of the policy holders, the same being the Reverend John and the designated members of his family. A trust, it is submitted, is proved on the evidence and the assessments properly issued against it. The Tribunal agrees with these submissions. The taxpayer has been rightly designated.

THE DAUGHTER AMD SON-IN-LAW

The assessments raised against the two individuals have already been detailed, and the amounts sought to be brought to tax identified.

Section 26(e) of the Income Tax Assessment Act provides:-

The assessable income of a taxpayer shall include –

............

(e) The value to the taxpayer of all allowances, gratuities, compensations, benefits, bonuses and premiums allowed, given or granted to him in respect of or for or in relation directly or indirectly to any employment of or services rendered by him, whether so allowed, given or granted in money, goods, land, meals, sustenance, the use of premises or quarters or otherwise

....................."

Section 190 of the Act provides, inter alia, that the "burden of proving that the assessment is excessive shall lie upon the taxpayer". That is, the onus of disproving the assessment rests upon the taxpayer. In Politi v Commissioner of Taxation 88 ATC 5029, it was said:

Section 190 of the Act provides so far as relevant that the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive shall lie upon the taxpayer on a review before the Tribunal under Part V, Division 2. It is now well established that subs.190 (b) imposes the burden on the taxpayer to establish that elements in the assessment are wrong. Often the taxpayer must show not only that the assessment is wrong, which is a negative proposition, but positively what correction should be made in order to make it right: Trautwein v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (No. 1) [1936] HCA 77; (1936) 56 CLR 63 especially per Latham C.J. at 87; McCormack v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 7 9 ATC 4111 per Gibbs J. at 4121-4122, per Stephen J. at 4123, per Murphy J. at 4132 and Macmine Pty. Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation 79 ATC 4133. This approach is supported by the operation of sub-s. 177(1) of the Act, and in principle by the circumstance that the facts relating to the taxpayer's income are peculiarly within the taxpayer's own knowledge.

The section thus imposes upon the taxpayer the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive. The Act does not place any onus on the Commissioner to show that the assessment was correctly made, nor is there any statutory requirement that the assessment should be sustained or supported by evidence.

With respect, the Tribunal agrees with and adopts this statement of the law.

Other than by illustrating the situation as it existed prior to the relevant years, that is prior to the taxpayers becoming employees of the Church of S.J., no evidence was placed before the Tribunal putting at issue the calculations made by the respondent and the values put upon the various benefits. No evidence was adduced or submission made so as to raise an issue as to the correctness or appropriateness of the Respondent's calculations. Indeed, the reverse was so. Elsewhere in these reasons, I have instanced the value ascribed to the part played by the taxpayers in the development of the park at Anglers Reach and the value that they derived from the benefits conferred upon them by the organization there carrying on business.

The Reverend John prepared all of the material referable to and contained in the income tax returns lodged by the daughter and son-in-law.

The home in which the daughter and son-in-law resided (with their family was described in evidence as a very neat and nice cottage recently refurbished. It "was not like an old style appearance on the outside and very modern on the inside, and it was very comfortable". They were paid small wages in cash and all private expenses were paid for "by the church". Substantial payments were made on behalf of the taxpayers for private expenditure and these were identified. The value to be ascribed to the accommodation was determined by the Respondent following upon inquiries made as to what would be paid in the area for comparable housing, and the amounts elsewhere detailed were decided upon as reasonable for this benefit.

The Tribunal does not consider that either the daughter or son-in-law has discharged the onus resting upon them, and the assessments, so far as primary tax should, consistent with these reasons and findings, be issued against them.

PENALTIES

The basis upon which the assessment of the penalties was calculated has been set forth earlier in these reasons. Section 226, as it was prior to the amendment act of 1984, is said to be the applicable provision. Section 226(1) was applied in relation to the assessments that issued in respect of the 1982 and 1983 financial years. It was with respect to these years that no returns were lodged. The Commissioner adopted and applied a rate of 10% and then 20%. Whilst the quantum of the penalties will need to be altered as a consequence of the assessments now to issue consequent upon these reasons and findings, the Respondent otherwise seeks to maintain the rates of penalties applied, notwithstanding the fact that there was an exemption granted to the Church of S.J. still existent in these years. The justification for this approach is that, by letter of 5 September 1983, if not by an earlier letter of April 1983, the church was requested to lodge the 1982 and 1983 returns, and it did not in fact do so, this even after the exemption had in due course been withdrawn. As to the 1984 financial year, whilst an income tax return was lodged, it was simply a one page document that returned a nil income. In relation to this year, the Respondent applied a flat rate of 75% per annum upon the basis of there being an evasion of tax, and the "nature of the one page" income tax return.

As to the individual taxpayers, Section 226(2) is relied upon in all instances. Whilst they did in fact lodge returns, the same did not disclose the proper amount of Section

26 (e) benefit.

It was submitted on behalf of the respondent that there was evidence available to the effect that not only was the property at Anglers Reach registered in the names of the individuals, but that "the entirety of the Queensland loans are in fact in the name of the taxpayers", even be it that it is said that these assets are held in trust for the policy holders. This may well have been so.

DECISION ON ASSESSMENTS

The Tribunal, having considered all of the evidence and the submissions made on behalf of the taxpayers and the Respondent Commissioner, is of the opinion that the assessments that would issue on the taxable income for the 1982, 1983 and 1984 financial years against the Church of S.J. Trust in accord with and as set forth in these reasons are proper and refers the matter back to the Respondent for the issuing of assessments consistent with these reasons. Likewise, the issue of appropriate assessments against the daughter and son-in-law consistent with the matters set forth in these reasons is also referred back to the Respondent for appropriate action.

On the question of the imposition of penalties, it is the opinion of the Tribunal that the Reverend John, honest to himself, believed that the mechanism that he had set in train was effective and such as to not attract the incidence of taxation. Deceit or dishonesty did not enter into the series of transactions. Whilst it is the view of the Tribunal that they are not real transactions, the same were not carried into effect other than was consistent with the views genuinely held by the Reverend John, The Reverend John deluded himself into believing that the church, the insurance company and the union were, at the relevant times, such as he maintained that they were and effective to the ends sought by him. In all the circumstances of this matter, it is the view of the Tribunal that the imposition of additional tax should be remitted, or merely set at a nominal amount.

So far as the individual taxpayers are concerned, their evidenced financial position and state of health, in the view of the Tribunal, warrant additional tax being remitted or likewise set at a nominal amount.
