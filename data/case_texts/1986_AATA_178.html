Re Peter Briggs (No 2) and Australian Taxation Office [1986] AATA 178 (30 June 1986)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: PETER BRIGGS (No. 2)

And: AUSTRALIAN TAXATION OFFICE

No. N85/481

AAT No. 2738

Freedom of Information

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

The Hon. Sir William Prentice (Senior Member)

CATCHWORDS

Freedom of Information - documents relating to applicant's taxation and investigations as to his business affairs - various claims to exemption from disclosure.

Freedom of Information Act 1982 - ss 36, 37(1), 37(2), 38, 40, 41, 42, 45.

Re Peter Briggs (No. 1) and Australian Taxation Office N85/197 (of even date);

Department of Health v. Jephcott [1985] FCA 370; (1985) 62 ALR 421;

Re Witheford and Department of Foreign Affairs (1983) 5 ALD 534 at p. 542.

HEARING

SYDNEY

30:6:1986

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review in accordance with the Schedules annexed to the Reasons for Decision herein.

DECISION

Mr. Briggs had applied to the Australian Taxation Office in Perth for access under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 ("the FOI Act") to certain records relating to his income tax assessments over the years 1976 to 1981 inclusive. His request was processed and decisions taken as to claims for exemption from release of some of those documents. These decisions became the subject of appeal to this Tribunal in matter N85/197. The Respondent considered that certain documents on the Departmental files in Perth and Canberra, were outside the ambit of that application. The applicant met this situation by making a further application for access to these further documents. Access has been refused as to certain of them either in part or totally, on various grounds.

2. This application is concerned with appealing against the lastmentioned decisions. During the hearing of matter N85/197, it was requested that the Tribunal as constituted therein should hear this present matter; but owing to the unavailability of Members it became necessary to reconstitute the Tribunal for hearing this matter though the arguments to be presented in both matters were of a similar nature. I presided over the hearing of the earlier matter N85/197 which we have designated "Peter Briggs (No. 1)".

3. The Tribunal is herein concerned then with 26 documents of which 17 have been released with deletions, and 9 are claimed to be totally exempt from release. Seven Sections of the FOI Act (eight grounds in effect), are relied upon to justify the deletions and refusals to release, namely Sections 36, 37(1) and (2), 38, 40, 41, 42 and 45.

4. The written evidence tendered specifically in this application consisted of the two affidavits of Mr. G. T. Wiggins of 7 and 13 February 1986 and of Mr. B. W. Gallagher of 4 February 1986, and the s.37 (or "T") documents. As Mr. Wiggins' affidavits are replete with references to a further affidavit of his of date 19 September 1985, it was agreed that the Tribunal should treat that lastmentioned affidavit and Mr. Wiggins' oral evidence in chief in cross-examination as given in Briggs (No. 1) (N85/197) as having been received in this application also.

5. Oral evidence was given herein by Mr. Gallagher who is the Principal Advising Officer in the FOI Section of the Respondent's Canberra office, and further oral evidence given by Mr. Wiggins who is an Investigation Officer in the Respondent's Department.

6. Copies of the documents which are in dispute have been handed up in confidence for the Tribunal's perusal. Perusal of them indicates that the descriptions of them seriatim in Mr. Gallagher's affidavit gives a satisfactory picture of them.

7. All the grounds of exemption claimed in this application except those relying on Sections 37(2) and 45, were raised in matter Briggs (No. 1) (N85/197). Having been a Member of the Tribunal in that matter, I consider it necessary in this matter (as to the Sections other than the two lastmentioned), to say merely that I have proceeded to consider the relevant documents in this application in the light of what the Tribunal expressed in the earlier matter to be the appropriate interpretation of the Sections concerned in their application to the facts, which of course are common to the two matters.

8. These relevant facts include the inability of the Respondent to obtain what it regards as accurate information as to the applicant's affairs and specifically sources of income and disposal thereof, despite frequent recourse to process in the attempt to do so; the issuing by the Commissioner of discretionary ("arbitrary") assessments as provided for by the tax legislation; challenges by the applicant to these assessments; the failure of the applicant to submit any returns of income since 1981; the existence of investigations over a considerable number of years which are ongoing, into alleged complicated attempts by the applicant at tax avoidance and evasion on a very large scale.

SUB-SECTION 37(2)(b)

9. Sub-section 37(2)(b) is relied upon in this application in regard to 12 documents. So far as relevant it reads as follows:-

"(2) a document is an exempt document if its disclosure

under this Act would, or could reasonably be

expected to-

(a) . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

(b) disclose lawful methods or procedures for

preventing, detecting, investigating, or

dealing with matters arising out of, breaches

or evasions of the law the disclosure of which

would, or would be reasonably likely to,

prejudice the effectiveness of those methods

or procedures; ..."

10. The remarks of their Honours in the Full Court in the decision in Department of Health v. Jephcott [1985] FCA 370; (1985) 62 ALR 421 in regard to sub-section 37(1)(a) would appear to have equal relevance to the interpretation of sub-section 37(2)(b).

11. It is my opinion that disclosure of the material in the documents listed in Schedule Three would disclose the methods and procedures which have been had recourse to so far by the Respondent in its efforts to deal with what it considers massive tax evasions of a complicated pattern with international ramifications; and that disclosures would be reasonably likely to prejudice the effectiveness of the methods and procedures adopted to deal with what is alleged to be ongoing schemes, by alerting the applicant as to possible alternative means of frustrating investigation of his affairs.

SECTION 45

12. S.45 is relied upon as to 2 documents only, being folio 144 and folios 237-238. The Section provides that:-

"(1) a document is an exempt document if its disclosure

under this Act would constitute a breach of

confidence..."

13. In Re Witheford and Department of Foreign Affairs (1983) 5 ALD 534 at page 542 the Tribunal, presided over by His Honour the President, stated in relation to the Section:-

"This category" (of confidence) "covers documents

disclosure of which would constitute a breach of

confidence where that confidence was reposed and

received not on the faith of a legal duty to respect

that confidence but on the faith of a voluntary

undertaking or understanding to respect it either in a

specific case or as part of a tradition of dealing, and

where it was so reposed and received at a time when

there was an expectation that having once been committed

to writing it would not be subjected to disclosure to

the public or to a specific person."

14. Having examined the contents of the 2 documents in question here in association with each other and having regard to the identity of the informant, and noting that the information relates to banking transactions, I conclude that the information contained in the relevant passages must have been given under at least an implied pledge of confidentiality; and that the Respondent's reliance upon s.45 as to this should be affirmed.

15. I consider that the decision the subject of review should be affirmed to the extents respectively claimed as to the documents in question, in accordance with the grounds and to the extent only, as stated in the Schedules annexed hereto. The references are to the details contained in the Schedule handed up on 17 March 1986.
