Re Alex Lakatos; Ex Parte Alex Lakatos v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1996] FCA 1607 (5 July 1996)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

RE ALEX LAKATOS; EX PARTE ALEX LAKATOS v. DEPUTY

COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. QB 1734 of 1996

FED No. 543/96

Number of pages - 6

Bankruptcy

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

GENERAL DIVISION

BANKRUPTCY DISTRICT OF THE STATE OF QUEENSLAND

COOPER J

CATCHWORDS

Bankruptcy - Review of sequestration order made by Registrar in Bankruptcy - hearing de novo - whether bankrupt "able to pay his debts".

Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth) s 31A(6), 52(2)(a)

Ginnane v Diners Club Ltd [1993] FCA 167; (1993) 42 FCR 90 (FC)

Re Kwiatek and Kwiatek; Ex parte Big J Ltd v Pattison (1989) 21 FCR 314

Re Hunter; Ex parte Webb (unreported, Federal Court of Australia, 26 May
1995, Lee J)

Re Brindle; Ex parte F B and F A McMahon Pty Limited [1992] FCA 203; (1992) 35 FCR 506

Sarina v Council of the Shire of Wollondilly [1980] FCA 138; (1980) 48 FLR 372 (FC)

Re Stirling; Ex parte Webb Ross and Co (1990) 1 NZLR 569

Re Poulson; Ex parte Hempenstall Bros Ltd (No 2) (1929) 1 ABC 54

Re Jones; Ex parte Clutterbuck Bros (Adelaide) Ltd (1930) 2 ABC 89

Re Noye; Ex parte Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1956) 18 ABC 77

Sandell v Porter [1966] HCA 28; (1966) 115 CLR 666

HEARING

BRISBANE, 1 July 1996

5:7:1996

Counsel for the Bankrupt : M Martin

Solicitors for the Bankrupt : Baker Johnson

Counsel for the Respondent : J Logan

Solicitors for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

THE COURT ORDERS THAT:

1. The application filed 20 June 1996 be dismissed.

2. The respondent's costs of and incidental to the application

be costs in the administration of the bankrupt estate.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Rule 124 of the Bankruptcy Rules.

DECISION

COOPER J On 29 May 1996 District Registrar Ramsey, acting as the Registrar in Bankruptcy for the Bankruptcy District of the State of Queensland ("the Registrar"), made a sequestration order against the estate of Alex Lakatos on the petition of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation for the Commonwealth ("the Deputy Commissioner"). By application filed 20 June 1996 Mr Lakatos seeks to have the order made by the Registrar reviewed pursuant to s 31A(6) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth) ("the Act").

2. An application for review pursuant to s 31A(6) of the Act is by way of a hearing de novo on the evidence presented to the court on the hearing of the application (Ginnane v Diners Club Ltd [1993] FCA 167; (1993) 42 FCR 90 at 94 (FC); Re Kwiatek and Kwiatek; Ex parte Big J Ltd v Pattison (1989) 21 FCR 314) and is to be distinguished from an application pursuant to s 14(5), which is a review by the court of an administrative act (Re Hunter; Ex parte Webb, unreported, Federal Court of Australia, 26 May 1995, Lee J cf Re Brindle; Ex parte F B and F A McMahon Pty Limited [1992] FCA 203; (1992) 35 FCR 506).

3. On 27 April 1995 the Deputy Commissioner obtained a final judgment against Mr Lakatos in the Magistrates' Court at Southport in the sum of $6,842.85. A fourteen day bankruptcy notice was issued on 19 June 1995 and was served upon Mr Lakatos by personal service on 5 July 1995.

4. Mr Lakatos did not pay the amount demanded in the bankruptcy notice within fourteen days after service. A creditor's petition was presented on 11 January 1996 and served on 11 April 1996. As noted, the Registrar made a sequestration order against the estate of Mr Lakatos on 29 May 1996.

5. Before me, counsel for Mr Lakatos submits that the order made by the Registrar should be set aside and the bankruptcy annulled on the ground that Mr Lakatos is solvent. An affidavit of Mr Lakatos filed in support of the application deposes as follows:-

"12. My wife and I conduct a motor wrecking business

known as Burleighgardens Motor Wreckers. The business is

operated by my Wife and I as Partners and has approximately

seventy (70) vehicles as wrecking stock together with dismantled

vehicles constituting shelf stock. The business has been in

existence for ten (10) years. I have been unable in the short

amount of time to obtain a formal valuation of the business from

my Accountant. The business trades profitably. Taking into

account the value of the stock and a small amount for goodwill I

would estimate that the business is worth approximately ONE

HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE THOUSAND DOLLARS ($125,000.00).
There

are no securities over the business. In addition to the

business my Wife and I own a property situated at 13 Anne

Street, Southport which property is described as Lot 4 on

Registered Plan Number 160022 County of Ward Parish of Nerang.

13. I recently obtained a valuation from HTW Valuers in respect

of a re-finance. The property is valued at ONE HUNDRED AND

FIFTY-FIVE THOUSAND DOLLARS ($155,000.00).

14. Now produced and shown to me and marked with the letter "C"

is a true copy of the valuation dated 3rd August, 1995.

15. We also own a property situated at 189A Nerang Road,

Southport. This property is more particularly described as Lot

9 on Registered Plan Number 102951 County of Ward Parish of

Nerang and is own (sic) commercial industry. I have to hand a

valuation of the property as at 26th July, 1994 which values the

property at approximately TWO HUNDRED THOUSAND DOLLARS

($200,000.00). Now produced and shown to me and marked with the

letter "D" is a true copy of the valuation from HTW Valuers

dated 26th July, 1994. Due to the shortness of time I have been

unable to obtain more up to date valuations. Please note that

the valuation of the property 189A Nerang Road, Southport

although dated 26th July, 1994 was carried out at about the

same time as the valuation of the Anne Street property. A

typographical error appears on the valuation and it should be

26th July, 1995.

16. Additionally my Wife and I own a Time Share at Tangalooma

Island Resort valued at approximately SEVEN THOUSAND DOLLARS

($7,000.00). We have a small amount of savings in (sic) the

tune of approximately ONE THOUSAND DOLLARS ($1,000.00) and

personal furniture and effects to the value of approximately

FIFTEEN THOUSAND DOLLARS ($15,000.00).

17. Additionally we have two (2) motor vehicles. Both are

unencumbered and have a combined value of approximately EIGHT

THOUSAND DOLLARS ($8,000.00).

18. In summary therefore my Wife and my own assets are as

follows :-

Burleighgardens Motor Wreckers $125,000.00

13 Anne Street, Southport $155,000.00

189A Nerang Road, Southport $200,000.00

Contents of the house $ 15,000.00

TOTAL LIABILITIES $278,759.33

20. In addition to the above my wife and I are owed the sum of

approximately TWENTY THOUSAND DOLLARS ($20,000.00) in the form

of Debtors owed to the wrecking yard business. I would estimate

that the majority of the Debtors are collectable. In the

circumstances a Sequestration Order should not have been made.

.....

22. Alternatively I am prepared to enter into an Agreement to

Wife has informed me and I verily believe that she is prepared

.....

25. My Wife and I have listed the business for sale. It is

AND TWENTY-FIVE THOUSAND DOLLARS ($125,000.00). We have had a

couple of people look at the business and are hopeful of

achieving a sale of the business reasonably quickly."

6. The only other evidence relevant to the question of solvency is a document signed by Mr Lakatos and his wife and dated 11 June 1996 which purports to be a proposal by them to have their affairs dealt with under Part X of the Act. The document was sent to the Australian Tax Office under cover of a letter dated 17 June 1996. The document contains the following:-

"In the pursuance of sec 188 of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 We,

Alexander Lakatos and Maria Lakatos, both motor wrecker

employees, of 13 Anne Street, Southport, in the State of

Queensland, hereby propose that our affairs be dealt with under

part X in the following manner:

1. A deed of arrangement entered into in pursuance of part X.

2. We request that the trustee refrain from selling our home

for 3 months from the date that this proposal is accepted, to

allow us to either refinance, find a buyer, or pay all debts.

At the end of the 3 months we agree to co-operate fully with the

trustee to realise the (sic) any divisible assets.

3. I, Maria Lakatos agree to lend to Alexander Lakatos the

necessary funds to fully pay out all debts of both parties

should his share of the divisible assets be insufficient to

cover his debts.

4. We request that the trustee distribute the proceeds from

our divisible property in accordance with the priorities set out

in Section 109 of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 and that all creditors

accept the distribution from the Trustee in accordance with this

proposal in full and final settlement of their claims."

7. If a debtor is able to pay his debts within the meaning of s 52(2)(a) of the Act the court in bankruptcy will ordinarily dismiss a petition brought against him although s 52(2)(a) is a permissive rather than a mandatory provision (see Sarina v Council of the Shire of Wollondilly [1980] FCA 138; (1980) 48 FLR 372 (FC); Re Stirling; Ex parte Webb Ross and Co (1990) 1 NZLR 569).

8. Mr Lakatos bears the onus of establishing an ability to pay his debts (Re Poulson; Ex parte Hempenstall Bros Ltd (No 2) (1929) 1 ABC 54 at 59 - 60). It is not sufficient for him to show that his assets exceed his liabilities (Re Jones; Ex parte Clutterbuck Bros (Adelaide) Ltd (1930) 2 ABC 89; Re Noye; Ex parte Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1956) 18 ABC 77). The test of ability to pay debts is that stated by Barwick CJ (with whom McTiernan and Windeyer JJ agreed) in Sandell v Porter [1966] HCA 28; (1966) 115 CLR 666 (at 670):-

"... Insolvency is expressed in s 95 as an inability to pay

debts as they fall due out of the debtor's own money. But the

debtor's own moneys are not limited to his cash resources

immediately available. They extend to moneys which he can

procure by realization by sale or by mortgage or pledge of his

assets within a relatively short time - relative to the nature

and amount of the debts and to the circumstances, including the

nature of the business, of the debtor. The conclusion of

insolvency ought to be clear from a consideration of the

debtor's financial position in its entirety and generally

speaking ought not to be drawn simply from evidence of a

temporary lack of liquidity. It is the debtor's inability,

utilizing such cash resources as he has or can command through

the use of his assets, to meet his debts as they fall due which

indicates insolvency."

9. It is not disputed that Mr Lakatos' assets far exceed his liabilities. However, the material does not disclose an ability on his part to pay his debts as they fall due. Mr Lakatos has not deposed to the level of his recurrent expenses, which include repayments of a substantial mortgage debt, nor to any ability to meet them out of recurrent earnings or otherwise. Mr Lakatos does not depose to the trading performance of the business owned by he and his wife, except to say that it "trades profitably". There is no evidence as to the speed with which any of his assets could be realised to meet the debt which founded the petition or any other debt. Similarly, there is no evidence that Mr Lakatos would be able to borrow against his assets or any of them in order to pay his debts.

10. The document signed by Mr Lakatos and his wife on 11 June 1996 shows that, more than one year after the Deputy Commissioner obtained judgment in the Magistrates' Court, Mr Lakatos still required three months to pay the debts owing by him. Further, the document is not expressed in terms which would suggest any confidence that the debt would be paid after that time. Indeed, it raises the prospect that upon sale of his divisible assets Mr Lakatos may still need the assistance of a loan from his wife in order to pay all the debts owing by him.

11. Such evidence as is before me does not establish that Mr Lakatos is solvent. He is not experiencing any temporary lack of liquidity but is unable to pay his debts as they fall due out of his cash resources or out of funds he can procure within a relatively short time by sale or pledge of his assets. I am satisfied that Mr Lakatos is not able to pay his debts within the meaning of s 52(2)(a) of the Act.

12. The Official Trustee in Bankruptcy ("the Official Trustee") is the trustee in bankruptcy of Mr Lakatos' estate. Counsel for Mr Lakatos raises an issue as to the way in which the Official Trustee's costs upon an administration of the estate of Mr Lakatos would be assessed, viz, as a percentage of the value of the assets realised. Counsel seeks to raise this matter as being relevant to the issues on this application. The submission is misconceived. The Official Trustee's statutory entitlement to have his costs of the administration of the bankrupt estate paid, the manner in which those costs are assessed and the level of them are not factors relevant to the issues before me on this application.

13. I am satisfied of the matters in s 52(1) of the Act and that this is an appropriate case for the making of a sequestration order. The application filed 20 June 1996 will be dismissed. The respondent's costs of and incidental to the application will be costs in the administration of the bankrupt estate.

14. THE COURT ORDERS THAT:

1. The application filed 20 June 1996 be dismissed.

2. The respondent's costs of and incidental to the application

be costs in the administration of the bankrupt estate.
