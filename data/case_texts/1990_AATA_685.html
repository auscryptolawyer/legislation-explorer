Burgstett and Secretary, Department of Social Security [1990] AATA 685 (21 September 1990)

Burgstett and Secretary, Department of Social Security [1990] AATA 685 (21 September 1990)

Last Updated: 10 June 2008



SOCIAL SECURITY - Sickness Benefit - paid to deceased husband - application by widow - lump sum compensation - preclusion clause - whether deceased received compensation and Sickness Benefit for same incapacity - decision affirmed



Social Security Act, 1947 ss.115, 115B(3), 115E

Workers Compensation Act 1971-84 ss.51, 59, 67, 70, 72

Social Security and Veterans' Affairs (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act, 1986 s.42



Re Cocks and Secretary to Department of Social Security (1989) 18 ALD 160

Secretary to the Department of Social Security v a'Beckett a decision of Von Doussa J dated 31 August 1990 (G.49 of 1990)

Secretary to the Department of Social Security v Siviero (1986) 68 ALR 147

Re Secretary, Department of Social Security and Cavaleri (1989) 19 ALD 101

Hartwell v Electricity Trust of South Australia (1982) 49 S.A.I.R. 894

REASONS FOR DECISION



21 September 1990 Mr J.A. Kiosoglous (Senior Member)



1. This is an application by Erna Monika Burgstett, as administrator of the estate of Gerhardt Georg Burgstett ('the deceased'), seeking a review of a decision of a delegate of the Secretary to the Department of Social Security dated 13 December 1988 (T59) rejecting a recommendation of the Social Security Appeals Tribunal ('the SSAT') dated 17 October 1988 (T57).



2. The issue in this matter concerns the recovery by the Department of Social Security ('the Department') of an amount of $7,028.80 for Sickness Benefit paid to the deceased during the period 19 April 1985 to 27 February 1986. A delegate determined that the deceased was precluded from receiving benefits because he had received a lump sum compensation payment by way of a consent award. The question to be resolved is whether or not the deceased received compensation for the same incapacity for which he was qualified to receive Sickness Benefit.



3. The Tribunal had before it the documents lodged by the respondent pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act, 1975 ('the T documents') (T1 to T61), together with one exhibit (Exhibit R1). No oral evidence was called by either party. In addition, the Tribunal received submissions from Miss T. Johnson of counsel for the applicant and Ms M. Boylan of counsel for the respondent.



4. Briefly, the facts in this matter are that the deceased was born in Germany on 31 July 1924. He migrated to Australia, arriving on 20 February 1955, and married the applicant on 27 June 1956. On or about 2 February 1984, in the course of his employment with Castalloy Limited of North Plympton ('the employer'), he injured his back and was off work until 26 March 1985. He returned to work for a short time but ceased work officially on 18 April 1985.



5. In the meantime, the deceased lodged a claim for Sickness Benefit with the Department (T3) on 15 April 1985, which was granted on 1 May 1985 (T7) effective from 26 April 1985. Sickness Benefit totalling $11,197.64 was paid to him for the period 26 April 1985 to 20 August 1986. The deceased completed a Compensation and Damages Questionnaire advising the Department of his claim for compensation (T5). The Department forwarded notices to the Royal Insurance Company Ltd (T8), the employer (T9), the deceased (T10) and the solicitors for the deceased (T12), indicating that in the event of compensation being paid to the deceased the Department may seek recovery of the whole or part of the amount of Sickness Benefit paid.



6. The deceased was medically assessed for the Invalid Pension on 22 July 1986 (T26), applied for the Invalid Pension on 7 August 1986 (T28) and was granted that pension on 26 August 1986 (T31). When the deceased was transferred to the Invalid Pension, the Department wrote again to the Royal Insurance Company (T32), the employer (T33), the solicitors for the deceased (T34), and the deceased (T35), advising that in accordance with s.115 of the Social Security Act, 1947 ('the Act') the amount of benefit paid during the period 26 April 1985 to 20 August 1986, namely $11,197.64, was recoverable from the compensation received in respect of the same period.



7. On 10 March 1987 (T37) the solicitors for the deceased wrote to the Department advising that on 16 February 1987 the Industrial Court of South Australia ('the Industrial Court') awarded the deceased lump sum compensation of $39,900 pursuant to s.70 of the Workers Compensation Act 1971-84 ('the Compensation Act'). A copy of the transcript of the proceedings before the Industrial Court showing the judgment was enclosed (T37). In the letter the solicitors enquired whether or not the Department was prepared to waive its claim for reimbursement of Sickness Benefit, as the deceased had not received weekly payments of compensation for the period during which he had received Sickness Benefit.



8. The Minutes of Order of the Industrial Court, dated 16 February 1987 (T37), set out, inter alia, that the sum awarded, namely $39,900, represented an assessment of compensation for the injury pursuant to s.70 of the Compensation Act. A sum of $100 was also awarded representing a redemption of the employer's liability pursuant to s.72 of the Compensation Act to pay the worker's future expenses incurred pursuant to s.59 of the said Compensation Act.



9. The Department then wrote to the solicitors for the deceased on 24 March 1987 (T38) advising that before it could determine what amount, if any, of Sickness Benefit would be refunded to the Department pursuant to s.115 of the Act, it would first need to determine how much of the settlement was for incapacity. In response, the solicitors wrote to the Department on 31 March 1987 (T39) submitting that no amount of the deceased's settlement was paid for incapacity for work, pursuant to either s.70 or s.59 of the Compensation Act. In support of this it was submitted that it was presumed that the compensation payment had been made in the light of the reports from Mr T. Hillier, orthopaedic surgeon, dated 16 July 1985 (T39) and 17 August 1986 (T39) respectively.



10. The Department wrote to the deceased on 22 April 1987 (T41) advising that a delegate of the respondent had decided that, for the purposes of the Act, $34,614.00 may reasonably be regarded as compensation for incapacity resulting from the injury suffered on 2 February 1984 in the course of his employment. In accordance with s.115B(2)(a) of the Act the delegate determined that the $34,614.00 represented compensation at the rate of $437.20, being the current All Male Total Average Weekly Earnings rate, for the period from 19 April 1984 (the day after the date last worked) to 23 October 1986. This period is calculated by dividing the $34,614.00 by the said $437.20. The deceased was advised that as he had been in receipt of Sickness Benefit for this incapacity during the period 26 April 1985 to 20 August 1986 at less than $437.20, a refund of $11,197.64 was due to the Department. A similar letter was sent to the solicitors for the deceased (T43).



11. On 11 June 1987 the solicitors wrote to the SSAT (T45) advising that the deceased had died in Germany on 30 May 1987 and that, prior to his death, he had instructed them to lodge an appeal with the SSAT. The solicitors advised further that an application of appeal to the SSAT (T45) had been lodged on behalf of the estate and instructions to apply for Letters of Administration had been received from the applicant.



12. On 17 July 1987 the Department wrote to the employer's insurer (T46) and the solicitors for the deceased, now acting on behalf of the applicant who was administrator of her husband's estate (T47). Similar questions were asked in both letters, as follows:



'1. On the basis that Gerhard BURGSTETT claimed in his application for compensation, filed in the Industrial Court on 22.09.86, to be totally, or in alternative partially incapacitated for work, could you advise what component of the gross award was for part or future wage loss.



  1. If you consider that no allowance was included for loss of earnings, please advise why not and provide any documentary evidence in support of your opinion.

  2. Could you please forward copies of medical reports used as a basis for settlement of this claim and copies of any evidence (medical or other) that were used to dispute Gerhard BURGSTETT claims for weekly payments of compensation.'

13. Stanley & Partners, the applicant's solicitors, advised the Department on 23 July 1987 (T48) that the terms of the Minutes of Order of the Industrial Court indicated that no portion of the amount awarded could be regarded as payment in respect of an incapacity for work, stating as follows:



'In answer to Question 1 of your letter we say -



The gross award contained no component for past or future wage loss.



In answer to Question 2 we say -



Compensation was paid only for loss of function of the back pursuant to Section 70 of the Workers Compensation Act. The Minutes of Order were drawn up in accordance with the offer which was made and accepted.



The worker is required to elect as to his entitlements pursuant to Section 70 and/or Section 72 of the Workers Compensation Act. For documentary evidence we refer you to the Minutes of Order and to the Workers Compensation Act.



In answer to Question 3 we say -



No medical reports were agreed as the basis of settlement nor do we know which evidence, if any, was used by the employer's solicitors to dispute our client's claim for weekly payments of compensation.'



14. Ross McCarthy & Nosworthy, representing the employer's insurer, wrote to the Department on 29 September 1987 (T49) advising as follows:



'1. The claim was settled pursuant to Section 70 and the amount of $39,900 was paid under that heading. The fact that it was paid pursuant to Section 70 reflects payment for "actual or potential" incapacity for work but no amount was allocated with respect to past or future wage loss as such.



  1. Not applicable.

  2. We enclose such medical reports as were available to us at the time of settlement.'

A number of medical reports, including x-rays, were provided and are included in the T documents (T49).



15. Upon receipt of the information from the applicant's and the employer's solicitors, the Department requested further details in letters dated 22 December 1987 (T50 and T51). It is appropriate to set out these letters in full, together with the responses from the respective solicitors. The Department asked similar questions in the case of questions 1 and 2, but question 3 was different in each case.



'QUESTION 1



(a) Can you please confirm that the settlement sum represented a compromise bearing in mind the workers entitlement under the Workers Compensation Act and the common law potential of the claim.



(b) If you answer (a) in the negative, please state precisely what the settlement did represent.



(c) If your answer to (a) was in the affirmative, can you please confirm that the likely success or otherwise, in the Industrial Court, of his claim for arrears of weekly payments would have been one factor taken into account in reaching the compromise agreement. If not, please clearly explain why.



QUESTION 2



(a) Were any offers of settlement made on behalf of Mr Burgstett?



(b) If so, were these offers, or any of these offers based in whole or in part on the value of the arrears of weekly payments and/or a redemptive amount.



(c) Were any offers of settlement made to Mr Burgstett?



(d) If so, were these offers, or any of these offers based in whole or in part on the value of the arrears of weekly payments and/or a redemptive amount?'



In the case of Ross McCarthy & Nosworthy, for the employer's insurer, Question 3 was as follows:



'(a) Prior to the date of the Consent Agreement, was any estimate obtained of the potential value of a common law claim?



(b) If so, what percentage of the total potential value was in respect of past and future economic loss.'



Whereas in the case of Stanley & Partners, for the applicant, Question 3 was as follows:



'I note that as part of the Consent Agreement Mr Burgstett agreed to discontinue Supreme Court Action No 2659 of 1986.



(a) No doubt a Statement of Claim was filed in the Supreme Court. Would economic loss have been an element of any common law claim pursued by Mr Burgstett? If not please fully explain why.



(b) Prior to the Consent Agreement, did you (or someone on your behalf) estimate the value of the common law potential?



(c) If so, what proportion of the total potential was in respect of part and future economic loss?'



16. In response Stanley & Partners wrote to the Department on 5 January 1988 (T52) as follows:



'1. The settlement sum represented a compromise, bearing in mind the risk of litigation. The terms of the settlement are set out in the Minutes of Order, a copy of which is enclosed.



  1. (a) Yes.

(b) No.



(c) Yes.



(d) No.



  1. (a) In our view Mr Burgstett would not have recovered compensation or damages for economic loss beyond April 1985 being the date which he had resigned from employment.

(b) Yes.



(c) ...'



17. The insurer's solicitors, Ross McCarthy & Nosworthy, wrote to the Department on 1 July 1988 (T53) advising as follows:



'1. (a) Yes, it did represent such a compromise.



(b) Not applicable.

(c) Yes it was one of the factors taken into account in reaching the agreement.



  1. (a) $80,000.00 plus costs and outstanding medical expenses in settlement of all claims.

(b) I am unable to say.



(c) We offered to settle for $25,000.00 plus costs and outstanding medical expenses and after a further counter proposal made by the plaintiff to settle for $50,000.00 in addition to those same items, the matter was finally resolved for $40,000.00 in addition to those items.



(d) All of the offers made on behalf of the defendant took those items into account.



  1. (a) Advice was given indicating that the potential of the claim at common law was in the order of $50,000.00. This took into account a minimal allowance for loss of earning capacity owing to the age of the plaintiff.

(b) No specific breakdown appears in the file but it probably would be fair to apportion the $50,000.00 equally between pain and suffering and allowances made for past and future loss of earning capacity.'



18. On 17 October 1988 the SSAT found that there was clearly no capacity in law for the Department to claim weekly payments after April 1985 and that because of the time at which the consent agreement was entered into the Department could only look to any incapacity component in the lump sum award that related to the specific period for which Sickness Benefit was paid. The SSAT supported the application on this basis and recommended to the Department that no claim could be made for repayment of Sickness Benefit (T57). A delegate of the respondent rejected the SSAT's recommendation (T59) and affirmed the original decision on 28 July 1988 for recovery of the amount of $7,028.80 from the deceased's estate (T56). The Department wrote to Stanley & Partners on 16 December 1988 (T60) affirming the delegate's decision and advising that the amount recoverable had been reduced from $11,197.64 to $7,028.80. The Department advised also that there were no outstanding factors which would justify the exercising of the discretion, contained in s.115E of the Act, to disregard any part of the incapacity component of the compensation award. On 22 December 1988 (T61) the Department wrote to Stanley & Partners requesting immediate payment of the amount of $7,028.80. Through her solicitors, the applicant lodged an appeal to this Tribunal against the decision of the delegate (T1).



19. In making his decision, the delegate declined (T59) to accept the recommendation made by the SSAT on 17 October 1988 that the applicant's appeal should be upheld (T57). The SSAT was of the view that because of the time at which the consent agreement was entered into, the Department could look only to any incapacity component in the lump sum award that related to the specific period for which Sickness Benefit was paid. In support of this the SSAT cited Secretary to Department of Social Security v Siviero (1986) 68 ALR 147. The SSAT was of the view that there was no capacity in law to claim weekly payments after April 1985 and hence the Department was not able to recover the amount of Sickness Benefit paid.



20. The relevant legislation to be considered in this matter is sub-s.115B(3) of the Act, which provides as follows:



'115B. (3) Where the Director-General is of the opinion that a payment by way of a lump sum, or a series of periodical payments, received by a person who is, or has been, in receipt of sickness benefit in respect of an incapacity (whether the payment was, or all or any of the payments were, received before, during or after the close of the period of receipt of sickness benefit) is a payment that is, or are payments one or more of which is or are, in whole or in part, a payment or payments by way of compensation in respect of that incapacity, the Director-General may, by notice in writing served by post or personally on the person, direct the person to pay to the Commonwealth an amount specified in the notice, being an amount equal to -



(a) the amount of sickness benefit received by the person in respect of that incapacity; or



(b) the amount of the lump sum payment or periodical payment or payments or such part of that amount or of those amounts as, in the opinion of the Director-General, relates to that incapacity,



whichever is the lesser amount.'



The whole of Division 3A of Part VII of the Act was repealed on 1 May 1987 by virtue of the provisions of s.42 of the Social Security and Veterans' Affairs (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act, 1986, No.106 of 1986. However, notwithstanding this amendment, in relation to Sickness Benefit which commenced to be paid before 1 May 1987 that Division continued to apply.



21. In her submissions, Miss Johnson stated that the SSAT found favourably for the deceased because of his voluntary retirement on 18 April 1985 and that the compensation payment, subsequently settled on 16 February 1987, was not in respect of incapacity for the period after that date. She submitted further that the compensation payment of $39,900 was a s.70 payment and the $100 for future medical expenses related solely to the deceased's disability or physical injury and not incapacity for work.



22. Miss Johnson contended that the Department had concluded that the payment included, at least partially, an element of incapacity for work but she argued that, although there may be such an element to some s.70 awards, this could not be the case here as the deceased could not have recovered compensation for economic loss due to his voluntary resignation in April 1985. She argued further that this course of action precluded him from obtaining weekly payments pursuant to s.67 of the Compensation Act. It was stated also that he could not treat his incapacity as total incapacity and gain weekly payments pursuant to s.51 of the Compensation Act because he did not make himself available for work due to the fact that he had resigned.



23. Ms Boylan of counsel for the respondent stated that the issues before the Tribunal are firstly, whether or not the deceased received a payment by way of compensation for an incapacity within the provisions of Divison 3A and hence whether the Department is entitled to recover Sickness Benefit pursuant to that Division; and secondly, whether the method adopted by the Department to calculate the amount recoverable was, in all the circumstances, fair and reasonable.



24. With regard to the first point, Ms Boylan submitted that part of the total settlement sum of $40,000 was payment for incapacity being the same incapacity for which Sickness Benefit was paid. This was not disputed and the Tribunal accepts this and so finds. She submitted further that the delegate of the respondent was required to form an opinion pursuant to s.115B of the Act, as it then was, as to whether any part of the compensation award was payment for incapacity and to do so the delegate was entitled to look not only at all the available evidence but also to go behind the consent order to identify the basis upon which compensation was paid (Re Cocks and Secretary to Department of Social Security (1989) 18 ALD 160.



25. The Tribunal, in arriving at its conclusion, has considered the evidence as a whole, including the submissions of counsel. Furthermore, consideration has been given to a number of cases of the Federal Court, the Administrative Appeals Tribunal and of the Industrial Court of South Australia including the following: Secretary to the Department of Social Security v a'Beckett a decision of Von Doussa J dated 31 August 1990 (G.49 of 1990); Secretary to the Department of Social Security v Siviero (supra); Re Cocks (supra); Re Secretary, Department of Social Security and Cavaleri (1989) 19 ALD 101; Hartwell v Electricity Trust of South Australia (1982) 49 S.A.I.R. 894; Salmon Street Ltd (In Liquidation) v Douglas a decision of the Full Industrial Court of South Australia dated 16 October 1987 (W41/1987); Meyer v The Whyalla Shipbuilding and Engineering Works a decision of the Industrial Court of South Australia dated 19 February 1988 (W12/1988); Batty v K.F.B. Shipbuilding Pty Ltd a decision dated 12 September 1986 (W61/1986); and F.C.N. Transport Pty Ltd v Culhane a decision dated 17 April 1986

(W32/1986).



26. The Act requires that the applicant establish that the lump sum settlement is, in whole or in part, a payment by way of compensation in respect of that incapacity. In the event of there being such a component present, or if it can be ascertained that such exists, then a process of calculation takes place to determine the period during which Sickness Benefit was payable and hence recoverable by the respondent under the Act.



27. In so doing the Tribunal needs to be satisfied of the following:



(a) that the deceased was qualified to receive a pension at all relevant times. It was not disputed, and the Tribunal so finds, that at all relevant times the deceased was qualified to receive Sickness Benefit;



(b) that the deceased received a lump sum payment by way of compensation, whether before or after becoming so qualified. The Tribunal is satisfied and so finds that this requirement has been met;



(c) whether the amount in question is a 'lump sum payment by way of compensation'. The Tribunal is satisfied and so finds that the payment in question was a payment in settlement of a claim for damages and, that being so, it was a payment by way of compensation;



(d) whether it was a 'lump sum'. In this regard the Tribunal adopts the meaning given to these words in Re Cavaleri (supra) namely, that 'lump sum' is to be defined as 'including a number of items taken together or in the lump'. The Tribunal is satisfied and so finds that this requirement has been met; and



(e) whether it is 'in whole or in part in respect of an incapacity for work'. This issue was in dispute. The Tribunal is satisfied and so finds, in accordance with the findings in Re Cocks (supra), that part of the settlement claim was in respect of an incapacity for work.



28. The Tribunal, having satisfied itself that the settlement payment complies with the provisions of the Act, finds that the delegate of the respondent was correct in regarding part of the settlement as being payment by way of compensation for incapacity. Notwithstanding this, the Tribunal is further satisfied, and finds on the evidence as a whole, that the deceased consented to the terms of the Order accepted by the Industrial Court.



29. With regard to the second point submitted by Ms Boylan, as to whether or not the method adopted by the Department in calculating the amount recoverable was in all the circumstances fair and reasonable, the Tribunal takes into account that the delegate considered several methods of calculation and that the method chosen produced the lowest figure. In effect, half of the award was regarded as payment for incapacity. The Tribunal accepts and so finds that the method adopted by the delegate was fair and reasonable.



30. The Tribunal, for the reasons outlined, is satisfied that the decision of the delegate dated 13 December 1988 (T59) is correct and that the amount of $7,028.80 paid by way of Sickness Benefit is recoverable. Accordingly, the decision under review is affirmed.
