Re Black and Decker (Australasia) Pty Ltd v the Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1989] FCA 386; 89 Atc 5012 20 Atr 1268 (22 September 1989)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: BLACK AND DECKER (AUSTRALASIA) PTY LTD

And: THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

Nos. V G130 and 164 of 1988

FED No. 573

Sales Tax

89 ATC 5012

20 ATR 1268

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Northrop J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Sales Tax - whether returns made by taxpayer disclosed sale value of goods sold - whether deduction from those values of credits for goods previously sold and returned as defective a non-compliance with sales tax legislation - nature of a credit for tax paid - power of Commissioner to make an assessment - nature of an assessment - distinction between an assessment and a refund decision - nature of a review of an assessment - liability for additional tax.

Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) 1930 sections 3, 11, 17, 18, 19, 21, 24, 25, 26, 29, 30, 39A, 40, 41, 45, 47, 73

Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 6) 1930 sections 3, 10

Sales Tax (Exemptions and Classifications) Act 1935 s.5, Item 144 of the First Schedule

Sales Tax Regulations Reg. 48(1)(m)

Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation v. Ellis & Clark Ltd. [1934] HCA 54; 1934 52 CLR 85

HEARING

MELBOURNE

22:9:1989

Counsel for Applicant: Mr Archibald Q.C. with Mr H. Reicher

Solicitors for Applicant: Blake Dawson Waldron

Counsel for Respondent: Mr Davies

Solicitor for Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The application be dismissed with costs.

Note: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order 36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

These two proceedings are references to the Court under s.41 of the Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) 1930 ("Act No. 1") of decisions made by the Commissioner disallowing objections to assessments. Each proceeding relates to an objection lodged by the applicant as the taxpayer against assessments of sales tax for goods sold during the period 1 June 1985 to 31 August 1986. In the first proceeding, the objection was against an assessment of sales tax pursuant to sub-section 25(2A) of the Act No. 1. In the second proceeding, the objection was against an assessment of sales tax pursuant to sub-section 10(2A) of the Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 6) 1930 ("Act No. 6"). The two proceedings were heard together. The determination of the second proceeding, in reality, depends upon the determination of the first. Accordingly, consideration is directed principally to the first proceeding.

2. Act No. 1 relates to the imposition of tax upon the sale value of goods manufactured in Australia. Act No. 6 relates to the imposition of tax upon the sale value of goods imported into Australia and sold by the importer. The taxpayer is the manufacturer in Australia of a wide range of goods including electrical goods for domestic use. In addition it imports goods of a similar nature for domestic use within Australia. The taxpayer sells the goods by wholesale to various retailers who sell to the public. On occasion defects exist or develop in the goods purchased by members of the public. To meet the problem so occasioned, the taxpayer entered into an arrangement with its retailer customers. The arrangement was described as the rapid exchange or RX program. The essential feature of the RX program was that if the member of the public who purchased from the retailer the goods which were or became defective, hereinafter called the consumer, returned the goods to the retailer within the warranty period of 1 year, the retailer would supply replacement goods from its stock at no cost to the consumer. As a variation, the consumer could return the defective goods to a service centre conducted by the taxpayer and receive a replacement. There were a number of variations to this general arrangement, but they need not be considered in detail. One variation was where the retailer did not have in stock replacement goods or the goods were no longer available. Under the RX program the retailer returned the defective goods to the taxpayer which gave a credit for that item and forwarded a replacement to the retailer and charged the retailer for the cost of the replacement. Again there were some variations to this but they need not be considered here.

3. The RX program was implemented pursuant to an arrangement between the taxpayer and its retailers. The terms of the arrangement were set out in a brochure given to the retailers as well as in a letter. The terms of the arrangement were changed slightly by a later brochure. For example, under the second version, if a particular item was no longer available, a substitute item would be provided by the taxpayer. It is not necessary to consider the details of the documents setting out the arrangements. It should be noted that the same program applied irrespective of whether the goods were manufactured by the taxpayer or were imported. In the latter event, Act No. 6 applied instead of Act No. 1. Otherwise the factual position and the legal position were identical.

4. The broad basis of the program, as expressed by the documentation, can be seen from the summary set out in an affidavit filed on behalf of the taxpayer:-

"(a) The retailer sells an RX tool to the consumer

accompanied by an RX guarantee card.

(b) The consumer returns the defective RX tool to

the retailer from whom it was purchased and is

provided either with an exchange tool of the

same specification as the defective tool (on

which sales tax has been paid), or another of

the Applicant's (taxpayer) products, or a claim

check or cash refund.

(c) The retailer returns the defective tool to the

Applicant (taxpayer) with a claim for credit in

respect of that tool.

(d) If the claim for credit is verified by the

Applicant (taxpayer) the retailer is issued a

credit note either for the amount of the

verified claim or for an amount representing

the Applicant's (taxpayer's) estimated sale

price of the defective tool.

(e) The Applicant (taxpayer) either discards or

repairs the defective tool. If repaired, the

tool is resold from one of the Applicant's

(taxpayer's) retail and service outlets as

re-built goods."

5. Under the sales tax legislation, the taxpayer was required to pay sales tax on the value of goods it sold to retailers for sale by retail. In conformity with the legislation, the taxpayer paid sales tax on goods so sold. When a retailer in conformity with the RX program returned goods to the taxpayer together with a claim form, the claim was checked by an officer of the taxpayer who verified the amount of the claim, or if no amount was specified, determined the price based on the basis that the sale had taken place some three months earlier. The officer then issued a credit note to the retailer which set out the price of the goods and the amount of sales tax paid. In practice, the amount of sales tax did not always coincide with the amount actually paid. This resulted from a change made in the rate of tax during the relevant period as well as from the method by which the amount of sales tax was calculated by the officer. It would have been too time-consuming and expensive for the officer to check the actual price and tax paid with respect to each item returned against the accounts sent to the retailer so the "broad brush" approach was adopted. The taxpayer, normally, then forwarded the replacement item to the retailer and charged the retailer for that item. Normally the taxpayer would have paid sales tax on the value of the replacement item. It follows therefore, that the taxpayer would have paid sales tax on the value of the goods returned when first sold to a retailer and also paid sales tax on the value of the replacement goods sold to the retailer. It was this fact which gives rise to the central issue in this case, since the taxpayer took the view that it was required to pay sales tax once only and not on goods returned. It adopted an accounting procedure by which it deducted from the total values of sales for any one month, the total of the values of the returned goods for that month without making a disclosure of this to the Commissioner. The result was that during the period in dispute, the sales tax paid by the taxpayer was less than the total of the sales tax liable to be paid on the value of goods sold to retailers during the period. A matter of principle is raised by these references. It is not necessary to consider the details of the amount of tax that should have been paid. Before considering further the relevant facts, reference is made to the provisions of the sales tax legislation.

6. At first impression, the sales tax legislation appears to be extremely complicated and confusing. In reality it is fairly simple but its application can give rise to difficulties. In considering the legislation it is important to remember that the imposition of tax is based on monthly sales and is imposed upon persons who must be registered under the legislation. A registered person is required to keep proper books of account, to render true statements of all sales made and pay all sales tax required to be paid. The nature of the sales tax legislation was described in 1934 by Dixon J. in a passage which has equal application today. In Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Ellis & Clark Ltd. [1934] HCA 54; (1934) 52 CLR 85 commencing at p 89 his Honour discussed at some length the nature of the sales tax legislation. A part of that passage is set out:-

7. Because of some apprehension as to the possible effect of sec. 55 of the Constitution on its validity, if the sales tax legislation were enacted in one assessment Act and one taxing Act, it was passed in the form of nine separate machinery statutes and nine separate taxing Acts. They constitute, however, a single legislative scheme to the complete operation of which all are necessary, and they should be construed together. Moreover, the legislation depends in a remarkable degree upon the regulations made under the power which it confers on the Executive. Without the regulations, not only is it unworkable, but the expression of legislative policy is so inadequate as almost to be unintelligible. Although the tax levied by the enactments is called a sales tax, it is not a tax upon all sales of commodities. It is a tax levied upon one only of the transactions which commonly take place in respect of goods before they reach the consumer after they are imported into or produced in Australia. It appears that it was not intended that the retail price of goods should be increased by the incorporation in it of more than one amount of tax. The general policy of the legislation is to levy this tax upon the last sale of the goods by wholesale, that is upon the sale to the retailer by the last wholesaler. To give effect to this policy, every person who engages, whether exclusively or not, in the sale of goods by wholesale is required to register. Upon registration, he becomes bound to keep proper books, make returns of his sales, and pay the tax. He receives a certificate bearing a number and this certificate he is bound to quote when he buys goods, unless, besides being a wholesale merchant, he is a retailer and he sells principally by retail. A sale to a person who quotes his certificate is not the subject of tax, and thus, if there be successive sales to wholesalers, the goods do not incur tax until the last wholesaler sells them to the retailer. Then an ad valorem duty is imposed on that sale, which presumably was considered likely to be at a price higher than the preceding sales. It was not the object of the legislation to levy the tax on sales by retail.

8. The taxpayer is a registered manufacturer under Act No. 1 and thus is required to comply with the requests of the sales tax legislation. Sub-section 17(1) imposes a sales tax as follows:-

"17(1) Subject to, and in accordance with, the

provisions of this Act, the sales tax imposed by the

Sales Tax Act (No. 1) 1930 shall be levied and paid

upon the sale value of goods manufactured in

Australia by a taxpayer and sold by him or treated

by him as stock for sale by retail or applied to his

own use."

9. A similar provision is contained in s.3 of the Act No. 6 with respect to goods imported into Australia by a taxpayer.

10. Section 18 provides for the ascertainment of the sale value of goods. For the purposes of these proceedings, the relevant parts of that section are set out:-

"18(1) ... where goods ... have been sold by the

manufacturer to an unregistered person or to a

registered person who has not quoted his certificate

in respect of the sale, the sale value of the goods,

for the purposes of this Act, is:-

(a) if the goods were sold by wholesale - the

amount for which the goods were sold; or

(b) ..."

11. For the purpose of these proceedings, the taxpayer, as manufacturer, sold the goods by wholesale to retailers being, for this purpose, not registered under Act No. 1.

12. Section 19 imposes a liability for tax and which, for present purposes, provides:-

"19. Sales tax shall be paid by the manufacturer

of goods manufactured in Australia and:-

(a) sold by the manufacturer to an unregistered

person ...".

13. Section 21 of Act No. 1 imposes an obligation on manufacturers to furnish returns of sales. The part of that section relevant to these proceedings is set out:-

"21. Every manufacturer who, during any month-

(a) makes any of the sales specified in s.18

... shall, within 21 days after the close

of that month, furnish to the Commissioner

a return of those sales ... in a form

approved by the Commissioner containing

such information as the form requires and

such other information as is required."

14. Part VI of the Act contains provisions for the collection and recovery of tax. The relevant parts of these provisions are set out:-

"24(1) A person liable under this Act to pay sales

tax upon the sale value of any goods:-

(a) sold by him;

(b) ...

during a month shall, within 21 days after the close

of that month, pay sales tax upon the sale value of

the goods.

(2) Subject to sections 27 and 28, sales tax is

due and payable at the end of the period of 21 days

referred to in sub-section (1) of this section.

25(1) Where the Commissioner finds in any case

that tax or further tax is payable by a person, the

Commissioner may make an assessment in relation to

the person."

15. At this point, it should be noted that in Act No. 1 the word "assessment" is defined in s.3 to mean:-

"(a) the ascertainment of the sale value of

goods and of the sales tax payable on

that sale value; or

(b) the ascertainment of additional tax

payable under Part VIII;"

16. Section 25(2A) provides:-

"(2A) Where-

(a) a person makes default in furnishing a

return;

(b) the Commissioner is not satisfied with a

return furnished by a person; or

(c) ...

the Commissioner may determine an amount to be the

amount upon which, in the opinion of the

Commissioner, sales tax should be paid and may make

an assessment in relation to the person."

17. A number of procedural matters are contained in Part VI but they need not be referred to. Provision is made for refund of tax paid, for example, where bad debts have been written off in circumstances where sales tax had been paid on the sale value of those goods. Section 29 contains provisions with respect to additional tax. Under s.30, tax is deemed to be a debt due to the Commonwealth and payable to the Commissioner when it becomes due and payable. The other provisions of Part VI are not relevant to these proceedings.

18. Part VII of Act No. 1 relates to objections. In substance a person may make an objection to an assessment and the Commissioner is required to consider the objection and may either disallow it or allow it wholly or in part. He must give notice of his decision and the objector may request that the decision be referred to the Federal Court; see generally s.40 and s.41. On a reference, the objector is limited to the grounds stated in the objection and the burden of proving that an assessment is excessive lies on the objector; see s.42E.

19. Part VIII of Act No. 1 relates to penalty tax. Under sub-section 45(2), a taxpayer may be made liable to pay additional tax. That sub-section is set out:-

"45.(1) ...

(2) Where-

(a) a taxpayer-

(i) makes a statement to a taxation

officer, or to a person other than a

taxation officer for a purpose in

connection with the operation of a

relevant sales tax law, that is false

or misleading in a material

particular; or

(ii) omits from a statement made to a

taxation officer, or to a person

other than a taxation officer for a

purpose in connection with the

operation of a relevant sales tax

law, any matter or thing without

which the statement is misleading in

a material particular; and

(b) the tax properly payable by the taxpayer

exceeds the tax that would have been

payable by the taxpayer if it were assessed

or determined on the basis that the

statement were not false or misleading, as

the case may be,

the taxpayer is liable to pay, by way of penalty,

additional tax equal to double the amount of the excess."

20. Under s.47, the Commissioner has the power to remit the whole or part of additional tax.

21. Pursuant to directions given by the Court, the Commissioner gave the legal and factual grounds of the assessment objected to by the taxpayer as follows:-

"1. The assessment was made under sub-section

25(2A) Sales Tax Assessment Act (No. 1) 1930

("Act No. 1").

2. The Commissioner was not satisfied with the

returns made by the Applicant for the period

1st June, 1985 to 31st August 1986 and had

reason to believe or suspect that the Applicant

was liable to pay sales tax in respect of that

period.

3. In each of the said returns, the Applicant

failed to set forth the total value of taxable

goods sold by it during the month of the return

and the total taxable sale value of such goods.

4. In making each of the said returns, the

Applicant wrongly deducted from each of the

total value of taxable goods sold by it and the

total taxable sale value of such goods, an

amount equal to the whole or part of credits

given to its retail customers for goods

returned to it under a customer warranty

programme known as the RX Programme.

5. There is no provision in the sales tax

legislation which authorises or entitles a

taxpayer in calculating the sale value of goods

manufactured and sold within the meaning of

section 17 of Act No. 1, to make a deduction in

respect of credits given for returned goods as

set out in paragraph 4 hereof.

6. In each of the said returns, the Applicant

omitted to set forth a statement that in

calculating the total value of taxable goods

sold and the total sale value of such goods, it

made a deduction in respect of credits given

for returned goods as set out in paragraph 4

hereof.

7. By reason of paragraphs 3-6 hereof, in each of

the said returns the Applicant made statements

which were false or misleading in a material

particular and omitted matters or things

without which statements made in each return

were misleading in a material particular within

the meaning of paragraph 45(2)(a) of Act No.

1."

22. The Commissioner then gave particulars of the assessment. The amount of the assessment, apart from the additional tax, was not raised before the Court. The issue of liability was raised and on the determination of that issue, the amount of tax can be determined by the parties. It is sufficient to state that the assessment was for the sum of $132,533.25 together with additional tax in the sum of $16,982.54. In essence, the assessment was based on the sales tax components of the credits given by the taxpayer to its retailers under the terms of the RX program during the period 1 June 1985 to 31 August 1986.

23. Counsel for the Commissioner contended that there were three main issues raised by these references. First, whether, for each of the relevant months, the taxpayer filed incorrect returns. It was contended that it had filed incorrect returns in two respects namely (a) the taxpayer understated the total taxable sales made by it during the relevant month and thus understated the total sale value of taxable goods sold during that month and the tax payable thereon; and (b) the taxpayer failed to state in its returns that it had reduced the total taxable sales made in each of the months by amounts equal to the credits given for goods returned which had been sold in previous months.

24. The second issue related to the question of jurisdiction. To some extent, this raised the real issue between the parties. The Commissioner contended, in reality, that the taxpayer was seeking a refund of tax paid, the refund being sought under s.26 of Act No. 1, or reg. 48(1)(m) of the Sales Tax Regulations, or item 144 of the First Schedule of the Sales Tax (Exemptions and Classifications) Act 1935 ("the Exemptions and Classifications Act") with respect to the tax paid on the sale value of the goods sold and which were returned under the RX program and replaced by the replacement goods. The Commissioner contended that this question was not raised by the objection and so could not be raised in these references.

25. The third issue raised was that if the Court had jurisdiction to determine the matters raised by the second issue, then neither s.26 of Act No. 1, reg. 48(1)(m) nor item 144 permitted a refund of sales tax paid with respect to goods returned under the RX program.

26. The taxpayer contended there were four main issues before the Court. The first issue was concerned, as counsel said, "with the contractual process of adjustment to the provisional price established in respect of the sales of goods" by the taxpayer to its retailers which goods were replaced by the taxpayer under the RX program.

The second issue related to the construction and

application of reg. 48(1)(m). The third issue related to

the construction and application of item 144 in the First

Schedule of the Exemptions and Classifications Act ("item

144"). The fourth issue related to the assessment of the

penalty tax.

27. From this summary, it is apparent that counsel for the taxpayer contended that any refund under s.26 of Act No. 1 formed part of the assessment which was the subject of the objection and thus should be considered under the first issue as stated by counsel for the Commissioner. They contended that the sale value of goods sold subject to the RX program was not the true sale value of those goods but was subject to variation if the goods were returned and a credit given.

28. There is no real dispute on the facts with respect to the issues raised but there is strong dispute as to the results flowing from those facts. It must be remembered that as a manufacturer registered under Act No. 1 to whom a certificate had been issued, the taxpayer is required by sub-section 11(6) to keep proper books or accounts for the purposes of the Act, to render true statements of all sales made as and when required by the Commissioner and to pay all tax required by or under the Act to be paid by him. The Commissioner has power to inspect all these records but normally a taxpayer, in making returns under the Act, shows final figures only without details as to how those amounts are determined. The tax is calculated by applying the rate determined by the relevant sales tax legislation to the sale value of goods sold, for present purposes, to retailers during a month. This follows from the combined effect of sections 17, 18 and 19 of Act No. 1.

29. Under s.21 of Act No. 1, the taxpayer was required to furnish to the Commissioner a return of sales for each month "in a form approved by the Commissioner containing such information as the form requires". The taxpayer furnished returns on such a form for each of the relevant months but in so doing did not disclose that the amounts stated for the sale value of goods upon which tax was payable had in fact been reduced by the amounts of credits given for returned goods. The approved form contains 10 columns. Columns 1 to 5 inclusive are listed under the general heading "Part A - Details of Taxable Goods". There is set out in column 1 a description of goods according to groups corresponding to the rate of tax to be paid on the various groups. The next four columns are under the general sub-heading "Sale value (Exclusive of Tax) upon which tax is payable". It will be remembered that the sale value of the goods is the amount by which the goods were sold to the retailers; see sub-section 18(1). Under the general sub-heading, column 2 is headed "Goods Sold". Prima facie the taxpayer should have set out the totals of the sale values of goods sold by the taxpayer to the retailers during each relevant month. In fact, the amounts set out were the balance of the sale value of those goods after deducting the value of the goods credited to retailers under credit notes raised by the taxpayer under the RX program. This fact was not disclosed by the amounts appearing on the return and would not be apparent to the Commissioner. Columns 3, 4 and 5 are not relevant for the purposes of this case. They make provision for the setting out of the value of goods liable to tax under other heads of liability. The taxpayer had no such liability and so these columns did not need to be filled in.

30. Columns 6 to 10 inclusive are listed under the general heading "Part B - Calculation of Tax". Column 6 is headed "Total taxable value at each respective rate of tax (total of amounts in columns (2) to (5) above)". In the present case, column 6 contains the same amounts as are set out in column 2. These amounts set out the sale value of the goods sold in each respective month. These amounts suffer the same defects as those set out in column 2 in that they do not state the total amount of the goods sold. Column 7 sets out the rate of tax for each group of goods while column 8 contains the gross amount of tax payable with respect to each group. Column 9 is headed "Less tax credits (See Note 9)". This column is designed to allow a taxpayer to make claims to avoid the payment of excessive or double tax on the sale of goods, for example where discounts are given on bulk sales or early payment. These matters will be considered in greater detail later in these reasons, but Note 9 is set out:-

"(9) Tax Credits - This column should contain the

amounts of sales tax involved at each rate class

in the following deductions:

(a) Discounts Allowed and Returns Inwards -

Credits should be claimed in the return for

the month in which they are actually allowed,

i.e. the month in which the purchaser pays

his account or returns the goods. Such

deductions apply only to transactions which

have been subject to tax. Discounts allowed

on retail sales where tax is payable on an

amount less than the retail sale price are

not deductible.

(b) Bad Debts - The amount on which tax may be

claimed is that part of the taxable sale

value which bears the same proportion to the

amount written off as the taxable sale value

bears to the actual sale price. For example,

if goods were sold for $200 and tax paid on a

sale value of $160 only, and if $100 is

written off as a bad debt, the deduction

allowable would be the tax paid on $80 only.

N.B. Tax is payable on any amount recovered

subsequently to write-off.

(c) Other Credits - For example, in respect of:

(i) the quotation of a certificate or the

furnishing of a conditional exemption

certificate by the purchaser after tax

has been paid; or

(ii) tax paid on importation or at time of

purchase of goods sold in non-taxable or

conditionally exempt circumstances.

N.B. Where the rate of tax has changed, the

amount of tax claimed as a credit is to be

calculated at the rate at which tax was paid

on the original transaction."

The returns made by the taxpayer do contain claims under column 9 but none of the claims relate to the RX program. Column 10 sets out the net tax payable being the gross amount of tax less tax credits. The taxpayer paid the amounts of tax set out in column 10 to the Commissioner.

31. At no stage has the taxpayer made a formal claim to the Commissioner for credits for tax paid on the sale value of goods sold by it to retailers, returned to it under the RX program and with respect to which a credit was given to the retailer. The taxpayer deducted the amount of credits given each month from the total sale value of goods sold during that month. It justified this course on the basis that the sale value of goods subject to the RX program sold during any one month was provisional only and could be deducted from the sale value of goods sold during a subsequent month. This argument is rejected.

32. The provisions of Act No. 1 are explicit and clear. By the combined operation of sections 17, 18 and 19 of Act No. 1 a liability was imposed on the taxpayer to pay tax on the amount for which the goods were sold to the retailers. Under s.21, the taxpayer was required to make a return of those sales in the approved form. Under s.24, the taxpayer was required to pay sales tax "upon the sale value of any goods sold by him" during each month. The liability to pay and the payment of tax depends upon the sale to the retailer. Once that sale is completed, the sale value of the goods sold is determined and liability is imposed. The sale value of goods so returned cannot be used to manipulate the sale value of goods subsequently sold. The tax legislation provides a method whereby credits against tax may be given to relieve a taxpayer from paying double tax. In reality, the application of these provisions gives rise to the real issue between the parties. Before turning to consider these provisions, it must be noted that the Commissioner disputes the fact that they can be raised, but in order to determine that question, reference should be made to the relevant provisions.

33. Section 26 of Act No. 1 provides for the refund or giving of credit for tax overpaid. Sub-section 26(1) is set out:-

"26.(1) Subject to sub-section (1A), where the

Commissioner finds in any case that tax has been

overpaid by a person, the Commissioner shall-

(a) refund the amount of any tax overpaid; or

(b) apply the amount of any tax overpaid

against any liability of the person to the

Commonwealth, being a liability arising

under, or by virtue of, an Act of which the

Commissioner has the general

administration, and refund any part of the

amount that is not so applied."

34. Other sub-sections of s.26 contain specific provisions relating to refunds which have no application to the facts of this case. The relationship between s.26 and Column 9 of the authorised form of return is apparent.

35. In the present case, the taxpayer, on this part of the case, relied upon reg. 48(1)(a) and item 144. Under s.73 of Act No. 1 the Governor-General is empowered to make regulations, among other purposes:-

"(a) for prescribing cases in which, and the extent

to which in those cases, refunds or payments

may be made for the purpose of relieving a

taxpayer from double taxation (whether direct,

or direct and indirect)-

(i) in respect of any goods where sales tax

is imposed upon a sale value of those

goods under one or more Acts relating to

the payment of sales tax; or

(ii) under one or more Acts relating to the

payment of sales tax, in respect of any

goods and in respect of any materials

used in, wrought into or attached to

those goods in a process of manufacture

or in any other process;"

36. Regulation 48 was made for that purpose. Reg. 48(1)(a) is as follows:-

"48(1) Subject to this Part, refunds or payments

to prevent double taxation, as the case may be, may

be made to a person-

(a) ...

(m) who has paid tax upon a sale value of any

goods and, after the occurrence of the act,

operation or transaction in respect of which

the tax was paid, has, for the purpose of

replacing free of charge the whole or any

part of those goods because of defects in

them, used other goods-

(i) in respect of a sale value of which he

has paid, or becomes liable to pay, tax;

or

(ii) which he has purchased at a price which

includes tax paid by some other person

in respect of a sale value of those

other goods,

and who has satisfied the Commissioner that

the goods so replaced will not subsequently

be used for the purpose, or for a purpose

similar to the purpose, for which they were

manufactured.

(2) ..."

37. On its face, the words of the opening paragraph of reg. 48(1)(m) would justify the making of a refund for the purpose of relieving the taxpayer from double taxation on the sale value of goods sold and subsequently replaced under the RX program, but the words of the last paragraph limit the refund to cases where a taxpayer has satisfied the Commissioner "that the goods so replaced will not subsequently be used for the purpose ... for which they were manufactured". The evidence given in this case shows that some of the goods returned and replaced may have been discarded, others were repaired and sold as re-built goods. The taxpayer did not keep records of the goods so discarded or so re-built. In these circumstances it is difficult to see how reg.48(1)(m) has any application to the facts of this case.

38. Section 5 of the Exemptions and Classifications Act provides, in substance, that sales tax is not payable upon the sale value of any goods covered by an item or sub-item in the First Schedule under specified Acts including Act No. 1 and Act No. 6. Item 144 is the relevant item for present purposes. The wording of item 144 was changed on 20 August 1986. Before 20 August 1986, item 144 provided as follows:-

"144(1) Goods for use in replacing free of charge any

defective part of other goods, if-

(a) the cost of the goods so used in

replacement is recouped in the prices for

which-

(i) those other goods and goods of the

same kind as those other goods; or

(ii) goods of the same kind as those so

used in replacement,

are sold by wholesale; or

(b) the defective part replaced will, after

repair-

(i) be used in replacing free of charge

another defective part; or

(ii) be used in, wrought into or attached

to goods to be manufactured

(2) Goods for use in the repair of, so as to form

part of, the defective part first-mentioned in

the last preceding sub-item if that part will,

after repair-

(a) be used in replacing free of charge

another defective part; or

(b) be used in, wrought into or attached to

goods to be manufactured."

39. After 20 August 1986, the opening words of item 144 were varied to make it clear that the item applied with respect to goods used in replacing free of charge any defective goods if the cost of the goods so used in replacement is recouped as prescribed. In the present case, the monthly returns involved ended with the return for August 1986. Thus, for all practical purposes, the old form of item 144 applies. At the hearing, the taxpayer led evidence tending to show that it came within item 144, but before considering that matter, consideration will be given to the question of whether the Court has jurisdiction to determine the issue.

40. The notice of objection to the assessment by the Commissioner is dated 19 December 1986. The objection was to the "assessment to sales tax based upon sales made during the period 1 June 1985 to 31 August 1986". For the purposes of this issue, the grounds of the objection were:-

"3. ... sales tax has already been paid in respect

of goods sold and the assessment gives rise to an

overpayment of sales tax which the Commissioner is

required by the provisions of Section 26 of the Act

to refund".

Ground 4 was an elaboration of ground 3.

"5. ... the goods are exempt from sales tax

pursuant to Item 144 of the First Schedule of "the

Exemptions and Classifications Act."

41. By decision dated 12 April 1988 the Commissioner disallowed the objection.

42. It will be recalled that in the relevant sales tax legislation the word "assessment" is defined to mean "the ascertainment of the sale value of goods and of the sales tax payable on that sale value". Under the general scheme of the sales tax legislation, normally the Commissioner is not required to make an assessment. The amount of tax to be paid by a taxpayer is determined by the sale value of goods set out in its returns. Apart from its use in the definition in s.3 of Act No. 1, the word "assessment" first appears in that Act in s.25. Sub-section 25(1) has been set out earlier in these reasons but is repeated:-

"Where the Commissioner finds in any case that tax or

further tax is payable by a person, the Commissioner

may make an assessment in relation to that person."

43. By inserting the defined meaning of the word "assessment" that section reads:-

"Where the Commissioner finds in any case that tax or

further tax is payable by a person, the Commissioner

may make an ascertainment of the sale value of goods

and of the sales tax payable on that sale value in

relation to the person."

44. The rate of tax is determined by the various Sales Tax Acts. The Acts relevant to these proceedings are usually referred to as the Rating Act No. 1 or No. 6 as the case may be. The Rating Acts impose sales tax at specified rates "upon the sale value of goods". It is in this respect that the simplicity of the sales tax legislation is apparent. In the normal case of a sale of goods to a retailer, the sale value of goods is readily ascertainable. Sales tax at a specified rate is imposed on that sale value. In an assessment under s.25, the Commissioner is empowered to ascertain the sale value of goods. It is then a mathematical calculation to determine the sales tax payable on the sale value. On a reading of the legislation, questions of refunds or credits do not form part of the assessment. Those questions depend upon different provisions of the Act and are subject to a different review procedure.

45. Reference has been made already to the provisions of Act No. 1 with respect to objections to assessments. There is a corresponding set of provisions with respect to refund decisions. These provisions, as are the provisions with respect to objections to assessments, are contained in Part VII of Act No. 1. In that Part by s.39A the words "refund decision" are defined to mean:-

"a decision made on or after 1 July 1986 in respect

of an application -

(a) for action to be taken under section 26 or;

(b) under the Sales Tax Regulations for the refund

or payment of an amount of money to prevent

double taxation or for the purpose of

effectuating an exemption from sales tax;"

46. It will be noted that paragraph (b) applies with respect to reg. 48(1)(m) and to item 144.

47. Section 40 of Act No. 1 applies with respect to objections. Sub-section 40(2) is set out:-

"(2) An applicant for a refund decision who is

dissatisfied with a decision made on the

application may, within 60 days after service

on the applicant of notice of the decision,

lodge with the Commissioner an objection in

writing against the decision."

48. Thereafter, there are similar provisions with respect to objections against assessments and objections against refund decisions. Sub-section 40(3) is of importance in that it provides that sub-section (2) does not apply in relation to a refund decision unless the application for the refund or payment is made within 60 days after the "transaction, act or operation (not being the payment of tax) that is claimed to entitle the applicant to the refund or payment" or within a further time allowed by the Commissioner.

49. In the present case, no application for a refund decision has been made by the taxpayer with respect to the RX program. This is not suprising, since the taxpayer, wrongly in my opinion, deducted an amount which it estimated to be the sale value of goods returned and replaced under the RX program from the sale value of goods sold in subsequent months. By adopting this course, the taxpayer deprived itself of the opportunity of making an application for a refund or payment under s.26 of Act No. 1, under Reg. 48(1)(m) or under item 144. No application for such a refund having been made there is no refund decision which can be reviewed. In the circumstances, reference may be made to reg. 50(7) and regs. 51, 52, 54, 55 and 56 but they need not be considered further.

50. The taxpayer sought to have a review under the guise of the objection to the assessment. The sales tax legislation draws a clear distinction between an assessment, as defined, and a refund decision, as defined, and in my opinion a taxpayer cannot review a non-existing refund decision as if it formed part of an assessment. A refund decision does not, and, under the sales tax legislation, cannot, form part of the assessment.

51. In any event, from what has been said earlier, the taxpayer does not come within Reg. 48(1)(m). On the evidence given, the taxpayer in my opinion, does not come within item 144 in that there is no sufficient evidence to support a finding that the cost of the goods so used in replacement is recouped in the prices for which those goods were sold.

52. Counsel for the taxpayer made lengthy and detailed submissions against the conclusions I have made. In reality, the submissions were based on giving a practical effect to the sales tax legislation based upon common sense and business equity. In my opinion, there is no room for the acceptance of these submissions to the facts of this case. Section 5 of the Sales Tax Procedure Act 1934 provides that the return to be made by manufacturers under the various Sales Tax Assessments Acts shall be in the prescribed form, or if, as is the fact, there is no prescribed form, a form authorised by the Commissioner. The returns made by the taxpayer were on approved forms. Act No. 1 and the returns are based on tax being imposed on the sale value of goods sold each month. The procedure adopted is a form of self assessment by the taxpayer based upon the sale value of goods sold. The conclusions made do give a practical effect to the sales tax legislation relevant to the facts of the review. Questions arising with respect to refunds and exemptions are raised by the giving of particulars in column 9 of the returns. Particulars so given may well constitute an application for a refund decision. That question does not arise here. It is impermissible, in my opinion, to raise in a review of an assessment, as defined, questions as to refunds which have not been claimed. Note 9 on the approved return makes it clear that credits should be claimed in column 9 for goods returned inwards. On this being done, the Commissioner is made aware of the claim and that claim can be tested in accordance with the terms of Act No. 1.

53. The taxpayer contended further that the Commissioner could not have been dissatisfied, under paragraph 25(2A)(b) of Act No. 1 with the returns furnished by the taxpayer since, on the true application of all the provisions of the sales tax legislation and regulations, there had been no underpayment of tax. This submission, in my opinion, cannot succeed. Having regard to the provisions of sections 17, 18, 19 and 21 of Act No. 1 and the form of the authorised return, the taxpayer is required to make a disclosure of the sale value of all goods sold each month. A comparison of the books of account of the taxpayer with the returns submitted, shows clearly that the taxpayer did not make such a disclosure. There were ample grounds for the Commissioner to make the assessment under s.25. The real issue between the parties relates to the refund sought and no application for a refund was made by the taxpayer.

54. Finally, consideration is given to the imposition of the additional tax under sub-section 45(2) of Act No. 1. The ascertainment of the additional tax formed part of the assessment made by the Commissioner; see definition of assessment set out earlier in these reasons. Sub-section 45(2) has been set out earlier in these reasons. Additional tax may be imposed where either sub-paragraphs 42(2)(a)(i) or (ii) applies and paragraph (b) applies. Counsel contended that neither (i) nor (ii) was satisfied since the amount of tax to be paid was in fact correct. That argument has been rejected. The returns made by the taxpayer come within (i) and (ii) as well as (b). On this basis, the liability to pay additional tax equal to double the amount of the excess has been established.

55. In coming to this conclusion, reference is made to Sales Tax Ruling No. 2419 dated 2 June 1988. This ruling relates to what may be described as refunds including claims under s.26 of Act No. 1, reg. 48(1)(m) and item 144. The ruling suggests that some taxpayers had adopted a practice, similar to that adopted by the taxpayer in this case, of claiming the refund as a rebate. Under the heading "Facts" the ruling says:-

"7. Rebates should be claimed in the "tax credits"

column of the sales tax return, i.e. column 9.

8. Some taxpayers, when claiming rebates, do not

complete column 9 on the return form. They

instead reduce the sale value upon which tax is

payable (columns 2-6) and the gross amount of

tax payable (column 8) by amounts calculated to

reflect the rebate being claimed."

56. The ruling is then given as follows:-

"9. The practice described in para, 8 is contrary

to the requirements for compilation of the

return form as outlined in subsec. 5(2) of the

Procedure Act in that the gross taxable sale

value and gross tax payable figures are

understated and the amount of credit claimed is

omitted.

10. In addition, because returns prepared in this

manner do not accurately reflect the taxpayer's

taxable transactions as required by subsec.

5(1), they constitute false or misleading

statements. Such returns may render a taxpayer

liable to prosecution under sec. 8K, 8N and 8P

of the Taxation Administration Act.

Prosecution action will be taken in appropriate

cases."

57. This ruling is in conformity with the views expressed by the Court. Evidence given at the hearing suggests that prior to 2 June 1988, the Commission, at least implicitly, accepted the rebate system adopted by the taxpayer. Even if that had been done, that does not alter the proper construction of the law.

58. Further, in this case, there was evidence that at all relevant times, officers of the Commission knew of the practice being adopted by the taxpayer. Nevertheless, on the evidence, I find that the taxpayer was aware of the difficulties arising with respect to this matter, but it did not pursue the matter according to law. In imposing the additional tax, the Commissioner did not direct that the taxpayer pay additional tax equal to double the amount of the excess; see sub-section 45(2) of Act No. 1. He exercised his discretion in favour of the taxpayer by imposing additional tax based on interest calculated on the estimated amount of sales tax not paid. This amounted to 20% of the amount of tax not paid. In all the circumstances, I see no basis for interfering with that discretion.

59. The review under Act No. 6 raises identical issues and for the reasons given, the Court finds against the taxpayer.

60. In the result, each application is dismissed with costs.
