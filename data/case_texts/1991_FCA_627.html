Re Philip Raymond La Roche v Jan Cormack; Anne Ealey; Stephen Eastwood and Commissioner of Taxation [1991] FCA 627; (1991) 33 FCR 414 (1991) 26 ALD 458 (Extracts) (13 December 1991)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: PHILIP RAYMOND LA ROCHE

And: JAN CORMACK; ANNE EALEY; STEPHEN EASTWOOD and COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

No. V G202 of 1991

FED No. 917

Administrative Law

[1991] FCA 627; (1991) 33 FCR 414

[1991] FCA 627; (1991) 26 ALD 458 (extracts)

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Olney J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Administrative Law - application for judicial review - likelihood that no benefit could accrue to applicant even if successful - power of Court to not proceed with application.

Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977, s.16

Federal Court of Australia Act 1976, s.23

Federal Court Rules, O.20 r.2

Orison Pty. Ltd. v Strategic Minerals Corporation NL and others 81 ALR 183

Beitseen v Johnson and others (unreported: Full Court: 29 November 1989)

HEARING

MELBOURNE

13:12:1991

Counsel for the applicant: Mr McInnes

Solicitors for the applicant: Mallesons Stephen Jaques

Counsel for the respondents: Mr P. Rose

Solicitors for the respondents: Australian Government Solicitor

DECISION

This application for review, pursuant to the provisions of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act, relates to the decision of a promotion appeal committee dismissing an appeal by the applicant relating to appointments made to two positions of a temporary nature for which the applicant was one of the unsuccessful applicants.

2. The successful appointees were each appointed for a specific term expiring on 24 December 1991. The application for review was filed on 24 July 1991 and has progressed now to the stage of readiness for trial and, indeed, has been listed for trial today. I am not sure exactly when that notification of this date was given but it was not long ago and it is obvious that the matter was brought on today because of the imminent expiry of the term of the temporary appointments.

3. Counsel appearing for the respondents at the outset raised the question of whether the Court ought to proceed with hearing the application on the ground, if I can put it in fairly general terms, that no useful benefit could flow even from a decision favourable to the applicant for the reason that the temporary appointments in question expire on 24 December 1991, a matter of some 11 days hence. It is said that at the best the Court could only deal with the matter by setting aside the original decision and referring the matter back to the committee for further hearing according to law, and that by the time that process is gone through there would be little or no benefit to be derived by the applicant even if he were proved to be a successful appellant on the reconsideration.

4. The matter that has exercised my mind is whether the Court in these circumstances has the authority to simply not hear an application for review, and I have found no real assistance in the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act. The passage quoted by counsel from Lamb v Moss [1983] FCA 254; 49 ALR 533 at p 549, indicates that there is an obligation upon a Court, once the jurisdiction of the Court is properly enlivened by a sufficient application, to entertain that application, and indeed, the passage that follows suggests simply that that obligation is not inconsistent with the power of the Court to refuse relief, notwithstanding that the statutory pre-conditions for a grant of relief are satisfied. I think that it is generally understood in this jurisdiction that when, because of the effluxion of time, the making of orders of the type contemplated by section 16 of the ADJR Act would be rendered inappropriate, some declaration of right may be appropriate in order to indicate the result of the review application.

5. In this case evidence has been given, which I accept, that the two positions in question will, for practical purposes, cease to exist after 24 December 1991. It may be that the items will remain on the establishment of the particular department but it is obvious that if they do remain they will not be funded and no appointments will be made to fill them, either on a temporary or permanent basis.

6. In Orison Pty. Limited v Strategic Minerals Corporation NL and others, 81 ALR 183, French J. found that benefits to be derived from the relief that was being sought in that application were tenuous, speculative and riddled with contingency and that the futility of the orders sought was so patent that to proceed with the application would be an abuse of the process of the Court. In those circumstances his Honour summarily dismissed the claims in question.

7. Another authority which perhaps is more relevant but which is not reported is Beitseen v Johnson and others, a decision of the Full Court of this Court (Woodward, Northrop and Ryan JJ.) given on 29 November 1989. That case involved an appeal from a single judge who had made orders concerning the performance of the rules of an industrial union relating to the filling of an office by one of the parties to the proceedings. The officer in question, who was unsuccessful before the single judge, had been appointed as assistant secretary for the unexpired term of office of the previous incumbent and it was common ground that the unexpired term would expire on 5 December 1989. The appeal was heard on 25 October 1989 when the Full Court announced that it would not proceed further with the hearing of the appeal. Detailed reasons were given on 29 November 1989. At the time of the hearing, an election was in progress to fill the vacancy that would occur after the expiration of the then current term. Without going into the details of the case any further and the arguments that were put, the Court came to the conclusion that it should not proceed with the appeal.

8. In that case there was a suggestion that the position of the assistant secretary in respect of his salary entitlement might in some way be prejudiced if the appeal was not heard, but the Court came to the conclusion that to proceed with the appeal would be tantamount to giving an advisory opinion which would be of no more than persuasive authority if the litigation with respect to his salary ever eventuated.

9. The Full Court was of the view that it had power not to proceed further with the appeal. That power was conferred on the Court by implication by section 23 of the Federal Court Act and Order 20 Rule 2 of the Federal Court Rules. At page 5 of the print of the reasons their Honours said:

We have been persuaded to exercise that power in this case

primarily because the passage of time has, in the

circumstances, deprived the issues in controversy of real

practical significance. However, the Court has been

influenced as well by an indication that the cost of at

least some of the parties to the appeal may fall on the

public purse and any course which minimises those costs

without doing violence to genuine and substantial rights of

the parties is to be favoured.

10. Having regard to the foregoing I am satisfied that in a proper case the Court does have power to not proceed with an application for judicial review.

11. An alternative approach that could be adopted would be to try as a preliminary issue the question of whether, assuming the applicant were successful, the Court would in its discretion exercise the power to make an order. It is clear that the Court would not be bound to make any order pursuant to section 16 even though the applicant were entirely successful. Probably the same considerations would apply in that circumstance as would apply to the type of situation which was discussed in the Beitseen v Johnson appeal.

12. Counsel for the applicant has said that the adverse decision of the appellant tribunal which is sought to be reviewed is something which will remain on the applicant's employment record and would tend, in some way, to be unfavourable to him in his future career. Counsel for the respondents denies that this would be so and I feel that I cannot reach any conclusion one way or the other. I am, however, of the opinion that the circumstances of the case are such that it would be futile to proceed with the application to review at this stage and in the circumstances I am disinclined to proceed to the hearing.

13. This circumstance is not of the making of any of the parties and it should in no way be construed as a criticism of either the applicant or of the respondents. By the same token I am reluctant to use the phraseology that is found in Order 20 Rule 2 of the Federal Court Rules which refers to a proceeding being an abuse of process. Although there is some English authority that suggests that the concept of abuse of process is one that ought to be understood in a very liberal way and not be indicative of some criticism of the party pursuing proceedings found to be an abuse of process, I wish to avoid any such connotation being attached to the conclusion that I have reached. (See article at 52 ALR 678).

14. I indicated this morning that I would have some reluctance in making an order simply dismissing the proceedings, as to do so might suggest that the applicant has been unsuccessful in pursuing his relief. My preference is to simply order that the proceedings be permanently stayed.

15. There is another consideration which does exercise my mind namely, that as the state of the proceedings and the fact that little or no real benefit could ever have flowed to the applicant from them are matters that must have been well-known to the parties well in advance of this hearing, irrespective of the ultimate outcome, it could well be that in the exercise of the Court's discretion in relation to costs, the resulting order may not necessarily be favourable to the applicant.

16. In my opinion the proper order is that the application be permanently stayed and that there be no order for costs. I will make available to the parties at my earliest convenience a copy of the reasons that I have delivered this afternoon.
