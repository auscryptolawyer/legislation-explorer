Daniel and Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1999] AATA 212 (7 April 1999)

Last Updated: 12 April 1999

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION [1999] AATA 212

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No N1998/1529

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re BELINDA DANIEL

Applicant

And DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal Senior Member M D Allen

Date 7 April 1999

Place Sydney

Decision The decision under review is affirmed.

\

(Sgd) M D ALLEN

..............................................

Senior Member

CATCHWORDS

FREEDOM OF INFORMATION - Application to amend HECS Information Statement to show no debt existed. Effect of earlier Tribunal decision. Power of Commissioner of Taxation to amend assessment to defer repayment not a power to waive debt.

Freedom of Information Act 1982 - s48 and s51

Higher Education Funding Act 1988 - ss34, 63, 71, 72 and 74

Higher Education Funding Legislation Amendment Act 1993 - s16 and s17

REASONS FOR DECISION

7 April 1999 Senior Member M D Allen

1. By application lodged 6 October 1998 the Applicant sought, pursuant to subsection 56(1A) of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 (as amended), review of a deemed refusal of the Respondent to amend a document.

2. This matter came on for hearing before me at Sydney on 29 March 1999. At that hearing the Applicant was represented by her husband and the documents prepared for the Tribunal, pursuant to s37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, were taken in as exhibits T1 to T22 inclusive. Exhibit A1 is the Applicant's Statement of Facts and Contentions together with documents annexed thereto.

3. The document the Applicant sought to be either amended or annotated by the Respondent is not included in the section 37 documents but Document T12 is in identical terms. That document is entitled "Higher Education Contribution Scheme Information Statement" and sets out various years and figures, the net result of which is a claim that the Applicant has an outstanding Higher Education Contribution Scheme (HECS) debt in the sum of $765.00 as at 1 June 1998.

4. On 15 June 1998 (see Document T8) the Applicant had written to the Respondent in the following terms, namely:

"RE: HECS STATEMENT - TFN

I am writing in regard to the above matter, and to:

a. advise that, to the best of my knowledge, I do not have an alleged HECS debt: and

b. request a statement, pursuant to section 13 of the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 and/or section 28 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, in relation to the decision to issue the HECS information statement dated 1 June 1998 to me.

I assume that your records indicate that my alleged HECS debt was appealed to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal in 1994, and was settled (by consent) in approximately October 1994 before the Tribunal located at Sydney.

Please do not hesitate to contact me if any further details etc are required. And I sincerely appreciate your assistance with this matter."

5. On 31 August 1998, the Applicant wrote to the Respondent requesting an amendment of the HECS Information Statement so as to show a total balance outstanding of nil.

6. Section 48 of the Freedom of Information Act 1972 states:

"Where a person claims that a document of an agency or an official document of a Minister to which access has been lawfully provided to the person, whether under this Act or otherwise, contains personal information about that person:

(a) that is incomplete, incorrect, out of date or misleading; and

(b) that has been used, is being used or is available for use by the agency or Minister for an administrative purpose;

the person may apply to the agency or Minister for:

(c) an amendment; or

(d) an annotation;

of the record of that information kept by the agency or Minister."

Whereas section 51 states inter alia:

"Where an agency or Minister decides not to amend a document or official documents wholly or partly in accordance with an application under section 48, the agency or Minister must:

(a) take such steps as are reasonable in the circumstances to enable the applicant to provide a statement of the kind mentioned in paragraph 51A(c); and

(b) subject to subsection (2), annotate the document or official document concerned by adding to it the statement so provided.

(2) Paragraph (1)(b) does not apply if the agency or Minister considers the statement to be irrelevant, defamatory or unnecessarily voluminous.

..."

7. Apart from disputing the accuracy of the calculations in the HECS Information Statement, the gravamen of the Applicant's complaint is that, pursuant to an earlier consent decision by this Tribunal, the Respondent had agreed to waive the repayment of any HECS debt that might be owed by the Applicant.

8. On 12 October 1994, the Applicant had sought review by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal of a decision described in the Application for Review in the following terms, namely:

"Refusal by the Commissioner of Taxation to defer and/or reduce an assessment made under section 71 of the Higher Education Funding Act 1988."

That application was given Tribunal File No NT94/448.

9. Section 42C of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 states inter alia:

"42C. (1) If, at any stage of a proceeding for a review of a decision:

(a) agreement is reached between the parties or their representatives as to the terms of a decision of the Tribunal in the proceeding or in relation to a part of the proceeding or a matter arising out of the proceeding that would be acceptable to the parties (other than an agreement reached in the course of a mediation under section 34A); and

(b) the terms of the agreement are reduced to writing, signed by or on behalf of the parties and lodged with the Tribunal; and

(c) the Tribunal is satisfied that a decision in those terms or consistent with those terms would be within the powers of the Tribunal;

the Tribunal may, if it appears to it to be appropriate to do so, act in accordance with whichever of subsection (2) or (3) is relevant in the particular case.

(2) If the agreement reached is to the terms of a decision of the Tribunal in the proceeding, the Tribunal may make a decision in accordance with those terms without holding a hearing of the proceeding or, if a hearing has commenced, without completing the hearing.

..."

10. On 13 April 1995 a decision was made pursuant to section 42C(1)(a) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 in matter NT94/448, the operative part of which decision read:

"THEREFORE, in accordance with section 43C(2) of the Act, the Tribunal makes the following decision:

(1) The decision of the Commissioner of Taxation dated 13 April 1994 and affirmed on 14 September 1994, be set aside; and

(2) In substitution thereof:

The Respondent agrees to amend the Applicant's taxation assessment No 256765/003 for the year 1991 to exclude the HECS debt of $703. contained therein. Further, the Respondent agrees to pay to the Applicant any interest due on that amount."

(See Document T4 in these proceedings.)

11. The wording adopted by the parties in their consent decision follows the wording of the Higher Education Funding Act 1988, as it existed as at 16 February 1993 at the time the Applicant made her original application to the Respondent for relief.

12. Section 34 of the said Act defines Commissioner as meaning the Commissioner of Taxation and sections 71, 72 and 74 read:

"Assessment

71. The Commissioner may, from any information in the Commissioner's possession, whether from a return or otherwise, make an assessment of:

(a) the amount of the accumulated HEC debt of a person at 1 June immediately preceding the making of the assessment; and

(b) the amount required to be paid in respect of that accumulated HEC debt under section 68.

Application of Income Tax Assessment Act

72. (1) Subject to this part, Parts IV and V, and Division 1 of Part VI, of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 apply, so far as they are capable of application, in relation to an HEC assessment debt of a person as if it were income tax assessed to be payable by a taxpayer by an assessment made under Part IV of that Act.

(2) Sections 222 and 223 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 have effect as if any HEC assessment debt of a person were income tax payable by the person in respect of the year of income in respect of which the assessment of that debt was made.

Power of Commissioner to defer assessment or reduce assessment to nil

74. (1) The Commissioner may, on written application by a person, defer, for such period as the Commissioner considers appropriate, the making of an assessment in relation to the person under section 71 if the Commissioner considers that, were the assessment to be made, payment of the assessed amount would cause serious hardship to the person or considers that there are other special reasons that make it fair and reasonable to defer the making of the assessment.

(2) The Commissioner may, on written application by a person, amend an assessment made in relation to the person under section 71 so that no amount is payable under the assessment if the Commissioner considers that payment of the assessed amount has caused or would cause serious hardship to the person or considers that there are other special reasons that make it fair and reasonable to make the amendment.

(3) Where an application referred to in subsection (1) or (2) is made, the Commissioner shall as soon as practicable consider the matter to which the application relates and notify the applicant of the Commissioner's decision on the application."

(These sections being in similar terms to sections 106T, 106U and 106W of the Higher Education Funding Act 1988 as amended by the Higher Education Funding Legislation Amendment Act 1993, being Act No 116 of 1993 which was assented to on 24 December 1993.)

13. Having regard to the difference in wording between subsections 74(1) of the Higher Education Funding Act 1988 which refers to a deferment of an assessment, and subsection 74(2) which refers to an amendment so that no amount is payable, then it becomes clear that, by its wording, the consent decision is one which waived the Applicant's HECS debt, for the purposes of the assessment issued for the 1991 tax year.

14. Although the Respondent did exclude the Applicant's HECS debt it was in relation to the assessment for the 1991 tax year. Given the scheme of the Act, the Commissioner had power, pursuant to Division 4 of Part 4.4 of the said Act (now Division 3 of Part 5A.2) to amend an assessment in any tax year but the power to waive the indebtedness is restricted to the Secretary of the now Department of Education, Training and Youth Affairs as per section 63 which read:

"(1) Where a student has incurred a HEC semester debt, the Secretary may, on written application made by the student within 3 months after the debt was incurred, if the Secretary is satisfied that there are special circumstances that justify so doing, by writing signed by the Secretary remit the whole, or such part as the Secretary thinks fit, of the debt.

(2) Where an application referred to in subsection (1) is made, the Secretary shall as soon as practicable consider the matter to which the application relates and notify the student of the decision on the application.

(3) Where the Secretary remits the whole or a part of an HEC semester debt, the Secretary shall give to the Commissioner written notice setting out the name of the student, the amount that has been remitted and such other particulars as the Commissioner reasonably requires."

(Section 63 was replaced by Section 106L which is in even more restrictive terms.)

15. To accede to the Applicant's request to make amendments or annotations to the HECS Information Statement would in effect be to amend the decision of the Tribunal dated 13 April 1995. Whereas I am satisfied that the Applicant understood the decision to mean that her HECS debt was waived so that it no longer existed, such a determination was beyond the authority of the Respondent. All the Respondent could do was in any tax year amend the assessment so that the debt, or any part thereof, was not payable in that year.

16. Sections 48 and 51 of the Freedom of Information Act 1982 cannot be used, either directly or indirectly, to appeal or set aside decisions of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal. So far as the HECS Information Statement is concerned, it is prima facie not incomplete, incorrect, out of date or misleading. If the Applicant alleges that some of the mathematics in the document are incorrect, that is a matter which can be taken further with the Respondent but there was nothing placed before me to enable me to say the sums are correct or incorrect. The document is accurate in that it correctly states that a HECS debt chargeable to the Applicant is extant.

17. Given the decision above, I consider that it would be irrelevant to make any annotation to the said document pursuant to section 51 of the Freedom of Information Act 1982.

18. The decision under review will therefore be affirmed.

I certify that the 18 preceding paragraphs are a true copy of the reasons for the decision herein of:

Senior Member M D Allen
