NT85/5849 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 417; (1987) 18 ATR 4002; 87 ATC 1180 (20 November 1987)

Last Updated: 30 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

RECOUPMENT TAX - vendor and former shareholder - objection to assessment of target company as liability to Division 7 tax -dividend distributed within first two months of "prescribed period" - no continuity of beneficial ownership during those two months - discretion to treat as sufficient distribution.

INCOME TAX - private company - undistributed profits tax dividend distributed within first two months of "prescribed period" - no continuity of beneficial ownership during those two months - discretion to treat as sufficient distribution.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - Division 7, ss 6, 103(1), 104(1). 105A(1), 105A(5), 105A(12)

Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 - ss 5(1), 5(4).

Giris Pty. Limited v. FCT [1969] HCA 5; (1969) 119 CLR 365; NT85/625 & Ors (Decision handed down 5 November 1987);

Slutzkin v. FCT (1977) 140 CLR 314.

REASONS FOR DECISION

20 November 1987 Mr Peter M Roach (Senior Member)

  1. In this reference the question is whether an assessment by the Commissioner of Taxation made on 18 May 1983 against a company ("Producer") is excessive. By that assessment a sum of $36,279 was levied as tax imposed in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1979 pursuant to the provisions of Division 7 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 <"the Tax Act").
  2. The possibility that such a dispute should arise flows from the fact that Producer was generating taxable income from year to year as a "private company" (as defined by the Act). That being so, each year it was liable to be assessed to pay tax pursuant to Division 7 of the Act if it had not effected a "sufficient distribution" by way of dividend within "the prescribed period" meaning "the period of one year commencing two months before the end of the (relevant) year of income" [s103(1)]. "Year of income" meant "the financial year next preceding the year of tax" (s6), which in turn meant "the financial year for which income tax is levied" (s6). It is' common ground that "the prescribed period" for Producer commenced 1 May in each year, and in each case concluded on 30 April in the following year.

3. In April 1979 Producer resolved to distribute $30,130 by way of dividend to its only shareholder (Medium). The cheque effecting payment of that dividend was debited to the bank account of Producer on 1st May, 1979, and credited to the account of Medium by the same bank on the same day. 1st May, 1979 was the first day following completion of the prescribed period to which that dividend related, but nothing turns on that. There is no assessment presently before the Tribunal suggesting that that dividend payment did not constitute a "sufficient distribution"; and there has been no relevant assessment to Division 7 tax in relation to that year.

4. In May 1979, Producer declared, and paid to Medium as its sole shareholder, a further dividend of $105,200.29. It is common ground that, if the only relevant provisions of the Act had been those which applied in relation to the earlier dividend, the result would have been the same. However, the Commissioner contends, and the Applicant acknowledges, that by reason of certain amendments to Division 7 effected in 1973, the question of liability to Division 7 tax in relation to the latter dividend turns upon a consideration of sub-sections 105A(5) and (12) of the Tax Act; provisions which, in the circumstances, did not have any application to the distribution of the former dividend. Before considering those sub-sections, it is appropriate to consider to what extent (if any) the circumstances giving rise to' the issue of the assessment and the prosecution of the objection presently before this Tribunal are relevant to that question.

5. When the Commissioner issued the assessment under review on 18 May 1983, he served notice of that assessment on each of several persons ("the Vendors") who had at one time been holders of all the issued share capital in Producer. The notices of the assessment so made as issued to those Vendors were accompanied by a letter forewarning the Vendors of the Commissioner's intention in due course to issue "vendor recoupment" assessments against them pursuant to the Taxation (Unpaid Company Tax) Assessment Act 1982 ['the T(UCT)A Act']. By that letter, the Commissioner also advised the Vendors that they could nominate a representative who could object to the assessments so made against the company ("Producer") and pursue all rights of objection which would have been open to Producer. The Vendors availed themselves of those opportunities. They nominated a representative ("the Applicant") and the company so nominated lodged an objection with the Commissioner against the assessment of Producer. When that objection was disallowed by the Commissioner, the Applicant requested the reference on of that objection for independent review. The Applicant has appeared in these proceedings to protest the assessment of Producer. By doing so, the Applicant has contended that the assessment of Producer is excessive. In these proceedings the Applicant does not protest any T(UCT)A Act assessment against any of the Vendors for the simple reason that at this time no assessment has issued against any of them. Further, there cannot be any such assessment against any of the Vendors until such time as the present proceedings are finally" determined, and even then only if the assessment against Producer is confirmed, at least to some extent.

6. Whether the assessment is excessive does not depend in any respect upon the provisions of the T(UCT)A Act because that legislation has nothing to say about the determination of taxable income of any target company. Neither can the provisions of the T(UCT)A Act affect the interpretation of those provisions of the Tax Act which must be applied in order to determine whether or not the assessment of Producer is excessive. That arises because there is no scope for the view that the T(UCT)A legislation of 1982 proposes, either expressly or by implication, any modification of the operation of the provisions of the Tax Act which fix the quantum of liability of any target company. Against that background, I proceed to set forth the relevant findings of fact.

7. Producer was incorporated in 1940 and traded for many years under the direction of its founding promoter. Following his death it ceased to carry out the business activities it had engaged in during his life and it consolidated its assets in the form of interest-earning loans. Its entire share capital came to be held by the Vendors. They decided that they wished to realize their interest in the company by disposing of their shareholdings. 8. By an agreement in writing of 24 (sic) April 1979 the Vendors agreed to sell the entire shareholding in Producer to a purchaser company (Medium), introduced to them by their accountant. The sale price was $1,113,238 and the Vendors warranted the accuracy of the Balance Sheet previously mentioned. The only common link between the Vendors and Medium was that they retained the services of the same accountant. Following completion of the sale the Vendors had no further interest in, and no control over, the affairs of Producer. The transfer of shares was effected by attorneys on a Northern Territory Register. (Nothing turns on the circumstance that the attorneys sometimes confused the identity of their principals, only to then correct their errors).

  1. At that point it was likely that, in the ordinary course of events. Producer would become liable to ordinary company tax upon its taxable income to 30 June 1979 and, if it failed to satisfy the requirements of Division 7 of the Act, to assessments pursuant to that Division. That would effect Medium.
  2. Then on 27 April 1979 Producer declared, and on 1 May 1979 paid, to Medium, a dividend of $30,130. (No point is made by the Commissioner of the date of payment - cf. ante - pa. 3). Further, on 3 May 1979 Producer declared, and on 11 May 1979 paid to Medium, a dividend of $105,220.29.

The terms of the resolution were as follows:-

13. Following 30 June 1979 it became the responsibility of the Commissioner pursuant to the Act to effect appropriate assessments against (inter alia) Producer but, for reasons which were not explained, he did not issue the assessment in question before me until 18 May 1983. By that date the T(UCT)A Act had come into effect. The Commissioner then moved to discharge his duty under the Tax Act by issuing a Division 7 assessment against Producer on the basis that it had not effected such a "sufficient distribution" by way of dividend as would enable it to avoid liability to a Division 7 assessment. In doing so the Commissioner determined that the extent of the insufficiency of distribution was $72,558. On that basis he levied tax of $36,279. It is undisputed that the quantum of tax was correctly assessed if there had not been a sufficient distribution.

  1. The issue as argued revolved around the correct construction of two sub-sections introduced to Division 7 in 1973. They are sub-sections 105A(5) and (12). To understand those sub-sections, it is appropriate to consider them in the context of the entirety of Division 7 as it stood in the relevant year; and against the back-ground of the historical development of Division 7.
  2. The Tax Act provides for the annual assessment of liability for income tax. Assessments ordinary lie against both individuals and companies. Companies are taxed on their "profits", just as individuals are. However, until recently, in addition individuals were taxed on most dividends they received from companies. Because companies may only declare dividends out of profits, but have no obligation to declare dividends, companies by the decisions of their shareholders could manage their dividend policies to the fiscal advantage of shareholders, and in doing so defer the date upon which the Revenue could levy tax against individuals on profits which might have been derived by way of dividend. Recognising that. Division 7 was enacted to provide that, where closely held companies (as defined) failed to effect "a sufficient distribution" by way of dividend within the time allowed, the company which had so failed to distribute could be subjected to Division 7 tax. As a "sufficient distribution" could be effected without distributing all that was available for distribution, many companies (including Producer) had built up and retained substantial accumulated profits out of the trading of earlier years. In addition there could be "current year profits".

THE PRE-1973 POSITION

16. Prior to the passage of the 1973 amendments to the Act the relevant provisions of Division 7 of the Act were:-

S.104(1) "Subject to this section, a private company which is not, by section 105A, to he deemed to have made a sufficient distribution in relation to the year of income is liable to pay additional tax upon the undistributed amount at the rate declared by the Parliament by the Act declaring the rates of income tax for the year of tax."

(2)

S.105A(1) "For the purposes of this Division, a private company shall be deemed to have made a sufficient distribution in relation to a year of income if it has, during the prescribed period, paid in dividends (other than special fund dividends and prescribed dividends) an amount not less than the excess of the distributable income of that year of income over the retention allowance in respect of that distributable income".

( 2).......

In determining whether the requirements of those provisions were satisfied nothing turned on -

  1. the identity of the payees;
  2. the liability to tax of the payees;
  1. the segment of the "prescribed period" during which the dividend was paid;
  1. whether there was continuity in beneficial ownership of shares by the payees of the dividend after the dividend was paid;
  2. what relationship, if any, existed between transferors and transferees upon any change in the beneficial ownership;

f) whether, upon any change in ownership of shares, the transfer was by way of sale, gift or inheritance.

17. It is common ground that, but for the 1973 amendments, the dividend declared and paid in May 1979 would have constituted an effective "sufficient distribution" notwithstanding the later sale from Medium to Omega.

THE 1973 AMENDMENTS

18. So far as is material upon this reference the provisions of the amending Act (No. 51 of 1973) may be reduced to two: S.105A (5) which extended the base for the levying of Division 7 tax ("the tax imposition"); and S.105A(12) ("the exemption provision"), which provided for relief from the tax otherwise to be imposed in consequence of sub-section (5). It was acknowledged during the hearing that sub-section (5) would operate in the circumstances arising on this reference, unless sub-section (12) was operative. In order to understand the power to exempt, it is appropriate to first consider the amendment whereby the base for the tax imposition was extended.

Section 105A(5) provides:-

"(5) Notwithstanding sub-section (1) but subject to the following provisions of this section and to sections 105AAA and 105AAB, an amount paid in a dividend by a private company during the first two months of the period that is the prescribed period in relation to the year of income (not being an amount paid on the day that is the first or last day of that period of two months) shall not be taken into account in ascertaining' whether the company is deemed to have made a sufficient distribution in relation to the year of income unless the company satisfies the Commissioner that, at all times during the part of that period of two months that followed the day on which that amount was paid, shares in the company carrying between them -

(a) the right to exercise more than one-half of the voting power in the company;

(b) the right to receive more than one-half of any dividends that may be paid by the company; and

(c) the right to receive more than one-half of any distribution of capital of the company,

were beneficially owned by persons who, at all times during the part of that period of two months that commenced on the first day of that period and ended on the day on which that amount was paid, beneficially owned shares in the company carrying between them rights of those kinds."

19. Expressed more simply by omitting terms not relevant to this reference and by referring to continuity of beneficial ownership in general terms, sub-section (5) can be stated as follows:-

" subject to (sub-section 12 of this section) ..............an amount paid in a dividend by a private company during the first two months of the period that is the prescribed period in relation to the year of income (....) shall not be taken into account in ascertaining whether the company is deemed to have made a sufficient distribution in relation to the year of income

unless the company satisfies the Commissioner that,

* at all times during the part of that period of two months that followed the day on which that amount was paid,

* shares in the company (controlling at least a 50% interest)

* were beneficially owned by persons who,
  • * at all times during the part of that period of two' months commenced on the first day of that period and ended on the day on which that amount was paid,
  • * beneficially owned shares in the company carrying between them rights of those kinds".
  1. Clearly then, as has been acknowledged by the Applicant, subject to sub-section (12), the dividend in question is to be excluded from account in calculating a "sufficient distribution". The persons who beneficially owned the shares on and from the first day of the two month-period (Medium) through to the day upon which the dividend in question was paid did not continue to beneficially hold at least a 50% interest in the company during that part of the two-month period following the day on which the dividend was paid.
  2. It is also to be observed that, nothing in the operation of sub-section (5) turns upon
    • (a) the identity of the payees;
    • (b) their tax status;
    • (c) on any relationship between transferors and transferees in relation to their shareholding; or
    • (d) whether the transfer was effected by way of sale, gift or inheritance.

Nor was the operation of sub-section (5) conditioned to be limited to situations in which the payees of the dividend so distributed had not returned the dividend so received as assessable income; or had not had the dividend so received included in the calculation of taxable income; or had not been taxed upon the dividend so received (whether because the dividends were exempt from taxation or because the payee was" exempt from taxation). That being so, unless sub-section (12) prevent it, sub-section (5) would operate to make a company-liable to Division 7 tax even where a dividend had been distributed to shareholders who in turn had paid tax on the dividend so received.

  1. In such a case sub-section (5) would have greatly expanded the scope of Division 7 because it would have taxed shareholders as a consequence of them receiving the distribution of dividend which Division 7 was designed to ensure would be made; and it would also have rendered the company liable to Division 7 tax which previously was only to be levied in default of a sufficient distribution.
  2. However, sub-section (5) did not operate in isolation. Sub-section (12) provided:-

"Sub-sections (5) to (11) inclusive do not apply in relation to an amount paid in a dividend if the Commissioner considers that, having regard to the persons who were the beneficial owners of shares in the company at the time when the amount was paid, it would be unreasonable for those sub-sections to apply in relation to that amount."

24. In two recent decisions I have had occasion to consider other provisions of the T(UCT)A Act conferring a wide discretion. Applying a view expressed in the High Court of Australia in Giris Pty Limited v FCT [1969] HCA 5; (1969) 119 CLR 365, I then took the view - there expressed by Barwick, CJ - that "a discretion so widely cast is a 'discretion' in the nature of a dispensing power" enabling the person exercising the discretion to determine whether a person will be assessed under one section rather than' another (as in Giris) or whether a person shall he assessed at all (as in the recent decision NT35/625 & Ors. (5 November 1987).

25. In the context of Division 7, sub-section 105A(12) is not as broad as it might have been because it is conditioned upon the imposition of the tax being considered unreasonable "having regard to the persons who were the beneficial owners of shares in the company at the time when the amount was paid..." In the context of this case that means that the discretion could only be exercised if it would be unreasonable that sub-section (5) should apply having regard to Medium as the beneficial owner of the shares at the time the dividend was paid. How the identity of or other considerations relating to, earlier former shareholders could be relevant to the exercise of a discretion to relieve the company from liability is not clear to me and seems to be not clear to Counsel for the Commissioner either.

26. However, the passage of the T(UCT)A Act does provide an explanation as to why former shareholders might come to be concerned as to the liabilities of a company after they had ceased to be shareholders in it. But although the passage of the T(UCT)A Act may explain their concern, it cannot provide an explanation as to the interpretation of sub-section 105A(12) because, when the amendments to S.105A were enacted in 1973, the passage of the T(UCT)A legislation in 1982 was not even in prospect. Although the transactions considered by the High Court in Slutzkin v FCT C(1977) 140 CLR 3143 had occurred as early as November 1968, the decision of the High Court was not to' be handed down until February 1977.

27. Having analysed the issues to that extent, in my view the answers to the questions raised before me by the Applicant in opposing the assessment raised against Producer are clear:-

(1) the only issue before the Tribunal for determination is whether the assessment of Division 7 tax against Producer should be held to be excessive;

(2) as is conceded, the assessment against Producer must be upheld unless the Tribunal, standing in the shoes of the Commissioner, considers it unreasonable - in the sense provided for by sub-section 105A(12) - for sub-section 105A(5) to apply;

(3) for sub-section 105A(12) . to apply, the assessment must be unreasonable "having regard to 'Medium' as beneficial owner of the shares in Producer at the time the amount was paid";

(4) nothing in the evidence before me as to Medium would enable me to say that it is unreasonable that the assessment against Producer should stand;

(5) in so far as the evidence stands before me, there is nothing in the operation of the Tax Act as to the liability of Producer which would have adverse consequences for either Medium or the Vendors;

(6) whether affirming the liability of Producer under the assessment may lead to adverse consequences for the Vendors represented by the Applicant will' depend upon the application of the T(UCT)A Act and, in all probability, on a determination as to whether "under a scheme... some or all of the shares in (Producer) were sold" by the Vendors - as distinct from Medium - in circumstances within the scope of sub-section 5(1) of the T(UCT)A Act;

(7) whether any such assessment would lie against any of the Vendors is not a question for any present determination;

(8) if any such assessment is raised against any one or more of the Vendors it will be open to each person so assessed to challenge the assessment as not authorised by the provisions of the T(UCT)A Act;

(9) whether any such assessment will then be held to be excessive will depend upon the taxpayer so assessed establishing -
  • (a) that the conditions for liability stipulated for by the T(UCT)A Act have not been satisfied; or
  • (b) that, even if they are otherwise satisfied, it should then be determined that, for example, "the primary taxable amount shall not be taken to exist or to have existed in relation to the person" by reason of an exercise of discretion such as is provided for in sub-section 5(4) of the T(UCT)A Act.

28. As this matter stands before me, I affirm the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection.
