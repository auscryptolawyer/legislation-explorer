TT86/462, TT86/463 and Commissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 428; 87 ATC 159 (19 December 1986)

Last Updated: 6 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Assessable Income - Acquisition of leased truck and sale at a profit - Whether profit assessable - Dominant purpose - Burden of proof.

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) (Cth) S 25(1), 25A, 190(b)

Admin Exploration Pty Limited (in liq) v FC of T 72 ATC 4253

Gillespie-Jones v FC of T 86 ATC 4183

McCormack v FC of T [1979] HCA 18; (1979) 143 CLR 284

Nos. TT86/462 TT86/463

AAT Decision No 3114

19 December 1986

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No.

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicants

And:

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. On 11 January 1984 a partnership sold a log truck which it had purchased on or about 14 December 1983 for $5,800. The sale was made pursuant to a contract entered into on 19 December 1983. The sale price was $39,203 generating a profit of $33,403. The question upon these references is whether the Commissioner was correct in treating the profit as assessable income of the partnership and in increasing the taxable incomes of the partners accordingly.

2. The partners were a husband (H) and his wife. Mrs H died prior to the hearing. It was agreed that the applications would be heard together and that any finding to be made as to the state of mind of H would be applied to his late wife also.

3. The partnership carried on business as logging contractors an exercise which involved (inter alia) transporting mill-logs and saw-logs from the forest to Mill sites. The logs were carried by trucks which had to be capable of operating under load over surface conditions ranging from highways to rough forest tracks. Before commencing to carry on that business the partnership had acquired an International Acco 30/70 Eagle cab and chassis on the terms of a commercial lease. The lease was for a term of four years commencing on 11 January 1979. Monthly rental was at the rate of $1,663.15 per month (payable in advance) and a residual value of $21,750 was attributed to the Unit. H's belief was that at the commencement of the lease the unit might have been purchased for a price of the order of $58,000. At the expiration of that lease, a lease for a further 12 months was entered into commencing 18 January 1983 at a rental of $1,147.10 per month (payable in advance) with a residual value of $5,800. On the expiration of the latter lease the partnership purchased the truck for $5,800 and continued to use it in the course of the partnership business.

4. The evidence of H, the surviving partner, was that at the time the decision was made to acquire title to the truck his intention was to rebuild the International at a estimated cost of some $45,000; to continue to use it in the business for an indefinite period; and at that date he had no thought in mind of acquiring any new truck. He acknowledged reluctantly in the course of cross-examination that at the time of the purchase he knew that the International truck had a market value in excess of $30,000.

5. H explained the change of intention by reference to a short series of events commencing with an unexpected meeting with an old acquaintance. That meeting occurred when H went to a "Bushmen's show" held over the course of a week and promoted by the local Logging Association. All manner of machinery used in the logging industry was on display. H was not able to be more precise in identifying when that was than to say that it was on a Saturday early in November, 1983. His evidence was that while at that show he met S, a salesman, with whom he had had dealings in the past. He then learned that S was selling on behalf of the local agent for Scania trucks. Although he had no interest in purchasing any new truck he allowed himself to be taken for a short test run in the course of which he formed the opinion that "for a Swedish truck it went reasonably well". The test run was over suburban roads with the truck unladen. H and S differed in their recollections as to the extent of the trip but that is of little moment - both agreed that the trip was very short and the evidence of each was that it was conducted under conditions far removed from bush work. H said that he left S quite certain that he had no interest in acquiring any new truck, let alone a Scania. S, who also gave evidence, said that in his judgment H was definitely not interested in buying despite what had transpired that day, but that S nonetheless still thought that H might be persuaded to change his mind.

6. On 16 November 1983 S's employer placed an order on its supplier for delivery of a new Scania Truck, identifying H as the customer. The truck so ordered, incorporating all options as specified, was supplied to the dealership and came to be the truck acquired by the partnership. H denied knowledge of the order at the time it was placed and denied having authorized it. I accept that. I accept the explanation of S that the dealership was only able to take possession of new units on the representation by the dealership that firm orders were in hand and that it was the practice of the dealership to order ahead so as to ensure the availability of stock should an opportunity for a sale arise. Although I accept that evidence I observe that the placing of the order was consistent with a greater confidence in the prospect of selling to H at that date than he first stated; and that it reflected a greater awareness of the likely requirements of H should he decide to acquire a Scania truck.

7. The next stage occurred when S contacted H with a view to inspecting the International Truck and appraising its value with a view to persuading H to invest in a new truck. H consented to the request and expressed the view that such approaches by salesmen were so commonplace as to be an unavoidable incidence of being in the industry. S inspected the truck when it was in the truck workshop of a third-party undergoing maintenance. S said that H advised him that the truck would be available there for inspection of that particular date. I accept in preference to H's assertion that he did not know how S was able to locate the truck in order to carry out his appraisal.

8. The final stage in negotiations came when S arranged by telephone to call on H at his home on the evening of 19 December, 1983. Within the space of some three hours H was persuaded and, with the approval of his late wife agreed - after some haggling over price - to sell the International for $39,203 and to take on lease the Scania which was to be delivered shortly. That was on the night of 19 December 1983. In his evidence H described the decision as being made "in a spirit of madness" and a matter of "Christmas spirit". He also said that what had impressed him was the prospective fuel savings by comparison with the International and the prospect of reduced "down-time". However there was no evidence to suggest that there had been any attempt to verify the representations as to fuel-economies to be achieved with the Scania truck or of any comparative assessment between that class of truck and any other. In summary H's evidence was that in the course of that evening he changed from being a person with no thought of selling the International and acquiring another truck to being a person committing his partnership to the sale of the International at $39,203 and the acquisition on lease of another truck - conforming precisely to a specification formulated by S and his principal without the benefit of any suggestions from H -with the all-up price for the new unit being $103,203.

9. Bearing in mind that the purchase of truck in question had occurred after the Show in early November and on or about 14 December 1983, the critical question for determination is: What was the state of mind of each partner at the date of the acquisition of title to the International on or about 14 December 1983? There was no suggestion in the evidence that the partnership carried on business dealing in trucks so as thereby to be liable for assessment pursuant to Section 25(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act"). That being so the question is whether profit arising will be assessable pursuant to section 25A of the Act which provides:

"(1) The assessable income of a taxpayer shall include profit arising from the sale by the taxpayer of any property acquired by him for the purpose of profit-making by sale,....."

Section 25A is conditioned for its operation upon the purpose of profit-making by sale existing at the date of acquisition of the property in question. That property was of course the International truck. As to that issue of fact the Applicants bear the burden of proof (Section 190(b)). That is not an onerous burden although it may be hard to discharge. It will be satisfied if they persuade the Tribunal as to the facts of the issue on the balance of probabilities. As Mason, J said: in Admin Exploration Pty Limited (in liq) v FC of T 72 ATC 4253 at page 4259:

"That profit-making by sale was not the sole or dominant purpose for acquisition in a given case may be demonstrated by showing that the dominant purpose actuating the acquisition was another and inconsistent purpose, as for example, retention as an investment. So also the taxpayer may show that, although profit-making by sale was a purpose, or a consideration, which actuated the acquisition, it was not the dominant purpose, either because another and inconsistent purpose actuated the acquisition in a equal degree or because before and at the time of acquisition the taxpayer held no firm view as to what he would do with the property and the prospect of its sale at a profit was recognised as no more than a possibility to be later considered with other possibilities which at or before the time of acquisition appeared no less remote."

10. In weighing the evidence I must carefully consider the sworn evidence of H that at the time of acquisition he did not have the relevant purpose but I must assess that evidence in the light of all the surrounding circumstances because it may be that:

"the surrounding circumstances, as an objective matter, make it almost impossible to accept that anyone in the (taxpayer's) position could have approached the matter in the way that he now says he did. (Gillespie-Jones v FC of T 86 ATC 4183 at 4187.)

In weighing the evidence I start without any assumption that the evidence of the taxpayer is prima facie unacceptable. I take guidance particularly from observations of his Honour, Gibbs, C.J., when he said:

(a) 'Although evidence given by a taxpayer as to the purpose with which he acquired property must, for obvious reasons, 'be tested most closely and received with the greatest caution' (Pascoe v FC of T....) it would be wrong for a Judge to regard the evidence of a taxpayer as prima facie unacceptable' (McCormack v FC of T [1979] HCA 18; (1979) 143 CLR 284 at p 301 - 302); and

(b) 'The taxpayer's evidence must, of course, be considered on its merits in the light of the circumstances of the case, without any prepossession, favourable or unfavourable, (ibid. p. 302.)"

11. Having weighed the evidence as to the formation of the decision to acquire the Scania I am not persuaded that the evidence I have heard constitutes a "full and true disclosure". I reject it. But rejecting that evidence does not establish affirmatively that the International was acquired for the dominant purpose of profit-making by sale. Even though I have rejected the evidence of H as to the acquisition of the Scania, it does not automatically follow that the case falls within section 25(A) (cf. ibid, 302). When I come to weigh the evidence I am conscious, as H was, that the market value of the International greatly exceeded the cost of its acquisition: the potential for profit-making was there. However, so too was the possibility of continuing to use the now aging truck - not originally selected as a logging truck - in the logging business. H claimed that it was his firm intention to rebuild the International, but I am not persuaded that that was anything more than a possibility or that it was a possibility more likely to eventuate than the likelihood of acquiring a new truck in the near future. In particular I note that the evidence of the economic feasibility of rebuilding the old truck in preference to the acquisition of a new one was of the most generalised kind only.

12. At the end of the day I am not persuaded that at the date of acquisition the dominant purpose was not to profit-make by sale. My judgment on the matter is that the more probable situation is that at the date of acquisition of the International it was intended to effect a sale of the truck as soon as a suitable opportunity to acquire a replacement truck on acceptable terms arose, with the old unit to be used in the business in the interim.

13. I affirm the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection.
