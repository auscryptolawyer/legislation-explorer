INCOME TAX - Assessable Income - Annuity by way of superannuation pension - Purchase of annuities by taxpayer for himself and his spouse - Undeducted purchase price of annuity - Relevance of greater life expectation of spouse -spouse only contingently entitled.

Income Tax Assessment Act (1936) Ss. 26AA, 27H

Income Tax Assessment Amendment Act (No. 3) (No. 47 of 1984) ss. 2, 12, 15, 61

REASONS FOR DECISION

26 May 1989 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

  1. In March 1984 the Applicant retired from the service of the State of New South Wales. He was then in his 60th year. He was a married man and his wife was then in her 54th year. By reason of superannuation contributions he had made to the State Superannuation Fund over many years, upon his retirement he was entitled to a superannuation pension and, in the event of his death, his widow would be entitled to a superannuation pension. It was agreed that, as at the relevant date:
    • (a) The price he had paid for superannuation benefits, and for which he had not been allowed an income tax deduction, amounted to $64,685;
    • (b) His life expectancy calculated by reference to the prescribed life tables was 16.4 years;
(c) His wife's life expectancy calculated by the same tables was 26.04 years? and

(d) The period of the year ended 30 June 1984 during which he was a superannuant was correctly represented by the fraction
  1. In assessing the taxable income of the Applicant for the year of income ended 30 June 1984 the Commissioner allowed a tax deduction in the sum of $762 calculated by the following formula:
    1. x = $762
  2. The Applicant contends that the correct calculation should have been:
    1. x = $1,210

and that, alternatively, some denominator less than 26.04 should have been used in the calculation.

  1. At the date of retirement of the Applicant the provision of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") which provided for the assessment of annuities was Section 26AA. It provided:

"(1) - The assessable income of the taxpayer shall include the amount of any annuity, excluding, in the case of an annuity which has been purchased, that part of the amount of the annuity which represents the undeducted purchase price."

It went on Sub-section (2) to provide that, in circumstances such as these, the denominator by which to divide the undeducted purchase price of the annuity was to be:

"the number of years in the complete expectation of life of the taxpayer, as ascertained by reference to the prescribed life tables, at the time when the annuity first commenced to be derived."

  1. Subsequently, on 19 May 1983 the Honourable the Treasurer, in his Budget Speech to the House of Representatives, foreshadowed an intention on the part of the Government to submit amending legislation to the Parliament for its consideration. In due course the Parliament adopted Bills which, upon receiving the Royal Assent on 20 June 1984, came to constitute the Income Tax Assessment Amendment Act (No. 3) 1984 (No. 47 of 1984 - "the Amending Act"). The Amending Act provided:
    • (a) By Section 2: that it would come into operation on the day on which it received the Royal assent;
    • (b) By Section 12: that Section 26AA of the Principal Act was repealed;
    • (c) By Section 15: for the insertion after Section 27 of the Principal Act of -

"Subdivision AA - Superannuation, Termination of Employment and Kindred Payments" - a subdivision introducing Section 27H to the Act (cf. post); and

(d) By Section 61 that -

"(1) Notwithstanding the amendments made by Sections 12 and 15, where the period in respect of which an annuity was first payable, commenced before 1 July 1983, Section 26AA of the Principal Act continues to apply in relation to the annuity and, subject to Sub-section 2 of this Section, Section 27H Principal Act as amended by this Act does not apply in relation to the annuity.

By reason of the latter provision, the Applicant was to be denied recourse to the provisions operative at the date of his retirement.

  1. Following 30 June 1984 the Applicant lodged his income tax return with the Commissioner. He returned his annuity as assessable income, but only after excluding so much of the undeducted purchase price as he considered appropriate under the old formula. In essence his claim was that the denominator in the relevant fraction should have reflected his life expectancy; he being the person who had purchased the annuity. However, the Commissioner took the view that, under Subdivision AA of the Amending Act, the relevant denominator was to be the greater of the figure for the life expectancy of the person presently entitled to an annuity or the comparable figure for a person contingently entitled to an annuity upon his death.
  2. In short the issue as posed is whether the denominator to be applied should be that appropriate to the life expectancy of the Applicant as the only purchaser of the annuity and as the only person presently entitled to an annuity; or whether the denominator should be the figure representing the life expectancy of the Applicant's wife as a person contingently entitled to an annuity, being, in addition, a person with a greater life expectancy than the Applicant.
  3. The issue is to be resolved by reference to the provisions of Section 27H (introduced by the Amending Act) which in so far as it material provides:

"27H (1) The assessable income of a taxpayer of a year of income shall include –

(a) the amount of any annuity derived by the taxpayer during the year of income excluding, in the case of an annuity that has been purchased, any amount that, in accordance with sub-section (2) or (3), is the deductible amount in relation to the annuity in relation to the year of income; and

(b) .........

(2) Subject to sub-section (3), the deductible amount in relation to an annuity derived by a taxpayer during a year of income is the amount (if any) ascertained in accordance with the formula, where -

A. ..........

B. is the amount of the undeducted purchase price of the annuity;

C. ..........

D. is the relevant number in relation to the annuity.

(3) Where -

(a) the deductible amount ascertained under sub-section (2) in relation to an annuity derived by the taxpayer during the year of income is calculated by reference to a relevant number ascertained in accordance with paragraph (a) and (b) of the definition of 'relevant number' in sub-section (4); and

(b) the Commissioner is of the opinion that the deductible amount so ascertained is inappropriate having regard to -

(i) the terms and conditions applying to the annuity; and

(ii) such other matters as the Commissioner considers relevant,

the deductible amount in relation to the annuity derived by the taxpayer during the year of income is so much of the annuity as, in the opinion of the Commissioner, represents the undeducted purchase price having regard to -

(c) the terms and conditions applying to the annuity;

(d) any certificate or certificates of an approved actuary or approved actuaries stating the extent to which, in the opinion of the approved actuary or approved actuaries, the amount of the annuity derived by the taxpayer during the year of income represents the undeducted purchase price; and

(e) such other matters as the Commissioner considers relevant.

(4) In this section -

'annuity' ......

'approved actuary' ......

'life expectation factor', in relation to a person in relation to an annuity, means the number of years in the complete expectation of life of the person as ascertained by reference to the prescribed Life Tables at the time when the annuity first commenced to be payable;

'relevant number', in relation to an annuity in relation to a year of income, means -

(a) where the annuity is payable for a term of years certain - the number of years in the term;

(b) where the annuity is payable during the lifetime of a person and not thereafter - the life expectation factor of the person; and

(c) in any other case - the number that the Commissioner considers appropriate having regard to -

(i) the terms and conditions applying to the annuity;

(ii) the number of years in the total period during which the annuity will be, or may reasonably be expected to be, payable;

(iii) any certificate or certificates of an approved actuary or approved actuaries stating the extent to which, in the opinion of the approved actuary or approved actuaries, the amount of the annuity derived by the taxpayer during the year of income represents the undeducted purchase price;

(iv) any change during the year of income in the circumstances that were taken into account in ascertaining the relevant number in relation to the annuity in relation to the immediately preceding year of income; and

(v) such other matters as the Commissioner considers relevant;

'relevant share', in relation to an annuity derived by a taxpayer during a year of income, means -

(a) in a case where the annuity derived by the taxpayer is a share of an annuity (which annuity is in this paragraph referred to as the 'total annuity') payable to the taxpayer and another person or other persons -the fraction ; or

(b) in any other case - the number 1.
  1. The legislation is most difficult of interpretation. In Case U89 (87 A.T.C. 513) I adverted to many of those difficulties. I do not here repeat what I then said but I will ensure that a copy of that decision is provided to the Applicant. It follows that I am not persuaded that the approach to interpretation adopted by the Commissioner in his Income Tax Ruling IT 2157 is correct. Notwithstanding the certitudes expressed and notwithstanding the clarity in all relevant respects of the directions there given, and no matter how admirable a guide the Ruling may be as a record of the directions given by the Commissioner to his officers, and no matter how valuable an aid it may be to the community in understanding just what directions have been so given, it is not an authoritive interpretation of the Act.
  2. But that alone will not entitle the Applicant to succeed. It may be that the correct denominator to be used as the "relevant number" is the number of whole years of the life expectancy of the Applicant. But if that view is taken then it would seem to follow that it is only his annuity which is to be considered as "the annuity"; and that it is only the costs incurred in relation to the purchase of his annuity which should be taken into account in determining the undeducted purchase price. If so, the result will be that the undeducted purchase price must always be a lesser sum than which has been assumed by the parties to be the relevant undeducted purchase price in this case. The simple fact of the matter is that, throughout the period during which he contributed to the superannuation scheme, the Applicant was outlaying monies to secure two contingent entitlements: the first, an annuity to himself should he survive to retiring age; and the other, an annuity to his wife should she survive him.
    1. Whether "the annuity" referred to in the sub-division is to be considered as only the annuity on the life of the Applicant or whether it is to be considered as embracing the contingent annuity which might become payable to his widow, it is clear that Section 27H of the Act provides that the "relevant number" determined by the statutory prescription is the figure to be brought to account as the denominator in the calculation of the deductible amount. If "the annuity" is "payable for a term of years certain", the denominator is to be "the number of years in the term". Similarly, if "the annuity" is payable during the lifetime of a person and not thereafter - a condition which, on one interpretation of "the annuity", may be the present case -the relevant number is the life expectation of the person. Further, "in any other case", it is "the number which the Commissioner considers appropriate".
    2. But S.27H (3) of the Act also provides that in the first two cases, if application of the formula provides a result which the Commissioner considers to be "inappropriate" having regard to "the terms and conditions applying to the annuity; and such other matters as the Commissioner considers relevant" the deductible amount comes to be such other amount as, "in the opinion of the Commissioner" represents the undeducted purchase price.
  3. The analysis indicates that, convenient though it may be to have regard to life expectation factors as measured by the prescribed life tables of persons in relation to an annuity, it is by no means the only matter which may be taken into account.
  4. However, as the evidence stands before me, there was nothing which indicated that the life expectation of either the Applicant or his wife should be considered as anything other than that determinable by reference to the life tables and nothing other than life expectancy which was relevant to the determination called for. As the evidence stands, in relation to each of them, the life tables can be considered as fair an indicator of likely longevity as anything else.
  5. Therefore the issue resolves itself into a question as to whether it is a denominator of 16.4 (the life expectation factor for the husband) or 26.4 (the life expectation factor for the wife) which is an appropriate denominator. If the latter then the Applicant has clearly failed. But, if the former, he has not succeeded either because he has not persuaded me that the amount allowed was inadequate. For if the appropriate denominator is 16.04, the value of the undeducted purchase price relevant to his annuity is something less than $64,685 and, in the absence of further evidence, I am not able to determine what that figure should be.
  6. In those circumstances the determination of the Commissioner upon the objection under review must be affirmed.
