NT 92/112-113 and Commissioner of Taxation [1992] AATA 806; (1992) 24 ATR 1112; 92 ATC 356 (12 October 1992)

Last Updated: 26 May 2009



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION



INCOME TAX - Income tax audit - understated income - company and shareholder assessed on amount of understatement - applicant a shareholder in the company - shareholder's only source of income from activities of company - alleged that the sum understated came from sale of personal goods, moneys in respect of board received from flatmates, and repayment on account of funding of purchase of a fur coat on behalf of the shareholder's mother - onus not discharged - decision affirmed



Income Tax Assessment Act - s.223, s.190, s.25(1), s.6(1), s.108, s.51(1)

REASONS FOR DECISION



12 October 1992 The Hon. Mr Justice Rodney N. PURVIS

(Presidential Member)



THE APPEALS



The appeals which have been heard together arise out of an income tax audit conducted into the affairs of the personal taxpayer C.K. in respect of the year ended 30 June 1989.



The audit was carried out upon a source and application of funds basis and resulted in an apparent understatement of income of $19,409. As the only source of income available to C.K. in the subject year was from the business of N. Holdings Pty Limited, the Commissioner deemed the understated income as having been, in the first instance, that of the company and assessed the company accordingly. Amended assessments were issued. The company was assessed to tax at company rates on the said amount. C.K. was assessed to tax at the personal income tax rate on the said amount. Additional tax pursuant to s.223 of the Income Tax Assessment Act was imposed, the same being remitted as to part.



At the hearing of the appeals, $12,750 of the apparent understated income was put at issue by the taxpayer, it being said by him that such amount of the moneys was not attributable to funds emanating from the business activities of the company, but was received by him, as:



  1. $7800 - a sharing of rent and living expenses with a flatmate or flatmates;

  2. $2500 - sale of water skis, waterbed, refrigerator and washing machine;

  3. $2450 - refund by taxpayer's mother of moneys spent by the taxpayer on her behalf.

It was said on behalf of the Commissioner that initially the perceived understatement had been as high as approximately $30,000, but after extensive discussion and "bargaining with the taxpayer's agent", it had finalised in the amount of $19,409, being a figure that could not, it was said, be "satisfactorily explained".



At the hearing, the taxpayer appeared on his own behalf. The Commissioner was represented by Mr I. Marriott.



OTHER FACTS IN EVIDENCE



As at 30 June 1989, C.K. was the holder of 50 per cent of the shares issued in the capital of N. Holdings Pty Limited. The company carried on the business of a restaurant and nightclub, the income derived from that business being as to approximately 25 per cent from the restaurant and 75 per cent from the sale of liquor. Such income was substantially received in cash, only about 3 per cent being on credit card.



The takings of the business, both as to cash and on credit card, for the relevant financial year averaged $17,500 per week. The restaurant advertised as trading five days a week. It was, however, licensed to open seven days a week and did for special occasions or functions trade on a Sunday. The 1989 year was, according to C.K., "a good year for us".



The holder of the other 50 per cent of the issued shares did, during the 1990 financial year, withdraw from the business. At the time of the hearing, C.K. was the beneficial owner of all of the shares issued in the capital of the company.



C.K. had, at all relevant times, responsibility for the management of the company's business, a keeping of records and the accounting for takings and income. The business was substantially a cash flow business. The taxpayer said that he reconciled the cash with cash register tapes, and would have expected the total of a night's takings to "sometimes" exceed the amount shown on a tape. All of the employees, other than the shareholders, were casual.



The holder of the other interest in the company was, during the 1989 financial year, employed as the chef for the restaurant, and had primary responsibility in this regard. Both shareholders were employed by the company drawing salaries, after tax deductions, of either $350 or $385 each week. C.K. was not able to be more precise in this regard.



STATUTORY PROVISIONS



Notices of Amended Assessment issued in October 1990 against N. Holdings Pty Limited and C.K., assessing the company to tax on a taxable income of $70,880 and C.K. to tax on a taxable income of $76,551. The total amount payable pursuant to the assessment in each case included not only tax on the taxable income, but additional tax for "incorrect returns".



S.190 of the Assessment Act provides that on review before the Administrative Appeals Tribunal, or on appeal to a Court, the burden of proof that the assessment is excessive lies upon the taxpayer.



S.25(1) provides that the assessable income of a taxpayer, whether a company or an individual, includes, where the taxpayer is a resident, the gross income derived directly or indirectly from all sources, whether in or out of Australia. A dividend is defined by s.6(1) of the Act to include any distribution made by a company to any of its shareholders, whether in money or other property. S.108 of the Act provides that if a private company pays an amount to an associated person by way of an advance or loan, or pays or credits an amount on behalf of or for the individual benefit of an associated person, so much of the amount paid or credited, as in the opinion of the Commissioner, represents a distribution of profits, shall be deemed to be a dividend paid by the company to the associated person as a shareholder in the company out of profits derived by the company and on the last day of the year of income of the company in which the payment or credit so referred to is made. For the purposes of s.108, a transfer of property is deemed to be the payment of an amount equal to the value of the property. A reference to an associated person in relation to a company is a reference to inter alia a shareholder in the company.



ALLEGED SOURCES OF THE CONTENDED UNDERSTATED INCOME



As to the alleged board or rental contribution - $7800



The taxpayer, in his Notice of Objection, stated that he was unable to substantiate the receipt of the board money. "However, should the Taxation Office insist on treating the receipt of the income as assessable under s.25(1) of the Act, the "matching principle" should be applied and a deduction to the full amount of $7800 be allowed in accordance with s.51(1) of the Act. Hence net income would be nil."



In the view of the Tribunal, this statement confuses the issue. The matter for determination is as to whether the taxpayer can satisfy the decision-maker and hence the Tribunal as to the moneys allegedly on account of board being received by C.K. during the year of income, this in an amount of $7800. It is not relevant to know on what account he might have spent the money, assuming he received it.



The taxpayer said, in his written submissions to the Tribunal in relation to this contention:



"My flatmates (names have previously been provided) shared the rent, food and telephone costs which averaged out at $7800 over the year. The lease agreement was in my name and the original lease indicated at all times two people would be living there. I was responsible for the lease expenses."



In his oral evidence, C.K. named two ladies, Alexandria and Isabella, as having flatted with him and shared rent. When cross-examined in relation to the various statements, he said that the amount of $7800 had been computed by multiplying the amount of $150 a week by 52 weeks. He had not kept any records of moneys received from alleged flatmates. He did say that he moved from rented accommodation at Miranda into a home that he had built at Gymea in March 1989 and had not shared accommodation from that month onwards.



He said that at this time, his wages from the company were $350 per week, although this was later varied to $385 per week, and said that the difference between this amount and amounts banked to the credit of his cheque account with the State Bank at Sutherland was attributable to moneys received from his flatmates. When his attention was drawn, however, to deposits made in April, May and June 1989, which, omitting amounts received on account of director's fees, averaged $760 a week, he was unable to explain the difference between this amount and his salary. He had said the difference was rent or an amount on account of shared accommodation, but also that there were not any flatmates with him during the April to June period.



An officer of the respondent involved in the investigation called to give evidence said that C.K. had informed him of having had up to two flatmates at any one time, that they were not the same ones over the relevant year, and that he was not able to provide names. He did later provide some names in writing. With reference to the ladies, "Alexandria -" and "Isabella -", the officer was not able to locate a record of existence of these ladies, on the records of the respondent, as taxpayers.



It was submitted on behalf of the Commissioner that the taxpayer had not discharged the onus resting on him of showing that he had, during the 1989 financial year, received $7800 on account of board, and/or part reimbursement of expenses, nor had he established that he had received any amount on this account during the subject year which should be taken into consideration in reduction of the said understated income. The contradictions in evidence of the taxpayer were sufficient to justify the Tribunal in not accepting the statement of the taxpayer in this regard.



I consider that there is substance in the submission so made on behalf of the Commissioner, and I am satisfied that the taxpayer has not discharged the onus resting upon him so far as the amount alleged to have been received by him on account of board was concerned.



As to the sale of water skis, bed, refrigerator and washing machine - $2500



The taxpayer, in his Notice of Objection, stated:



"The sale of these items are of a purely capital nature and hence are not assessable under s.25(1) of the Act. As these items are non listed personal use assets and consideration received for these items for less than $5000 each, there are no consequences of the acquisition and disposal of the assets for the purposes of Part IIIA (re Section 160ZG and 160ZQ)."



Clearly, if moneys obtained from the sale of these items of personalty had been received by C.K. and banked to the credit of his bank account, or even not so banked, no question would have arisen as to the amounts being assessable to tax. The sales would have been of items of a personal nature.



However, the taxpayer says that these moneys were received by him and that the receipt explains in part the contended understated income. As with the board or sharing of expenses, so with this statement. The onus is on the taxpayer to satisfy the decision-maker, and now the Tribunal, that these items were sold and the proceeds received by C.K. in the relevant year of income and are to be taken into account in explaining the contended understated income.



The taxpayer stated in his written material that "the sale of water bed, fridges and washing machine for $1600 ..... were paid in cash ..... the sale of water skis of $900 was sold for cash from an advertisement in the Sydney Morning Herald".



In his evidence-in-chief, C.K. said that the water bed was sold to an employee of the restaurant and that he received $1000 in cash for it. He kept the cash and used it to buy things for his home. As to the refrigerator and washing machine, he said that these items were also sold to an employee for $600 cash, and that the moneys were used to buy things for his home. The water skis were sold for $900, the money, he said, so received was banked to his account with the State Bank at Sutherland. He did not identify the deposit in the pass sheets referable to the account.



Later in his evidence, when under cross-examination, C.K. said that the water bed so sold was ten years old, and that he had received $1000 from "a friend". As to the refrigerator, this, he said, was two or three years old when sold to "a personal friend", the washing machine being sold to the same person. No one of the persons to whom he said he had sold these items, including the water skis, was called to give evidence or provided a statement corroborating the contention.



There is an inherent unlikelihood of a ten year old water bed being sold for $1000, even be it to a "friend" or as C.K. said, to a "friend employee. The taxpayer was not able to further identify the moneys by reference to the use to which they were put, assuming that they had been received by him. There is not any evidence as to such amounts being referable in whole or in part to his understand income.



The Tribunal does not, on the basis of the above evidence only, consider that the taxpayer has discharged the onus resting upon him of showing that the amount of $2500 allegedly received as abovementioned should be included as part of the contended understated income.



As to the fur coat - reimbursement by taxpayer's mother - $2450



The taxpayer, in his Notice of Objection, stated:



"Evidence was produced to the Taxation Office ..... which confirmed the payment of $2450 on behalf of the taxpayer's mother, as well as evidence which confirmed the reimbursement for the fur coat by the taxpayer's mother. The latter form of evidence was that of a copy of the taxpayer's mother's savings passbook showing a withdrawal of money. Hence the expenditure is neither deductible under s.51(1) of the Act or assessable under s.25(1) of the Act."



There is no question of the "expenditure" being deductible. Either the money was received by the taxpayer and formed part of the understand income, as he says, or it was not. The onus is on the taxpayer.



The taxpayer stated in his written material:



"At this time, my mother and stepfather were residing in Queensland. I was taking holidays at the time, my mother asked to pay off the lay-by in Sydney and bring the fur coat with me on my visit to Queensland.



The money withdrawn from my mother's savings account was paid to me in cash ..... I now present to you:



(1) invoice from Cornelius Furs

(2) receipt " " "

(3) copy of mother's savings account."



Indeed, C.K. did produce to the Tribunal, the same being admitted into evidence, documents emanating from Cornelius and a photocopy of one page of a savings bank passbook in the name of the taxpayer's mother. The material from Cornelius showed that the mother had in June 1988 paid $300 to Cornelius on account of the purchase of a pastel mink. There was owing on account of the purchase then $2450, which amount was paid to that firm on 24 August 1988. On 6 September, there was withdrawn from the savings account of the mother an amount of $2000, and on 9 September 1988, there was withdrawn an amount of $500. The taxpayer said that the amount of $2450 was paid to Cornelius by way of a charge on his American Express account, and that the withdrawal of $2000 and $500 was in aid of repaying to him the amount charged to such credit card.



The taxpayer said in-chief that he received $2450 in cash from his mother, and that this money was banked to the credit of his account with the State Bank, Sutherland, this in September 1988. During the course of his cross-examination, the taxpayer said that he arranged with Cornelius to pick up the coat, that he did so, took it to Queensland, and that his mother then withdrew the two amounts from her savings account and handed the money to him. He was not able to say how it was that she was able to withdraw these moneys from her account which was maintained at North Parramatta when she was in Queensland, but he did say that the moneys so received by him were banked to his cheque account and represented part of the understated income. The taxpayer was not able to say if he banked the money to his account whilst he was in Queensland, or whether he brought it back with him to Sydney. He was not able to identify any deposit to the credit of his account which was inclusive of the moneys received by him allegedly from his mother.



The evidence of the taxpayer in relation to this alleged source is not satisfactory. At one time, he said that his mother paid $300 in cash and that he presented his credit card at that time in satisfaction of the balance owing. The documentation of Cornelius does not show this to have been so, but rather that a period of two months elapsed between the date on which the deposit was paid and the date on which the balance owing was paid. The taxpayer said that he was reimbursed $2450, yet the banking records produced show that withdrawals were made in two amounts on two days three days apart from each other.



The Tribunal is not satisfied that if moneys were received by the taxpayer from his mother that they were banked as alleged by him and formed part of the contended understated income. The taxpayer was not able to say where the money had been banked into his account, and there was no identifiable deposit referable to money said to have been received from the mother. The Tribunal in unable to find that the account of the transaction as given by C.K. is true.



Accordingly, on the basis of the findings above made, and for the reasons given, the Tribunal is not able to accept the explanation given by C.K. to the effect that the understated income did not emanate from the business of the company but from the three sources as alleged by him.



As to the company



In its Notice of Objection forwarded by the taxpayer company's accountants, it was stated:



"As stated in our letter of objection on behalf of the abovementioned taxpayer, we believe that the items pertaining to the "understatement" are of a personal nature and thus have no relevance to company income. Hence it is a most unjust form of double taxation and is contrary to the spirit of the legislation. As referred to [J.K's] Notice of Objection, the source of funds were identified but not substantiated in accordance with the substantiation rules. However, this does not warrant the income being deemed to be income of the company and has no relevance under s.108 of the Income Tax Assessment Act in relation to [C.K.] and s.25(1) of the Act in relation to the company."



It is clear that if the taxpayer has not discharged the onus resting upon him that the understatement is not referable to income of a personal nature, or at least, has not been established as so being. Hence the understatement can only be of income of a business nature, and hence a use by the taxpayer of moneys of the company which were not disclosed by it as income or included in its income as returned. Although the taxpayer contended that the issue of the two amended assessments means "there has been double taxation", it is quite proper, in the view of the Tribunal, for the Commissioner to bring into account the non-disclosed or understated income as income of the company and issue an amended assessment in relation to such income. The taxpayer received the cash "income", property of the company, and applied it to his own use.



The Commissioner was acting properly in regarding the same as having been received by C.K. on account of dividend income, such not being a deductible item so far as the company is concerned. There is no question of a gift, a loan, an advance or an amount received on account of capital. The taxpayer has not discharged the onus resting upon him in relation to the three amounts totalling $12,750, nor that these moneys were other than income of the company, its property, which were appropriated by the taxpayer to his own use. This is clearly a case where cash income of the company was obtained by the taxpayer, a shareholder, and used by him. Thus, the actions of C.K. involved a distribution by the company of income that would otherwise have formed a part of its assessable income.



The taxpayer has failed to discharge the onus of proving the assessments are excessive and that the understand income was not income of the company which he had appropriated to his own use.



The taxpayer has not discharged the onus resting upon him of proving that the assessments are excessive.



PENALTY



Whilst in the Notices of Objection mention was made of additional tax imposed as to $3470.24 so far as C.K. was concerned, and $1734.17 so far as the company was concerned, no further evidence was adduced and no submissions were made by the taxpayer orally or in writing as to why the same should be varied. The Notice of Objection of the company had stated inter alia:



"The taxpayer objects against the penalties as being excessive and requests that the penalties imposed should be reviewed with specific reference to the culpability component. We feel that these penalties are to the extreme excessive, and that is only lack of awareness by the taxpayer that the substantiation provided was minimal."



and:



"We request that the culpability component of the penalties for the additional tax to be waived ..... on the basis that there was no evasion of income tax and that the capital expenditure was written off previously as the taxpayer "did not know and could not be expected to know" the fine line between repairs and capital expenditure, which is the subject of many disputes under the Act."



The Commissioner stated that the taxpayers became liable by reason of the Act to pay additional tax in terms of s.223, part of the same was remitted, and the circumstances did not warrant any further remission. The evidence before the Tribunal is such as to show that there was not any "lack of awareness by the taxpayer that the substantiation provided was minimal". The taxpayer was simply unable to satisfy the Tribunal as to the three amounts being part of the understatement of income. Again, the Tribunal is not satisfied that there was not an evasion of income tax. There is and was no question of "capital expenditure" being written off.



Accordingly, in the view of the Tribunal, there is not any basis, on the material before it, justifying a variation of the decision of the Commissioner so far as penalty is concerned.



DECISION



The taxpayer has not satisfied the onus resting upon him. He has not established that the understatement of income was attributable to any or all of the alleged moneys being in fact received by him.



Accordingly, the decisions under review are affirmed.
