Re Jeanette Margaret Kelly and Commissioner of Superannuation [1988] AATA 144 (6 May 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: JEANETTE MARGARET KELLY

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. A87/79

AAT No. 4319

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.K. Todd (Deputy President)

Dr. D.J. Howell (Member)

N.J. Attwood (Member)

CATCHWORDS

HEARING

CANBERRA

6:5:1988

ORDER

The Tribunal varies the decision under review by deleting therefrom the conditions therein specified and substituting therefor the conditions "degenerative disc disease" and "intervertebral disc lesion". The Tribunal otherwise affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

This is the review of a decision, made under delegation, in respect of the abovenamed applicant pursuant to s.154(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") to:

" (i) Vary the decision taken by a delegate of the

Commissioner on 15 July 1986 to issue

pursuant to s.16(11) of the Act a benefit

classification certificate specifying the

conditions "Intravertebral Disc Lesion" and

"History of Right-Sided Sciatica" by deleting

both conditions and substituting the

condition "History of Disc Disorder";

(ii) Confirm the decision, also taken by the

delegate of the Commissioner on 15 July 1986,

to be of the opinion under s.66(2)(c) of the

Act that the incapacity which was the ground

for (the applicant's) retirement was caused,

or was substantially contributed to by a

condition or conditions specified in the

benefit classification certificate or by a

condition or conditions connected with such a

condition or such conditions."

2. The essential factual basis underlying this application is hardly in dispute, and may be based initially on the statement lodged by the respondent pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act").

Entry to the Superannuation Scheme

3. The applicant was born on 19 December 1933 and became an eligible employee on 28 August 1978 following her permanent appointment as a telephonist with Telecom Australia ("Telecom"). She had been working continuously, but on a temporary basis, with Telecom since 7 May 1973. She underwent a medical examination, pursuant to s.16(2) of the Act, by a Commonwealth Medical Officer ("CMO"), Dr. A. Dunnett on 18 September 1978 and the report of that examination was duly furnished to the Commissioner.

4. In providing details of her personal medical history in connection with that examination the applicant answered "no" to the following questions:

"Are you now suffering or have you ever suffered

from any of the following disabilities -

...

1(j) Disturbance of sensation or muscular

activity

...

5(a) Lumbago, paralysis or disorder of the limbs

or spine

..."

5. The applicant did not provide any answer to the

question:

"12(b) Have you had any complaints, illness or

injury not mentioned above?"

6. Under heading "C" however ("Details of Yes Answers by Applicant and CMO's Comments") certain complaints were filled in on the form, including appendicitis when the applicant was less than 2 years old. None however related to past problems with her back.

7. On the basis of his examination, including the applicant's disclosures, Dr. Dunnett was of the opinion that the applicant met the general medical standard ("GMS"). Dr. H.S. Turner oversaw the report and as adviser to the Commissioner indicated that she also considered that the applicant met the GMS.

Events leading to retirement

8. On 26 May 1982, the applicant was examined by Dr. J.E. Knight, CMO, for the purpose of assessing her fitness for continued duty. Dr. Knight indicated that he considered that the applicant was incapacitated as follows:

"Laminectomy 100%

following IVD (intervertebral

disc) lesion".

Dr. Knight commented:

"Developed acute pain in R. hip on way to work in

early January 1982. Spent 2 weeks in bed at home

from 29/1/82. Laminectomy performed by Dr Stubbs

on 22/2/82.

3/12 later on 26/5/82 still has much difficulty in

walking. All movements restricted. Has been on

physiotherapy which is not improving (the)

condition.

(Prognosis) Unlikely to improve."

9. Dr. Knight indicated that he considered that the applicant was unfit for continued employment and should be retired on the ground of invalidity.

10. In a letter dated 3 September 1982, Dr. G. Stubbs, an orthopaedic surgeon, reported to Telecom Australia on the applicant's condition as follows:

"Mrs. Kelly first came under my care at the Woden

Hospital on 10.2.82 at the request of her general

practitioner Dr. Marinos. She was then suffering

from lumbago and right sciatica - the sciatica had

been present for some four to five months and had

first commenced after a long car trip in a

four-wheel drive over outback roads.

She had struggled on with this mild level of

symptoms until three weeks prior to her admission

when the pain had become much worse. On

examination on admission there was a diminished

right ankle jerk consistent with a classic right S1

nerve root entrapment.

Initial management was by an epidural steroid

injection with only partial benefit. A lumbar

radiculogram was performed which confirmed the L5

S1 intravertebral disc prolapse and on the 22.2.82.

the patient was taken to theatre where a

fenestration laminectomy was performed. A

sequestrated piece of intravertebral disc material

1 cubic centimetre in capacity was removed from the

S1 nerve root and a further 4 c.c.'s of nuclear

material was removed from the remainder of the

Intravertebral disc.

There was a gratifying relief of the sciatica

immediately post-operatively. Since then Mrs

Kelly's progress has been fairly satisfactory - on

the 2nd March 1982 the wound was cleaned and the

sutures removed and a flexion exercise regime was

started. Her progress was complicated however

when last seen in June 1982. She had been

admitted to a private hospital in Sydney for a

breast biopsy and had suffered a recurrence of her

lumbago, but not sciatica, following this

procedure. On the 2nd June 1982 she was free

from sciatica but had persisting low back pain in

the left sacro-iliac joint region. The pain came

and went and was increased by arising from sitting,

bending and lifting and was eased if she was warm.

Examination showed that she was stiff, straight leg

raising was good - 70 degrees right equals left

with a negative traction sign, but the ankle jerk

had not returned. It was thought then that Mrs

Kelly may be suffering from some lumbar segmental

instability and treatment with Indomethacin was

commenced.

I have not seen Mrs Kelly since and I cannot

therefore comment on her present situation; it was

however expected that the lumbago would be

controlled with exercise and medication and I did

not at that time see any need for her to stop

working."

11. After the events described in Dr. Stubbs' report a series of medical examinations and reports in relation thereto followed in which varying opinions were expressed in relation to the fitness of the applicant to resume work (T2/5-12), but ultimately a delegate of Telecom, after an evaluation of the applicant's medical condition and having decided that a suitable position was not available for the applicant's continued employment, on 13 November 1985 approved, pursuant to s.56(1)(c) of the Telecommunications Act 1975, the invalidity retirement of the applicant due to the conditions "Chronic low back pain Right Sciatica".

The Legislation

12. The provisions of s.16(11) of the Act were amended by Act No.151 of 1986, but at the time of the delegate's decision, s.16(11) was in the following terms:

"(11) Where -

(a) a person ceases to be an eligible employee by

reason of death or retirement on the ground

of invalidity;

(b) his period of contributory service is less

than 20 years and, on the day on which he

ceases to be an eligible employee, he has not

attained his maximum retiring age;

(c) a benefit classification certificate is not

in force in respect of the person immediately

before his death or retirement or, if a

benefit classification certificate is in

force in respect of him at that time, the

Commissioner is not of the opinion that the

death or incapacity which was the ground for

his retirement was caused, or substantially

contributed to, by a physical or mental

condition or conditions of the person

specified in the certificate, or by a

physical or mental condition or conditions

connected with such a condition or such

conditions; and

(d) the Commissioner is satisfied -

(i) that, at or in connexion with a

medical examination which the person

was required to undergo under this

section, the person failed to

furnish any information required to

be furnished by him or furnished

false information; and

(ii) that, if the person had not failed

to furnish that information or had

not furnished that false

information, there would have been

in force in respect of the person,

immediately before his death or

retirement, a benefit classification

certificate in which there would

have been specified the physical or

mental condition or conditions which

caused, or substantially contributed

to, the death or retirement, or a

physical or mental condition or

conditions which caused, or

substantially contributed to, the

death or retirement, or a physical

or mental condition or conditions

connected with such a condition or

such conditions,

the Commissioner shall issue in respect of the

person a benefit classification certificate in

which there is or are specified the physical or

mental condition or conditions of the person

which, in the opinion of the Commissioner -

(e) in a case where a benefit classification

certificate is in force in respect of the

person at the time he dies or retires - would

have been the physical or mental condition or

conditions of the person specified in the

certificate at that time; or

...

if the person had not failed to furnish that

information or had not furnished that false

information and, for the purposes of this Act, the

certificate shall be deemed to have been in force

in respect of the person immediately before his

death or retirement."

13. For s.16(11) to be applicable in the applicant's case the decision-maker needed to be satisfied of the following matters:

(i) that the applicant was an eligible employee

immediately before her retirement on 24

January 1985 (s.16(11)(a) of the Act);

(ii) that the applicant ceased to be an eligible

employee by reason of invalidity retirement

(s.16(11)(a));

(iii) that the applicant's period of contributory

service was less than 20 years

(s.16(11)(b));

(iv) that on retirement the applicant had not

attained the age of 65 years (s.16(11)(b));

(v) that either a benefit classification

certificate ("BCC") was not in force in

respect of the applicant immediately before

her retirement or, if one was in force, it

did not specify a condition or conditions

related to her retirement incapacity

(s.16(11)(c));

(iv) that at or in connection with the entry

medical examination held on 18 September

1978, the applicant had failed to furnish

information which she was required to

furnish, particularly with reference to the

medical questionnaire and questions put to

her by the examining Commonwealth Medical

Officer;

(vii) that if that information had been furnished

as required then a benefit classification

certificate would have been in force in

respect of the applicant immediately before

her retirement;

(viii) that the condition(s) specified on such BCC

either -

. caused the applicant's incapacity on

retirement; or

. substantially contributed to her

incapacity on retirement; or

. was/were connected with another

condition(s) which in turn caused her

incapacity on retirement; or

. was/were connected with another

condition(s) which in turn

substantially contributed to her

incapacity on retirement.

14. Documents on the respondent's file were said by the delegate to indicate that the applicant's case met the requirements of the legislation in regard to criteria (i) to (v) above, that is to say the applicant ceased to be an eligible employee at age 51 by reason of invalidity retirement on 24 January 1985 after 6 years of contributory service, and there was no BCC in force in respect of Mrs. Kelly immediately before her retirement. With all of this we agree. It is at the point of consideration of criterion (iv) that the difficulties commence.

15. Criterion (iv) - Failure to furnish information required to be furnished or furnishing false information (s.16(11)(d)(i) of the Act.

16. What were the facts in relation to a history of back problems?

(i) In about 1960 the applicant, then living at

Canowindra, "twisted" her back while

sweeping the verandah. She had pain below

the belt line, and at the base of the hips,

with a numbing sensation. She saw a local

doctor who performed a form of manipulation

or stretching which, while apparently quite

unusual, was seemingly successful.

(ii) The applicant had no further trouble until

1974, some two years after she moved to

Canberra. In June of that year her leave

records show her as having one day off with

"arthritis", but the applicant said that she

recalled neither having symptoms of

arthritis nor having one day off. On 27

August 1974 however she saw her general

practitioner, Dr. Marinos. His notes

(Ex.3) indicate that he was given a history

of two months pain in the lateral right hip,

thigh and leg, and a left side backache.

The applicant told him of the 1960 incident

and of the doctor at that time saying that

it might have been a slipped disc. On 11

September 1974 she saw Dr. Marinos again

still with pain in the right hip, but X-rays

had shown no abnormality. Physiotherapy

was recommended.

(iii) On 2 June 1975 the applicant saw Dr. Marinos

again. His notes report lumbar backache

which had started at work on 30 May, and

reference was again made in his notes to a

slipped disc 15 years previously. The

applicant had one day off work.

(iv) On 6 August 1976 the applicant attempted to

catch a falling cupboard and said that she

jarred her back. Prior to this she said

that she had on occasions been experiencing

pain in her lower back. She had gone to

casualty at the Woden Valley Hospital on 9

August 1976 and on 11 August she went again

to Dr. Marinos. She was given some

pain-killers and was off work for a week.

Dr. Marinos inter alia noted pain in the

right lumbosacral region. He mentioned in

his notes "lumbosacral disc lesion", and in

his oral evidence he said that that was his

diagnosis at that point. He then wrote:

"Rest this week

WVH physio and exercises/Discussed++

Digesic/Mogadon"

In evidence Dr. Marinos said that the words

"Discussed ++" indicated that "I think

probably I would have a reasonable

discussion with the patient concerning what

I thought the problem was and what the cause

of the problem was". For the applicant it

was observed that he had written the

expression "Discussed ++" adjacent to the

treatment prescribed by him and not

alongside the diagnosis of "lumbosacral disc

lesion" recorded by him.

(v) On 29 September 1977 the applicant again saw

Dr. Marinos with low back pain which had

come on after loading furniture at her son's

house. Dr. Marinos noted that she was

"off tomorrow then has rostered off for 3

days". He prescribed Digesic, ray lamp and

back exercises. She was off work for one

day.

17. This then being the applicant's history, the essential features of the medical evidence as given at the hearing were as follows, Dr. Marinos and Dr. Stubbs having been called to give evidence for the applicant, and Dr. Andrews having been called for the respondent:

(i) Dr. Marinos' opinion was less clear than that of Dr. Andrews or Dr. Stubbs, but in August 1976 his notes record his diagnosis at that time as having been "lumbosacral disc lesion". His overall view as expressed in his reports and in his oral evidence was simply that the applicant had a "chronic lumbar disc problem". The important point is that, as expressed in his report of 24 September 1986, his opinion was that "...in view of the consistency of this woman's complaints of low back pain and right-sided sciatica both prior to 1978 and following September 1978 that it is most likely and probable that the underlying disc problem was the same".

(ii) Dr. Andrews' opinion, based on the history he was given, in particular from a reading of Dr. Marinos' report of 27 September 1985, was that the applicant had "probably had a ruptured L5/S1 disc dating from that time", meaning, as he explained in oral evidence, from August 1974. He added in his report (T8/134): "I would say her retirement has largely resulted from a pre-existing L5/S1 disc prolapse going right back to 1974". In his oral evidence, Dr. Andrews said that his opinion that there was an actual rupture back in 1974, and not just a bulging of the disc which he said is asymptomatic in most cases, was that it was unusual for a bulging disc to cause the sciatic pain which the applicant had had. He added that it was possible to have a bulging disc that would settle down and remain asymptomatic for long periods.

(iii) The opinion of Dr. Stubbs, an orthopaedic surgeon, was clearly expressed. It was Dr. Stubbs who had operated on the applicant in 1982, and at that operation he had found a sequestrated piece of disc material pressing on the right S1 nerve root. He was for himself quite certain that there was no such sequestrated piece of disc material in her spinal canal prior to this time. He thought that the sequestration was an "unforeseen and unusual circumstance and not the normal progression of her pre-existing back problems." This view, as expressed in his report of 30 September 1986, seems to have been rather coloured by a belief that the applicant's sciatica commenced in August 1981, whereas it is clear from Dr. Marinos' notes that such condition was present from at least 1975. His report also tended to underestimate the number of consultations in respect of, and the amount of sick leave connected with, the applicant's back problems in 1974-1977. But for present purposes it is important, and indeed enough, to note:

(a) That Dr. Stubbs agreed that Dr. Marinos'

diagnosis as expressed in 1976 of "lumbosacral

disc lesion" was a reasonable presumptive

diagnosis to make at that point, which he

himself would have made as an identification of

"the place to start looking for back problems if

they became serious enough to warrant further

treatment";

(b) That there was underlying degenerative disc

disease prior to 1981 "because in a normal disc

it is extremely uncommon to see a disc injury

occur in a completely healthy normal

intervertebral disc". We accordingly

understood Dr. Stubbs to have agreed that the

four-wheel drive trip that preceded the 1981

lesion (as he understood it to have been) was an

aggravation of disc disease and not the author

simpliciter of the disc lesion.

18. At the hearing, Mr. Whithear abandoned any claim that the applicant had furnished false information. But with this history it seems to us almost axiomatic that the conclusion follows that the applicant failed, at or in connection with her medical examination on 18 September 1978, to furnish information required to be furnished by her. Her counsel, in a full and careful submission, relied on Fry v Commissioner for Superannuation (1984) 6 ALD 229 and argued that the applicant satisfied s.16(11)(d)(i) of the Act if she answered the questions truthfully according to her actual knowledge and state of mind. This, it was said, she had done.

19. The difficulty with this argument is however exposed when the applicant's answers to questions asked in the course of her evidence are set out. It will be recalled that the question asked was: "Are you suffering or have you ever suffered from any of the following disabilities - ...". (underlining supplied) The passages are:

In examination-in-chief:

Q.: "Can you tell the Tribunal what you thought

in 1978 was meant by the question,

'Disturbance of Sensation?'---A.: "Well, I

did not have any sensations at the time

when I was at the medical officer at all."

Q.: "I am asking you to say what you understood

by the expression 'Disturbance of

Sensation'?"---A.: "Well, I would say

numbness or cramps, or things like that, I

should imagine it would mean."

Q.: "All right. Do you think that was

different to 'or muscular activity' that is

also part of that question?"---A.: "Well, I

did not have any disturbance of muscular

activity at the time."

Under cross-examination:

Q.: "Now, why did not you - considering you

have said that the 1960 incident involved a

numbing sensation, why did not you answer

yes to that question, 1J - 'Disturbance of

sensation or muscular activity?'"---A.:

"Because I never thought about it, I

suppose."

Q.: "When you were asked what you thought

disturbance of sensation meant, you said

'numbness or cramps'?"---A.: "Yes, that is

right."

Q.: "So, therefore, if you suffered from

numbness?"---A.: "Yes, but at the time I

had this I did not suffer from numbness or

anything."

Q.: "So, the reason you said 'no' was because

you did not suffer from it at the time you

did the examination?"---A.: "I did not have

any of that (sic) symptoms at the time I

did the examination."

Q.: "What do (you) think the words 'ever

suffered from' meant. The first part of

the question says, 'Are you now suffering,

which is when you are doing the examination

or filling in the questionnaire. Then

there are the additional words, 'have you

ever suffered from'?"---A.: Well, I did not

do it intentionally, I can assure you."

Q.: "...If I could now turn over ... to

...question 12B, 'Have you had any

complaints, illness, or injury not

mentioned above', and you have mentioned

appendicitis further down in Part C, and

operation on left breast. Now, you

disclosed those two problems, why did not

you disclose back pains?"---A.: "Well, I

did not have it at the time."

Q.: "The question still remains, why did not

you make reference to your back

problem?"---A.: "Well, I had not ... had

surgical treatments for it at that time."

Q.: " Have you had any complaints?"---A.:

"Well, at the time I did not think that

they were anything to be worried about."

Q.: "So, that was your view that your back

problems had resolved, had they, at that

time?"---A.: "I did not think they were

going to come against me, let us put it

that way."

Q.: "...Well, what about in 1974, what about

your back problem in 1974?"---A.: "Well, it

still had not had surgery or anything."

Q.: "But that is question 12(a). What do you

understand to be a complaint?"---A.: "Well,

a complaint, I thought, would be an

operation or something like that, and I had

not had it at that time."

Q.: "Well, why did not you provide details of

these incidents in much the same way as you

provided details of the operation of the

appendicitis?"--- A.: "Well, an operation

is something that you have got a scar from,

this other has not got anything.

In Re-examination:

Q.: "Can you tell the (Tribunal) what your

understanding of disability was in

1978"---A.: "Disabilities as far as I was

concerned that you cannot get out of bed;

that you cannot tie your shoes; or you

cannot do anything."

20. The applicant had had four significant consultations in relation to her back, in 1974, 1975, 1976 and 1977, not to mention the much earlier incident in 1960. She appears from her own evidence to have concluded that it was unnecessary to refer to any past history that was currently asymptomatic at the time of her examination, although she was happy to record complaints etc. that were irrelevant to her future capacity to work. We consider that she must be regarded as having simply ignored a significant portion of the questions that she was required to answer. We are of opinion that the principle stated in Fry's case and set out in paragraph 12 above cannot be held to be applicable in the case of a failure to disclose a repeated history of a significant and relevant condition, a failure which, however honest it may have been, was based on a construction of the question asked that was in our opinion as deliberate as it was self-serving. The applicant's answers as set out above demonstrate to us not that she misunderstood the question but rather that she was prepared to, and did, decide to put her own incorrect construction on it. Without, with respect, wishing to appear to approve or to disapprove what was said in para 34 of the Reasons for Decision in Re Sosnowski and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision No.3515, 29 May 1987), we are of opinion that the applicant's attitude cannot be characterised as a "genuine misunderstanding". We were given no idea as to why the applicant construed the questions as being related only to a currently active condition. She rather simply asserted, in the passages set out above, the untenable, as it seems to us, construction which she had adopted.

21. Mr. Lucas, for the applicant, contended that the word "disability" as used in the questionnaire had the connotation of being something that prevented, or had in the past prevented, the applicant from working. In this sense, it would not be requisite that she refer to anything that had not prevented her from working. The applicant had not so understood the question, since she referred to past histories that had not had such an effect. But the question remains as to what is the true construction of the word. Mr. Lucas' argument is supported by meanings given in the Random House Dictionary of the English Language and in the Shorter Oxford Dictionary. In Random House it is: "1. lack of competent power, strength, or physical or mental ability; incapacity. 2. a permanent physical flaw, weakness, or handicap, which prevents one from living a full, normal life or from performing any specific job. 3. the state or condition of being disabled." But while the word has this meaning in vacuo, as used in the questionnaire, we do not consider that it can be given that meaning as used in the questionnaire. There are conditions referred to which the questionnaire clearly requires to be revealed, which while capable of leading to incapacity, need not do so, and yet the questionnaire regards them as disabilities, for example: dyspepsia; diabetes; skin eruption or rash; hay fever or allergic rhinitis; dermatitis or eczema. And 12(b) asks whether the applicant has had "any complaints, illness or injury not mentioned above", equating, as it seems to us, "complaints, illness or injury" to disability. We agree that "disability in the context refers to anything which interferes with the ordinary functions of the human body in the ordinary course of life. But even if we were wrong in this, and Mr. Lucas' submission found correct, it is enough to say that the applicant's sick leave records (Ex.5) show that the applicant's back problems had led to absences from work and that she had been subject to disability in the sense of that word as contended for by Mr. Lucas.

22. With this background, the specific questions which the applicant should in our opinion have answered were:

(i) Q.(j):"Disturbance of sensation or muscular activity"

To this the answer should have been affirmative, with reference being made to

(a) the 1974 symptoms and consultations

(b) the June 1975 symptoms and consultation

(c) the August 1976 symptoms and consultations

(d) the September 1977 symptoms and consultation.

23. We consider that what Mr. Whithear for the respondent described as "the picture of continuing treatment" constituted by (a), (b), (c) and (d) above should have been revealed by the applicant, and that the failure to do so constituted a relevant failure to furnish information. The 1960 incident, being so much further back, perhaps requires a less clear conclusion, but the applicant has managed to remember it clearly enough at later times, and on balance we consider that this incident too should have been disclosed.

(ii) Q.5(a): "Lumbago, paralysis or disorder of the

limbs or spine"

As to lumbago, while Dr. Marinos said that he had

mentioned lumbago to the applicant in June 1975, her

evidence was that she only discovered what lumbago was

shortly before the hearing. We do not consider that

the applicant should be held to have failed to furnish

information on this ground.

As to "disorder of the limbs or spine", the applicant

should in our view have answered affirmatively and we

refer to our comments in relation to Q.1(j).

As to Q.12 "Any complaints, illness or injury not

mentioned above?" it is simply noteworthy that here the

applicant did not apply what may be called her test of

"currently active condition". She revealed a number of

past but irrelevant conditions. Mr. Whithear observed

as to this, and we agree, that s.16(11) cannot be held

to embody a test whereunder an examinee may make a

subjective evaluation of what conditions should or

should not be disclosed. It is for the Commissioner to

make that evaluation according to the legislative

criteria. If a past condition is so minor and/or

irrelevant as not to justify the issue of a BCC, such

issue is not open and there is a right of review

available if it is wrongly issued. That is the

protection that a prospective employee has, and it is

not for that person to make a subjective decision not to

disclose on the grounds of perceived unimportance or

irrelevance.

24. Having found that the applicant failed to furnish information that she should have furnished, the next question is whether a BCC would have issued. By s.6 of Act No.80 of 1986 s.16(8)(a) of the Act was amended in consequence of the decision in Re Bewley and Commissioner for Superannuation (1985) 8 ALD 293, with the result that a BCC became required to be issued where there was "a real risk" that the prospective employee would not continue to be an eligible employee until maximum retiring age. The proposition put by Mr. Whithear was that as this provision had been amended with effect from 1 July 1976, the "real risk" test as legislatively ordained should be applied in the present case, in which the decision was made on 15 July 1986. We do not see how this can be so. The question under s.16(11) is whether a BCC "would have been in force", which is a question of fact. How an amendment made retrospectively could be held to overlay a prior factual situation of some 8 years standing escapes us. But in our opinion the point is of no moment, for, despite Mr. Whithear's gracious concession in argument that the view taken in Re Bewley was probably "more literally correct", in Re Dakin and Commissioner for Superannuation (1986) 10 ALN N129 a Tribunal presided over by a judicial Presidential Member declined to follow Re Bewley, holding that the words of the legislation do not require that it be more probable than not that premature retirement occur but only that it be something that "may well happen". This test is in our opinion indistinguishable from the "real risk" test adopted in the amending legislation. It follows that it is effectively the "real risk" test that is to be applied in any event.

Would a BCC have issued?

25. Two factors lead us to an assurance that, had the history of back problems in 1975-1977 been furnished to the examining medical officer at the time of the examination, his report and the consideration thereof by the respondent would have led inexorably to the issue of a BCC.

26. We are not sure that the applicant would not have met the GMS as correctly set out at T5/53. It speaks, in relation to the "Skeletal and Muscular System", of "deformities". Unless that word is used in a wider sense, we have some difficulty with the proposition that the applicant suffered, or had ever suffered from, a "deformity". The standard goes on to relate the problem more to work ability, and in 2(b) refers to a "high degree of adaptation to the deformity or disability has occurred" (underlining supplied).

27. The more important point however is that, quite apart from the strict terms of the GMS as it then stood, it would have been quite clear that there was a real risk, in the sense referred to above, that the applicant would not serve to her maximum retiring age. Dr. Stubbs, who was called for the applicant, was referred to Dr. Marinos' report (Ex.3). He did not agree that the applicant had had significant problems with her lower back prior to September 1978, for as already explained it was his thesis that what she later suffered, and what led to her retirement, was an actual escape of invertebral disc material from the confines of a disc, a true disc lesion. But he was asked whether, looking at the question, as did Dr. Marinos in his report, from 1978, there was anything to suggest that what she was treated for in 1982 was something "that would necessarily have arisen in 1982 or 1981?". To this Dr. Stubbs answered: "No, on a percentage basis I think there was - you know, it was 10 per cent likely or something like that, but certainly not inevitable and not probable". This was the best evidence on this point for the applicant.

28. Dr. Andrews, who it will be recalled agreed with Dr. Marinos' original diagnosis as expressed in his notes that the applicant had by 1978 had lumbosacral disc rupture causing sciatica, was asked what was his estimate of the prospects, viewed from 1978, of that disc rupture being sufficiently symptomatic to cause extensive sick leave resulting in invalidity retirement. He answered: "Well, I suppose in trying to give it a ball park figure I would anticipate in a four or five year period the risk of say 20 per cent that the disc would have produced severe symptoms".

29. In his report of 24 September 1988 referred to above, Dr. Marinos said that "there must have been a possibility that this problem would cause retirement from work although I think this would have been difficult to predict in 1978".

30. Taking all of this evidence together, we conclude that a BCC would properly have been in force had there been proper disclosure.

31. We now have to deal with the "connection" issues raised by s.16(11) of the Act, that is to say deciding what BCC would have issued and what conditions would have been specified therein; what was the incapacity that led to the retirement; and what was the condition that caused the incapacity that led to retirement.

32. Taking the first point, Dr. Andrews spoke of the applicant in 1978 having had an intervertebral disc rupture, and Dr. Marinos spoke of a lumbosacral disc lesion, a distinction without a difference. Dr. Stubbs spoke rather of degenerative disc disease. We consider that in the light of the known facts there is no doubt that the conditions which should be supposed to have been specified in the BCC were "degenerative disc disease" and "intervertebral disc lesion".

33. As to the incapacity that led to retirement, there is no doubt in our minds that the incapacity should be described as an inability to sit for the periods required in her employment or to climb stairs, which was equally so requisite. The conditions or symptoms that led to this incapacity were described at T9/112 by Telecom as chronic low back pain and sciatica. It is worth pointing out again that there is a distinction to be drawn between an incapacity and the condition that gives rise to such incapacity.

34. We are then left with four questions the equivalent, mutatis mutandis, of those posed in Re Burr and Commissioner for Superannuation (1980) 3 ALN N1 at N3:

(i) Was the incapacity which led to the applicant's

retirement caused by the conditions which we have found

would have been specified in the BCC or either of them?

We are clearly of opinion that there was, on the

footing of the evidence of Dr. Marinos and Dr. Andrews,

a direct causal link between the incapacity and each of

the conditions of degenerative disc disease and

intervertebral disc lesion.

(ii) Having regard to the answer to question (i) it follows

necessarily that the incapacity was substantially

contributed to by each of the supposed BCC conditions.

(iii) The equivalent of the third question posed in Re Burr

is unnecessary to be answered here, given our answer to

Question 1. Out of caution, however, we should answer

it. It would be asked on the footing that Dr. Stubbs'

view was correct rather than that of Dr. Andrews and

Dr. Marinos, and thus would be based on the

supposition, contrary to what we have found, that but

one condition would have been specified in the BCC,

namely "degenerative disc disease". It is: Was the

incapacity that caused the applicant's retirement

caused by the condition "intervertebral disc lesion"

and was that condition connected with the condition

"degenerative disc disease"? Had this been the

question, we would be bound to answer that the relevant

incapacity was caused by intervertebral disc prolapse,

which was a condition clearly "connected" with the

supposed sole specified condition of degenerative disc

disease. As Dr. Stubbs himself said, it is extremely

uncommon to see a disc injury occur in a completely

healthy normal intervertebral disc. The test laid

down by the Federal Court in Commissioner for

Superannuation v Miller (1985) 8 FLR 153 and in

Commissioner for Superannuation v Benham (14 April

1988, ACT G68 of 1986) requires that the connection

between the two conditions be a causal connection, and

on the evidence that requirement is here clearly

satisfied.

(iv) The final question posed in Re Burr is again

unnecessary to be answered owing to the direct

causality found to exist in the answer to Question 3,

such a direct causal link having, in accordance with

his general thesis, been found by Dr. Stubbs to have

existed.

35. Having regard to the foregoing, our decision will be to affirm the decision under review subject to a variation thereof expressing our finding that the conditions specified in the BCC that would have been in force were "degenerative disc disease" and "intervertebral disc lesion".
