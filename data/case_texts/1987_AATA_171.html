Re Norman Gunzburg and Commissioner of Superannuation [1987] AATA 171 (7 May 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: NORMAN GUNZBURG

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. W86/300

AAT No. 3432

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.D. Nicholson (Deputy President)

B.C. Lock (Member)

Dr P.A. Staer (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - reduced benefit - Provident Account - relevant condition "raised blood pressure" - relationship with incapacity being the ground for retirement

HEARING

PERTH

7:5:1987

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

Decisions Under Review

The Applicant seeks review of the following decisions taken by the delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation on 25 July 1985:-

(1) To issue pursuant to sub-section 184(2) of the

Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") in respect of the

Applicant a Benefit Classification Certificate ("BCC")

specifying the condition "Raised Blood Pressure";

(2) To be of the opinion under paragraph 66(2)(c) of the Act

that the incapacity which was the ground for the Applicant's

retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to,

by a condition or conditions specified in the BCC or by a

condition or conditions connected with such conditions.

Jurisdiction

2. The matter arises before the Tribunal pursuant to section 154 of the Act, the necessary reconsideration of the decisions under review required by that Section having taken place. The application is within the time allowed.

Facts

3. It is convenient in this proceeding to set out the facts sequentially with emphasis on the more important events:

7. 5.62 - The Applicant commenced employment in a temporary capacity as a fitter and turner with the Postmaster General's Department ("PMG") (as Australia Post was then called).

31. 5.66 - The Applicant received a permanent appointment on probation. He commenced paying contributions to the Superannuation Fund in advance of the confirmation of his permanent appointment.

13. 6.66 - The Applicant underwent his first medical examination. His blood pressure was measured at 145/95. It was recommended he be deferred for 3 months. The reason given was "overweight". No reference was made to the blood pressure other than recording the figures relating to it.

15. 6.66 - The medical officer's report of the first examination was received in the Commonwealth Public Service Inspector's office, Perth.

16. 6.66 - An unidentified author wrote onto the report the words:

"Defer three months overweight (max. 15.1) b/p"

4. It was not contested that the letters "b/p" should be read as referring to blood pressure.

21. 6.66 - The Public Service Inspector's office wrote to the Applicant stating that before his probationary appointment could be confirmed it would be necessary for him to reduce his weight and advised him that the normal standard for acceptance in his case was 15 stone 1 lb.

16. 9.66 - The Applicant was examined by Dr. Smart who recorded that his height was 5 ft. 11 ins. and his weight 14 st. 8 lbs. and that he was fit for appointment to the Superannuation Fund.

21. 9.66 - The medical examination report was received back in the office of the Public Service Inspector, Perth.

29. 9.66 - The medical examination report was stamped "Superannuation Fund". This was subsequently crossed out and what appears to be a new stamp reading "Provident Account" was stamped above it. The probability is that the second stamp was placed onto the report at a later date without the initials and the date of the original stamping being amended.

26.10.66 - A letter was written from the acting Superintendent of the personnel branch of the Post-Master General's Department to the Applicant at the Engineering Division, requiring him to report to the Commonwealth medical officer at 2.15p.m. on 28 October 1966. The Applicant stated in sworn evidence he did not receive this letter and he attended as requested solely as a consequence of a request orally conveyed to him through his supervisor. This would be consistent with a letter being received by the supervisor but not having reached the Applicant. No record can be found of the reason for the further examination being requested or who occasioned it to be requested.

28.10.66 - The Applicant left work as requested and drove to the office of the Commonwealth medical officer for the purpose of a third medical examination. This examination was recorded on this date by way of notation by the Assistant Director (Medical) on the medical report previously completed by Dr. Smart. The notation reads:

"WT 14/8 lbs. B.P. after rest 150/90. Fit for

permanent appointment and contributor to Prov:

Account only"

5. Associated records show the two blood pressure readings as 160/95 and 150/90. The Applicant's evidence was that while he rested between these readings being taken, he did not lie down.

4.11.66 - The Postmaster General's Department forwarded to the Public Service Inspector, Perth, the Applicant's medical papers following re-examinations on 16 September and 28 October.

8.11.66 - A Delegate of the Public Service Board certified that the health and physical fitness of the Applicant justified his acceptance as a contributor to the Provident Account with effect from 31 May 1966. He commenced contributing to the Provident Account.

23. 7.84 - A Commonwealth medical officer examined the Applicant for the purposes of determining his fitness for continued duty or retirement on the ground of invalidity. The medical officer considered the Applicant was suffering from the effects of coronary heart disease, diabetes mellitus, hypertension and obesity and recommended he be retired on the grounds of invalidity.

3.12.84 - Dr. P. Thompson, cardiologist, reported in relation to the Applicant, in the course of which he stated:

"There is no question that he has significant

underlying medical problems including coronary

artery disease which is likely to progress,,

hypertension, mild diabetes and obesity. He

experiences a sense of extreme frustration with

his work and naturally this is not good for him.

Although I am not convinced he is medically

incapable for continuing with his work I think

there is little doubt if he did continue to work

his general medical problems would rapidly

progress as a direct result of his working and for

this reason I would feel that an early retirement

on medical grounds is justified."

22. 1.85 - The Applicant's retirement took effect.

28. 3.85 - The advice from Telecom Australia to the Commissioner for Superannuation "Notification of Separation" was received in the Commissioner's office (T10/67).

6. It is apparent from the correspondence in the "T" documents that between 28 March 1985 and 25 July 1985 there were a number of letters from the Commissioner to the Applicant, in particular:

19. 4.85 - The Commissioner wrote to the Applicant

concerning his invalidity and the payment of an interim

benefit pending further inquiries (referred to in T11/68).

20. 5.85 - The Commissioner wrote to the Applicant advising

him of a rate of pension he would be receiving from that date

onwards (T12/69).

25. 7.85 - A Delegate of the Commissioner issued the BCC specifying as the relevant condition "raised blood pressure". The delegate of the Commissioner also formed the opinion that the stated incapacity which was the ground for retirement was caused or was substantially contributed to by the condition specified in the BCC or by a condition connected with the condition so specified and that the Applicant should therefore receive a reduced benefit.

26. 7.85 - The Commissioner advised the Applicant that the BCC had been issued and of the opinion formed by the Commissioner. Then followed an exchange of correspondence between the Applicant and the Commissioner and between the Commissioner and various medical referees, all concerning the Applicant's request for reconsideration of this decision and which led to a final medical report from Dr. Thompson. We are satisfied that there was no undue delay on the part of either party in dealing with this correspondence.

26. 8.86 - Dr. P. Thompson reported further:

"I would place the following percentage

contributions for each condition as follows:-

Coronary artery disease - 70%

Obesity - 15%

Hypertension - 10%

Diabetes mellitus - 5%

The reasons for the heavy emphasis on coronary

artery disease were because he had experienced

severe complications from his coronary artery

disease and these had required major surgery.

While the surgery had relieved his symptoms, the

underlying problem remained and was likely to

progress with his continued work.

There is no doubt that the high blood pressure and

the overweight which Mr. Gunzberg (sic) had at the

time of his entry to the superannuation scheme in

1966 were connected with his subsequent

development of coronary artery disease. There is

no doubt that there is a link of a causal kind

between high blood pressure, even of the

relatively mild levels of 145/95 recorded in 1966,

and the subsequent development of coronary artery

disease. High blood pressure is recognised as a

major risk factor for coronary artery disease and

this is compounded by the presence of obesity."

24.11.86 - A delegate of the Commissioner carried out an extensive reconsideration of the matter and affirmed the decisions under review.

7. For the purposes of sub-section 5(2) of the Superannuation Act 1922 the practice of the delegate of the Public Service Board, at the time the decision was made to certify the Applicant as acceptable as a contributor to the Provident Account, was to make reference to the Medical Handbook. Issue of Benefit Classification Certificate

9. The BCC was issued pursuant to sub-section 184(2) of the Act which read at the time of the issue of the certificate (cf. Re Hare and Commissioner for Superannuation (1981) 3 ALN N81):

"184. (2) Subject to sub-section (4), where -

(a) a person to whom this section applies who,

immediately before the commencing day, was

(otherwise than by reason of an election made

under section 81 of the superseded Act) a

contributor to the Provident Account ceases

to be an eligible employee by reason of death

or retirement on the ground of invalidity;

(b) his period of contributory service is less

than 20 years and, on the day on which he

ceases to be an eligible employee, he has not

attained his maximum retiring age; and

(c) the Commissioner is of the opinion that the

death of the person or the incapacity which

was the ground for the retirement of the

person was caused, or was substantially

contributed to -

(i) by the physical or mental condition of

the person that was relevant for the

purposes of the superseded Act or, if

there was more than one such condition,

by those conditions or one or more of

those conditions; or

(ii) by a physical or mental condition or

conditions of the person connected with

the physical or mental condition of the

person that was relevant for the

purposes of the superseded Act or, if

there was more than one such condition,

with those conditions or one or more of

those conditions,

the Commissioner shall issue in respect of the person

a benefit classification certificate in which there is

or are specified the physical or mental condition or

conditions of the person that was or were relevant for

the purposes of the superseded Act and, for the

purposes of this Act, the certificate shall be deemed

to have been issued under sub-section 16(4) and to

have been in force in respect of the person

immediately before his death or retirement."

10. The Applicant is a person to whom the section applies in accordance with sub-section 184(1) and sub-section (4) has no limiting effect in this matter. The question is how paragraph 184(2)(c) should be applied. In our view it gives rise to four questions:

(cf. Re Vaughan and Commissioner for Superannuation (1979) 2 ALN 366 (N36); reported in full as Decision No. 180, 27 April 1979 - see p 10):

(i) were any of the conditions caused by the raised

blood pressure;

(ii) were any of the conditions substantially

contributed to by the raised blood pressure?

(iii) were any of the conditions caused by a condition

connected with the raised blood pressure?

(iv) were any of the conditions substantially

contributed to by a condition connected with

raised blood pressure?

Determination of relevant condition

11. Paragraph 184(7)(c) of the Act provided at the time of the issue of the BCC:

"184. (7) In this section -

(a) ...

(b) ...

(c) a reference to the physical or mental

condition or conditions of a person that was

or were relevant for the purposes of the

superseded Act shall be read as a reference

to the physical or mental condition or

conditions of the person that, in the opinion

of the Commissioner, was or were the physical

or mental condition or conditions of the

person by reason of which -

(i) the Superannuation Board was not

satisfied under sub-section 5(1) of the

superseded Act that the health and

physical fitness of the person were

such as to justify his being accepted

as a contributor to the Fund; or

(ii) the person was not, by virtue of a

provision of the superseded Act (other

than sub-section 5(1) or 79(2) of the

superseded Act), accepted as a

contributor to the Fund, ..."

12. It is therefore necessary to determine what was the physical or mental condition or conditions which resulted in the Applicant not being accepted as a contributor to the Superannuation Fund, being a condition in existence at a time he became a contributor to the Provident Account. In order to do this, it is necessary to examine the evidence of the three medical examinations undergone by the Applicant.

13. The first difficulty in undertaking this examination is that in 1966 neither the Superannuation Board nor its delegate recorded reasons for decisions. This, however, is not a novel situation. It has been faced by this Tribunal in other decisions. In Re A.K. Bailey and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision No. 352, 24 December 1979), the Tribunal said:

"In a case such as the present, where there is no

direct evidence to establish the physical or mental

condition or conditions by reason of which the

Superannuation Board did not accept the applicant as a

contributor to the Superannuation Fund, it appears to

us that the proper test to apply when considering the

material before the Board when it reached its decision

is - what condition or conditions must have influenced

the Board to accept the person into the Provident

Account rather than the Fund. Unless, on a fair

consideration of that material, the Tribunal can feel

satisfied that a particular physical or mental

condition was, in fact, a reason which influenced the

Board's decision, the Tribunal would not, in our view,

be justified in finding as a fact that the condition

should be taken as being specified in the benefit

classification certificate which is deemed, by section

184(2) of the Act, to be in force in respect of the

applicant."

14. Bailey involved a medical examination carried out pursuant to sub-section 5(1) of the Superannuation Act 1922. Here, however, the applicant's medical examination was carried out pursuant to sub-section 5(2) of that Act and involved the report going to the Public Service Board. The factual evidence such as is available, relates to the decisions of the Public Service Board and not the Superannuation Board to whom the Public Service Board certified pursuant to that sub-section. Mr. Whithear for the Respondent informed the Tribunal that in those days the decision of the Superannuation Board under section 5(2) of the Superannuation Act 1922 was only exercised in a negative fashion. In any event, on the balance of probabilities, the Tribunal concludes that the Superannuation Board made a decision upon certification by the Public Service Board because it accepted this Applicant as a contributor to the Provident Account.

15. On behalf of the Applicant it was argued that the absence of a record of reasons by the Superannuation Board wrought an injustice to the Applicant particularly in a situation where he had completed almost 20 years' service, the accomplishment of which would have excluded him from the provisions of sub-section 184(7). In the view of the Tribunal it must, as a matter of law, approach the matter as set out in Bailey. The absence of reasons will not, in our opinion, have wrought an injustice if the facts are approached in the manner there indicated.

16. In Re Richardson and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision No. 478, 5 June 1981), it was said that sub-section 184(7) of the Act requires reference to a condition that has actually been taken into account by the Superannuation Board for the purposes of coming to a conclusion as to the health and physical fitness of the person. Approaching the matter as in Bailey, we consider the following evidence forces the conclusion that the Superannuation Board, by acting on the certification from the Public Service Board, actually took into account for such purpose the condition of "raised blood pressure":

(1) The notation at the first medical examination on 13.6.66 of

the Applicant's blood pressure at 145/95 which exceeded the

standard for acceptance to the Superannuation Fund but was

within the standard for acceptance to the Provident Account.

(2) The notation on 29 September 1966 by the officer from the

Public Service Inspector's office of "bp".

(3) The absence of reference to blood pressure by Dr. Smart in

the report of the applicant's second medical examination on

16.9.66 which suggests that this was the reason for the

third examination.

(4) The record at the third examination on 28.10.66 of the

Applicant's blood pressure at 150/90, was again beyond the

standard for acceptance to the Superannuation Fund and on

the upper limit for acceptance into the Provident Account.

17. On behalf of the Applicant it was argued that the following evidence showed it was not correct to conclude that raised blood pressure was the relevant condition:

(a) the emphasis placed on the Applicant being overweight in the

first and second medical examination and the reference to it

in the third examination;

(b) the conflicting matters referred to in the medical reports;

(c) the want of substantial evidence.

18. In relation to these arguments, the Tribunal considers it is clear the Applicant's weight condition could not have been the relevant condition. The Applicant lost weight (as envisaged by paragraph 143 of the Handbook) after a period of deferral. The Applicant's weight being eliminated as a potential condition, only blood pressure remains. The readings of the Applicant's blood pressure in all three examinations place him above the limits envisaged by section 58 of the Handbook as being necessary to qualify for the Superannuation Fund and place him within the limits eligible for Provident Account benefits only. Examining the evidence as a whole, the Tribunal also finds it not in conflict, the evidence in relation to blood pressure being consistent. The evidence which is available, rather than being insubstantial, establishes in the view of the Tribunal, on the balance of probabilities, that the Delegate of the Public Service Board and therefore the Superannuation Board acted on the relevant condition of raised blood pressure.

19. On behalf of the Applicant it was also argued the Applicant had not been allowed any period of recumbency between blood pressure readings at the third medical examination as envisaged by paragraph 58 of the Handbook; that no referral had been made to a specialist as would have been possible; and that the Handbook was utilized by lay persons. These matters lie beyond the competence of this Tribunal to consider on this review. They go to the accuracy, adequacy and appropriateness of the medical examinations rather than to the decisions under review. This Tribunal must take the facts of the medical examinations and Public Service Board records as it finds them.

20. For these reasons the Tribunal is satisfied that on the balance of probabilities, raised blood pressure is the relevant condition for the purposes of paragraph 184(7)(c) of the Act as it was at the time of the issue of that certificate.

Relationship between relevant condition and incapacity which was the ground for retirement

21. In accordance with the provisions of paragraph 184(2)(c), it is necessary for the Tribunal to examine the relationship between the relevant condition and the incapacity which was the ground for retirement in relation to each of the four questions earlier identified as arising under that paragraph.

- incapacity the ground for retirement

22. It is first necessary to identify the incapacity which was the Applicant's ground for retirement. Dr. Thompson's letter of 26 August 1986 makes apparent that coronary artery disease, obesity, hypertension and diabetes mellitus were such conditions. It is to these diseases that the relationship of the relevant condition "raised blood pressure" must be found. For the Applicant, it was argued that there were several probable causes of his retirement and that it was unreasonable to conclude that his retirement was due to coronary heart disease. In particular, it was argued that work-related stress could have been a factor. This view, however, is simply contrary to the evidence of Dr. Thompson which, in the view of the Tribunal, substantially outweighs that probability.

- questions (i) and (ii)

23. The Tribunal is satisfied that the incapacity of coronary artery disease which was one of the grounds for retirement was caused or was substantially contributed to by the relevant condition of raised blood pressure. It is so satisfied on the balance of probabilities, particularly in the light of the evidence by Dr. Thompson which establishes the necessary causal relationship. In our view the causal relationship is either entire or substantial. We apply the requirement of substantiality as requiring the relationship to be "in the main" or "essentially": Commissioner for Superannuation and Scott (1987) Q G62 of 1986, Federal Court of Australia. Dr. Thompson's evidence was that even relatively mild levels of high blood pressure were causally linked to subsequent development of high blood pressure and that this latter condition is recognized as a major risk factor for coronary artery disease. To be taken into account in this respect also is the blood pressure (hypertension) present as an incapacity on retirement.

24. It was also argued on behalf of the Applicant that the Applicant was entitled to a finding that the relationship between the relevant condition and the incapacity upon retirement was not established in the absence of statistical evidence in support of such relationship. In reaching the conclusions which it has, this Tribunal is acting consistently with other decisions which require the Tribunal to be satisfied on the balance of probabilities on the evidence before it: see Re Birtles and Commissioner for Superannuation (Decision No. 1309, 22 November 1983) and Re Burr and Commissioner for Superannuation (1980) 3 ALN N1, each of which were addressed in the delegate's comprehensive reasons for decision.

25. Nor does the Tribunal consider that the opinion of Dr. Thompson should be given less weight because of the letter which was addressed to him seeking his opinion on any relationship between the conditions "raised blood pressure" and "obesity". The Tribunal accepts that when the Applicant entered the Provident Account overweight was not a problem. Dr. Thompson, however, in his response of 26 August 1986, distinguished between high blood pressure and overweight after initially linking them. The crucial sentences in his letter are:

"There is no doubt that there is a link of a causal

kind between high blood pressure, even of the

relatively mild levels of 145/95 recorded in 1966, and

the subsequent development of coronary artery disease.

High blood pressure is recognized as a major risk

factor for coronary artery disease and this is

compounded by the presence of obesity."

26. Obesity was seen by him as a compounding effect but his view independently of obesity is enough of itself to establish the necessary causal relationship.

- questions (iii) and (iv)

27. There is nothing in the evidence which entitles the Tribunal to conclude that obesity or diabetes mellitus had the necessary causal relationship with the relevant condition. Nor is there present any third condition shown to be connected with raised blood pressure to which questions (iii) and (iv) would require application. In our view questions (i) and (ii) are the applicable questions and the answer to each of those questions should be affirmative.

- Commissioner's view

28. The Commissioner took the view that section 184(2)(c)(1) did not apply. To quote his words:

"... I am not of the opinion that the condition 'Raised

Blood Pressure' relevant at entry can be said to have

substantially contributed to the retirement incapacity

in any direct sense in that it is clearly not

essentially the same on-going condition as the

'Coronary Heart Disease' which I have satisfied

myself, substantially contributed to the retirement

incapacity."

29. We have taken a different view based on the evidence of Dr. Thompson that the incapacity which was the grounds for retirement namely coronary artery disease was caused by 'Raised Blood Pressure' the relevant condition specified in the BCC. The Commissioner in his reconsideration adopted the view that the incapacity which was the ground for retirement was caused by or substantially contributed to by a physical condition connected with the relevant condition in the BCC. We have difficulty fitting the words of the Section into this interpretation, for read literally the Section would then read, 'the coronary artery disease' which was the grounds for retirement was caused by or substantially contributed to by 'coronary artery disease' a condition connected to the relevant condition in the BCC namely raised blood pressure.

30. We are of the view that the application of section 184(2)(c)(ii) requires the interposition of a third condition. It requires in this case that there must have been a condition causing or substantially contributing to the coronary artery disease and that condition must have been connected with raised blood pressure. (See Re Vaughan (supra)). There is no evidence of such third condition in this proceeding.

31. We therefore reach the same result as the Commissioner but do so for different reasons.

Amendment to Relevant Law

32. The above reasoning has been made in relation to the law as it was at the date of the issue of the BCC and the formation of the Commissioner's opinion. On 18 December 1986 section 184 of the Act was amended by section 76 of the Superannuation Legislation Amendment Act (No. 2) 1986. So far as is relevant, the portions of section 184 previously quoted were amended in the following terms:

"76. Section 184 of the Principal Act is amended -

(a) by omitting paragraphs 2(a) and (b) and

substituting the following paragraphs:

"(a) a person to whom this section applies who,

immediately before the commencing day, was

(otherwise than because of an election made

under section 81 of the superseded Act) a

contributor to the Provident Account -

(i) ceases to be an eligible employee

because of death or retirement on the

ground of invalidity; or

(ii) becomes entitled to partial invalidity

pension under section 78 because of a

decrease in the annual rate of the

person's salary that the Commissioner

is satisfied can properly be regarded

as attributable to physical or mental

incapacity;

(b) at the time when the person so ceases to be

an eligible employee or becomes entitled to

partial invalidity pension, as the case

requires, the person's period of

contributory service is less than 20 years

and the person has not attained the

person's maximum retiring age; and";

(b) by omitting from paragraph (2)(c) "or the

incapacity which was the ground for the

retirement of the person" and substituting ",

the incapacity which was the ground for the

retirement of the person or the incapacity to

which the Commissioner was satisfied that the

decrease in the annual rate of salary of the

person could properly be regarded as

attributable, as the case requires,";

...

(e) by inserting in paragraph (7)(c) ", at the time

when the person became, or last became, a

contributor to the Provident Account" after "by

reason of which"; and

(f) by omitting from paragraph (7)(c) all the words

after sub-paragraph (c)(ii) and substituting

"other than a physical or mental condition that,

in the opinion of the Commissioner, the

Superannuation Board was, as a result of a

medical examination under sub-section 79(2) of

the superseded Act, satisfied no longer existed

unless the Commissioner is satisfied that, but

for the failure of the person to give any

information required to be given by the person

or the giving of false information at or in

connection with that medical examination, the

Board would not have been so satisfied."

33. The amending Act is silent as to its application to past or pending matters. Its language is neutral so that no inference in that respect can be drawn from it. In such a situation the normal rule of statutory construction is that the statute has no retrospective operation so as to confer or impose or otherwise affect rights or liabilities which the law had defined by reference to past events (Maxwell v. Murphy [1957] HCA 7; (1957) 96 CLR 261, 267; Maxwell on Interpretation of Statutes 12th Edn. p 218).

34. Whatever the effect of the amending Act in that respect, a matter it is unnecessary to pursue in this proceeding, the amendments do not affect the Applicant's position nor give him a prospect of success which previously he lacked. There is nothing in the amendments which would lead us to reach a conclusion different from that which in our view pertains under the law previously considered. Indeed the amendment introduced by sub-section 76(f) of the amending Act could only make it more difficult for the Applicant to succeed.

Conclusion

35. For the reasons given the Tribunal will therefore affirm the decision under review.
