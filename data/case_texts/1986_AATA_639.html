QT86/2133 and Commissioner of Taxation [1986] AATA 639; (1986) 18 ATR 3164; 87 ATC 190 (15 December 1986)

Last Updated: 5 August 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Superannuation Fund – failure to meet specified percentages of investment in public securities - whether Commissioner should have disregarded this failure – whether the failure was by reason of temporary delay in investment.

Income Tax Assessment Act (1935-1983) s 121C

DECISION

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

AAT Decision No 3143

Place: Brisbane

Date: December 15, 1986

Dr P Gerber (Senior Member)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO. QT86/2133

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

In this case, the Tribunal is charged, so to speak, with doing over again the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion pursuant to sub-section 121C(4) of the Tax Act (now sub-section 121C(7))in relation to the tax position of certain superannuation funds and, in particular, the fund in this case established by the applicant company and designated as its superannuation fund.

2. This fund was established in the 1980 tax year. It was common ground that for reasons in the main relating to the rural recession, the company was in no position to comply with the so-called 30/20 rule, needing all the financial resources at its disposal, including a not insubstantial bank overdraft for the running of its business.

3. It was some time in 1981 that the company made certain forecasts. At that point in time, it had substantial agricultural machinery on hand, and having ordered additional tractors from Italy, by the end of the 1982 financial year, its stock on hand was valued at $800,000, or thereabouts.

4. Again, it was common ground that no attempt was made until the 1983 tax year to bring the fund within the ambit of sec 121C. In short, until that year, there was a 100 per cent loan back to the company.

5. It was in the 1983 year, and. after some consultations with the AMP society, which for reasons I need not go into, subsequently took over the fund, that the company began to make an attempt to comply with the 30/20 rule. It achieved this largely by the sale of some 19 tractors initially, and some six tractors later, which gave it a limited cash-flow. In October 1982 it made the first payment to the fund of $10,000, which was then invested in specified bonds for purposes of the section. The next payment was made in May 1983, by a number of instalments, $20,000 which were put into Aussie Bonds, and a further $10,000 into SEC bonds. However, it was not until 10 June 1983, when a further payment of $14,000 was made to the fund that strict compliance with the 30/20 rule was achieved.

6. Subsequently, the balance of the loan was paid back on or about 30 June 1983. In order to achieve that kind of cash-flow, the company reduced much of its inventory and also increased its overdraft with its bank.

7. The situation is fairly clear-cut in that the section itself provides for a mandatory compliance, subject only to a limited discretion, which enables the Commissioner, in some circumstances, to disregard non compliance. For purposes of the section, the Commissioner shall disregard any failure of the assets of a superannuation fund to include, at all times during a particular year of income, assets as provided by the section if he is satisfied -

(a) that the trustee of the fund made a genuine and bona fide attempt to ensure that the assets of the fund included, at all times during that year of income, assets as so provided; or

(b) that the failure was by reason of a temporary delay in investment and that, in all the circumstances, it would be reasonable to disregard the failure.

8. The evidence of Mr Betts, on behalf of the applicant fund, was refreshingly honest, and I accept it without hesitation. The thrust of his evidence was that the company had at all times a facility with the bank, which it could, had it sought to do so, utilise for purposes of the business rather than borrow from the funds, and by this device deprive the funds of the tax advantages which, had they been invested in compliance with section 121C, they would have had.

9. Mr Betts was closely questioned as to why the company elected to utilise the superannaution funds rather than agreed facilities - by agreed, I mean, funds which it could have borrowed from the bank – and no matter how hard Mr Betts was pressed, at no time did he really provide a sufficiently satisfactory explanation as to why the company elected to use the superannuation funds in preference to increasing its overdraft facilities with the bank. If was at one time faintly suggested that the reason was to involve the staff in the day-to-day operation of the company. However, I find such explanation unconvincing.

10. On the evidence, I am unable to conclude that the trustee of the fund made a genuine and bona fide attempt to ensure that the assets of the fund included at all times during the year of income assets as required; nor am I satisfied that the failure was by reason of a temporary delay in investment.

11. The reason, if one had to be advanced, as to failure to comply, is that the company elected of its own choice, for reasons which need not concern me here, to utilise the readily available funds of the superannuation fund in preference to increasing its facility with its bank. In those circumstances, neither of the provisions of section 121C(4) have been made out, and the Commissioner properly exercised his discretion under sub-section (4) against the fund.

12. I recognise that the company had its back to the wall, that it was working in an industry hard-pressed by depression, and that it did its utmost to ensure that the company remained viable and its employees fully employed. But as was pointed out on behalf of the Commissioner, this is not a ground sufficient to invoke the Commissioner's discretion. The fund, after all, is one to provide superannuation benefits rather than to ensure full employment for its members.

13. For these reasons the application must be dismissed.
