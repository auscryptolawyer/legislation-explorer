QT89/255-258 and Commissioner of Taxation [1990] AATA 754; (1990) 21 ATR 3791; 91 ATC 101 (6 November 1990)

Last Updated: 3 June 2009



Income tax - Allowable Deduction - whether interest claimed was incurred under a loan agreement which had the purpose of deriving assessable income - whether loan agreement was a sham - whether purpose of purchasing a tax deduction



Snook v. London and West Riding Investments (1967) 2 QB 786

Lau v. FCT 84 ATC 4618 at 4621-2, and 84 ATC 4929 at 4941-3

Alloy Weld Pty Ltd v. FCT 84 ATC 4328 at 4330

Cranstoun v. FCT 84 ATC 4876 at 4881

REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



Date: 6 November 1990



Place: Brisbane



The question for decision is whether any deduction is allowable in relation to claims for interest by the applicants as partners in the years of income ended 30 June 1978, 30 June 1979 and 30 June 1980. The applicants are husband and wife. The applications were heard separately before the Tribunal but the Tribunal will not participate any further in the histrionics of the applicants by providing separate reasons. These reasons apply to the applications by the husband and the wife ("H" and "W").



2. H and W carry on business in partnership in a confectionery business. The income tax return lodged by the partnership in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1978 included the following (Exhibit A):



"Taxpayers borrowed the sum of $100,000 which was used exclusively for the acquisition of shares in C Pty Ltd.



Income from this investment has been returned as assessable income derived during the year 30th June 1978. A copy of the relevant Loan Agreement is enclosed herewith. A deduction is claimed for the sum of $75,000.00 representing interest incurred by the taxpayers in gaining or producing their assessable income."



3. Exhibit A does not include a copy of a loan agreement but a copy of such an agreement is contained in Exhibit C. That agreement dated 17 March 1978 is said to be an agreement between Quassia Finance Corporation Pty Ltd (Quassia), a company said to be incorporated in the Australian Capital Territory and the party named in the first schedule and described as the "Borrower". Exhibit C also includes a document headed "First Schedule" which states that the name and address of the Borrower is H and W, together with their address. Notwithstanding that they are the Borrower the agreement has been signed on their behalf by a Sophia Kemp as duly appointed attorney. Sophia Kemp also signed the agreement as the duly appointed attorney of Quassia Finance Corporation Pty Ltd and the document purports to have been executed in the Australian Capital Territory. The amount of the loan is not quantified in the document.



4. Copies of documents purporting to be powers of attorney, dated 16 March 1978 and signed by H and W respectively appointing Sophia Kemp as their attorney also form part of Exhibit C.



5. The general effect of the loan agreement is that Quassia undertook to lend H and W $100,000 repayable on 31 December 2017. Interest at 13.8% calculated on daily rests and payable monthly from the date of advance to 31 December 2012 after which the interest payable was to be at the rate of 8.75% payable monthly.



6. Clause 4 of the agreement is unusual in that it provided in effect that the lender could call for payment of the first 65 month's interest on or before 31 March 1978 with the interest thereafter to be calculated at 8.75%.



7. By a notice dated 20 March 1978 (copy included in Exhibit C) Quassia required H and W to pay 65 months interest calculated at 13.8% on the principal amount advanced. Although not the precise amount H and W claim that they complied with this requirement by the payment of an amount of $75,000 to Quassia.



8. By a notice of assessment dated 21 March 1978 Quassia purported to assign its interest in the Loan Agreement to a company I shall call "Engineering" which is controlled by H and W. The notice of assignment (copy included in Exhibit C) does not disclose any consideration for the assignment. However, by another document dated 20 March 1978 Quassia offered to assign the principal sum of $100,000 with interest under the Loan Agreement dated 17 March 1978 to Engineering for consideration of $32,725 payable within seven (7) days (Exhibit D).



9. The partnership used the $100,000 loan, so I am told, to purchase shares in a company I will call C Pty Ltd. That amount was paid to C Pty Ltd by a cheque drawn on the partnership bank account and dated 17 March 1978 (Exhibit 9). The cheque was deposited to the bank account of C Pty Ltd, the funds cleared and the $100,000 deposited to the bank account of another company which I will call Sin Pty Ltd (Exhibits 9 and 10) on the same day. In fact these transactions were part of a classic round robin of cheques as evidenced by the copy documents in Exhibit 9. As Exhibit 12 establishes, the partnership never received the borrowed $100,000 into its bank account.



10. The net result of the round robin was that the partnership was out of pocket to the extent of $75,000 claimed to have been paid as interest in advance - $74,750 is the correct figure according to the terms of the loan agreement and the amount of $32,725 paid by Engineering for the assignment of the loan. Exhibit 8, which is a diagrammatic explanation of the round robin, suggests that Engineering was reimbursed the $32,725. On the evidence I cannot be certain that is the situation, although it is more likely than not to be the case and I so find.



11. Therefore the partnership, although purporting to borrow $100,000 for the purpose of investing in C Pty Ltd, apparently borrowed $100,000 and then paid out in the next three days a total amount of $107,475. The reality is, of course, that it paid out $7,475 to purchase a tax deduction of $74,750. That is the only explanation for the transactions. No other rational explanation can be discerned from the transactions. In coming to that conclusion I am fortified by the fact that the notice of objection consists of forty-seven typewritten pages of legalese designed to further obfuscate the bemusing transactions.



12. The transaction in question, together with other round-robin transactions not relevant to these proceedings, resulted in the company C Pty Ltd having a paid up capital of $200,000. Although it was effectively without funds I accept H's submission that by so capitalising C Pty Ltd it was enabled to acquire another business and that the transaction was for the long term good of H and W and their associated companies. However, that is not the issue in these proceedings.



13. The question is whether the amount of $74,750 (or $75,000 as claimed) is an allowable deduction. The only basis put forward was that the amount claimed was interest incurred in the course of subscribing for shares in C Pty Ltd for the purpose of deriving dividend income on those shares. To the date of hearing the shares had produced one dividend amounting to $500.



14. The amount of $74,750 can be characterised as interest paid in advance in accordance with the terms of the loan agreement, or as an outgoing incurred to achieve capitalisation of C Pty Ltd.



15. The question then arises as to whether the amount of $76,676 as claimed in the objections is an allowable deduction to the partnership within the terms of subsection 51(1) of the Act. I am unable to explain the variation in the amount claimed.



16. The respondent's first submission is that the arrangements evidenced by Exhibit C were in fact a sham. The respondent's counsel relies on the decisions in Snook v. London and West Riding Investments (1967) 2 QB 786 at 802, Lau v. FCT 84 ATC 4618 at 4621-2, and 84 ATC 4929 at 4941-3, Alloy Weld Pty Ltd v. FCT 84 ATC 4328 at 4330, Cranstoun v. FCT 84 ATC 4876 at 4881. Counsel also relied upon the decision of the Federal Court in Ilbery v. FCT 81 ATC 4661.



17. In my view the answer is straightforward and need not be complicated by detailed references to the authorities. The whole basis of the claim depends upon the efficacy of the loan agreement in Exhibit C as evidencing a loan of money by Quassia to H and W. However, that document does not support the applicant's case for two reasons. Firstly it does not evidence the making of a loan to H and W by Quassia. No quantified amount is evidenced by the document and there is no evidence before me of any amount ever loaned by Quassia to H and W. The reason for this is readily apparent from the purported transactions. It is clear that Quassia never intended to lend H and W money as such and H and W never intended to borrow money as such from Quassia. All that H and W intended to do was capitalise C Pty Ltd and purchase a tax deduction. All Quassia intended to do was sell a tax deduction. I am therefore satisfied that the loan agreement in Exhibit C was not intended to have any effect at law for the loan of money as between the parties to the agreement and was therefore a sham in the sense defined by Diplock LJ in Snook's Case in these words at page 802:



". . . for acts or documents to be a "sham", with whatever legal consequences follow from this, all the parties thereto must have a common intention that the acts or documents are not to create legal rights and obligations which they give the appearance of creating."



18. Secondly I am satisfied that the arrangements entered into were for the purpose of purchasing a tax deduction. Therefore, even if the loan agreement was effective to create the relationship of lender and borrower and the liability to pay interest, any interest incurred under the agreement was incurred for the purpose of purchasing a tax deduction and not for the purpose of deriving assessable income or carrying on a business to derive assessable income. The partnership itself did not use the funds in its business so it cannot be correct to say that moneys were borrowed for the purpose of carrying on the business of the partnership. I am not satisfied, therefore, even if the loan was made, that it was obtained for a purpose related to the production of income.



19. I am not satisfied that the assessments are excessive and the objection decisions under review in respect of the years of income ended 30 June 1978 will therefore be affirmed.



20. It follows that the objection decisions in relation to the later years concerning carry forward loss claims must also be affirmed.



This and the preceding five (5) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matters of QT89/255-256 and QT89/257-258.
