Re Dhonal Maurice Mccarthy and the Commissioner of Superannuation [1987] AATA 481 (23 December 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: DHONAL MAURICE McCARTHY

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. N87/222

AAT No. 4044

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

C.J. Bannon QC (Deputy President)

Dr A.P. Renouf (Senior Member)

Dr D.J. Howell (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - "eligible employee" - "period of contributory service" - former employee of State Authority - no election of payment into Commonwealth Fund - wrongful advice from Commonwealth - estoppel.

Superannuation Act, 1922 (Cth.) ss.13, 29, 60AV(1),(3) (renumbered s.109)

Superannuation Act, 1976 (Cth.) ss.3(1), 4(1), 193(2)(a), 194(1)(a)(j), Regulation 4

HEARING

SYDNEY

23:12:1987

ORDER

That the decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

Dhonal Maurice McCarthy ("the applicant") is an officer of the Commonwealth Public Service, employed in the Department of Administrative Services. From 1943 to 1959 he was an officer of the Rural Bank of New South Wales. On 13 July 1959 he transferred from that employment to become an officer of the Commonwealth Taxation Department as a Valuer Grade 1. There was no break in the continuation of his service in the two positions. The applicant had been a contributor to a State Superannuation Scheme when he was an officer of the Rural Bank. His contributions were refunded to him, together with interest, upon his ceasing to be such an officer. The sum involved was about $600. The applicant seeks a review of a decision on 9 March 1987 (T22 at p.50) confirming a decision made on 17 October 1982 (T17 at p.41) that his period of service with the Rural Bank is not to be taken into account in calculating a retirement pension when that becomes payable.

Applicant's Length of Service and Deemed Service

2. Section 3 of the Superannuation Act, 1976 (Cth.) ("the 1976 Act") defines an "eligible employee" in terms of which paragraph (a) of the definition alone is relevant as meaning:

"(a) a person who, immediately before the commencement

of this Act, was an employee for the purposes of

the superseded Act and has continued to be, and is,

such an employee;"

The superseded Act is the Superannuation Act 1922 (Cth.) ("the 1922 Act").

3. Section 3 of the Act also contains the following definition:

"'first day of service' in relation to a person who is,

or has ceased to be, an eligible employee, means -

(a) except where paragraph (b) applies - the day

on which he became an eligible employee; or

(b) where the person has become an eligible

employee on more than 1 occasion - the day on

which he last became an eligible employee."

4. Accepting for the present that the somewhat circular definition of "first day of service" in the 1976 Act is intended to bring within its ambit persons who fell within the definition of "employee" in section 4 of the 1922 Act, we do not consider that the applicant fell within that definition until he commenced working in the Taxation Department on 13 July 1959. The definition of "employee" in section 4(1) of the 1922 Act relevantly included:

". . . a person employed under section eighty-one D or

eighty one P of the Commonwealth Public Service Act

1922-1958 who, immediately prior to his becoming so

employed, was a contributor to a State Fund as defined

by section sixty AN of this Act, . . ."

5. Persons employed under sections 81D and 81P of the Public Service Act, 1922 were defined in section 81A of that Act as persons transferring from particular areas of work, or who in the opinion of the Public Service Board had become redundant for specified reasons. None of the categories in the definition were established as being applicable to the applicant.

Election as to Pension Contribution Units

6. A somewhat different consideration arose under the 1922 Act with regard to units of pension contribution by employees who formerly worked for a State or for an authority of a State who were contributors to State pension funds. When the applicant transferred from State to Commonwealth employment the relevant statutory provisions were those in the 1922 Act. Under the provisions of that Act, superannuation entitlement depended upon the number of contributory units in the superannuation fund held by the person concerned upon retirement as well as upon the person attaining a prescribed retiring age (section 29). The number of units for which a member was entitled to contribute depended on the person's salary (section 13). Special provision was made in Part IVD in relation to former State employees. At the time the applicant became a member of the Commonwealth Public Service, sub-sections 60AV(1) and (3) (inserted by Act No. 49 of 1951 and, amended by Act No. 11 of 1954, and subsequently renumbered section 109 pursuant to Act No. 102 of 1959) read as follows:

"60AV. (1.) An employee who became or becomes a

contributor on or after the first day of

November, One thousand nine hundred and

fifty-one, and, immediately before becoming

an employee, was a contributor to a Public

Service Superannuation Fund may, within

three months after the date of his becoming

an employee, elect to pay to the Board the

amount refunded to him from the Public

Service Superannuation Fund upon his ceasing

to be a contributor to that Fund.

. . .

(3.)Where an amount referred to in sub-section

(1.) of this section is paid to the Board,

the Commonwealth Actuary shall certify the

number of fully paid units (including, where

necessary, a fraction of a unit) which

should be credited to the contributor, and

the contributor shall be deemed to be a

contributor for that number of units, but is

not required to make contributions in

respect of those units."

7. It would therefore appear that a former employee of a State authority who did not make the election outlined in section 60AV within three months was not liable to contribute for units equivalent to those for which he would have been entitled if he had continued to contribute to the State Fund, but only for such units as would correspond to his salary when commencing in the Commonwealth Public Service.

8. Section 60AV (as the renumbered s.109)) was repealed by Act No. 46 of 1971 and replaced by a new section 109. The substituted provision gave similar rights of election to those in Section 60AV.

9. There was a special provision made for elections outside the three month period set out in section 60AV(1), by section 76A (inserted by Act No. 53 of 1942, amended by Act No. 15 of 1945 and renumbered by Act No. 102 of 1959 as section 135). No application has ever been made under that section. We accept the submission advanced by the respondent, that following the repeal of section 60AV (renumbered section 109), and the Superannuation Act, 1922 having been superseded, it is now too late for the applicant to seek to make an election out of time. (Pearce, Statutory Interpretation 2nd edition, paragraph 128.)

Periods of Contributory Service under 1976 Act

10. The applicant is a person to whom paragraph (a) of the definition of "eligible employee" in section 3(1) applies. With regard to such persons, section 183 of the 1976 Act permits the making of Regulations modifying the Act or a provision of the Act. Such Regulations were made (Statutory Rules No. 105 of 1977). Regulation 4 provides that the modifications apply to persons who have ceased to be eligible employees. The present applicant will be in that position upon retirement. The Regulations are intended to have a prospective operation.

11. The Regulations modify the terms of the definition of "period of contributory service" in section 3(1) of the 1976 Act and provide for additional periods to those periods of service under the 1976 Act. By virtue of sub-section 193(2)(a) and section 194 (both added by Regulation 4), the applicant's contributory service is to be calculated pursuant to section 194(1)(a) from the commencement of his contributions under the 1922 Act until 30th June 1976 (commencement of the 1976 Act was on 1 July 1976).

12. If the applicant had made the election under section 60AV (later section 109) of the 1922 Act to pay the contributions refunded to him by the Rural Bank Superannuation Fund into the Commonwealth fund under section 194(1)(j) as set out in the Regulations an additional period of contributory service would have been available to him being:

"194(1)(j) where the person -

(i) became liable to contribute to the

existing Fund on or after

1 November 1951;

(ii) was, immediately before he became an

employee for the purposes of the

superseded Act, a contributor to a

Public Service Superannuation Fund

within the meaning of Part VIII of the

superseded Act; and

(iii) made an election and payment to the

Board under section 109 of that Act,

subject to sub-section (3), the period,

being the whole or a part of the period of

his previous employment, during which he was

a contributor to the Fund referred to in

sub-paragraph (ii);"

13. However he did not do so, and as indicated, we consider it is too late to make such an election.

14. To the period calculated under section 194(1)(a) there is added, pursuant to section 3(1)(d) (as modified by the Regulations amended by Statutory Rules No. 50 of 1986), the "period of contributory service" being:

"the period commencing on the commencing day and ending

on the person's last day of service (less any period

during that period when the person was on leave of

absence without pay and in respect of which sub-section

51(1) applies or when the person was absent from duty

and in respect of which sub-section 51A(1) applies);"

15. The modified section 195 has no application because Mr McCarthy's service to date from 13 July 1959 is already in excess of 20 years.

16. We accept the submissions made on behalf of the respondent that Mr McCarthy:

(i) no longer has the right to elect to pay in his

refund from 1959;

(ii) has his pension rights determined under the

1976 Act and Regulations with his period of

contributory service being from 13 July 1959 until

the date of his retirement.

Estoppel and Natural Justice

17. On 13 April 1959 the applicant wrote to the Secretary of the Public Service Board (T7 at p.27). The letter, which was sent on to the Commonwealth Superannuation Board, stated in part:

"I understood that in transferring from the Bank, which

is a Semi-Governmental institution with broadly similar

staff requirements to the Commonwealth Public Service,

that I would be taking with me full credits for Long

Service Leave and Superannuation Contributions and that

there would consequently be no break in Permanent

Service, which I understand could lead to legal

complications in the transfer of such rights.

. . .

Would you kindly consider the matters raised above and

officially advise in due course as to:-

. . .

(2) Whether credits for Long Service Leave and

Superannuation can be transferred, and if so,

whether full credits can be retained."

18. A reply to that letter was sent by the Public Service Board. Omitting formal parts the reply dated 28 April 1959 (T8 at p.29) reads as follows:

"The conditions of your appointment as Valuer, Grade 1,

Taxation Office, New South Wales, have been varied to

provide that your appointment will be effected under

Section 44, without probation, subject to prior receipt

of a satisfactory medical report, and also a copy of

your birth certificate.

Subject to there being no break between your permanent

service with the Rural Bank and your permanent

Commonwealth Service, you will be given credit for your

previous service for all purposes, including furlough.

However, if you have taken any furlough, or received

payment in lieu, your credits will be reduced by the

amount of furlough availed of or the amount for which

payment was received.

If you cease duty with the Rural Bank on a Friday and

commence duty with the Commonwealth Service on the

following Monday, this will not constitute a break in

your service.

The question of your eligibility to elect to pay to the

Commonwealth Superannuation Fund, the amount refunded to

you by the State Fund will be taken up with the

Commonwealth Superannuation Board, which will advise you

direct in the matter."

19. This was followed by a letter from the applicant to the Superannuation Board dated 30 April 1959 (T9 at p.30) which, omitting formalities, reads as follows:

"Attached is copy of a letter I have received from the

Secretary of the Commonwealth Public Service Board and

your attention is invited to it.

With regard to the final paragraph, I wish to state that

if there is any choice in the matter, I would prefer to

retain the refund of my capital contributions from the

State Superannuation Fund and contribute to the

Commonwealth Fund at the necessarily higher rate.

In this connection it is advised that for the last

sixteen years I have been on the staff of the Rural Bank

of New South Wales and while on Country Service have

occupied rented cottages. It will now be necessary for

me to find accommodation in Sydney and there is no

alternative to purchasing a home to satisfy this need.

The refund from the State Superannuation Board will

greatly assist in the purchase of a home and I therefore

request the consideration of your Board to allow me to

retain the refund and commence immediate contributions

to the Commonwealth Fund, at the appropriate rate,

following the taking up of my appointment.

As I am at present conducting preliminary negotiations

in the matter of home purchase, the favour of an early

decision would be very much appreciated."

20. The Superannuation Board then replied to the applicant by letter of 14 May 1959 (T11 at p.32) which omitting formalities is as follows:

"I desire to acknowledge the receipt of your letter of

the 30th April, 1959, relative to your proposed

appointment to the Commonwealth Public Service.

It will be in order for you to accept the refund of

contributions due to you from the State Fund, and enter

the Commonwealth Superannuation Fund as a new

contributor for the number of units applicable to the

maximum of your salary group.

Details of age and salary are not known in this office,

but the attached Superannuation Booklet will assist you

in working out the cost of units."

21. The applicant claimed that the period of his service with the Rural Bank should be counted in determining his superannuation rights when he retires from the Commonwealth Public Service. That claim was put forth in a letter of 24 April 1972 (T12 at p.33) and that view appears to have been accepted initially at 2 June 1972 (T13 at p.34).

22. However by letter of 16 July 1981 (T15 at p.37) the respondent informed the applicant that his service with the Rural Bank was recognised as "eligible employment" for the purposes of the Superannuation Act, 1976 but not as contributory service.

23. Submissions by Mr McCarthy suggest that there is some sort of estoppel or alternatively a denial of natural justice should the respondent be now permitted to resile from the attitude previously expressed. We do not agree. At all times the applicant's pension rights have been regulated by the Statutes or Regulations in force. If incorrect advice was given, the respondent is still bound to administer the 1976 Act according to law. In any event this Tribunal is not invested with power to determine questions of the type raised. It stands in the shoes of the respondent and likewise is bound by the 1976 Act and the Regulations.

24. Mr McCarthy informed the Tribunal that he has applied to the Department of Finance for an ex gratia payment because of the circumstances affecting his transfer from State to Federal employment. We note this fact and merely observe that it is a matter for the Department concerned.

Result

25. The decision under review is affirmed.
