AT86/276 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 412; 87 ATC 1116 (17 November 1987)

Last Updated: 30 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - deduction – requirement to move for employment -cost of maintaining residence - whether incurred in gaining assessable income

Income Tax - deduction - expense not claimed in return or upon objection - whether power to hear

Income Tax - deduction - membership of clubs - specific provision of Act

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss.51(1) , 51AB

Case U91 87 ATC 525 Case B18 70 ATC 78

Deputy President RK Todd

REASONS FOR DECISION

17 November 1987 Deputy President RK Todd

  1. The applicant in this reference, who presented his own case at the hearing, was a senior Commonwealth public servant who claimed various deductions said to have been incurred in relation to his transfer from Canberra to a State capital to work in a regional office. He moved at the request of his superiors on a "term transfer" which commenced on 4 July 1983.
  2. The matters in dispute were set out in the applicant's submission as being:

"(i) whether or not I was entitled to claim that part of the cost of my earning my 1983/84 primary income were the inescapable costs of retaining my home and other unusable facilities in Canberra and M (a seaside town), as follows-

preliminary agreed net income 54044

less Canberra - rates 500

int' on mortgage 5747

various clubs 247

M - rates 450

int' on mortgage 2850

Total 10794

Thus net taxable income 83/84 43250

(ii) in the event of the first issue being decided in my favour, whether my late wife paid any or all of the above amounts."

  1. As no claim had been made for the expenses relating to M in the applicant's taxation return for the year ended 30 June 1984, which was the year before the Tribunal, there is therefore no objection decision on that matter and consequently the Tribunal has no power to decide whether or not the deduction claimed is allowable.
  2. The claim for the cost of membership of various clubs was based on the argument that:

"Maintenance of these types of memberships of Clubs and related facilities in circumstances of term postings is clearly a cost associated with the function of earning ones prime income and, as such is clearly deductible."

  1. Unfortunately the applicant had not considered the provisions of s51AB of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the ITAA") which, notwithstanding any other provision in the Act, state specifically that such membership expenses are "not an allowable deduction". As all of the clubs listed are clearly within the definition of "club" in s51AB (1) the amounts claimed are not deductible.
  2. In his taxation return, the applicant claimed and was allowed a deduction of the rent which he paid in the State capital. The basis for the allowance of this deduction appears to be that the applicant received an allowance of an approximately equal amount from his employer which was returned as income. The allowance of this deduction is interesting taking into account the Commissioner's disallowance of a very similar claim, on the basis that it is private expenditure, in a number of other matters currently before the Tribunal.
  3. The applicant returned an amount of $1,400 as rental income on his Canberra house and claimed deductions totalling $6,247 relating to that property. Both on assessment and objection the Commissioner disallowed the deductions and treated the income as non-assessable. From the applicant's return it would appear that he had been claiming deductions in respect of the house which he maintained in Canberra totalling $6,247 as having been incurred in earning $1,400 of rental income. At the hearing, however, he argued that these costs were incurred in earning his primary income.
  4. Both in his submission, and in answer to questions from the Commissioner's representative, the applicant agreed that the renting arrangement was "a family arrangement". I believe that this is a very correct concession and I will set out briefly the facts that confirm that conclusion:

(i) The occupants of the house during the rental period were the applicant's stepdaughter and her husband

(ii) The house was owned by the applicant, his wife, his stepson and the stepdaughter in equal shares as tenants-in-common.

(iii) An agreement, which was unsigned but said by the applicant to express the intention of the parties, between the four owners and the stepdaughter's husband contained a clause stating that the stepdaughter and her husband "live rent-free" in the house while the applicant was in the State capital

(iv) Because he was worried about damage that may be done by tenants not known to him, the applicant made no effort to find other tenants even after the stepdaughter and her husband vacated the house in April 1984

(v) The rent charged was $50 per week compared with the estimate of $130 given by a Taxation Office Valuer as the appropriate market rent.

  1. Having determined that the deductions should not be dealt with on the basis that the expenses were incurred in gaining or producing the rental income or in the carrying on of a business, it is necessary now to consider the applicant's submission that the expenses were losses or outgoings incurred in gaining or producing his income from the Commonwealth, and were not of a capital, private or domestic nature.
  2. Reference was made during the hearing to Case U91 87 ATC 525 which involved a claim by a senior public servant for a deduction for the cost of auctioning a home when he was required to move cities by his superiors. In that case I quoted Mr. G.R. Thompson (Member) in Case B18 70 ATC 78 in which he stated at p87:

"The problem with which the Board is concerned, it seems to me, is one stage more remote from the income producing process than the payment of fares from home to place of employment, for here the items in question relate to the disposal of one home base and the acquisition of another. This takes the task of equating the losses or outgoings claimed by the taxpayer, and now under objection, with the process of gaining or producing assessable income out of the realm of mere difficulty into the area of impossibility. Deductibility under either limb of section 51(1) is thus denied without turning to the express exception of losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature to be found in that section.

If it were necessary to consider that exception, and it were necessary to describe the essential nature of commission and stamp duties relating to the sale and purchase of family homes, one could not find a more apt adjective than 'domestic', for the Shorter Oxford English Dictionary includes the following meaning to that word: 'Of or belonging to the home, house or household; household, home, family'."

  1. The only factual distinction between these cases is that those above involve the cost of disposing of a domestic residence, whereas the immediate case involves the cost of retention of a private dwelling.
  2. I find it to be an inescapable conclusion that the rates and interest payments relating to the Canberra house were in no way incurred in the production of the applicant's income. The only expenses that may be characterised as such are the rent payments, discussed above, that were made in the State capital, although the deductibility of even those would be doubtful.
  3. This finding makes it unnecessary for me to consider the matter raised in para 2(ii) above. For the above reasons the Commissioner's decision on objection is affirmed.
