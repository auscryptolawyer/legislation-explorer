VT88/600 and Commissioner of Taxation [1991] AATA 350 (18 January 1991)

Last Updated: 1 July 2008

Taxation - applicant and wife directors and only shareholders of Trustee Co. of their family trust - applicant a media personality - Trustee Co. had management and control of applicant's performing services - Trustee Co. received payment from X Co. as part of arrangement where Trustee Co. breached existing contract with N Co. - issue was whether that payment was assessable income of the applicant - Tribunal found that money was properly paid to Trustee Co. as trustee of trust and not as agent or representative of applicant - s. 260 and Part IVA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 considered but found not applicable - objection allowed in full



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 Part IVA, ss. 189, 260



Jones v Dunkel [1959] HCA 8; (1959) 101 CLR 298



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. VT88/600

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re: VT88/600

Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



DECISION



Tribunal : Deputy President I. R. Thompson



Date : 18 January 1991



Place : Melbourne



Decision : The objection decision under review is set aside and the taxpayer's objection is allowed in full.



(Sgd I. R. Thompson)

....................

Deputy President

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. VT88/600

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re: VT88/600

Applicant



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



REASONS FOR DECISION



18 January 1991 Deputy President I. R. Thompson



1. The objection decision of the respondent which is under review in these proceedings was referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal pursuant to section 189(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). The referral of the decision is deemed by section 189(2) to constitute the making by the taxpayer of an application to the Tribunal for review of the decision. In these proceedings I shall refer to the taxpayer as the applicant. At the hearing of his deemed application he was was represented by Mr P. K. Searle, of Counsel; the respondent was represented by Mr B. J. Shaw, of Her Majesty's Counsel, and Mr G. A. A. Nettle, of Counsel.



2. The applicant may appropriately be described as a radio and television personality. In order to comply with the provisions of sub-section (2C) which is taken to be added to section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 for the purposes of proceedings such as these (see section 14ZK of the Taxation Administration Act 1953), it is necessary to state the facts in fairly broad terms so that so far as is practical, his identity is not disclosed in this statement of the reasons for the decision in these proceedings. However, they are fully set out in documents received in evidence at the hearing.



3. The applicant has worked in the radio and television industry for a considerable number of years. In the mid-1970s he and his wife, W, incorporated a company, H Co. At all times since then the only shareholders of the company have been the applicant and W; they have also been its directors. The applicant has acted on behalf of H Co. in its dealings with other persons. In 1980 a family trust, the H Trust, was established with H Co. as the trustee, with the specified beneficiaries being W and the applicant's children, with the general beneficiaries being substantially as provided for in most family trust instruments and with the applicant and W as additional members of the class of general beneficiaries. Since the trust was established, the applicant has had the power to remove the trustee, to appoint an additional trustee or trustees and to appoint a new trustee or trustees.



4. In May 1986 a company, X Co., which owned and operated a radio station broadcasting in Melbourne, paid a substantial sum of money into H Co.'s bank account. In the income tax return of the H Trust for the taxation year which ended on 30 June 1986 the receipt of that sum of money was disclosed. It was stated to be a capital receipt. A note explaining why it was considered to be a capital receipt was attached to the return. Also attached to the return was a copy of minutes of a meeting of the directors of H Co., that is to say the applicant and W, at which a resolution was passed for the distribution of the income of the trust. In effect the whole of the amount of the payment shown in the return as a capital receipt was distributed to W and C, who is a child of W and the applicant. The receipt by W and C of the amounts so distributed was disclosed in their income tax returns for that year but was claimed to be a distribution of capital; a note of the reasons why it was so regarded, which was in similar terms to the note attached to the H Trust's return, was attached to each of their returns. No part of the amount of the payment shown in the H Trust's return as a capital receipt was disclosed as income of the applicant in his income tax return; but again a note, similar in its terms to that attached to the H Trust's, W's and C's returns, was attached to the applicant's return.



5. The respondent formed the opinion that the whole of the amount was assessable income of the applicant; he assessed accordingly the applicant's income and his liability to pay income tax. The applicant objected to the inclusion of that amount in his assessable income and to the liability to pay income tax in respect of it but the respondent decided to disallow his objection. That is the objection decision which is under review in these proceedings. It must be stressed, therefore, that in these proceedings the Tribunal is concerned only with whether the amount which was received and stated in the returns of the H Trust, W, C and the applicant to be a capital receipt was assessable income of the applicant. The Tribunal is not concerned with any liability of the H Trust, W or C to pay tax in respect of the amount. At the completion of the hearing of the evidence and the addresses Mr Shaw acknowledged that the respondent's case depended on a finding being made either that the money was received by H Co. as agent or representative of the applicant or otherwise on his behalf or that, notwithstanding that it was received by H Co. on its own behalf or as trustee of the H Trust, because of the provisions of either section 260 or Part IVA of the Act it was to be taken to form part of the assessable income of the applicant.



6. In order that the evidence relating to the payment may be viewed in the context in which it was made, it is necessary to set out some facts relating to the arrangements which previously existed for the applicant to work for a company which operated another radio broadcasting station, N Co., and to the other activities of H Co. and the H Trust. The documents tendered in evidence disclose that in 1981 and again in 1983 H Co. entered into an agreement with N Co. to provide N Co. with the applicant's services to undertake certain work as a radio broadcaster. The agreements provided for payment to be made by N Co. to H Co. for H Co. doing that. In each of the agreements it was recited that, by reason of an agreement between H Co. and the applicant, H Co. had the management and control of the applicant's performing services.



7. In the first of the agreements H Co. formally warranted that it had entered into a service contract with the applicant by which it was enabled to enter into the agreement to provide his services. That agreement was signed on behalf of H Co. by the applicant and W. Annexed to it was a deed of guarantee by the applicant guaranteeing the due performance and observance by H Co. of its obligations under the agreement. The second agreement was signed on behalf of H Co. by the applicant. Among the documents tendered in evidence was an agreement between H Co. and the applicant made in March 1983, at the time when the second agreement with N Co. was entered into, in which again the recitals included a statement that H Co. had by contract the management and control of the applicant's performing services and which provided that H Co. would enforce and prosecute to the full all its rights and remedies against N Co. if and whenever the applicant required it to do so.



8. Also tendered in evidence was an unsigned draft agreement prepared for execution on 2 November 1984 providing for H Co. to engage and employ the applicant for a period of three years and for the applicant to perform such services as were required of him pursuant to contracts entered into by H Co. The applicant gave oral evidence that the draft agreement, which was incomplete because the amount of the salary to be paid to him by H Co. had not been inserted, was not signed because he believed that there was a valid subsisting agreement to provide his services to H Co. He said that an extensive search had been carried out to try to locate that agreement but had been unsuccessful. In view of the documentary evidence of which I have given the details above, and in particular in view of the fact that both the agreements with the radio broadcasting company which contained the recitals, and additionally in one case the warranty, were signed by the applicant on behalf of H Co., I accept his evidence that there was for a number of years before May 1986 an agreement for H Co. to manage and control the applicant's performing services.



9. The income tax return of the H Trust discloses income from activities other than the performance of services by the applicant. The applicant gave oral evidence of the nature of those activities and there was other documentary evidence relating to them. They were related mainly to the design, manufacture and marketing of articles of wearing apparel in the taxation year which ended on 30 June 1986. The income from those activities exceeded $75,000, although the expenditure incurred in producing that income exceeded $65,000. It is clear that, at least by 1986, H Co. as trustee of the H Trust was engaging in substantial business activities in addition to managing and controlling the applicant's performing services as a radio broadcaster.



10. The circumstances in which the payment of the amount which is in issue in these proceedings was made was that early in 1986 there was a subsisting agreement between H Co. and N Co. for H Co. to provide to N Co. the services of the applicant as a radio broadcaster. The applicant was approached by a person, Y, representing X Co. The applicant gave evidence, which I accept, that Y asked the applicant to meet him and as a result a meeting was held in April 1986. At that meeting Y told the applicant that X Co. wished to obtain the applicant's services, that he was aware that those services were being performed currently for N Co. under a contract between N Co. and H Co. and that X Co. was willing to meet the cost of that agreement being breached by the applicant's ceasing to perform as a broadcaster for N Co. The applicant gave evidence that there was agreement in very broad terms about what X Co. would pay for the applicant's services, and that it was agreed that he should stop performing services for N Co. when notified by X Co. to do so and should commence performing services for X Co. at a later date to be notified to him by Y. In the meantime he was not to disclose to anyone the agreement which they had made and he was to regard himself as committed to X Co.; he was therefore not to respond to any approach which might be made to him by the operator of any other radio broadcasting station. The applicant said that Y knew very well that the agreement with N Co. was made by H Co., not by the applicant personally. Consequently, he had assumed that Y was dealing with him as director of H Co. and intended that the agreement for the applicant to perform services for X Co. would be made between H Co. and X Co.



11. His evidence of what occurred at the meeting in April 1986 did not fully accord with facts set out in a statement of further and better particulars provided to the respondent by the applicant's solicitor on 4 September 1990. In that statement the facts were alleged to be that at the meeting it was agreed that H Co. would provide the services of the applicant to X Co. from 30 June 1986 in return for a specified sum of money per week and that it had further been agreed that the sum of money which is the subject of the dispute in these proceedings would be paid to H Co. in consideration for it agreeing not to negotiate with, or provide the services of the applicant to, any other radio station in Australia during the currency of the agreement. The applicant gave evidence that he had provided that information to his solicitors in September and had at that time believed it to be true. Subsequently, however, he had discussed with his wife the sequence of events and had realized that the agreement reached at the meeting in April had not been in such specific terms and had instead been as related by him in his evidence to the Tribunal.



12. Evidence was given that Y was now dead. If, therefore, the applicant had given oral evidence that the facts were as set out in the statement of further and better particulars, it is unlikely that the respondent would have been able to contravert what he had said. Further, the evidence at the hearing which he gave could not, I consider, have been perceived by him to be more advantageous to his case than the facts set out in the statement of further and better particulars. I have come to the conclusion, therefore, that I should accept that he was telling the truth when he gave his evidence and that what occurred at the meeting in April 1986 was as he stated it to be in giving that evidence.



13. The applicant gave evidence that at all relevant times Z Co., of which A was the principal, was entitled to be paid a commission in respect of all fees paid for his services. Those fees were to be paid because Z Co. represented H Co. in negotiations with persons for whom the applicant was to perform services. He said that, after his meeting with Y in April 1986, he told A of the meeting so that A should conduct the negotiations with Y from then on. He gave evidence that advice was also given by the firm of accountants which performed accounting services for H Co., the applicant, W and C. He said that the payment of the sum of money which is in dispute in these proceedings was made as the result of the negotiations which took place between A and Y. He had himself not taken part in those negotiations. A had told him of the purpose for which the sum of money was paid to H Co; he had relied on what A told him.



14. The documents tendered in evidence disclose that the agreement between H Co. and N Co. provided for a substantial amount of money to be paid to H Co. in the period between 8 January 1986 and 7 January 1987 in consideration of the provision of the applicant's services to N Co. It was to be paid by 52 instalments; but the first instalment was an amount more than one-half of the total of all the instalments. The documents before the Tribunal disclose also that in May 1986 N Co., H Co. and the applicant entered into an agreement by which N Co. released H Co. and the applicant from all their obligations under the agreement and which provided for H Co. to pay to N Co. an amount described as "a refund of amounts received over and above the pro-rata weekly entitlement to the annual sum" payable to H Co. under its agreement with N Co. to provide the applicant's services.



15. The applicant gave evidence that he ceased performing services for N Co. on 30 April 1986 and did not commence performing services for X Co. until 30 June or 1 July. When he was cross-examined, it was put to him that the amount of the payment which is the subject of the dispute in these proceedings was approximately the sum of the amount paid by H Co. to N Co. under the release agreement and an amount which was one-sixth of the total amount that would have been payable by N Co. for the whole 12 months and, therefore, compensation to H Co. for the loss of income for the period from 1 May to 29 or 30 June when the applicant was not performing services for N Co. or X Co. The applicant agreed that the payment might be seen as having that character. However, he said that he had been told by A that it was made in consideration of H Co. not negotiating with, and not providing the applicant's services to, the operator of any other radio station.



16. The applicant gave evidence that at the time of the hearing A was still the agent who negotiated with radio and television stations for the provision of his services. No evidence was given that A was not available to give evidence. However, he did not do so. In those circumstances it is proper for the Tribunal to draw an inference that, if A had given evidence, his evidence would not have assisted the applicant to establish facts supportive of his case (Jones v Dunkel [1959] HCA 8; (1959) 101 CLR 298). Mr Shaw asked the Tribunal to draw such an inference. He pointed out that section 190(b) of the Act imposes on the applicant the burden of proving that the assessment is excessive and, therefore, of adducing evidence to show that. I accept that the submission was sound. While it is impossible for the Tribunal to make any firm finding as to the reason why the amount was paid by X Co. into H Co.'s bank account, I am satisfied that it was paid either to compensate for the loss of income in May and June and for the payment by H Co. to N Co. under the release agreement or in consideration of H Co. undertaking not to negotiate with, and not to provide the services of the applicant to the operator of any other radio station during the currency of whatever agreement was entered into between X Co. and H Co. for the provision of the applicant's services to X Co. or in consideration of H Co. undertaking not to negotiate with, and not to provide the applicant's services to the operator of any other radio station before the end of June 1986. If the fact that the payment was made for any one or other of those purposes gave it the character of assessable income of the applicant, he must take the consequences of not having arranged for A to give evidence which might have disproved that fact.



17. It is possible now to deal with the first of the bases on which the respondent asserts that the money paid into H Co.'s bank account by X Co. in May 1986 was assessable income of the applicant, that is to say that it was received by H Co. as the agent or representative of the applicant or otherwise on his behalf and not in its capacity as trustee of the H Trust. In addition to the evidence to which I have already referred, documentary evidence was received of an agreement entered into in 1984 between H Co. and a company which operates a television station for H Co. to provide to that company "the talents and services" of the applicant as a television performer. There was also a letter, apparently written in March 1987, by X Co. to the applicant personally in which X Co. set out the terms on which H Co. would provide the applicant's services to X Co. for radio work.



18. It is clear from all the documentary evidence, as well as from the oral evidence given by the applicant, that since at least 1981 all agreements for the applicant to perform work for the operator of a radio station or of a television station have been made by H Co.; it has agreed to provide his services to the operator. That being so, I have no doubt whatsoever that the arrangements which were made initially by the applicant and Y at their meeting in April 1986 and subsequently by A in the negotiations with Y which led to the payment of the money which is the subject matter of the dispute in these proceedings were for the services of the applicant to X Co. to be provided by H Co. I accept the applicant's evidence that, from the time when the H Trust was established, H Co. did that in its capacity as trustee of the H Trust. I am satisfied that the money was paid to H Co. because it was the principal in the agreement with X Co. for the provision of the applicant's services.



19. If the payment was made in consideration for an undertaking that no negotiations would be conducted with, and no agreement would be made to provide the applicant's services to, the operator of any other radio station, whether the undertaking related to the period to the end of June 1986 or the duration of the agreement to be made with X Co. for the provision of the applicant's services to it, I am satisfied that the money was paid to H Co. as trustee of the H Trust. If it was paid, in Mr Shaw's words, "to fill the gap" left by payment of money by H Co. to N Co. under the release agreement and by the loss of income in May and June, again I am satisfied that it was paid to H Co. as trustee of H Trust. I find, therefore, that H Co. did not receive the money as agent for or representative of the applicant or otherwise on his behalf.



20. The money paid to H Co. cannot, therefore, be included in the applicant's assessable income unless either section 260 or Part IVA of the Act is applicable. Section 260 applies only to contracts, agreements and arrangements made or entered into before 28 May 1981 (see section 260(2)); Part IVA applies only in respect of schemes entered into or carried out after 27 May 1981 (see section 177D(1)).



21. So far as section 260 is concerned, Mr Shaw submitted that in May 1986 there was an arrangement which had been entered into before 28 May 1981 which had the purpose or effect of altering the incidence of income tax and of relieving the applicant of his liability to pay income tax and which was, therefore, absolutely void as against the Commissioner. That arrangement, he submitted, came about as the result of the incorporation of H Co., the establishment of the H Trust and the agreement between the applicant and H Co. that H Co. should control and manage the provision of the applicant's services as a radio or television performer.



22. The applicant gave evidence that he was advised by his accountant to set up H Co. and to establish the H Family Trust. He said that he had intended to enter into contracts elsewhere than in radio and television and had been advised that he should take steps to obtain limited liability. It was for that purpose that H Co. had been incorporated; it had not been incorporated for the purpose of gaining relief from taxation. He admitted that from the time H Co. was incorporated contracts for the provision of his services had been made by H Co. So far as the H Trust was concerned, he said that it was established because of the precarious nature of a career as a radio and television performer; at times the income from it might be considerable but at other times it might be very small. That evidence accords with what is publicly known of the effect of the vagaries and inconstancy of public taste on the earning capacity of such performers. The applicant said the family trust was established so that in the good years there could be distribution to members of his family, which would set them up to withstand the bad years.



23. The income tax returns of the H Trust for the years before 1986 were not tendered in evidence but in the return for 1986 the income was brought to account under two different heads, on the one hand as "personal exertion income" of the applicant and on the other as "trust income". The resolution passed by the directors of H Co. for the distribution of the income of the trust provided that all income deemed to be personal exertion income attributed to the applicant was to be distributed to him. The applicant gave evidence that that resolution was prepared by the accountant of H Co.; the directors passed it without further consideration. If the amount which is the subject of the dispute in these proceedings is deducted from the balance of the distributed income, the remaining amount distributed to W and C was less than $10,000 in total. The applicant's accountants considered that the payment at issue was a capital receipt and that the provisions of Part IIIA of the Act (which make capital gains subject to taxation in certain circumstances) were not applicable to it.



24. If the arrangement referred to by Mr Shaw had been entered into for the purpose of diverting the applicant's income from his personal exertions to members of his family, it might have been expected that the accounts would not have been presented in the return in the manner in which they were and that a resolution would have been passed to distribute more of the income of the trust to W and C than it was resolved to distribute. I regard the fact that the accounts were presented as they were and that the distribution to W and C did not include any amount which was unarguably attributable to income from the provision of the applicant's personal service as cogent evidence that the arrangements referred to by Mr Shaw were in 1986 and by inference in previous years a proper and sensible means of ordering affairs and were not entered into for any of the purposes referred to in section 260 of the Act. Consequently, I find that section 260 does not render those arrangements void as against the respondent.



25. Section 177F of the Act empowers the respondent to make determinations having the effect of cancelling tax benefits which would but for that section be obtained by taxpayers in connection with schemes to which Part IVA applies. The obtaining of a tax benefit by a taxpayer in connection with a scheme encompasses the situation where an amount is not included in his assessable income which would have been included, or might reasonably be expected to have been included, if the scheme had not been entered into or carried out. A scheme includes any arrangement, whether or not enforceable or intended to be enforceable by legal proceedings, and also a course of action or conduct. Part IVA applies to such a scheme if, when regard is had to the matters set out in section 177D, it would be concluded that the scheme was entered into or carried out for the purpose of enabling the taxpayer to obtain a tax benefit in connection with the scheme. In the present case, therefore, a determination could be made under section 177F only if the amount which was paid by X Co. to H Co. in May 1986 would have been included, or might reasonably be expected to have been included, in the applicant's assessable income if the scheme had not been entered into or carried out.



26. At the hearing there was considerable argument whether the payment was received by H Co. as income or as a capital receipt. In my view it is unnecessary for that question to be decided in these proceedings; a decision whether there was a scheme which had the purpose of enabling the applicant to obtain a tax benefit can be reached without doing so. If there was a scheme, the arrangement or course of conduct which might be regarded as constituting it could be the whole of the arrangements and course of conduct from 1981, when H Co. entered into the agreement with N Co. to provide the applicant's services to it, up to the date when the payment was made by X Co. to H Co. Alternatively, it might be regarded as limited to the dealing by the applicant and A with Y in April and May 1986. So far as the first of those possibilities is concerned, the reasons which have caused me to conclude that arrangements entered into before 28 May 1981 and continued up to the date of the payment by X Co. to H Co. did not bring section 260 into operation lead to a similar conclusion in relation to Part IVA in respect of the arrangements and course of conduct followed by the applicant and H Co. in 1981 and thereafter. I find, therefore, that those arrangements and that course of conduct, which included the making of the agreement by H Co. and N Co. in force in 1986 until terminated by the release agreement on 25 May 1986, was not a scheme which was entered into or carried out for the purpose of enabling the applicant to obtain a tax benefit in connection with it.



27. If, in the alternative, the scheme is taken to have been constituted by the dealings with Y in April and May 1986, the existence of the agreement between H Co. and N Co. is a matter of great significance. The first instalment of the fees payable under that agreement to H Co. for providing the applicant's services had been paid to H Co. It was H Co. which bore any liability which had arisen from the applicant ceasing to do work for N Co. Consequently, if the payment by X Co. was made to compensate for the payment which had to be made by H Co. to N Co. in respect of that first instalment of the fee and for the loss of income from the provision of the applicant's services in May and June 1986, the payment was appropriately made to H Co.; it would have been inappropriate if it had been made to the applicant personally. On that basis, the arrangements or course of conduct did not constitute a scheme to which Part IVA was applicable. If, on the other hand, the payment was made in consideration of an undertaking being given that no negotiations would be entered into with, and no agreement made for the provision of the applicant's services to, the operator of any other radio station, whether that undertaking related to the period for which H Co. was to provide the applicant's services to X Co. or to the period up to 30 June 1986 when it was to commence providing them, the course of conduct followed by the applicant and H Co. in the past made it entirely appropriate that it should be H Co., and not the applicant personally, which gave the undertaking. Consequently, it was H Co. which was legally entitled to receive the payment. On that basis also, therefore, the arrangement or course of conduct which resulted in that legal entitlement was, I am satisfied, not a scheme to which Part IVA of the Act applied.



28. In coming to the conclusions which I have reached, I have taken into account the fact that the payment was recorded by H Co. as a capital receipt and not as income. If it was not income and it was a capital receipt (as to which I make no finding), it was properly brought to account in that manner. However, it is possible that Part IIIA of the Act had the effect that it was taxable as a capital gain. Nevertheless, even if it should be so characterized or if it was income from the provision of the applicant's services, the reasoning which forms the basis of the conclusions which I have reached is not affected by that. The arrangements or course of conduct which involved H Co. entering into agreement with others for the provision of the applicant's professional services was one of long standing, predating by several years the addition of Part IIIA to the Act. The dealing with Y and X Co. has to be viewed in the context of that long-standing arrangement or course of conduct. When that is done, the manner in which the money received was brought to account by H Co. is seen to be consistent with the long-standing arrangement or course of conduct and not as evidence of a departure from it. As I have already stated, I am satisfied that no scheme was entered into or carried out for the purpose of enabling the applicant to obtain a tax benefit in connection with it. H Co. was legally entitled to be paid the amount which it received. Whether or not the money was a capital receipt, and whether or not Part IIIA of the Act was applicable to it (as to which I express no opinion), H Co. was legally entitled to receive it; the applicant was not legally entitled to receive it. There is no provision of the Act by reason of which it is to be taken to have been part of his assessable income.



29. Accordingly, the objection decision under review is set aside and the objection allowed in full.
