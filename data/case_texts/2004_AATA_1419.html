Senior Member McCabe

CATCHWORDS

PROFESSIONAL QUALIFICATIONS – tax agents – eligibility for original registration –applicant is not a fit and proper person – applicant does not have appropriate relevant experience – registration refused

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss251BC, 251JA

Income Tax Regulations 1936 s156

REASONS FOR DECISION

14 January 2005 Senior Member McCabe

INTRODUCTION

1.Ms Judith Xavier applied to the Tax Agents’ Board of Queensland to become registered as a tax agent. The Board rejected her application on 21 June 2004. Its reasons for doing so are set out at folio 3 of the documents filed pursuant to s 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (the T-documents). Ms Xavier asked the Tribunal to review the decision. The matter was heard on 20 December 2004. After hearing all of the evidence and considering the law, I decided the decision should be affirmed. I gave reasons for my decision on that occasion. The applicant has asked me to provide a written statement of those reasons.

THE MATERIAL BEFORE THE TRIBUNAL

2.The Tribunal was provided with the T-documents. The applicant also filed a schedule of her employment, describing the extent of her experience in completing taxation returns.
3.The applicant gave evidence, as did Mr Langdon. Mr Langdon is a partner in an accounting firm and a tax agent. He supervised the applicant’s work.
4.The applicant was represented at the hearing by Professor Ian Zimmer. Professor Zimmer is a professor of accounting at the University of Queensland. The respondent was represented by Mr Matthews of counsel.

THE STATUTORY REGIME REGULATING THE REGISTRATION OF TAX AGENTS

5.A person seeking original registration as a tax agent must satisfy the criteria set out in s 251JA of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (the Act). That section says the Board must be satisfied the applicant is a "fit and proper person": s 251JA(1)(a)(i). Section 251BC(1) sets out a number of matters that will prevent a person from being considered a fit and proper person for the purposes of s 251JA. One of those matters relates to the person’s experience. If a "person does not hold such qualifications (whether academic, by way of experience or otherwise) as are prescribed", they cannot be considered a fit and proper person: s 251BC(1)(b)(ii).
6.The prescription of qualifications or experience can be found in the regulations. Section 156(1) of the Income Tax Regulations 1936 sets out the requirements in some detail. Those provisions refer in particular to an expectation that the applicant has been engaged in relevant employment (or its equivalent) for an appropriate period of time. The expression relevant employment is defined in s 156(2) of the regulations. The provision says:
In this Regulation relevant employment means employment by a person or a partnership or as a member of a partnership in the course of which there has been substantial involvement in income tax matters including:

(a) the preparation or examination of a broad range of income tax returns;

(b) the preparation or examination of objections to assessments issued in respect of such returns; and

(c) the provision of advice in relation to income tax returns, assessments or objections.

CAN THE APPLICANT ESTABLISH SHE IS A FIT AND PROPER PERSON?

7.I am satisfied the applicant is unable to establish she is a fit and proper person within the meaning of s 251JA because she does not possess the prescribed qualifications. Specifically, she does not have appropriate relevant experience as that expression is defined in s 156(2) of the Regulations.
8.The applicant conceded in her evidence that she had not been involved in the preparation of objections to assessments, and she had little experience in examining or advising in relation to objections. That is fatal to her application. I note from the schedule detailing her work experience that she has limited experience in preparing and examining returns for partnerships, companies, trusts and super funds.

CONCLUSION

9.I am not satisfied the applicant has been engaged in other employment that might be a substitute for the relevant experience required under the Act and the regulations. In those circumstances, the application must fail. There is no need in the circumstances to consider the other evidence.


I certify that the 9 preceding paragraphs are a true copy of the reasons for the decision herein of Senior Member McCabe.
