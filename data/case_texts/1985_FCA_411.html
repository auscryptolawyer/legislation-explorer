Re Murray v Vilkelis-Curas Ex Parte: Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1985] FCA 411 (4 November 1985)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: MURRAY V. VILKELIS-CURAS

Ex Parte: DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

No. P360 of 1985

Costs

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

GENERAL DIVISION

BANKRUPTCY DISTRICT OF THE STATE OF VICTORIA

Jenkinson J.

CATCHWORDS

Costs - bankruptcy - dismissal of creditor's petition on ground of debtor's solvency - necessity for enquiry by petitioner as to debtor's solvency - discretion of award costs to petitioner.

Re Australia and New Zealand Savings Bank Ltd. : Mellas v. Evriniadis [1972] VicRp 79; (1972) V.R. 690

The Commissioner of Taxation and Ors. v. The Australia and New Zealand Banking Group Ltd. (1979) 143 C.L.R. 499

Re Noye; Ex Parte Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1956) 18 A.B.C. 77

Sarina v. Council of the Shire of Wollondilly [1980] FCA 138; (1980) 48 F.L.R. 372

Bankruptcy Act 1966 - ss. 52(2)(a), 118

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s.264

Magistrates (Summary Proceedings) Act 1975 (Vic.) - Part XIV

HEARING

MELBOURNE

4:11:1985

DECISION

Application for an order that the petitioning creditor's costs of his petition for a sequestration order against the debtor's estate, which is to be dismissed, be paid by the debtor; and an application by the debtor that his costs of the petition be paid by the petitioner.

2. The petition was founded on the debtor's failure to

comply with the requirements of a bankruptcy notice, which was based on a final order of the Magistrates' Court at Melbourne, made on 9 November 1983, that the debtor pay the petitioner $1,278.16 and $529.50 for costs. The amount claimed in the bankruptcy notice to be due was $2,043.19, which sum included statutory interest on the amount ordered by the Magistrates' Court to be paid. The original indebtedness of the debtor, out of which the rest grew, was to the Commonwealth for income tax assessed in respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1979, in the sum of $930.71. The income tax remaining unpaid, additional tax accrued.

3. From the time of assessment in 1980 the debtor had

explained to the Commissioner that he was unwilling to pay the tax in augmentation of public funds out of which, as he alleged, the subvention of voluntary abortion was being effected. He proposed that he be "given a guarantee declaring that my money will not be used in subsidising or aiding abortions", or that "I be allowed to make an equivalent payment of my taxation dues to a mutually acceptable charity or a government department not participating in these acts".

4. In March 1983 the debtor deposited approximately $1,430

in the Mildura Parish Provident Fund. The evidence does not disclose the legal effects of that transaction, but might justify an inference that the sum deposited was repayable to the debtor on demand and that interest accrued at the rate of 7 per centum per annum on the amount deposited. Mr. Tavolaro, who appeared for the petitioning creditor, was content to assume that some legal person owed the amount of the deposit to the debtor and was under a contractual liability to repay the debt on demand. I shall make the same assumptions. But the submission of Mr. P. Santamaria, of counsel for the debtor, that satisfaction of the Magistrates' Court order might have been had by attachment of the Mildura Parish Provident Fund debt, under Part XIV of the Magistrates (Summary Proceedings) Act 1975, fails for lack of evidence as to the terms on which the deposit was repayable : see Re Australia and New Zealand Savings Bank Ltd; Mellas v. Evriniadis [1972] VicRp 79; (1972) V.R. 690. I will, however, assume that satisfaction of the order might have been had by proceedings under Part XIV of that Act or by execution in some other mode.

5. On the hearing of the petition it appeared by evidence

adduced on the debtor's behalf that the case was of the description specified in s.52(2)(a) of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 - the Court was "satisfied by the debtor ... that he is able to pay his debts". The debtor was unwilling, but not unable, to pay the Crown debt which the petitioner sought to recover. Mr. Tavolaro conceded that the circumstances were such that the appropriate exercise of the discretionary power conferred by s.52(2) was to dismiss the petition. (See Sarina v. Council of the Shire of Wollondilly [1980] FCA 138; (1980) 48 F.L.R. 372.)

6. Mr. Santamaria's submission was that before the

bankruptcy notice had issued the petitioner might have assured himself of the debtor's solvency and that, having regard to the circumstances known by the petitioner, and to the petitioner's capacity both to ascertain the debtor's solvency and to obtain satisfaction of the Magistrates' Court order, the commencement and prosecution of bankruptcy proceedings by the petitioner had been unreasonable.

7. In support of these submissions Mr. Santamaria was able

to rely on uncontradicted evidence that, within a month after deposit of $1,430 by the debtor in the Mildura Parish Provident Fund, officers of the Taxation Department had sought, and had been given by the Parish priest, information concerning that and other deposit accounts of the debtor in the Fund. The officers had been informed by the debtor of the existence of the accounts, and informed that the deposit of $1,430 was regarded by him as the source from which he would pay the income tax claimed, "were it not", as the debtor put it in evidence, "for the breach of fundamental moral principle with which I refused to be involved". At that time, in April 1983, the income tax and additional tax claimed was less than $1,430. Mr. Santamaria relied also upon the absence of any evidence to justify an inference that the petitioner had ground for doubting the correctness of the debtor's assertions to him and his officers that the debtor was solvent. Mr. Santamaria pointed to s.264 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 as the source of the petitioner's power to compel the debtor to give an account on oath of the facts which would have disclosed whether or not the debtor was able to pay his debts. This case was, according to Mr. Santamaria's submission, distinguishable from Sarina's Case, supra, and from Re Noye; Ex Parte Deputy Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1956) 18 A.B.C. 77. In Sarina's Case it was submitted on behalf of the debtor (48 F.L.R. at 377) that the petitioner's knowledge of the debtor's ownership of assets worth a great deal more than the amount owing to the petitioner disentitled the petitioner to an order that the debtor pay the petitioner's costs of a petition for sequestration, which had been dismissed upon proof that the debtor was able to pay his debts. The Full Court of this court pointed out (48 F.L.R. at 378) that knowledge of assets without knowledge of liabilities, income and expenditure gave no ground for a conclusion as to solvency.

8. I very much doubt whether the powers conferred by s.264

of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 are exercisable for the purpose contemplated in Mr. Santamaria's submission. (See The Commissioner of Taxation and Ors. v. The Australia and New Zealand Banking Group Ltd. (1979) 143 C.L.R. 499.) But I will assume that the debtor could have been examined under oath for that purpose, if not in exercise of the power conferred by s.264, at all events in the course of an examination of the kind for which s.132 of the Magistrates (Summary Proceedings) Act 1975 makes provision. But the failure of the petitioner to expend time and money in determining whether the judgment debtor was able to pay his debts before deciding to institute these bankruptcy proceedings ought not in my opinion to be considered conduct disentitling the petitioner to an award of costs in the proceedings. Apparently frank and apparently solvent the debtor was, at the time the bankuptcy notice issued. But the petitioner, as judgment creditor, ought not in my opinion to be required, at the risk of being denied his costs of bankruptcy proceedings, either to trust the debtor's word, or to expend money in procuring testimonial verification, that the debtor is solvent and that accordingly payment of the judgment debt may be enforced without fear that bankruptcy at the suit of some other creditor may supervene. I do not intend to deny that the institution of bankruptcy proceedings against a debtor whose solvency is either obvious or susceptible of reliable verification at a cost substantially less than that of bankruptcy proceedings may be unreasonable, or that such unreasonableness may influence the exercise of the discretion as to costs. But it was not, as I think, unreasonable of this petitioner to form no opinion concerning this debtor's solvency upon the information which he had, nor unreasonable to prefer bankruptcy proceedings to an execution of the Magistrates' Court order which might be frustrated by the operation of s.118 of the Bankruptcy Act 1966, and to an attempt to satisfy himself about the debtor's solvency by proceedings under s.132 of the Magistrates (Summary Proceedings) Act 1975. Proceedings of the latter kind might have left the petitioner still uncertain of the debtor's solvency. The debtor had disclosed to the petitioner that he was conducting a restaurant in Mildura. It might be said that the solvency of that business could have been ascertained by the petitioner without much expense. But a community of which so many members enjoy the mobility a motor car provides, and which maintains so many sources of credit, does not readily yield to a judgment creditor reliable information about the solvency of his debtor. To prefer bankruptcy proceedings to execution of the judgment in such a community will not commonly suggest unreasonableness on the creditor's part. The evidence in this case does not show, to me, unreasonableness on the part of the petitioner in instituting and prosecuting these proceedings.

9. There is no question that the debt was owed, or that it

passed regularly into judgment. I did not understand it to be submitted - nor could it be accepted, in my opinion - that the debtor's tenderness of conscience, as the cause of his unwillingness to pay the debt, gave him a claim upon the court's consideration in exercise of the discretion as to costs.

10. The orders disposing of the petition are that it be

dismissed and that the petitioner's costs of the petition (including reserved costs) other than those relating to the bankruptcy notice, the affidavit of service of the bankruptcy notice, the preparation of the petition and the affidavit verifying paragraphs 1, 2 and 3 of the petition, be taxed and paid by the debtor.
