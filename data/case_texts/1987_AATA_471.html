Re P and Commissioner of Police [1987] AATA 471 (18 December 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: P.

And: COMMISSIONER OF POLICE

No. A87/90

AAT No. 4019

Gun Licensing

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.K. Todd (Deputy President)

CATCHWORDS

Gun Licensing - renewal - pistol licence - criteria to be applied - business in N.S.W. - money carried into A.C.T.

Gun Licence Ordinance 1937, ss.6, 7P(2)(b), 14

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, s.37

Re Hollands and Commissioner of Police (Decision No.470, 22 May 1981)

Pochi v. Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs [1982] HCA 60; (1982) 43 ALR 261

Re E. and Commissioner of Police (Decision No.3392, 22 April 1987)

HEARING

CANBERRA

18:12:1987

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the decision under review and remits the matter to the Commissioner of Police for reconsideration in accordance with the direction that he advise the Registrar of Gun Licences forthwith that he does not object to the renewal of the applicant's pistol licence and that he recommends that it be so renewed.

DECISION

The applicant in this matter applied to the Tribunal for review of a decision of the delegate of the Commissioner of Police recommending, pursuant to s.14 of the Gun Licence Ordinance 1937 ("the Ordinance"), that the applicant's pistol licence not be renewed. Power to review this decision is vested in the Tribunal by s.7P(2)(b) of the Ordinance.

2. As the facts disclosed at the hearing indicated that the applicant is at times in possession of large sums of cash these Reasons are stated in a form that seeks not to disclose his identity.

3. The applicant is a businessman who has held a pistol licence in the Australian Capital Territory for about 12 years. He also holds a New South Wales licence in respect of the same weapon, and has done so for approximately 3 years.

4. In the documents lodged with the Tribunal pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the respondent gave the reason for the decision to oppose the renewal of the licence as being:

"...that the Applicant has no business in the

A.C.T. that requires him to carry large amounts of

cash outside normal business hours."

5. Oral evidence was given at the hearing of this matter by the applicant. Since the late 1950s the applicant has operated various culinary outlets in the Canberra area. These were all within the A.C.T. until 1984 when he opened a business in Queanbeyan and ceased operations in the A.C.T.. He set out in evidence his hours of operation and explained that he was present at his business whenever it was trading. After he closes each evening at 10 p.m., he explained, he returns to his home in Canberra where he secures the day's takings. The following morning he counts the money, a large task requiring great concentration, and not easily done late at night at the end of many hours work, prepares it for banking and then leaves home both to do the banking and to visit various suppliers. Due to his close relationship with the manager of a Canberra branch of his bank, he is able to complete his banking before normal opening hours at that particular branch. This whole procedure enables him to count the money, to do the banking, to visit his suppliers and to still arrive at work before he commences trading.

6. Evidence was also given setting out the sums of money carried by the applicant, which are substantial.

7. It was suggested by the respondent that the applicant should use a branch of his bank in Queanbeyan, thus removing any need for him to carry the money into the A.C.T.. Although the respondent suggested a number of ways in which the applicant could do his banking in Queanbeyan, it was clear to me from the evidence that these were either impractical or would inconvenience the applicant to a substantial extent.

8. Sections 6 and 14 of the Ordinance are of little assistance in determining what criteria should be applied in deciding whether a person should be granted a renewal of a licence. The parts of those sections that are relevant state:

"6.(1) Subject to this section, the Registrar

may, as he thinks fit, in pursuance of applications

made in accordance with Form A in the Schedule -

(a) grant a gun licence to any person who

satisfies him that he is not less than

sixteen years of age; and

(b) grant a pistol licence to any person who

satisfies him that he is not less than

eighteen years of age,

and renew any such licence."

"14.(1) The Commissioner of Police or a member

of the Police Force of the Territory thereto

authorized in writing by him shall, from time to

time, inspect each register of licences kept in

pursuance of this Ordinance, and shall certify

therein in the space provided in the register

whether or not he objects to the renewal of any

licence.

(2) If the Commissioner of Police or a

member of the Police Force of the Territory so

authorized certifies that he objects to the renewal

of any licence, any application for the renewal of

that licence shall be submitted to the Commissioner

of Police for report, and, if he recommends that

the licence should not be renewed, the Registrar

shall not renew the licence but shall forward to

the applicant a notice in writing informing him of

the recommendation of the Commissioner of Police."

9. As was stated in Re Hollands and Commissioner of Police (Decision No.470, 22 May 1981) at paragraph 10 by Mr. A.N. Hall, Senior Member as he then was:-

"As will be observed, however, s.6 of the Ordinance

is silent as to the considerations which should

guide the exercise of the discretionary powers

conferred on the Registrar and the Commissioner of

Police respectively. It does not follow,

therefore, that the powers are arbitrary or

completely unlimited: cf Water Conservation and

Irrigation Commission (NSW) v. Browning (1947) 74

CLR 492 at 505 per Dixon J. They are powers which

may appropriately be exercised 'for the purpose of

attaining the object and securing the purpose' of

the legislation, 'such object and purpose being

ascertained by an examination of the terms' of the

legislation: per Latham C.J. in Shrimpton v.

Commonwealth [1945] HCA 4; (1945) 69 CLR 613 at 620. Within the

limits of the considerations which the statute by

implication makes relevant, the discretionary

powers are nevertheless very wide: cf R. v. Murphy;

Ex parte Clift (1980) QdR. 1 at 8."

The decision in Re Hollands is reported as (1981) 3 ALN N78, but the above passage was not set out therein in full.

10. Guidelines have been prepared by the Australian Federal Police in relation to the licensing of pistols. These guidelines, which were tendered at the hearing, are however, with respect, poorly drafted. The guidelines set out three criteria that must be met:

"1. The applicant has attained the age of eighteen

years.

2. The applicant has no criminal convictions.

3. The applicant is not the associate of criminals

or of persons of notoriously bad character."

The guidelines, in part, continue:

"HOWEVER, should he/she satisfy those three

requirements, he/she must then satisfy the

Authorised Police Officer that he/she requires the

pistol licence for one or more of the following

reasons:-

1. The applicant does not (sic) require the pistol

for his own personal protection.

2. The applicant conducts a Security Business

where there is a requirement in the contract

for escorts to be armed.

3. That the applicant conducts a business that, by

its nature, large sums of cash are accumulated

outside of normal banking hours.

4. That the applicant conducts a business, that by

its location, adequate banking facilities are

not readily available and that he/she requires

the pistol for the immediate transporting of

that money from the business premises to the

banking house.

5. That the applicant conducts a business where

his employees are paid in cash and that the

total payroll exceeds $30,000. (NSW Police

requirement).

6. That the applicant satisfies the authorised

Police Office that he/she can provide adequate

security for the pistol when not being carried

by the applicant.

....

10. That the applicant is in lawful possession of a

pistol that is a family heirloom, antique or

war relic and satisfies the Authorised Police

Officer that he/she can provide adequate

security for the pistol. (as in 6)."

11. Various other categories including veterinary surgeons are also listed.

12. In respect of the criteria set out above I make the following comments. The word "not" is apparently incorrect in Criterion 1 or else nearly all members of the community could satisfy it. Criterion 3, it would seem, needs a limitation such as that imposed in Criterion 4. I am of the view that Criterion 6 is not sufficient on its own and, in fact, should have to be satisfied in all cases. Criterion 10 has been set out above as it is the only criterion which specifies that the applicant must be able generally to provide adequate security for the pistol. It is also noted that there appears to be no provision for issuing licences to members of pistol clubs.

13. While it is encouraging to the Tribunal that criteria have been established for the licensing of pistols, which does not appear to have occurred in relation to guns, it is clear that the guidelines are in urgent need of redrafting to correct the obvious flaws and inadequacies contained therein.

14. Evidence was provided by the applicant about the precautions which he takes for the security of his pistol. It is sufficient to say that these appeared to the Tribunal to be satisfactory and no contrary submission was put by the respondent.

15. The primary submission for the respondent was that the applicant did not conduct his business in the A.C.T. and that "business" in Criterion 3 meant "business in the A.C.T.".

16. While the Tribunal must take departmental policy into account when determining how to exercise a discretion (Pochi v. Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs [1982] HCA 60; (1982) 43 ALR 261) it is not the role of the Tribunal to interpret the policy, especially where it is as poorly drafted as in this case.

17. The criteria envisage that business people who are required to carry large sums of cash should be able to carry pistols for their protection (Criteria 3, 4 and 5).

18. On the evidence before the Tribunal it is clear that the proper operation of the applicant's business makes it necessary for him to travel through portions of the A.C.T. to his home in Canberra with large amounts of money. It seems to me to be nothing to the point that the aggregation of the money which is claimed to give rise to the need for possession of a pistol occurred outside the A.C.T.. Once it is accepted, as I do, that the applicant's conduct within the A.C.T. of his business, represented by and including the means adopted by him for the management of the cash produced by the business including its overnight security, the tiresome task of its counting, and its banking, are reasonable, the question of the extraterritorial origin of the money becomes irrelevant to the exercise of the discretion to renew the licence.

19. No evidence was produced to show, nor was it at any stage implied, that the applicant was anything other than a fit and proper person to hold a pistol licence. He has in the past taken instruction in the use of a pistol and he was for a while a member of a pistol club, thus demonstrating a sense of responsibility. He established a need for a licence which is within the general tenor of the guidelines provided by the Police. The Tribunal will therefore set aside the decision under review and remit the matter to the Commissioner of Police for reconsideration in accordance with the direction that he advise the Registrar of Gun Licences forthwith that he does not object to the renewal of the applicant's pistol licence and that he recommends that it be so renewed.

20. Before concluding I repeat what I stated in Re E. at paragraph 15:

"Comment must be made on the inadequacies of the

Ordinance in this area. The Tribunal endorses

what Mr. Ewart Smith (Senior Member) said in Re

Sewter and Commissioner of Police - (26 September

1983, Decision No. 1247), at paragraph 15:-

'Before concluding, I think I should observe

that the evidence in this case demonstrates the

desirability of the inclusion in the Ordinance

of specific criteria to be applied by the

Registrar or the Commissioner of Police in

considering applications for gun or pistol

licences. This matter was adverted to by Mr.

A.N. Hall (Senior Member, now Deputy President)

in Re Hollands and Commissioner of Police (1981)

3 ALN N78 (Note 46). He commented, inter alia,

as follows:

"(iv) Finally, as the (Gun Licence) Ordinance

specifies criteria by reference to which

other forms of licences or approvals may be

granted (cf ss.7F(2) and 10(3)), and as the

requirement that an applicant for a gun or

pistol licence must be a 'fit and proper

person' to hold such a licence appears, quite

properly in my view, to be fundamental to the

administration of the gun and pistol

licensing provisions, it may be more

satisfactory if that requirement, and any

other appropriate requirements (cf s.7F(2)),

were specified in the Ordinance rather than

being left to implication."

I would respectfully adopt those comments. If

the control of firearms by individuals is to be

left as it is under the Ordinance, it would seem

that only a person who has a recorded offence or

is notoriously an associate of criminals is

likely to be challenged on an application for a

gun licence however unfitted he may be to

possess firearms. The question whether there

should be a requirement on an applicant for a

licence to demonstrate some positive evidence of

fitness to possess the gun in respect of which

the licence is sought - if only in the form of

character references from responsible persons in

the community - is a matter to which the policy

Department might with profit direct its

attention.'"

21. Although criteria have been set out in relation to pistol licences it would appear to the Tribunal to be preferable that these be included in the Ordinance, properly drawn.
