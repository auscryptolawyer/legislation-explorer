TAXATION - whether appeal to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal lodged by a person who is, or subsequently becomes, bankrupt is “property” under the provisions of the Bankruptcy Act 1966



Bankruptcy Act 1966 ss.5, 60, 116(1), 187(1)



Taxation Administration Act 1953 s.14ZZA



Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ss.42A(1B), 43(1)



NT88/1748-56 and Commissioner of Taxation

(unreported AAT Decision N° 5635, delivered 25 January 1990)



WT93/67-99 and Commissioner of Taxation [1993] AATA 371; 27 ATR 1183



Fuller v Beech Petroleum NL and Claremont Petroleum NC [1993] FCA 453; (1993) 117 ALR 235 (Full Fed Ct); (1996) 137 ALR 1 (HC)



Griffith v Civil Aviation Authority [1996] FCA 1502; (1996) 137 ALR 521



REASONS FOR DECISION



21 May 1997 Deputy President G.L. McDonald



1. This is a challenge to the jurisdiction of the Tribunal to hear and determine several applications, each seeking review of a decision of the Commissioner of Taxation (“the Commissioner”) disallowing notices of objection filed by the applicants against assessments made by the Commissioner.



2. The facts are as follows:



  • on 6 August 1992 the applicants lodged notices of objection against income tax assessments raised by the respondent,

  • on 12 January 1994 the applicants executed a deed of arrangement under Part X of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 (“the Bankruptcy Act”). Under the deed a trustee was appointed to administer the affairs of the applicants,

  • on 24 April 1994 the respondent disallowed the objections filed on 6 August 1992 and notified the applicants in writing of his decision,

  • the applicants each lodged appeals to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (“the AAT”) to review the decision of the Commissioner. The right of review is granted in s.14ZZA(1) of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 (“Taxation Administration Act”),

  • The respondent in its submission challenges the right of the applicants to lodge and pursue the right of review on the basis that the statutory right of appeal, granted by the Taxation Administration Act is “property” and as such could only be dealt with by the trustee under the terms of the deed of trust. The applicants have elected not to make any submission to the Tribunal.

3. The trustee in a letter dated 7 October 1994 has said that he does not wish to adopt the applications.



4. Under clause 3 of the trust deed, the applicants (debtors) assign to the trustee all of “the estate”. By clause 1(a) of that deed, “the estate” is defined to mean all property of the debtors specified in the Schedule (but not including after acquired property). The Schedule simply refers to “all the divisible property of the debtors”. Clause 1(f) of the deed also defines “property” by reference to the Bankruptcy Act, s.5(1), which is in the following terms:



“ Real or personal property of every description, whether situate in Australia or elsewhere, and includes any estate, interest, or profit, whether present or future, vested or contingent arising out of or incident to any such real or personal property.”



The term “the divisible property” is defined by reference to s.187(1) of the Bankruptcy Act, which defines it to mean:



“ . . . property . . . that would be divisible among his creditors . . . .”



5. The definition of “what is property divisible amongst creditors” is found in s.116(1) of the Bankruptcy Act, which is in the following terms:



“ Subject to this Act:



(a) all property that belonged to, or was vested in, a bankrupt at the commencement of the bankruptcy, or has been acquired or is acquired by him, or has devolved or devolves on him, after the commencement of the bankruptcy and before his discharge;



(b) the capacity to exercise, and to take proceedings for exercising, all such powers in, over or in respect of property as might have been exercised by the bankrupt for his own benefit at the commencement of the bankruptcy or at any time after the commencement of the bankruptcy and before his discharge;



(c) property that is vested in the trustee of the bankrupt’s estate by or under an order under section 139D; and



(d) money that is paid to the trustee of the bankrupt’s estate under an order under section 139E;



is property divisible amongst the creditors of the bankrupt.”



6. The respondent in its submission points to different decisions taken by differently constituted tribunals to this issue. In an unreported decision (NT88/1748-56 and Commissioner of Taxation, AAT Decision N° 5635, dated 25 January 1990) Senior Member Roach referred to s.60 of the Bankruptcy Act which subject to exceptions not relevant here, has the effect of staying an action by a person who becomes bankrupt until the Trustee in Bankruptcy makes an election to proceed or discontinue the action, and determined proceedings by the Tribunal form appropriately within the bankruptcy definition of “civil proceeding”. On the other hand, Senior Member Fayle in WT93/67-99 and Commissioner of Taxation, [1993] AATA 371; 27 ATR 1183, determined that a taxpayer who sought an extension of time in which to lodge objections to assessments with the Commissioner was not engaged in “an action”. Senior Member Fayle also commented that the right of a taxpayer to apply for review under the provisions of s.14ZZA of the Taxation Administration Act was not “property” within the meaning given to that term in s.5 of the Bankruptcy Act.



7. In Fuller v Beech Petroleum NL and Claremont Petroleum NC [1993] FCA 453; (1993) 117 ALR 235 the Full Court of the Federal Court of Australia determined that the definition of “property” in s.5 of the Bankruptcy Act was broad enough to encompass a right held by the bankrupt to appeal from a single judge of the Federal Court to the Full Federal Court. While the majority of the High Court upheld the appeal (137 ALR 1), it is clear from the decision that the High Court took the view that, with the exception of rights of action of a personal nature, everything answering the description of “property” vests in the trustee.



8. In Griffith v Civil Aviation Authority [1996] FCA 1502; (1996) 137 ALR 521 the Full Federal Court determined that an appeal to the Full Federal Court to challenge a decision of the AAT concerning the applicant's aviation licences was not “property divisible among the creditors”. The Court held that where a right of appeal is personal to the bankrupt and has no implications for the estate of the bankrupt, it will not constitute property vesting in the trustee.



9. On an appeal the AAT may affirm, vary or set aside and substitute another decision for or remit a decision with a direction or recommendation for further consideration to the original decision-maker (s.43(1) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (“the AAT Act”)). In carrying out its function the Tribunal stands in the shoes of the original decision-maker and has the material before that decision-maker as well as any other material or evidence brought by either of the parties. While it may not be common, there are certainly cases where an applicant may find him or herself disadvantaged by appealing to the Tribunal. This arises because the Tribunal may take the view that, on the material before it, it is satisfied that the correct or preferred decision is different to the original decision and that that difference may result in a finding less favourable to an applicant. If that was to occur, then it may result in the estate of a bankrupt being placed in a worse position than if the review had not been carried out. As a general matter of policy, the Trustee in Bankruptcy would clearly have an interest in any such appeal just as he would if an applicant was to succeed and receive a benefit.



10. It seems to the Tribunal that in those circumstances a right of appeal to the Tribunal does amount to a proprietary right which vests in the trustee. Accordingly, in the case such as this where the trustee declines to participate in the continuation of the appeal, the Tribunal should regard the appeal as being discontinued under the provisions of s.42A(1B) of the AAT Act.



11. For that reason, the applications should be dismissed.
