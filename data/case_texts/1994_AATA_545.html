WT93/76 and Commissioner of Taxation [1994] AATA 545; (1994) 28 ATR 1241; 94 ATC 314 (15 June 1994)

Last Updated: 15 May 2009



INCOME TAX : Extension of Time - delayed application for review - failure of tax agent to act on instructions - extension granted



PRACTICE AND PROCEDURE : extension of time - failure of tax agent to act on instructions



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 : Ss.187, 188, 188B

Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No 3) 1991 : S.116



AAT Decision No 9549



DECISION AND REASONS



Re: APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT



No : WT93/76

Date : 15 June 1994

Place : Perth

Tribunal : Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)



DECISION



The Tribunal decides that the time for lodgment of the application for review in this Tribunal be extended to 1 November 1993.



(Sgd) K L Beddoe (SENIOR MEMBER)



ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) NO WT93/76

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re:

APPLICANT



And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

RESPONDENT



REASONS FOR DECISION



Before: Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member)

Date: 15 June 1994

Place: Perth



The applicant seeks an extension of time to lodge an application for review. Stating that simple question is easy, but I must say that this case has become confused in the way it has been handled on the applicant’s behalf and, for that matter, by the applicant himself.



2. In mid-April 1987 an income tax return was lodged on behalf of the applicant by a firm of tax agents, Newton and Rashleigh. That return showed income from personal exertion by way of salary from a State Government department and also further salary derived from a company. Income from the State Government department ceased on 7 March 1986 and from that point until the end of the financial year the applicant was employed by his own company, carrying on business as a heritage and environmental consultant. No income was derived from that consultancy but the applicant did derive income as a marketing consultant. Deductions for outgoings in an amount exceeding $7,000 were claimed, and a further amount of $3,613 was claimed in respect of depreciation. Although it is not before me, I understand that the respondent assessed the applicant on the basis of the income tax return as lodged in an amount of taxable income of $29,273.



3. Once again the notice of assessment is not before me but I understand that in 1988 the respondent issued an amended notice of assessment which notified a number of adjustments to the taxable income as previously assessed. Those adjustments to taxable income apparently included an amount in excess of $7,000 for omitted interest income in relation to a building society account held in the name of another person and undisclosed deposits in relation to sales of computers in an amount of $117,638. Deductions were allowed for amounts paid for freight in connection with the importation of computers and also for purchase of systems. The net result was a taxable income of $133,385. The notice of amended assessment dated 14 April 1988 resulted in a letter from Newton and Rashleigh dated 6 May 1988 in which they requested details of the basis of the amended assessment and indicated that they would be lodging an objection in due course. They also asked that the matter be given urgent attention. Whether that request was given urgent attention I do not know, but by a document dated 8 June 1988 addressed to the Australian Taxation Office in Perth, the applicant personally lodged an objection against the amended assessment. He disputed the amount included for computer sales, alleged that in fact the computer sales business had not made a profit after allowing for expenses and also there was a dispute in the notice about the Medicare levy assessed and the additional tax for late return, and additional tax for incorrect return. Because the notice of assessment is not before the Tribunal, I am not aware of the details of the assessment of additional tax.



3. By a letter dated 21 June 1989 a different firm of accountants, Roseman Clancy and Associates, supplied a substantial amount of information to the Australian Taxation Office in relation to the applicant. That information was apparently supplied in response to a requisition from the Australian Taxation Office dated 3 October 1988 and referred to the notice of amended assessment for the year of income ended 30 June 1986.



4. By a notice dated 23 August 1991 the respondent notified the applicant of the decision on the objection. That notice was addressed to the applicant c/- T M Clancy and Co. I think I can infer that T M Clancy is the same person who was previously a partner in Roseman, Clancy and Associates. The notification indicated that the objection against the amended assessment had been considered and the objection allowed in part. The notice sets out a summary of the basis on which the objection decision had been made. The notice also draws attention to section 187 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (“the Act”) and in particular that a request may be made to this Tribunal for review of the decision within sixty days after service of the said notice. The notice also drew attention to the necessity for a fee of $300 and that the sixty day period nominated may be extended at the discretion of the Tribunal.



5. At the same time the Australian Taxation Office forwarded copies of the notice of decision on the objection and the attached documents to the applicant at an address in the Philippines. The letter addressed to the applicant in the Philippines included a statement that copies of all relevant documents had also been forwarded to Mr Clancy.



6. By letter dated 26 August 1991 the Commonwealth Ombudsman wrote to the applicant at the same address in the Philippines referring to a letter of the applicant’s dated 27 March 1991 about delay by the Australian Taxation Office in finalising an objection. After discussing the issue raised with the Ombudsman, the officer of the Ombudsman’s office then drew attention to the provisions of the taxation legislation for review of decisions in this Tribunal, and drawing attention to the fact that a sixty day period applied for making application.



7. It seems that nothing happened until a letter dated 4 June 1993 addressed to the Taxation Office by the applicant from the Philippines in which he requested information on the progress in settling his case. That letter specifically refers to receipt of the respondent’s letter of 23 August 1991 and goes on to say that that the applicant had instructed Mr Clancy to lodge an “objection” and had supplied him with responses to all the queries raised by the Taxation office. The letter also refers to the response he received from the Ombudsman dated 26 August 1991. The essence of the letter is a complaint that he had been in contact with Mr Clancy on a number of occasions and he says that Mr Clancy advised him that he had been unable to make any progress with the Taxation Office. The applicant then goes on to say that he suspects it was the accountant who was the problem. There is no explanation in the letter as to why the applicant had failed to make an application for review in time as he was advised by the Australian Taxation Office, and also by the Ombudsman.



8. In a letter dated 17 August 1993 the Australian Taxation Office responded to the applicant’s letter of 4 June 1993 by setting out a brief history of the matter and also asserting that no contact had been made by Mr Clancy with the Taxation Office requesting reference of the decision for review in either the Tribunal or the Federal Court. The letter from the Taxation Office also stated that because no request for review had been lodged, the Taxation Office now regarded the matter as finalised. The letter again stated that application may be made to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal for review of the decision and drew attention to the requirements of the $300 filing fee and set out the address of the Perth Registry of the Tribunal. The letter from the Taxation Office also states that the last contact between the Taxation Office and the applicant’s accountant had been on 12 July 1991 and that three further attempts to discuss the matter with him were unsuccessful. No explanation was given as to why or when the Taxation Office had been attempting to make these contacts with the accountant.



9. The Taxation Office letter of 17 August 1993 brought forth a prompt response from the applicant in a letter dated 23 August 1993, in which he states that on 29 August 1991 he wrote to his tax agent in Melbourne and directed that he lodge an appeal and sent the accountant a telegraphic transfer of $A300 for that purpose. The letter also states that the tax agent in Melbourne who, I infer, is Mr Clancy, had asserted that he had in fact lodged an application for review. The applicant notifed appointment of Priestly and Morris of Perth as his tax agents to handle the final settlement of the matter. Attached to that letter was an application for a telegraphic transfer in favour of T M Clancy and Co, of East Malvern, Victoria, in an amount of $1,874 Hong Kong dollars. The telegraphic transfer was apparently processed on 12 September 1991 (Document T20).



10. Although there is a substantial amount of material before me relating to the merits of the dispute between the applicant and the taxation office, I am unable to form any view in relation to the applicant’s prospects of success from that material, but it would appear that there is good reason to doubt that the amended assessments, as made by the Taxation Office, are in fact a correct assessment of the taxable income of the applicant.



11. The Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No 3) 1991 being Act No 216 of 1991, repealed provisions of the Act applicable to objections and review of decisions made by the respondent Commissioner. The new provisions, which are contained in Part IVC of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 were proclaimed to come into effect from 1 March 1992. Section 116 of Act No 216 of 1991 contains transitional provisions relating to the application of the new law as it applied to objection decisions notified after the commencement of operation of section 116. The applicant’s case does not come within the terms of section 116 because the respondent notified the decision on the objection on 23 August 1991 and there is no doubt that the applicant received that notification in the ordinary course of post. This matter must therefore be decided in accordance with what is euphemistically described in Act No 216 of 1991 as the old law (sub-section 116(2)).



12. So far as it is relevant section 187 of the the Act provides that a taxpayer who is dissatisfied with the decision under section 186 on an objection by the taxpayer may, within sixty days after service on the taxpayer of notice of the decision, lodge with the Commissioner, in writing, a request to refer the decision to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal.



13. Sub-section 188(2) of the Act provides that where the period for the lodgment by the taxpayer of a request under section 187 had ended, the taxpayer may, notwithstanding that the period had ended, send the request to the Commissioner together with an application in writing asking that the request be treated as having been duly lodged. Such an application shall state fully and in detail the circumstances concerning, and the reasons for, the failure by the taxpayer to lodge the request as required by the Act (sub-section 188(3)). In this case the procedural step of lodging the request with the Taxation Office and the Taxation Office referring the request for an extension of time to the Tribunal, has not been followed. Nothing was made of that failure before me, and in view of the change in the law which now requires such applications to be made direct to the Tribunal, I do not think I should give any weight to the procedural failure on behalf of the applicant. It is relevant to note, however, that the Tribunal sent a copy of the application to the Commissioner in accordance with sub-section 29(10) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.



14. Sub-section 188B(4) provides that the Tribunal may grant or refuse the application and sub-section 188B(5) provides that where an application has been granted the taxpayer shall, for the purposes of Part V of the Act, be treated as having duly lodged the request for which the application relates.



15. It seems to me there are a number of difficulties with this case, including the obvious one which was drawn to my attention by the officer who represented the respondent in these proceedings. He submitted that the Taxation Office had, at all times, acted upon information supplied by the taxpayer and when it heard nothing more about the matter after the determination of the objection, it was entitled to accept that the taxpayer was satisfied with the decision on the objection. In that context there is no explanation by the taxpayer as to why he, in effect, sat on his hands for two years in relation to the Taxation Office. He had on an earlier occasion complained to the Ombudsman about delays in the matter. The Ombudsman’s explanation was that there had been delays in the Taxation Office in the handling of the objection, but there had also been delays caused by the taxpayer’s agent, Mr Clancy. Given that explanation by the Ombudsman, I have some difficulty in understanding why the taxpayer allowed the matter to run for another two years without any real action on his part to overcome the problems. He was aware, courtesy of the Ombudsman, that the Taxation Office may delay the consideration of a matter but he was also aware that his own agent, Mr Clancy, was likely to be the cause of problems. He was well aware of the situation and it is surprising to me that the applicant appears to have taken no effective action to maintain control over the lodgment of his application for review in this Tribunal. The evidence shows that he did instruct Clancy to lodge an application for review and that he remitted the $300 fee to Clancy. The evidence also shows that Clancy apparently asserted that he had lodged the application for review and in that regard I have instituted an independent investigation of the Tribunal’s Registry to confirm that no such application was lodged in the Tribunal. It has not been asserted before me that an application was lodged in 1991 and the investigation of the Tribunal’s records indicate that is the case, that the application in fact was not lodged in the Tribunal. Given the state of the law in 1991 an application for review was required to be lodged with the Taxation Office.



16. The applicant was not called to give evidence, nor was Mr Clancy called to give evidence. Presumably both could have shed some light on the history of the matter and it is somewhat surprising to me that they were not so called. One possible explanation for the applicant’s absence is that he resides outside Australia. As to any explanation for Mr Clancy not being called, none occurs to me.



17. The history of this matter is one of substantial delays caused both by the applicant and the respondent when taking into account the time that it took the respondent to determine the applicant’s objection against the amended assessment. It was not suggested to me that there would be any prejudice to the respondent other than the fact that the respondent thought the matter had been resolved. I understand that there is still outstanding tax to be paid, and in that sense, therefore, the matter between the applicant and the respondent is not resolved. As to the merits of the application it seems to me, on the material before me, that the applicant has an arguable case as to the basis of assessment adopted in the amended assessment by the respondent Commissioner. That was conceded in part by the respondent’s decision on the objection. As to whether that has resolved the difficulties with the amended assessment, I am unable to say but I am satisfied that the applicant does have an arguable case.



18. Given that it was apparently always the applicant taxpayer’s intention to seek a review in this Tribunal and given that the failure to have an application lodged in time was not for want of instructions by the applicant but rather the failure by his tax agent to carry out his instructions, I think the circumstances of the case justify an extension of time.



19. I will therefore direct that the letter of 28 October 1993 filed in this Tribunal on 1 November 1993 by Priestly and Morris be accepted as a valid application for review in this Tribunal. I note that the letter enclosed a filing fee of $300.



15. The decision of the Tribunal is to extend the time for lodgment of the application for review to 1 November 1993.



I hereby certify that this and the preceding seven (7) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of WT93/76 and COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION.
