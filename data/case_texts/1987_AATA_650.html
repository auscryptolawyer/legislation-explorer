NT86/5913 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 650; 87 ATC 716 (29 May 1987)

Last Updated: 20 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - default assessment s167 - discrepancies between funds available, funds expended and incomes returned - issues in dispute: fund statement living expenses', funds drawn on housing loan - burden of proof to be discharged by applicant; Case K15 followed.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss167, 190(b)

Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986

Case K15 78 ATC 143

DECISION AND REASONS

Re :

And : COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

NT86/5913

AAT Decision No 3513

Tribunal : Mr B.J. McMahon (Senior Member)

Date : 29 May 1987

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. NT86/5913

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

29 May 1987 Mr B.J. McMahon (Senior Member)

1. The applicant received assessments for additional taxation for the years ended 30th June 1979, 1980 and 1981. The assessments were made pursuant to the provisions of section 167 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 which is in the following terms:

"167 If -

(a) any person makes default in furnishing a return; or

(b) the Commissioner is not satisfied with the return furnished by any person; or

(c) the Commissioner has reason to believe that any person who has not furnished a return has derived taxable income,

the Commissioner may make an assessment of the amount upon which In his judgment income tax ought to be levied, and that amount shall be the taxable income of that person for the purpose of section 166."

2. Through his agent, the applicant gave notice of objection in the following terms:

"Date. 10th Oct. 1983

NOTICE OF OBJECTION

Reference: Name: [name of applicants

Address: [address of applicant]

File No.

Assessment No. (1979)

(1980)

(1981)

[Full references given]

[name of applicant] , HEREBY OBJECT AGAINST THE ASSESSMENT OF INCOME TAX BASED ON INCOME DERIVED BY ME DURING THE YEAR OF INCOME ENDED JUNE 30, 1979, '80 & '81 ISSUED TO ME BY NOTICE OF ASSESSMENT DATED 14TH SEPT., 1983, AND CLAIM THE FOLLOWING:-

THAT THE INCOME ASSESSED FOR EACH YEAR WAS WRONG AND THEREFORE TAX ASSESSED FOR EACH YEAR WAS WRONG. PENALTY FOR INCORRECT RETURN SHOULD NOT BE IMPOSED.

THE GROUNDS UPON WHICH I RELY ARE AS FOLLOWS:-

THAT FOR EACH YEAR OF RETURN LODGED, 1979, 1980 & 1981 THE INCOME DECLARED AND EXPENSES CLAIMED THOUGH ESTIMATED FOR LACK OF RECORDS WERE AS CLOSE TO ACTUAL FIGURES AS POSSIBLE BASED ON THE RECOLLECTION AND HONEST BELIEF OF THE TAXPAYER WHEN THE RETURNS WERE PREPARED. THE TAXPAYER IS WILLING TO GO TO COURT BECAUSE HE BELIEVES THAT HE SHOULD NOT BE TAXED AS SUCH AND BE PENALIZED WITH HUGE SUMS OF MONEY LIKE SOMEONE WHO WAS TRYING TO AVOID TAX BY SOME BOTTOM OF THE HARBOUR' SCHEME.

IF THE TAXPAYER IS BEING PENALIZED FOR NOT KEEPING RECORDS IT WILL BE MORE APPROPRIATE. BUT TO BE PENALIZED FOR FILING INCORRECT RETURNS WHEN HE HAD ACTUALLY SPENT THE MONEY IN BUSINESS EVEN INCURRING LOSSES IS GROSSLY UNJUST.

PLEASE REVIEW AND RETURN ASSESSMENT TO ORIGINALS RECEIVED PREVIOUSLY.

TAXPAYER'S SIGNATURE:

FOR TAXPAYER:

CONSULTANT'S SIGNATURE: [Consultant's Signature)

TAX AGENT'S NUMBER: [given]."

3. The objection having being disallowed, the applicant then requested that the objection decision be referred to a Board of Review. The request was in the following terms:

"15-10-84

Date

Deputy Commissioner of Taxation

P.O. Box 422

PARRAMATTA 2150

REQUEST FOR SUBMISSION TO BOARD OF REVIEW

Your Reference: Name : [name of applicant!]

Address : [address of applicant]

File No. :

Ass. No. : (1981) Date: 14/9/83

Notice of Objection Lodged-Date : 10/10/83

Ass. No. (1979)

Ass. No. (1980)

[Full references given]

Dear Sir,

With reference to the above taxpayer, please find enclosed a cheque for $2 each year and $6- to cover the fee for submission to a Board of Review re: Objection Disallowed for:

Additional income assessed for each year 1979 to 1981.

In the notice of objection, I stated that:

Please see notice of objection lodged and also copy of letter requesting copies of Documents, etc from Taxation Department under the Freedom of Information Act so we can be enlightened as to how they arrived at their figures.

In the meantime that we do not have those information requested, we are submitting this request to comply with the time requirement.

Taxpayer's Signature:

For Taxpayer:

Consultant's signature: [sgd)

Office Address : [address stated]

Telephone No. : [no. stated]."

  1. It will be seen that neither the objection nor the request is a model of precision. Fortunately, it was possible to agree on the issues at the hearing of the application.
  2. By virtue of the operation of the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986, the matter comes before this Tribunal for review of the objection decision.
  3. The assessments were arrived at after the construction of a fund statement. This purports to show the source and application of funds in the years in question. Discrepancies between funds available and funds expended on the one hand, and the incomes returned for taxation purposes on the other hand, were treated either as understatements of taxable income or as overstatements of expenses. Summaries of the calculations for 1979 and 1980 were tendered in evidence. They purport to show the cash flow of the taxpayer's funds in the two relevant years. They compare cash available at the beginning of a period, take into account cash received during that period, and contrast it with cash expended during the same period, reconciled with cash on hand at the end of that period.
  4. At the commencement of these proceedings, it was agreed between the parties that the only matters in issue were two in number. In the 1979 fund statement an amount of $8,884 is shown as having been spent by the applicant for living expenses. He contended that this amount was too high. If his contention were to succeed, the funds expended would be reduced by the difference between that sum and the amount that he was able to establish as cost of living in the relevant period. This would result in a reduction of the computed income and thus tax.
  5. The second item related to two sums of money said to have been drawn down on a housing loan from the Commercial Banking Company of Sydney Limited (CBC) by way of progress payments. The 1979 fund statement shows progressive drawings of $7,950 during that year, and the 1980 statement shows a corresponding amount of $18,050. These, of course, total $26,000. It was the applicant's contention that the amounts advanced by the bank were, in fact, $31,140, the total cost of the house. If his contention is correct, then the funds available in the relevant years will have been increased by the amount for which he contends and the equation between funds available and funds expended will be consequently affected.
  6. The applicant had no records to speak of. From May 1979 to March 1980 he had been in business in partnership with his then wife in the operation of a milk bar or coffee shop. He gave evidence that he kept records of this business in a cardboard box. He was unable to say what had happened to the box or to the records, except that his marriage had been particularly unhappy in 1979 and that he and his wife had separated in June of that year. In the general emotional upset, he could only conclude that he must have mislaid the records. The calculations were therefore reconstructed by an investigator who gave evidence before this Tribunal. He had access to bank statements for a period from 6 November 1978 to 31 May 1979. During the whole of that period the applicant was employed as a forklift driver. He did not acquire the milk bar until May 1979. The investigator assumed in favour of the applicant that all monies that he earned would have been paid into the account in that period, and that the many cheques drawn over that period were for living expenses. He then extrapolated the total of cheques drawn in that period of approximately 7 months to a proportional amount for the whole of the 12 months period and arrived at the amount in question, namely $8,884. The basis of the applicant's challenge to this calculation was that, in his opinion, he could not have spent so much in living expenses in that particular year.
  7. He gave evidence that he had married in 1974 and had thereafter with his wife moved in with her parents. He said that for some years they lived in what was referred to as the old house, while his wife's parents built the new house on an adjacent block of land. He then said that he moved into the new house with his wife and baby daughter. His wife's father was employed as a foreman in a metal works factory.
  8. He said that as he and his wife and child lived as a family with her parents, he did not have to find any money for living expenses. He thought that at one stage board of $10 per week had been paid for some months in the old house, but he was quite sure that no board was paid in the new house. He also affirmed that he did not contribute towards the cost of any food, but said that on occasions, his wife would go shopping with her mother and would buy goods for the house. His wife's brother and his family also lived in the house, but they had contributed to the cost of building it. From July 1978 to November 1978 he was employed as a motor mechanic and was quite able to afford board. However, he said that he was not called upon even to contribute to electricity or telephone expenses. He said that the only living expenses he had during the whole of the year ended 30 June 1979 were those for registration of his car and for the occasional shopping expenses of his wife. On the face of it, this is, in my opinion, an inherently improbable state of affairs. I cannot and do not accept that a young married man with a wife and a young child, sheltered by a family in no position to be financially generous, spent no more on living expenses in an entire year than these two items.
  9. He was unable to support this statement with any documents, or indeed with any corroboration. He gave all of his wages to his wife. He said that she may have had some arrangement with her mother but he was not told of it. In the course of the investigation, he was given by the respondent's investigator a form covering all possible items of living and personal expenditure and was invited to fill it in. The form, which was tendered in evidence, showed a total expenditure for living expenses in one year of $495. Even that form, however, contradicted his earlier evidence in that it shows amounts paid for telephone, light, power and gas.
  10. The applicant's former wife was called to give evidence. She had not been present while the applicant gave his evidence. Her recollection differed materially from the applicant's in many respects. According to her evidence, they at no time lived with her parents. They occupied the old house which was "a basic two bedroom timber house" throughout the time that they were together. She remembered that the applicant's brother (not her brother) lived with them for a number of years. Her parents lived near by in their new house.
  11. She agreed that she received regular amounts of money from the applicant for the purchase of food and other household expenses. She did not remember whether he gave her his pay packet. After the milk bar was purchased she still continued to receive irregular amounts for household expenses. Although the cheque account was in joint names, she did not know whether she had authority to sign cheques. She certainly did not have a cheque book. She thought that the cheques drawn by her then husband could only be for living expenses.
  12. She agreed that no rent was paid to her parents. Originally a sum of $20 per week had been agreed upon, but they later agreed to allow the applicant and his wife to live in the old house rent free. She does not recall whether they had a telephone or not.
  13. Undoubtedly living expenses are personal and difficult to corroborate if not paid for by cheque. Furthermore, expenses incurred some 8 years previously may be difficult to remember and may be recalled differently by different people. I do not consider that either the applicant or his former wife gave other than their honest recollections. It is clear however that these are far from precise and almost certainly, in the case of the applicant, are not accurate. Against this honesty, however, the applicant was unable to offer any explanation of the amounts drawn from the cheque account at a time when he was an employed forklift driver. Clearly none of the items could have related to business expenses. He was unable to show that the method of calculation was wrong, or that the items upon which the calculations were based were not items of living expenses, nor was he able to demonstrate the correct amount that should have been attributed to his living expenses in the relevant period.
  14. The second item in dispute turns on whether CBC agreed to lend and did in fact lend $26,000 or $31,140.
  15. The evidence of the applicant and his former wife were in agreement that the bank had agreed to advance the full cost of the house and that this amount, including extras, was $31,140. They argued that if the amount advanced had been only $26,000, a short fall of $5,140 would have had to be found. Both agreed that neither of them had ever written a cheque for that amount or had caused such an amount to be paid to the builder.
  16. The documentary evidence that was produced was contradictory and confusing. The respondent produced a copy of the closing bank statement dealing with the housing loan. That clearly showed the amount of the advance as $26,000 in August 1979. It was repaid, together with outstanding interest on the sale of the house in March 1980 when a sum of $27,045.57 was debited to the account resulting in a nil balance.
  17. On the other hand, copies of statements from the builder indicate receipt of progress payments of $30,940 (not the full price of $31,140 as the applicant asserted). The statements, however, do not confirm that these payments were received from CBC. The applicant was aware of this and made efforts to establish whether there had in fact been a second loan contrary to his recollection. Letters from the builder and the hank that were tendered in evidence indicate that he was unsuccessful in this quest.
  18. In other words, the applicant has not been able to show that the bank statement was incorrect in that it purported to show the full amount of the loan as $26,000. The copies of the statements of the builder's accounts do not assist him in establishing that fact. A mere failure to recall having arranged for payment of the shortfall of approximately $5,000 is not sufficient for the applicant's purposes.
  19. It is well established that the burden of proving that an assessment was excessive, which lays upon the taxpayer pursuant to section 190(b) of the Act, is equally applicable in a case of assessments made under section 167 of the Act. The decisions establishing this proposition and the way in which they have been applied, were set out in the closely reasoned decision of No. 2 Board of Review in Case K15, 78 ATC 143. It is not necessary to repeat their reasoning. It is sufficient to say that I respectfully adopt it and the conclusions arrived at by the Board.
  20. Having regard to the applicant's burden of proof, it is clear on the above evidence that he has not established that the assessments are excessive in the two particulars upon which he chose to dispute them. The evidence leaves me in no doubt in fact that the living expenses for 1979 to which he admits are grossly understated, and that the living expenses contended for by the respondent appear to be reasonable and have a rational basis for calculation. The evidence concerning the extent of the bank loan is not so clear. Nevertheless, the onus which the applicant carries has not been discharged.

24. Accordingly, the objection decision under review is affirmed.
