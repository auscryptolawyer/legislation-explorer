Re Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner [1994] AATA 351; (1994) 95 ATC 119 (24 November 1994)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: INSURANCE AND SUPERANNUATION COMMISSIONER

No. Q94/54

AAT No. 9858

Number of pages - 9

Retirement Benefits

(1994) 95 ATC 119

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

S.A. FORGIE (Deputy President), K.L. BEDDOE (Senior Member) AND J.D. HORRIGAN

(Member)

CATCHWORDS

Retirement Benefits - whether Fund should be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions - whether borrowings made on or before 11 June, 1986 - whether special circumstances such that Fund should be treated by Commissioner as having satisfied conditions - Commissioner's discretion exercised in respect of first year of income but not subsequent years - whether Trustees misled by that exercise in relation to subsequent years - consideration of circumstances in which investment made - decision affirmed.

Income Tax Assessment Act

Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 - Sections 5, 5(2)(b), 7, 7(1), 12, 12(3), 12(6), 12(6A), 13, 13(1)(b)

Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations - Regulations 16(1)(b), 16(3), 16(3A), 16(5)

Re The Southern Brazilian Rio Grande De Sul Railway Company Limited (1905) 2 Ch 78

HEARING

BRISBANE, 8 November 1994

24:11:1994

Counsel for the Applicant: Mr McPherson

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr Fogarty

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decisions under review.

DECISION

S.A. FORGIE, K.L. BEDDOE AND J.D. HORRIGAN On 18 January, 1994, the Trustees of the Fund lodged an application for review of two decisions of a delegate of the Insurance and Superannuation Commissioner ("the Commissioner") dated 28 July, 1993 and confirmed by another delegate in a decision dated 10 September, 1993. The Trustees were notified of the decision to confirm by a letter dated 16 December, 1993. The effect of the decision was that the Commissioner was not satisfied that the Fund satisfied the superannuation conditions in relation to the years of income ending 30 June, 1988 and 30 June, 1989.

2. At the hearing, the Trustees were represented by Mr McPherson, who is an accountant, and the Commissioner by Mr Fogarty, who is a member of his office. Various documents, to which we shall refer, were admitted in evidence and oral evidence was given by the Trustee of the Fund.

The issues

3. There were two issues. The first was whether the Trustees had, on or before 11 June, 1986, borrowed money otherwise than to secure temporary finance. The second was whether, because of special circumstances existing in relation to the Fund during the years of income, it would be reasonable for the Fund to be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions during those years.

Background

4. The facts in this case were largely agreed upon between the parties. In light of that and in the light of the evidence we will set out the findings of fact which we have made.

5. The Fund was established on 6 April, 1982. On 16 December, 1985, the Treasurer and the Minister for Employment and Industrial Relations jointly issued a statement and released for public comment draft operational standards for superannuation funds. The Government considered the submissions it received on those draft operational standards as well as the comments it received during the discussions which it held with various interested bodies. It then reviewed the draft operational standards and, following the completion of its review, the Treasurer announced that the Government had decided to proceed with the implementation of the operational standards which were outlined in attachment I to his statement. Superannuation funds and approved deposit funds, the Treasurer announced, would be required to meet those standards in order to be eligible for relevant taxation concessions.

6. The Treasurer also announced that the Government had decided to establish a new statutory position of Occupational Superannuation Commissioner who would be responsible for oversighting compliance with the standards. It was expected that the Commissioner would be fully operational by mid 1987 and that he would also assume the responsibilities for the administration of non-revenue supervisory functions applicable to superannuation which were then administered by the Commissioner of Taxation under the provisions of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

7. Under the heading of "Investment Standards" in Attachment I, it was stated that the standards would apply to all eligible superannuation funds, whether existing or new, from 1 July, 1986 (clause 16). In clause 19, it was written:

"... The assets of any fund will not be able to be charged

except to obtain temporary finance such as overdraft or any

other similar facility that might be prescribed. In the

case of funds which have charged their assets beyond this

limit as at the date of this announcement, such charging of

assets will be phased down in accordance with the rules

which apply to the phasing down of 'in house' assets

referred to in paragraph 16."

The "in house assets" provisions are not relevant in this case.

8. The operating standards were provided for in Part II of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Act 1987 ("the Act") which came into operation on 21 December, 1987. On 22 December, 1987, the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations ("the Regulations") came into operation. Also on that day, the Occupational Superannuation Group of the Insurance and Superannuation Commission ("the Commission") issued Circular No.5 headed "Explanation of the Occupational Superannuation Standards Regulations - Completion and lodgement of annual returns for 1986/87 by superannuation funds and approved deposit funds" ("Exhibit 6").

9. Section 12 of the Act provides that the Commissioner shall give a written notice to the trustee of the fund stating whether or not he is satisfied that the fund satisfied the superannuation fund conditions in relation to the year of income. In making this decision, the Commissioner must have regard to the return and certificate by the trustee and the certificate by an approved auditor which the trustee of the fund must give the Commissioner in relation to the year of income together with any other information which is available to him (sub-section 12(3)). Sub-section 12(6A) provides that the Commissioner may refuse to give a notice under sub-section 12(3) if a levy payable on the return remains unpaid. There is no suggestion that there is any such unpaid levy in this case. Sub-section 12(6) relates to the ameliorating provisions of section 13 to which we shall refer later in these reasons.

10. Section 5 sets out what is meant by a reference to a fund's satisfying the superannuation conditions in relation to a year of income. It sets out a number of conditions which must be satisfied in relation to the fund in relation to the year of income. The only condition which is of concern to us in this case is that found in paragraph 5(2)(b) which provides that the reference means that

"(b) at all times during the year of income when the fund was

in existence and there were in force regulations for the

purposes of subsection 7(1) prescribing standards applicable to

the fund, the fund complied with those standards."

11. Sub-section 7(1) provides that the Regulations may prescribe standards applicable to the operation of superannuation funds. Paragraph 16(1)(b) of the Regulations was made pursuant to the power given under section 7 of the Act. It provides that

"Subject to sub-regulation (3), the trustees of a superannuation

fund shall not borrow, or maintain an existing borrowing of,

money, whether by way of a secured or unsecured loan, otherwise

than to secure temporary finance;"

12. Sub-regulation 16(3) is relevant as is sub-regulation 16(3A) with which it is linked. As there is no question that the Fund is a private, rather than a public, sector fund, we will omit those provisions relating to public sector funds in setting out those sub-regulations:

"(3) If:

(a) the trustees of a private sector fund had, on or before

11 June 1986; ...

(b) ...

borrowed money in a manner that does not comply with the

standard set out in paragraph (1)(b), the trustees must, as

soon as practicable but in any event not later than the end of

the applicable day, make such arrangements as are necessary to

comply with that standard and, until such arrangements are

made or the end of the applicable day, whichever happens

first, that standard does not apply to the fund in respect of

the money borrowed by the trustees on or before the day

specified in paragraph (a) or (b), as the case requires.

(3A) In subregulation (3), 'the applicable day' means:

(a) in relation to a private sector fund - 30 June 1995; or

(b) ..."

13. The term "temporary finance" used in paragraph 16(1)(b) is defined to mean "... finance arranged by borrowing in order to overcome cash flow problems in the payment of superannuation benefits" (sub-regulation 16(5)).

14. On 11 June, 1986, the Trustee signed a contract for the purchase of a property for $79,000. The contract was expressed to be "subject to finance approval". Approval for a loan amounting to $40,000 was given to the Trustees on 18 June, 1986 (T documents, page 35). Funds were advanced and the property transferred to the Trustees in August, 1986.

15. Before the contract for the purchase of the property was completed, the Trustees took advice from their then accountants as to whether they had the appropriate authority and power to purchase it. They were advised that they could do so. Their bank, however, advised them that there was nothing in the Trust Deed of the Fund to allow borrowings to be made (T documents, page 37). In view of that advice, the Trustees arranged for the Trust Deed to be changed to allow such borrowings and the Deed was changed on 2 July, 1986 (T documents, page 39).

16. Potentially, therefore, there were three groups of advisers who might have been in the position to advise the Trustees that there borrowings would be in breach of the proposed operating standards. Even if they had been given that advice, we note that the contract for the purchase of the property had become an unconditional contract upon the bank's advising the Trustees that their finance had been approved on 18 June, 1986.

17. In March, 1988, the Trustees became aware that they were in breach of the operating standards when their then accountants sent them a circular advising them of the alterations to the law. In its letter of 28 March, 1988, the accountants advised that the trustees of all funds were required to lodge with the Commission an annual return and an auditor's certificate for the financial year ending 30 June, 1987. While most had to be lodged by 31 March, 1988 the deadline for the Fund in this case was 29 April, 1988. Under the heading of "Investment Standards", the accountants advised that investment standards were to apply to all eligible superannuation funds, whether existing or new, from 1 July, 1986. They went on to specify particular standards including that with which we are concerned in this case i.e. that the trustees of a fund are prohibited from borrowing money at any time after 11 June, 1986 except to obtain temporary finance such as overdraft or similar prescribed facility.

18. Upon becoming aware that the Fund could be in breach, the Trustees approached their then accountants and sought their advice as to whether or not they were actually in breach of the operating standards. They were advised that they were and that they should request the Commissioner to exercise his discretion.

19. The annual return for the year of income ending 30 June, 1987 contained explanatory notes. Under the heading, "Question 13: Government's Operational Standards for Superannuation Funds", appeared a number of paragraphs. One of those paragraphs was headed "Investments" and stated:

"Investments are required to be directed towards achieving

maximum returns to members consistent with the sound

management of fund assets. Investments should generally be at

arms length and be consistent with the Government's 'in-house'

asset restrictions. From 11 June 1986, superannuation funds

should not borrow except to obtain temporary finance by way of

overdraft and there is a requirement for the phasing down of

the charging of assets charged before 11 June 1986.

(Regulation 16 of OSS Regs, section 121C of ITAA and OSC-IC2."

(Exhibit 2)

20. We find that at some time on or before 30 June, 1988, the Trustees had instructed their accountants to lodge the return. The return was completed and a certificate by an approved auditor signed on 28 January, 1989 obtained. The form was lodged on 9 March, 1990 (which was quite some time after the due date of 29 April, 1988) with a request that the Commissioner exercise his discretion under section 13 of the Act and with an explanation as to why the standards had not been complied with.

21. Section 13 ameliorates the requirements of the Act and the Regulations that superannuation funds must comply with the operating standards. In relation to section 13 the only question in this case is whether the provisions of paragraph 13(1)(b) have been complied with. That paragraph states:

"(1) Where, in relation to a fund in relation to a year of

income of the fund:

(a) ...

(b) the trustees of the fund satisfy the Commissioner that,

because of special circumstances that existed in relation to

the fund during the year of income, it would be reasonable

for the fund to be treated as if it had satisfied the

superannuation fund conditions; ...

(c) ...

the Commissioner shall give notice in writing to the trustees

of the fund stating that the Commissioner is satisfied that

the fund should be treated as if it had satisfied the

superannuation fund conditions in relation to the year of

income."

22. In their request for the Commissioner to exercise his discretion, the Trustees set out the information consistent with that we have set out above and then wrote

"The property acquisition when viewed in total, has proved an

excellent investment for the fund.

If required, the trustees are prepared to dispose of the

property to repay the above debt. In these circumstances the

fund would achieve a substantial capital gain which would more

than justify the acquisition in terms of return on investment.

The trustees are of the opinion that the value of the property

is currently in excess of $115,000.

A specific request for a determination by the insurance and

Superannuation Commission is requested. The trustees believe

that having regard to the circumstances as outlined above, the

Commission should exercise its discretion and make the

determination that the borrowing made by the fund was in order

and that the fund was not in breach of the I.S.C. guidelines

for the 1986/87 and subsequent years.

The trustees are of the opinion that it may not be in the best

interests of the fund members to sell the investment property

in question. However, they are prepared to sell the property

and repay the debt if requested by the I.S.C. or should the

I.S.C. rule unfavourably and determine that the fund is

taxable." (T documents, page 46)

23. In March, 1990 the Trustees approached another firm of accountants for advice. On 22 March, 1990 they were sent written advice by that firm advising them that it appeared that the idea of paying off the property using current investment moneys was a sound one and that they should accumulate funds to do that. At the same time they should be seek advice as to whether they should sell certain shares either then or at some time in the future. The Trustees were told that it was important that they had a meeting and that they "minute specifically and completely" their intended action to clear the mortgage on the property. That action was to be taken to "rebalance the fund" (T documents, page 47).

24. The annual return for the year of income ending 30 June, 1988 was received in the Commission on 9 March, 1990 (again, well after the lodgement date of 30 April, 1989) (T documents, pages 112-115) and that for the year of income ending 30 June, 1989 was received on 5 June, 1990 (T documents, pages 106-110). The Trustees again asked for an exercise of the Commissioner's discretion and attached an explanation in substantially the same terms as that attached to the first return (see paragraph 22 above). The instructions for the annual return for the year of income ending 30 June, 1988 included a number of notes as did the return for the previous year. Paragraph (d) of those notes was in a different format and used different wording from that used in the previous year. In particular, it advised those completing the forms to refer to regulation 16 of the Regulations and to Circular 8. It drew particular attention to the requirement that "from 11 June, 1986, superannuation fund should not borrow except to obtain temporary finance and their is a requirement for the phasing down of respective borrowings before 11 June, 1986" (Exhibit 3). A similar statement was included in the instructions for the annual return for the year of income ending 30 June, 1989 (Exhibit 4).

25. On 25 June, 1990, the Trustees' then accountants sent them by facsimile a copy of a letter which they had received from the Commissioner. That letter was undated but was headed with the relevant tax file number and the year of income was shown as "1986/87". The letter advised that

"... the Commissioner is satisfied that, in respect of the year

of income shown above, the fund satisfied the relevant fund

conditions set out in the Occupational Superannuation Standards

Act 1987. The Commissioner of Taxation will be advised

accordingly." (T documents, page 63)

26. At the foot of the covering page of the facsimile message is a handwritten but unsigned note dated 26 June, 1990. It recorded that the author had spoken with a person who is presumably a member of staff of the Trustees' then accountants. The note went on to say

"Expects no change in the following years. (invest OK.

Suggested better NOT to pay off TG now ( ... I decide to

accept this and will NOT pay out loan. ( need to invest

$30,000." (T documents, page 64)

Consideration

27. The first issue to consider is whether the trustees had borrowed money otherwise than to secure temporary finance and whether they had done so on or before 11 June, 1985. We find on the evidence which is quite clear that the Trustees borrowed money to purchase the property. It was a long term borrowing to be repaid over time. It was not borrowed in order to overcome cash flow problems in the payment of superannuation benefits. Consequently, it could not be described as temporary finance.

28. There was some debate during the hearing as to whether the Trustees entered a formally binding contract to purchase that property on 11 June when they signed the contract, on 12 June when the vendors signed that contract or on 18 June when the Trustees' finance was approved and the contract for sale and purchase of the property became unconditional. It is unnecessary to determine the date of the contract for, whatever the date, the date of the contract does not determine the date of the "borrowing". In this case the "borrowing" is that which is borrowed and "that implies repayment at some time under some circumstances" (in Re The Southern Brazilian Rio Grande De Sul Railway Company Limited (1905) 2 Ch 78 per Buckley J at page 83). That which is borrowed is the money the Trustees required to complete the contract for the sale and purchase of the property. The borrowing is the subject of a separate contractual arrangement between the Trustees and the bank. It is not the subject of the contract between the vendor and the Trustees although that contract is conditional upon the Trustees' entering a separate contract for the borrowing. It follows that the date of the contract of sale and purchase does not determine the date on which the borrowing occurred.

29. On the facts in this case we are satisfied that the borrowing occurred after 11 June, 1986 but do not need to consider whether it occurred on 18 June, 1986 when the bank approved the loan, in August, 1986 when the money was actually advanced by the bank at the settlement of the contract between the vendor and the Trustees or at some time in between. It follows from this finding that sub-regulation 16(3) to enable the trustees of certain private sector funds to re-organise their affairs does not extend to the Trustees in this case. Consequently, the Trustees do not have the opportunity to take advantage of the provisions which allow the trustees of certain superannuation funds until 30 June, 1995 to ensure that they comply with the operating standards. The Trustees in this case must comply forthwith unless paragraph 13(1)(b) applies.

30. Are there special circumstances that satisfy the Commission or that it would be reasonably for the Fund to be treated as if it had satisfied the superannuation fund conditions? The Commissioner was so satisfied in relation to the year of income ending 30 June, 1987 and, in view of the almost contemporaneous occurrence of the Treasurer's statement on 11 June, 1986 and the Trustees' entering the contract for the purchase of the property, that decision seems to us to have been eminently reasonable. It would have been extremely difficult for the Trustees to have known of the Treasurer's statement and to arrange their affairs accordingly.

31. We are not, however, asked to review that decision but to review the Commissioner's decision in relation to the following two years. By the end of the following year on 30 June, 1988, we find that the Trustees knew of the superannuation standards for they had been sent a circular by their then accountants. They took advice and were advised to seek an exercise of the Commissioner's discretion. This was eventually done in January, 1989 when they lodged the return substantially out of time some ten months later. They had to wait some 18 months for the Commissioner's reply but, as his letter was undated, we do not know where the delay lay. In any event, the Trustees did not receive it until 25 June, 1990 and we find that they only then became aware that the Commissioner had exercised his discretion in their favour for the year of income ending 30 June, 1987.

32. Mr McPherson submitted on the Trustees' behalf that the Commissioner's favourable exercise of the discretion led the Trustees into thinking that it would be exercised favourably in the future. This was particularly so, he said, because the Trustees' initial request had been in respect of both "1986/87 and subsequent years" (see paragraph 22 above). We do not accept this submission. As the Trustees did not receive the decision until 25 June, 1990, it cannot have influenced their actions between their requesting the exercise of the discretion in January, 1989 and its being exercised in June 1990. In addition, it was clear from their then accountants' letter of 28 March, 1988 that the Commissioner had to be satisfied in relation to each year.

33. We find that it is clear from their then accountants' letter of 28 March, 1988 and also from the return for the year of income ending 30 June, 1987 that Trustees should not borrow after 11 June, 1986. The Trustees should have been put on notice by those statements as they were aware that their Fund had borrowed after that date. That they were aware that they should take some action is supported by the fact that they sought advice from a second accountant before they received notice that the Commissioner had exercised his discretion for the year of income ending 30 June, 1987. They were advised by that second accountant to re-pay the loan to the bank.

34. When the Trustees were advised that the Commissioner had exercised his discretion, they accepted the advice from their then accountants that they expected "no change in the following years" (see paragraph 26 above). There was no apparent basis for this advice either in the then accountants' own advice, in Circular No.5 or in the annual return for the year of income ending 30 June, 1987. In light of their visit to the second accountant and his conflicting advice it cannot be said that they were uninformed. They were in a position in which they made a choice as to the course they would adopt.

35. On the basis of both the Trustees' inaction in taking any steps to repay the loan to the bank and on the basis of their explanation to the Commissioner, we find that the Trustees made their decision as to whether or not they should sell the property after taking into account what was the best investment for the Fund and regardless of the restrictions imposed by the superannuation fund standards. We find that the preservation of the arrangements they had made was of paramount importance to them. We also find that the Trustees could have repaid the loan, or a substantial part of it, in June 1990 and make that finding on the basis of the note stating that there was a need to invest some $30,000 (see paragraph 26 above).

36. Taking into account the legislative restrictions which Parliament has generally imposed upon all superannuation funds on the one hand and the facts that the Trustees had been made aware of the restrictions and of their application to their Fund, their failure to take active steps to bring the Fund into line with the operative standards despite their ability to do so and despite the fact that they had, unknown to them at the relevant time, been given a period of grace as it were in respect of the first year of income on the other, we are unable to find that there are any special circumstances justifying the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion under paragraph 13(1)(b) of the Act.

37. For these reasons, we affirm the decisions under review.
