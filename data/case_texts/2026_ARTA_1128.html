Statement of Reasons

BACKGROUND

The issue in this interlocutory decision

  1. The individual Applicants (QQCS, CZMV, JRFK, PJXX) are a family comprised of parents and two children, and the other Applicant (WHVG) is the corporate trustee of a Superannuation Fund (“the Fund”). The individual Applicants were formerly co-trustees of the Fund. At all relevant times, the directors of WHVG were QQCS and CZMV. The Fund is a self-managed superannuation fund (“SMSF”).
  2. The substantive case relates to administrative penalties imposed upon the Applicants relating to annual returns filed by the Fund for the years from 2015-2018 (inclusive) (the “Relevant Years”). The administrative penalties related to a statement in those returns that is said by the Commissioner to be false or misleading.[1] That statement was that a named SMSF auditor had completed an audit of the Fund in each of the Relevant Years.
  3. The Applicants filed their respective Applications for Review in this Tribunal on or around 1 May 2025.[2] The Applications for Review related to objection decisions from the Commissioner dated on or around 8 April 2025[3] and on or around 22 January 2026.[4] Those decisions disallowed an array of objections including objections dated 4 December 2024[5] and 15 September 2025.
  4. The substantive proceedings are set down for a hearing on 20 July 2026.
  5. This interlocutory decision is about whether there is a new ground of objection contained in an amended Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions (“SFIC”) filed by the Applicants on 18 May 2026, [6] and if there is, whether the Tribunal should exercise its discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 (Cth) (“TAA”) to allow the Applicants to expand their grounds of objection in the substantive proceedings to include the proposed new ground of objection. In a case before the Tribunal, paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA limits the Applicants to their grounds of objection “unless the ART orders otherwise”.
  6. For the detailed reasons outlined below, the Tribunal concludes that:
    (a) the new ground of objection described below at paragraph ‎22 is new; and

    (b) it will not exercise its discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA to allow the Applicants to expand their grounds of objection to cover the new ground of objection before the Tribunal.

Relevant procedural history

  1. The procedural history of this matter is convoluted, and it suffices to summarise the main relevant events.
  2. Following a case management directions hearing where the Tribunal heard from the parties, by Directions and Orders dated 9 July 2025 the Tribunal issued an order that all of the proceedings would be heard together. This was to streamline the proceedings.
  3. Recorded in the Tribunal’s Directions and Orders dated 9 July 2025 was the following description of events at the case management directions hearing:
    (a) At the case management directions hearing, in response to questions from the Applicants about the number of penalties imposed, the Commissioner provided an explanation as to the nature of the joint and several liability of the Applicants in the Relevant Years for the penalties at issue. The Commissioner said that penalties were imposed upon all of the individual Applicants (QQCS, CZMV, JRFK, PJXX) jointly and severally as trustees of the Fund in 2015 and 2016, and jointly and severally on the directors of the corporate Applicant (WHVG) in 2017 and 2018.[7]

    (b) The Applicants signalled both before and after the Commissioner’s explanation that they would be seeking to expand their grounds of objection.

    (c) The Tribunal explained paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA, and the process to seek leave from the Tribunal to expand the grounds of objection. The Tribunal gave the Applicants a period in which to do so i.e., prior to 8 August 2025. The Tribunal referred the Applicants to relevant case law to consider.

  4. On 28 July 2025, the Applicants sought to expand their grounds of objection, enclosing a letter seeking leave and a document entitled “Reframed Grounds for ART Review”, and copying the Commissioner.
  5. The Tribunal heard from the parties at a case management directions hearing on 8 August 2025. The Tribunal considered all of the Applicants’ grounds of objection, not merely those in the “Reframed Grounds for ART Review.” At the 8 August 2025 hearing the Tribunal highlighted its jurisdictional limitations, particularly that it did not have the power to consider judicial review or administrative law grounds. The Applicants were advised that if they wanted to pursue matters of that nature, they needed to proceed otherwise than before the Tribunal.
  6. On 19 August 2025, the Applicants made a further attempt to recast some of the “Reframed Grounds for ART Review” following the 8 August 2025 hearing. An opportunity to do so had been provided to them by the Tribunal.
  7. By Interlocutory Decision and Orders dated 3 September 2025, the Tribunal recorded its decision on the Applicants’ grounds of objection and the “Reframed Grounds for ART Review”.
    (a) The Tribunal dismissed the following grounds because it has no jurisdiction to hear them:
    1. Ground 1: Doctrine of Res Judicata and Anshun Estoppel (from the Applicants’ original objections)
    2. Ground 2: Failure to consider relevant circumstances for Penalty Remission
    3. Ground 3: Inconsistent Decision-Making Process

    (b) The Tribunal exercised its discretion to allow the Applicants to pursue the following additional grounds of objection noted in its “Reframed Grounds for ART Review” document, noting that these grounds were likely part of the existing objection grounds in any event, and as such there was no prejudice to the Commissioner:

    1. Ground 1: Failure to Apply Correct Legal Standard for Materiality
    2. Ground 4: Temporal impossibility – Corporate Trustee not Appointed until after Relevant Period
    3. Ground 5: Incorrect Application of the penalty provisions.

    (c) The Tribunal noted that the Applicants could pursue a ground that the Commissioner should remit the penalties but was concerned to ensure the Commissioner considered that ground properly. For that reason, the Tribunal set out a process whereby the Applicants objected and the Commissioner issued an objection decision. The proceedings were put on hold and a December 2025 hearing date vacated pending that.

  8. On 19 January 2026, 22 January 2026, and 29 January 2026 (as applicable)[8] the Commissioner issued decisions following objections from the Applicants which did not allow for remission of penalties. Those decisions will be considered at the substantive hearing.
  9. By Orders and Directions dated 25 February 2026, the Tribunal exercised its discretion to allow the Applicants to proceed before the Tribunal with the additional ground that the Commissioner should remit the penalties and captured the January 2026 objection decisions in the current proceedings.
  10. The proceedings were set down for a hearing on 20 July 2026, and Tribunal directions to the hearing date were issued on 25 March 2026.
  11. Confidentiality orders were issued by the Tribunal in accordance with section 70 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) (“ART Act”) on 30 April 2026.
  12. In accordance with the Tribunal’s 25 March 2026 directions, the Applicants filed a SFIC and evidence (including witness statements) on 6 May 2026. However, the witness statements of PJXX and JRFK were unsigned.
  13. Prior to the Commissioner filing its SFIC and evidence,[9] correspondence ensued between the parties about the unsigned witness statements and the Tribunal was copied. All such witness statements were required to be filed by the Applicants on 6 May 2026. This all culminated in the Tribunal intervening by email dated 14 May 2026 to give the Applicants a deadline of 19 May 2026 to file signed versions of the unsigned witness statements, giving the Applicants indulgence given their self-representation.
  14. On 18 May 2026, the Applicants filed the finalised witness statements that had previously been unsigned but also sought to amend an earlier signed witness statement for CZMV and the earlier SFIC (the “Amended SFIC”) to add the new ground of objection at issue in this interlocutory decision. No extension of time had been granted to the Applicant. The explanation from QQCS was that:

I seek leave to file the Amended SFIC and amended witness statement of [CZMV]. The amendments resulted when I was informed by [JRFK] and [PJXX] that they had not signed the impugned Annual Statements, and accordingly did not make any alleged false statements. Prior to that notification, I had not looked at the returns for a long time, and had simply forgotten about the actual content of those statements.

  1. By emails dated 21 May 2026 and 22 May 2026, the Tribunal identified to the parties that the Applicants were seeking in the Amended SFIC and amended witness statement to add a new ground of objection. The Tribunal provided time to the Commissioner to confirm its position. The Tribunal advised the parties it was treating the Applicant’s 18 May 2026 email as an application for leave to add a new ground of objection.
  2. The Tribunal identified the new ground of objection as comprising (in summary) whether QQCS was the “agent” of the other individual Applicants when signing SMSF returns and when making statements to the Commissioner in accordance with section 284-75 and section 284-95 of Schedule 1 to the TAA (“new ground of objection”). This is an argument that at least some of the Applicants did not make a false or misleading statement[10] to the Commissioner because QQCS was not the “agent” of the other Applicants in their capacity as co-trustees and/or co-directors of the corporate trustee of the Fund.
  3. The Commissioner filed its SFIC and evidence on 27 May 2026.
  4. By email dated 29 May 2026, the Commissioner opposed the addition of the new ground of objection. The Commissioner also noted that the witness statements that had been previously unsigned had also been amended, and amendments were not merely made to the witness statement of CZMV as had been highlighted by the Applicants.
  5. Later, the Applicants filed a reply to the Commissioner’s SFIC on 8 June 2026 (“Applicants’ Reply SFIC”). The Applicants’ Reply SFIC was accompanied by a witness statement from QQCS dated 8 June 2026. The Applicants’ Reply SFIC sought to rely upon the new ground of objection although the Tribunal had not exercised its discretion to allow the new ground of objection at that stage.[11]

The process adopted to determine this interlocutory matter

  1. In accordance with subsection 106(6) of the ART Act, and prior to the Applicants’ Reply SFIC being filed, the Tribunal proceeded to engage with the parties to determine whether to exercise its discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA to accept the new ground of objection on the papers, without holding an interlocutory hearing. The Tribunal sought submissions from the parties in accordance with subsection 106(7) of the ART Act.
  2. After considering the submissions it had received, on 3 June 2026 the Tribunal decided it would consider the addition of the new ground of objection without holding an interlocutory hearing. That was because the issues for determination could be considered adequately on the papers, and, given timing (particularly the hearing date), it was reasonable to decide this interlocutory matter without holding a hearing.
  3. The Tribunal advised the parties of that decision and provided each party an opportunity to file submissions on whether the Tribunal should accept the new ground of objection exercising the discretion in section 14ZZK(a) of the TAA. Those submissions were required on or before 10 June 2026. The parties filed those submissions.[12]

The parties’ submissions

  1. The Commissioner said that the Tribunal should not accept the new ground of objection. This is because:
    1. The new ground of objection might be important to the Applicants, but its merits are not strong. The Commissioner points to what it says are inconsistencies in the Applicants’ objections as to who made the relevant statements at issue and maintains that the objections as a whole demonstrate acceptance that the statements are the Fund’s statement and QQCS was agent for the Fund.[13] The Commissioner’s argument is that the Applicants’ new ground of objection is inconsistent with their previous position as set out in their objections.
    2. The Commissioner says the delay in raising the new ground of objection is considerable and the explanation for that delay is inadequate.[14]
    3. The Commissioner says there is real prejudice to the Commissioner including additional cost and concerns about access to evidence as a considerable period has passed from when the Fund’s returns for the Relevant Years were filed[15]
    4. The new ground of objection has been raised at a late stage of the litigation.[16]
  2. The Applicants said that the new ground of objection is not a new ground at all. In particular:
    1. The Applicants refer to objections they say are dated 11 December 2024 (but which are dated 4 December 2024) where they contend that there was a ground to the effect that “the statements were not made by you”.[17]
    2. The Applicants add that the Commissioner’s Objection Decisions dated 8 April 2025 refer to this ground saying the Applicants contended that “the statements were not made by you”. [18]
    3. The Applicants say that the ground was inadvertently omitted from the Applications for Review due to the complexity of managing multiple proceedings, but the Applicants’ Amended SFIC cures this in circumstances where there is no procedural prejudice to the Commissioner.[19]
    4. The new ground of objection is significant to the Applicants’ case for a number of reasons, including because it is a threshold issue before penalties can be imposed and because the Commissioner did not consider it properly.[20]
    5. The Applicants made a number of arguments in support of the new ground of objection to support their view that it has significant merit.[21]

ANALYSIS

Whether there is a new ground of objection

  1. The first issue is whether the new ground of objection is a new ground at all. The Applicants say it is not new. Looking at the objections filed by the Applicants which are dated 4 December 2024:[22]
    (a) There is a statement at paragraph 4 of the CZMV, JRFK and PJXX objections that they “did not make the statement in the annual returns of [the Fund] for the years 2014-2015 or 2015-2016.[23] Rather the statement was made by [QQCS] who made the declaration in each of those annual returns on behalf of [the Fund].”[24]

    (b) There is a statement in the objections of JRFK, PJXX, and CZMV objections that they “made no such statement [being in context, a false or misleading statement] in any Annual Return for [the Fund] for any year of income.”[25]

  2. However, those statements in the context of those objections very clearly express an argument that the relevant individual Applicants did not make the statement, and dovetail into arguments that any statement (if false) is made about another taxpayer not JRFK, PJXX and CZMV, [26] any statement does not impact their tax liability, and any statement is not false in any material particular. That is clear from the face of the objections.
  3. When considering whether the new ground of objection is new, WHVG’s objection is relevant.[27] There the objection is that:

The assessment of an administrative penalty against [QQCS] as director of [WHVG], and against [WHVG], does not arise pursuant to section 284-75 of Schedule 1 Taxation Administration Act 1953 because [QQCS] did not make a statement on a tax-related matter in respect of [QQCS’] income tax affairs or which relates to the administration or management of [QQCS’] tax affairs.

  1. It would be expected if the new ground of objection had been previously raised that WHVG and the other Applicants would say that the returns for the Fund were filed on an unauthorised basis as QQCS was not authorised by co-trustees or co-directors to file those returns.[28] Instead, the focus throughout each of the objections is on the argument that the statement was not the relevant Applicant’s statement or not QQCS’ statement but the Fund’s statement, and the statement did not impact the underlying tax affairs of any of the Applicants. That is the context in which the comments about QQCS’ involvement were raised by CZMV, JRFK and PJXX.
  2. Paragraph 14ZU(c) of the TAA states it is for the person making a taxation objection to “state in it, fully and in detail, the grounds the person relies on”. Nowhere in any of the objections do any of the Applicants articulate an argument that QQCS did not act as the agent for co-trustees or co-directors when signing and filing the Fund’s tax returns.
  3. The Applicants also did not include the new ground of objection in their Applications for Review either (accepting that the QQCS Application for Review was the main document setting out those grounds), due, the Applicants say, to oversight. I have concluded the new ground of objection was not included in the Applications for Review precisely because it is new.
  4. The objection decisions do not consider the new ground of objection. Rather, they consider the issue raised in the objections – that is that “the statements made were not statements relating to your tax affairs.” and (other than for QQCS) “were not made by you” [29] not on any basis relating to QQCS’ status as an agent. To the extent those decisions refer to statements made by “you” they are merely quoting the statutory provisions,[30] not considering an argument the Applicants never raised. In any event, when considering whether there is a new ground of objection the focus must be on the objections, not the Commissioner’s correspondence.
  5. The Tribunal finds that the new ground of objection was not raised in the objections and rejects the Applicants’ Submissions otherwise. I turn to consider whether to exercise my discretion to accept the new ground of objection.

Whether to exercise the discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA

Case law

  1. Case law[31] provides guidance to the Tribunal on whether to exercise its discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA to accept a new ground of objection. In summary, the case law says that the Tribunal should consider:
    (a) The adequacy of the explanation for the delay in raising the new ground of objection.

    (b) The legislative purpose for time limits and the efficient management of litigation before the Tribunal and/or the Courts.

    (c) Prejudice to the Applicants and/or to the Commissioner.

    (d) The merits of the new ground of objection.

    (e) The interests of justice[32] which includes considering the importance of the new ground to the Applicants’ case, considering the position for other cases and litigants, and the point at which the litigation has reached relative to a trial when the application to add a new ground of objection is made, the interests of the administration of justice.[33]

Analysis of the factors from case law

  1. In this case, the explanation for the delay in raising the new ground of objection, is, in my assessment, entirely inadequate. The substance of the Applicants’ explanation was that QQCS did not notice the issue until some of the other Applicants raised it with her in or around May 2026.
  2. I have taken into account that there is self-representation by the Applicants in these proceedings. However, the weight of that is countered by the fact that QQCS (who represents the Applicants) was formerly a registered tax agent and has considerable experience. In addition, many of the Applicants, and QQCS in particular, are experienced in managing their own litigation before the Courts and consequently would appreciate practice and procedure, including the significance of time limits in the administration of justice. In any event, as was noted in McLean at 4451, when considering the discretion to allow additional grounds of objection, the primary issue is the interests of justice:

[T]he Tribunal, and the Court, are required to dispense justice according to law as between the litigants and not according to the abilities of those representing them.

  1. The new ground of objection has been raised extremely late. The review by the Commissioner commenced in 2019 in relation to returns from 2015-2018.[34] The assessments at issue were issued on 17 and 18 October 2024, the assessments were objected to on 4 December 2024, and the time to object expired on or around 16 December 2024. Section 14ZU of the TAA requires a taxpayer to state in their objection the grounds on which the taxpayer relies “fully and in detail.” The procedural history of the matter before the Tribunal also demonstrates that the grounds of objection were considered in detail from July 2025 to September 2025 and this included the discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA. There has been extensive case management by the Tribunal. It is a little over a month out from the hearing date. The Applicants have been given every opportunity to raise the new ground of objection long before now. The Applicants’ explanation for the delay is inadequate in the light of all of those facts. This factor is against the Tribunal accepting the new ground of objection.
  2. The legislative purpose for time limits is referred to in the case law. This factor was considered in Gilder[35] at [46]:

The discretion [to allow the raising of a further ground of objection] is unfettered. But that is not to say that regard should not be had to the time limits imposed by the Act. The Act gives effect to a policy that taxation affairs should be dealt with efficiently and promptly. There are time limits for the lodgment of returns (s.161), for the lodgment of objections (s.185), for the lodgment of requests for reference (s.187), and upon the Commissioner for the amendment of assessments (s.170). This policy should be taken into account. And so also should the policy of this Court that proceedings in the Court be handled efficiently. These present proceedings, like other proceedings in the Court, have been the subject of directions hearings in which a Judge has given directions to ensure that the parties were aware of the legal and evidentiary issues which would arise at the hearing. In such circumstances, the Court will rarely permit additional and different issues to be raised at the hearing, certainly not unless good reason for doing so has been shown, which in the present case has not been done.[36]

  1. In Radge at [40] the Tribunal described the legislative requirements relating to the grounds of objection in tax proceedings under Part IVC of the TAA:

It is equally clear that Parliament has intended that it is, in the usual case, to be expected that the grounds of the objection and so the parameters of the dispute between the taxpayer and the Commissioner will be circumscribed by the limits drawn by the taxpayer at the outset. In the normal case, that circumscription will extend to the review in the Tribunal. The result is that the limits of the dispute are known between the taxpayer and the Commissioner even if there remains significant work to be done developing those grounds and gathering the evidence. That is in contrast to the normal situation in the Tribunal where the decision is reviewed afresh and without any restriction as to the grounds on which that review may be conducted.

  1. For similar reasons to those outlined when considering the first factor, this factor is against the Applicants. The time limits in the legislation are important to the overall tax system. The new ground of objection could have been raised in 2024 when the objections were filed, it could have been raised with the Tribunal in 2025 when the grounds of objection were considered, and it was not. The Tribunal has issued extensive directions, there has been detailed case management, and this case is to be heard in just over a month from now. Although now retired, QQCS is an experienced practitioner. I do not accept QQCS did not have the ability to consider the new ground of objection long before now. It would undermine the purpose of the time limits to allow the Applicants to raise a new ground of objection now.
  2. In terms of prejudice there would be prejudice to the Applicants if they are unable to raise the new ground of objection that they wish to raise and consider important to their case. Equally, there is prejudice to the Commissioner. The Applicants raised the new ground of objection a week out from when the Commissioner had to file its SFIC and just over a month out from the hearing date. The Applicants have known the Commissioner’s case since at least 2024 when the Commissioner issued Position Papers and Reasons for Decision to them.[37] To raise the new ground of objection now is unfair. I accept the Commissioner’s Submissions to the effect there is additional cost and potential evidential prejudice for the Commissioner. I reject the Applicants’ Submission that there is no procedural prejudice to the Commissioner. Nevertheless, the Tribunal has (narrowly) assessed this factor as neutral, as both parties would suffer prejudice.
  3. When considering the merits, it is not my role in an interlocutory proceeding to finally determine the new ground of objection. Instead, I am to assess its merits at a high level when considering my discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA.[38] I am also not assessing the merits of the Applicants’ entire case, as that is for the substantive hearing. I am considering the merits of the new ground of objection at a high level.
  4. As is noted above at paragraph ‎22, the Applicants’ new ground of objection is an argument that at least some of the Applicants did not make a false or mislead[39]g statement39 to the Commissioner because QQCS was not their “agent” when signing and filing the Fund’s returns – considering the Applicants in their capacity as co-trustees or co-directors of the corporate trustee of the Fund.
  5. The new ground of objection has little real merit. This is because:
    (a) Firstly, it is the Fund that made the relevant statement in the Fund’s tax returns. There are statutory provisions which provide that trustees have joint and several liability for civil penalties imposed on a trust such as the Fund (although that specific provision cannot apply to the Relevant Years so has not been given any weight)[40] or as co-directors of a corporate trustee of an SMSF.[41]

    (b) Secondly, there are statutory provisions which deem documents such as tax returns provided to the Commissioner under a taxation law that purport to be signed on behalf of an entity[42] to be prima facie evidence that they are made by that entity and/or with that entity’s authority.[43] The Fund returns were lodged some time ago.

    (c) Thirdly, there are evident difficulties with the new ground of objection being raised many years after the Fund’s returns were filed in circumstances where QQCS has a familial relationship with the individual Applicants, and where those Applicants have never raised any concerns before now. Under section 35D of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 (Cth) each trustee of a SMSF must ensure the Commissioner is given an annual return, and the Applicants have relied on QQCS to meet that obligation. The evidence is that QQCS was acting in her capacity as Fund trustee when the Fund returns were filed,[44] the Applicants appear to acknowledge that in their objections by saying, in substance, that the statement at issue was made by the Fund via QQCS. All of that, in turn, means a statement by the Fund was made by all of the trustees of the Fund at that time. QQCS was the Fund when she filed the returns.[45] Demonstrating agency at law is not necessary. There is no allegation from any of the Applicants that QQCS acted in breach of the Fund rules or in breach of trust when filing the returns in the Relevant Years.

    (d) In any event, if agency is needed, there are evidential difficulties. The T documents reflect a clear pattern of QQCS acting for all of the Applicants and for the Fund. For instance, QQCS acts for all of the Applicants in these proceedings. The Tribunal also accepts the Commissioner’s Submissions at [5]. The Applicants’ objections each refer to the relevant statement as being made by QQCS in the Fund’s returns and were made on behalf of the Fund. It is now inconsistent to say that QQCS was not acting for the Fund because she was not the agent[46] for her co-trustees or co-directors.[47]. The Applicants also may not have considered the other litigation they have been involved in before the Federal Court and the Full Federal Court where (for instance) the Fund’s tax returns at issue were introduced into evidence, in order to determine whether there is any inconsistency with the position they are now seeking to take before the Tribunal.

    (e) Finally, there is case law about the new ground of objection[48] which is not supportive.

  6. When considering the Applicants’ Submissions, the arguments raised do not deal with the statutory provisions at issue in the TAA and rely upon matters which in my assessment do not have merit.
  7. In the overall circumstances, I assess the merits of the new ground of objection to be weak.[49] On balance, this factor is not in favour of the Tribunal accepting the new ground of objection.
  8. The last and predominant factor[50] requires the Tribunal to consider the interests of justice. In my assessment, it is not in the interests of justice to allow a further ground of objection to be relied upon by the Applicants before the Tribunal given the following:
    (a) The purpose of the legislative time limits applicable to objections. Allowing such a late addition of a ground of objection undermines that purpose as well as undermining the time limits applicable to the administration of justice.

    (b) The Tribunal has engaged in detailed case management to date, including more than two months in 2025 considering the grounds of objection and the discretion in section 14ZZK(a) of the TAA.

    (c) The late stage at which these proceedings are at where there is a little over a month to the hearing date.

    (d) The importance of the new ground of objection in the overall circumstances of these proceedings. While I acknowledge the Applicants’ view of the significance of the new ground of objection, I do not assess the new ground of objection as being of any real importance to the Applicants’ overall case, given its inherent flaws. The Applicants’ Submissions dated 10 June 2026 have not persuaded me as to the importance of the new ground of objection.

    (e) The unfairness and prejudice to the Commissioner from an issue raised so late in the overall circumstances of this case.

    (f) The Tribunal’s objectives in the ART Act. For example, raising the new ground now is contrary to the objectives of the Tribunal to have this matter heard as efficiently as possible.[51] I am concerned it would lead to inevitable delay in a case that has already been delayed.[52] The hearing has been set down for a lengthy period.

    (g) Precedential cases - The discretion to accept late grounds of objection has not been exercised in other cases involving broadly similar circumstances (that is, a new ground of objection raised late, close to a hearing date or in a hearing).[53]

  9. I have concluded that the above factors outweigh the factor in favour of the Applicants - the prejudice to the Applicants from not being able to raise the new ground of objection.
  10. It is not in the interests of justice for me to exercise my discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA to allow the new ground of objection to proceed before the Tribunal.

Conclusion

  1. On balance, for those reasons, the Tribunal refuses to exercise its discretion in paragraph 14ZZK(a) of the TAA to allow the Applicants to raise the new ground of objection in the proceedings before it.
  2. For clarity, that means:
    (a) The new ground of objection will not be considered in the hearing on 20 July 2026, and evidence in support of that ground will be given no weight.

    (b) The Tribunal will consider the Applicants’ SFIC filed on 6 May 2026 and the admissible witness statement of CZMV will be the statement filed on 6 May 2026.

    (c) To the extent that the Applicants’ Reply SFIC seeks to rely upon the new ground of objection that will be disregarded by the Tribunal.

    (d) To the extent that the unsigned witness statements of JRFK and PJXX on 6 May 2026 were amended in the signed versions provided by the Applicants on 18 May 2026 to give evidence in support of the new ground of objection, those amendments will be given no weight by the Tribunal at the hearing.

    (e) To the extent that the QQCS Witness Statement dated 8 June 2026 seeks to produce facts in support of the new ground of objection those facts will be given no weight by the Tribunal at the hearing.
