Peter Mcnally and Tax Agents' Board of Queensland [1994] AATA 198; (1994) 94 ATC 2099 (15 July 1994)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

PETER McNALLY v. TAX AGENTS' BOARD OF QUEENSLAND

No. Q93/595

AAT No. 9609

Number of pages - 5

Tax Agent

(1994) 94 ATC 2099

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

D.W. MULLER (Senior Member), J.D. HORRIGAN (Member) AND E.T. KEANE (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Tax Agent - fit and proper person - 'prescribed qualifications' criteria - application containing false information

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975: S.37

Income Tax Assessment Act: S.251K(2)(d)

Income Tax Regulations 156(1)

HEARING

BRISBANE, 8 June 1994

15:7:1994

The Applicant appeared in person.

Counsel for the Respondent: Mr Innes

Solicitor for the Respondent: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision of the Tax Agents' Board of Queensland to cancel the registration of Peter McNally as a Tax Agent.

DECISION

D.W. MULLER, J.D. HORRIGAN AND E.T. KEANE This is an application to review a decision of the Tax Agents' Board of Queensland (the Qld Board) dated 26 October 1993 which cancelled the registration of Peter McNally, the applicant, as a tax agent.

2. This matter was heard in Brisbane on 8 June 1994. The applicant appeared on his own behalf and the respondent was represented by Mr. Innes of Counsel, instructed by the Australian Government Solicitor.

3. Oral evidence was given by the applicant and by William John Vickers, the Secretary of the Qld Board. The Tribunal also received the following documents as evidence in the case:

(a) The documents lodged under Section 37 of the Administrative

Appeals Tribunal Act 1975.

(b) Certificate of service from Golden Fleece Petroleum dated

31 May 1982.

(c) Reference from Repco Gasket Company dated 5 April 1984.

(d) Letter from O.T. Lempriere and Co. Ltd dated 7 October 1969.

(e) Handwritten letter of applicant to the Tax Agents' Board of New

South Wales ("the NSW Board") dated 15 January 1992.

(f) Statement by Neville W. Stitt dated 17 June 1992.

(g) ASA 2 Excerpt

(h) Letter from Queensland University of Technology to the Qld

Board dated 5 November 1991.

(i) Application dated 17 May 1990 to the Qld Board.

(j) Application dated 30 September 1991 to the Qld Board.

(k) Letter from the Tax Agents' Board of New South Wales.

(l) Letter from the Tax Agents' Board of Victoria.

4. Taking all of the material placed before the Tribunal into account the Tribunal finds as follows:

(a) Mr. McNally commenced work as an accountant in 1950 with

Thompson and Webb, a chartered public accounting firm. He remained

at that firm for 14 years and in that time he qualified as a member

of the Australian Society of Certified Practising Accountants. He

then worked for 2 years for a chartered accounting firm in New Guinea

where he qualified as a member of the Chartered Institute of

Secretaries. He returned to Australia and from the mid-1960's to the

present he has worked for various commercial firms, including 12

years with Golden Fleece Petroleum Limited, a short period of time in

1982 with Repco Gasket Company Pty Ltd and approximately two years

with O.T. Lempriere and Co. Ltd. In 1990 Mr. McNally commenced a two

year contract with Neville W. Stitt and Co. which he completed in

1992.

For a brief period of time after that he went into practice himself

and since then until the present he has been working as an accountant

for Peabody Constructions.

(b) On 29 October 1990, Mr. McNally applied to the Qld Board for

registration as a tax agent. His application was rejected.

(c) On 16 December 1991, Mr. McNally applied again to the Qld Board

for registration as a tax agent. His second application was also

rejected. On both occasions the Qld Board found that the combination

of Mr. McNally's academic qualifications and his professional

experience was not sufficient to satisfy the conditions necessary for

acceptance.

(d) On 15 January 1992, Mr. McNally wrote to the Tax Agents' Board

of New South Wales (the NSW Board) indicating his qualifications and

relevant employment and indicating interest in being registered as a

tax agent in New South Wales. The letter read as follows:

"In the near future I intend to relocate myself and family to

Northern NSW and if all arrangements can be made to commence practise

as a Public Accountant (CPA) and Income Tax Agent.

I was recently successful in the examination conducted by the

Australian Society of Certified Practising Accountants for the

"PUBLIC PRACTISE CERTIFICATE". This area is well under control.

However it is in the area of Income Tax Agent that causes me some

concern. Regulation 156 in respect academic requirements does not

specifically cover accountants who successfully complete the

examinations conducted by the Australian Society of Accountants (now

CPA) and who did not attend a tertiary institution.

I studied accounting by correspondence and passed the following exams

to qualify as an accountant with the Australian Society of

Accountants.

Stage I Accounting-auditing-commercial law A

Stage II Company Law and Procedure-Company Accounts-

Commercial law B-Monetary Theory and Practise

(economics)-Income Tax Law and Practise

Stage III Advanced Accounting A-Advanced Accounting

Auditing and Business Investigations

I also passed the exams of the Chartered Inst of Secretaries to

become a member of that Institute. These examinations were completed

in the 1960's and at that time was considered the most acceptable

method of qualifying as an accountant. This qualification is

considered by the profession as at least equal to a tertiary degree.

'Relevant employment' does not appear to present a problem:

1950-1968 Chartered Accounting Firms about 35% Income Tax

and 65% Accounting

1969-1989 Senior Commercial Accounting posts for various

companies (include Company Income Tax)

1990 to

present date Chartered Accounting Firm - about 45% Tax and

50% Accounting - 5% audit

Before I proceed further with my plans may I request an answer in

principle - do I comply with Regulation 156(1A) in respect to

academic requirement as "SUCH OTHER QUALIFICATION AS THE BOARD

REGARDS AS EQUIVALENT"? If yes - my Relevant employment since 1990

is adequate and I will then submit your formal application and

references. This seems the best course and I accordingly request

your kind indulgence."

(e) In early May Mr. McNally received a telephone call from the NSW

Board affirming that his qualifications and experience were adequate.

On 19 May 1992 Mr. McNally received an application form in the mail

from the NSW Board.

(f) On 22 June 1992 the NSW Board received an application for

registration from Mr. McNally signed by him on 30 May 1992 under

cover of a letter dated 19 June 1992. The application form contained

a number of questions which required answers, if relevant. In

particular, question 16 asked:

"Have you ever been refused membership or registration of, expelled,

suspended or cancelled from a Professional Body or Registration Board

(this includes a Tax Agents' Board), or disbarred or suspended from

the practice of your profession?"

Mr. McNally put a tick in the box marked "No".

(g) In late August 1992 Mr. McNally received the following

documents from the NSW Board, addressed to his home in Aspley,

Queensland, and dated 20 August 1992.

(i) A letter stating that the NSW Board had approved his

application and granted registration as a tax agent on 11 August

1992.

(ii) A copy of a certificate of registration as a tax agent issued

by the NSW Board, with the word "cancelled" on it.

(iii) A letter containing the following:

"Advice recently to hand indicates that you are currently a resident

of Queensland.

Income Tax Regulation 169(1) requires that this Board transmit all

documents relating to your registration to that State. A new

Certificate of Registration will be issued by the Queensland Board.

Receipt of the returned Certificate of Registration is hereby

acknowledged."

(h) On 14 September 1992 the Qld Board noted the transfer and

instructed the Secretary to issue a new certificate of registration

to Mr. McNally. On or about 28 September 1992, Mr. McNally received

a certificate of registration as a tax agent from the Qld Board.

(i) At a meeting held on 17 May 1993 the Qld Board considered a

reported summary of facts prepared by the Secretary to the Qld Board.

The Board noted that in his application to the NSW Board Mr. McNally

marked the response "no" to question 16 (noted in paragraph (f)

above). The Board further noted that in his application to the NSW

Board and in his letter of 15 January 1992 Mr. McNally stated that if

he was registered as a tax agent in New South Wales he intended to

relocate himself and his family and practise in New South Wales but

after registration on 11 August 1992 Mr. McNally continued to reside

and work in Queensland. The Board resolved to require Mr. McNally to

show cause why, in view of the false information supplied, his

registration should not be cancelled.

(j) A letter dated 7 June 1993 was sent by the Qld Board to Mr.

McNally requesting that he show cause why his registration should

not be cancelled, either by writing or by personal appearance before

the Board.

(k) Mr. McNally responded by a letter dated 10 June 1993.

Mr. McNally stated as follows:

".... Regrettably an error was made in my application to the NSW

Board in that I wrongly ticked the "NO" box in question 16. An

oversight that now causes me considerable embarrassment. I have

never been expelled or refused membership of a professional body,

unfortunately I overlooked the reference to the Tax Agents' board.

This has never occurred to me before, my integrity has never been in

question.

......

In respect of the other matter you raise, I am making every effort to

locate a suitable Practice to purchase. This is taking more time

than I originally envisaged. As my means are not large I am looking

for a small Practice of about $50000. In view of the delay I have

now broadened my area from NSW to the Brisbane area as well. I do

not see why this should be an issue. If you feel however I should

firstly seek the NSW Boards permission then so be it.

Lastly, at present I am in full time employment with a construction

company - Peabody Resources Limited, as an accountant, and where I'll

remain until I can secure a Practice or am retrenched. I commenced

here on 1st February, 1993. Prior to this and after completing a 2

year contract with the Chartered Accountant firm of Nevill W Stitt

and Co on the 30th September, 1992 I was unemployed. Additionally I

do some spare time work in accounting and tax for a small

manufacturing company and 2 partnerships (3 clients). I do not feel

this constitutes "practising in Queensland" as your letter suggests."

Mr. McNally attended before the Qld Board on 29 June 1993.

(l) At a meeting on 13 September 1993 the Qld Board considered

Mr. McNally's registration as a tax agent and decided that

Mr. McNally's registration should be cancelled under Section

251K(2)(d) of the Income Tax Assessment Act upon being satisfied that

Mr. McNally was not a fit and proper person to prepare income tax

returns and transact business on behalf of taxpayers.

(m) The Qld Board wrote to Mr. McNally by letter dated

17 September 1993 and, inter alia, stated:

"The Board found that you had a duty to disclose in your application

to the New South Wales Board that you had been refused registration

by this Board. The Board found that you breached that duty and

thereby demonstrated either a lack of candour towards the New South

Wales Board or neglect of such a kind that the Australian Taxation

Office could not be confident that you would deal with them or be

able to deal with them on behalf of taxpayers in the manner which the

office requires.

The Board therefore found that you were not a fit and proper person

to prepare income tax returns and transact business on behalf of

taxpayers in income tax matters and resolved to cancel your

registration as a tax agent pursuant to Section 251K(2)(d) of the

Income Tax Assessment Act."

(n) Mr. McNally's academic qualifications do not satisfy the

provisions of Regulation 156(1)(a)(i) of the Income Tax Regulations

relating to the registration of tax agents.

"156(1)

(a) the person:

(I) shall have completed the academic requirements for the award of a

degree, diploma or other qualification from an Australian university,

college of advanced education or other tertiary institution of an

equivalent standard, and have passed examinations in such subjects,

under whatever name, which an appropriate authority of the

university, college of advanced education or other tertiary

institution certifies to the Board to represent a course of study in

accountancy of not less than 3 years' duration and in commercial law

of not less than 18 months' duration or shall possess such other

qualifications as the Board regards as equivalent to those

qualifications"

(o) Mr. McNally's academic qualifications do satisfy the provisions

of regulation 156(1)(d)(ii) but his experience does not satisfy the

provisions of regulation 156(1)(d)(i).

"156(1)

(d) the person

(i) shall have:

(A) been engaged in relevant employment on a full-time basis for not

less than a total of 8 years in the preceding 10 years;

(B) otherwise been engaged in relevant employment to an extent that

the Board regards as equivalent to that referred to in

sub-subparagraph (A); or

(C) been engaged in such other employment and for such time as the

Board regards as equivalent to being engaged in relevant employment

as referred to in sub-subparagraph (A); and

(ii) shall:

(A) be a member of and entitled to vote at meetings of the Australian

Society of Accountants, the Institute of Chartered Accountants in

Australia or the National Institute of Accountants; or

(B) have, by written examination or examinations set by a college of

technical and further education (or an examination or examinations of

an equivalent or higher standard), successfully completed a course of

study in basic accounting principles and have, by written

examination, successfully completed a course of study in Australian

income tax law acceptable to the Board."

5. The issue for the Tribunal is whether Mr. McNally's registration as a tax agent should be cancelled because he is not a fit and proper person to be registered as a tax agent in that either:

(a) he does not satisfy the "prescribed qualifications" criteria

pursuant to Regulation 156 of the Income Tax Assessment Act and/or;

(b) he breached his duty to disclose to the NSW Board the fact that

he had been refused registration by the Qld Board.

6. The Tribunal finds that the applicant has not acquired the necessary combination of academic achievement and experience in 'relevant employment' for the appropriate time, to allow him to qualify for registration as a tax agent. The NSW Board should not have accepted his application for registration.

7. The Tribunal finds it unnecessary to decide the question of whether or not Mr. McNally deliberately misled the NSW Board when he made his application to that Board.

8. For the reasons stated above the decision to cancel the registration of Peter McNally as a tax agent will be affirmed.
