Last Updated: 25 January 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - chartered accountant in private practice - dispute with Institute in relation to elections to State Council - action taken against Institute and State Registrar for declaration that an election be declared invalid and an order that applicant he appointed - action dismissed with costs - applicant still making allegations of impropriety - whether expenditure allowable in terms of sections 51(1) and 64A.

Income Tax - chartered accountant in private practice - overseas travelling in 1981 and 1983 years of income - 1981 trip to machinery exposition and also to Disneyland and Hawaii - 1983 trip to accounting convention in Mexico and then to Mew York (sister lives there), London and return to Hobart via Perth whether wholly allowable in terms of section 51(1).

Income Tax Assessment Act (Cth) - sections 51(1) and 64A

Hallstroms Pty Ltd v FC of T (1946) 72 CLR 534

The applicant objected stating "the assessment should be reduced by: -

Travel expenses - $5,018 or some lesser amount. The grounds on which I rely are:-

  1. That the trip for business purposes was completely organised before leaving Hobart. The Institute of Chartered Accountants in Australia, The Institute of Chartered Secretaries and Administrators and the Australian Society of Accountants were all notified and all three bodies provided letters of introduction.
  2. The whole of the expenditure is deductible pursuant to the first limb of section 51(1) being incurred in gaining or producing assessable income.

As in:

  • visits to AICPA library
  • discussions of a technical nature with CPAs and other Chartered Accountants
  • increased knowledge gained from visits to New York., London Stock Exchanges
  • collection of technical material for evaluation of possible implementation, journals, manuals and course guides
  • expenditure incidental to or connected with my operations or earnings of the year, as required by Amalgamated Zinc (de Bavay's) Ltd v FC of T (1935) 54TCLR at 307 (High Court).
  1. In addition the whole of the expenditure is deductible pursuant to the second limb of section 51(1) being expenditure necessarily incurred in the carrying on of a business for the purpose of gaining or producing assessable income.

First in:

  • business of public accountant, registered tax agent, secretary and company auditor being carried on
  • collection of technical material on running of and accounting firm, training staff, firm promotion, book lists and study courses.
  • discussions with CPAs and other Chartered Accountants on the running of accounting firms (eg Mike Jennings on recruitment practices).
  • contact with other professionals in different countries, discussions on the state of world economy, state of world markets, state of the profession, trends in the profession etc.
  • firm here has no national association, so knowledge of other firms must be done on a first hand basis.

Expenditure "necessarily incurred" as it was clearly appropriate or adapted for carrying on the business of a Chartered Accountant, as required by Ronpibon Tin ML v FC of T [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47.

It is unnecessary, in the case of a continuing business, to establish a direct connection between the loss or outgoing and the actual assessable income produced by the business (FC of T v Snowden & Wilson Pty Ltd).

It is not for the court or the Commissioner to say how much a taxpayer ought to spend in obtaining his income (or in carrying on a business), but only how much he has spent (Tooheys Ltd v C of T (NSW)).

  1. If it is not accepted that the whole of the amount claimed is an allowable deduction then some, amount greater than presently should be allowed.
    • on a time basis of apportionment the proportion allocated for business does not take into account the days spent with Chartered Accounting firms, with CPAs and CPA firms in America, at the New York and London Stock Exchange, with the Institute of Chartered Accountants, the Institute of Chartered Secretaries, the Society of Accountants; all of which are for business purposes nor does it allow for days spent travelling or weekends/days for recreation, all of which should be allowed as business proportion.

It is contended that if apportionment is deemed necessary then Commissioner should have regard to the predominant motive for the expenditure, as in FC of T v Kowal (1984) 15 ATR 125 and 27 CTBR (NS) Case 64, where it was held that the proper method of determining the appropriate apportionment of expenditure was to determine the degree of prominence to be attached to the objects or purposes of the expenditure. In the case at hand, because of the reasons listed elsewhere in this objection, it is considered that the overwhelming purpose falls within either of both limbs of section 51(1) and so the overwhelming majority of the expenditure should be allowed.

8. The objection was disallowed with the covering letter stating:-

Consideration has now been given to your objection of 28 June 1984 against your assessment for the year ended 30 June 1983.

$2,524 of your claim for $7,210 overseas travelling expenses has already been allowed, representing the proportion of your expenses applicable to attendance at the XII International Congress of Accountants.

Your claim for the balance of expenses, $5,018 falls for consideration under section 51(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act. This section authorises a deduction for losses and outgoings incurred in the course of gaining or producing assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, other than losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital private or domestic nature.

Visits to large accounting firms and stock exchanges are neither incidental nor relevant to your accounting practice. As there is insufficient nexus between the expenditure, and the derivation of your income, no deduction is allowable under the first limb of the section.

In the absence of such a nexus, the expenditure cannot be said to be necessarily incurred, clearly appropriate or adapted for your carrying on a business as a Chartered Accountant; and no deduction is allowable under the second limb of the section. In any event, the expenditure is private in nature.

As only seven days of the thirty-six day trip were for business purposes the predominant motive for the expenditure would seem to be private. Accordingly, the method of apportionment of expenses outlined in 71 ATC 238 Case C54 and 72 ATC 17 Case D4, has been followed.

9. Turning now to the questions involved in these applications it will be convenient to take them seriatim.

Legal Expenses

10. A member of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in Australia (Tasmanian Branch) the applicant has endeavoured to become elected to the State Council for a considerable period of time. His endeavours have not been successful due to "Joe Blow" blocking him. In June 1977 the State Registrar issued a notice in the following terms:-

"The June Journal of the Institute contains a Notice of the State's Annual General Meeting to be held in Launceston in October 1977."

This notice indicates that Mr T G Challen is retiring as a State Councillor and is eligible for re-election.

"Members are now advised that Mr T G Challen will NOT be standing for re-election and accordingly there will be two vacancies on State Council."

Mr R J Dwyer retires and is eligible for re-election.

"Members are reminded that nominations in the prescribed form are to be in my hands by 1st July 1977."

The applicant nominated and. his and another's application were the only ones received by 1 July 1977 - as the only two applicants for the two vacancies both would have been elected. However on 19 August 1977 the State Registrar advised all members as follows:-

"You were previously advised that Mr T G Challen did not propose to stand for re-election as a State Councillor at the next annual general meeting of the Tasmanian Dranch of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in Australia to be held on 28 October 1977."

However as no nomination was received from a Northern member by 1st July 1977 Mr Challen being eligible was requested to again offer himself for re-election. He has agreed to do so.

11. Following the receipt of this notice the applicant on 8 September 1977 wrote to the State Registrar stating, inter alia, Mr Challen is not eligible for re-election under By-Laws No 104, that the present North-South arrangement of directing one member each way has no legal status and that, if Challen's name was included as a candidate, action would be taken to declare the election null and void. Challen's name was not removed and he and Dwyer were declared elected. Further correspondence ensued (including to the National Registrar) and a writ no. 2262 of 1978 was lodged in the Supreme Court of Tasmania seeking:-

"(a) A declaration that the election of Mr Trevor George Challen as a State Councillor to the Institute of Chartered Accountants in Australia, Tasmanian Branch be declared invalid.

(b) An order that the Applicant be placed in the stead of the said Mr Trevor George Challen as a State Councillor for the Institute of Chartered Accountants in Australia, Tasmanian Branch.

(c) An order that the Respondent pay the costs of and incidental to the application.

(d) An order that the Court make such further or other Orders as it deems fit."

12. By memo of 9 October 1979 the applicant's solicitors advised him

"We have been advised by the solicitors for the Institute of Chartered Accountants that their clients have instructed them at this stage to resolve this matter by the Court in the absence of discontinuation by yourself.

As previously advised in the line (sic) of the declaration by Mr Moore that at no time did he receive advice in writing from Mr Challen that Mr Challen was going to retire it would seem, that there is no way that we can finally win this action and although you have been wronged by the action of Mr Moore there appears to be no legal redress as there has been no legal breech of the By Laws."

and further by letter of 21 April 1980:-

"We advise that this matter has been listed for hearing on the 6th May 1980. Accordingly something will have to be done before that time. We repeat the writer's advice that this matter cannot succeed and unfortunately the only course we can see is to consent to judgment against us."

The final result was as indicated in the solicitor's letter (order of Mr Justice Cosgrove of 6 May 1980) and on 24 June 1980 the Taxing Officer taxed the Institute's cost of $1,955.80 and allowed the same at the sum of $1,800. The Institute's solicitors on 3 July 1980 wrote to the applicant's solicitors enclosing a copy of the Certificate of Taxation and asking for payment within 10 days. His solicitors advised him of same by letter of 8 July 1980 and he drew a cheque for $1,800, delivered it to his solicitors on 5 August 1980 who forwarded their cheque for the same amount to the Institute's solicitors by letter of 7 August 1980. By letter of 5 December 1980 his solicitors forwarded their account of $870.30 to the applicant stating "we have not dated it' in case you wish to back-date it for tax purposes" and subsequently issued a receipt for this amount dated 23 February 1981.

13. In view of the details set out in the preceding paragraph it is doubtful if it can be said that the amount of $2,770 claimed in the 1980 year of income was in fact incurred in toto in that year. However no argument on this aspect was advanced on behalf of the Commissioner and it may be put to one side. What was advanced was that, if the expenditure did in fact relate in any way to the applicant's practice, it related to the acquisition of status and was an affair of capital. It was submitted also that it did not have the character of a working expense and was too remote to qualify as a deduction. Reference in support was made to Sun Newspapers Ltd & Associated Newspapers Ltd v FC of T [1938] HCA 72; (1938) 61 CLR 337, Hallstroms Pty Ltd v FCC of T [1946] HCA 34; (1946) 72 CLR 634 and John Fairfax & Sons Pty Ltd v FC of T [1959] HCA 4; (1959) 101 CLR 30.

14. It is clear from the evidence that the applicant felt very strongly that others were determined to deny to him what he saw as his right as a long standing member to serve on State Council. He saw the occurrence in question as a further instance of that animosity to him and as one which he could use to defeat those concerned. Despite the order of the Court in May 1980 the applicant has persisted in his endeavours - no confidence motion October 1980 - and has taken or threatened further legal actions. In June 1981 he wrote to the National President stating, inter alia, "This time I will not be pressurized into withdrawing the case as in my opinion the Institute belongs to all members and not just a few members". A letter of 6 September 1982 written by the applicant to another stated, inter alia, "The State Council must be called upon to 'stand up' and explain their persistent and continued actions. The Institute belongs to all members not just a few and so 'The Old School Club' must be outlawed" whilst a further letter of the same date to the Executive Director of the Institute in Sydney closed with the sentence "If I do not hear from you within 7 days that you have intervened then I will instruct my Solicitor to continue Legal Action for Improprietary (sic) and claim unspecified damages". The documents tendered also show that by letter of 28 March 1983 the applicant was seeking a "Legal Opinion on my intention to continue legal action against the State Council of the Institute and in particular, the State Registrar and the two senior members for impropriety and claim unspecified damages". Although the applicant may have felt election to the Council could assist his practice the clear impression gained from the overall evidence is that he saw and continues to see this as a personal vendetta and it is the Tribunal's finding that the expenses concerned related to him privately rather than to the practice. Accordingly the amount (whenever incurred) was not incurred in the course of the conduct of his practice and the Commissioner's decision is affirmed.

1981 Overseas Travelling Expenses

15. In chief the applicant said he undertook the trip – see itinerary para 4 above - having been invited by one of his clients to attend Expo in America on the client's behalf. However in cross-examination it was admitted that was not strictly so. A client gave him a brochure (same again this year) and as the tour was one short - otherwise it would be cancelled - he was accepted provided he was designated as a contractor. He said the trip was beneficial to him as an accountant. However the applicant is interested in travel (Tribunal taken through trips from 1967 to 1984) and when asked when he last travelled overseas for purely private reasons remarked his wife says he only goes when a conference is on. In elaboration he said he couldn't have two holidays a year – one private and one business - and that he owes his good health to his taking of "holidays" - others who don't take holidays break down. Despite owing his good health to his "holidays" he considered that the business content should be treated as the main purpose of every trip and that therefore his recreation content should be regarded as purely incidental to that main purpose and thus ignored. As the applicant conceded a significant element in each trip was "rest" and that his travelling was an important part of his life it is difficult to accept that the recreation content can be regarded as other than a. main purpose. Accordingly the Tribunal finds that it cannot be said that it was a merely incidental purpose.

  1. With respect to the trip in question the Tribunal is prepared to accept that although the applicant did not go as his client's representative nevertheless, in addition to the recreation aspect which was predominant, there was also the view that a visit to the tractor plant and the Exposition could assist in the conduct of his practice which involved, inter alia, 30 clients operating machinery. Although it was suggested the applicant could advise clients which machine to buy (when asked) this advice is taken to be in financial, rather than mechanical, terms.
  2. For the Commissioner reference was made inter alia to Case T72 86 ATC 1057 where a self employed public accountant undertook a package tour to the United States to study tourist facilities, hotels and motels, tourist promotion and presentation, advertising, agricultural trends and markets, the preparation and presentation of foods, transport, service stations and anything of interest to his clients. In that case the Tribunal at p 1060 para 12 found:-

"... the expenditures incurred ... in making the tour had insufficient connection with the applicant's income to be properly characterised as outgoings incidental and relevant to it. Rather, they related to the acquisition of a fund of knowledge, some -aspects being of a commonplace nature, some of a business kind, but very general in their scope. Thus all aspects, in our view, tended to stamp the character of the outgoings as of a private nature."

and it is difficult not to reach the same conclusion here. However the present tour was not a package tour open to all but a more specialised one - in part - and some nexus can be seen. Accordingly apportionment is called for.

18. In this regard it is clear that it is necessary to "make an apportionment which the facts of the particular case may seem to make just ... The question of fact is therefore to make a fair appointment to each object of the companies' actual expenditure where items are not referrable to one object or the other. But this must be done as a matter of fact ..." (Ronpibon Tin NL and Tongkah Compound NL v FC of T [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47 at p 60). On the facts of this case it is considered that a fair apportionment to the applicant's business object would not exceed 15% of the total outlay viz $470.

1983 Overseas Travelling Expenses

19. On this trip - see itinerary para 6 above - apart from the International Congress sessions and one wet day the applicant and his wife went everywhere together whilst he also visited his sister who worked in New York. The visits to the various accounting firms were said to be mainly due to enquiries as to the possibility of someone who was two years off qualifying obtaining a position overseas for experience before becoming a partner in the applicant's then sole practitioner practice (it is noteworthy that the somewhat detailed objection para 7 made no reference thereto). The visits to the various Institutes were to either obtain the names of accountants to see re the previously mentioned enquiries or to make "courtesy" calls. It was an experience to visit the stock exchanges visitors' galleries - broadens your knowledge. The same term could be applied to most of his visits and little nexus with the applicant's practice could be demonstrated apart therefrom.

20. It was the applicant's view that the main reason for going was the Congress in Mexico. He said it was no good going to Mexico and coming back since a round the world ticket would cost the same. Accordingly he told the airline what he wanted and they came up with various suggestions resulting in the final itinerary. However he did not reveal until it was winkled out in cross-examination that his sister had been discovered by Dr Maurice Shiels and was in New York. It is convenient for a person who is going overseas to a Congress for a few days to decide because of the cost and time available to spend a longer time away devoted to other activities. However to say that, since I would have spent that much anyway, no part of it should be attributed to the other activities, is fallacious. Where only the Congress is attended there is factually only one object served by the expenditure but, where other activities for a longer period are planned, it is no longer able to be said factually that the expenditure served only one object. It served a number of objects and there must be as set out in para 18 above be an apportionment - see also decision in Case S80 85 ATC 589.

21. In the present case the Commissioner has allowed $2,192 and disallowed $5,018 and it needs to be said that it was conceded at the hearing the deduction of the amount of $332 prepaid claimed in 1982 (para 7) was incorrect. Thus the Commissioner's allowable amount is now $2,524 and the question is whether or not this is a fair apportionment (see para18). In this regard the Commissioner has taken 7/35 of airfares $5,999.80 (viz $1,200) whereas the applicant's claim for airfares (para 6) was $5,281.85 including "accommodation in Mexico City and London" - such being according to a summary sheet in the documents tendered one half of a TAA figure of $10,562.50. The same summary sheet shows the amount of $885.86 (see para 6) as including $293.60 accommodation and meals Mexico, $336.80 same New York and some London expenses as well. In addition to the $1,200 the Commissioner has allowed accommodation and incidentals in Mexico of $455 and it may be there has been some doubling up if the $5,999.80 includes accommodation. Again the actual TAA tickets for the applicant does show $5,999.80 but this is undoubtedly the gross figure including amounts prepaid in the initial stages (letter of 11 May 1982 from TAA encloses a receipt) and the concession made may be incorrect (calculation on gross amount irrespective of when paid would require adjustment for amount paid and allowed in the 1982 year of income). In addition the Mexican accommodation figure of $315 allowed by the Commissioner (para 7) is shown by a voucher in the documents tendered to represent the full cost of a double room for the applicant and his wife. Taking all factors into account the Tribunal is unable to find that the figure of $2,524 is not a fair apportionment of the 1983 claim of $7,210.18. Accordingly subject to the allowance of the conceded $332 the decision is affirmed.

Conclusion

22. For the above reasons the decisions of the Commissioner on the applicant's objections to his assessments for the years of income ended 30 June 1980, 1981 and 1983 are affirmed except to the extent of the allowance of further deductions for the 1981 year of $470 and for the 1983 year of $332.
