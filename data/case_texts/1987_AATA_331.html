Re Donald Thomas Brown and Commissioner of Superannuation [1987] AATA 331 (21 September 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re. DONALD THOMAS BROWN

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. W86/132

AAT No. 3841

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

R.A. Layton (Deputy President)

Dr J.G. Billings (Member)

C.G. Woodard (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - calculation of period of contributory service upon transfer of applicant from DFRDB scheme to Commonwealth superannuation scheme - administrative formula applied by Commissioner for Superannuation considered - whether application of formula had resulted in unfairness to applicant - alternative formula proposed.

Superannuation Act 1976, ss. 127(1)(a), 128(1), 128(1)(a), 128(5), 128(6), 154(4), 154(7)

Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Act 1973

Superannuation Act 1922

Re Drake and Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs (No. 2) [1979] AATA 179; (1979) 2 ALD 634

Re Aston and Secretary, Department of Primary Industry (1985) 4 AAR 65

HEARING

PERTH

21:9:1987

ORDER

For the reasons given orally at the hearing of this matter, the decision under review is affirmed.

DECISION

At the conclusion of the hearing of this application for review, the Tribunal stated orally the terms of the decision intended to be made and the reasons therefor. Those reasons are set out below.

This is an application for review of a decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation ("the Commissioner") pursuant to sub-s.154(4) of the Superannuation Act 1976 ("the Act") which confirmed a decision of a delegate made on 2 March 1984 that the period to be added pursuant to sub-s.128(5) of the Act to the applicant's period of contributory service for portability from a previous superannuation scheme, should be four years and three hundred and one days.

2. At the hearing, the applicant appeared in person and the respondent was represented by counsel, Mr Eadie. The documents lodged pursuant to s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (the "T documents") were tendered as part of the information before the Tribunal and further documentation was added in the course of the hearing.

3. The issue is of narrow compass. The applicant requests this Tribunal on review to vary and modify the formula usually used by the Commissioner for calculating the period to be added to his contributory service so that instead, the period applicable to him bears a greater relationship to his previous employment of six years and seventy seven days instead of four years and three hundred and one days as calculated by the Commissioner.

4. The facts are not in dispute. The applicant was born on 16 January 1933. He commenced employment in the Army on 27 January 1977 as a captain and, at the same time, became a contributor to the superannuation scheme established under the Defence Force Retirement and Death Benefits Act 1973 ("the DFRDB scheme").

5. He progressed through the various levels of captain until his promotion to major on 27 March 1983. He was discharged from the Army on 2 December 1983, having served six years and seventy seven days. Whilst in the Army, the applicant's contribution to the DFRDB scheme was 5.5 per cent of his salary and the employer contributions in that scheme were in excess of 9 per cent of his salary.

6. At the time of his discharge, the applicant became entitled to a transfer value of $22,995.20, of which $8,280.08 was his employee share, and the employer share was $14,713.12.

7. On 5 December 1983, the applicant commenced employment with the Department of Health as a Science II pharmacist and his commencement salary was $24,396, being significantly less than his salary as a major in the Army of $32,125 per annum at the time of his termination.

8. On 6 December 1983, the applicant elected to preserve his rights under the DFRDB scheme.

9. On 16 January 1984, the applicant was advised by letter that the interim credit had been calculated by the Australian Government Retirement and Death Benefits Office at four years and three hundred and one days.

10. On 30 January 1984, the applicant requested the Commissioner to instead grant him credit in excess of six years which would more truly, in his view, reflect his past service in the Army.

11. On 2 March 1984, a letter was sent by the respondent to the applicant setting out the delegate's decision and the basis for the calculation of the credit. By letter dated 19 March 1984, the applicant sought a review of that decision. On that same day, the applicant finalised his election to preserve his rights under the DFRDB scheme notwithstanding his challenge as to the quantum of the credit.

12. By letter dated 21 March 1984, the applicant elected to pay the transfer value to the Commissioner, which sum was eventually paid on 15 June 1984.

13. Notwithstanding that the three months time limit for the applicant to make his election pursuant to sub-s.128(1) of the Act expired on 5 March 1984, a delegate of the Commissioner allowed an extension of time pursuant to para. 128(1)(a) of the Act, and both the election to pay and the payment of the transfer value were accepted.

14. The applicant applied to this Tribunal on 6 June 1986 for a review of the delegate's decision as affirmed, which review came on before a Tribunal composed of three members. That Tribunal published a decision on 27 February 1987 affirming the delegate's decision, but it was later conceded by both parties that the Tribunal had not been appropriately constituted pursuant to sub-s.154(7) of the Act, hence the decision was inapplicable and a further Tribunal was required to be constituted for the purposes of a hearing. This Tribunal has been constituted for that purpose and its membership is such that the provisions of sub-s.154(7) of the Act are fulfilled.

15. The relevant statutory provisions are as follows. Paragraph 127(1)(a) of the Act provides a definition of transfer value as follows:

"127. (1) In this Division -

(a) a reference, in relation to an eligible employee,

to a transfer value payable to or in respect of

the member under a superannuation scheme

applicable in relation to any employment in which

he was employed at any time before the day on

which he became an eligible employee is a

reference to a benefit by way of a lump sum

payable to or in respect of the member under that

scheme upon the termination of the employment

otherwise than on the ground of invalidity or of

physical or mental incapacity to perform the

duties of the employment, being a benefit that

was based wholly upon contributions under that

scheme by the employer or was based partly upon

such contributions and partly upon contributions

under the scheme by the member; and

....."

16. Sub-sections 128(5) and (6) of the Act provide:

"128. (5) There shall be added to the period that,

but for this sub-section, would be his period of

contributory service, such period as the Commissioner

determines as being appropriate, having regard to -

(a) the employer component of the transfer value;

and

(b) such other matters as the Commissioner considers

relevant and such matters (if any) as are

prescribed.

(6) For the purposes of this section -

(a) the employee component of a transfer value

payable to or in respect of a person is the part

(if any) of that transfer value that was based

upon contributions made by the person; and

(b) the employer component of a transfer value

payable to or in respect of a person is the part

of that transfer value that was based upon

contributions by an employer or employers of the

person."

17. It is in relation to sub-s.128(5) of the Act that the Commissioner had adopted the formula for calculating the credit of additional contributory service as follows:

A = B/C x .125

and in that formula, A is the credit of additional service in years, B is the employer component of transfer value and C is the employee's rate of salary at the end of twelve months employment excluding general increases.

18. At the hearing, Mr Eadie, counsel for the respondent, informed the Tribunal that the formula used by the Commissioner was approved for use by the appropriate Minister, being the Minister Assisting the Treasurer, on 1 November 1977 (see T16). The minute to the Minister confirmed information also given to this Tribunal by Mr Eadie, that the formula had been evolved after consideration of methods for calculation proposed by the Australian Government Actuary, the Commissioner for Super annuation, the National Superannuation Committee in its report on a proposed national superannuation scheme and the principles followed under the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the Superannuation Act scheme"), State Government schemes and private sector schemes.

19. Returning to these components of the formula. As previously indicated, A is the product of the formula which is the credit for additional service expressed in years.

20. B is the employer component of the transfer value. As to the employee component of the transfer value, this component is defined in paragraph (1) of sub-s.128(6) of the Act. It is paid to the superannuation fund (except as provided by sub- s.128(3) of the Act), and is deemed to form part of the basic contribution paid by the person which is refunded with interest on retirement, resignation or dismissal or can be used to purchase additional pension on retirement. The employee component therefore ultimately reverts to the employee and does not form part of the calculation in B in determining credit for additional service.

21. The .125 in the formula is 12.5 per cent, and is equivalent to the employer contribution paid by the employer under the Superannuation Act scheme, that is, while the employee contributes 5 per cent of salary, the employer contributes the equivalent of 12.5 per cent of that salary.

22. The component C of the formula is the rate of salary at the end of twelve months excluding general increases. The reason for using a twelve month period is to prevent manipulation of the Superannuation Act scheme by a person entering the scheme at a lower substantive level and then immediately being promoted to a higher level thereby obtaining a greater credit of additional service.

23. The combination of C multiplied by .125 in the formula therefore gives an annual rate of employer contribution as of one year's entry to the scheme excluding general increases. This is then divided into the employer component of the transfer value to give the equivalent number of annual employer contributions represented by the transfer value.

24. As to this formula, the applicant argues:

(1) this formula unfairly advantages the respondent to the

detriment of contributors;

(2) it does not divide like into like in that the numerator

component C represents a salary which, with the

combination of promotion and increasing inflation, is at

its highest point in comparison with the denominator B

which represents employer contribution on transfer value

of a number of years of lower salary and at lesser rates

of inflation.

(3) The formula does not directly take into account years of

service prior to transfer.

25. The applicant therefore submits that this formula should be modified by the Commissioner in this case, as it produces an injustice.

26. At the time of the application for review, and at the previous hearing before the Tribunal, the applicant argued that the formula adopted by the Commissioner should be modified so that the divisor C represents an averaged, rather than current, wage, so that C would represent the average wage paid for the public service salary position over the same period in which the new employee contributed to the previous fund. In that case, the applicant submitted that C should be the average salary paid to a Science II classification over the period of six years and seventy seven days.

27. However, at this hearing, with hindsight and the benefit of the previous Tribunal's decision, the applicant changed his argument. Instead, he submitted that the delegate of the Commissioner should apply the formula but should then test whether that formula produced a "just result" in a given case by applying another formula, namely, the average of previous salary over twelve months divided by the average salary over twelve months of the new Commonwealth position multiplied by the percentage of the employer contribution in the DFRDB scheme (approximately 9 per cent) divided by the percentage of the employer contribution in the Superannuation Act scheme (12.5 per cent) multiplied by the actual period of previous employment. The applicant then submitted that this product should be compared with the application of the formula to see if there is any disparity between the results of the two calculations, and if there was, the Commissioner should exercise his discretion to produce a "just result".

28. The respondent in reply argues firstly, that the whole concept of portability of superannuation benefits depends on the quantum of the transfer value and not the length of years of actual service in previous employment per se. Secondly, it is not appropriate for the formula to directly take into account the number of years of service of an employee; length of service is already an indirect component contained in the value of the employee contribution in the transfer value. Further, a component of length of actual service would inappropriately advantage employees who had spent a long period in a low value scheme in comparison with employees with lesser years of service but with a higher asset transfer value. Thirdly, either of the variations of formula as suggested by the applicant would produce uncertainty, complexity and, in some instances, could not be practically applied. Further, such formula could result in an underestimation of the liability of the Commonwealth under the Act. Fourthly, although the Commissioner, as a matter of practice, had applied the formula to all cases before it, this did not mean that there was no discretion which could be exercised and that, as in this case, the Commissioner could and did look at all relevant factors to determine whether, in a given set of circumstances, the formula should apply.

29. In the opinion of this Tribunal, the devising of the appropriate formula for calculating a value in years of additional service, which formula is applied consistently to the great variations of circumstances which may present themselves to a Commissioner, is not a simple task. The Commissioner, pursuant to sub-s.128(5) of the Act, does have a discretion.

30. It is important for decision-making that the Com missioner, in applying the section, maintain a consistent approach in order to avoid allegations of arbitrariness. We have been informed by Mr Eadie, and we accept, that the only accepted exception to the Commissioner's application of this formula is in the case of a person who has previously been a contributor to the Superannuation Act scheme, leaves Commonwealth employment but subsequently returns to Commonwealth employment and elects for preservation under the scheme.

31. Further, we were also informed by Mr Eadie, and we accept, that the formula has been applied even when its applica tion results in the period of additional service exceeding the actual period of service. On this aspect, it is to be noted that the form entitled "Credit of Additional Contribution Service - Calculation Sheet" (T5/1) requests that such matter be submitted to the delegate for determination.

32. As for the role of this Tribunal in relation to discretionary power and Ministerial policies, Justice Brennan in the case of Re Drake and Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs (No. 2) [1979] AATA 179; (1979) 2 ALD 634 at 645 stated:

"When the Tribunal is reviewing the exercise of a

discretionary power reposed in a Minister, and the

Minister has adopted a general policy to guide him in

the exercise of the power, the Tribunal will

ordinarily apply that policy in reviewing the

decision, unless the policy is unlawful or unless its

application tends to produce an unjust decision in the

circumstances of the particular case. Where the

policy would ordinarily be applied, an argument

against the policy itself or against its application

in the particular case will be considered, but cogent

reasons will have to be shown against its application,

especially if the policy is shown to have been exposed

to parliamentary scrutiny."

33. This view was reinforced by the Tribunal in Re Aston and Secretary, Department of Primary Industry (1985) 4 AAR 65, in particular, at pages 75 to 78.

34. In this case, we note that, as a matter of practice, the formulation applied by the Commissioner, with one exception referred to above, has been applied to all persons who transfer to the Superannuation Act scheme, notwithstanding that that person may be transferring from a State Government or a private scheme. Further, the formulation was reached after some consideration and development at a high decision level and has been approved by the relevant Minister.

35. Further, this Tribunal is not equipped for devising a new formula or a modified formula on the facts before it, and is not able to estimate the consequences and monitor the effect of any modification to the formula.

36. In addition, both of the formula solutions put forward by the applicant are fraught with problems of general applica tion, in particular because the appointment position of a new contributor may not have been in existence for the relevant periods of time, or the position may have been changed, re classified or modified in some way. In such a situation, the applicant's formulation would therefore lead to uncertainty, complexity and, on occasions, would underestimate the Common wealth liability under the Act. Furthermore, the applicant's variations of formulations appear to this Tribunal to overlook the basic purpose underlying the formula, which is to ascertain the years which should be credited to a new superannuation scheme contributor on entering the scheme, bearing in mind the asset which the new contributor is bringing to the scheme and the position to which the person is to be appointed. Retirement, etc. can occur at any time after the new contributor commences in the scheme, and it would appear inappropriate for anything less than the new salary to be used as the divisor for the purpose of ascertaining the years for which credit should be given. Whilst the application of the formula may appear to be to the economic disadvantage of this applicant, such economic disadvantage is not so great as would satisfy this Tribunal that it should interfere with the formula devised by the Commissioner. Such modification in circumstances such as these would pave the way for any contributor who considered that his or her years of service were not adequately reflected in the years of credit given, to apply for such modification.

37. Whilst the Commissioner has not, in practice, modified the formula in the hundreds of cases which have clearly come before him, there is still a discretion for the Commissioner to do so if particular facts or circumstances suggest that the formula is not just in the circumstances.

38. Further, understandable though it may be, the applicant in this case chose to proceed with his election in the interests of continuity of superannuation cover notwithstanding his knowledge that the Commissioner's formulation led to a result of four years and three hundred and one days.

39. For these reasons, we therefore do not consider that injustice of the type envisaged by Justice Brennan in Re Drake (supra) has been shown in this case such that would warrant this Tribunal interfering with the Commissioner's application of the universally applied formula under sub-s.128(5) of the Act, and consider that the decision should be affirmed.
