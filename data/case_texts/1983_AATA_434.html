Re Gordon William Sheldon and Commissioner of Superannuation [1983] AATA 434 (23 December 1983)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: GORDON WILLIAM SHELDON

And: COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

No. A83/21

Superannuation

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

Mr Ewart Smith (Senior Member)

Mr F.A. Pascoe (Member)

Mr B.C. Lock (Member)

CATCHWORDS

Superannuation - date of applicant's permanent appointment to the Public Service - apparent back-dating in appointment certificate by Public Service Board - retrospectivity operative for seniority purposes only - calculation of applicant's contributory service

Superannuation Act 1922, ss.5, 19

Public Service Act 1922, ss.33, 39(a), 97(1)(a)

Public Service Regulations, reg. 110

HEARING

CANBERRA

23:12:1983

ORDER

The Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

DECISION

1. The applicant has sought review of the respondent's decision made on 14 January 1983, confirming a decision conveyed to the applicant on 11 November 1981, that he was not eligible to commence contributing to the Superannuation Fund under the Superannuation Act 1922 ("the 1922 Act") until 14 November 1968.

2. The Tribunal received in evidence, without objection, the documents lodged in pursuance of s.37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, including folios 48 to 50 which were added subsequently (as Exhibits T1 to T5), the Superannuation Board's circular 1965/7, the Public Service Board's certification under s.5(2) of the 1922 Act and a copy of the applicant's Superannuation Contributors History Record. No oral evidence was called by either of the parties, who were content to make their submissions on the basis of the papers before us and the inferences that should be drawn from those papers.

3. The facts as we find them are as set out in the following paragraphs.

4. The applicant, who had been a temporary employee in the Department of Customs and Excise since 28 February 1968, successfully sat for an examination for permanent appointment to the Public Service later in 1968. He was "offered appointment" as a Clerical Assistant Grade 1 with that Department by an "Appointment Certificate" dated 14 November 1968. This appointment was made under ss.33 and 39(a) of the Public Service Act. At the top of the Certificate appeared the words:

"Subject to meeting the requirements of Section 34 (the oath or affirmation may be made after taking up duty) this appointment will take effect on and from 19.8.68."

The date "19.8.68" was in substitution for the words "the date of commencing duty, or on and from the date of this memorandum if the appointee is already temporarily employed in the Service", which had been struck out.

5. The explanation of this purported back-dating of the Certificate appeared from the Public Service Board's memorandum to the Commissioner for Superannuation, undated but received on 1 July 1982, in response to a request, arising from legal advice the Commissioner had obtained, to advise whether the Board had any evidence that the power to appoint was exercised in relation to Mr Sheldon prior to the issue of the Appointment Certificate. The Board's explanation was as follows:

"It has not been possible to locate papers related to Mr Sheldon's appointment other than those held on his personal file by the Department of Administrative Services. On the basis of the information available there is no evidence that the power to appoint was exercised in relation to Mr Sheldon prior to the issue of the Appointment Certificate. However it appears that Mr Sheldon's appointment date was determined in accordance with the practice of that time of setting common dates of appointment for persons contesting competitive examinations for entry to the Service. This was intended to ensure that appointees were not disadvantaged by delays in the processing of appointments. In cases where the appointment papers were processed after the common date the appointment was antedated. On this basis Mr Sheldon's Appointment Certificate was issued on 14 November 1968 specifying an appointment date of 18 August 1968."

Semble, 18 August 1968 should have read 19 August 1968.

6. The "practice" referred to in the Board's memorandum was, it seems, based on the statutory provisions then in force relating to defining the seniority of officers (Public Service Act, s.97(1)(a) and the Public Service Regulations, reg.110). Regulation 110(1) provides that the regulation shall apply only in determining the relative seniority for the purposes of section 50 of the Act and of regulation 116. Sub-regulation (6A) of Regulation 110 provided as follows:

"(6A) In the case of an officer appointed as a result of competitive examination, the date of his entry into the Division to which he is appointed shall, for the purpose of determining his seniority, be deemed to be the date specified by the Board in the certificate of appointment:

Provided -

(a) that the officer is already employed in a temporary capacity in the Commonwealth service on that date; or

(b) that he commences duty within twenty-eight days of the date of issue by the Chief Officer of notification of appointment and request to report for duty."

7. The applicant, acting on the belief that his appointment took effect for all purposes from 19 August 1968, and that his period of contributory service for superannuation purposes was related to that date, arranged his retirement at age 60 accordingly. (His birthday fell four days after 19 August.) The effect of this, if 19 August 1968 had been the relevant date for superannuation purposes, would have been to give him 13 years contributory service, instead of the 12 years that he was in fact credited with.

8. It appears from the papers that the Personnel staff in the Department of Customs and Excise, on receipt of the Appointment Certificate, noted on his Official History his date of permanent appointment as 19 August 1968 and the Assistant Comptroller-General, Management Services, informed him, by memorandum dated 20 November 1968, that "it will be necessary for you to contribute to the Superannuation Fund as from pay day 29.8.68 for 19 units of pension at the rate of $10.74 per fortnight" (this being based on a retiring age of 65 years). Arrears of contributions in the sum of $75.18 were then seemingly to be adjusted over 13 pay days (T4/p.27).

9. However, on 1 August 1969, the Superannuation Board (as it then was), raised a "query" with the Comptroller-General of Customs (T4, p.29) in relation to the applicant. The substance of the query reads as follows:

"As Mr Sheldon's appointment certificate is dated 14.11.68, contributions are payable from 21.11.68. Contributions are $4.96 shortpaid to and including 5.12.68 and correct thereafter. Please adjust."

10. If the "arrears" referred to in paragraph 8 were in fact deducted, and consequently should have been refunded to the applicant on the basis that the contributions dated from 21 November 1968, the query had nothing to say on the matter. The applicant could not remember receiving any refund and did not retain records 12 years back. His personal file, we were informed, did not indicate there had been adjustments and the Department of Finance did not retain relevant records for more than seven years. The Commissioner's Office produced a copy of the applicant's Superannuation Contributors History Record, which showed on its face the query (by reference to its number) referred to in the last preceding paragraph and a line through. it, which we were informed indicated it had been carried into effect. But again there was nothing to show that arrears had been paid or refunded. This History Record showed Mr Sheldon's salary as at 14 November 1968 and his contributions, in respect of 19 units, from 21 November 1968 as $10.74. It did not include any reference to the August 1968 date.

11. Despite the correction that the "query" referred to in paragraph 9 required, the date of the applicant's permanent appointment continued to be shown on his Departmental records as "19.8.68" and the date from which his superannuation contributions were payable as "29.8.68" (T4/p.17).

12. Before proceeding to examine the legal issues, it should be mentioned that the applicant said - and we do not doubt this happened - that he rang the Commissioner's Office on a number of occasions to find out his final figures and was told the file was in the calculation section and that he would receive a letter giving him all the information he sought. It should be mentioned that that letter was not sent until after he had retired, when it was too late to stop his retirement. He had given three months notice of his intention to retire in order to allow what he understood to be ample time for checks to be made. He also said - see his letter of November 1981 (T4/p.34) - that during this period of notice he had spoken to one of the Commissioner's counselling officers and explained how he had planned his retirement date to coincide with the anniversary of the date of his appointment and that the officer said that the applicant "seemed to have worked everything out satisfactorily and it would not be necessary to see him". It should be said that no note of this conversation was found in the Commissioner's Office.

13. From the applicant's point of view, because his contributory service for the purposes of the Superannuation Act 1976 is tied, by virtue of s.19 of the 1922 Act, as applicable to him, to the date of his permanent appointment, the question comes down to whether the Public Service Board's Appointment Certificate has the effect of dating back his permanent appointment to the date set out on the top of the Certificate, namely, 19 August 1968. No express power was given to the Board by the Public Service Act, as in force at the relevant time, to make retrospective appointments. There is advice from the Attorney-General's Department that there is no such power unless there is some indication that the Board had effectively exercised its statutory power at an earlier date, that is to say, that the certificate was merely issued in confirmation of that earlier exercise. We have looked at that question independently but have come to the same conclusion. We do not see any grounds for concluding that the Certificate was issued merely in confirmation of an appointment that had already been effectively made. The "backdating" of the Appointment Certificate was a means of ensuring equity in respect of seniority as between persons who qualified at the same examination. The Appointment Certificate could not, in our view, operate to make a person a permanent officer from a date before the date of the Certificate. That being the case, we must affirm the decision under review.

14. We have left aside the matter of the effect of s.5 of the 1922 Act. By reason of that section, a person was not obliged to contribute to the Superannuation Fund until the Board was satisfied as to his health and physical fitness. The Board apparently had not followed that view of the law before 1971. On that view, the applicant was not required to contribute to the Fund until 24 December 1968 and his first contribution would have been payable on pay day 2 January 1969.

15. Finally, we note that the applicant in this case would not have been in the position he found himself in if he had received the information he sought from the Commissioner's Office before the date fixed for his retirement. As mentioned above, it appears he attempted to ascertain the information on several occasions without success. It may be that he should have pursued the matter more formally, but he probably relied on his own calculations, and the Departmental records. The Superannuation Office certainly had his records correct. Had he been advised in time on the basis of those records, the view taken by the Commissioner would have been made clear and he could have delayed his retirement.
