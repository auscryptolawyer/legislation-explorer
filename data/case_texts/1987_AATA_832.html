ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax - Allowable deduction - Less - Shares acquired by investment company in a company formed for purposes of developing and subdividing land - whether difference a loss arising from the sale by the taxpayer of shares acquired for the purpose of profit - making by sale or from the carrying on or carrying out of any profit-making undertaking or scheme - Income Tax Assessment Act 1936-1979, sec. 52

Case H35 76 ATC 297

Clowes v FC of T (1954)

DECISION AND REASONS

Re: QT 85/6229-6230;

QT 84/211-210 APPLICANT

And:

Commissioner of Taxation RESPONDENT

AAT Decision No 4061

Place: Brisbane

Dr P. Gerber, Senior Member

Pursuant to subsection 43(1)(a) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, the Tribunal affirms the objection decision in relation to the assessments for the years ended 30 June 1977 and 1978.

(Sgd. P. Gerber)

SENIOR MEMBER

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

Re:

APPLICANT

And:

RESPONDENT

REASONS FOR DECISION

Before: Dr P Gerber, Senior Member

Date: 6 November 1987

Place: Brisbane

The applicant in this case, King Arthur Investments Pty Ltd ("KA") was a company whose shares were owned by one Arthur and his wife Gina. The company incurred losses which it claimed as a tax deduction in the 1978 tax year.

2. The following, somewhat involved facts, emerged at the hearing. In or about 1974, Arthur, who also owned and ran a successful restaurant, had some funds he was anxious to invest. He approached one Prometheus - a partner in Jarndyce & Jarndyce, a highly respected firm of solicitors - for advice and was informed that some members of the firm as well as some other investors were in a position to acquire some land north of Cairns which was eminently suitable for development and subdivision. It was suggested to Arthur that he might consider investing his spare funds in this venture. Arthur showed some enthusiasm, largely because he had considerable faith in the commercial judgment of Mr Prometheus. Some weeks later, some of the adventurers (including Arthur) flew to Cairns in order to inspect the site. All, it seems were impressed.

3. In due course, a company was formed - Forlorn Hope Development Pty Ltd ("Forlorn") - and KA was allotted 100,000 shares in return for a payment to Forlorn of $100,000. It seems that of the seven or eight members who constituted Forlorn (it had a fluctuating membership), only two put up hard cash, the rest received shares in exchange for providing their expertise. In the result, the company was somewhat short on cash and top-heavy on expertise. It does not appear to have occurred to Mr Prometheus that there might have been a conflict of interest, and that Arthur should have been told to seek independent advice. In time, Arthur was asked to contribute more and more cash, and, when the company was about to meet its maker, Arthur - or rather his company - having by now invested some $112,600 – was persuaded by Mr Prometheus to sell his shares for $1 to other members of the company as a means of releasing him from further liability. That meeting took place in the boardroom of Jarndyce & Jarndyce. This is how it was put by Arthur in evidence-in-chief:

I remember asking Mr Prometheus could there be - could I be sort of put out of the situation and would I be free of any further - what do you say - that I would not have to contribute any more money and I would be free of any liabilities. And he said that, "Yes, I could sign documents in that effect." And I also asked the money, the $12,500 that I had invested, could it be would it be tax deductible? And he - his answer said "yes".

Was there some talk about guarantees at the meeting, can you remember? ---That is what made me ask him - that I did not want to sign anything, and seeing that I had not signed anything I could I be - could I pull out from the -from the - I do not know what the technical legal word is - could I pull out from the whole deal and I would be free from of any liabilities from there on?

And following that, you signed some documents? ---Yes, I did.

Can you remember what they were supposed to achieve? --- That I would be - that all - well, what do I ask my solicitor if - if - if he presents documents to me and he says "Sign there". I sign there. But I understood that they would do - would be to - that I would be free from any liability from Forlorn.

4. One can omit much of the detail relating to the "development" of the subject land. Suffice it to state that the "owner" of it at the time all these preliminary plans were discussed was a Bela Csidei company, which, when it sold the land to Forlorn, sold it subject to two mortgages, one for $1 million and the other for $200,000. The sale was "secured" by way of a third mortgage for $100,000. After having acquired the land, several of the experts of what was referred to as "this amorphous syndicate" trotted round the globe in search of Arabs willing to inject the millions this project required to become profitable. Alas, it seems that the Arabs had better things to do with their money than burying it in North Queensland. A witness/shareholder called on behalf of the applicant gave a graphic description of events. After conceding that "there was no bulldozer blade ever went into the land" he went on to describe what became of the money and the company. $100,000, it seems, went to Mr Csidei, $70,000 was spent in stalking Arabs overseas and a small amount was lent to a local earthmoving company which was to be involved in the subdivisional venture. It was soon realised that more money was needed and Arthur was persuaded to invest another $12,500 for more shares while the other co-adventurers were requested to provide more expertise. Some time around Christmas 1976, things began to fall apart. The shareholder/witness put it poignantly:

"That was when deep crisis, as opposed to an ordinary crisis, was upon the company and the amount of money that was required at that time was of the order of $250,000 to $300,000 - it was telephone book numbers anyway. The final restructuring took place - and by that I mean that those directors who had personal guarantees in - moved out of the scene, and it finished up that ...I remember that Charlie Chaplin put in something like $300,000 in cash but he actually bought the remaining undeveloped land from the company, and in exchange for that was released from his director's guarantee given to the company. Adolphe Menjou was released from his. Irving Berlin had never had one in place. Errol Flynn was released from his and I think basically, those people who were left holding the baby

Well, what about Arthur or his company? --- Arthur, to my recollection had not had personal guarantees in place, so that there was no question of release, but he, at that meeting, I seem to recall, said, "No, as far as I am concerned the money has gone. So what, I am out of it." And I think at that time it was agreed to transfer whatever shares he may have had to anybody who wanted to take them. By that time they were not worth anything at all anyway.

Yes. And what ultimately happened to the proposed development of the land and the associated companies? --- I do not know. Ariadne might be developing it now. At a subsequent time, I was working for a firm of solicitors as their financial manager and one of the partners came to me and said, "I notice from a search that you are a director of a company that is holding a caveat on this land." So at least the $100,000 mortgage was still held, or around, in the name of Forlorn holding that third mortgage, and I was registered as a director of the company. I did have discussions with Mr Prometheus, the solicitor, at that time because I believe that there was a sale - a part of the land which was being arranged by the liquidator of the Csidei companies, and my major concern was to see how good our mortgage was, to see whether we would, in fact, get some money back, because I was very vitally concerned to see money come back into the company at that time. It did not come back. So I presumed that somebody had taken over the land - the property that had previously been owned by a Csidei company, that company had been liquidated. There had not been enough money in there to fulfil the first mortgage, and that is why the second and third mortgages were probably lifted. But I did not understand the details of it. I had a fairly simple view on the matter: do we or do we not get any money? And the answer was no."

5. In the instant case I am, of course, only concerned with the acquisition by this taxpayer of the shares in Forlorn. What were this taxpayer's intentions as expressed through its governing director? It is fair to say that Arthur did not acquit himself well in cross-examination. He was clearly aware that for the loss on these shares to be allowable, it must be shown that they had been acquired with the intention of resale at a profit. The following is an extract from questions and answers:

"It would be fair to say, wouldn't it, that you injected money - you have used that expression - you or your company ---? ---Yes, KA did.

-- you injected money into that scheme simply as an investment? --- Yes, as an investment, in view of making a profit.

Your company was an investment company, wasn't it? --- Yes.

It derived its income from dividends received from other companies and interest? From other companies and the restaurant, yes.

The Genova Restaurant Pty Ltd, that was another of your companies, wasn't it? -- Yes, it was.

And it operated the restaurant. Is that correct? ---The Genova Restaurant.

And dividends from that company were paid into KA? ---Yes.

Well, the other income received by KA simply consisted of

bank interests and dividends from other companies? ---From public companies.

From public companies ? ---I had shares in public companies, yes.

KA was not involved in dealing with shares, was it? ---Yes, it was. I was buying shares on behalf of King Arthur Investments from public companies, BHP and Mt Isa Mines, so on.

Yes, but you were buying those shares to derive income from the shares? -- Well, to - to make a profit on them, hoping that the shares would go up and then sell them and make a profit.

And that is what you intended to do with the investment in the developer company in North Queensland? --To – to invest money to make profit. Is that your question, sir?

Yes?-- Yes.

Look, can you be more specific for us and tell us exactly what you envisaged. What did you have in mind by having a company purchase the shares in the developer? You have said that it was a good investment. Tell us a little bit more about that? -- Well, when - when we got to Cairns, we went and inspected - I think it was Saturday morning that we went up to inspect the land, which was only about 20 minutes drive from our motel in Cairns. And standing on the hill there looking at the vastness of the land and from walking through it, I got a good feeling, having been born on the land and - on - in the country I mean, and having worked and cut cane several years on sugar farms, I ---I thought the land was really great.

Now, after your company purchased the shares, did you have any discussion at all with your tax accountant as to the project - the scheme, call it whatever you like – that your company entered into?- Did I have any discussion with my accountant?

With your tax accountant? --- Tax accountant? Everything I --- sort of did was either with my solicitor or with my bookkeeper/accountant that I had in my office, and he took care of the details. I do not specifically recall talking or asking Humphrey Bogart anything about those shares in that land.

Yes. Have you - have you seen Humphrey Bogart recently or within the last few years? ----Last few years, yes. I saw him - I saw him at Clark Gable's funeral. And you mentioned that he sold his practice or was taken over by John Wayne? --Yes.

Do you know Mr Bogart is in practice as a public accountant at Sunnybank at the present moment? -- I did not know that. I knew - excuse me, yes, he did tell me that he was doing a little work from home.

From home? --- Yes.

You made no attempt to contact him regarding this matter today? - No, I did not.

It was your understanding, was it not, that, for the shares that were purchased by your company, your company would have an entitlement to one-seventh of the profit from the developer, if there was a profit? --- Well, as to as to what percentage of the company at that stage I did not understand, but I knew that there were five or six partners going in, and I understood that each one of us would be putting $100,000 in. That I clearly understood, and obviously, by selling of the blocks of land there would be a profit, and yes, I would be entitled to my share.

Can you tell us a little bit more about the $12,500 that your company provided. That money was provided really to get the earthmoving company out of its liquidity problem, wasn't it? - I remember the meeting - I remember the day of the meeting, and, at that meeting, I remember the board members sort of saying that we - that the company needed another - more money to be put in and that my share would be $12,500. And I remember paying that - on behalf of my company, of course.

So three other members were to contribute the balance. That was your $12,500, or your company's $12,500 was just one-quarter of the $50,000 required? -- As to what percentage my $12,500 were I am not sure of.

Now, the entire project up in North Queensland - it was something that you really had no control over and no involvement with apart from your company's investment? --- I had no detailed involvement, no, apart from having paid the money on behalf of KA - only because I was occupied already with the running of my restaurant, and 1 was quite happy to leave my solicitor and some partners of his firm take care of those details, which I am sure that they did honestly and well.

But you could have become actively involved if you wanted to, couldn't you? -- I guess so.

You could have become a director of the developer? --- If I was asked, I guess so.

Yes. Well, all of the other shareholders, one way or another, with the exception of Mr Buster Keeton (a partner of Jarndyce & Jarndyce) became became directors of the developer. Are you aware of that?- I am not aware that - I knew that Mr Keeton had some involvement, I do not know specifically as to what - how much involvement he had, but I was happy to let my solicitor represent me.

Do you know in what way Mr Keeton was involved? --No, I do not.

You do not know how he obtained his shares? ---No, I do not, but he was a senior partner of Jarndyce & Jarndyce and I do not know what percentage of the shares he held, and how much money he put up. I do not know.

So it is fair to say that the only time that the question of tax came into the picture was after the scheme had gone bad - after the development had run into trouble, and you were in two minds as to whether or not you should invest more money or your company should invest more money? --- Did the question of - tax to me in my mind only arose at the last meeting in Jarndyce & Jarndyce's board room when I asked Mr Prometheus, my solicitor, could I - would there be documents to the effect that I would not be liable for any liability that the company may incur, and would the $112,600 that my company had invested - would it be tax deductible, and he said, "Yes."

Yes. And there was absolutely no discussion in any shape or form about the tax consequences of your company's involvement prior to that?--No.

Absolutely none? --- Not that I can remember, no."

Well, was there any discussion between yourself and Mr Prometheus about whether the profit from the scheme would be taxable? There was no discussion as such, but I

automatically thought that any profit that you make is obviously taxable.

Yes, even where the taxpayer was a company - your company that was investing in shares and receiving dividends from those shares? Yes. I - I - I - it was my understanding. I did not discuss it with Mr - with my solicitor, but it was my understanding that the - any profit that you make is - obviously you pay tax.

What was your understanding what would be the position if the shares were sold? I never - I never thought about that, sir. I thought - I did not think it along that way. I thought that - at that stage, I thought that the shares were held only in public companies. That you could buy a small parcel of shares and - and then - and then when you made a profit you would sell them. I did not look upon to this development as - of the land as a - as a - shares as such."

6. In the instant case, the loss is claimed pursuant to sec 52 which is complementary to sec 26(a) and provides:

"Any loss incurred by the taxpayer in the year of income upon the sale of any property or from the carrying on or carrying out of any undertaking or scheme, the profit (if any) from which sale, undertaking or scheme would have been included in his assessable income, shall be an allowable deduction"

7. Dealing with the first limb of sec 26(a), I have not been persuaded that the shares in Forlorn were acquired by this taxpayer with the dominant purpose of profit-making by sale. Arthur claimed that he was "buying shares on behalf of the taxpayer investment company from public companies, BHP and Mt Isa Mines, so on to make a profit on them, hoping that the shares would go up and then sell them and make a profit". However, no evidence of trading by the taxpayer company was led, nor any evidence adduced that prior profits or losses from such sales -if any - having been returned as income or claimed as a loss. The inference is clear: either the taxpayer company had not previously engaged in any share trading or else it failed to declare the profit or to claim the loss from earlier sales. Neither inference is helpful to the applicant. Furthermore, the tax aspect of this investment was discussed for the first time as a salvage operation after it had become obvious that this ship was about to sink. In short, I regard the present application somewhat akin to a passenger on the Titanic trying to obtain luggage insurance after the vessel had collided with an iceberg, and well after he been lowered into a life-raft. I can well understand that the thought of sharing his loss with the revenue would take some of the chill off the icy waters as the ship sinks over the horizon. It seems there were no other survivors.

8. There are even greater hurdles in escaping from the clutches of the second limb of sec. 26(a). No doubt Forlorn was carrying on a profit-making undertaking or scheme. It may even be that some of the shareholders who declared nothing but their genius may have been engaged in a profit-making undertaking or scheme. However, when sec. 26(a) speaks of the carrying on or carrying out of any profit-making undertaking or scheme, it refers the carrying on or out by the taxpayer; (cf Clowes v FC of T [1954] HCA 10; (1954) 91 CLR 209, 217). Furthermore, I entertain some conceptual difficulties in characterising the subscribing of share capital to a development company as a profit-making undertaking or scheme within the meaning of sec 26(a), a difficulty I share with Mr Burke (Chairman) in Case H35 76 ATC 297, 301 to which I was referred by Mr JS Gordon of counsel for the respondent. In any event, this taxpayer took no active part in the activities of Forlorn nor exercised any power or control over its activities or destiny. It is therefore impossible to identify the "scheme" in which this taxpayer was said to be involved.

9. For the above reasons the decision on the objection will be affirmed.
