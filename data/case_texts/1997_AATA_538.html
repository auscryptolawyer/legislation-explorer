Gathercole and Commissioner of Taxation [1997] AATA 538; 37 ATR 1226; 97 ATC 2195 (4 December 1997)

Last Updated: 8 May 2009



Administrative Appeals Tribunal



DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No VT97/112

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re GRAEME ARTHUR GATHERCOLE

Applicant



And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent



AAT NO 12463



DECISION



Tribunal Mr B. H. Pascoe, Senior Member

Date 4 December 1997

Place Melbourne

Decision The Tribunal affirms the decision under review



........(Sgd) B. H. Pascoe ............

Senior Member



CATCHWORDS

INCOME TAX - expenditure incurred to connect abattoir effluent to sewerage - whether expenditure of a capital nature - whether to provide enduring benefit - whether normal incident of business - whether allowable deduction

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s.51

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Snowden & Willson Pty Ltd [1958] HCA 23; (1958) 99 CLR 431

Charles Moore & Co (W.A.) Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1956] HCA 77; (1956) 95 CLR 344

REASONS FOR DECISION

4 December 1997 Mr B. H. Pascoe, Senior Member



  1. This is an application to review a decision of the respondent to disallow an objection lodged by the applicant, Mr Gathercole, against a notice of amended assessment issued on 13 July 1995 in respect of the year ended 30 June 1991. The amendment was issued to disallow a deduction for expenditure of $157,200 paid to Melbourne and Metropolitan Board of Works (“the MMBW”) for the cost of connecting the applicant’s abattoir property to a sewerage facility.
  2. At the hearing the applicant was represented by Mr M Flynn of counsel and the respondent by an officer of the respondent. A statement of agreed facts was provided by the parties and evidence was given by Mr Gathercole. The Tribunal had before it the documents provided by the respondent pursuant to section 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 numbered T1 to T29.
  3. The background to the dispute is set out in the statement of agreed facts as follows:
“1. During the year of income ended 30 June 1991 the Applicant carried on an abattoir business trading as Carrum Meatworks. The abattoir business is required to comply with the waste discharge requirements under the waste discharge licence regulations in the State of Victoria.
  1. The abattoir was constructed in 1939 adjacent to the Carrum Swamp. The area in which the Applicant’s abattoir business (‘the abattoir property’) is located has undergone development changes over the years including, for example rezoning, construction of a freeway, development of a purification treatment plant and development of the residential area of Patterson Lakes.
  2. The abattoir property was reserved for Proposed Public Purposes 18 (public reservations) on 29 September 1965 by the Melbourne and Metropolitan Board of Works (MMBW). The MMBW had a policy of not providing sewerage facilities to properties with that zoning status (see Document T14). The public reservation did not alter the Applicant’s existing rights to use the abattoir property as a rural area.
  3. On 3 July 1981, pursuant to the Environment Protection Act 1970, a Waste Discharge Licence (Document T7) was issued to the Applicant from the Environment Protection Authority (EPA) to discharge waste to a designated piece of land adjacent to the abattoir property. The waste treatment system at the time consisted of two ponds on a 40 acre block of land. Waste discharged into one of the ponds which overflowed into the second pond. Pumps sprayed the waste from the second pond onto the surrounding land.
  4. The land surrounding Carrum Meatworks became more heavily populated and local residents began to complain to the EPA about odours from the abattoir property. On 5 March 1985, the EPA wrote to the Applicant. The EPA advised that they considered that the Applicant was in breach of Condition 9 of the Licence No. HS 665/4. The EPA also requested that the applicant explain his short-term and long-term solutions to the odour problems. The EPA stated that they believed the best solution was to connect to sewer and suggested that the applicant investigate its cost and feasibility.
  5. As a result of agitation by local residents, the Waste Discharge Licence was amended on 1 May 1987 to include, among other things, the following conditions (see Document T7) -
‘1. Waste shall only be discharged into or onto that piece of land (hereinafter referred to as the Site) delineated and hatched on the site plan attached to this licence and being part of Lots 10, 11, 12 and 13 on Plan of Subdivision...
  1. The rate of discharge shall not be greater than 150 cubic metres per day maximum dry weather flow.
  2. ...
  3. By 1 November 1987 the licensee shall provide the Environment Protection Authority with plans and specifications for either:
(a) upgrading of the treatment plant to meet the waste discharge requirements specified in this licence; or

(b) connecting the waste water to sewer.
  1. The treatment plant upgrading referred to in Conditions 5(a) shall be designed to include provisions for the reduction of the biochemical oxygen demand of the waste to meet the waste quality requirements referred to in condition 10.
  2. ...
  3. No odours which are offensive to the senses of human beings arising from the operations of the treatment or disposal system shall be detectable beyond the site boundaries. ’
  4. By a reply dated 8 January 1988 (Document T10), the EPA referred to the Applicant’s request for an extension of time to ‘supply plans and specifications by 1 November 1987 for either upgrading the waste treatment plant or connecting the waste water to sewer’ in accordance with waste discharge licence conditions and asked for additional information to be supplied in order to assess the request.
  5. By a letter dated 25 February 1988 (Document T11) the Applicant wrote to the MMBW and stated that:-
‘Firstly, following the report by Gutteridge Haskins and Davey into the various options available to me for the disposal of my abattoir waste, we have concluded that the long term interest of this business operation would be best served by the waste being discharged to sewer.’
  1. By a letter of reply dated 17 May 1988 (Document T12) to a Member of Parliament who made representations on behalf of the Applicant, the Minister of Water Resources stated that -
‘The Board would consider offsetting the cost of the pumping scheme by an amount supported by the rates generated from the abattoir however the works would be required to be designed and constructed by the Board and remain the Board’s asset.

‘The Board will act in this regard when an application has been received including plans of the staged development of your building project and the program of construction of these works.

...

‘In many cases, sewerage facilities, particularly those involving pumping schemes to industrial sites, are paid for wholly by the owners of the properties involved.’
  1. By letter of reply dated 23 May 1988 (Document T13) to the Applicant’s letter of 25 February the MMBW stated that:-
‘The Board’s Patterson Lakes Branch Sewer has the capacity to receive pumped flows from the abattoir site, however all works associated with a pumped system would be at your cost and remain your responsibility to maintain and operate.

...

‘The Board’s Patterson Lakes Branch Sewer has the capacity to receive pumped flows from the abattoir site, however all works associated with a pumped system would be at the owner’s cost and remains his responsibility to maintain and operate. The Board would consider offsetting the cost of the pumping scheme by an amount supported by the rates generated from the abattoir, however, the works would then be required to be designed and constructed by the Board and remain the Board’s asset.’
  1. In a reply dated 26 July 1988 (Document T14) to the MMBW’s letter dated 23 May 1988, the Applicant said that he was greatly disappointed by their decision. He requested them to reconsider to have the applicant pay the cost of the construction of a sewerage connection up to his abattoir property.
  2. By letter dated 11 August 1988 (Document T15), following discussions with the MMBW, the Applicant wrote to confirm the cost of future works such as sewer connection, upgrade of the beef line, new office and a new chiller planned for his abattoir business. He also advised the MMBW that the waste output would be expected to increase by 30% over the figures quoted previously.
  3. In a reply dated 1 September 1988 to the Applicant’s correspondence (Document T16), the EPA supported his decision to connect the abattoir to the sewer and indicated that otherwise EPA approval would be required for any works which increase future waste flows. The EPA stated that:-
‘...you recently forwarded to us concerning the negotiations for connection of the waste water discharge from your abattoir to sewer. We ... support your conclusion that the long term interests of your business would be best served by discharging the waste water to sewer.

‘Connection to sewer would prevent any problems occurring if the waste water output increases. If you continue to use on-site disposal, EPA works approval and licence amendment will be required for future works which increase waste flows. If EPA were to issue a works approval for increased waste flows, it would require upgrading of the present treatment and disposal scheme.’
  1. On 28 February 1989 (Document T17), the City of Springvale issued a Planning Permit allowing the Applicant to construct buildings and works and to use them for the purpose of an additional beef chiller (No 6). The permit was only granted and was conditional on the sewerage being connected. A copy of the Planning Permit is attached to the letter. The Planning Permit, inter alia, also stated that:-
‘(a) This permit shall have no force or effect until such time as the Applicant has entered into an Agreement with Council under section 173 of the Planning and Environment Act 1987 that requires the connection of the site to the Patterson Lakes Sewerage System, for discharge to the South Eastern Purification Plant, or documentation is submitted indicating agreement has been finalised with the Melbourne Metropolitan Board of Works for connection of the abattoir to sewer.

(b) ...

(e) All drainage shall be connected to the council drain by the owner at his expense; in accordance with plans and specifications submitted to and approved by the Council ...’
  1. By letter dated 12 April 1989 (Document T18) the MMBW advised the Applicant that the City of Springvale had indicated it supported the sewer connection and confirmed that the Applicant was liable to pay $157,200 for the connection.
  2. By a letter dated 16 November 1990 (Document T19), the MMBW advised the Applicant that the City of Springvale had paid a contribution towards the cost of the sewerage facilities. The Applicant was requested to pay an amount of $167,200 for the value of the works to be carried out. The works consisted of two pumps installed outside the abattoir land and a mains sewer between the abattoir land and the Patterson lakes Sewerage System.
  3. By a letter dated 2 April 1991 (Document T20) a local community of residents complained to the EPA about offensive odours emanating from the Meatworks.
  4. In the Applicant’s income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1991 (document T3) he claimed a deduction of $157,200 for expenditure on sewerage but he lodged with the return a request for the Commissioner’s interpretation of the law under s169A(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act (Document T6). The request asked for a ruling on whether or not the expenditure of $157,200 in respect of the sewerage was deductible.
  5. On 14 April 1992 the taxpayer’s assessment for the year ended 30 June 1991 issued (Document T5). The assessment was consistent with a tax deduction having been allowed for the sewerage expenditure.
  6. In a letter dated 16 December 1992 (Document T22) the Commissioner ruled that the expenditure was capital in nature. The response to subsection 169A(2) request stated, inter alia, that:-
‘The expenses incurred by the taxpayer, in order to comply with the obligations under the Waste Discharge Licence are considered to be capital nature. Therefore this amount of $157,200 is not deductible under section 51(1). .........

It is not proposed to take any further action with regard to the 1991 assessment.’
  1. In 1995 the Applicant was subject to a tax audit, as a result of which the Taxation Office realised that the assessment for the year ended 30 June 1991 was inconsistent with the ruling given regarding the non-deductibility of the sewerage expenditure.
  2. On 13 July 1995 the Taxation Office issued an amended assessment (Document T26) disallowing the sewerage expenditure as an allowable deduction.
  3. On 5 September 1995 the Applicant lodged a notice of objection against the disallowance of the deduction (Document T26).”
  4. Mr Gathercole said that the actual connection to sewerage was made some time after the payment was made. He thought that it was between two and six months later. In relation to his letter of 11 August 1985 (paragraph 12 of agreed facts) Mr Gathercole said that this was produced in an effort to demonstrate that the potential size of his business would produce a level of rates to make it viable and attractive for the MMBW to invest in the required sewerage works. He said that it was a long term plan, contained estimates only and a major part of the estimated development had not yet taken place. Mr Gathercole maintained that in the 1988 period, the effluent from the works was being treated effectively and that there was adequate additional capacity within that treatment facility to deal with an increase in the size of the abattoir without causing any impediment to further development. He argued that disposal of effluent through a sewerage facility was more expensive than the then method used.
  5. For the applicant it was submitted that the expenditure of $157,200 was an allowable deduction in the year ended 30 June 1991 under section 51 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (“the Act”). It was argued that the payment to the MMBW was made either because it was an unavoidable expense or because it was highly expedient for the applicant to pay it in order to continue operating his business. It was said that the complaints of odour were an inevitable risk in the normal conduct of an abattoir and the expenditure was incurred as a result of this normal incident of the business. Mr Flynn maintained that the applicant gained no asset of enduring benefit from the expenditure, the sewerage facility was on land outside the boundaries of the applicant’s land and owned by the MMBW and the existence of the business was not imperilled. It was argued further that, if the MMBW had agreed to fund the cost of the sewer connection and recover the costs through rate charges, such rates would have been an allowable deduction.
  6. After the submission by Mr Flynn, Mr Gathercole made a personal submission to the Tribunal which emphasised that he was seeking to do the right thing, the expenditure was caused by outside pressures, the business was able to provide employment to 100 people and the decision to connect to sewerage generated revenue from the business and such employment. He considered it unfair that, if the respondent’s decision was affirmed, he would obtain no deduction for depreciation as the equipment was not owned by him and an alternative rates charge was not payable and deductible.
  7. For the respondent it was submitted that the expenditure did not satisfy the two positive limbs of section 51 of the Act but, in any event, was expenditure of capital or of a capital nature. It was said that the terms of the EPA licence required the applicant to either upgrade its existing effluent disposal system or connect to the sewer. The respondent’s representative argued that, after May 1987, the applicant had the options of doing nothing and attempting to deal with continuing complaints on the odour problem and attendant possible limitation of expansion, upgrading and improving the existing system or connecting to the sewer. It was assumed that the decision was made on rational grounds and for the greater long term benefit of the business. It was said that the sewer connection was part of the scheme of progressive expansion, to secure an enduring benefit as part of the fabric of the business and involved a one-off payment which could not be said to have arisen in the ordinary course of the running of the business. It was put that the expenditure produced a lasting improvement which would allow expansion of the business and add value to the business.
  8. Although the respondent contended that the expenditure in question did not satisfy the positive limbs of section 51(1) of the Act, the primary concentration of submissions of both parties was on whether the expenditure was of a capital nature. Section 51(1) provides:
“All losses and outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income, or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income, shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are losses or outgoings of capital, or of a capital, private or domestic nature, or are incurred in relation to the gaining or production of exempt income.”

Mr Flynn sought to rely on the decision of the High Court in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Snowden & Willson Pty Ltd [1958] HCA 23; (1958) 99 CLR 431 and, in particular, the judgement of Fullagar J at page 445 where His Honour was commenting on the distinction drawn by the English Courts between expenditure which adds to capital and expenditure which merely protects or preserves capital and said:

“...Here the distinction has not been regarded as irrelevant, but it has not been treated generally as decisive. It is to be noted on the one hand that, although the relevant statutes differ in terms, the English courts, taking the view that capital expenditure is never deductible, have in many cases in substance addressed themselves to the question of what constitutes an outgoing of a capital nature, though often (the Borax Case (1941) 1 K.B. 111 being a notable exception) subsuming it under the general question of what constitutes expenditure ‘wholly and exclusively’ laid out in the producing income.”

In that case the Court was concerned with advertising and legal costs incurred by the company in relation to a Royal Commission established to enquire into and report on allegations made in Parliament reflecting on the integrity of those conducting the company’s business and upon the fairness of transactions with customers. Mr Flynn sought to relate the expenditure incurred in that case with the expenditure in question here as having been incurred to defend the business from complaints. However, in Snowden & Willson (supra) Fullagar J went on to say (at pages 459 and 460):

“For present purposes I do not think that the general question need be pursued further. Whether or not the decision of Lawrence J. in the Borax Case (2) be accepted as correct, and whether or not the distinction above referred to ought to be regarded as decisive, I am quite unable to regard the expenditure now in question as being of a capital nature. It is true that the allegations against which the company was defending itself were calculated to affect adversely the goodwill of the company, and that fact was doubtless present to the minds of the directors. But that is very far from being the whole of the truth. The allegations were made in specific cases, and were capable of directly affecting the past present and future revenue of the company as such. They were made by persons who stood in an existing legal relationship to the company, either as contractors or a mortgagors. The object of those who made them can only have been to obtain ultimately some reduction in their obligations to the company––either by was of repayment of moneys paid or by way of partial cancellation of future indebtedness. If they had pursued this object in the courts, there could have been no doubt about the position. Expenditure incurred by the company in an action or suit to enforce a contract or a mortgage, or in resisting a claim for relied by a contractor or mortgagor, must have been deductible from the company’s assessable income in the year in which it was incurred. It can surely make no difference that contractors or mortgagors chose a less direct (and perhaps in the long run less satisfactory) means of achieving the object they had in view.”

In this case, the applicant was not dealing with complainants who stood in an existing legal relationship with him and not incurring expenditure by way of advertising or legal costs but in a one-off substantial sum to change completely the method of disposal of effluent. That change was required as a condition of the EPA licence and the City of Springvale planning permit for a new beef chiller.

  1. Further reliance for the applicant’s argument was placed on the decision of the High Court in Charles Moore & Co (W. A. ) Pty Ltd v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1956] HCA 77; (1956) 95 CLR 344 where, in a joint judgment, the Court referred to the submission that money representing part of trading receipts and stolen on the way to the bank was part of the company’s capital resources and said (at page 351):
“For the purposes of this case it is enough to refer to the passages cited and to point out that we are here dealing with a loss incurred in an operation of business concerned with the regular inflow of revenue, not with a loss of or concerning part of the ‘profit yielding subject’, the phrase in which Lord Blackburn in United Collieries Ltd. v. Inland Revenue Commissioners ((1930) S.C. 215 at p. 220) summarised the characteristics of a business undertaking or enterprise considered as an affair of a capital nature.”

In my view the position here is quite different. The disposal of waste in an abattoir is a significant part of the profit yielding subject. It is difficult to see that expenditure on creating a facility to enable effluent to be discharged into a sewer is, in substance, any different from expansion of the then existing method of effluent disposal, the provision of additional or improved refrigeration system or any other building or plant additions required to ensure that the operations of the business were carried out in a safe and efficient way without being open to complaints. If the applicant had been required to erect a roofed area to protect stock, meat or employees from the sun and weather to comply with regulations or to avoid complaints, there would have been little doubt that such expenditure would be of a capital nature. Whilst the expenditure in question here did not result in plant equipment or building owned by the applicant and situated on his land it was a once and for all payment to enable the applicant to discharge waste into the sewer.

  1. In the words of the applicant in his letter of 25 February 1988 to the MMBW, it was “concluded that the long term of this business operation would be best served by the waste being discharged to sewer”. The expenditure secured that long term benefit to enable the business to satisfy the requirements of licensing and planning authorities and to allow further expansion of the business. Notwithstanding the evidence of Mr Gathercole that the then existing effluent disposal system provided adequate capacity for expansion, this seems at odds with the terms of the EPA licence of 1 May 1987 which required Mr Gathercole to provide by 1 November 1987 the plans and specifications for either upgrading of the treatment plant or connecting the waste water to sewer. In addition it appears to conflict with the condition imposed by the City of Springvale on 28 February 1989 for connection to sewerage.
  2. Delays in arranging the sewer connection were occasioned by Mr Gathercole’s attempts to have the MMBW pay for the works required and recover the cost by additional rates. If this had occurred such additional charges would have been deductible. However, this did not happen and it is not appropriate to seek to convert a capital sum expended to reduce future annual costs into being itself expenditure on revenue account.
  3. I am satisfied that the $157,200 incurred by the applicant to enable his business to connect to sewerage was expenditure of a capital nature and, therefore, not an allowable deduction under section 51(1) of the Act. It follows that the decision under review should be affirmed. I accept Mr Gathercole’s concern that the fact that he does not own a physical asset as a result of the expenditure means that no deduction is available by way of depreciation or any other way but this is a consequence of the way the legislation works. Fairness is not necessarily a criterion for deductibility nor is expenditure incurred on facilities to generate business and employment.

I certify that this and the ten (10) preceding pages are a true copy of the decision and reasons for decision herein of



Mr B. H. Pascoe, Senior Member
