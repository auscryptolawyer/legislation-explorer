Tasmanian Conservation Trust Inc and Minister for the Environment and Seahorse Aquaculture Pty Ltd [1998] AATA 684 (1 September 1998)

Last Updated: 4 September 1998

Administrative

Appeals

Tribunal

INTERLOCUTORY DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No T98/61

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re TASMANIAN CONSERVATION TRUST INC.,

Applicant

And MINISTER FOR THE ENVIRONMENT

Respondent

And SEAHORSE AQUACULTURE PTY LTD.,

Party Joined

Decision No 13240

INTERLOCUTORY DECISION

Tribunal Deputy President A M Blow OAM, QC.,

Date 1 September 1998

Place Hobart

Decision 1. The Tribunal orders pursuant to s.35 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 that until further order access to (a) the authors' names, addresses and organisations in relation to documents T9, T11, T13 and T29; (b) the parts of document T35 and the T documents index where those names, addresses and organisations appear; (c) documents T22, T32 and T33; and (d) the reference to the author and organisation of document T32 contained in document T35 and the T document index be restricted to members and staff of this Tribunal in the course of performing their duties and the respondent, his staff, and officers of his department, and their legal and expert advisors and witnesses.

2. The Tribunal further orders that the applicant have liberty to apply for the discharge or variation of this order.

[Sgd A M Blow]

Deputy President

CATCHWORDS

Practice and Procedure - documents lodged by respondent - confidentiality order - grounds for making - commercially sensitive material - material provided in confidence.

Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 - s.35

REASONS FOR INTERLOCUTORY DECISION

1 September 1998 Deputy President A M Blow OAM QC.,

1. These proceedings concern a decision made by the respondent Minister on 12 January 1998 under s.10A(2) of the Wildlife Protection (Regulation of Exports and Imports) Act 1982 to declare Hippocampus abdominalis, pot-bellied seahorse, harvested from Tasmania by Seahorse Aquaculture Pty Ltd ("the Company") to be controlled specimens for the purposes of the Act, subject to a number of conditions. The effect of that decision is that 600 seahorses of the species Hippocampus abdominalis may be taken from the wild by the Company for broodstock purposes otherwise than in accordance with a management programme approved under s.10 of the Act. The applicant has applied to this Tribunal for the review of that decision, pursuant s.80(1)(ab) of the Act.

2. Pursuant to s.37(1)(b) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 ("the AAT Act"), the respondent has lodged with this Tribunal copies of every document in his possession or under his control considered by him to be relevant for the review of that decision. However the respondent has applied for an order under s.35 of the AAT Act prohibiting the release to the applicant of copies of some of those documents, and parts of others. The relevant provisions of s.35 read as follows:-

"(2) Where the Tribunal is satisfied that it is desirable to do so by reason of the confidential nature of any evidence or matter or for any other reason, the Tribunal may, by order:

...

(b) give directions prohibiting or restricting the publication ... of matters contained in documents lodged with the Tribunal ...; and

(c) give directions prohibiting or restricting the disclosure to some or all of the parties to a proceeding ... of the contents of a document lodged with the Tribunal ... in relation to the proceeding.

(3) In considering:

...

(b) whether publication, or disclosure to some or all of the parties,... of a matter contained in a document lodged with the Tribunal ... should be prohibited or restricted;

the Tribunal shall take as the basis of its consideration the principle that it is desirable that ... the contents of documents lodged with Tribunal ... should be made available to the public and all parties, but shall pay due regard to any reasons given to the Tribunal ....why publication or disclosure of ... the matter contained in the document should be prohibited or restricted."

3. The application for the confidentiality order was made on behalf of the respondent by a letter dated 27 May 1998. The relevant parts of that letter read as follows:-

"I also seek a confidentiality order under section 35 of the AAT Act in relation to the following documents or parts of documents:

1. the authors [sic] names, addresses and organisations contained in documents T9, T11, T13 and T29 and also references to those names, addresses and organisations in document T35 and the T document index; and

2. documents T22, T32 and T33 and reference to the author and organisation of document T32 where it appears in the T document index and document T35.

Documents T9, T11, T13 and T29 relate to public comments received by this Department on the Seahorse Aquaculture P/L harvesting proposal, as required under section 9B of the Wildlife Protection (Regulation of Exports and Imports) Act 1982 (the Act). It is this Department's practice not to publicly disclose names of authors of comments received under s.9B of the Act. These names and addresses are also referred to in the advice to the Minister for the Environment from the Designated Authority (document T35).

Documents T22 and T33 comprise documents provided by Seahorse Aquaculture Pty Ltd to this Department regarding the Company's seahorse culturing and maintenance protocols. A confidentiality order in regard to these documents is sought on the basis that these protocols were provided by the Company under the condition that they would be treated as "commercial in confidence".

Document T32 contains information provided on the proposal by a community organisation who provided the information "In Confidence".

The Terms of the confidentiality order sought are that access to the authors [sic] names, addresses and organisations of documents T9, T11, T13 and T29 and T35 and the T documents index (where those names, addresses and organisations are referred to), documents T22, T32 and document T33 and the reference to the author and organisation of T32 contained in the T document index and document T35, be restricted to the following:

- Members and Staff of the Tribunal in the course of performing their duties;

- the Minister for the Environment, staff of the Minister's office, officers of the Department of the Environment, that Department's legal advisers and such other persons as are directly involved in the preparation or conduct of the Minister's case."

4. The applicant opposes the making of a confidentiality order in respect of some, but not all, of the documents in question. Submissions have been made on its behalf both orally and in writing. It does not oppose the making of a confidentiality order in respect of the authors' names, addresses and organisations in relation to documents T9, T11, T13 and T29. Some of that information is repeated in document T35 and in the index to the T documents. It has not opposed the making of confidentiality order in respect of document T32, which was submitted by a community organisation and marked "confidential". Its submissions have concerned only documents T22 and T33.

5. Document T22 comprises four pages of comments by Professor G N R Forteath, Professor of Aquaculture at the University of Tasmania, and a covering letter from him dated 17 November 1997.

6. Document T33 comprises a letter from Mr J Love of the Company, a document prepared by Professor Forteath headed "Seahorse Protocols" and marked "Commercial-in-Confidence", and appendices thereto setting out technical information in relation to the farming of seahorses.

7. The Company supports the respondent's application for confidentiality orders in respect of documents T22 and T33. It argues that the protocols it observes in relation to such matters as water quality, tank design, nutrition, stock control, and culling constitute confidential information that would be of great value to its competitors, including foreign competitors. Mr. Love submitted on behalf of the Company that releasing the information to the applicant would involve an unacceptable risk of the information finding its way into the hands of a foreign competitor.

8. A representative of the applicant has explained that the applicant has two major concerns in relation to the decision under review. First, it is concerned that the collection of 600 seahorses from the wild for broodstock purposes might have too great an impact on Australia's wild populations of those animals. Secondly, the applicant is concerned that the developing of a market for Australian seahorses overseas might encourage and facilitate the poaching of wild stocks of Australian seahorses to supply overseas markets. The applicant contends that the decision under review was premature, and that adequate controls to protect Australian seahorses in the wild should be in place before any decision permitting seahorses to be harvested for broodstock purposes is made.

9. In submissions made on behalf of the respondent, Dr. Bigwood told me that departmental officers had requested the information provided in document T33 because they wished the Company to satisfy them that it would not ultimately want to take more seahorses from the wild over and above the original 600. As I understand it, the Company sought to demonstrate that its seahorse farming techniques were sufficiently developed and sufficiently reliable for the Minister to be confident that the seahorses taken by it for the wild would be well managed, and that no request for additional seahorses from the wild would be likely at a later date.

10. The applicant is not contending that the farming techniques developed by the Company do or might involve an unacceptable risk that the original 600 seahorses will not be enough for its purposes. At least at the present, it is basing its case on the matters referred to in paragraph 8 above. In reviewing the Minister's decision, the Tribunal must regard as relevant any risk that the seahorses taken by the Company from the wild will be wasted, or that the taking of 600 will prove inadequate. If the applicant were contending that any such risk was so strong as to be unacceptable, procedural fairness might require the information in document T33, or much of it, to be made available to individual representatives of the applicant and/or its lawyers (if any) and/or it's expert witnesses, on a restricted basis. However it appears to me that the technical information in document T33 does not relate to any issue that is presently contentious.

11. Much of the information in document T33 appears to be in the nature of trade secrets, the very sort of confidential information which a court of equity would protect. It is in the interests of the respondent to preserve the practice that sensitive commercial information may be given to his department's officers in confidence and kept confidential. In my view the interests of the Company and the respondent outweigh the interest that the applicant has in gaining access to the contents of document T33 in the present proceedings. I have therefore decided to make a confidentiality order prohibiting the applicant from having access to that document.

12. Document T22 is in a different category. Professor Forteath wrote to a departmental officer on 17 November 1997 responding to a bulky submission sent to that officer by an overseas academic pursuant to s.9B of the Act (document T13). Parts of document T22 are relevant to the question of what impact the removal of 600 seahorses from the wild by the Company might have. That of course is one of the two central issues raised by the applicant in its challenge to the decision under review
