VT85/1325, VT85/1326 and Commissioner of Taxation [1988] AATA 426; (1988) 19 ATR 3356; 88 ATC 403 (9 March 1988)

Last Updated: 24 June 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL



Income Tax - assessable income of trustee - discretionary trust - whether minor beneficiaries are in receipt of income from more than one trust - whether appropriation made by trustee under Deed of Settlement constituted 'separate' trust - application of sub-s.7A(l) of Income Tax (Rates) Act 1976 - effect of Deputy Commissioner exercising discretion under sub-s.7A(2) in regard to application of sub-s.7A(l) - Cases UIII, U157 V4 V27 distinguished.

REASONS FOR DECISION



9 March 1988 Mrs J R Dwyer, Senior Member



1. This case proceeded on an agreed statement of facts. It is not necessary to set out the statement in full. The relevant matters are summarised and set out below.

2. The taxpayer is the trustee of a settlement created by Deed of Settlement made 4 November 1973. Among the beneficiaries of the trust are two infant children "A" and "I". In the year ended 30 June 1979 the settlement derived net income of $4,405.76. The net income was allocated to five infant beneficiaries. A and I received an allocation of $1,145.94 each.

3. The balances standing to the credit of A and I in the balance sheet of the settlement after that allocation were $4,336.32 for A and $4,336.31 for I. Those amounts represent the income of the settlement distributed in earlier years as well as the allocation for the year ended 30 June 1979.

4. During the year ended 30 June 1979, the settlement paid $319.00 interest to each of A and I, which was calculated at the rate of 10 per cent per annum on the amount standing to their credit at the beginning of the year.

5. On 21 December 1979 the Deputy Commissioner issued Notices of Assessment to the trustee as trustee for A and I. The trustee was assessed on account of each beneficiary on a taxable income of $1,464.00 comprised of the trust distribution of $1,145.00 and the $319.00 interest income. The tax assessed in each case was $212.00 together with a further $212.00 provisional tax.

6. On 12 February 1980 objections were lodged against the two assessments. The objections claimed that the taxable income of A and I should be reduced by $319.00. The grounds on which the trustee relied were stated as follows:

"1. That the said amount of $319 was paid to the said [A or I] as interest upon a loan of money to the said Settlement and the principal of such loan is an amount to which the said [A or I] is absolutely entitled and is referred to in the accounts of the said Settlement under the heading "Beneficiaries' Trusts" only by way of indication that the amount is held by the Trustee upon an absolute trust for the relevant beneficiary until an appropriate receipt or release is obtained.

  1. That Division 6 of Part III of the Income Tax Assessment Act does not apply to the income of a taxpayer which is derived by way of interest on funds held by a nominee or custodian trustee and in which the taxpayer has an absolute vested interest.
  2. The said amount of interest was paid to the taxpayer by cheque which was duly deposited in a savings account with the Bank of New South Wales on 2nd July 1979 and such savings account is beneficially owned by the said [A or I].
  3. As the said [A or I] is liable as taxpayer in respect of the said amount of interest paid to him under Section 25 it is not appropriate that the Trustee should be assessed in respect of the same amount under Section 98."

7. On 22 December 1980 amended assessments were issued disclosing a taxable income of $1,1450.00 but the tax assessed in each case was increased to $383.57 together with provisional tax of $413.00. The adjustment sheet explained the adjustment as follows:

"The section 98 assessment has been amended to reduce the assessment from $1,464 to $1,145. (This amendment satisfies in full your objection dated 12 February 1980) in regard to the ... settlement it is considered that the interest payable to the beneficiary constituted income of a separate trust fund and accordingly the income to which the beneficiary was presently entitled from the main trust and sub trust came within the provisions of s.7A of the Income Tax (Rates) Act 1976.

Whilst the view expressed above is maintained it has been observed that circumstances exist which would warrant the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion not to apply s.7A of the Income Tax (Rates) Act 1976 to the income derived by the sub trust."

8. On 10 February 1981 the trustee objected to the amended assessments. The grounds of those objections were stated as follows:

"1. Circumstances exist which warrant the exercise of the Commissioner's discretion not to apply Section 7A of the Income Tax (Rates) Act 1976 in respect of the income paid to the beneficiary as interest on moneys on loan with the settlement.

  1. The said Section 7A is in any case not applicable in the circumstances.
  2. Division 6 of Part III of the Income Tax Assessment Act does not apply to income derived as interest on funds in which the recipient has an absolute vested interest.
  3. The interest which it is alleged constitutes a distribution from another trust was duly paid to the taxpayer by cheque and deposited by his parents in a bank account kept solely for his benefit and the funds in which are absolutely vested in the taxpayer/beneficiary.
  4. The interest alleged to be a distribution from another trust has been disclosed as income of [A or I] derived from another source in his return and is assessable only under Section 25 of the said Act (ITAA)."

9. It is necessary to turn to the legislative provisions to understand why the tax assessed on a taxable income of $1,145.00 in the amended assessments, was more than the tax assessed on a taxable income of $1,464.00 in the original assessments. The relevant legislation is the Income Tax (Rates) Act 1976 ("the Act") which contains provisions declaring different rates of tax for the financial years commencing 1 July 1976, 1 July 1977, 1 July 1978 and 1 July 1979 and subsequent financial years. It provides, for the year commencing 1 July 1978, as follows:

"6F(1) Subject to section 7A, where -

(a) the trustee of a trust estate (other than the estate of a deceased person) is liable to be assessed under section 98 of the Assessment Act in respect of a share of the net income of the trust estate to which a beneficiary under the age of 16 years on the last day of the year of income is presently entitled; and

(b) the amount of that share of the net income does not exceed $1,040,

no tax is payable under sub-section 6E(4) in respect of that share of the net income."

Sub-section 6F(2) provides that, subject to s.7A, where the amount of the share of a beneficiary under 16, of net income from a trust estate exceeded $1,040.00 but did not exceed $3,151.00 the amount of tax payable by the trustee should not exceed 50 per centum of the amount by which the beneficiary's share exceeded $1,040.00. Sub-sections 7A(1) and (2) provide:

"7A(1) Subject to sub-section (2) of this section, sub-section 6C(1) or (2) or sub-section 6F(1) or (2) does not apply in relation to a trustee of a trust estate who is liable to be assessed and to pay tax in pursuance of section 98 of the Assessment Act in respect of the share of the net income to which a beneficiary under the age of 16 years on the last day of the year of income is presently entitled if -

(a) the beneficiary was presently entitled to a share of the net income of the year of income of one or more other trust estates of the kind referred to in paragraph (a) of that sub-section; and

(b) the sum of the amounts of the shares of the beneficiary of the net incomes of the first-mentioned trust estate and that other trust estate or those other trust estates exceeds $1,040.

7A(2) Sub-section (1) does not apply in relation to a share of the net income of a trust estate to which a beneficiary under the age of 16 years on the last day of the year of income is presently entitled if the Commissioner is of the opinion that it would be unreasonable that that sub-section should apply in relation to that share of the net income."

Sub-section 7A(3) sets out various matters to which the Commissioner shall have regard in deciding whether or not to exercise his discretion under sub-s.7A(2).

10. The effect of these provisions is therefore to provide a concession in regard to beneficiaries whose sole trust income is derived from one estate. If they have a taxable income of less than $1,040.00 no tax is payable. If the taxable income exceeds $1,040.00 but does not exceed $3,051.00 the tax is to be no more than 50 per centum of the amount of the excess. Sub-section 7A(1) provides that these concessions do not apply if the beneficiary was also entitled to a share of the net income of one or more other trust estates and the sum of the amount of the shares of the beneficiary of the first mentioned trust estate and that other trust estate exceeds $1,040.00.

11. The first argument put on behalf of the trustee was that A and I did not in the relevant year derive a share of net income from one or more trust estates. This argument presents some conflict with the first ground set out in the objection lodged on 12 February 1980 which states that the amounts representing accumulated shares of income allocated to A and I over the years were held by the trustee upon trust for A and I. However the matter becomes quite clear when one refers to the Deed of Settlement which, in similar terms to the deed in Case UIII (87 ATC 667), expressly provides in Clause ll(b)(ii):

"ll(b)(ii) Any income which the Trustees may determine to appropriate for the benefit of any infant may be retained together with any income arising from the investment of such appropriated income for that infant during the minority of such infant and the Trustees may postpone the payment of such retained income to such infant until the "date of vesting". Such appropriations in respect of any infant shall be deemed to be a separate trust for that infant subject to the like powers and conditions as set out in this deed in relation to the Trust Fund." (emphasis added)

Thus it is clear that, if the trustee held the earlier appropriations of income pursuant to the power and authority contained in the Deed, it did so under "a separate trust for that infant" (the "sub-trust").

12. It was put on behalf of the trustee that although it had done exactly what it was empowered to do under clause ll(b)(i) of the Deed of Settlement, it had not acted under the Deed but pursuant to an oral loan agreement with the parents of A and I who were acting as trustee under the Deed for their children. In view of the fact that the trustees had express power to retain income allocated from the trust to a beneficiary, and that it did so retain the trust income, I am satisfied that it held that accumulated income pursuant to clause ll(b)(ii) of the Deed on a separate trust for A and I. This is the position reflected in the balance sheet of the Settlement. I rely on and adopt the reasons of the Tribunal on this point in Case UIII and Case U157 (87 ATC 912) and Case V4 (88 ATC 123).

13. The evidence establishes that, at least for the years beginning 1 July 1977 and 1 July 1978, the interest on the accumulated income was paid out to the parents of A and I and banked in accounts in the parents' names bearing the words "as trustee for" A and I. This does not alter the character of the fund comprised of accumulated income in the hands of the trustee of the Settlement but simply means that A and I are the beneficiaries of a further trust to which they have "an absolutely vested beneficiary interest in possession" which but for their minority they could legally recover from the trustee. (See Case V27 83 ATC 262).

14. The first condition for the application of sub-s.7A(1) of the Act, namely entitlement to a share of income from one or more trusts is therefore satisfied, although the trustee is the same in both cases. Tax is therefore payable pursuant to sub-s.7A(1) on the total share of income to which the beneficiaries are entitled from the trust and the "sub-trust". The concessions provided in s.6F would therefore appear on the facts so far recited not to be available to the trustee.

15. There is however a distinction between this matter and the four similar cases referred to above. In this matter, as the Deputy Commissioner explained in the adjustment sheet, a decision was made not to apply sub-s.7A(1) of the Act to the income derived by the "sub-trust". During the hearing the Tribunal expressed some doubts as to whether it was possible for the Deputy Commissioner to exercise the discretion in sub-s.7A(2) of the Act and yet apply sub-s.7A(1). This is because sub-s.7A(2) expressly provides that sub-s.7A(1) does not apply to a share of net income if the Commissioner is of the opinion that it would be unreasonable for it to apply to that share of income.

16. The representatives of the taxpayer and the Deputy Commissioner both submitted that the decision to apply sub-s.7A(2) in regard to the interest of $319.00 was not before the Tribunal in this matter. The Commissioner exercised his discretion under sub-s.7A(2) and there was no objection to the exercise of that discretion. The Tribunal accepts that submission. The only issue therefore, is whether sub-s.7A(1) of the Act can still apply to this matter, after the Commissioner has exercised his discretion not to apply that sub-section in regard to the sum of $319.00.

17. Sub-section 7A(1) of the Act operates to exclude the beneficial application of sub-ss.6F(1) and (2) in relation to tax payable by a trustee in respect of an infant beneficiary's share of income if two conditions are satisfied. Those conditions are:

(a) that the beneficiary was also entitled to income from one or more other trust estates; and

(b) that the sum of the amounts of the shares from the first and the one or more other trust estates is more than $1,040.00.

18. Those conditions were both satisfied in this matter until the Deputy Commissioner chose to exercise his discretion under sub-s.7A(2). The effect of exercising the discretion is that sub-s.7A(1) does not apply in relation to the $319.00 income from the sub-trust. The only effect of the application of sub-s.7A(1) is to remove a concession offered by sub-ss.6F(1) and (2) where two conditions are satisfied. Sub-section 7A(1) does not itself impose any rate of tax in relation to any share of trust income. Its application is dependent upon:

(a) the beneficiary receiving income from one or more trust estates other than the trust in question; and

(b) the sum of income from the trust estates exceeding $1,040.00.

19. I consider that where sub-s.7A(1) does not apply in relation to the income of $319.00, that income can not be used either to satisfy the condition in paragraph 7A(1)(a), or as one of the two constituent parts of the sum required under paragraph 7A(1)(b). If the amount of $319.00 were used in the sum then sub-s.7A(1) would be applied "in relation to" that share of income. Once the amount of $319.00 is removed from the application of sub-s.7A(1) there can be no sum of amounts as there is only one amount remaining, namely the figure of $1,145.94. Thus the conditions necessary for the application of sub-s.7A(1) are not satisfied and the tax payable is to be ascertained by reference to s.6F of the Act. The income from the settlement constituting the trust estate in the relevant year was $1,145.94 for each of the infant beneficiaries. That is more than $1,040.00 therefore sub-s.6F(2) applies.

20. This application for review therefore succeeds on Ground 2 as set out in the notices of objection dated 10 February 1981, namely that s.7A was not applicable in the circumstances. The objection decision will be set aside.
