The Christ Circle Orionia Community Inc and Commissioner of Taxation [1995] AATA 468 (13 March 1995)

Last Updated: 23 September 2008

REASONS FOR DECISION



13 March 1995 Mr T E Barnett (Senior Member)



  1. This is an application by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation (“the respondent”) that the application of the Christ Circle, Oriona Community Inc. (“the Community”) be dismissed as frivolous and vexatious pursuant to the discretion given to the Tribunal by s.42B(1) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 (“the AAT Act”). The substantive matter concerns an application for exemption under the Debit Tax Administration Act 1982 (“the Act”) on the grounds that the Community is “a public benevolent or religious institution”.
  2. The main submission of Mr Carey, for the respondent, is that even if all the facts submitted on behalf of the Community are accepted as proven, its case is so utterly hopeless that it fits within the principles of modern authorities that would allow a finding of “vexatious” in a case which is, objectively, “obviously untenable or manifestly groundless so as to be utterly hopeless.”
  3. According to Mr Carey, the fatal flaw in the Community’s case is that it cannot possibly ever be accepted as “a public benevolent or religious institution” as on the authority of the Full Federal Court in Pamas Foundation (Inc.) v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation [1992] FCA 154; (1992) 23 ATR 189, it has too few members, has been in existence for too short a time and is too “local” in its operations to be considered an “institution” for the purpose of the Act.
  4. Mr Johnston, for the applicant, on the other hand, submits that the matter is not so clear cut as that and he points to the fact that the respondent has been aware of those aspects of the Community for a long time but has been responding to the applicant’s statements and documents as if a credible claim was being considered. He points out that size, longevity and acceptance by society are only some of the indicia which need to be considered when deciding whether a community body is an institution and that Pamas is not a clear cut authority which would bind the Tribunal in this case, where the question of State harassment, specific charitable work in Albany and acceptance by the local society in Albany have been raised.
  5. The Tribunal is a review body which has been established to provide applicants with an inexpensive forum to enable them to play an active part in the review of government decisions which have adversely affected them. It’s procedures are free from the technical rules of evidence and it is geared to provide natural justice to parties, many of whom are unrepresented. In the absence of evidence of actual mala fides, the Tribunal will be reluctant to deny this forum to an applicant merely because its case appears, prior to the hearing, to be weak, especially when the alleged weaknesses have been known to the respondent for many months during which it has allowed the applicant to expend great time and probably cost in preparing its case for hearing.
  6. The list of qualities required of an “institution” have not been closed by the Federal Court decision in Pamas and I am not satisfied that the applicant’s case, which will be presented at the Hearing, will be so utterly hopeless as to fall within the term, ‘frivolous and vexatious’ used in s.42B(1) of the AAT Act.

Decision

  1. The application is therefore refused.
