Gardner and Commissioner for Superannuation [1978] AATA 78 (8 December 1978)

Last Updated: 14 March 2007





Superannuation - Benefit classification certificate deemed to be in force - Invalidity retirement on ground of condition specified in certificate - Application for review of decision confirming benefit entitlement under s.66(2) of Superannuation Act 1976 - Evidence of later illness causing invalidity - Benefit entitlement of applicant to be determined under s.66(1) - Superannuation Act 1976, ss.66(1), 66(2), 154, 184(2).



JESSIE ELIZABETH MARY CARDENER

THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

AAT Decision No 118

No: 78/10032

R.K. Todd (Senior Member)

V.J. Skermer (Member)

Dr. M. Glick (Member)

8 December 1978

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No: 78/10032

Re: JESSIE ELIZABETH MARY GARDNER

And: THE COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

DECISION

The decision of the Tribunal is that the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation made pursuant to s154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 whereby he confirmed a decision taken on his behalf by his Delegate on 23 May 1977 that the benefit entitlement of Mrs. Gardner on her ceasing to be an eligible employee on 25 November 1976 by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity should be determined under s 66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 should be set aside, and in substitution therefor the Tribunal decides that her entitlement should be determined under s.66(1) of that Act.

DATED this 8th day of December 1978.

REASONS FOR DECISION

1. The issue in this application was whether the retirement of the applicant on 25 November 1976 was caused, or was substantially contributed to by the condition of hypertension specified on the benefit classification certificate deemed to be in force in respect of the applicant by virtue of s184(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976.

2. The applicant commenced her employment with the Department of Defence in 1955. She became in due course secretary to the Director of Naval Intelligence, then secretary to the Director of Naval Security. Then she went overseas on holiday. After her return, she became, in 1968, typist in charge of a typing pool, in the same area.

3. The applicant had had some medical problems and in August 1971 she was declared unacceptable as a contributor either to the Superannuation Fund or to the Provident Account because of the following medical conditions:

(i) High blood pressure being treated with Raudixin and Aprinox;

(ii) Peptic ulcer which had perforated with surgical treatment;

(iii) Possible Lymphoma treated by radiotherapy,

(iv) Migraine.

4. In August 1973, further to successful treatment for high blood pressure and having been free of symptoms of peptic ulcer for three years she was accepted as a contributor to the Provident Account as from 18 September 1973. It was common ground that there was deemed to be in force under s184(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976, in respect of the applicant, a Benefit Classification Certificate specifying the conditions of hypertension, migraine and history of peptic ulcer.

5. In 1973 the applicant began to suffer a new health problem. She developed a numbness in her right leg and found it difficult to walk, then pains developed down her right arm and into her hand. The numbness of the leg caused her to fall and once she fell she could not get up again. She had numerous tests but the problem was not initially diagnosed. Eventually, however, it was diagnosed as a spinal tumour arising from a nerve root. An operation for removal of the tumour was performed in September 1974, and whilst the operation may be described as a success, she was left with some loss of sensation and diminished motor functions of the ring and little fingers of her right hand, sufficient to prevent her from being an efficient typist. She was left with a permanent tremor in the right hand which she could not and still cannot control. Also, she still had a tendency to fall, and her husband used, as before, to have to drive her into an area of the Defence Department, ingress to which required special permission from security, so that she could be brought right to the door and be helped to her work area. Having got there, she was unable properly to perform her duties, and as a result she became embarrassed, frustrated and emotionally upset. She took pride in her work, but did not feel satisfied with the work she did after the operation. Asked if work was returned to her after the operation she answered: "Plenty of times, yes, for erasures". It should be added that in August 1976 she had a particularly bad fall.

6. The applicant's difficulties with her work made her continuously emotional, nervous and anxious. She felt most unsteady, that she was going to fall, and that she needed somebody to help her everywhere. With her hand shaking, she said she "was in a dreadful state". She went to see Doctor Craigie about the shaking of her hand. She took Panadeine for the pain but had no other medication other than occasional Digesic tablets. She never went to discuss with him anything but her hand, but he took her blood pressure on each occasion while she was there. The applicant was adamant that her hypertension has never worried her, and she has never sought medical advice about it. She has never, in fact, been aware of it or had any symptoms of it but it is revealed upon examination by doctors. We emphasise that on the evidence she has not received any treatment for hypertension since 1966.

7. The applicant was on sick leave for some 10 weeks prior to her retirement on 25 November 1976. The certificates actually put before us, however, were as follows: On 11 October1976 the applicant was examined by a Dr. Banks, who was a locum tenens of Dr. Craigie. Dr. Banks certified that the applicant "is still suffering from hypertension and depression and she will be unfit for work from 11.10.76 until 25.10.76 inclusive". On 5 November Dr. Craigie certified that he had examined the applicant and that in his opinion she was suffering from "Anxiety/Depression" and that she would be unfit for work from 8 November 1976 and would be fit to return to work on 15 November 1976. On 12 November he gave a similar certificate for the period 15 November to 22 November 1976 but specified her condition as being "Essential Hypertension". On 5 November 1976 he had also, however, given the applicant a certificate as follows: "This is to certify that on medical grounds, Mrs. Jessie Gardner ought to be retired".

8. By now the applicant felt that she had to retire. She was asked why she felt as she did, and the abovementioned medical certificates must be read in the light of her evidence:

"I just could not do my work; I just felt nervous which was causing anxiety. I just had a feeling that if I kept on something worse would happen. I was very apprehensive. I just could not concentrate on my work. I was extremely nervous. My husband had to take me practically everywhere, I was just extremely nervous at the time.

Q. As a result of the operation were you affected in any other way apart from the obvious tremor?

A. Yes, I still felt very hesitant where I was walking and I was still falling. My husband had permission to take the car into the quadrangle and bring me up into my office.

Q. If it was not for the condition of your hand would you have kept working?

A. Yes, I would have kept working. I wanted to work until my husband retired."

9. On 1 December 1976 the applicant was examined for fitness to remain at work by a Medical Officer of the Department of Health, who reported that the applicant was suffering from hypertension and depression. The doctor was not called to give evidence, but his report states: "Has been under treatment for Hypertension for some years and latterly has been on S.L. for 10 weeks continuously for depression. Recurrent and frequent headaches. 1973 tumour removed from Cervical spine area and now has loss of sensation and muscle power in R. hand". His clinical notes revealed a blood pressure reading of 190/110, and referred to partial paralysis with loss of sensation in the area of distribution of the right fifth cervical nerve and to her being "emotionally unstable and apprehensive about returning to work". His assessment was that she was unfit for further service and should be retired. Once again, the applicant had, upon examination, been found to have hypertension, although it should be said that if the Medical Officer's Report was intended to indicate that she was still then receiving treatment for hypertension, it was in error. In our view the hypertension was not a factor in her retirement, and all the certificates which refer to hypertension must be seen in the light of the evidence which was given before us by the applicant and her husband and which was tested by cross-examination. The applicant and her husband were the only witnesses, and we accept their testimony without hesitation.

10. It seems clear to us that the applicant's disabilities resulted from the operation referred to above which took place after her admission to the Provident Account, and in view of the fact that she has not, since 1966, received treatment for high blood pressure, we are unable to support the opinion that has been expressed (vide the letter of Dr. Turner of 26 June 1978) that hypertension was the major cause of the incapacity which was the ground for the applicant's retirement on the ground of invalidity. On the evidence before us, we find that it neither caused nor did it substantially contribute to such incapacity.

11. Our decision must therefore be that the decision of the Commissioner for Superannuation made pursuant to s154 of the Superannuation Act 1976 whereby he confirmed a decision taken on his behalf by his Delegate on 23 May 1977 that the benefit entitlement of Mrs. Gardner on her ceasing to be an eligible employee on 25 November 1976 by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity should be determined under s 66(2) of the Superannuation Act 1976 should be set aside, and that in substitution therefor the decision of the Tribunal should be that her entitlement should be determined under s 66(l) of that Act.
