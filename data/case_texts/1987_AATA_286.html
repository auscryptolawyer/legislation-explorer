Re Taxation Appeals [1987] AATA 286 (3 August 1987)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. NT85/4811

AAT No. 3749

Income Tax

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION

Justice Rodney N. Purvis (Presidential Member)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - Merchant Bank - Mandate to arrange finance for Mining Co - Fee agreed for successful negotiation - Mandate terminated - Expenses incurred - Damage to Reputation Standing and peremptorily Goodwill - Legal Proceedings threatened - Offer of lump sum made to settle all matters in dispute with no apportionment - Offer accepted was "lump sum" - Whether settlement amount liable to tax

McLaurin v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1961] HCA 9; (1960-1961) 104 CLR 381

Rylands v Fletcher [1868] UKHL 1; (1968) LR 3 HL 330

Allsop v The Commissioner of Taxation [1965] HCA 48; (1964) 113 CLR 341

Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Myer Emporium Limited 87 ATC 4363, 4366, 4367

Heavy Minerals Pty Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1966) 115 CLR 512

Commissioner of Taxation (NSW) v Meeks [1915] HCA 34; (1915) 19 CLR 568, 580

H.R. Sinclair and Son Pty Limited v Commissioner of Taxation (1964) 114 CLR 537

HEARING

SYDNEY

3:8:1987

ORDER

The Tribunal sets aside the objection decision in relation to the assessment for the year ended 31 October 1980 and allows the objection in full.

DECISION

Generally

This is an application by S. Securities Limited for review of the decision on an objection in relation to an assessment issued by the Respondent Commissioner against it on the 19 March 1981, the same being in respect of the year ended 31 October 1980.

2. The Respondent in the assessment treated as income and brought to tax a sum of $200,000, such amount having been received by S. Securities Limited in the relevant financial year consequent upon a dispute between S. Securities Limited and another company, M.C. Limited having been resolved.

3. The taxable income of S. Securities Limited as returned was increased by an "amount of $200,000 received as a result of damages claim included as assessable income".

4. The dispute the subject of the claim arose as a sequel to M.C. Limited cancelling a mandate that it had given to S. Securities Limited whereby the latter company was to endeavour to raise finance for M.C. Limited locally or preferably off-shore.

5. The applicant has the burden of satisfying the Tribunal that the decision of the Respondent Commissioner was wrong.

The factual situation

6. The evidence before the Tribunal both by way of affidavit and oral evidence establishes that the factual situation leading up and subsequent to the payment of the said sum was as follows:

1. S. Securities Limited has at all relevant times,

and had for a period of about two years prior to

the happening of the events detailed hereunder,

been carrying on business as a merchant bank. It

was in 1978 being promoted in the Australian

financial sector.

2. M.C. Limited was an established mining company

seeking to attract funding for a substantial

development project in South Australia. It was

however not, at that time, well known outside of

Australia.

3. In 1978 an approach was made by an officer of M.C.

Limited to S. Securities Limited seeking to retain

the company in order to arrange financing in the

amount of US$75,000,000 to fund development of the

project. Only a few banks world wide had the

necessary expertise to finance development work

the like of that envisaged and S. Securities

Limited sought a mandate to arrange the financing.

4. In November 1978 an arrangement between the

companies was formalised by way of letter. The

mandate was larger than any other financial

engagement of S. Securities Limited to that time.

Success in arranging the finance would enhance the

company's standing in the Australian finance

market and assist in establishing it in the

international finance market.

5. Negotiation took place as to an appropriate fee to

be paid to S. Securities Limited in the event that

an introduction was successful and resulted in an

agreement for the provision of finance to M.C.

Limited.

The fee to be received by the taxpayer was

initially to be 1 1/2% of the loan amount raised.

The fee was to be payable only in the event of

negotiations being successful and resulting in a

final agreement between M.C. Limited and the

lender or lenders introduced by S. Securities

Limited. The fee initially agreed was relatively

high, M.C. Limited being then not well known in

Australia or overseas and hence the raising of

funds to prove more difficult. The rate was

later, by agreement, reduced to 1 1/4%.

6. S. Securities Limited set about contacting

representatives of international banks. The

agency position between S. Securities Limited and

M.C. Limited was at all times disclosed by

S. Securities Limited. All negotiations were to

be directly with S. Securities Limited. The fact

of the company having been specifically retained

by M.C. Limited to negotiate on its behalf and put

together and co-ordinate any arrangement was made

known to any bank to whom approaches were made.

The mandate was assured.

7. In late 1978 and early 1979 contact was made by

S. Securities Limited in Australia and overseas

with representatives of the International Energy

Bank Limited, the Chemical Bank International

Limited and the Royal Bank of Canada. Meetings

and negotiations took place, in depth submissions

to company representatives were prepared and the

outline of a loan proposal was received.

M.C. Limited was kept informed of the status of

negotiations.

8. By March 1979 the negotiations had matured into a

preparedness on the part of a consortium to

underwrite a US $75,000,000 facility to M.C.

Limited. An offer was made to M.C. Limited, the

same to remain open for one month. By resolution

of the Board of M.C. Limited the offer was

accepted, the consortium and S. Securities Limited

being so informed. The taxpayer company was to

continue its involvement and negotiate any further

details and otherwise hold itself available to

co-ordinate the funding. The consortium was

further so informed of the role still to be

maintained by S. Securities Limited. M.C. Limited

confirmed to S. Securities Limited the percentage

of the loan to be paid by way of the fee.

9. At the annual general meeting of M.C. Limited held

shortly thereafter, the Chairman referred to the

agreement reached as to the funding with the

consortium the same being "subject only to usual

documentation and approval of the Reserve Bank of

Australia". The involvement of S. Securities

Limited was expressly mentioned as was the finding

being "the largest loan raised by any South

Australian company" to that time.

10. It was on the same day as M.C. Limited wrote to

S. Securities Limited informing the taxpayer of

approval being given by the Board of M.C. Limited

to the financing by the consortium that

legislation was introduced in the Parliament of

the State in which M.C. Limited was incorporated,

limiting single shareholding in M.C. Limited to a

particular percentage. At this time a company

that had been also instrumental in arranging the

funding was a consultant to M.C. Limited. The

consultant had a material interest in the

profitability of M.C. Limited, having a

shareholding in the company at a level beyond the

then new statutory limited. The legislation

required the shareholder to dispose of such

excess.

11. In July 1979 the Managing Director of M.C. Limited

told S. Securities Limited that a decision had

been made to terminate the mandate and that

thereafter dealings referable to the finance

facility would be handled direct and not through

the agent. Meetings were held between

representative of the two companies.

S. Securities Limited sought to have the mandate

reinstated "because we had put our credibility on

the line". The Deputy Chairman of M.C. Limited

said that reinstatement was unlikely but that "he

would see what he could do". Shortly thereafter

the taxpayer was advised that "our position was

irreversibly terminated:. M.C. Limited was then

put on notice as to the inevitability of legal

action.

12. S. Securities Limited had sought legal advice as

to whether the taxpayer had a cause of action

against M.C. Limited and the prospects of success

in any action that might be taken. Expenses,

substantially comprised of costs of travelling

overseas and accommodation, incurred by

S. Securities Limited to the time of termination

of the mandate totalled approximately $30,000.

Advice was given.

13. Board discussions ensued. In September 1979 the

taxpayer wrote to M.C. Limited a letter, the

material parts of which read as follows:

"I refer to our meeting in Sydney in

July in which you suggested that we

reconsider the question of our fees for

negotiating the financing on behalf of

(M.C. Limited) with the consortium . . . . .

We are more than a little disappointed

with the outcome of our association with

(M.C. Limited) in this matter. We spent

an extensive amount of time in arranging

a firm and binding offer to provide

finance from a major international

banking consortium. In the process we

had serious discussions with many other

banking groups including for example,

Chemical Bank International Limited,

Royal Bank of Canada and a number of

others. After laying our credibility on

the line it was extremely disappointing

to be told that the (consortium) had

been instructed to deal direct with you

and that you wanted us to consider a

reduction in fees.

We believe that we have a firm

commitment from (M.C. Limited) to pay

our fees for arranging the financing at

a rate of 1 1/4% of the amount of the

loan negotiated, namely the Australian

Dollar equivalent of US$75,000,000.

That represented a reduction on our part

of a quarter percent of the amount

originally agreed with us, namely 1 1/2%

of the amount of the loan . . . . . .

When we negotiated our fee originally we

anticipated that we would be involved in

the preparation of all the final

documentation. We are happy to continue

with the involvement. We believe

however, that having reached an

agreement with you as to fees and having

successfully completed our side of that

agreement and as always subject to the

(engineers') report it is now

inappropriate for you to seek any

further reduction in our fees . . . . . . "

14. In October 1979 the Managing Director of

S. Securities Limited in a discussion with the

Deputy Chairman of M.C. Limited stated that the

company had received legal advice to the effect

that it would be able to sue M.C. Limited and

claim its actual costs in performing its

obligations under the mandate and fees which it

would have been entitled to receive in the event of

the mandate not having been terminated, although

it was conceded that there may be problems with

the latter claim because M.C. Limited had

accepted the loan offer before the mandate was

terminated, the negotiations with the consortium

not then being completed and the loan not having

been drawn down. The Deputy Chairman was further

advised by the Managing Director of S. Securities

Limited that advice had been received from the

taxpayer's solicitors that the company's most

substantial claim against M.C. Limited would

however be for damage to its reputation and

goodwill and that this would be made the primary

ground for relief in any proceedings taken.

15. Negotiations ensued in an endeavour to "settle the

matter". In December 1979 the Managing Director

of M.C. Limited "came up with his fixed final and

firm offer" and said that the company would pay

the taxpayer $200,000 "in settlement of this

matter. That is our first and last offer. If

you accept you will have to drop any claims

against (M.C. Limited)."

16. In due course S. Securities Limited agreed to

accept the $200,000 offer "in full and final

settlement of all claims it had against (M.C.

Limited) arising out of the termination of the

mandate and the damage caused to its reputation".

17. On the 23 April 1980 the agreement so reached

between S. Securities Limited and M.C. Limited

was evidenced by a discharge executed by

S. Securities Limited which discharge provided

inter alia that in consideration of M.C. Limited

paying to S. Securities Limited a sum of $200,000

and agreeing to request that the taxpayer be

offered the opportunity of being sub-underwriter

of a pro rata issue of shares and purchaser of

certain rights in such issue of non-resident

shareholds of M.C. Limited, S. Securities Limited

thereby accepted:

"The said agreement on the part of

(M.C. Limited) to pay the said sum of

Two Hundred Thousand Dollars ($200,000)

in full and final satisfaction,

settlement and discharge of all actions,

costs, claims, charges, demands and

expenses whatsoever which (S. Securities

Limited) now has or hereafter may have,

or but for the execution hereof might

have had against (M.C. limited) or its

servants or agents for or on account of

or in any way whatever arising out of or

connected with any advice, assistance or

services provided or offered by (S.

Securities Limited) to (M.C. Limited) or

any other person in connection with the

raising or proposals to raise for (M.C.

Limited) a loan of the equivalent of

Australian Dollar, Sixty Five Million

(A$65,000,000) or otherwise and

(S. Securities Limited) hereby releases

and discharges (M.C. Limited) and its

servants and agents and each of them

from all such actions, costs, claims,

charges, demands and expenses whatsoever

and (S. Securities Limited) hereby

undertakes not to commence or proceed

with any action or proceedings against

(M.C. Limited) or any of its servants or

agents in respect of any claim for any

fee, remuneration, expenses or damages

arising out of or in any way connected

with any such advice, assistance or

services or otherwise and (S. Securities

Limited) agrees if called upon by (M.C.

Limited) so to do, to execute a formal

deed of release in respect of the

premises for the purposes of giving

effect hereto and (S. Securities

Limited) agrees that this discharge may

be pleaded in bar to any action or other

proceeding now or at any time hereafter

commenced by (S. Securities Limited)

against (M.C. Limited) or any of its

servants or agents for any such fee,

remuneration, expense, or damages as

aforesaid."

18. Payment of the said sum of $200,000 was made on

the 30 April 1980.

19. The consortium that was to provide the finding was

comprised of significant national banks it having

been formed to develop a specialist energy

division which in due course effectively became

the International Energy Bank. The banks

comprising the consortium were ones with whom

S. Securities "was expecting to deal in the

future".

Evidence was available to the Tribunal to the

effect that the termination of the mandate of

S. Securities Limited by M.C. Limited would have

damaged its then reputation. Having in mind the

mandate given to S. Securities Limited the company

being a relatively new organisation in the

merchant banking industry, the approaches then

made by it to overseas banking institutions, the

same known to be under the mandate, and the

termination of same before finance was drawn down,

the Tribunal is satisfied that damage the like of

that alleged by S. Securities Limited could have

been suffered.

20. The relevant financial year of the taxpayer

company was one ending on 31 October 1980. In

its "detailed profit and loss account"

accompanying its return of income for that year,

the taxpayer showed a "nett profit" before income

tax of $920,559. Included in the total income

from which the net profit was derived was the

amount the subject of this application there

described as "compensation for damages claimed

$200,000". In the return the item was said to be

"exempt income".

COMPENSATION - FOR LOSS OF INCOME OR UNLIQUIDATED DAMAGES ONLY

7. The case advanced by the applicant, and it has the onus of proving such case, was that the sum of $200,000 received by it was by way of an agreed lump sum without any apportionment, the same in respect of unliquidated damages arising out of the peremptory termination of the mandate given to it by M.C. Limited. As a subsidiary aspect, it was also submitted that not only was there no apportionment, but there was not any evidence as to a part of the lump sum being on account of income.

8. Payment it was said was made in recognition of the right and/or capacity of the applicant to make a claim against M.C. Limited based on the matters that had been communicated by its Managing Director to executives of M.C. Limited and its then Deputy Chairman, discussed between them, and covered in general terms by the wording of the release. Such matters included expenses incurred, commission to which it would have been entitled, and actual and prospective harm caused to the reputation and goodwill, such as it then was, of S. Securities Limited.

9. There was not any evidence that the quantum of out-of-pocket expenses incurred had been communicated, to M.C. Limited although the fact of S. Securities Limited having incurred disbursements was a fact well known to M.C. Limited.

10. At the time of termination of the mandate, there was possibly not a present entitlement to any part of the commission, the funds negotiated to be received by M.C. Limited not then having been drawn down. Indeed, the entitlement to any commission would, in the ordinary course, accrue "after the deal was done".

11. There was not any evidence, as to the way in which the amount of $200,000 had been calculated, the same having been put, after all the discussions, by M.C. Limited to S. Securities Limited as a "first and last offer".

12. The evidence establishes that the amount of $200,000 was offered, accepted and paid:-

(i) by way of a lump sum;

(ii) in respect of unliquidated damages;

(iii) after a consideration of the various possible

heads of damage to business standing or

reputation;

(iv) as a "first and last offer" and "in release

of all claims" without any break up of

component parts being communicated between

the parties;

(v) without apportionment or any indication as to

the inclusion or non-inclusion of a fee

element.

13. Of what significance is it then to assessability that the amount was so offered, accepted and paid as in accord with (i) to (v) above.

14. Was the amount so paid, as is alleged by the Respondent Commissioner simply a revenue receipt and a transaction carried on or carried out in the ordinary course of the taxpayer's business without an element of goodwill being contained in the amount so settled? This is not a case, it was argued on behalf of the Commissioner, where goodwill was irrepairably damaged; the taxpayer still earnt its income in that year and was able to continue so doing.

15. In McLaurin v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1961] HCA 9; (1960-1961) 104 CLR 381, the High Court had reason to consider some of the above factors in their relevance to whether a receipt was of an exempt or non-exempt nature. Here payment had been made by a Commissioner of Railways and by way of a lump sum in full settlement of a claim made by McLaurin, arising out of fire damage caused to a pastoral property. Action had been taken against the Commissioner of Railways seeking recovery of damages on account of loss incurred, the same being particularised in information provided prior to the commencement of the action and as an appendage to the relevant declaration. It was after the commencement of proceedings and negotiations that an offer of settlement was made and in due course accepted. The Commissioner of Taxation contended that the sum so received was on account of income and assessable. The High Court did not agree.

16. The appellant's case in McLaurin was to the effect that the Commissioner of Railways was liable to him in damages, for not having taken reasonable care in relation to the fire, and on the principal stated in Rylands v Fletcher [1868] UKHL 1; (1968) LR 3 HL 330. The amount claimed in the relevant writ was Thirty Thousand Pounds (30,000 pounds). Before the issue of the writ McLaurin supplied particulars under a number of heads, of the damage sustained, and of the amounts which he intended to claim in respect of each head of damage. In due course the Commissioner indicated that he was prepared to offer a lump sum of Twelve Thousand Three Hundred and Fifty Pounds (12,350 pounds) together with costs, to settle the case. He gave no information as to how the sum offered had been arrived at and McLaurin, without knowing how it had been calculated, agreed to accept it. There would be little reason to doubt, although it was not so found, that the Commissioner in arriving at the lump sum had before him a list of items based upon the particulars originally supplied by McLaurin.

17. In the course of delivering their reasons for judgment and having noted that the offer that was made was not equated to a total of itemised amounts, but comprised of a single undissected amount, the court said:

". . . . . . In point of law it would plainly

be unsound to allow a determination of

the character of a receipt in the hands

of the recipient to be affected by a

consideration of the uncommunicated

reason which led the payer to agree to

pay it . . . . . ."

And continued,

". . . . . . It is true that in a proper case,

a single payment or receipt of a mixed

nature may be apportioned amongst the

several heads to which it relates and an

income or non-income nature attributed

to portions of it accordingly . . . . . . But

while it may be appropriate to follow

such a course where the payment or

receipt is in settlement of distinct

claims of which some at least are

liquidated or are otherwise

ascertainable by calculation . . . . . . It

cannot be appropriate where the payment

or receipt is in respect of a claim or

claims for unliquidated damages only and

is made or accepted under a compromise

which treats it as a single undissected

amount of damages. In such a case the

amount must be considered as a

whole . . . . . ."

18. With reference to the particular facts of the case it was said (392) in McLaurin that:

". . . . . . It is impossible to see a basis

in fact for the contention that

the 12,350 pounds which the appellant accepted

in settlement of his claims for every

kind of damage which the fire had caused

him was in truth of the nature of

compensation for loss of profits. The

fire caused him losses of sheep and

cattle and damage to wool of surviving

sheep; it put him to expense for the

eradication of rabbits to the incursion

of which the destruction of fences had

exposed the property; it also caused

losses of or damage to capital assets

such as pastures, fencing and buildings.

But the whole of the damage which it

did, whether covered by the appellant's

list of particulars or not, was

compensated for by the one entire sum;

It is simply not true that that sum took

the place in the appellant's hands of

assessable income . . . . . ."

19. It was contended on behalf of the Commissioner in the present application that if the receipt of the $200,000 could be categorised as being moneys received in the ordinary course of business then McLaurin's case would not apply. The item would clearly be of an income nature. The applicant however submitted that this was not the position on the factual situation as proved, including:

- the evidence, as to the matters conveyed on

behalf of the applicant to senior executives

of M.C. Limited;

- a clear communication of the loss of standing

goodwill and reputation that had would and/or

could be experienced;

- the wording of the release extending to "all

claims" that S. Securities Limited had or

might have had against M.C. Limited.

20. It was conceded on behalf of the Commissioner that the express presence of a goodwill item in the lump sum payment was not necessary; it was necessary for there to be a finding of there having been contained in the sum a goodwill ingredient and this because of what the parties thought they were settling.

21. The assessability or otherwise of an undissected lump sum payment, this time on account of an overpayment of permit fees, was considered in Allsop v The Commissioner of Taxation [1965] HCA 48; (1964) 113 CLR 341.

22. For a period of years Allsop had carried on business as an interstate carrier of goods by road for reward and had paid to the Transport Commissioner amounts as and by way of prescribed fees for permits pursuant to the State Transport (Co-ordination) Act. In due course it was held by the Privy Council that the provisions of the said State Transport (Co-ordination) Act requiring that application be made for a licence and all provisions consequential thereon were inapplicable to the appellant while operating its vehicles in the course of and for the purpose of interstate trade. The appellant sought then to recover the moneys that it had previously paid.

23. Prior to the action coming on for trial, negotiations took place between the carrier's solicitors and those of the Commissioner for Motor Transport and ultimately as a result of the negotiations, settlement was reached and a deed of release executed pursuant to which a lump sum was paid to the carrier's solicitors. The deed recited that action had been commenced, set forth the nature of the action, and after referring to the payments made, declared that the settlement was " . . . . . in full satisfaction and discharge of all actions, suits, claims and demands which the appellant might then have, or at any time thereafter have, against . . . . . "

24. The Commissioner of Taxation sought to bring to tax the lump sum settlement figure. The High Court held in answer to a cast stated that the lump sum was not assessable income, as neither the whole or any part of it could be attributed solely to a refund of the permit fees.

25. At 331 in their reasons for judgment Barwick CJ and Taylor J said,

"The primary question in the case is

whether the payment to the appellant of

the sum of 37,500 pounds constituted in the

circumstances a refund of part of the

fees which had been paid by him. In our

opinion it did not. There is sufficient

in the case to enable it to be said that

during the period in question there had

been unlawful interferences with the

appellant's vehicles and his business

operations and in respect of these

matters he had valid claims against the

Commissioner. His claim for a refund of

the fees paid by him was not admitted by

the Commissioner and the amount payable

upon the execution of the release was

the consideration not only for a release

of his claim against the Commissioner in

respect of the fees paid by him for

permits, but also for his release of all

claims for anything done in purported

pursuant of the State Transport

(Co-ordination) Act. There is no

suggestion that the release was illusory

or that it was not designed to operate

according to its tenor and that being

so, we do not regard the allegations

contained in paragraph 10(g) of the case

stated (viz, negotiations which took

place between the appellant's solicitors

and the solicitors for the Commissioner

for Motor Transport for the settlement

of the action which had been commenced

and the same being referable solely to

the recovery of permit fees) as relevant

matters for out consideration. But

even if they are taken into

consideration, they would not affect the

conclusion that the amount payable was

an entire sum paid by way of compromise

of all these claims, and no part of it

can be attributed solely to a refund of

the fees paid by the appellant for

permits. In these circumstances there

is no warrant for regarding the amount

paid by way of compromise of all these

claims, and no part of it can be

attributed solely to a refund of the

fees paid by the appellant for permits.

In these circumstances there is no

warrant for regarding the amount paid by

him or any part of it as a refund or

recoupment to him of any revenue

disbursement made in carrying on his

business or for regarding it on any

other ground as an income receipt."

26. At 352 Windeyer J, after agreeing with the answer proposed to be made by Barwick CJ and Taylor J to the question asked in the stated case said that the appellant "had commenced an action to recover from the Government of New South Wales, a sum of money as money had, and received to his use. He took the settlement money, not simply in satisfaction of his claim in that action, but in consideration of his release by deed of a variety of claims that he had or might be thought possibly to have against the Government." It did not appear, His Honour continued, from the material then before the court that the lump sum or any definite part of it, was computed, paid and received as a refund of particular amounts that had been paid by the appellant for road charges, and which had been allowed as deductions in the assessment of his taxable income.

27. The Commissioner of Taxation in the present application for review contends that McLaurin's case and the reasons therein given are not apposite to the situation propounded by the applicant in these proceedings. It was submitted that the principle enunciated in McLaurin's case is to the effect that where capital and revenue items are apparent but inexplicably bound up in a settlement lump sum figure, then unless an apportionment can be made, all of such sum is to be treated as capital. In McLaurin's case capital items and revenue items were clearly apparent from the particulars given. In the subject application, the money received was simply a revenue receipt, the transaction being one in the ordinary course of the business of S. Securities Limited. The evidence given was consistent with a claim being made for fees, not able to be earnt, and the same compromised. It was submitted that it could not be said that an element of goodwill, that is a capital item, was contained in the settlement figure. The deed of release placed emphasis on fees and not on loss of goodwill. There was not anything, it was further submitted, unusual about the efforts to raise the loan, except that they were undertaken relatively early in the applicant's business endeavours and related to a transaction of a substantial nature. The following matters, established on the evidence, were said to be significant and sufficient in the context of establishing that the transaction was simply one carried out in the ordinary course of the taxpayer's business. These were:

1. S. Securities Limited described itself as a

Merchant Bank, its business comprising the

negotiation of loans from financial institutions

to fund users for a fee. The receipt of such fees

was a major component of the Merchant Bank's

income.

2. In the course of such business the applicant

negotiated a loan to M.C. Limited, the same for a

fee, the latter to be a percentage of the loan so

negotiated.

3. The taxpayer claimed to be entitled to receive its

fee upon the mandate being terminated, the dispute

in due course being settled by execution of the

instrument of release, the same resulting in

payment of $200,000 to S. Securities Limited.

4. S. Securities Limited recorded the $200,000 as

income in its accounts.

28. Thus, it was said the money so received was a profit or gain made in the ordinary course of carrying on a business. Even be it that the profit or gain was entered into otherwise than in the ordinary course of business, this does not, it was further submitted, mean that the receipt was not income (Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Myer Emporium Limited 87 ATC 4363, 4366).

29. The "gain" of the $200,000 arose, it was said, from a transaction entered into by the taxpayer with the intention or purpose of making a profit or gain, and the same thence constituted income. The circumstances were such as to give rise "to the inference that the taxpayer's intention or purpose in entering into the transaction was to make a profit or gain" (Myer Emporium Limited (supra) 4367), and the profit or gain was hence income, be it that the transaction might be an extraordinary one judged by reference to the ordinary course of the taxpayer's business.

30. To negate the conclusions that were sought to be drawn by the applicant from McLaurin's case and Allsop's case, the Commissioner referred to what was said by Windeyer J. in Heavy Minerals Pty Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation [1966] HCA 60; (1966) 115 CLR 512. The matter there under consideration was as to whether sums received by the taxpayer as compensation arising on the cancellation of contracts by it of rutile were assessable income or receipts of a capital nature. The said sums had been received on the cancellation of forward selling contracts and on the giving of a release to the purchasers under the contracts from their various obligations. The moneys were received after negotiations had taken place. At 516 when dealing with a submission that the agreements the subject of the cancellation were in themselves a capital asset, and thence the money received of that nature Windeyer J referred to Commissioner of Taxation (NSW) v Meeks [1915] HCA 34; (1915) 19 CLR 568, where at 580 it was said:

" . . . . In my opinion damages received as

compensation for non-performance of a

business contract stand on the same

footing as the profits for the loss of

which the damages are paid. It cannot

therefore make any difference in

principle whether the money is actually

earned as profit, ascertained by

deducting expenses from receipts, or

paid as compensation for the loss of the

opportunity of earning that profit or in

the latter case, whether the amount of

compensation is assessed by a jury or by

mutual agreement."

31. The essential question in Meeks case was whether the sum paid upon the cancellation of a contract was income taxable under the law of New South Wales as profits from a business carried on in New South Wales. That it was income was not seemingly in question.

32. It was contended in Heavy Minerals Pty Limited (supra) that certain terms of the relevant contracts that had been cancelled were unusual, they creating a relationship not ordinarily so it was said, existing between a buyer and a seller. At 517 Windeyer J. said:

"Even if these contracts were such

that they seemed to ensure that the

taxpayer would have a secure market and

some regular customers, that would not

of itself make them part of the capital

of its business . . . . . The appellant

sought to liken the moneys which the

buyers paid to be released from their

contracts to a price received as a

consideration for going out of

business . . . . . but there is no analogy.

The taxpayer's business was rutile

mining and dealing in rutile. Its

capital assets were the mining lease and

the plant. After the contracts were

cancelled it still had these. It was

free to mine its rutile and to sell it

if it could find buyers; and it tried to

do so. The taxpayer was not put out of

business by the cancellation of its

overseas contracts . . . . . "

33. There are, in my opinion, various significant differences between the factual situation in the present application and that apparent in Meeks (supra) and Heavy Minerals Pty Limited (supra). There was in Meeks seemingly no issue as to the receipt of money being income, the issue being as to whether it was income taxable under the law of New South Wales as profits from a business there carried on. In Heavy Minerals Pty Limited, it was contended that the agreements that were there cancelled had been a capital asset and that money received on their cancellation was so coloured. The court did not accede to this submission being of the view that the capital of the taxpayer still remained in the mining lease and its plant. The benefit derived on the cancellation of the contracts was on the same footing as the profits that might have resulted from the carrying out of the obligations under the contracts. The Commissioner sought in the present application to thence say that the $200,000 was on such a same footing as the fee that might have been derived from M.C. Limited and that the business of S. Securities Limited continued and profits were earned in spite of the termination. This submission does not, as it seems to me, deal with the true nature of the termination agreement, the release and the character of the receipt in question, nor with the compromise expressed in the release of inter alia, all claims and demands.

34. In H.R. Sinclair and Son Pty Limited v Commissioner of Taxation (1964) [1966] HCA 39; 114 CLR 537 (a case where the taxpayer considering that he had paid by way of royalty to the Forests Commission amounts greater than he was obliged to pay, obtained a proportionate refund of such royalties, although less than it had alleged ought to be refunded), it was held that an amount received by way of a refund of royalty was a voluntary refund, not a sum receipt in compromise of past and future claims and that it formed part of a taxpayer's assessable income. Taylor J with whom Menzies J and Owen J concurred said at 843,

"The question therefore resolves

itself into a consideration of the

character of the receipt in question.

There was a suggestion that the payment

was made and received, not merely as a

voluntary refund, but by way of

compromise of past and future claims.

In my opinion however, there is no

substance in this suggestion, the

payment represents no more than a

voluntary refund of part of the

royalties which had been legally

exacted. The refund was made because

after protracted representations and

negotiations, the Commission conceded

that it had, from time to time,

incorrectly applied the formula so far

as the appellant was concerned and

decided that apparently in common

fairness, it should make the refund in

question. In these circumstances I can

see no reason why the amount should not

be regarded as properly taken into

account in determining the proceeds of

the appellant's business of the year in

which it was paid. Its attempts -

which in the end were successful - to

obtain a refund of amounts which it

contended had been exacted as a result

of the misapplication of the formula

were just as much an activity of the

business as would have been an attempt

to avoid an over-charge in the first

instance and the amount recovered in the

year ended under review must be taken to

have formed part of the appellant's

income of that year."

35. The question before the Tribunal in this application resolves itself into a consideration on the whole of the evidence of the character of the receipt of the subject $200,000; whether the amount received by way of settlement of the dispute between S. Securities Limited and M.C. Limited was part of the company's assessable income in the year for which it was received. Did the compromise have a legal effect upon the future rights and liabilities of S. Securities Limited and/or M.C. Limited? Was the payment made in settlemtn of a variety of claims, some relating to the past and some to the future? (H.R. Sinclair and Sons Pty Limited v Federal Commissioner of Taxation (supra) 546).

36. In my opinion the character of the receipt of the sum of $200,000 resulting from the peremptory termination of the mandate given by M.C. Limited to S. Securities Limited was one whereby for a lump sum payment all claims that S. Securities Limited had, or might have had, against M.C. Limited were resolved. The claims so resolved included a claim for the effect that the termination had or might have had upon the reputation, standing and goodwill of S. Securities limited and it may have included an amount in respect of expenses or disbursements incurred. There was however, no differentiation of the one item from the other; it was a lump sum settlement. The compromise so reached as evidenced by the release document was intended to have a legal effect, not only upon the rights of S. Securities Limited but on those of M.C. Limited. It was intended to preclude any right of action in the future that the one may have had against the other, arising out of the mandate that had been terminated. The payment made was in settlement of a number of claims as evidence by the matters put during the settlement discussions and negotiations.

37. The Tribunal considers that the facts of this application fall within the framework delineated in McLaurin and Allsop and is accordingly of the view, the lump sum not being capable of being apportioned amongst several heads to which it relates differentiating between income and non-income items, that the payment in the subject application and its receipt, is in respect of a claim for unliquidated damages made and accepted under a compromise which treated it as a single undissected amount of damages. Accordingly, the amount must be considered as a whole. It was received in full satisfaction and discharge of all actions, costs, claims, charges, and expenses whatsoever which S. Securities Limited had, or thereafter may have had, against M.C. Limited. The amount paid was an entire sum paid by way of compromise for all of such claims, no part of it being attributable solely to or identifiable with the fees that, but for the termination, might have been earnt under the mandate or expenses incidentally incurred.

38. Accordingly, in the circumstances of the application, there is not any warrant for regarding the amount received by S. Securities Limited or any part of it as a receipt by S. Securities Limited of a revenue nature and being one derived by it in the ordinary course of its business. Whilst the factual situation as itemised on behalf of the Commissioner and as earlier set forth in these reasons (pp 22, 23) if considered alone, might well have justified a conclusion that the money had so been received by way of a revenue receipt and in the ordinary course of the business of S. Securities Limited, the various other factors that have been identified draw the distinction rendering the sum received one that is not liable to tax and one that is not to be categorised as part of the assessable income of the taxpayer in respect of the relevant year.

DECISION

39. The Tribunal sets aside the objection decision in relation to the assessment for the year ended 31 October 1980 and allows the objection in full.
