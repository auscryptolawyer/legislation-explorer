Keith and Commissioner for Superannuation [1978] AATA 86 (20 December 1978)

Last Updated: 14 March 2007

Superannuation - Invalidity benefit - Cause of retirement - Decision made on erroneous report. Superannuation Act 1976 ss.3, 66, 154, 184.

Neil Keith

Acting Commissioner for Superannuation

AAT Decision No 126

No. 78/12019

Smithers J. (Deputy President), R. B. Hutchison, Dr. M. Glick (Members)

20 December 1978

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION

No. 78/12019

RE: NEIL KEITH

AND: ACTING COMMISSIONER FOR SUPERANNUATION

DECISION

The Tribunal decides to review the decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation dated 25 May 1978, orders that it be set aside and that s 66(1) of the Superannuation Act 1976 should apply to the benefits payable to the applicant on retirement.

Dated the 20th day of December 1978





REASONS FOR DECISION

This is an application pursuant to s.154 (6) of the Superannuation Act 1976 (the Act) by Neil Keith for review by the Administrative Appeals Tribunal of a decision made by the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation dated 25 May 1978, pursuant to s.66(2) of the Act. That sub-section provides that:-

"Subject to sub-sections (3) and (4) of this section and section 79 where:

(a)a person ceases to be an eligible employee by reason of retirement on the ground of invalidity before attaining his maximum retiring age; and
(b)there was in force in respect of the person, immediately before the person's retirement, a benefit classification certificate;
(c)the Commissioner is of the opinion that the incapacity which was the ground for his retirement was caused, or was substantially contributed to, by a physical or mental condition or conditions specified in the certificate or by a physical or mental condition connected with such a condition or such conditions."

then certain entitlements and consequences flow.

The applicant is a person who prior to his retirement on the ground of invalidity on 29 August 1977 was an eligible person within the meaning of s.66(2)(a). He retired before attaining his maximum retiring age. Immediately before his retirement there was deemed to be in force in respect of the applicant a benefit classification certificate. According to that certificate, dated 4 April 1977 but reflecting the situation originally established by the recommendation of the medical officer who in 1962 dealt with the applicant's original application for acceptance as an employee with superannuation benefits, the opinion of the delegate of the Commissioner for Superannuation was that Mr. Keith was not likely to continue as an eligible employee until he obtained his maximum retiring age by reason of a physical or mental condition described as "history of anxiety state". Sections3 (definition of existing contributor) and 184 (medical benefit classification certificates) of the Act explain the emergence of the certificate as at 4 April 1977 rather than in 1962 which is the real date of establishment of the facts to which it relates.

The reasons for the decision of 25 May 1978 were , that:-

"(1) following an examination of the medical evidence of 9 November 1962 and the medical standards applicable, I am satisfied that the reason why Mr. Keith was not accepted as a contributor to the fund under part III of the Superannuation Act 1922 was due to a history of Anxiety State; and

(2) following an examination of the medical evidence of 17 February 1977 and the opinion of the Department of Health that the cause of retirement was ‘Anxiety Neurosis with Arthritis as a contributing factor’. "

Reference to the report of the Commonwealth Medical Officer, Melbourne dated 17 February 1977 discloses that in his opinion the applicant was then suffering from neurosis and anxiety arising from old war injuries. It was also stated in the report that he was highly strung, that the prognosis was poor, and that he was taking certain drugs. It was noted that another disability suffered by the applicant was arthritis with lack of co-ordination. The contents of the report other than the above are not presently relevant.

If the report had accurately stated the true condition of the applicant's health the decision of the Acting Commissioner could not have been questioned. But evidence called before this Tribunal establishes that the report did not accurately state the position.

The applicant was engaged by the CSIRO at Melbourne as a plumber onwards from 1962. He was, as from 1968, personally known to Mr. William Morris Balding who in August last was Secretary of the Division of Mineral Chemistry of the CSIRO. It was that division in which, in recent times, the applicant was employed. Mr. Balding explained that he knew of the applicant considerably earlier than 1968 when as a senior staff officer of CSIRO the applicant's personal file had been familiar to him. Mr. Balding said that since 1968 the applicant was actually a member of his divisional staff. He had risen from his initial employment as a plumber to laboratory craftsman, then to senior laboratory craftsman class 3, which was his classification at the time of retirement. Mr. Balding said that especially since the applicant joined his division he had seen the applicant practically every working day. Far from observing any evidence of neurosis or irritability or bad temper he knew the applicant as a man of particularly placid and pleasant temperament. His work was responsible work. He was the only certified plumber at his place of work. He was responsible for supervising and approving the work of others including sub-contractors brought in for special installations. Much of this work involved nervous strain but no evidence of anxiety or tension had been manifested in the applicant's conduct or demeanour. He was regarded as a first class workman. But over the years especially the last two years the applicant had experienced difficulty through arthritic condition in the right shoulder and hand. He had difficulty in handling certain tools which he used in his work. His complaints in these respects increased as time went on. There was no evidence to contradict or qualify what Mr. Balding said. He was a most responsible and convincing witness. It is a fair inference from his evidence that the applicant's inability to continue to work had nothing to do with anxiety or neurosis. It was a plain case of physical disability due to arthritis.

The evidence of one Mr. Jomartz, an administrative officer of the Mineral Chemistry Division of CSIRO, also a convincing witness, was to the effect that he had known the applicant in his employment with CSIRO during the last seven years. He had known him quite well and had observed him in his duty from day to day. He said that in February 1977 the applicant was referred to the Commonwealth Medical officer because of a medical certificate indicating a disabling injury. He said that it was quite evident to him, Mr. Jomartz, that the reason for the applicant's failure to perform his duties satisfactorily was "the noticeable effect of his arthritis on his work".

In addition on 23 November 1978 Dr. Cosgriff gave evidence to the Tribunal. He and his partner had been the applicant's doctors for fifteen years and he Dr. Cosgriff had seen much of him throughout that period. He said that in the early part of his association with the applicant the latter did suffer from a significant degree of anxiety or neurosis. He said however, that over the last seven or eight years:-

"...we really have not seen him for what could even be remotely connected with an anxiety state but we have been seeing more of him because of pain particularly affecting his right shoulder which was making it increasingly difficult for him to earn a living. Over the two or three years - perhaps two years - prior to 1977 when I wrote the initial certificate recommending his retirement we have discussed whether or not he should keep going to work or not. It was on a purely physical plane becoming increasingly difficult for him to cope.

By-mid 1977 he had reached the stage that really he was not coping and I wrote a note then recommending early retirement. In that note I put the only ground - and I believe this to be true - was arthritis."

He added:-

"Looking through my notes here there are plenty of prescriptions being written for arthritis. This is over the last eight years and there isn't one written for anxiety state."

It is a proper inference from the evidence of Dr. Cosgriff that the inability of the applicant to continue to work beyond mid-1977 was not related to anxiety or neurosis but was due only to his arthritis. There had been no evidence of an anxiety state during the previous seven or eight years.

As mentioned above a medical report of 17 February 1977 contained statements that the applicant was then suffering from anxiety. The officer who made the report gave evidence. Suffice it to say that he did not desire to support those contents of his report as reflecting an opinion based upon an actual personal medical examination conducted by himself with that degree of intensity which would have been necessary to sustain a personal assessment on such a matter. He explained the contents of the report as reflecting his reference to many medical reports which were before him which did refer to a condition of anxiety for which at one period there had been considerable treatment and to records of a rather uninformative nature about repatriation treatment in more recent years. On 15 September 1978 the officer had had an opportunity to consider the views of Dr. Cosgriff and other materials and he had sought by letter to correct the impression conveyed by the earlier certificate. He wrote, "I would -wish to restate the retirement disability as arthritis due to war service producing a degree of anxiety".

It follows that although the decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation was reasonably made on the materials before him the facts revealed in the evidence before the Tribunal indicate that those materials did not accurately present the situation.

In the result it is clear that the opinion that the disability of the applicant which was the ground for his retirement was caused or substantially contributed to by a physical or mental condition specified in the certificate or by a physical or mental condition connected-with such a condition cannot be supported.

Accordingly the Tribunal decides to review the decision of the Acting Commissioner for Superannuation dated 25 May 1978 and being the subject of this application and orders that it be set aside and that the retirement benefits of the applicant be those appropriate to retirement by him on grounds of invalidity due to the onset of an arthritic condition.
