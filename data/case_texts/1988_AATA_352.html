INCOME TAX - Division 7 Tax - sufficient distribution -whether income derived from personal exertion - distribution by trustees to beneficiaries - beneficiaries having obligation to indemnify trustee.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s. 6(1), Division 7.

AAT Decision No 4806

Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

REASONS FOR DECISION

2 December 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. This reference arises out of a confusingly presented complex of arrangements involving individuals; partnerships; companies; trusts; and (it is said) "nominees". Prior to 1 July 1983 five individuals ("THE FIVE") - all qualified as chartered accountants - carried on practice in partnership as chartered accountants as "FIRM ONE". In association with the practice, FIRM ONE had established a company ("ADMIN-CO") to employ the staff of the practice. In that period ADMIN-CO was wholly under the control of THE FIVE. One feature of the practice of FIRM ONE was a long-standing, undocumented, but highly remunerative arrangement whereby FIRM ONE provided services to an independent organization ("PACTOLUS"). One of the members of FIRM ONE wished to retire from practice as a chartered accountant but was keen to retain his interest in, and the monetary benefits flowing from, the arrangements with PACTOLUS. As a result, with effect from 1 July 1983 the other four partners ("THE FOUR") took over and continued to operate the professional practice of FIRM ONE, and THE FIVE continued in partnership as FIRM TWO retaining their claim to the benefits of the arrangement with PACTOLUS. One by-product was that THE FIVE set about formalizing the arrangement with PACTOLUS. That was ultimately to be achieved by an agreement in writing entered into in May 1984 with purported effect from 1 July 1983. But, as at 1 July 1983, that was a matter for the future. For the time being the benefit of the arrangements with PACTOLUS continued to flow through in the interests of THE FIVE.

2. Then, on 30 September 1983, a lady whom I shall refer to as "MRS. JOHN", executed a Deed of Trust. She was the wife of JOHN, one of THE FIVE. By the terms of the Deed, MRS. JOHN appointed a company ("MANAGEMENT-CO") to be Trustee of a settlement of $550 to be held on the terms of the Trust Deed. No information was provided as to the date of incorporation of or as to the Memorandum of Association or Articles of Association of MANAGEMENT-CO. The trust fund was to be divided into $1 units and, by force of the Deed, those units came to be held by persons identified by the Deed and associated with THE FIVE. I shall refer to the trust as the "A MANAGEMENT TRUST". 138 of those units came to be held by the Applicant Company - "JOHN-CO".

3. By 30 November 1983 FIRM TWO proposed in writing to PACTOLUS that the management arrangement should be formally documented. It explained to PACTOLUS that "the staff who are wholly engaged in the (service of PACTOLUS) are employed through the company (ADMIN-CO). This company is wholly owned by the partners of (FIRM TWO)" of which it was then said that the partners were THE FIVE and MANAGEMENT-CO. The proposal stated that MANAGEMENT-CO:-

"is the Trustee of a Trust the beneficiaries in which are the family interests of the respective partners."

4. Next day THE FIVE met in their capacity as Directors of MANAGEMENT-CO. The decisions then made were minuted in the following terms:-

PARTNERSHIP: Resolved that the Company as Trustee of the A MANAGEMENT TRUST enter into a Partnership Agreement this day with each of (THE FIVE) for the management of (PACTOLUS) and that the seal of the Company be affixed thereto pending completion of an agreement with (PACTOLUS).

  1. MANAGEMENT TRUST: Resolved that the Trust invite applications from the existing unit holders for a further 550,000 $1 units to provide funds to enable the acquisition of an interest in the partnership of (FIRM TWO) for their benefit.

5. On the same day two agreements were entered into. By the first, MANAGEMENT-CO agreed with JOHN-CO and the other Trust beneficiaries that each of the beneficiaries would apply for additional units; and that

"2, The Trustee will complete the acquisition of the equitable interest in the management contract for the benefit of the Beneficiaries jointly."

"3. The Trustee will hold the proceeds from the said contract jointly on behalf of the Beneficiaries and for their benefit in proportion to their interest in the Trust and will arrange for the proceeds to be distributed progressively at their discretion."

"4. The Beneficiaries jointly agree to indemnify the Trustee against any liabilities which may arise in carrying on the Management Contract and undertake to apply for additional units or reimburse proceeds which have been distributed without provision for such liabilities as the Trustee advises within seven days of such advise and evidence being given."

I am satisfied that the Beneficiaries did apply for and acquire units to bring the issued units to 550,550 units, held in the same proportions as the original 550 units had been held.

6. By the second agreement entered into that day THE FIVE were severally named as parties of the first to fifth parts; MANAGEMENT-CO "(as trustee for the beneficiaries of the A MANAGEMENT TRUST, hereinafter described jointly as the beneficiaries)" of the sixth part; and ADMIN-CO of the seventh part. All seven parties were to be "hereinafter described as 'the Partners'." It was acknowleged that the first five-named parties (THE FIVE) had, since 1 July 1983, carried on business in partnership as FIRM TWO; that certain negotiations for the introduction of a new partner had not been finalised; and that

"1.3 ... the Partners have agreed to introduce (MANAGEMENT-CO), as Trustee for the beneficiaries of the (A......... MANAGEMENT TRUST) as an additional party and to enter into a new formal agreement with (PACTOLUS) to cover the continuing management arrangements." The agreement went on to provide (inter alia) "2.1 The Partners will continue to manage the affairs of (PACTOLUS) in accordance with previously existing arrangements under the agreement dated 1 July 1983." "2.2 In accordance with those arrangements the fee payable by (PACTOLUS) for management shall be paid to (ADMIN-CO) as 'Administration Manager' to pay therefrom the wages of all staff engaged in the management of (PACTOLUS) and all expenses related thereto and to apply any surplus in accordance with the subsequent clauses of this agreement."

"2.3 (ADMIN-CO) shall provide for such fees as may be agreed, to be paid to (FIRM ONE), and they shall make themselves available as may be required to advise and consult in the management of PACTOLUS to the extent that such services can be provided in their professional capacity."

"2.4 Each of the Partners shall ensure that they or such other of the beneficiaries of the (A........ MANAGEMENT TRUST) shall continue to provide the non-professional management services which are required in the conduct of the agreement with (PACTOLUS)."

"2.5 (ADMIN-CO) shall provide any physical assets required for the management of (PACTOLUS) and shall be entitled to retain from any surplus remaining an allowance to cover the capital employed by it in providing those assets and working capital required and shall pay the whole of any remaining surplus of the fee received from (PACTOLUS) to or for benefit of the remaining partner (MANAGEMENT-CO), as Trustee for the beneficiaries of the (A MANAGEMENT TRUST) who shall be required to indemnify the Trustee in respect of its obligations and performance under this agreement."

Subsequent provisions provided for the duration of the agreement and for winding up of the arrangement in the events specified.

7. Subsequently, an agreement in writing made the eighth day of May 1984 was entered into between PACTOLUS of the one part and THE FIVE and MANAGEMENT-CO of the other part. THE FIVE and MANAGEMENT-CO were described by one word: "A.....", the word which particularly distinguished the full names of ADMIN-CO and MANAGEMENT-CO - a circumstance which was to cause confusion. The agreement made express provision (inter alia) as follows:-

"1. APPOINTMENT OF MANAGER. PACTOLUS hereby appoints A...... the Manager and A .......hereby agrees to take up the appointment of Manager on the terms and conditions hereafter appearing.

2. DURATION. The appointment of A as manager

under this agreement commenced on 1st July 1983 and shall continue until 30 June 1988 and thereafter from year to year subject to the determination as hereinafter provided."

  1. ... (defined the duties of A ).
  2. Provided for the remuneration of A

8. Despite the assertion in the latter agreement that it took effect in favour of A on 1st July 1983, there has been no suggestion in these proceedings that it took effect any-earlier than 1st December 1983. What subsequently occurred must be inferred from such evidence as was presented commencing with the first communication to the Commissioner in relation to these matters.

9. On 16 August 1984 JOHN completed and FIRM ONE lodged with the Commissioner a Trust Estate form of income tax return for the year ended 30 June 1984, presented in the name of "The A Management Trust". The return was described as a "First Return". The Balance Sheet which was presented as the Trust Balance Sheet showed an issued capital of $550,550 represented by "equitable interest in management contract (PACTOLUS) at cost $550,000" and by formation expenses and stamp duty $550. The Profit and Loss account which related to the period only from 1 December 1983 to 30 June 1984 was simplicity itself. It recorded:-

"Fees Received":

Net Surplus from (PACTOLUS) Management Contract $499,141."

That profit was shown as having been wholly distributed to unit holders in proportion to their holdings of units. JOHN-CO received $125,239. Notes to the Balance Sheet recorded that the trust had been formed on 30 September 1983 by MRS. JOHN; that on 1 December 1983 the unit holders had been invited to apply for additional units; that the proceeds were then applied to acquire from the partners in FIRM TWO their equitable interest in the management contract with PACTOLUS.

10. On 3 August 1984 JOHN-CO lodged with the Commissioner its return of income to 30 June 1984. Its Balance Sheet accounted for shareholders funds of $4,724,968 including investments in the A .......MANAGEMENT TRUST $138,000; shares in a subsidiary TRY-CO $71,256 and in other subsidiaries $3,502,441. By its Profit and Loss account it disclosed the receipt of -

Dividends $31,092

The amount of tax was assessed at the rate of 50%, thereby giving rise to a tax liability upon the assessment of $17,724.50.

13. The objection of JOHN-CO is that the Commissioner has fallen into error: the error being that the Commissioner has treated the "Trust distribution" recorded by JOHN-CO in its own return of income - consistently with the recorded return of A Management Trust - as being a distribution to JOHN-CO by the Trustee by virtue of its Trust obligations as Trustee. JOHN - who gave evidence - sought to argue that, when the agreements placed in evidence were properly understood, and when the claim of JOHN-CO to participate in the net profits generated by dealings with PACTOLUS was appreciated, the correct view of the matter is that what was derived by JOHN-CO was derived by way of personal-exertion income.

14. The case for the Commissioner is that the disputed moneys came to JOHN-CO as, and only as, a beneficiary under the Trust. It follows, according to the submission of the Commissioner's representative, that the disputed moneys did not constitute "income from personal exertion" and, therefore by reason of the definition in s6(1) of the Act, it constituted "income from property". Accordingly, it was argued, a retention allowance of only 10% was allowable, rather than the 80% which would have been allowed had the income constituted income from personal exertion. In support of those contentions, the Commissioner's representative pointed to:-

(a) the actions of the Trustee which showed that it was deriving income; that it derived that income in its character as a Trustee; that it had no beneficial entitlement to that income; and (by its income tax return) that it had distributed that income to beneficiaries in the unit trust in that capacity; and

(b) the action of JOHN-CO in acknowledging its interest in the trust; and its entitlement to and derivation of the income distributed by the Trustee.

15. The case for the Applicant was sought to be put on a basis that:-

(a) what was paid by PACTOLUS was paid pursuant to the Management Agreement; that by its terms the Trustee and THE FIVE were together entitled to the income; but that

(b) as between the Trustee and THE FIVE, THE FIVE were entitled to nothing and that the Trustee held its entitlement for the Beneficiaries - as their "nominee"; and that

(c) as the Beneficiaries, upon subscribing for additional units, had each agreed to indemnify the Trustee in respect of their due proportion and share of all liabilities that occurred in relation to the conduct of the Management Contract on their behalf, and as personal services had been rendered as needed under the terms of the Management Agreement itself, that therefore the moneys received by JOHN-CO constituted income from personal exertion in its hands.

16. As the evidence stands before me, the argument fails on many fronts. In my view, an undertaking by a beneficiary to indemnify a trustee in relation to liabilities arising out of a proposed course of conduct by the trustee does not alter the nature of the interest of the beneficiary in the Trust. Further, as the evidence stands, the beneficiaries were not called on to provide any such indemnification. As to the provision of services, the only evidence I have is that JOHN, as an employee of TRY-CO - a wholly owned subsidiary of JOHN-CO provided some services in relation to the Management Agreement. But that, in my view, did not constitute the provision of services by JOHN-CO or constitute the distribution by the Trustee as a reward for such services as JOHN provided under the Management Contract in the course of his employment by TRY-CO. As a result, as the evidence stands before me, I am not persuaded that JOHN-CO, so far as is relevant, was anything other than a beneficiary in the Trust; that JOHN-CO had any claim on MANAGEMENT-CO other than as a Trustee; that JOHN-CO assumed any liabilities to the Trustee such as to generate a right to remuneration, or so as to constitute the distribution in question as a remuneration or reward for any such undertaking; that JOHN-CO rendered any services to the Trustee or that the moneys distributed by the Trustees constituted in any way a reward or remuneration for any services rendered to it by or on account of JOHN-CO.

17. In short, notwithstanding a close analysis of such documents as were presented in evidence, and notwithstanding assumptions that other agreements referred to in the documents produced before me were implemented, I am not persuaded that the income in dispute derived by JOHN-CO is to be sourced in anything other than the Trust Deed and the entitlements of JOHN-CO as a beneficiary under that Trust Deed.. As each case must be determined in light of the facts relevant as established at the hearing, it is not appropriate to speculate as to what might have been. For those reasons, the determination of the Tribunal will be that the decision of the Commissioner upon the objection under review shall be upheld.
