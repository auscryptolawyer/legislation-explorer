Re Taxation Appeals [1988] AATA 255 (19 August 1988)

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Re: TAXATION APPEALS

No. N87/162

AAT No. 4565

Veterans' Affairs

COURT

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

VETERANS' AFFAIRS DIVISION

Hartigan J. (President)

CATCHWORDS

Veterans' Affairs- disability pension- whether "depressive reaction" was a war-caused disease- reasonable hypothesis linking war-caused eczema with depressive state- whether chain reaction in fact caused by motor vehicle accident.

Webb v. Repatriation Commission (Full Federal Court of Australia, unreported, 30 March, 1988).

Re Dalton and Repatriation Commission (unreported, 4413, 19 May, 1988).

HEARING

BRISBANE

19:8:1988

ORDER

The decision under review be set aside.

The respondent is liable to pay the applicant a disability pension pursuant to s.13 of the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986 in respect of incapacity arising from the condition "depressive reaction".

That the pension is payable from 20 June, 1984.

DECISION

The applicant, Walter Cope, seeks the review of a decision of the Veterans' Review Board, dated 17th November 1986. The Veterans' Review Board affirmed the decision of the Repatriation Board given on 24th August 1971 which decided that the applicant's claim, that "depressive reaction" be added to the applicant's list of accepted disabilities, be rejected.

2. Pursuant to the provisions of ss. 43 and 44 of the Veterans' Entitlements (Transitional Provisions and Consequential Amendments) Act 1986 the Tribunal must consider this claim as if it were made under the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986.

3. There was no dispute before the Tribunal that Mr. Cope had in fact rendered "operational service" within the meaning of s.6 of the Veteran's Entitlements Act.

4. The applicant's claim is put in the alternative. First, it is said that the incapacity, namely depressive reaction, was due to a disease that would not have been contracted but for war service (s.9(2) of the Veterans' Entitlements Act). Secondly, the disease resulted from an occurrence that happened while the applicant was on operational service (s.9(1)(a) of the Veterans' Entitlements Act) or, it is then said, that the disease arose out of or was attributable to eligible war service (s.9(1)(b) of the Veterans' Entitlements Act). By virtue of s.7(1)(a) of the Act a person who has rendered operational service has also rendered eligible war service.

5. The applicant's case was that his depressive reaction arose as a result of his dermatitis, which was an accepted disability. Counsel for the applicant identified the issue in dispute as being whether the condition of depression, suffered by the applicant, antedated a severe car accident in which the applicant was involved in 1962. If it did, Counsel would then concede that the accident aggravated the depressive state, but, as it already existed this was no bar to the applicant succeeding in his claim for a pension.

6. The applicant gave evidence that he contracted a skin complaint which is attributed to his overseas service and is an accepted disability. He said from about 1950 onwards he was in and out of hospital quite a lot for the treatment of his eczema. I accept that he saw doctors, received treatment and, indeed, was hospitalised on occasions. He could not remember how many times he saw doctors, nor the names of the doctors that he saw during this period. Nor could he recall the treatment he received. When in hospital he said he felt very depressed. He had "quite horrible hands" as he described them. In fact, he said, they were so horrible that on one occasion the doctors put a photograph of them on display for medical students to view. I understood this to have been a severe case of eczema.

7. The skin disease troubled the applicant on and off over many years and he thought that when it was bad his disposition was at its worst. He gave some examples. In 1950 he was in hospital when his wife was 21 and he was very unhappy. From my assessment of the witness I understood him to be saying that he was more than just temporarily miserable. During the 1950s, when building his house he got so upset he threw all his tools away. This was given by him as a general example of his feelings. Again by way of example, he also said, that if someone dropped something in the house his wife had to "pull him off the ceiling". I accept his simple and unsophisticated evidence.

8. The applicant's wife gave evidence of his moods. They have been married for about forty years. She said that his moods arose from about 1950 and continued from that time. She said he became quiet and said he just wanted to be left alone. She said that he had no problem to which he would admit. Mrs. Cope did not know if an aggravation of the skin problem preceded the emotional state about which she spoke or not, but, she said that they always occurred together. Mrs. Cope said that the applicant was self conscious, so far as his skin disease was concerned, and he would wear long shirts; for example, even on the beach. She said that he preferred working the night shift on the railways so people would not see his arms. I would note at this stage that I accept the evidence of Mr. and Mrs. Cope as being truthful and accurate. Where Mr. Cope was not able to remember details I am satisfied that it was only because of the passage of the years. After the motor vehicle accident in 1962, to which I shall refer in detail below, the evidence before me was that the applicant became very aggressive and very short-tempered. He would go off on his own and he would speak to no one. This continued for some 12 years. After the accident I accept that he became worse, in that, his moodiness was enhanced and he became more aggressive. I accept the evidence of Mrs. Cope on this. This settled in time to something like his pre-accident condition (Transcript p 29). In recent times, his skin problem has settled down apart from outbreaks which occur from time to time. I accept that, as Mrs. Cope said, his moods now are like those prior to the accident. Mr. Callioni, who appeared for the respondent, submitted that the evidence demonstrated that the depressed state suffered by the applicant, related to the car accident and to Mr. Cope's personal qualities.

9. On behalf of the respondent Dr. Robbie was called. Dr. Robbie saw the applicant in November, 1980. He gave a report which was admitted as exhibit 2. Dr. Robbie is a psychiatrist. In that report of the 17th November, 1980 he concluded that the applicant suffered from a mild chronic anxious-depressive condition. In that report the Doctor expresses a view that the applicant has endogenous eczema which has been influenced in its course by nervous problems and that the nervous problems have been influenced in their course by the eczema. The Doctor stated that it would not be unreasonable to assume that the man's eczema has contributed substantially to the man's emotional state.

10. At the time of that report Dr. Robbie was unaware of earlier medical reports which revealed the occurrence of the motor vehicle accident in 1960 and the sequelae suffered by the applicant as a result of that accident. From the evidence it appeared that Mr. Cope was sitting in the back seat of a car at the time of the accident and upon impact his head collided with that of another passenger. Mr.Cope said he was in a coma for about 14 days and immediately after the accident an operation was performed to remove blood clots from his brain. The medical opinion did not favour coma but accepted amnesia as a likely result. In a subsequent report given on 14 April, 1981 (p 143 of Exhibit 1) Dr. Robbie raises the question that the depressive reaction may be related to the motor vehicle accident and the head injury. In another report of 3rd June, 1981 Dr. Robbie refers to a report of Dr. Rowe, a psychiatrist, made on 1st July, 1971. Dr. Robbie's inference from Dr. Rowe's report was that it unequivocally suggested to Dr. Robbie that Mr. Cope's emotional deterioration was the result of the motor vehicle accident. Dr. Robbie went on to say in that report that "the dermatitis pales by comparison, and I am in little doubt he does not have a valid claim of a chain reaction nature." The last comment may have been in relation to a claim that the nervous state had caused a heart attack. I should explain that at an earlier time the applicant had attempted unsuccessfully to have a heart condition included as an accepted disability. In a report of 14 April, 1981 (p 143 of Exhibit 1) Dr. Robbie was dealing with the suggested chain reaction heart attack. In the report of (p 144 of Exhibit 1) it is not clear to me whether the Doctor refers to the heart attack chain reaction or the nervous reaction dermatitis link.

11. Dr. Robbie, when called to give evidence, described depression as follows: "a fairly consistent unhappiness that is regarded as pathological rather than just a temporary reaction. That implies that it is going to last more than a short time, it is not going to last for a couple of days, it is not going to last for weeks or sometimes a good deal longer. It also implies that the mood is fairly consistent, although, in the less serious forms of depression the mood can fluctuate quite a bit and it is possible to be reasonably cheerful for some of the time, but, depressed for most of the time." (Transcript p 34).

12. The Doctor also said that at the time of his report in November, 1980 he considered that the chronic anxious-depressive condition was mild. He went on to say in his evidence that he would assess it as twenty percent which indicates, according to Dr. Robbie, that the condition was a minimal one. Mr. Callioni, for the respondent, outlined to Dr. Robbie the nature of the motor vehicle accident in which the applicant had been injured. He also outlined some of the sequelae of the accident. This appears at pp 36 and 37 of the transcript. Dr. Robbie (Transcript p 40), said that the accident, with its effects, was a dramatic event involving the brain as it did and that the accident seemed to him to be the origin of the dissatisfactions (the word used by Dr. Robbie) that he saw in 1980. In coming to that view Dr. Robbie had adverted to the earlier reports of a Dr. Collins and Dr. Rowe. Dr. Robbie came to a view which he expressed (at p 46). This view was that the emotional condition or depressive state was caused by the accident and Mr. Cope's head injury. The Doctor also said that he "did not see a strong relationship between his emotional state and his skin conditions."

13. The Doctor expressed a further view (at p 47-49) in these terms:

"The underlying pathological entity. The concept

that his skin condition might have contributed

to this emotional state, I would allow that that is

reasonable; I would allow it is rational, but I

think in this particular case, the relationship is

very small, well, small. I have gone from minimal

to small."

There is another important passage which occurred in cross-examination (at p 50):

"Assuming that it (depression) was present prior to

the accident, is it reasonable to assume that the

accident could have aggravated it and caused it to

become more severe? ....Yes, of course."

14. During the course of cross-examination Dr. Robbie's attention was drawn to a note in the documents forming Exhibit 1. This occurred at p 53 of the transcript. The note refers to a mention of a depressive reaction in 1971 which at that stage had existed for 20 years. The Doctor said this:

"I do not believe it accords with the flavour of my

examination of him and I do not know where that

came from, but one has to accept it is written

there and it is interesting and if that is accepted

as a valid statement then a depressive condition

precedes the accident."

15. It is interesting that this note on the file dated 1971 accords with the evidence of the applicant's wife as to the onset of the condition or state which she described in her evidence as commencing in the 1950s.

16. Dr. Robbie agreed that if a skin rash is of sufficient severity then such a rash can cause emotional problems (at p 54-5).

17. Dr. Robbie certainly inclined to the view that the motor accident and its results caused the depressive condition. He pointed to the recorded personality change at that time. He also pointed to what he considered to be a lack of strong association between deterioration of the eczema and emotional deterioration. He also raised the question of the accident and its results leading Mr. Cope to be more concerned with his eczema problem. He also raised other matters set out at p 56 of the transcript. Much of Dr. Robbie's opinion was based on a report of Dr. Rowe (part of Exhibit 1). However, Dr. Robbie considered that Dr. Rowe's report did not eliminate the fact that a nervous condition could have been present before the accident.

18. During re-examination I consider that it became plain that the Doctor's opinion was based on Dr. Rowe's reports and that caused some problems. This passage occurred:-

"MR. CALLIONI: Doctor, just a couple of questions.

On the basis of the information that you have

available to you, is it your opinion that the

clinical state which Dr. Rowe observed in 1963 was

present before the motor vehicle accident? --- How

should I know? I have to accept what Dr. Rowe has

written. He has given a very dramatic event with

definite pathology, a decided personality change on

the statement of his wife and his presentation to

Dr. Rowe accorded with his presentation to me

later. By reductionism you connect the two.

Obviously there are other explanations but you have

to use reductionism in the answer or you are in

awful trouble."

19. This evidence leads me to conclude that vital factual questions have to be determined by this Tribunal. If the Tribunal finds that the evidence of Mr. and Mrs. Cope reveal the existence in fact of a long standing, that is, chronic anxious-depressive condition which existed in tandem with very bad eczema then the medical opinion exists which justifies the Tribunal in finding that a cause, indeed a likely cause, of the depressive state is the war-caused eczema. If that is so a finding that the pre-existing emotional or depressive state was aggravated by the accident would naturally flow but this would not disturb the basic finding as to the cause.

20. Dr. Gertler was called on behalf of the applicant. Dr. Gertler is a psychiatrist. The Doctor had produced a report dated 28 September, 1987. That report became Exhibit 3 before the Tribunal. Dr. Gertler said in evidence (at p 63) that he was still of the same opinion as he had expressed in his report. In that report he said:

"As such, I feel that there is a possibility that

Mr. Cope's war service, and his accepted

disabilities related to that, has contributed to

the development of his recurrent depressive

reactions."

21. From his oral testimony it appears that Dr Gertler was not aware of Mrs. Cope's evidence as to the pre-motor vehicle accident state of the applicant (p 64) at the time he wrote his report.

22. Dr. Gertler in his oral evidence did say that:

"The accident did not cause the depressions; the

accident facilitated them. If he would not have

had the accident, he might have carried on being

moody until maybe some other stress, but I think

that at some stage in his life, he would have been

unable to cope with the dermatitis and would have

become quite depressed as he did."

23. I understand his evidence to mean that a likely cause of the depression was the eczema but that the stress of the accident made him unable to cope with the dermatitis and this enhanced the extent of the depression after the motor vehicle accident. His inability to emotionally cope with the eczema would have been detrimentally affected at some time by some other stress if the accident had not occurred. In that sense the accident, which the Doctor said did not cause the depression, aggravated the symptoms. On that view the likely cause of the depressive state is the war caused eczema.

The Doctor's evidence can be summarised in a passage which occurs at p 68:

"And that fits in with your view that it was a

breakdown in the coping facilities that he had

available to him?....Yes, as a result of the

accident."

24. I find that the exzema was a cause of the depression because after 1962 the applicant was by then unable to cope with his eczema. I did put some of Mrs. Cope's account to Dr. Gertler in a passage at p 68-9. Dr. Gertler's comment was:

"Well, that he was having some difficulty handling

the dermatitis even then. I mean, depression is a

word that is used - when I use depression, I use it

in a clinical sense, and by that, I mean it is a

severe illness."

25. A further passage was put to Dr. Gertler at p 68 but the doctor's reply focussed only on the question of aggression and does not come to grips with the general question posed. In the event the question as to whether there existed a depressive condition before the accident is not in my view satisfactorily addressed by Dr. Gertler. Again, it is for the Tribunal to decide this question of fact on the evidence before it and making what use it can of the medical opinions expressed in the evidence.

26. As I have said I accept the evidence of Mrs. Cope as being truthful and accurate. I accept the evidence of Mr. Cope as being truthful and subject to the qualification I have made earlier, accurate. In this regard the Tribunal, having heard the witnesses examined and cross-examined, is in a better position than that available to any of the doctors to ascertain the facts concerning the existence or otherwise of an emotional condition before the date of the motor vehicle accident. Accepting the evidence of the Copes I repeat my finding that the eczema which was very severe in 1951 caused the onset of an emotional condition in 1951 and that this pre-existed, being sometimes more noticeable than at other times. This emotional condition was more serious than merely downturns in mood brought on by the eczema. The condition which was described by Mr. and Mrs. Cope was, and I do find, of more extensive and durable nature. In a long passage commencing at p 22, which I accept as an accurate account, Mrs. Cope described her husband's state from 1951 to the accident. I accept that the factual conditions described by both Mr. and Mrs. Cope meet the requirements of Dr. Robbie's description referred to earlier in these reasons. I find that the condition variously described as an emotional condition or as a depressive condition suffered by the applicant existed prior to the motor vehicle accident which occurred in 1962 and that the condition was more probably than not caused by the eczema which the applicant contracted during his war service. I also find that the motor vehicle accident aggravated the emotional or depressive condition. Indeed, Mrs. Cope confirms that in her evidence at pp 24-28.

27. On the view I take of the evidence I find that the depressive state or emotional condition here antedated the motor vehicle accident. I also find that the depressive state or emotional condition was caused by the applicant's eczema.

28. Even if the depressive state or emotional condition was not manifested before the accident I would find on the medical evidence that the eczema caused depression in that after the accident Mr. Cope was not able to cope with his eczema so that severe depression came about. In that case I would find that the eczema was a cause that contributed to the depressive state or emotional condition from the time of the motor vehicle accident in 1962.

29. The task which I must undertake to resolve this case is to ascertain whether on the whole of the material before me a reasonable hypothesis has been raised connecting the applicant's incapacity with his war service. Taking into account the whole of the material and in particular my finding that the depressive or emotional reaction antedated the accident there exists a reasonable hypothesis linking the applicant's incapacity from a depressive state or emotional condition with his war service in that the "war-caused" condition of eczema directly caused the depressive state or emotional condition. I find, perhaps out of an abundance of caution, that there is no evidence before me which causes me to conclude that the reasonable hypothesis has been dispelled (see Webb v. Repatriation Commission, Full Court, Sydney 30 March, 1988, unreptd. at p 11 & 12 of Davies J.'s reasons for judgment.).

30. If I am wrong as to a state of depression antedating the accident I make the following findings as an alternative hypothesis which arises on the medical evidence. In the event that there was no pre-existing depressive state or emotional condition existing prior to the motor vehicle accident but only "down moods" as Mrs. Cope termed them, I find that the opinion of Dr. Robbie, that the accident did not cause "the depressions but facilitated them", is the correct view of the position. Accordingly, I find that the depressions were caused at least in fact by the eczema in that the change in personality after the accident no longer allowed the applicant to cope with his eczema and his down moods. In that event the eczema has, in my view, directly contributed to the depressive state and is a cause, if not necessarily the only cause of that state. Again, I would find that on the whole of the material there is a reasonable hypothesis raised which links the war service with the incapacity caused by the depressive state or emotional condition; see Re Dalton and Repatriation Commission (unreported decision, 4413, 19 May, 1988) and the authorities there cited.

31. Accordingly my decision is as follows:-

(a) that the decision under review be set aside,

(b) the respondent is liable to pay the applicant a disability pension
pursuant to s.13 of the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986 in respect of incapacity arising from the condition "depressive reaction",

(c) that the pension is payable from 20 June, 1984.
