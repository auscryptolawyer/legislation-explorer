Re Garrick Lewis Gray v the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia v [1985] FCA 435 (25 November 1985)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: GARRICK LEWIS GRAY

And: THE DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

V No. G 153 of 1985

Administrative Law

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

VICTORIA DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Northrop J.

CATCHWORDS

Administrative law - judicial review - Income Tax Assessment Act - review of decision to institute recovery proceedings and refusal to grant extension of time to pay tax - application to extend time for review under Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act.

Practice and procedure - objection to competency pursuant to O.54 r.4 Rules of Court - motion to dismiss application for failing to disclose reasonable cause of action pursuant to O.20 r.2 Rules of Court.

Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 ss.5, 7, 8, 11, 13

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss.177, 201, 204, 206, 208, 209

Bankruptcy Act 1966 s.52(4)

HEARING

MELBOURNE

25:11:1985

ORDER

The Court Orders That the application be dismissed with costs. (Settlement and entry of Orders is dealt with in O.36 of the Rules of Court.)

DECISION

By application dated 27 June 1985 and filed on 28 June 1985, Garrick Lewis Gray ("the taxpayer") commenced proceedings under the Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 ("the Judicial Review Act") seeking orders of review in respect of eight decisions of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia ("the Deputy Commissioner"). The taxpayer alleges that each of the eight decisions was a decision of an administrative character made under the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Assessment Act"). The taxpayer identifies the eight decisions as follows:-

1. The decision made on 19 December 1984 to apply to

the Court to be substituted as the petitioning

creditor in proceedings numbered P 353 and P 663 in

the Court for sequestration orders under the

Bankruptcy Act 1960 against the taxpayer.

2. The decision made on 26 June 1985 not to agree to

an adjournment of the hearing of the two petitions

to a date after the final determination of

objections lodged by the taxpayer against

assessments and amended assessments for income tax

for the years ended 30 June 1977 to 30 June 1981

inclusive.

3. Alternatively to 2., the failure to agree to the

adjournment.

4. The decision to proceed with the petitions

notwithstanding the request of the taxpayer to

refer the decisions disallowing the objections by

the taxpayer to a Board of Review for

determination.

5. Alternatively to 4., the failure to decide not to

proceed with the petitions notwithstanding the

request to refer the decisions disallowing the

objections to a Board of Review for determination.

6. The failure to grant an extension of time under

s.206 of the Assessment Act pursuant to a letter

from the taxpayer dated 8 March 1985.

7. Alternatively to 6., the decision not to grant an

extension of time under s.206. This decision and

the sixth decision are hereinafter referred to as

the s.206 decision.

8. The decision made on 29 March 1985 to reject

alternative proposals made by the taxpayer with

respect to payment of tax.

The application filed is a confusing document. It purports to be based on sections 5. and 7. of the Judicial Review Act, but the difference between those sections has not been recognised in the application.

2. Despite the provisions of O.54 of the Rules of Court, the application was the only document filed on 28 June 1985. On 11 July 1985 the Deputy Commissioner, pursuant to O.54 r.4, gave notice of objection to competency on the grounds that in respect of each of the decisions the application had not been made within the time limited by s.11 of the Judicial Review Act and that decisions numbered 1. to 5. and 8. were not decisions to which the Judicial Review Act applied since they were not decisions of an administrative character made under an enactment. At the same time, the Deputy Commissioner filed notice that he would move the Court pursuant to O.20 r.2 that the application be dismissed on the grounds that it failed to disclose a reasonable cause of action, was frivolous and vexatious and was an abuse of the process of the Court. The Deputy Commissioner relied upon an affidavit of an officer of the Australian Taxation Office, namely Maxwell James Beattie, sworn on 11 July 1985. On 25 July 1985, affidavits sworn by the taxpayer and his solicitor, Graeme Levy, respectively, were filed in support of the application and in opposition to the objection to competency and the motion of the Deputy Commissioner. On 31 July 1985, the taxpayer gave notice that he would move the Court for orders under s.11 of the Judicial Review Act that the time for the making of the application for review of the decisions numbered 1., 7. and 8. be extended to 28 June 1985, being the date the application was filed in the Court. The hearing of the two motions and the objection to competency were heard on 26 July, 31 July and 12 August 1985.

3. In order to understand the nature of the motions and notice, it is necessary to set out in some detail a summary of the facts leading up to the date of the issue of the application. On 15 September 1983, pursuant to the provisions of the Assessment Act, the Deputy Commissioner issued notices of assessment with respect to the taxpayer for the four years ending 30 June 1977, 1978, 1979 and 1980 respectively. Under the assessments, tax in the order of 12.2m dollars, including additional tax, became due and payable by the taxpayer on 18 October 1983. Thereafter, negotiations took place between the taxpayer and the Deputy Commissioner with respect to the re-examination of the taxpayer's affairs and the payment of tax by the taxpayer. On 16 November 1983, the taxpayer lodged formal objections to the four assessments and by letter dated 2 December 1983 requested that payment of tax be deferred pending the conclusion of the re-examination then being conducted. That request was granted and by letter dated 4 January 1984 the Deputy Commissioner notified the taxpayer that the payment was deferred until the completion of the re-examination or until 28 February 1984, whichever occurred first. In April 1984, the taxpayer lodged amended returns for the years ended 30 June 1979 and 1980 and returns for the years ended 30 June 1981, 1982 and 1983 respectively. By letter dated 30 October 1984, the Deputy Commissioner notified the taxpayer that his objections to the four assessments which had been issued had been considered, that notices of amended assessments for those years ended 30 June 1977 to 1980 inclusive had issued, but the objections had been disallowed. As a result of the amended assessments, the amount due and payable was of the order of 11.3m dollars. The letter contained the following paragraphs:-

"An amount of $7,770,937.15 income tax and

additional tax for late payment of income tax is

now required to be paid. ...

Should you wish to enter into discussions relating

to an extension of time for the payment of the

income tax and additional tax for late payment by

instalments over a relatively short period of time

then you should deliver within 7 days full details

of your assets and liabilities and current income

and expenditure, together with your payment

proposal.

It is envisaged that any payment arrangement

entered into would be supported by the provision of

adequate security."

The letter made reference to other matters including bankruptcy proceedings pending in the Court against the taxpayer and which had been instituted by other creditors of the taxpayer:-

"You are further advised that unless suitable

payment arrangements acceptable to this office are

entered into prior to 7 November 1984, it is

intended to be represented at that hearing and, if

necessary, to seek to be substituted as the

Petitioning Creditor."

4. On 20 December 1984, the taxpayer objected to the amended assessments issued on 30 October 1984 and requested the Deputy Commissioner to refer his decision to disallow the taxpayer's objections to a Board of Review, but the taxpayer did not request, under s.206 of the Assessment Act, an extension of time to pay the amount of tax then due and payable under those assessments. On 15 January 1985, the Deputy Commissioner disallowed the taxpayer's objections to the amended assessments and on 14 March 1985, the taxpayer requested the Deputy Commissioner to refer his decisions to disallow the taxpayer's objections to the amended assessments to a Board of Review. Meanwhile, on 17 December 1984, the Deputy Commissioner gave notice of assessment of tax for the year ended 30 June 1981. That tax became due and payable on 21 January 1985. On 13 February 1985, the taxpayer objected to that assessment, but did not request, under s.206 of the Assessment Act, an extension of time to pay the amount of tax then due and payable. The amount of tax due and payable by the taxpayer for the five years ended 30 June 1977 to 1981 respectively, was in the order of 15m. dollars.

5. Meanwhile, steps had been taken in the pending bankruptcy proceedings referred to in the letter of 30 October 1984. On 5 November 1984, the taxpayer and his legal adviser and other persons attended upon the Deputy Commissioner in relation to the bankruptcy proceedings. Thereafter, the taxpayer provided information to the Deputy Commissioner relating to his assets and liabilities. The hearing of the bankruptcy proceedings, identified by No's. P353 and P663, were adjourned to 19 December 1984. Before that date, the Deputy Commissioner on 26 November 1984, filed an application under s.49 of the Bankruptcy Act 1966 in each of those petitions seeking to be substituted as petitioning creditor. On 19 December 1984, the Court ordered that the Deputy Commissioner be substituted as petitioner in each of those petitions. The decision of the Deputy Commissioner to make that application is identified in these proceedings as the first decision sought to be reviewed. The hearing of the bankruptcy petitions was adjourned to 15 April 1985.

6. By letter dated 8 March 1985, the taxpayer asserted that the method adopted by the Deputy Commissioner in assessing tax for the five years ended 30 June 1977 to 1981 inclusive involved disputed and difficult principles. He requested an extension of time to pay the assessments. The relevant paragraphs of the letter are set out:-

"In these circumstances you are requested to

grant an extension of time for payment of the said

assessments until a final liability (if any) has

been established at the conclusion of the appeal

process, should such appeal action need be taken by

the taxation.

Would you please respond within fourteen

days."

7. At a meeting with the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation on 18 March, the taxpayer made proposals for the payment of the tax assessed for the five years. At that meeting, the taxpayer handed to the Deputy Commissioner a letter setting out alternative proposals contained in documents annexed to the letter. The introductory paragraphs of the letter were as follows:-

"I refer to previous correspondence and discussions

regarding income tax assessments issued to me for

the years ended 30th June 1977 and 1981 inclusive.

At our meeting on 10th December 1984 you agreed

that I may lodge a submission in relation to the

payment and/or settlement of the assessments during

March 1985".

The letter then made reference to the objections lodged by the taxpayer and to the difficult areas of law involved in making the assessments. The letter then set out the alternatives. By letter dated 29 March 1984, the Deputy Commissioner rejected those proposals. The letter contained the following paragraph:-

"It is advised that both the settlement proposal

and the alternative proposal for deferment of the

tax, which as you are aware is due and payable

under the assessments, are considered inadequate

and accordingly the proposals are not acceptable."

It should be noted that the sixth and seventh decisions sought to be reviewed, in these proceedings jointly referred to as the s.206 decision, are based on the letter from the taxpayer of 8 March 1985. The eighth decision sought to be reviewed is the decision to reject the alternative proposals made by the taxpayer in his letter of 18 March 1985.

8. On 10 April, the taxpayer made further representations to the second Commissioner of Taxation in Canberra. As a result of arrangements then made, the taxpayer agreed to make some payments to the Deputy Commissioner and the Deputy Commissioner agreed to consent to an adjournment of the hearing of the bankruptcy petitions to 1 July 1985.

9. On 16 April, the Deputy Commissioner referred the decisions made by him in respect of the four years ended 30 June 1977 to 1980 respectively, to a Board of Review. By letter dated 31 May 1985, the taxpayer made further proposals to the Deputy Commissioner with respect to the payment of tax then due and payable. By letter dated 19 June 1985, the taxpayer's solicitors requested the Deputy Commissioner to seek leave to withdraw the two bankruptcy petitions or to consent to an indefinite adjournment of the hearing of those petitions. The relevant part of the letter is set out:-

"We also note that you have not yet responded to

Mr. Gray's request, contained in a letter dated 8

March 1985, for an extension of time for payment of

the income tax pursuant to s.206 of the Act.

In view of the above matters, we request that you

seek the leave of the Court to withdraw the two

Bankruptcy Petitions or consent to an adjournment

of the Petitions until after the final

determination of the objections by a Board of

Review and any subsequent appeals as provided for

in Division 2 of Part V of the Act."

The Deputy Commissioner refused those requests. The decision not to seek the withdrawal of the petitions is identified in these proceedings as the fourth and fifth decisions sought to be reviewed. The decision not to agree to an indefinite adjournment of the hearing of the petitions is identified in these proceedings as the second and third decisions.

10. Pursuant to arrangements made on 28 June 1985 between the Deputy Commissioner and the taxpayer, including the payment of further moneys by the taxpayer, the hearing of the bankruptcy petitions was adjourned to 29 October 1985. On 28 June 1985, the present application was issued.

11. The essential decision sought to be reviewed is the decision relating to the application by the taxpayer for an extension of time to pay the tax assessed for the five years ended 30 June 1977 to 1981 respectively. The application was made under s.206 of the Assessment Act. Sub-section 206(1) provides:-

"206. (1) The Commissioner may in any case grant

such extension of time for payment of tax, or

permit payment of tax to be made by such

instalments and within such time as he considers

the circumstances warrant; and in such case the tax

shall be due and payable accordingly."

12. If an extension of time is granted, tax becomes due and payable in accordance with the terms of the extension or permission granted. If an extension of time is not granted, the tax is due and payable in accordance with the assessment, see sub-section 204(1) of the Assessment Act, is a debt due to the Commonwealth and is payable to the Commissioner, see sub-section 208(1), and may be sued for and recovered by the Commissioner, see sub-section 209(1). The fact that decisions of the Commissioner to disallow objections by a taxpayer to assessments have been referred to a Board of Review and are pending before a Board, does not interfere with or affect the assessments subject of the reference and tax may be recovered on the assessments as if no reference was pending, see sub-section 201(1). There is no doubt that a decision to refuse an application under sub-section 206(1) for an extension of time for the payment of tax is a decision of an administrative character made under an enactment and may be reviewed under the Judicial Review Act.

13. In the present case, the s.206 decision, being decisions numbered 6. and 7., is based on sub-section 206(1). Counsel for the taxpayer referred to the letter from the taxpayer dated 8 March 1985 seeking an extension of time to pay the tax assessed for the five years in dispute until after the hearing of the references to the Board of Review and of any appeals thereafter. He said that subsequently there is no letter from the Deputy Commissioner making specific reference to the letter of 8 March and therefore the Deputy Commissioner has failed to make a decision under sub-section 206(1) and seeks an order of review under sub-section 7(1) of the Judicial Review Act in respect of that failure. Alternatively, counsel contended that the Deputy Commissioner had made a decision to refuse to grant the extension of time sought and seeks an order of review under sub-section 5(1) of the Judicial Review Act in respect of that decision.

14. On the facts of this case, it is clear that the Deputy Commissioner has refused to grant the extension of time sought by the taxpayer in his letter of 8 March. The letter from the Deputy Commissioner dated 29 March 1985 sets out the terms of that decision. It is true that that letter makes no reference to the letter of 8 March, but it must be accepted that the meeting held on 18 March and the letter of 18 March were all directed to proposals for settlement of the liability to pay tax and the payment of that tax by instalment. Further, the letter of 18 March makes specific reference to previous correspondence and of necessity, this would include the letter of 8 March. The letter of 29 March was written in the light of those discussions, the letter of 18 March and after receipt of the letter of 8 March. Thereafter, further negotiations took place between the taxpayer and officers of the Taxation Office. Those negotiations would have proceeded only on the basis that the application under sub-section 206(1) had been refused. It follows therefore that decision no. 6., being the failure to grant an extension of time under s.206 pursuant to the letter of 8 March 1985, has no foundation and the relevant date for determining the time within which the application for review of the s.206 decision is on or about 29 March 1985; see paragraph 11(1)(c) and sub-paragraph 11(3)(b)(iii) of the Judicial Review Act.

15. The decision numbered 8. in these proceedings, in reality, is a decision based in part upon the application for an extension of time or for payment by instalments under s.206. Insofar as that decision forms part of the decision to refuse to grant the extension sought under s.206, it is a decision of an administrative character made under an enactment and in this regard is to be treated in the same way as the s.206 decision. Any consideration by the Deputy Commissioner of the proposals made by the taxpayer on 18 March must, of necessity, have involved consideration and the making of decisions described in paragraph (e) of Schedule 1 to the Judicial Review Act. This follows from the fact that the proposals put by the taxpayer included not only methods of payment of tax but also a reduction in the amount of tax due and payable. To the extent that the decision numbered 8. is a decision coming within paragraph (e) of Schedule 1 to the Judicial Review Act, it is not a decision to which that Act applies; see definition of "decision to which this Act applies" in sub-section 3(1) of that Act. At the present time, I put to one side the question of whether, in any event, the decision numbered 8. is a decision of an administrative character made under an enactment within the meaning of the Judicial Review Act.

16. The decisions numbered 1. to 5. inclusive, relate to the conduct of the bankruptcy proceedings. The action taken by the Deputy Commissioner in those proceedings is based on the fact that at all relevant times the Commissioner was a creditor of the taxpayer. The existence of the debt depends upon the provisions contained in ss.204 and 208 of the Assessment Act. In the absence of an extension of time or permission for payment by instalments, the total amount of tax assessed remains due and payable. Insofar as the decisions are related to decisions to refuse applications made under s.206 of the Act, they are decisions of an administrative character made under an enactment and come within the class of decisions which may be reviewed under the Judicial Review Act. Insofar as those decisions relate to decisions made in the process of enforcing a debt due to the Commissioner, counsel for the Deputy Commissioner has contended that they are not decisions which come within the Judicial Review Act. At the present time, I put that question to one side.

17. I turn now to consider whether the application to review the s.206 decision was made within the time prescribed by the Judicial Review Act and if not, whether the time for making that application should be extended to 28 June 1985. It will be remembered that the s.206 decision was contained in the letter of 29 March 1985.

18. Under s.11 of the Judicial Review Act, the application for an order of review of the s.206 decision should have been made within 28 days of the day on which the letter of 29 March 1985 was furnished to the taxpayer or "within such further time as the Court ... allows". The letter of 29 March 1985 was received by the taxpayer on 1 April 1985 and thus the application should have been made on or before 29 April 1985. The application was not made until 28 June 1985.

19. In support of the motion that the time for making the application for the order of review should be extended to 28 June 1985, counsel for the taxpayer relied upon facts contained in the affidavits sworn by the taxpayer and by his solicitor, Mr. Levy, respectively. Essentially, the facts deposed to by the taxpayer are not inconsistent with the facts sworn to by Mr. Beattie. They elaborate upon the facts set out in the affidavit of Mr. Beattie and contain much material directed to the issue of whether the assessments made by the Deputy Commissioner were correct or not. That material was objected to by counsel for the Deputy Commissioner since having regard to s.177 of the Assessment Act and the provisions of paragraph (c) of the First Schedule to the Judicial Review Act, the question of whether the assessments were correct or not could not be raised in these proceedings. I allow the objection, but in so doing, I am satisfied that there is a genuine dispute between the taxpayer and the Deputy Commissioner as to the correct method by which the assessments should have been made. That dispute has been referred to a Board of Review for determination. It must be remembered also that even if the provisions of s.201 may be said to be draconian, nevertheless Parliament has enacted that section and that section remains in operation. The amounts of tax assessed to be paid by the taxpayer remain debts due and payable and may be recovered as if no reference were pending.

20. From the affidavit of the taxpayer, it appears that at all material times he was aware of the provisions of the Judicial Review Act, but believed that the Deputy Commissioner had not made a decision to refuse the application under s.206 of the Assessment Act to extend the time for payment of the tax until after the hearing and determination of the references and of any appeals therefrom. He continued to negotiate with the Deputy Commissioner concerning proposals relating to the tax and in fact made payments on account of the tax owing. He was concerned to prevent the hearing and determination of the bankruptcy petitions. By June 1985, Mr. Levy of the firm of Madgwicks, Melbourne, was acting on behalf of the taxpayer in connection with the two bankruptcy petitions. Mr. Levy wrote the letter of 19 June 1985 referred to earlier in these decisions. In his affidavit, the taxpayer says that since the Deputy Commissioner, on 25 June 1985, notified him of his decision not to withdraw the bankruptcy petitions, his solicitors "immediately carried out my instructions to issue" the application under the Judicial Review Act. The affidavit of the taxpayer continues:-

"In substance, the Application for an Order of

Review seeks to review decisions taken or the

failure to make decisions associated with

applications for an extension of time for payment

of income tax made under s.206 of the Act.

Associated with that are decisions of the

Respondent in connection with the Bankruptcy

Proceedings against me and the Commissioner's

determination to keep those proceedings on foot

despite the fact that the income tax assessments

are genuinely disputed and that the Commissioner

has still not made a decision to grant an extension

of time for payment. While the Application is made

out of time in respect of some of the decisions the

delay has arisen because of continuing negotiations

with the Respondent and my expectation that he

would consider my application for an extension of

time expeditiously, I propose to apply to the Court

to exercise its discretion to extend the time in

which the relevant applications can be made. It

will be seen from the above that I have not been

dilatory in my negotiations with the Respondents,

and at all times I have been the initiator of those

negotiations. While it is true that I was unable

to make payment of $800,000.00 I had hoped to pay

on 31st May 1985. I have now paid $400,000.00

thereof and undertaken to pay the balance on 22nd

October 1985. The Respondent consented to this

further time being granted to make the payment

which I had undertaken to make.

While I consented to the Respondent's Application

to adjourn the Bankruptcy Proceedings to the end of

October 1985, I did so at his request and only to

enable him to further consider my submission of

31st May 1985."

21. The affidavit of Mr. Levy deposes in more detail to the negotiations in June 1985 between himself and officers of the Taxation Office with respect to the bankruptcy petitions, but is not directed to any reason for the failure to make the application under the Judicial Review Act within the time prescribed by that Act. In fact, Mr. Levy was not acting on behalf of the taxpayer at the time when the application for review should have been made.

22. In the circumstances, the only reasons given in support of the application for time to be extended to file the application under the Judicial Review Act are that the taxpayer believed that the Deputy Commissioner had made no decision under s.206 and that negotiations were continuing. It must be remembered that if the extension of time sought by the taxpayer under s.206 of the Assessment Act had been granted, the tax would have ceased to be due and payable and the standing of the Deputy Commissioner as a creditor of the taxpayer would have lapsed and he would not have been able to remain as a substituted creditor in the two bankruptcy petitions.

23. Reference should be made to the basis on which the application for judicial review is made. In an earlier decision, I used the expression that an applicant for judicial review had adopted a scattergun approach. That description applies with equal force to the present application. The taxpayer lists eight different matters which he seeks to review, ranging from the decision made in December 1984 to the decision or a failure to make a decision in June 1985. Five of those matters are described as decisions and three are described as the failure to make decisions in connection with three of those five decisions. The grounds of the application are based on six of the paragraphs contained in sub-section 5(1) of the Judicial Review Act, namely, paragraphs (a), (b), (d), (e), (h) and (j). Under paragraph 5(1)(e), reference is made to six of the paragraphs contained in sub-section 5(2), namely, (a), (b), (e), (f), (g) and (j). There has been no attempt to give any particulars of any of those grounds. No grounds are given based on the failure to make decisions; see s.7 of the Judicial Review Act. This is of special importance having regard to the belief of the taxpayer that the Deputy Commissioner had failed to make the decision sought under s.206 of the Assessment Act.

24. On the material before the Court, it is difficult to see any basis to support the application for the order of review of the s.206 decision. In reality, it comes down to an assertion that whenever a taxpayer has sought a reference of a disputed assessment and that dispute is genuine, the Commissioner should grant, under s.206 of the Assessment Act, an extension of time for payment of tax pending the determination of the reference or at least should permit payment of tax by instalments. In the present case, there is nothing to suggest that the application for review will succeed. The reason for not commencing the proceedings under the Judicial Review Act is based partly upon a mistaken belief that the Deputy Commissioner had failed to make a decision on the s.206 application. The taxpayer has not sought reasons under s.13 of the Judicial Review Act for the decision not to grant an extension of time, but having regard to the belief of the taxpayer that a decision had not been made, no weight should be given to that fact. Nevertheless, having regard to the negotiations that were continuing and the provisions of the Bankruptcy Act, particularly sub-section 52(4) and the provisions of the Act relating to the property of a bankrupt that vests in the Trustee in Bankruptcy upon a sequestration order being made, it seems strange that the true legal position was not understood by the taxpayer who is a practising solicitor. He was not cross-examined on his affidavit. More importantly, in my opinion, the reason for the non-commencement of the proceedings was because of the continuing negotiations between the taxpayer and the Deputy Commissioner. On the facts of this case, in my opinion, the taxpayer has shown no reason why he should have the time extended in which to bring his application under the Judicial Review Act in relation to the s.206 decision.

25. In the result, the taxpayer's motion under s.11 of the Judicial Review Act is refused insofar as it applies to decision numbered 7. in the application. For similar reasons, the motion is refused insofar as it applies to decision numbered 8. in the application. It follows that the Deputy Commissioner's objection to competency, insofar as it relates to decisions numbered 6., 7. and 8. must succeed.

26. The remaining decisions sought to be reviewed are those numbered 1. to 5. inclusive in the application. The decision numbered 1. was made on or about 19 December 1984. The taxpayer has moved the Court that the time within which the application to review that decision be extended to 28 June 1985. For the reasons given with respect to decision numbered 7., that part of the motion is refused also. It follows that the objection to competency insofar as it relates to decision numbered 1., must succeed.

27. Decisions numbered 3. and 5. are based, apparently, on s.7 of the Judicial Review Act and are the converse of the decisions numbered 2. and 4. On the facts already found, the Deputy Commissioner has made decisions referred to in decisions numbered 2. and 4. It follows therefore, that the Deputy Commissioner's objection to competency insofar as it relates to decisions 3. and 5., must succeed. In reality, the decisions numbered 2. and 4. depend upon the refusal by the Deputy Commissioner to grant an extension of time under s.206 of the Assessment Act until the determination of the references and any subsequent appeals therefrom. Since that decision cannot be reviewed in these proceedings, logically decisions numbered 2. and 4. should fail also. Counsel for the Deputy Commissioner has contended that those decisions, as well as decision numbered 1., are not decisions of an administrative character made under an enactment. There is much force in that contention, based as it is on the nature of the powers conferred by ss.8, 177, 208 and 209 of the Assessment Act, but I do not find it necessary to express any view on the correctness of that contention.

28. The application to review decisions numbered 2. and 4. was made within the time prescribed by s.11 of the Judicial Review Act. If they are decisions under the Judicial Review Act, they are decisions made in connection with the institution or conduct of proceedings in a Civil Court and thus are not decisions for which reasons can be ordered to be given under s.13; see sub-sections 13(1) and (11) and clause (f) of Schedule 2. to the Judicial Review Act. It may be that the decisions not to agree to the adjournment and not to seek leave to withdraw the petitions may be decisions with respect to the recovery of tax, but having regard to the other findings made in these reasons, the notice of the decisions numbered 2. and 4., the continuing negotiations between the taxpayer and the Deputy Commissioner, the fact that the Deputy Commissioner has refused to grant the extension of time sought by the taxpayer under s.206 of the Assessment Act, the fact that the hearings of the petitions have been adjourned from time to time and the fact that the bankruptcy petitions are before the Federal Court where the Judge hearing those petitions has powers conferred upon him by the Bankruptcy Act, the Court, as presently constituted, should not proceed further with respect to decisions numbered 2. and 4. On the facts of this case and on all the material before the Court and having regard to the high onus cast upon the Deputy Commissioner, I am satisfied that the application, insofar as it relates to decisions numbered 2. and 4., ought to be dismissed on the ground that it is frivolous and vexatious and is an abuse of the process of the Court.

29. In the result, the application is dismissed with costs.

30. During the hearing of the motions and the objection to competency, a large number of authorities were referred to by counsel. Those authorities have been considered but it has not been necessary to refer to each of them. For the sake of convenience, a list of those authorities is contained in the appendix to these reasons.
