ST86/925 and Commissioner of Taxation [1987] AATA 377; 87 ATC 1107 (26 October 1987)

Last Updated: 29 July 2009

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Taxation - applicant a private company - both trustee and a beneficiary of a discretionary trust - distribution to applicant as beneficiary - no dividends declared - liability to pay additional tax - whether income received as beneficiary of trust was income from personal exertion or property income - whether the proceeds of a business carried on by the taxpayer

Words and Phrases: "taxpayer"

"proceeds of any business carried on by the taxpayer

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss. 6(1), 96, 98,104, 105B

Syme v The Commissioner of Taxes for Victoria [1914] UKPCHCA 6; (1914) 18 CLR 519

Tindal v The Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1946) 72 CLR 609

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. ST86/925

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )

REASONS FOR DECISION

  1. The taxpayer is a private company. It is both trustee and one of the beneficiaries of a discretionary trust. As trustee it is empowered by the trust indenture to carry on any business and to employ in that business the whole or any part of the trust fund. In the year of income ended 30 June 1984 it carried on business employing assets of the trust fund and made a profit which it credited to the trust fund. It then exercised its discretion as to payment of that income to beneficiaries of the trust. One of the beneficiaries to whom it distributed the income was itself? the amount which it received was $315,578.00. It stated details of that income in its income tax return for that year.
  2. It had not paid any dividends during that year. As a private company, if it was not to be deemed to have made a sufficient distribution in relation to that year, it was liable to pay additional tax under section 104 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act"). The amount of the additional tax payable depended on the amount of the retention allowance to which it was entitled under section 105B of the Act. That in turn depended upon whether the income which it had received as beneficiary of the trust was income derived from personal exertion or income derived from property. The Deputy Commissioner of Taxation in Adelaide decided that the income was derived from property and calculated the retention allowance on that basis. The amount which the Deputy Commissioner decided was payable as additional tax pursuant to section 104 was considerably greater than it would have been if the retention allowance had been calculated on the basis that the income received by the applicant as beneficiary of the trust was income derived from personal exertion.
  3. The applicant lodged with the Deputy Commissioner an objection against the assessment on the ground that the income had been derived from personal exertion. The Deputy Commissioner disallowed the objection. The applicant then requested that the decision disallowing the objection be referred to a Board of Review. As a result of that request the decision was referred to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal under section 189 of the Act, as amended by the Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986.
  4. At the hearing the applicant was represented by Mr R W Hender, a public accountant who is also a Secretary of the applicant. The respondent was represented by Mr P McCullough, a departmental officer. At the hearing a schedule of agreed facts was presented by the parties, together with a number of documents, namely the trust indenture, the income tax return of the trust for the year of income which ended on 30 June 1984, the income tax return of the applicant for that year and a distribution notice sent to the applicant by the Deputy Commissioner on 14 May 1985. Those documents afford clear evidence of the facts stated above; all of them were agreed to by both parties. There is no need to state more facts.
  5. Both parties agreed that the only issue was whether the income received by the applicant as beneficiary of the trust was income derived from personal exertion or income derived from property. I am satisfied that that is the only issue and that it depends entirely on the law applicable to the above facts. "Income from property" is defined in section 6(1) of the Act in terms of what it is not, that is to say that it is income which is not income derived from personal exertion. So far as is relevant in these proceedings, "income derived from personal exertion" is defined as meaning "the proceeds of any business carried on by the taxpayer either alone or as a partner with any other person". Mr McCullough submitted that, because the business was carried on by the applicant as trustee it was not carried on by the taxpayer. Mr Hender, on the other hand, submitted that, as the applicant was the taxpayer and the business was carried on by the applicant, it was carried on by the taxpayer.
  6. Mr Hender referred to the decision of the Privy Council in Syme v The Commissioner of Taxes for Victoria [1914] UKPCHCA 6; (1914) 18 CLR 519. There it was held that, insofar as income of a beneficiary of a trust was attributable to business carried on by the trustee, it was income from personal exertion; its character did not change to income from property merely because it was paid out of the trust fund to the beneficiary. The definition of "income derived from personal exertion", he acknowledged, had changed the law as stated in Syme where the beneficiary was not the person who had carried on the business as a result of which the trust fund had derived its income. But, he submitted, where the beneficiary was the person who had carried on that business, as had occurred in the present case, the law stated in Syme remained unchanged.
  7. Mr McCullough, referred to passages in the judgments of Starke J, Dixon J (as he then was) and Williams J in Tindal v The Federal Commissioner of Taxation (1946) 72 CLE 609. He said that a distinction had to be drawn between the applicant qua beneficiary and the applicant qua trustee. The applicant was the taxpayer only in its capacity as beneficiary, not in its capacity as trustee. It was irrelevant, therefore, he submitted, that the applicant qua trustee had carried on the business which had produced the income which as trustee it had then paid out of the trust fund to beneficiaries including itself.
  8. The passage in Starke J's judgment is brief in the extreme. It reads: "Syme v Commissioner of Taxes (Vic) is distinguishable on the words of the Act". The passage from the judgment of Dixon J is more ample. It is at pages 629 and 630 and reads as follows:-

"What is income from personal exertion is defined in s6. All other income is income from property. The income derived by the taxpayer, as stated in the reference, does not fall within the definition of income from personal exertion. Syme v Commissioner of Taxes (Vic.) is inapplicable because, no doubt with that decision in mind, the draftsman of the definition introduced the words 'carried on by the taxpayer' after the words 'proceeds of any business'. The taxpayer did not carry on the business. She was a beneficiary, not a partner. Her trustee did carry on the business, that is as a partner."

9. The passage from the judgment of Williams J is at page 633 and is as follows:-

"The income derived by the trustees from the carrying on of the partnership business was income derived from personal exertion. But the appellant and not the trustee is the taxpayer and she is not a partner. When she receives her share of the net income under s97, it is not income which falls within the definition of income from personal exertion and is therefore income from property."

  1. The facts of Tindal's case were that the trustee carried on business in partnership and did so employing assets of the trust fund. It was itself a company. It had been appointed trustee under the will of Mrs Tindal's late husband. She was not a partner in the business; her right to receive income from it was only as beneficiary under the trust. So there could be no doubt that she was a person who had not, in any capacity whatsoever, carried on the business which produced the income of the trust fund which was eventually paid to her. The facts, therefore, were markedly different from those of the present case.
  2. Nevertheless, Mr McCullough submitted that the manner in which Dixon J. and Williams J. expressed themselves indicated that the capacity in which the income was derived, that is to say as a beneficiary rather than as trustee, was of paramount significance in relation to the question whether the business was "carried on by the taxpayer". Their reference to "the taxpayer" was, he said, to the beneficiary in the capacity in which she was liable to pay tax under the Act. Mr Hender did not accept that that was so. He argued that all that the High Court had decided was that, because the taxpayer, Mrs Tindal, had played no part in running the business from which the income was derived and had had no role other than as beneficiary, the income had not been derived from a business carried on by the taxpayer, and that in respect of that situation the definition of "income from personal exertion" had changed the law as stated in Syme. I accept that in Tindal the factual situation was such that the Court had no need to address the question with which we are concerned in this proceeding. Their Honours did not expressly address it; and I can find nothing in their judgments to indicate that they intended to do so. I find, therefore, that they afford no support for Mr McCullough's argument, nor indeed for Mr Hender's.

12. I consider that, in order to decide what is the intended effect of the requirement that for income to be "income derived from personal exertion" it must be the proceeds of a business carried on by the taxpayer, it is necessary to have regard to the context in which the expression "business carried on by the taxpayer" is used and to the scheme of the Act. First, it is to be noted that "taxpayer" is defined in section 6(1) as meaning "a person deriving income". Income itself is not defined but the meaning is now well understood, as is the meaning of "derive". It is not necessary for the purposes of the present case to discuss them; it is sufficient to say that the proceeds of carrying on a business are income. What is of significance, I consider, is the provision in section 96 of the Act that, except as provided in the Act, a trustee is not to be liable as such to pay income tax upon the income of the trust estate. Liability to pay tax on that income is imposed on the trustee by section 98 of the Act only in the circumstances referred to there. It is provided that in those circumstances he is "liable to pay tax in respect of . . . income of the trust estate . . . as if it were the income of an individual".

  1. What Mr Hender is, in effect, suggesting is that, once a person has been identified as the taxpayer in relation to any income, his name can be substituted for the word "taxpayer" wherever that word is used in the Act in a provision which is applicable to that income. I am unable to accept that that is correct. The Act provides for the imposition of a tax on incomes. In section 6(1) "taxpayer" is defined by reference to the derivation of income. In view of the purpose of the Act I am satisfied that it is the fact of deriving income that gives a person the character of a taxpayer and that references in the Act to "the taxpayer" are to the person in that character. So in the phrase "the proceeds of any business carried on by the taxpayer" in the definition of "income derived from personal exertion" the reference to "taxpayer" is to the person who by reason of the income has the character of a taxpayer, but to him only in that character.
  2. Accordingly, although the derivation of the income by the applicant as beneficiary resulted from distribution of income which the trust estate derived from the applicant's having carried on a business, the income derived as beneficiary was not income derived from personal exertion. Consequently, it must be treated as "income from property".

15. There is no dispute as to the calculation of the undistributed amount of the applicant's income for the purposes of Division 7 of Part III of the Act? it is $153,363.00. The additional tax payable under section 104 of the Act is 50% of that amount. That was the amount assessed by the Deputy Commissioner. Accordingly, his decision on the objection is affirmed.
