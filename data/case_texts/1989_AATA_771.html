Morcos and Commissioner for Employees' Compensation and Secretary, Department of Administrative Services [1989] AATA 771 (22 December 1989)

Morcos and Commissioner for Employees' Compensation and Secretary, Department of Administrative Services [1989] AATA 771 (22 December 1989)

Last Updated: 15 April 2008

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION

COMPENSATION - COMMONWEALTH EMPLOYEES - INCAPACITY - PARTIAL -AVAILABILITY OF SUITABLE WORK - AMOUNT PAYABLE - suitable work available - not availed of

Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees) Act 1971 s 46

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. N88/189

GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

And: COMMISSIONER FOR EMPLOYEES' COMPENSATION

First Respondent

And: SECRETARY TO THE DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES

Second Respondent

DECISION

AAT Decision No 5579

Tribunal : Mr B J McMahon (Deputy President)

Dr D T Howell (Member)

Mrs M T Lewis (Member)

Date : 22 December 1989

Place : Sydney

Decision : 1. The Tribunal sets aside the first respondent's determination of 13 January 1988.

2. The matter is remitted to the Commission for the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation of Commonwealth Employees with the direction that, at all material times between 27 April 1987 and 1 June 1988, the applicant was partially incapacitated for work as a result of a deemed injury received in the course of her employment by the respondent.

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No. N88/189 GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE DIVISION )

Re: ELLEN MORCOS

Applicant

And: COMMISSIONER FOR EMPLOYEES' COMPENSATION

First Respondent

And: SECRETARY TO THE DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES

Second Respondent

REASONS FOR DECISION

22 December 1989 Mr B J McMahon (Deputy President)

Dr DJ Howell (Member)

Mrs MT Lewis (Member)

  1. On 20 October 1981 liability was found under the Compensation (Commonwealth Government Employees) Act 1971 ("the Act") to pay compensation to the applicant in respect of incapacity caused by "chronic musculo-ligamentous injury of the cervical spine resulting in cervical sprain, rotator cuff lesion of the right shoulder in the form of right supraspinatus tendonitis, right external epicondylitis and flexor tenosynovitis with mild right carpal tunnel syndrome".
  2. The applicant continued to be incapacitated by these conditions for a number of periods, between 1981 and 1987 and was paid compensation pursuant to various determinations.
  3. According to a history taken by Dr Latif, she was absent from work: for an extended period from November 1985. According to a history taken by Dr Stephenson, the period began in November 1986. The difference is immaterial for present purposes.
  4. Her condition was subject to periodic reviews by various specialists. On 24 November 1986, Dr Tomlinson reported that he could find no evidence of any orthopaedic disability and that he considered the applicant fit to perform limited duties, to which reference will later be made. Subsequently the applicant was examined by an orthopaedic surgeon, Dr Stephenson at the request of the respondent, on 22 July 1987. However it was not until 29 October 1987 that Dr Stephenson reported his findings. It was largely as a result of his report that the applicant was, in his opinion, fit for normal work that the determination under review was arrived at.
  5. On 18 January 1988, a delegate under the Act determine C that "the effects of the personal injury sustained by the employee ceased to exist not later than 28/2/87 and thereupon the Department of Local Government and Administrative Services ceased to be liable to pay compensation under the said Act in respect of that personal injury".
  6. This application is formally brought to review that determination. However on the hearing, no attempt was made by the respondent to defend the determination in its terms. It was conceded that the effects of the personal injury still persisted at all relevant times and that the applicant was then partially incapacitated. The only issues that arose for determination were whether suitable light duties were, at all relevant times, available to the applicant or, put in other words, whether the applicant was capable of earning in some suitable employment the same amount that she was receiving prior to the commencement of the closed period in question.

7. That period was between 27 April 1987 and 3 June 1988. A statement of duties to operate from 3 December 1984 had been prepared by a Commonwealth medical officer, and had been approved by Dr Tomlinson. These duties were in the following terms -

“. Front counter enquiries.

. Handing out tender forms to the public.

. Using an auto-switchboard, at most 4 buttons to press - this can be done with the left hand. Volume of calls per day is not high; varies between 20-60.

. Will be assisted at all times, this includes use of tender board; will not be required to change the board."

8. Following a subsequent medical examination in April 1987, a Commonwealth medical officer added the following specifications to her statement of duties -

“. Not to use force

. Not to write more than 5 minutes in 15 minutes

. Not to lift weights more than 1 kg

. No repetitive stapling/stamping

. No typing/keying duties"

9. These details were made known to the applicant's supervisor, Mr O'Neill, who gave evidence on the hearing. He arranged for the applicant to be located at the enquiry desk of the Purchases and Sales group of the Department in Westfield Towers. She commenced her new duties on 24 April 1987. She was introduced to Ms Benson, another counter officer, who had instructed her as to the precise nature of the duties she was to perform. The applicant herself gave evidence that these duties were very light. She was required to handle the PABX switchboard only on a few occasions each day.

10. Her recollection was at fault in several respects. She thought that she had resumed work at Chifley Square, but on reconsideration agreed that she had gone to Westfield Towers. She thought that she had complained about the boring work and that she had requested something more interesting. However when confronted with Mr O'Neill's evidence, she agreed that this complaint had related to a previous occasion and not to the Westfield Towers job.

11. She gave evidence that her shoulder was still sore and that her hand was constantly painful. There was no evidence that her work caused these pains to increase. Nevertheless she left work after 4 days. She agreed that she had not complained to Mr O'Neill or to any other person about these pains or about her supposed inability to work. She then went to see a general practitioner, Dr Latif, who gave a certificate that she was unfit for all work from 27 April to 27 May 1987. It was submitted on her behalf at the conclusion of the evidence that this was evidence of total incapacity, at least during that period. However, Dr Latlf's evidence was not tested on cross-examination and is quite contrary to the written evidence of other qualified specialists including the applicant's treating specialist. Dr Guirgis, her orthopaedic surgeon, considered that the applicant was capable of carrying out light duties. Dr Dickson, a specialist in rehabilitation medicine, excluded from her capacity only the carrying out of repetitive movements for prolonged periods. The Commonwealth medical officer had certified that she was able to do the limited duties to which we have referred. In our view, the evidence of these 3 doctors is to be preferred to the extreme and undefended opinion of Dr Stephenson, who found that the applicant was capable of doing any work and to that of Dr Latif who found that she was capable of doing no work for one month. The fact is that Dr Latif gave the certificate at a consultation when the applicant saw him mainly to arrange for a referral to Dr Guirgis. It is more likely than not that Dr Latif's certificate was simply a "holding certificate" until better medical advice could be obtained. In our view, the evidence establishes that throughout the whole of the closed period the applicant had a limited capacity for work and, in particular, was capable of doing the light duties to which she was assigned in April 1987. Even Dr Latif concluded in June 1989 "She is not fit for her pre-injury duties but she can perform duties as front counter inquiries, courier-type work".

12. The applicant continued to see Dr Guirgis whilst she was off work. Two of his certificates were tendered in evidence. On 22 May 1987 he noted - "light duties offered were not suitable". The applicant denied that she had told him that they were not suitable. She said that she described the duties to him and he formed the opinion that they were not suitable. It was not possible to test the certificate of Dr Guirgis to see why he arrived at such an unlikely conclusion. Similarly, on 10 September 1987 he noted "no light duties offered". We did not have the opportunity to ask him on what basis this endorsement was put, to what period it referred, and whether or not it was a reference to the nature of the light duties or to the fact that no light duties of any description had at any time been offered. This last proposition certainly could not have been sustained. We accept these certificates as evidence of continuing incapacity but not as evidence of non-availability of suitable employment.

13. The applicant gave evidence that she telephoned her employer on a number of occasions and had asked for light duties. She specifically alleged that she spoke to a lady called Sue Way. However when it was pointed out to her that Miss Way had resigned prior to the relevant period, she agreed that she could have been mistaken. Evidence was given by Mrs Lauw, a Personnel Officer, concerning the contents of the applicant's file. This disclosed no conversation between the applicant and any officer of the Department during the relevant period in which she had requested a resumption of work or the provision of light duties. The applicant is an honest witness, in our view, but was quite confused. We would prefer the objective evidence of the file as to the absence of any such conversation. However, whether or not such conversations took place or whether the respondent wrote to the applicant about a resumption of work is, in the long run, irrelevant.

14. During the whole of the closed period, during which the applicant was away she was not paid compensation. She said that she telephoned in May 1987 to see why she had not been sent to a doctor. She was then told that all the doctors were "fully booked". It was not until early in July that she received a letter confirming an appointment with Dr Stephenson. As previously mentioned, it was not until some 3 months later that Dr Stephenson furnished his report. The applicant said that she had telephoned on a number of occasions to see whether his report had been received. The file showed no record of any such telephone call, although the evidence of Mrs Lauw was that such calls would have been recorded had they been made. It is possible that the applicant telephoned the office of the Commissioner for Employees' Compensation. However, whether or not she telephoned the Department is not, as we have said, material to the issues to be determined.

  1. It was not until the applicant received a letter dated 18 April 1988 pointing out that no further medical certificates had been received from Dr Guirgis and that she had continued to be absent without leave, and pointing out the consequences under the Public Service Act, that arrangements were made for her to resume work.
  2. This was done on 3 June 1988. Since that time the applicant has worked full time as a Pay Clerk, operating a computer. She has been trained to do certain work with certain breaks. However, during the last few weeks she said that the pain from which she had previously suffered had started to reappear.
  3. The respondent went to considerable trouble to provide the employment on which the applicant was engaged in April 1987. It was no part of the applicant's case that the nature and condition of that work caused a reappearance of her symptoms. The evidence of Mrs Lauw is that the job remained open during the whole of the period under review. There is no evidence in the file that the applicant requested any change in the nature of her duties. She herself gave evidence that she did not do so, even though she insisted she was anxious to return to work.
  4. It seems more likely, in our view, that the applicant did nothing positive about returning to work but merely awaited events. Even the financial disincentive of receiving no money during this period did not apparently produce results. The evidence that the light duties were suitable and that they were available throughout the whole of the closed period has not been attacked and we accept it unreservedly. That being the case, we must find, for the purposes of s 46 of the Act, that the amount which the applicant was capable of earning was the amount she was being paid for those duties.

19. The determination under review is not defensible on the evidence and must therefore be set aside. It follows that the respondent must pay the applicant's costs.

20. The matter will be remitted to the Commissioner's successor with a direction that, at all material times, between 27 April 1987 and 3 June 1988, the applicant was partially incapacitated for work as a result of a deemed injury received in the course of her employment with the respondent and that for the purposes of s 46 of the Act, the amount per week that the applicant was able to earn in some suitable employment or business during that period was the amount per week calculated at the rate payable to her from 24 to 27 April 1987, adjusted from time to time since then in terms of the Act.
