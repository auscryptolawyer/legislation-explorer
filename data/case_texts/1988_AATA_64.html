ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

BANK ACCOUNT DEBITS TAX - "public benevolent institution" -whether debits to accounts made "wholly and exclusively in furtherance of its objects".

Bank Account Debits Tax Administration Act 1982 - ss. 3(1), 8, 10, 11(1), 11(3), 11(4), 14;

Bank Account Debits Tax Act No. 141 of 1982 - s."4.

F.C. of T. v. Launceston Legacy 87 A.T.C. 4635; Case U63 87 A.T.C. 401;

Australian Broadcasting Corporation and Commissioner of Taxation, (Decision NT86/10365, 19 February 1988).

And: COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

REASONS FOR DECISION

19 February 1988 Peter M. Roach (Senior Member)

1. These five matters again come before the Tribunal having been remitted to it by the Federal Court of Australia for determination according to law. The applications relate to the affairs of the Legacy Club of Launceston ("Launceston Legacy"). Upon the enactment of the Bank Account Debits Tax Administration Act 1982 ("the Administration Act") Launceston Legacy applied to the Commissioner of Taxation for certificates of exemption pursuant to s.11 of the Administration Act in relation to five bank accounts which it operated. Legacy claimed to be entitled to certificates of exemption by reason of being a "public benevolent institution". The Commissioner refused the certificates of exemption and Launceston Legacy objected to each of the determinations so made. The matters in due course came on for hearing before the Tribunal and the claims of Launceston Legacy were upheld.

  1. It is worthy of note, and deserving of the attention of the Parliament, that had the issues arisen at this time, Launceston Legacy could not have pursued the challenge without paying fees of $1,200.
  2. The Commissioner appealed to the Federal Court of Australia and in due course Northrop, J. of that Court held that Launceston Legacy was "a public benevolent institution" within the meaning of the Act. However, as his Honour went on to say -

"This conclusion, however, does not dispose of the matter. It is but a step in deciding whether a certificate of exemption should be granted under s.11 of the Administration Act. As has been said earlier in these reasons, it is necessary that consideration be given to the application of s.11(1) to the five accounts the subject of these five appeals. It may be that the Commissioner does not dispute the granting of the certificates if Launceston Legacy is a public benevolent institution. If that is so, the granting of the certificates by the Tribunal will be a formality only. In the circumstances, each matter should be remitted to the Tribunal to be determined according to law."

4. I respectfully adopt the findings of fact made, and the reasons expressed by his Honour for determining that Launceston Legacy is a public benevolent institution. In particular, I adopt the conclusion of his Honour where he said (87 A.T.C. 4646):-

"In my opinion, the essential object of Launceston Legacy is benevolent. The objects and powers listed in the objects section of its constitution are incidental and ancillary to that essential object. If it be a fact that some of those powers and objects, considered alone, might not be regarded as benevolent, that fact would not prevent Launceston Legacy being benevolent......I agree with the opinion of the Tribunal that Launceston Legacy 'serves a group in need within the community'. In doing so it recognizes that need is not synonymous with financial poverty and that benevolence is a much broader concept than financial assistance."

Further, in so far as they are consistent with the reasons for decision of his Honour, I confirm the findings of fact previously made by this Tribunal (Case U63 87 A.T.C. 401).

5. For the purposes of determining the questions now arising for consideration it is agreed that the evidence taken upon the previous hearing should be taken into account along with the additional evidence presented upon the further hearing. That evidence establishes that (inter alia) Launceston Legacy operated five bank accounts:-

(a) one was the principal welfare account used in the provision of services to those persons whom Legacy existed to serve;

(b) two accounts were impressed accounts operated by branches for the same purpose;

(c) a fourth account was used exclusively in relation to a mortgage of the headquarters of the organization; and

(d) a "Members' Account". I

6. At the hearing it was conceded by Counsel for the Commissioner that the first four of the accounts satisfied the requirements of s.11 of the Act. Accordingly, the Tribunal will order that the certificates of exemption should have been granted. In consequence, the only matter which remains to be resolved is whether the fifth account - described as the "Members' Account" - is one in relation to which a certificate of exemption should have issued.

  1. The Members' Account is an account upon which twenty-two cheques were drawn during the year of income ended 30 June 1986. It was agreed that the evidence for that year should be taken as establishing the use made of the account in all years to date; and also the prospective use of the account as assessed at all material times. In that year the tax payable by Launceston Legacy, assuming it to be liable to pay Bank Accounts Debits Tax, would be $4.65. That figure does not adequately reflect the view of the Commissioner as to the importance of the issue. That importance may be better assessed by considering that the Commissioner retained Senior Counsel to travel interstate to argue the Commissioner's case. Launceston Legacy was represented by one of its members - an accountant.
  2. S.11 of the Administration Act 1982 deals with the granting and the revocation of certificates of exemption. It provides:-

"(1) Where an account holder in respect of an account kept in Australia applies to the Commissioner in accordance with this section for the issue of a certificate of exemption in relation to the account-

(a) if the Commissioner is satisfied that all debits made, or to be made, to the account are or are likely to be, either excluded debits or exempt debits - he shall issue a certificate of exemption in relation to the account; or

(b) if the Commissioner is not so satisfied - he shall refuse the application and shall cause notice in writing of his decision in relation to the application to be served, by post or otherwise, on the person who made the application.

(2) A certificate of exemption comes into force on a day specified in the certificate as the day of commencement of the certificate (which may be a day before the day on which the certificate is issued) and remains in force until the expiration of the day specified in the certificate as the day of expiry of the certificate or, if no day is specified as the day of expiry of the certificate, until the certificate ceases to be in force by virtue of sub-section (5).

(3) Where the Commissioner-

(a) is notified by the account holder, or either or any of the account holders, of an exempt account that an eligible debit has been, or is to be, made to the account; or

(b) becomes satisfied that an eligible debit has been, or is to be, made to an exempt account,

he may, in his discretion, by writing signed by him, revoke the certificate.

(4) Where-

(a) an eligible debit has been made to an exempt account; or

(b) an account holder of an exempt account expects that an eligible debit will be made to the account within the ensuing period of 30 days,

and the account holder, or one of the account holders, does not within 7 days notify the Commissioner in writing accordingly, the account holder, or each of the account holders, as the case may be is guilty of an offence punishable on conviction by a penalty not exceeding $1,000.

(5) Where the Commissioner has revoked a certificate of exemption in relation to an account, he shall serve, by post or otherwise, notice of that revocation-

(a) on the account holder or, if there are 2 or more account holders, on each of them; and

(b) on the bank with which the account is kept,

and, notwithstanding that any day of expiry shown on the certificate has not occurred, the certificate ceases to be in force in relation to the account when the notice is served on the bank.

(6) An application made for the issue of a certificate of exemption shall be in writing and the person making the application shall furnish such information as the Commissioner requires in connection with his consideration of that application."

The concept of "excluded debit" is defined by s.3(1) which, so far as is material, provides:-

"In this Act, unless the contrary intention appears-

excluded debit' means a debit-

(a) made to an account kept with a bank in the name of-

(vi) any of the following:

(A) a public benevolent or a religious institution;

(B) ........................................................................................

(C )

being a debit made in relation to a transaction or transactions carried out by or on behalf of the institution, wholly and exclusively in furtherance of its objects;"

9. The essential argument for the Commissioner is that, so long as any one of the twenty-two transactions of Launceston Legacy giving rise to the debits was not "wholly and exclusively in furtherance of its objects", no certificate of exemption could issue. If the argument is correct, it follows in the circumstances of the Applicant that, had Launceston Legacy operated a single account and the same twenty-two transactions as were conducted through the Members' Account had been conducted through that all purpose account, there would have been no entitlement to any exemption certificate. That would be despite any overwhelming preponderance of transactions which indisputably would have fallen within the "wholly and exclusively" test and despite the voluntary and charitable nature of Legacy and its status as a "public benevolent institution".

10. Such a conclusion at first instance would appear to lack the quality of common sense which is said to be a hallmark of the common law. But the question raised is not to be determined by any such simple generalizations. It must be determined by reference to the prescriptions for the imposition of the tax laid down in the Administration Act. It is, therefore, necessary to consider carefully and apply precisely the provisions of that Act. If the Act calls for an unjust or unreasonable conclusion, it is not for this Tribunal to correct it but rather for the Parliament to do so. On the other hand, it is not to be presumed that a construction of the legislation is to be favoured which produces unjust or unreasonable results.

11. To appreciate the use to which the Members' Account was to put it is necessary to understand the modus operandi of Launceston Legacy. It is founded in a fellowship born of war service in conflicts in which this Commonwealth has been engaged. It functions through those survivors of such conflicts who have joined together in Legacy to render service to the widows and children of deceased servicemen. They meet regularly to provide mutual support for each other in that undertaking and to organize the discharge of the responsibilities they have assumed. They take care to ensure that those providing the services – the Legatees who are the members of Launceston Legacy - shall not be beneficiaries of, or a financial burden to, the organization or to those whom it exists to serve. To that end a separate bank account - the Members' Account - is maintained. It is drawn on to meet any financial obligations which might be thought to be of personal benefit or advantage to the Legatees. It is wholly funded by the contributions of Legatees. The account is so managed and funded that a sufficient sum is available to enable an annual contribution to be made to the Gellibrand Scholarship (named for the founder of Legacy) and to the Welfare Fund.

12. The annual cycle of Legacy activities includes bi-monthly luncheons catered for by a Ladies' Auxiliary. City members contribute on a half-yearly basis and thereby pay for the costs of their meal. Normally more than half the City members attend. Country members contribute on an ad hoc basis on the occasions of their attendance. Donors are sometimes received as honoured guests. Guest speakers are also invited from time to time. Twice per annum cheques are drawn in favour of the Ladies' Auxiliary in payment for their services in providing the meals.

13. Following the Annual General Meeting, a formal dinner is held each year which is attended by the patron: His Excellency, the Governor of Tasmania. Members attending make contributions sufficient to cover the costs of the dinner and cheques are drawn to meet the costs of that function. In recognition of the importance of support from the families of Legatees, each year a social function is held which is attended by spouses. The Legatees contribute the costs of such an evening and payment for attendant expenses is made in due course. Liquid refreshments are available on all such occasions. They are wholly paid for by Legatees either as part of cost of attending the annual functions or on a "user-pays" basis at luncheons. The accounts of suppliers are settled on a monthly basis. On one occasion a wreath was provided on the occasion of a funeral of a person involved with Launceston Legacy.

  1. In summary then the cheques drawn were as follows :-

Scholarship and Welfare Fund 2

Annual Dinner and Ladies Nights

(excluding liquid refreshments) 4

Luncheons

(excluding liquid refreshments) 2

Liquid refreshments 13

Wreath 1

The number of cheques relating to "liquid refreshments" gives a false impression of the significance of that expenditure. The relatively large number of cheques is occasioned by resort to multiple suppliers and the practice of settling their accounts on a monthly basis.

15. The essential argument for the Commissioner is that, with the exception of the cheques effecting donations to the Gellibrand Scholarship and to the Welfare Fund, all the debits relate to the purposes of the Legatees and, in any event, are not aptly described as being debits made "wholly and exclusively in furtherance of (Legacy's) objects" of service to the widows and children of deceased servicemen.

16. I reject the argument. Each of the undertakings I have described, whether it be a bi-monthly luncheon, the annual dinner, the yearly social function, or the provision of a wreath, are directed to furthering the benevolent objects of Legacy.

17. It is argued that the Administration Act requires an "item-by-item" approach to the question of whether particular accounts should be the subject of exemption certificates. In that regard it is to be noted that, although certificates of exemption relate to accounts [s.11(1)], consideration of the inter-relationship between s.8 and s.14 of the Administration Act and the very terms of the Bank Account Debits Tax Act (No. 141 of 1982) support that view. The Tax Act provides by s.4:-

"Tax is imposed in respect of-

(a) each taxable debit made... to a taxable account;
  • (b) each eligible debit made... to an exempt account; and

(c) "

18. If a taxable debit is made to a taxable account, the bank with which the account is kept is liable to pay the tax [s.8(1)]. In this instance the Commissioner asserts that the banker conducting the Members' Account is so liable, and to recover the amount so paid from Launceston Legacy (s.10), but the Commissioner acknowledges that s.14 provides that, upon application made and proof to the satisfaction of the Commissioner that "an excluded debit (has been) made to a taxable account..." the Commissioner shall effect a refund. On the other hand, s.8(2) provides that if "an eligible debit (is) made to an account other than a taxable account" - that is to an exempt account - the account holder is liable to pay the tax. In order to determine whether debits made to accounts kept by entities such as "public benevolent institutions" constitute an "excluded debit", regard must be had to the question whether the debit was "made in relation to a transaction or transactions carried out by or on behalf of the institution... wholly and exclusively in furtherance of its objects".

  1. In support of the Commissioner's view, it is contended that his stance is also confirmed by a consideration of s.11(3) which provides that where "an eligible debit has been, or is to be, made to an exempt account, (the Commissioner) may, in his discretion, by writing signed by him revoke the certificate (of exemption)". Further, s.11(4) provides that even the making of a single eligible debit to an exempt account - which might cause loss to the revenue of as much as one dollar - renders the account holder guilty of an offence punishable on conviction by a penalty not exceeding one thousand dollars, even though there may have been no avoidance of tax.
  2. It would seem that further support for the argument might be had upon a consideration of s.14. If the argument for the Commissioner succeeds, it involves the proposition that the exemption certificate is to be denied because in this instance some of the twenty-two cheques will not occasion "excluded debits". The Commissioner concedes that two of the twenty-two cheques alone generated excluded debits. S.14 provides for an entitlement to a refund of tax. It can therefore be seen that refusal of an exemption certificate will not expose Launceston Legacy to a tax not properly exigible. What that will mean is that, instead of the public purse being called on to bear the expense initially of an item-by-item analysis of transactions likely to be undertaken on the account in order to ensure that the Revenue is not denied the benefit of the tax, the Applicant will have the expense of identifying to the revenue the refunds it claims and then the Commissioner, having examined the claim, will authorise the appropriate refund: in this instance, on the Commissioner's argument, 50 cents.
  3. A construction of the legislation which leads to such a result must be of doubtful merit. In my view any argument which leads to that result in these circumstances is unsound.
  4. Unquestionably the Act does provide for the imposition of the tax on an "item-by-item" basis. No doubt but for the existence of the mechanical efficiency and mindless insensitivity of computers, the cost of calculating the tax on an "item-by-item" basis would exceed the Revenue to be generated thereby. The Administration Act makes specific provision, through the definition of "exempt debit", to exclude from the scope of the tax debits not occasioned by the operation of an account by a customer. Provision is also made for relieving banks from liability to pay the tax in relation to some accounts identified to the banks by certificates of the Commissioner as exempt accounts. Whenever such a certificate is granted, the bank is exempted from liability so long as the certificate of exemption exists. Further, so long as such a certificate of exemption exists, no-one will be liable to pay debits tax unless an "eligible debit" (that is, a debit other than an excluded debit or exempt debit) is made to an account. If, despite the existence of a certificate of exemption, such, an eligible debit is made to an account, liability to payment of the tax falls on the account holder [s.8(2)], as does liability to conviction and punishment for an offence [s.11(4)].

23. In considering the construction to be given to the statutory requirement that "excluded debits" in relation to the accounts of "public benevolent institutions" are limited to debits "made in relation to a transaction or transactions carried out by or on behalf of the institution... wholly and exclusively in furtherance of its objects", it is appropriate to take into J account the need to apply the legislation in a practical way to accounts both large and small. In relation to Launceston Legacy, that has already called for a detailed examination of five accounts: an examination which has persuaded the Commissioner in four instances that certificates of exemption should be granted. Further, in relation to the one account of the five in relation to which he was not so persuaded the "item-by-item" approach requires only a consideration of twenty-two cheques. It stands in contrast to the task facing the Commissioner in considering claims for exemption and the task for this Tribunal in reviewing unsuccessful claims for exemption where even in one account it might be necessary to consider on an "item-by-item" basis forty thousand and more cheques [cf. Australian Broadcasting Corporation and Commissioner of Taxation decision this day (NT86/10365)].

24. In order to determine whether or not Launceston Legacy is entitled to the exemption sought I must be satisfied that the debits in question were incurred "in furtherance of its objects"; and then that it is apt to say that they were so incurred "wholly and exclusively".

  1. In my view each of the undertakings I have described, whether it be a bi-monthly lunch, the annual dinner, or the yearly ladies night or the provision of a wreath, are directed to furthering the benevolent objects of Legacy. Furthermore, I am satisfied that they were "wholly and exclusively" so incurred. In particular, I reject any contention that Launceston Legacy had some object of social fraternization to be considered independently of or in any other way than as subordinate to the benevolent objects which make it the "public benevolent institution" that it is. I do not accept that the institution of Launceston Legacy was something other than a "public benevolent institution", or as only a social fraternity which had some incidental, but nonetheless meritorious purposes which it effected in the way of public benevolence.
  2. In my view that appreciation provides a common sense and reasonably enforceable and practicable interpretation of the legislation. Once it is established that an "institution' satisfies the test of being a "public benevolent institution", it is to be expected that as a responsible community organization it will receive a certificate of exemption. If it abuses that privilege and permits its bank accounts to be used for other than its own proper purposes, it will remain liable to the tax and to prosecution for each transaction and substantial penalties.

27. The order will be that the certificate of exemption be granted without limit as to time.
