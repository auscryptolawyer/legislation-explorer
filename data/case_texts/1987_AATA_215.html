ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL

Income Tax – Allowable Deductions - Railway employee – cost of flying lessons.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 - s. 51(1)

REASONS FOR DECISION

The question in issue in this application is whether the applicant is entitled to an allowable deduction in respect of fees paid for a course of tuition during the year of income ended 30 June 1985. The course of tuition was in the nature of training as a pilot of aircraft so that the applicant could obtain his instructor's rating, the applicant having obtained a private pilot's licence during an earlier year of income and a commercial pilot's licence during the subject year of income.

  1. The statutory provisions which fall for consideration are section 48, sub-section 51(1) and section 82A of the Income Tax Assessment Act ("the Act").
  2. Section 48 provides that in calculating the taxable income of a taxpayer, the total assessable income derived by him during the year of income shall be taken as a basis and from it there shall be deducted all allowable deductions. Allowable Deduction is defined in sub-section 6(1) as a deduction allowable under the Act.

4. The only provision which could allow the deduction sought in this application is sub-section 51(1) which provides, so far as is relevant, that outgoings to the extent to which they are incurred in gaining or producing the assessable income or are necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for the purpose of gaining or producing such income shall be allowable deductions except to the extent to which they are outgoings of capital or of a capital, private or domestic nature.

  1. The applicant was represented by his accountant and the respondent by one of his officers.
  2. During the year of income the applicant was employed by the Railway Department. His income tax return disclosed income from the following sources:

Railway Department $17,482

Travelling Allowance 593

(paid by Railway Department)

Interest 186

$18,261

7. Deductions were claimed for various outgoings the only relevant one being the claim for flying costs amounting to $4950. In a schedule attached to the return this claim was supported by the following (edited in accordance with section 14ZK of the Taxation Administration Act 1953):

"Taxpayer took up a flying course to gain his private pilots licence which he obtained prior to 30th June, 1984. Taxpayer then decided as he could not ly for profit and it was his intention to earn his living from flying that he would undertake a Commercial Flying Course whereby he could fly for profit. Taxpayer undertook his Commercial Flying course with (blank) Air School, at a cost of $5752.75. During the period from gaining his private licence to gaining his commercial licence taxpayer was undertaking flights on a no profit basis. The following flights were organised and flown with the pasengers meeting only the costs involved:-

  1. 15 Hours Maroochydore/Birdsville/Return - 3 passengers Flying @ $60
  2. 15 Hours Maroochydore/Birdsville/Return - 3 passengers flying @ $60
  3. 4-1/2 Hours Maroochydore/Evans Head/Return - 3 passengers Flying @ $95

1984 4-1/2 Hours

Maroochydore/Evans Head/Return - 3 passengers Flying @ $95

1984 6 Hours

Maroochydore/Pt MacQuarie/Ret. - 4 passengers flying @ $95

1984 10 Hours

Maroochydore/Bathurst/Return - 3 passengers Flying @ $95

After obtaining his commercial pilots licence taxpayer decided to increase his income from flying it would be necessary to obtain his instructors rating. This he did at (blank). Total cost involved in the obtaining of his instructors rating was $700 accomodation and $4500 paid to (blank) a total of $5200.

It is claimed that of the total costs incurred by taxpayer the costs of obtaining his ratings up to the point when he could earn a living from flying, i.e. commercial ratings are not allowable as a deduction. It is claimed that the costs incurred in the further training to gain taxpayers instructors rating and amounting to $5200 are deductible as not being of a capital or private matter but incurred to increase taxpayers income to be derived from flying. Reliance for this claim is based on Case R76 No 3 Board of Review 3rd August, 1984.

Claim is for $5200 less $250 self education costs."

8. In evidence before the Tribunal it was established that the applicant undertook the course for instructor's rating immediately after he completed the Commercial Pilot's Licence training. The applicant's evidence was that he had formed the view that he was more likely to obtain a position as a commercial pilot if he upgraded his flying qualifications to instructor's rating.

9. The full-time flying training undertaken by the applicant during the year of income was made possible because he was granted leave by the Railways. He completed the training in June 1985.

10. Initially the applicant undertook the commercial pilot training with the intention of commencing his own air charter business. That intention seems to have changed during the time he was undertaking the training with the result he did not seek to carry on a business after he completed the pilot training. The applicant resumed his employment with the Railway Department until he commenced employment as a charter pilot with an airline in February 1986.

  1. Air Navigation Regulations and Air Navigation Orders were before the Tribunal. However I need not enter into a discussion of those regulations because it is quite clear that the applicant was not permitted to fly for profit while holding a private licence and that he was required to have logged a minimum of 175 hours aeronautical experience before being eligible to apply for a commercial pilot licence.
  2. This brings me to the non-profit charterwork set out in paragraph 7 above. The applicant's representative sought to argue that the applicant had commenced to carry on a business as a pilot when he organised the non-profit charter flights for friends and acquaintances. The respondent's representative argued that there was no assessable income derived by the applicant from these operations, the passengers merely paying the cost of hiring the aircraft. No payment was received by the applicant for his part in flying the hired aircraft, I think Miss Solly was correct in her submission and I therefore find that the applicant did not derive any assessable income from flying operations prior to February 1986.
  3. The applicant's case depended to a considerable degree on the decision of Taxation Board of Review No 3 in Case R76 84 ATC 530. The repondent's representative sought to distinguish the facts in Case R76 and relied upon the decision of the High Court in FC of T v. Maddalena (1971) 45 ALJR 426, 71 ATC 4161.
  4. The factual situation related in Case R76 is very similar to the facts of this application. The important point of distinction is, however, that the taxpayer in Case R76 commenced to earn assessable income after he obtained his commercial pilot's licence and while he was undertaking the instructor's rating course. He had therefore entered upon employment (on a part-time basis) as a commercial pilot while he was undertaking the instructor's rating course.
  5. The present case is different because of two factors:

(a) the applicant's evidence was that he undertook the instructor's rating course no as to improve his prospects of obtaining employment as a commercial pilot; and

(b) the applicant did not enter upon employment as a commercial pilot until at least 7 months after he completed the instructor's rating course.

16. The points of distinction may be thought to be fine ones but I think Miss Solly was correct in relying on the decision of the High Court in Maddalena's Case (not referred to by the Board in Case R76 although the principle was acknowledged). In Maddalena's Case the High Court held that travelling expenses incurred by a professional footballer in getting a new contract as a footballer were not deductible. That is the case here, the applicant undertook the instructor's rating course for the purpose of getting employment as a commercial pilot. The expenditure thereby incurred was incurred at a point too soon to be said to have been incurred in the course of gaining or producing the applicant's assessable income.

17. It is quite clear that the words "assessable income" in sub-section 51(1) include future assessable income of a taxpayer and are not confined to the assessable income of the particular year of income. (AGC (Advances) Ltd v. FC of T [1975] HCA 7; (1975) 132 CLR 175, 75 ATC 4057). However, to be deductible the outgoing must be found in whatever is productive of the assessable income or if none be produced, would be expected to produce assessable income (Ronpibon Tin NL v FC of T [1949] HCA 15; (1949) 78 CLR 47 @ 56-7).

18. The applicant fails those tests. The outgoings were incurred in qualifying the applicant for employment as a commercial pilot. While it is true that the applicant had qualified under the Air Navigation Regulations as a commercial pilot before he undertook the subject course; he needed to boost those qualifications before he could expect to obtain-employment and derive assessable income as a pilot. In other words, the subject expenditure was incurred for the purpose of enabling the applicant to change his employment from the Railway Department to the new field of employment as an aircraft pilot.

19. The objection decision will be affirmed.

This and the preceding seven (7) pages are a true and correct copy of the Decision and Reasons of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) in the matter of QT86/2214.

Deputy Registrar
