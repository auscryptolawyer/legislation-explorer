Panton and Commissioner of Taxation [1999] AATA 833; (1999) 43 ATR 1172; 99 ATC 2354 (9 November 1999)

Last Updated: 8 October 2009





DECISION AND REASONS FOR DECISION [1999] AATA 833

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEALS TRIBUNAL )

) No QT1998/141

TAXATION APPEALS DIVISION )



Re DOUGLAS BLAIR PANTON

Applicant

And COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION

Respondent

DECISION

Tribunal Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) Mr J D Horrigan (Member) Mr I R Way (Member)

Date 9 November 1999

Place Brisbane

Decision The Tribunal decides that: (a) the objection decisions under review are set aside and the matter remitted to the Commissioner to amend the assessments for the year of income ended 30 June 1988 on the basis that (i) the applicant derived assessable income by way of distribution from Doug Panton Motors Pty Ltd of an amount of $186,941; and (ii) the additional tax imposed by s 223 of the Act be remitted to an amount equal to 80% of the assessed tax payable; and (b) these proceedings have terminated in a manner favourable to the applicant.

Decision No (Sgd) K L Beddoe

Senior Member

CATCHWORDS

INCOME TAX : Assessable Income – Informal liquidation – Family Law property settlement – whether income drived



Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ss 6(1), 25, 44, 108, 223,



Gorton and Anor v F.C. of T. [1965] HCA 1; (1965) 113 CLR 604

W.P. Keighery Pty Ltd v FCT [1957] HCA 2; (1957) 100 CLR 66, 11 ATD 359



REASONS FOR DECISION



9 November 1999 Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member) Mr J D Horrigan (Member) Mr I R Way (Member)



1. The applicant seeks review of an objection decision which disallowed the applicant's objection against an income tax assessment. At issue is an amount of $186,941.00 included by the respondent in the applicant's assessable income for the year of income ended 30 June 1988. The notice of assessment also included an assessment of additional tax for incorrect return in an amount of $283,946.00.

  1. In so far as it is relevant s 25 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 ("the Act") provides that the assessable income of a taxpayer shall include the gross income derived directly or indirectly from all sources whether in or out of Australia. In this context there is no dispute that the applicant was a resident of Australia at the relevant time.
  2. Section 44 of the Act includes in assessable income of a resident taxpayer the dividends paid to the taxpayer by a company in which the taxpayer holds shares and which are paid out of profits derived by the company from any source.
  3. Section 6(1) of the Act defines "dividend" to include:

any distribution made by a company to any of its shareholders, whether in money or other property;

any amount credited by a company to any of its shareholders as shareholders; and

. . .

but does not include –

. . . ;

. . . ; or

. . . .



5. Section 108 of the Act has the general effect of deeming certain payments by a private company as dividends. In particular if a private company pays or credits an amount on behalf of, or for the individual benefit of, an associated person so much, if any, of the amount paid or credited, as in the opinion of the Commissioner represents a distribution of profits shall be deemed to be a dividend paid by the company:

to the associated person as a shareholder in the company;

out of profits derived by the company; and

on the last day of the year of income of the company . . .

6. There is no dispute that the subject company is a private company as defined (s 103A(1)).

7. For the purposes of s 108 a reference to an associated person, in relation to a company, is a reference to:

a shareholder in the company; or

a person who is an associate, within the meaning of s 26AAB, of a shareholder in the company.

This does not appear to us to be a case where the definition of associate in s 26AAB can have any operation. The contrary was not put to us.

  1. In respect of the year of income ended 30 June 1988 s 223 of the Act provided that where a person omits assessable income from a return of income derived by the person that person is deemed to have made a statement that the person did not derive the assessable income during the relevant period (s 223(7)).
  2. Section 223(1) provides, inter alia, that where a person makes a statement to a taxation officer that is false or misleading in a material particular and the tax properly payable exceeds the tax that would be payable in accordance with the statement then the taxpayer is liable to pay, by way of penalty, additional tax equal to double the amount of the excess (s 223(1)).
  3. Section 227(3) vests in the Commissioner and, therefore, this Tribunal a discretion to remit the whole or any part of the additional tax otherwise payable under the Act.
  4. Section 166 of the Act provides that the respondent shall make an assessment of the amount of taxable income of any taxpayer and of the tax payable thereon from the returns of income and from any other information in the respondent's possession.
  5. If the Commissioner is not satisfied with the return furnished by any person, then the respondent may make an assessment of the amount upon which in his judgment income tax ought to be levied, and that amount shall be the taxable income of that person for the purpose of s 166 (s 167).
  6. At the hearing the applicant conducted his own case and Mr Hack of counsel appeared for the respondent. The hearing was conducted in conjunction with the hearing of applications for review by the company. The documents lodged in the Tribunal for each matter pursuant to s 37 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975 were placed before the Tribunal as the T documents and further documents were tendered and marked as exhibits. Oral evidence was given by seven witnesses. Following completion of the hearing further documents were lodged in the Tribunal and admitted into evidence (Presiding Member's direction dated 21 July 1999 refers).
  7. In referring to the T documents we have added the prefix "C" to "T" documents when referring to the T documents relating to the company's applications.
  8. We make the following findings of fact from the documents and oral evidence before us. In that regard we have had difficulty with the applicant's evidence because we formed the opinion that it was reconstructive in much of its content. For that reason we have preferred the contemporaneous documentary evidence where there is a conflict between the applicant's oral evidence and the documents. We have accepted the oral evidence of the other witnesses.

The applicant is and was at all relevant times the controlling mind of businesses carried on through several entities of which Doug Panton Motors Pty Ltd ("the company") is the relevant one for present purposes.

The businesses had their beginnings in the 1950's with the company being incorporated on 20 March 1958 (CT8/124).

(c) The company's issued and paid up capital was apparently structured in a way similar to the company structure found in Gorton and Anor v F.C. of T. [1965] HCA 1; (1965) 113 CLR 604. At 30 June 1987 the capital and shareholdings were as follows:

Class No Shareholder

1% first non-cumulative preference 10,000 Applicant

1% second non-cumulative preference 4,000 1 Ex-wife Applicant

Ordinary shares 5 Applicant as trustee for the Susan Alexa Panton Trust

Deferred ordinary shares 5,000 Associated Company



The primary beneficiary of the Susan Alexa Panton Trust was the applicant's daughter, Susan. By deed dated 29 January 1986 Susan Alexa Panton assigned her beneficial interest in the Trust to her mother on trust for the mother and the applicant. (Exhibit E refers in so far as it was admitted into evidence.)

In an oral judgment on 20 March 1986 Elliott J of the Family Court decided that the daughter's assignment of her interest in the ordinary shares of the company had been perfected and without the need for a transfer of shares (CT30). The effect was to transfer the equitable interest to the applicant and his ex wife in equal shares thereby transferring beneficial ownership of the capital of the company to the applicant's ex wife and the applicant, subject to the limited rights on winding up of other shares not held by the applicant as trustee.

Consideration of the articles of association of the company has satisfied us that the net worth of the company belonged to the ordinary shareholders. The preference shareholders' interest in the company was limited to the face value of the shares but took priority to the ordinary shares in a winding up both as to capital and arrears of dividends. The preference shares enjoyed a right to a 1% fixed non-cumulative preferential dividend with further participation by way of preferential dividend as determined by the Directors. The latter dividend rights ceased on death of the original shareholder (Clause 11 – Articles of Association).

Similarly the deferred ordinary shares had rights to participate in dividends but apparently had no rights other than return of paid up capital on a winding up. The deferred ordinary shares were held by an associated company the capital of which appears to have been structured to take the associated company outside then s 105 of the Act (cf. W.P. Keighery Pty Ltd v FCT [1957] HCA 2; (1957) 100 CLR 66, 11 ATD 359). Nothing turns on this for present purposes. While the associated company was originally intended to be a depository for undistributed income it does not have a right as deferred ordinary shareholder to participate in a liquidation beyond return of capital.

(d) The assets of the company were identified by an affidavit of the applicant dated 6 May 1986 sworn in the Family Court proceedings. The company's assets with valuations were set out as follows:

The property known as "Talgai"

Homestead including all buildings, fencing and

Improvements $616,000.00

Plant Machinery and Equipment 90,356.00

Furniture 46,163.00

Cattle 71,333.00

Motor Vehicles 4,250.00

Unsecured loan to applicant 25,410.00

Secured loan Administration Pty Ltd $ 25,876.00

$874,388.00

(Incorrectly added in the affidavit as $834,388.00).

The affidavit also identified the liabilities of the company showing a total of $570,235.93 including a contingent liability of $136,000.00 being estimated cost for stabilisation of Talgai Homestead. Putting aside that contingent liability the net worth of the company may be calculated, $874,388.00 - $434,235.00 = $440,153.00 being the net assets of the company. Subject to the return of capital rights of the other classes of shares the net worth of the company belongs to the five ordinary shares the subject of the trusts. In this regard investigating accountants arrived at substantially higher valuations of the company but it is not necessary that we consider those valuations except that they confirm that the ordinary shares in the company had a substantial value in winding up in excess of $400,000.00.

(e) To comply with orders of the Family Court the applicant was required to arrange the sale of certain assets of the company including the Talgai Homestead property and the cattle on that property. To comply with these orders the proceeds were to be applied for the benefit of the applicant's ex wife in satisfaction of the orders made under the property settlement. The ultimate terms are found in the Deed of Compromise dated 1 May 1987. We are satisfied the Deed must be read in the context of the orders of Bell J made on 9 September 1986. So far as is relevant his Honour made a series of orders on the applicant which included that the applicant pay his wife two sums totalling $260,000.00 and that payment of the monies be secured by a Bill of Mortgage to be procured by the applicant over the company's real estate known as Talgai Homestead. The applicant failed to comply with those orders; by orders of Bulley J dated 22 January 1997 he was restrained from dealing with property and exercising his power as director of the company.

(f) The applicant subsequently entered into a Deed of Compromise whereby it was agreed, inter alia, that:

(i) The applicant take certain steps as director of the company to give effect to the deed;

(ii) The proceeds of sale of cattle owned by the company to be paid to the wife;

(iii) The applicant to instruct Primac to sell further assets of the company with proceeds payable for the benefit of the wife and in the event of these proceeds of sale exceeding $135,000.00 the wife undertook to "refund" the excess to the applicant (Exhibit 1).

(iv) The applicant to pay the wife a further sum of $125,000.00 on or before 9 April 1988; and

(v) The wife was required to transfer her shares in the company and resign as a director of the company but the Deed is silent as to the ordinary shares held on trust and the ex-wife's interest therein.

(g) The company's general ledger shows dividends paid in the period 1 July 1987 to 30 June 1988 totalling $373,882.41 of which $174,220.94 is shown as paid to the wife (including her solicitors) (T32 and Exhibit 3).

(h) By a contract in writing dated 2 March 1988 the company sold the property generally known as Talgai Homestead with improvements and chattels as set out in the contract for $1.7 million (Exhibit G).

(i) It was part of the Deed of Compromise executed by the applicant and his wife and the preceding orders of the Family Court that the wife's interest be protected by a bill of mortgage over the company's lands and this was executed for the company by the applicant and his wife as directors of the company (Exhibit M). The consideration for the mortgage is said to be an order of Bell J made on 9 September 1986.

The Applicant's Case

  1. In essence, as we understand it, the applicant puts his case on the basis that the company made a distribution on a partial liquidation of its assets to his wife either in her capacity as a shareholder or in her capacity as mortgagee of the company's real estate.
  2. In so far as the applicant submits that s 47 of the Act has operation there is no evidence of a liquidation in the year of income (s 47(1)) or a pending dissolution of the company (s 47(2) – 47(2B)) so that submission has no basis in fact.

The Respondent's Case

  1. The respondent says that the payments made to the ex-wife were not made to her in her capacity as a shareholder but in satisfaction of the applicant's obligations under Orders of the Family Court (and the Deed of Compromise).
  2. The respondent says there was no resolution of the company authorising payments of dividends and the withdrawals from the company should be treated as loans by the company to the applicant to which s 108 of the Act apply.
  3. However it is the respondent's primary contention that the distributions by the company were dividends as defined (s 6(1)) and within the terms of s 44(1).
  4. As to the additional tax assessed the respondent submits that no further remission of additional tax is warranted.

Consideration

22. It is clear to us and we think was equally clear to the Family Court that the company was the repository of a significant part of the family wealth of the applicant and his family. Elliott J recognised this in his judgment of 20 March 1986. His Honour also recognised the applicant as the controlling mind of the company and that the five ordinary shares held by the applicant as trustee for his daughter were the effective repository of the wealth of the company. His Honour held that the daughter's assignment of her beneficial interest in the trust was effective to transfer the wealth of the company to the applicant and his wife.

23. We are satisfied that the subsequent proceedings in the Family Court were for this purpose and effected in fact a distribution of portion of the net value of the company to the beneficial shareholders being the applicant and his wife.

24. In so far as the respondent asserts that the distribution was made to the applicant alone we have come to the conclusion that submission is not in accordance with the facts. While the mere fact of payment direct to the wife is not conclusive, bearing in mind the operation of s 19 of the Act, in this case there was an intent evident in the Orders of the Family Court and the Deed of Compromise that proceeds from the sale of company assets were to be applied to the benefit of the wife. The applicant was obliged to give effect to the orders and the Deed of Compromise both personally and in his capacity as permanent governing director. If there be any doubt about that we think the fact of the mortgage of the company property in favour of the wife as mortgagee makes it clear that the wife could look to the company to satisfy the terms of the property settlement.

25. We are satisfied that there were distributions of profits made by the company to its shareholders in the year of income ended 30 June 1988. We have quantified those distributions by relying on the dividend paid account in the general ledger (as prepared by the company's accountant) showing a total of $373,882.00 of which at least $174,220.00 was paid by the company to the applicant's wife (through her solicitors).

26. We have been unable to come to any firm conclusion as to the dissection of the $373,882.00. There have not been any resolutions or other formal action by the company in relation to payment of dividends that we are aware of. There is evidence however that a substantial proportion of the amount in issue was paid to the applicant's wife but we cannot quantify the precise amount. We are satisfied however that it was at least the $260,000.00 provided for in the orders of the Family Court and the Deed of Compromise, and we so find.

27. The fact that the applicant was required to facilitate the distribution to his ex wife does not mean, in our view, that the moneys distributed were thereby derived by him. The distributions were in fact received by the applicant and his ex-wife because of their beneficial interest in the shares held by the trust.

28. For these reasons we are satisfied that the objection decision should be set aside and the objection allowed in part by reducing the assessable income of the applicant by $186,941 being his ex-wife's ½ interest in the distributions by the company.

29. The applicant apparently lodged his 1988 income tax return in June 1995. No assessable income was reported. The return was false and misleading in that it did not report distributions by the company derived by the applicant. Section 223 of the Act therefore applies to impose additional tax at 200% on the tax payable on the assessed taxable income given that the applicant reported a "nil" taxable income. The Commissioner remitted only 21% of the additional tax assessed by the Act.

30. In our view to impose additional tax at approximately 158% of the tax payable could be justified only in extreme cases of tax evasion and is otherwise unfair because it is out of proportion to the tax sought to be avoided. This was subsequently recognised by the Parliament when it amended Part VII of the Act by Act No 101 of 1992.

31. We have come to the conclusion that there should be a further remission of additional tax so as to reduce the amount to an amount that is 80% of the tax payable. In determining the further remission we have taken into account the failure to lodge a return over several years and the failure to disclose income derived when the return was eventually lodged.

32. The objection decisions under review will be set aside and the matter remitted to the Commissioner to amend the assessments for the year of income ended 30 June 1988 on the basis that

(a) the applicant derived assessable income by way of distribution from Doug Panton Motors Pty Ltd of an amount of $186,941; and

(b) the additional tax imposed by s 223 of the Act be remitted to an amount equal to 80% of the assessed tax payable.

33. We will certify that these proceedings have terminated in a manner favourable to the applicant.



I certify that the 33 preceding paragraphs are a true copy of the reasons for the decision herein of Mr K L Beddoe (Senior Member), Mr J D Horrigan and Mr I R Way (Members)
