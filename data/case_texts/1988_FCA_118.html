Re Thorpe Nominees Pty Limited v the Commissioner of Taxation of the Commonwealth of Australia [1988] FCA 118; (1988) 80 ALR 81, (1988) 88 Atc 4886 (20 April 1988)

FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

Re: THORPE NOMINEES PTY. LIMITED

And: THE COMMISSIONER OF TAXATION OF THE COMMONWEALTH OF AUSTRALIA

No. NSW G149 of 1987

Income Tax

[1988] FCA 118; (1988) 80 ALR 81, (1988) 88 ATC 4886

COURT

IN THE FEDERAL COURT OF AUSTRALIA

NEW SOUTH WALES DISTRICT REGISTRY

GENERAL DIVISION

Bowen C.J.(1)

CATCHWORDS

Income Tax - appeals from Taxation Board of Review to the Supreme Court commenced before 1 July 1986 but heard and determined by Supreme Court after that date - Repeal of s.196 of Income Tax Assessment Act as from 1 July 1986 - Whether appeal still lies from Supreme Court to Federal Court - Effect of new ss.199 and 200 of the Act.

Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, ss.196, 199, 200.

Taxation Boards of Review (Transfer of Jurisdiction) Act 1986.

HEARING

SYDNEY

20:4:1988

Counsel for Appellant: Mr B.R. Pape

Instructed by: John Webb, Symons & Co.

Counsel for Respondent: Mr A.H. Slater

Instructed by: Australian Government Solicitor

ORDER

The motion be dismissed with costs.

NOTE: Settlement and entry of orders is dealt with in Order

36 of the Federal Court Rules.

DECISION

This is an application by the Commissioner of Taxation against Thorpe Nominees Pty Limited for orders that the appeal lodged in this matter be struck out or, alternatively, be dismissed as incompetent. The motion seeks also an order for costs.

2. The matter arises out of notices of assessment for income tax of Thorpe Nominees Pty Limited for the income years ended 30 June 1974 and 30 June 1976, which are contested by that company. Its objections were disallowed on 5 February 1979 and reference was then made of both matters to Taxation Review Board No. 1. On the 6th September, 1985 Board of Review No. 1 by majority decided that the Commissioner's decision on the taxpayer's objections should be upheld. The taxpayer then appealed from that decision to the Supreme Court of New South Wales.

3. Under the law as it stood prior to the 1st July, 1986 the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 provided by s.196 -

(1) The Commissioner or the taxpayer may appeal to a

Supreme Court from any decision of the Board that

involves a question of law.

and in s.196 (5) provided -

(5) The Commissioner or the taxpayer may appeal

against a decision of a Supreme Court on an appeal

or reference under this section -

(a) by leave of the Federal Court of Australia, to

that Court; or

(b) by special leave of the High Court, to that

Court.

4. The proceedings which progressed to the Supreme Court under these provisions came on for hearing before Mr Justice Enderby on the 15th and 16th September, 1986. He delivered judgment on the 27th March, 1987 dismissing the taxpayer's appeal. The taxpayer adopted the view that in light of the amendments to the Income Tax Assessment Act by Act No. 48 of 1986 he now had a right of appeal to the Federal Court and on the 16th April, 1987 he lodged a notice of appeal which did not seek leave pursuant to s.196(5) above. Act No. 48 of 1986 had repealed s.196 as from 1 July 1986 and had introduced changes to other sections in that Part of the Act.

5. It was submitted on behalf of the Commissioner that consequent upon the repeal of s.196 of the Income Tax Assessment Act by Act No. 48 of 1986 no right of appeal to the Federal Court lay from the decision of the Supreme Court either with or without leave. It was claimed accordingly that the taxpayer's appeal should be struck out or dismissed as incompetent.

6. It was submitted on behalf of the taxpayer that the proceedings heard by Mr Justice Enderby of the Supreme Court were validly heard by him in September 1986 notwithstanding the repeal of s.196 by Act No. 48 of 1986 as from 1 July 1986. It was submitted that proceedings which at the date of the repeal of s.196 were part heard or not the subject of a final order were kept on foot by the operation of s.8(c) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 and reference was made to ss.199 and 200 of the Income Tax Assessment Act.

7. Sections 199 and 200, as they stood before the 1st July, 1986, were as follows:

199.(1) The Supreme Court hearing an appeal under

section 197 may make such order as it thinks fit,

and may by such order confirm, reduce, increase or

vary the assessment.

(2) An appeal does not lie from an order referred

to in sub-section (1) except as provided in section

200.

200. The Commissioner or the taxpayer may appeal

against an order referred to in section 199 -

(a) to the Federal Court of Australia;

(b) with special leave of the High Court, to that

Court.

8. Section 199(1) refers to s.197. Section 197, as it stood before 1st July 1986, dealt only with appeals (so called) forwarded directly to the Supreme Court. In consequence ss.199 and 200 in their form at that time had nothing to do with matters in the Supreme Court which came forward as appeals from decisions of a Board of Review. It was upon ss.199 and 200 in their changed form as from 1st July 1986 resulting from the amendments made by Act No. 48 of 1986 upon which the taxpayer relied. The form of these sections as from 1st July 1986 was as follows:-

199. (1) A Supreme Court hearing an appeal under

this Part may make such order in relation to the

decision to which the appeal relates as it thinks

fit, including an order confirming or varying the

decision.

(2) An appeal does not lie from an order referred

to in sub-section (1) except as provided in section

200.

200. The Commissioner or the taxpayer may appeal

against an order referred to in section 199 -

(a) to the Federal Court of Australia;

(b) with special leave of the High Court, to that

Court.

9. It was submitted for the taxpayer that whereas under the provisions in force prior to the 1st July 1986 the provisions of s.199 were specifically related to the hearing of appeals under s.197 which was limited to cases where the original reference from the Commissioner was directly to the Supreme Court, under the form of s.199 after the 1st July, 1986 the position was different. It will be noted that s.199, as it stood when the matter was before Mr Justice Enderby, referred to an appeal "under this Part". This Part is Part V which contains the provisions dealing with both types of appeal to the Supreme Court whether following a hearing by a Board of Review or referred directly to the Supreme Court. Section 200 then provided the taxpayer might appeal against an order made under s.199 to the Federal Court of Australia. It did not provide that any leave was required. It was argued that this was the provision which was applicable to this case and was the provision which supported the lodging of the notice of appeal as it had been lodged on the 16th April, 1987.

10. It is not easy to determine the intention of the legislature in the successive amendments so far as it relates to cases of this particular type of which I am informed there are very few. One other such case is Federal Commissioner of Taxation v Malouf (1987) 2 A.T.C. 4851 heard by Mr Justice Lockhart of the Federal Court on the 22nd September, 1987. In that case his Honour had to consider whether a matter following the path of decision before a Board of Review followed by an appeal to the Supreme Court before the 1st July, 1986 was transferred from the Supreme Court to the Federal Court by operation of sub-s.4(2) and sub-s.4(3) of the Jurisdiction of Courts (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 1987 which operated from 1st September, 1987. His Honour held that cases of this type were not transferred and sent the matter back for hearing before the Supreme Court. He noticed, but left open, the question whether an appeal would lie from the decision of the Supreme Court to the Federal Court in such a case.

11. If the argument for the Commissioner is accepted then the resulting situation would be unusual so far as past income tax legislation on the subject was concerned. There would only be one Court hearing where cases came to the Supreme Court after a hearing before the Board of Review and no appeal would lie from that either with or without leave. If the submissions of the taxpayer are accepted, in the case of an appeal proceeding through the Board of Review and then being heard by the Supreme Court there is an appeal as of right to the Federal Court of Australia whereas previously such second hearing before the Federal Court only arose if leave was granted.

12. In my opinion, it is reasonable to suppose that Parliament did not intend to extinguish entirely the right of appeal to a second court which previously existed whether with or without leave. Such a conclusion would be equally adverse, in a sense, to the Commissioner of Taxation himself as to other litigants. Looking at the successive sets of provisions it appears that the change in wording of s.199(1) by the amending Act No. 48 of 1986 was deliberate and that when it referred to a Supreme Court hearing an appeal "under this Part" it was embracing both types of appeal provided for in Part V, that is, appeals to the Supreme Court from the Board of Review and appeals by direct reference from the Commissioner to the Supreme Court. The alteration in s.200 then refers in terms to that changed provision s.199(1) and gives an appeal as of right to the Federal Court of Australia.

13. In the result I am of the opinion that the motion must be dismissed with costs.
